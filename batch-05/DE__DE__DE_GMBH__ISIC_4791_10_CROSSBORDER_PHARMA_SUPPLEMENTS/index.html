Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a full member of the Eurozone; capital and current account convertibility are unrestricted under Article 63 TFEU. No FX licensing or central-bank pre-approval applies to trade or capital transactions. Repatriation of GmbH profits is unrestricted once a formal shareholders' resolution is adopted under §46 Nr.1 GmbHG and capital-maintenance rules (§§30-31 GmbHG) are satisfied; distributions bear 25% German withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) unless reduced by a tax treaty. Practical hurdles are limited to Bundesbank AWV statistical filings, bank-internal sanctions screening, and proof of tax clearance for larger distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation deadline on export proceeds. Only statistical duties apply: all cross-border payments (including export settlements) equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported by the German-resident enterprise to Deutsche Bundesbank via the Z-Portal within 7 working days of the payment date, using the official balance-of-payments code for the underlying trade transaction; month-end outstanding receivables/liabilities against non-residents above EUR 5 million must additionally be disclosed in the external-asset position report (Auslandsstatus).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Central Bank of Germany and competent authority for external-sector statistical reporting under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV); Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin - banking and payment-institution licensing and AML supervision); Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Klimaschutz (BMWK - foreign trade policy and rules); BAFA (export-control enforcement). Germany operates no separate foreign-exchange-control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High as acquiring/collection rails, Low for primary treasury. BaFin-licensed German EMIs (e.g., Solaris SE, Unzer) and EEA-passported EMIs (Adyen, Mollie, Airwallex, Ebury, WorldFirst) may lawfully provide merchant accounts, multi-currency IBANs, and FX conversion under PSD2/ZAG without a banking licence. Binding constraints: EMIs cannot take deposits or grant loans; client funds must be safeguarded through pooled accounts or insurance; dietary-supplement merchants are classed high-risk by card schemes, attracting 5-10% rolling reserves, 2.5-4.5% merchant discount rates, and monthly turnover limits; a Tier-1 bank account remains mandatory for share-capital escrow, tax payments, and settlement concentration.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.00% corporate income tax (Koerperschaftsteuer) plus 5.50% solidarity surcharge on the CIT amount, plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Effective combined statutory burden in major German cities is generally between 29.83% and 31.90%, depending on the municipality's trade tax multiplier.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.00% dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, producing an effective 26.375% statutory withholding rate on cash dividends paid by a German GmbH, unless an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive reduces or exempts it.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a GmbH with a calendar-year fiscal year, the tax return is due by 31 July of the year following the assessment period. An extension is available upon application under Section 109 AO; if a professional tax adviser prepares the return, administrative practice generally extends the due date to 28 or 29 February of the second year after the assessment period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German tax authorities generally require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for cross-border related-party transactions where annual transaction volumes exceed EUR 5,000,000 for goods and operations, and EUR 500,000 for services. For highly relevant intragroup transactions below these thresholds, documentation can still be demanded during an audit; a Master File is required where aggregate group revenues meet statutory thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply offshore-type Economic Substance Regulations, but the anti-abuse framework requires genuine economic substance to secure treaty benefits and intercompany pricing recognition. A GmbH selling dietary supplements online must maintain a substantive operational presence in Germany, including a physical registered office, management decisions, personnel, and actual logistics or digital sales functions. Section 50d(3) EStG denies treaty relief for arrangements lacking substance, and Section 42 AO permits the tax authorities to disregard letterbox structures. CFC rules under Section 8 AStG additionally impute passive income to German shareholders.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to five years under Section 370 AO; aggravated cases carry imprisonment of up to ten years. Criminal fines and confiscation orders apply in addition to full payment of evaded taxes, late-payment surcharges, and 6% annual default interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing officers face direct criminal prosecution, civil restitution claims by the tax authority, seizure of personal assets, travel bans imposed by the public prosecutor, and disqualification from acting as company directors.
Transfer pricing malpractice and missing documentation

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may estimate taxable income under Section 162 AO; a surcharge of at least 5% of the estimated additional income is imposed for failure to comply with documentation obligations, ranging from EUR 250 to a maximum of EUR 1,000,000; additional income and interest are assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors must establish the arm's length nature and preserve documentation. Personal liability arises where the director knowingly signs incomplete transfer pricing records or obstructs the tax audit; repeated deliberate non-compliance is prosecuted as tax evasion and can result in personal fines and imprisonment.
Shell company and economic substance abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief under Section 50d(3) EStG; disregard of the abusive entity under Section 42 AO; recharacterisation of transactions and retroactive tax assessments; failure to file annual financial statements triggers fines against the GmbH and responsible parties under Section 335 HGB of up to EUR 50,000; asset forfeiture can be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the GmbH's tax debts if assets have been withdrawn or insolvency was caused by wrongful trading; the corporate veil can be lifted for fraudulent misuse; courts may impose travel restrictions, custody, and full civil restitution in severe cases.
AML/regulatory non-compliance in high-risk pharmaceutical e-commerce

Statutory sanction: Violations of LFGB provisions can result in administrative fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment for intentional violations; breaches of the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) are subject to fines up to EUR 1,000,000 per violation (higher in aggravated cases up to 10% of annual turnover), including temporary bans from payment processing; the company may have its merchant acquiring agreements terminated by regulators.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to implement required licences, product notifications, or AML compliance programmes are personally prosecuted for organisational fault; they face personal fines, criminal sentences, confiscation of commission income, travel bans, and civil claims by injured acquirers or cardholders.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and federal tax matters; competent local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporation tax, trade tax, payroll tax and VAT returns. Electronic portals: BZSt Online-Portal and Elster/Mein Elster platform of the German tax administration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity intending to make taxable supplies in Germany must first register with the local Finanzamt via the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to obtain a German tax number (Steuernummer). Separately, it must apply to the BZSt for a value added tax identification number (USt-IdNr.) using VAT-ID application forms; additionally, an Economic Operators Registration and Identification (EORI) number is required for customs transactions. The application may be made through an authorised tax representative; confirmation of VAT registration normally issues within 2–6 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity intending to make taxable supplies in Germany must first register with the local Finanzamt via the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to obtain a German tax number (Steuernummer). Separately, it must apply to the BZSt for a value added tax identification number (USt-IdNr.) using VAT-ID application forms; additionally, an Economic Operators Registration and Identification (EORI) number is required for customs transactions. The application may be made through an authorised tax representative; confirmation of VAT registration normally issues within 2–6 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box entity election exists under German law. Available statutory mechanisms include: VAT fiscal unity / Organschaft election under Sec. 2(2) No. 2 of the German VAT Act (UStG) allowing the pooling of German subsidiaries for VAT purposes; small-business exemption election (Kleinunternehmerregelung) under Sec. 19 UStG for resident entrepreneurs with gross annual turnover not exceeding the statutory threshold; application for non-application of VAT exemption under Sec. 9 UStG for transactions subject to VAT exemption; and no hybrid mismatch entity election beyond the treatment under German anti-tax-haven and CFC rules, with German GmbH always treated as a corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax and trade tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de-minimis registration threshold for non-resident entrepreneurs making taxable supplies in Germany; registration is required from the first taxable supply. For resident entrepreneurs, the small-business (Kleinunternehmer) VAT exemption threshold is EUR 25,000 gross revenue in the preceding year and projected gross revenue not exceeding EUR 100,000 in the current year. For intra-Community distance sales and electronically supplied B2C services, an OSS (One-Stop-Shop) threshold of EUR 10,000 per calendar year applies for EU-established suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exports of goods applies when goods are physically dispatched or transported from Germany to a destination outside the European Union, with substantiating documentation, and the seller is identified as the exporter. For cross-border services (including email-SaaS and newsletter infrastructure), zero-rating is generally not applied as a distinct export refund mechanism; instead the place-of-supply rules determine whether the service is outside Germany. If the non-EU customer is a business, B2B reverse-charge rules apply in the customer's jurisdiction; if the customer is a private consumer, the place of supply is where the customer is established, resident, or has habitual residence, which normally excludes German VAT unless the customer is in Germany. Intra-EU B2B supplies of such services are subject to the reverse-charge mechanism in the recipient's member state, and the EU OSS scheme is required for B2C supplies to EU consumers if cross-border sales exceed the EUR 10,000 threshold.

  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / CMR / proof of export transport)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank payment receipt showing foreign-currency conversion and credit for export consideration)

From 1 January 2025, a domestic B2B electronic invoicing mandate is in force in Germany requiring that invoices be issued in a structured electronic format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0) where the supplier and buyer are both established in Germany. Transmission must be through an electronic data interchange (EDI) interface or an e-mail/API delivery mechanism capable of machine readability. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system; however, the taxpayer must preserve all invoice data for 10 years and make it available to the tax authorities on demand.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating claims are treated as tax evasion under Sec. 370 of the German Fiscal Code (Abgabenordnung – AO). Penalties include imprisonment for up to 10 years in aggravated cases, criminal fines up to EUR 500,000 per evasion act under Sec. 378 AO for negligent administrative violations, statutory late and concealment penalties, and mandatory interest under Sec. 233a AO at 0.5% per month from the original due date. The tax authority will retrospectively assess output VAT on the full invoice amount, disallow corresponding input VAT deductions, and impose penalty surcharges. Directors and managing officers may be held personally liable under Sec. 69 AO for tax losses caused by intentional or culpable breach of duties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility of the euro under EU free-movement-of-capital provisions. No distinction is made between current-account and capital-account restrictions in practice. Profit remittance and dividends may be transferred abroad without prior approval, subject to standard withholding taxes (Abgeltungsteuer plus solidarity surcharge, if applicable) and corporate income tax considerations. No exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no licensing of FX transactions exist for ordinary cross-border payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion obligation for export proceeds. EUR and foreign-currency export receipts may be freely credited to domestic or foreign accounts. However, cross-border payments and receipts exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction or aggregated according to the Bundesbank's statistical thresholds must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank within the prescribed statutory deadlines for external economic statistics (AWV reporting).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank), with delegated foreign-exchange statistical reporting and external payments monitoring under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung); BaFin oversees banking and payment services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary clearing rail, Low as a primary treasury or capital repository. Offshore digital EMIs may offer EU/EEA-located IBANs through licensed e-money institutions under a principal-agent model, but they do not substitute for a German credit-institution relationship. Regulatory constraints include BaFin notification/registration requirements, segregation of client funds, sanctions screening obligations, and reduced legal certainty for capital escrow or dividend-clearing use cases. A domestic German bank account must remain the core operating account.

26.375% (25.0% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 1.375% solidarity surcharge on the 25% withholding tax). This rate applies to dividend distributions before treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a calendar-year GmbH, the corporate income tax return for year Y is due by 31 July of Y+1, and is automatically extended to 28/29 February of Y+2 when prepared by a licensed tax adviser. Non-calendar-year entities must file within seven months of the fiscal year end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border controlled transactions exceeding EUR 500,000 per fiscal year trigger the mandatory Local File; domestic controlled transactions exceeding EUR 2,000,000 trigger the same obligation. A Master File is required if the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000 (Sec. 90 AO plus Transfer Pricing Documentation Regulation).

Economic substance scope: Germany does not prescribe a distinct Economic Substance Regulation of offshore type; however, German tax authorities and courts apply OECD-aligned substance principles. A GmbH that lacks personnel, premises, decision-making capacity, or real business activity can be challenged under Sec. 42 AO (abuse of legal form) and Sec. 50d(3) EStG, causing loss of treaty benefits and reallocation of profits. The EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive is implemented in German law to require genuine economic activity for preferential treatment, especially for licensing and financing arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years or a fine for Steuerhinterziehung under Sec. 370 AO; especially severe cases involving large volumes and shell structures attract up to ten years' imprisonment. Recovery of the full evaded tax plus 6% per annum interest under Sec. 233a AO is mandatory.

Director personal liability: Managing directors signing incorrect tax returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax debt under Sec. 69 AO if the underpayment results from willful or grossly negligent conduct. Criminal conviction triggers restitution orders, travel bans, extradition exposure, and disqualification from holding director positions under Sec. 6 GmbHG.
Shell Company / Artificial Structure

Statutory sanction: Loss carryforwards are forfeited under Sec. 8c KStG for substantial ownership changes; distribution and royalty income is recharacterized and taxed as hidden profit distribution; treaty relief is denied under Sec. 50d(3) EStG; administrative fines under Sec. 42 AO and possible penalty surcharge of up to 10% of the additional tax assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under Sec. 34 and Sec. 69 AO where the lack of substance was knowingly arranged. Personal travel bans, immigration alerts, and civil liability to creditors can arise if the GmbH is held to be a sham under German insolvency law (Sec. 15b InsO).
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failures

Statutory sanction: Upward income adjustment under Sec. 1 AStG; if documentation is not timely delivered, the tax authority may apply a 5% penal surcharge on the adjustment amount and, in cases of complete absence of required documents, a 10% added penalty. Interest on the adjustment accrues at 6% per annum, and mutual agreement procedure under the DTA is precluded when documentation failures are determined as a compliance breach.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties if they intentionally destroyed or withheld transfer-pricing documents; criminal prosecution under Sec. 370 AO may follow when the failure is used as a device for evasion. Travel restrictions and confiscation orders may be issued in serious cases.
AML and Cross-Border Payment Facilitation

Statutory sanction: Breach of the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) by an obliged entity can trigger fines up to EUR 100,000 per violation; unauthorized payment services under the KWG (Sec. 54) attract imprisonment up to five years and fines; money laundering under Sec. 261 StGB leads to asset forfeiture and custody penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors are individually liable for GWG breaches and may be prosecuted under Sec. 261 StGB in connection with knowingly accepting funds from illicit marketing schemes. Criminal courts may impose travel bans and passport seizure; sanction lists and US/EU border-entry alerts are triggered by money-laundering convictions.

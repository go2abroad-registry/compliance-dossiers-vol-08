Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) administers federal tax identification functions and German VAT identification numbers through the BZSt online portal. The competent local tax office (Finanzamt) administers corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT registration, tax returns, and tax numbers. Electronic filings are generally submitted through the ELSTER platform operated by the German tax authorities.

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH must register with the competent local Finanzamt after incorporation and commencement of business. The company submits the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER, providing its commercial-register details, registered office, directors, shareholders, business activities, expected turnover, expected profits, banking information, and VAT obligations. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer) for tax administration. The company separately applies to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer or USt-IdNr.) through the BZSt online application or via the tax registration process. A foreign entity with a German taxable presence follows the same Finanzamt registration process for its German permanent establishment or other taxable activities and must provide constitutional documents, proof of foreign tax residence, German business and management details, and evidence of the German place of business. Non-established foreign businesses may require a German VAT registration where they make taxable supplies for which Germany is the place of taxation and the reverse charge does not apply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is subject to German corporate income tax and solidarity surcharge at entity level and generally to municipal trade tax. Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a GmbH. Statutory structuring mechanisms include a tax-consolidated group (Organschaft) where the statutory conditions are satisfied, including financial integration and a profit-and-loss transfer agreement; participation exemptions for qualifying dividends and capital gains under the German Corporate Income Tax Act; and the statutory small-business VAT exemption where the applicable turnover limits are met. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive implementation provisions generally deny benefits for specified hybrid arrangements and do not provide a general elective hybrid classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany does not provide a general VAT registration threshold that permits an otherwise taxable entrepreneur to disregard VAT. A taxable business must register when it carries out taxable transactions in Germany, subject to place-of-supply rules, reverse-charge mechanisms, and specific exemptions. The small-business exemption under Section 19 of the German VAT Act applies where prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and transitional rules. The exemption generally prevents charging VAT and input-tax recovery; it is not a general exemption from tax registration or recordkeeping.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Germany generally treats qualifying exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union as VAT-exempt with input-tax recovery, provided the goods are transported or dispatched to a non-EU destination and the supplier can substantiate the export through objective documentary or electronic evidence. Qualifying intra-Community supplies to VAT-registered customers in another EU Member State are generally VAT-exempt with input-tax recovery if the goods are transported to another Member State, the customer provides a valid foreign EU VAT identification number, the supplier reports the transaction correctly in the recapitulative statement, and the documentary requirements are met. Services are taxed according to the applicable place-of-supply rule; business-to-business services supplied to a foreign customer may be outside German VAT or subject to reverse charge, but they are not automatically export zero-rated. Consumer-electronics sellers must distinguish exports from domestic sales, intra-Community distance sales subject to the EU One Stop Shop regime, and imports subject to import VAT.

  • Customs export declaration and export accompanying or exit confirmation, generally evidenced through the Automated Export System
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, courier export record, or postal documentation

Germany does not operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance system or mandatory digital tax stamping regime. For domestic business-to-business transactions between German-established businesses, recipients must be able to receive structured electronic invoices from 1 January 2025. Mandatory issuance is phased: generally from 1 January 2027 for issuers with prior-year domestic turnover exceeding EUR 800,000, and from 1 January 2028 for all in-scope domestic B2B transactions, subject to statutory transitional rules. Permitted structured formats include XRechnung and compliant ZUGFeRD formats. Public-sector invoicing is subject to federal and state e-invoicing requirements, including XRechnung or other accepted European-standard formats. Ordinary PDF invoices are not structured e-invoices and do not satisfy the future mandatory B2B issuance requirement. Electronic records must comply with the German principles for proper management and storage of books, records, and documents in electronic form (GoBD).

Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no universal 180-day repatriation requirement. Export VAT exemption can be denied or retrospectively assessed if transport outside the European Union, customer status, destination, or transaction documentation is not proven. The supplier may become liable for the output VAT, statutory late-payment interest, correction obligations, and related assessment consequences. Incorrect VAT returns may lead to administrative fines or criminal liability for tax evasion where intent or recklessness is established; tax evasion may result in imprisonment or fines under the German Fiscal Code. Deliberate or negligent misuse can also trigger correction of input-tax claims, denial of VAT refunds, audit-based assessments, and customs penalties or criminal sanctions for false customs declarations. Documentation must be retained for the statutory retention periods and be available for Finanzamt and customs audits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a fully liberalised currency and capital system. TFEU Articles 63-67 guarantee free movement of capital and payments. Both current account and capital account transactions are freely convertible into EUR, including investment inflows, divestment proceeds, dividends, interests, and royalties. There is no Central Bank approval requirement for profit remittance, no foreign exchange licensing, and no currency rationing. The only practical friction is tax-related: dividends paid by a German GmbH suffer 25% withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (total 26.375%) unless reduced by an applicable double-tax treaty or exempted under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where eligible. Beneficial ownership documentation and current bank account proof must be maintained to obtain treaty relief.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds in whole or in part, and no mandatory conversion ratio into EUR. Exporters are free to retain use of EUR or foreign-currency receivables. However, German residents are required to report cross-border payments and receipts to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistical purposes under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung). Any individual cross-border receipt or payment exceeding EUR 12,500 (including sale proceeds of exported electronics) must be reported electronically within five business days of the end of the reporting month using the Bundesbank's external economic statistics reporting interface (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik). No Central Bank approval or conversion order is attached to export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank; within the Eurosystem, subject to the European Central Bank's monetary policy and Banking Supervision frameworks)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium conditional feasibility. EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) with valid passporting into Germany under PSD2 and registration in the BaFin transparency register may be used for low-balance e-commerce settlement, payout routing, and expedited cross-border disbursements. They must hold client funds in safeguarded accounts. However, EMIs are not credit institutions under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz) and do not enjoy deposit insurance. They are not suitable for statutory capital escrow, substantial treasury reserves, or long-term liquidity holding. Offshore EMIs without a BaFin / EBA authorisation are legally ineligible to maintain a corporate account for a German GmbH and should be entirely excluded from treasury design, as their use would breach counterparty due diligence and audit expectations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer (CIT) plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag (0.825% effective) plus municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax); Berlin example: 3.5% assessment rate x 410% collection multiplier = 14.35% trade tax; combined effective burden on net taxable income approx. 28% - 30% depending on municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer on gross dividends paid by a German GmbH plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on that withholding, resulting in a combined statutory rate of 26.375% (Secs. 43(1), 32a EStG).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation per Sec. 90(3) AO and the German Administrative Principles on Transfer Pricing (Verwaltungsgrundsätze) is mandatory upon request where cross-border controlled transactions exceed EUR 1.5 million per annum for delivery of goods or provision of services, or EUR 750,000 per annum for all other transactions. Master File documentation is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million per annum. Sanction for failure to present documentation: presumption of taxation at the Finanzamt's estimated basis plus penalty under Sec. 162(4) AO of 5% - 10% of the TP adjustment (minimum EUR 5,000; maximum EUR 1,000,000).

Economic substance scope: Germany has no Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substance is a mandatory compliance doctrine enforced through Sec. 42 AO (general anti-abuse rule), CFC rules under Secs. 7-13 AStG, and effective-management jurisprudence. The GmbH must maintain a real registered seat and management seat in Germany, employ staff with decision-making authority, operate actual warehouse/logistics arrangements, and keep board minutes, contracts, invoices, and bank flows demonstrating commercial reality. A letterbox or conduit GmbH without operational substance will be disregarded for tax purposes, its transactions recharacterised, and DTA benefits denied under Sec. 50d(3) EStG. For the online retail model, the entity must evidence the physical flow of goods, return handling, and the German-based management of pricing, marketing, and supplier contracts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / intentional under-declaration of online retail revenue (Steuerhinterziehung, Sec. 370 AO)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 6 months up to 5 years; up to 10 years in aggravated cases (commercial scale, use of shell vehicles, exceptionally high evasion). Monetary fine up to EUR 50,000 or up to 360 daily rates; interest of 0.5% per month (6% p.a.) on evaded taxes; late-payment surcharge 1% per month; seizure of bank accounts, Stripe/PayPal merchant balances, warehouse stock, and real estate; publication of the final judgment; automatic deletion from state procurement lists.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under Secs. 34, 69 AO where their conduct caused the shortfall; they may be jointly and severally liable with the company for the full amount of evaded tax plus interest. Criminal conviction leads to asset freezing, travel bans, prohibition from serving as a director of any German company for up to 5 years, and, for amounts exceeding EUR 1 million, an inevitable prison sentence.
VAT non-compliance and/or participation in e-commerce VAT fraud (Umsatzsteuerbetrug)

Statutory sanction: VAT evasion carries the same criminal penalties as Sec. 370 AO: imprisonment up to 5 years (10 years for commercial-scale fraud). Administrative penalties: 0.5% per month of unpaid VAT under Sec. 18a(4) UStG; late-payment surcharge 1% per month; forced assessment and civil enforcement; permanent exclusion from the SEPA and payment-provider settlement networks in persistent cases.

Director personal liability: The director is personally liable for unpaid VAT under Sec. 128 AO, Sec. 69 AO, and under the insolvency-overpayment doctrine (Haftung für Steuerschulden bei Pflichtverletzung) where funds were used for other creditors instead of the Finanzamt; bank executives are obliged to report suspicious web-retail cash flows to the FIU; travel bans and seizure of personal assets are standard enforcement tools.
Non-filing / late filing of corporate tax returns and prepayments (Verspätungszuschlag, Säumniszuschlag)

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharge under Sec. 152 AO: EUR 25 per month up to 10% of assessed tax, maximum EUR 25,000; late-payment surcharge 1% per month on arrears; interest 6% p.a. under Sec. 233a AO; estimated assessments legally challenged and enforced; coercive fines up to EUR 25,000; bank account and payment-provider seizures; in persistent cases, liquidation proceedings against the GmbH.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to file returns or to pay taxes from managed assets become personally liable for the entire corporate tax debt under Secs. 34, 69 AO; liability attaches even after resignation where the obligation arose during their tenure; civil claims for insolvency trading and breach of duty under Sec. 64 GmbHG are available to the insolvency administrator.
Shell company / letterbox entity / sham transactions (Gestaltungsmissbrauch, Sec. 42 AO)

Statutory sanction: Whole structure disregarded: the Finanzamt taxes income as if the sham transactions did not occur, allocates the retail profits to the actual economic owner, denies DTA relief under Sec. 50d(3) EStG, applies CFC rules, and imposes 6% annual interest on the reassessed tax; supplementary penalty of 5% - 10% of reallocated income under Sec. 162(4) AO; administrative fines up to EUR 25,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling foreign shareholders are jointly and severally liable for reassessed taxes of the disregarded entity; in fraud cases, criminal liability for tax evasion with imprisonment; courts may order travel bans and seizure of foreign assets under the EU Asset Confiscation Regulation; the GmbH may be struck off the commercial register.
Anti-money-laundering breaches in digital payment processing (GwG, AML/CFT framework)

Statutory sanction: Fines under Sec. 56 GwG: up to EUR 1 million for the GmbH for serious breaches; administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual global turnover under the EU AML Regulation once fully applicable; payment-provider termination and blacklisting; possible closure of the Stripe/PayPal merchant account and seizure of funds; prohibition of the business activity.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML fines; the company cannot lawfully indemnify or insure them against GwG penalties; criminal prosecution under Sec. 370 AO for money-laundering facilitation where retail proceeds of counterfeit or fraud-derived gadgets are processed; travel bans and house arrest in aggravated cases.
Environmental and market-access non-compliance for consumer electronics (ElektroG / VerpackG)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 100,000 per violation under Sec. 69 ElektroG; fines up to EUR 200,000 under the VerpackG; immediate marketplace delisting by Amazon and eBay; confiscation of non-compliant stock; the Finanzamt may attach inventory and assert additional CIT/VAT where goods are written off or seized; repeated violations trigger prohibition of business operations.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible compliance officers are personally subject to the fines and can be held criminally liable for e-waste trafficking; persistent violation of the ElektroG serves as evidence of gross organisational fault, overriding the corporate liability shield; managing directors face personal insolvency liability for damages caused to the company and creditors.

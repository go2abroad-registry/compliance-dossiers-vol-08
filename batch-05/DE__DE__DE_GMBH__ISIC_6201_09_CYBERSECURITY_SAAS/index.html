Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the Local Court (Amtsgericht) at the company seat, with electronic filings submitted exclusively through a German notary via the secure EGVP/electronic legal transaction gateway; public searchable portal: Unternehmensregister (www.unternehmensregister.de).

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG), principally Sections 1-3 (formation and articles), 5 (minimum share capital of EUR 25,000), 7-8 (application for registration and 50% contribution rule), 10 (effect of registration), 11 (pre-registration liability), 14-16 (shares), 35-46 (managing directors), 48 (shareholders meeting), 59 and 78; supplemented by Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB) Sections 8-16, Handelsregisterverordnung (HRV), and the law on notarial costs (GNotKG).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag), or the statutory Model Protocol (Musterprotokoll) for simplified formation, together with the Application for Registration of the Company (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) signed by all managing directors in the form prescribed by the HRV.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The nominal share capital must be at least EUR 25,000. Before registration, at least 50% of each cash contribution (aggregate minimum EUR 12,500) must be paid in and be freely at the disposal of the managing directors; for a single-member GmbH the bank must confirm that the deposited amount cannot be repaid to the founder before registration. Contributions are placed on a dedicated company formation bank account held in the company's name; no formal state escrow is mandated by the GmbHG, but banks commonly impose a restricted-disposal notation to protect creditors. In-kind contributions must be fully covered and their value stated in the Articles of Association.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office, Bonn/Berlin (issuance of the USt-IdNr. under § 27a UStG, OSS/IOSS administration, and VAT-ID confirmation certificates under § 22 UStG); local Finanzamt (tax office) — exclusive jurisdiction over VAT assessments, payroll/trade tax, and assignment of the Steuernummer; electronic filing portal: ELSTER Online-Portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A resident German GmbH must file the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt within one month after formation; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer and forwards the VAT-ID request to the BZSt, which issues the USt-IdNr. (typical processing time 4–6 weeks) together with a certification under § 22 UStG. A foreign (non-resident) entity without a German seat or permanent establishment must file the same questionnaire/EU-foreign-entrepreneur VAT registration form with the locally competent Finanzamt (jurisdiction under § 21 AO; some Bundesländer centralize foreign-entity VAT registration in a designated Finanzamt) before commencing any taxable supply, and must then apply to the BZSt for the USt-IdNr.; no German fiscal representative is required for ordinary e-services, and paper-based filing is accepted where ELSTER digital authentication is not yet available.

(typical processing time 4–6 weeks) together with a certification under § 22 UStG. A foreign (non-resident) entity without a German seat or permanent establishment must file the same questionnaire/EU-foreign-entrepreneur VAT registration form with the locally competent Finanzamt (jurisdiction under § 21 AO; some Bundesländer centralize foreign-entity VAT registration in a designated Finanzamt) before commencing any taxable supply, and must then apply to the BZSt for the USt-IdNr.; no German fiscal representative is required for ordinary e-services, and paper-based filing is accepted where ELSTER digital authentication is not yet available.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Available election forms

German law offers no US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch 'elections'; the applicable statutory mechanisms include: (i) Organschaft (VAT group and corporate-tax fiscal unity) under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG and § 14 KStG, contingent on financial, economic, and organizational integration; (ii) option under § 9 UStG to waive VAT exemptions (e.g., real estate transactions); (iii) small-business regime under § 19 UStG for domestic entrepreneurs with prior-year turnover ≤ €25,000 and current-year forecast ≤ €100,000; (iv) OSS/IOSS election for intra-EU distance sales and B2C electronic services to avoid multiple foreign VAT registrations; (v) reverse-charge mechanism for cross-border B2B supplies. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are mandatorily neutralized by §§ 4k, 4l EStG implementing ATAD-2.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully liberalised foreign-exchange regime. The euro is fully convertible on both the current and capital accounts pursuant to Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital) and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG). There are no surrender, rationing, licensing, or waiting-list restrictions on capital inflows or outflows for a resident GmbH. Profit remittance is legally unrestricted: distributable profits may be transferred abroad by a shareholder resolution without any FX authorisation. The only frictions are tax-related (25% German dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, totalling 26.375%, prima facie reducible under the applicable double-tax treaty with the shareholder's residence country) and statistical (outbound dividend payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five business days).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Germany imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for the surrender or repatriation of export proceeds. A GmbH may retain SaaS subscription and export revenue in any foreign currency indefinitely in a foreign-currency account with a German credit institution. The only binding obligations are reporting duties under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV): cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 received or made by a German resident must be declared to the Deutsche Bundesbank's external statistics (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik / AMA – Allgemeines Meldeverfahren) within five business days, using the correct institutional-sector and purpose code.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank), in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) for euro-area monetary policy and with the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for prudential banking supervision, licensing, and sanctions enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational disbursements and multi-currency payment routing; Low for serving as the primary corporate treasury bank or as the statutory deposit institution for the GmbH's initial share capital. Cross-border digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) holding a European payment-institution licence under PSD2 / the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) may be passported into Germany and can open business accounts remotely. However, under the German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbH-Gesetz §7), the statutory capital contribution (Stammeinlage) must be placed at the free disposal of the managing director with a KWG-licensed credit institution in Germany; EMIs and payment institutions, which are not credit institutions under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz – KWG), cannot act as the notary-verified capital escrow bank. In addition, German Tier-1 banks commonly classify EMI-linked flows as higher-risk pass-through transactions, which may delay settlement of SaaS subscription receipts. EMIs are therefore recommended only as a secondary channel for USD/GBP conversion and cross-border vendor or employee payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections for DE_GMBH must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or the bank's corporate e-banking channel carrying full purpose-code fields) to a KWG-licensed German credit institution. For the initial formation, the non-resident shareholder wires the fully paid minimum share capital (EUR 25,000 minimum, with at least EUR 12,500 paid in per founding shareholder at registration) to the notary-designated Capital Escrow Account, with the SWIFT Field 70 containing the purpose text 'Stammeinlage zur Errichtung der GmbH' and the notary reference number; the bank then issues the deposit confirmation (Bestätigung über das eingezahlte Stammkapital). For subsequent capital increases or shareholder-loan injections, Field 70 must specify 'Kapitalerhöhung' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' together with the share-resolution or loan-agreement reference. The receiving bank will initiate the Bundesbank balance-of-payments notification process under the AWV/AMA taxonomy for every cross-border receipt above EUR 12,500, classifying the flow under the direct-investment capital code; the GmbH, as the resident party, remains legally responsible for the completeness and correctness of that report. The remitter's name and account must exactly match the shareholder recorded in the commercial register to prevent beneficial-owner screening delays; pre-submission of the SWIFT draft to the relationship manager is advisable for contributions above EUR 50,000.Operational clearing rails for a cybersecurity SaaS GmbH are layered as follows: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated payments to EU/EEA suppliers, cloud hosting vendors, subcontractors, and employees; (ii) SWIFT MT103 with correspondent bank routing for USD, GBP, and CHF payments covering international cloud infrastructure, US-based insurance and marketing platforms, and overseas contractors; (iii) commercial documentary letters of credit (UCP600) remain available at Tier-1 banks but are economically unjustified for digital SaaS trade and should be reserved for any future physical hardware procurement or resale transactions outside the current ISIC 6201 scope; and (iv) internal liquidity concentration may be achieved through the multi-currency current accounts at Deutsche Bank or UniCredit with daily automated sweeps into the EUR treasury account. The Stripe payment-acquirer wallet remains the primary inbound merchant rail for SaaS subscriptions; treasury policy should set Stripe settlement in EUR even for USD card transactions, using Stripe's transparent wholesale FX conversion, while wire-transfer invoices above EUR 10,000 are settled in the original invoice currency to preserve the FX-hedging programme and reduce conversion leakage.Outright FX forwards (deferred) in EUR/USD and EUR/GBP with a German KWG-licensed bank, sized against a realistic forecast of USD-denominated SaaS receivablesSpot/forward FX swaps for liquidity management, including the temporary conversion of expected USD inbound wires into EUR and re-conversion at maturityEuropean-style currency options in EUR/USD and EUR/GBP, purchased as caps or floors to protect material invoice exposures while retaining upside on favourable rate movementsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on emerging-market currencies (e.g., INR, BRL, IDR) where the local FX regime prohibits physical settlement and only US-dollar cash settlement is availableCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) and long-dated FX swaps for hedging shareholder loans, acquisition financing, or pre-IPO currency-risk exposure

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for operational disbursements and multi-currency payment routing; Low for serving as the primary corporate treasury bank or as the statutory deposit institution for the GmbH's initial share capital. Cross-border digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) holding a European payment-institution licence under PSD2 / the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) may be passported into Germany and can open business accounts remotely. However, under the German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbH-Gesetz §7), the statutory capital contribution (Stammeinlage) must be placed at the free disposal of the managing director with a KWG-licensed credit institution in Germany; EMIs and payment institutions, which are not credit institutions under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz – KWG), cannot act as the notary-verified capital escrow bank. In addition, German Tier-1 banks commonly classify EMI-linked flows as higher-risk pass-through transactions, which may delay settlement of SaaS subscription receipts. EMIs are therefore recommended only as a secondary channel for USD/GBP conversion and cross-border vendor or employee payments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 for controlled goods transactions; EUR 100,000 for controlled services (and intangibles/licences) per fiscal year

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain EU-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, general anti-abuse rule (§42 AO) and CFC rules (§§7–13 AStG) deny tax benefits where a GmbH lacks real business purpose and substance (personnel, management decisions, and operational activity in Germany). Shell structures are disregarded for tax purposes and attributed to beneficial owners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 10 years (§§369 AO, 370 AO; §263 StGB); monetary fine equal to lost tax; surcharge up to €25,000; seizure of assets

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (including withholding tax) if intentionally or negligently breached duties; travel ban under §132 AO; criminal conviction leads to loss of office.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of €1,500 per day up to €1,000,000 gross per documentation item; tax authorities may shift profits and impose upward pricing adjustments with 6% interest surcharge (§155 Bewertungsgesetz; §162 AO)

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign incorrect declaration or fail to enforce documentation may become debtors under §69 AO; management liability for resulting tax arrears.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Disregard of GmbH as taxpayer; profits re-allocated to beneficial owner; loss of treaty benefits; denial of trade tax basis; penalties up to €1 million

Director personal liability: GmbH directors or shadow directors may be personally liable for all corporate taxes uncovered after substance denial; possible extradition in cross-border fraud cases.
Withholding Tax Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Liability for unpaid withholding tax plus late payment surcharges (1% monthly); fine up to 10% of unpaid tax; for intentional evasion, criminal penalty

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for withheld taxes not transferred to tax authority; bank guarantees or direct seizure of private assets in insolvency proceedings if taxes due.

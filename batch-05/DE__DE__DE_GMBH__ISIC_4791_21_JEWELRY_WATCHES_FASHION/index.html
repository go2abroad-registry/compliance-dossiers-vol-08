Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF) and Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for tax/VAT ID and OSS schemes; local Finanzamt (tax office) with territorial jurisdiction for income, corporation, and trade tax assessment; sole electronic filing portal: ELSTER (Elster.de); export/customs authority: Generalzolldirektion (ATLAS system).

E-government filing portal

(1) On incorporation, notarize GmbH charter, evidence minimum capital of €25,000 (half paid-in minimum), register in Commercial Register (Handelsregister). (2) File the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the local Finanzamt within one month of business commencement via ELSTER to obtain the Steuernummer (tax number) for VAT advance returns and corporation/trade tax assessments. (3) Apply separately to BZSt for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) via the BZSt online portal. (4) Obtain an EORI number (Registration and Identification Number) from the Generalzolldirektion for export customs declarations. (5) Register for the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme with BZSt if making EU-wide distance sales to non-taxable consumers. (6) Submit all returns electronically (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung monthly/quarterly, annual tax returns, E-Bilanz) through ELSTER-certified software or a mandated tax advisor.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box concept; GmbH classification as an opaque, entity-level-taxed corporation is statutory under § 1 KStG. Available elections: (1) Organschaft (fiscal unity) for both corporate tax (§§ 14–17 KStG) and VAT (§ 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG) where a 95%-owned subsidiary is financially and organizationally integrated; (2) Small-business exemption option under § 19 UStG for low-turnover taxpayers up to €25,000 annual revenue; (3) Neutralization of hybrid mismatches is mandatory, not elective, applying §§ 4k and 4l EStG per BEPS Action 2, with no beneficial anti-hybrid election available; (4) Option to tax previously VAT-exempt real estate supplies under § 9 UStG (not relevant to jewelry e-commerce); (5) Participation exemption in dividend/capital gains taxation requires no election when holding ≥10% shareholding for ≥12 months.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory registration threshold: a GmbH is mandatorily VAT-registered for all supplies of goods and services from business commencement (§ 2 UStG). Optional Kleinunternehmer relief up to €25,000 annual turnover is structurally inapplicable to a capital-company e-commerce operation. EU cross-border distance-sales threshold: €10,000 aggregate across the EU; above this, customer-jurisdiction VAT via OSS applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries to third countries (outside the EU) are VAT-exempt at 0% under § 6 UStG provided: (1) the goods physically leave EU customs territory by the end of the third calendar month following the supply date; (2) the exporter retains full bookkeeping and documentary proof of exit per §§ 9–10 UStDV; (3) commercial terms and transport documents are consistent and issued in the exporter's name. Intra-Community B2B supplies are additionally eligible for zero-rating subject to valid customer VAT ID validation, EC Sales List filing, and proof of transport to the member state of destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration via ATLAS system: export clearance certificate (Austuhranmeldung) with Movement Reference Number (MRN) confirming physical exit from EU customs territory and third-country customs entry evidence (where required)
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement) or Air Waybill (Luftfrachtbrief) evidencing the consignment from Germany to the non-EU destination, including the commercial invoice describing the jewelry/watches
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank/payment-service-provider confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign-currency settlement from the non-EU buyer (FX repatriation evidence)

Finanzamt/BMF export verification audits demand full compliance with § 6 UStG and §§ 9–10 UStDV; incomplete or inconsistent export documentation automatically reverses zero-rating, triggering retrospective 19% VAT plus monthly interest surcharge (0.5% per month on arrears). Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under § 370 AO (imprisonment up to 5 years, aggravated cases up to 10 years, unlimited fines), and ancillary penalties under § 26a UStG apply to unlawful VAT documentation. Bookkeeping and export-proof retention is legally mandated for 10 years (§ 147 AO); customs authorities hold a 10-year limitation window for retrospective clawback in evasion cases (§ 169 AO).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates full current and capital account convertibility under EU law (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Art. 63) and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (AWV). No exchange control permits are required for any payments, including profit remittances. Dividend distributions to foreign shareholders are generally subject to 25% German withholding tax (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax) unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty (commonly 15% or 0% under EU parent-subsidiary directive). Profit remittance is free from foreign exchange restrictions. Banks must report all cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 to the Bundesbank's external statistics (AWV reporting) within seven days.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose any mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may freely hold export receipts in foreign currency accounts at German banks or abroad. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation and no requirement to convert foreign currency into EUR. However, all cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 (or equivalent) must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical and balance-of-payments monitoring purposes. The reporting obligation rests on the resident bank, but the exporter must provide accurate underlying data (purpose code and transaction details).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as auxiliary clearing rail; Low as primary treasury bank. Foreign digital EMI licences (e.g., Revolut, Wise) valid under EEA passporting can open accounts remotely and provide IBANs, but they are not credit institutions under German Banking Act and cannot provide credit facilities or complex FX swaps. For a Medium-Risk jewellery/watches e-commerce business, such EMIs are legally viable for payment collection via connected gateways but face enhanced scrutiny and may not support incoming capital injections or large trade settlement wires due to strict prefunding requirements and limited correspondent network. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax, plus a solidarity surcharge equal to 5.5% of the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal corporate income tax burden of 15.825%. Trade tax is imposed separately by the municipality; the nationwide average combined corporate and trade tax burden is approximately 29% to 30%, but the exact rate depends on the municipal trade-tax multiplier.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, resulting in an aggregate domestic withholding burden of 26.375%. A qualifying EU corporate parent may obtain a full exemption under the German implementation of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally requiring at least a 10% direct participation, a minimum holding-period condition, qualifying legal forms and taxation, and satisfaction of anti-abuse rules. A tax treaty may reduce the rate, commonly to 5% for a qualifying substantial corporate holding or 15% for portfolio holdings, but the exact rate depends on the applicable treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax and trade tax returns are generally due seven months after the end of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year company, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year. A professional tax adviser may generally obtain an extended statutory filing deadline, subject to the applicable annual extension rules. The tax office may impose an earlier deadline in specific circumstances.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For German dividend distributions, capital withholding tax is generally withheld at payment and remitted to the tax office by the statutory deadline applicable to the withholding event. For section 50a withholding tax, the payer generally files and remits on a quarterly basis by the statutory deadline following the relevant quarter. Exact deadlines must be confirmed for the payment type and current tax-office instructions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany does not impose a general monetary threshold below which the arm's-length principle or documentation duties are automatically irrelevant. For ordinary related-party transactions, contemporaneous documentation must be prepared under the German Foreign Tax Act and Administrative Principles. A Local File is generally required for material cross-border related-party transactions. Thresholds commonly relevant to the obligation to prepare a formal transaction-based record are annual consideration exceeding EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing, royalties, and similar dealings. A Master File is generally required for a German entity belonging to a multinational group where consolidated group revenue is at least EUR 750 million. Country-by-country reporting applies to qualifying multinational groups at the EUR 750 million consolidated-revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a single standalone economic-substance regime equivalent to the regimes in certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless material under the arm's-length principle, treaty beneficial-ownership analysis, domestic anti-treaty-shopping rules, principal-purpose testing, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, general anti-abuse provisions, and anti-hybrid rules. A German GmbH should maintain genuine decision-making capacity, appropriately qualified personnel, premises or operational infrastructure, commercial risk assumption, bank and accounting controls, inventory and fulfillment evidence, and business-purpose documentation. A paper conduit, circular financing, unsupported management-fee arrangement, or royalty flow may be recharacterized or denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Under section 370 of the German Fiscal Code, tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine. In especially serious cases, including substantial tax amounts, the penalty may be imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax, interest, confiscation, and restitution remain payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal prosecution, personal tax liability for intentional or grossly negligent breach of tax duties, seizure and confiscation measures, and civil claims. Imprisonment is legally possible. There is no automatic tax-related travel ban, but a court may impose procedural restrictions, detention, or other measures in an individual criminal case.
Late filing, non-filing, or failure to pay

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharges may generally be imposed at 0.25% per commenced month, subject to statutory minimum and maximum amounts. Late-payment surcharges, tax interest, estimated assessments, enforcement, account attachment, and collection proceedings may follow.

Director personal liability: A managing director may be personally liable under section 69 of the Fiscal Code where tax claims are not satisfied due to intentional or grossly negligent breach of duties. Liability risk is particularly acute for payroll tax, VAT, and withholding tax.
VAT fraud and platform-sales concealment

Statutory sanction: VAT evasion is subject to the criminal sanctions for tax evasion, including imprisonment or fines and aggravated penalties for serious cases. Administrative assessments, interest, denial of input tax, penalties, and joint-liability measures may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal tax liability, criminal exposure, and civil claims if they knowingly approve or negligently permit inaccurate VAT reporting. Payment-platform data and EU transaction records may be used in audits.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may increase German taxable income, deny deductions, impose withholding tax on hidden profit distributions, assess interest, and apply documentation-related penalties. Failure to provide required records may lead to estimation and a surcharge generally ranging from 5% to 10% of the income adjustment, subject to statutory rules and caps.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for tax losses caused by deliberate or grossly negligent approval of non-arm's-length transactions and may face criminal exposure if the arrangements are knowingly used to evade tax.
Shell company, conduit, or treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Treaty or EU relief may be denied under anti-abuse and principal-purpose rules. The tax authority may assess withholding tax, interest, penalties, and additional corporate tax. Deliberately false applications may constitute tax evasion or document-related offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid withholding tax and may face criminal proceedings if they knowingly submit false substance or beneficial-ownership information. There is no automatic piercing of the corporate veil solely because an entity has limited substance.
Money laundering and failure to perform customer due diligence

Statutory sanction: Violations of the German Anti-Money Laundering Act may result in administrative fines, supervisory measures, reporting obligations, and criminal liability where money laundering or predicate offenses are involved. Money laundering under the Criminal Code may result in imprisonment or fines, with aggravated consequences in serious cases.

Director personal liability: Managing directors, compliance officers, and employees may be personally prosecuted. Corporate fines, confiscation, reputational damage, payment-account closure, and loss of banking or payment-platform access may result.
Sanctions, export-control, and prohibited-counterparty exposure

Statutory sanction: Breaches of German and European Union sanctions and export-control rules may trigger substantial administrative fines, criminal prosecution, confiscation, transaction prohibitions, and business restrictions. The applicable penalty depends on the breached instrument and conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible compliance personnel may face personal criminal or administrative liability where they intentionally or negligently authorize prohibited transactions. A general automatic travel ban does not apply, but criminal procedural measures may restrict travel in an individual case.
Insolvency delay and preferential creditor payments

Statutory sanction: Breaches of insolvency filing and payment duties may result in director liability for damages, restitution, clawback of transactions, fines, and criminal penalties under insolvency and criminal law. The exact filing period depends on the insolvency ground and statutory exceptions.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for payments made after the company became insolvent, unpaid taxes and social contributions caused by breach of duty, creditor losses, and insolvency-estate claims. Imprisonment is possible for fraudulent insolvency or related criminal conduct.
Corporate-law capital maintenance and unlawful profit distribution

Statutory sanction: Unlawful distributions may be repayable to the GmbH and may trigger shareholder, managing-director, creditor, insolvency, and tax consequences. Hidden distributions may also generate corporate income tax and withholding-tax adjustments.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for unlawful payments, creditor damage, tax shortfalls, and insolvency-estate claims. Limited liability does not protect directors from breaches of their statutory duties.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register), Register Division (Registergericht) of the Local Court (Amtsgericht) at the company's registered office; central publication through Unternehmensregister (www.unternehmensregister.de) and the Bundesanzeiger.

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG), in particular §§ 1 (purpose), 2 (form of articles), 3 (content of articles), 5 (minimum capital), 6 (managing directors), 7 (registration application), 8 (content of application), 10 (legal effect of registration), 35 (management representation), 39 (director appointment filings) and 46 (shareholders' resolutions), together with §§ 106–108 Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB), the Handelsregisterverordnung (HRV) and the Beurkundungsgesetz (BeurkG) §§ 6–10 on notarial execution.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister (application for registration in the Commercial Register), supported by the Gesellschaftsvertrag (Articles of Association) or the Musterprotokoll (Model Protocol) under § 2 GmbHG and submitted electronically by the notary.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

A GmbH requires a statutory minimum share capital (Stammkapital) of €25,000. At least €12,500 (one half of the share capital) must be paid up in cash prior to registration; contributions in kind must be fully provided. Paid-in funds must be deposited with a German credit institution on an escrowed/blocked account (Sperrkonto/Anderkonto) opened in the name of the company in formation (GmbH i.G.). The funds remain blocked and at the free disposal of the managing directors only after the Commercial Register entry has been effected.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: operational fire safety certification by a licensed fire-protection inspector for any premises used by employees, conforming to the Model Building Code (MBO) and the applicable state building regulations, including escape routes, fire alarm and extinguishing equipment. Structural load: verification that the leased office space meets the rated floor-load capacity through the landlord's structural certificate; where a server or technical room is installed, a structural engineer's load check is required. Occupational: registration with the Employers' Liability Insurance Association (Berufsgenossenschaft), completion of the workplace risk assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung) under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (ArbSchG), appointment of a safety officer (Fachkraft für Arbeitssicherheit) and company doctor (Betriebsarzt), and notification of the first occupancy of the premises to the trade supervisory office.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) and other federal tax identification functions; the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) administers corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT returns, payroll taxes, and the domestic tax number (Steuernummer). Main digital channels are the BZSt online application services and ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung).

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated GmbH must be registered with the competent Finanzamt by submitting the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), which is generally assigned by the locally competent office and is not a single nationwide corporate TIN. The GmbH should separately apply for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) from the BZSt, either through the BZSt online service or, where applicable, through the tax registration process. Required information normally includes the registered name, registered office, commercial register details, managing directors, business activity, expected turnover, bank details, tax residence, and the date of commencement. A non-resident entity carrying on taxable activities in Germany must contact the responsible German Finanzamt, file the tax registration questionnaire, provide constitutional and commercial-register documents, identify its German taxable activities and representative, and apply for a USt-IdNr. if it requires intra-EU supplies or acquisitions. VAT registration is not automatically conferred merely by obtaining a commercial register number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is subject to entity-level corporation tax under the German Corporation Tax Act (Körperschaftsteuergesetz) and trade tax under the Trade Tax Act (Gewerbesteuergesetz). Germany has no general U.S.-style check-the-box election allowing a GmbH to elect pass-through treatment. Section 1a of the Corporation Tax Act permits certain eligible partnerships, not GmbHs, to elect taxation as a corporation. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules, principally Section 4k of the Income Tax Act and related provisions implementing ATAD, operate as statutory limitation rules and are not elective hybrid-classification mechanisms. A GmbH may make statutory accounting, fiscal-unit, loss-utilization, and tax-group elections where legally available, but those elections do not change its default corporate tax identity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general German VAT registration threshold for taxable business activity. A taxable person must register and account for VAT from the commencement of taxable transactions unless a specific exemption or special regime applies. The domestic small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG is available, subject to statutory conditions, where prior-calendar-year total turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, based on the rules applicable from 2025. The small-business exemption is an exemption from charging German VAT, not a general exclusion from tax registration, invoicing, or record-keeping obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax recovery under Sections 4(1)(a) and 6 UStG if the goods are transported or dispatched to a third-country territory and the exporter satisfies the documentary and accounting evidence requirements. The exporter must establish the export transaction, the recipient or contractual supply chain, the transport or dispatch, and the exit of the goods from the EU customs territory. Intra-EU supplies are generally VAT-exempt with input-tax recovery under the separate intra-Community supply rules, subject to valid customer VAT identification and proof of transport. Software, cloud document automation, electronic signature, and other electronically supplied services are not ordinarily treated as zero-rated exports of goods. For B2B services, the place-of-supply rules may place taxation in the customer jurisdiction and require reverse charge; for B2C electronic services, EU consumer-location rules and the One Stop Shop may apply. A service is not relieved merely because the customer pays from abroad or the supplier receives foreign currency.

  • Electronic export declaration and customs export records, including the export accompanying document or equivalent customs data and the electronic exit confirmation.
  • Commercial invoice, contract, order record, customer identification, product or service description, and accounting records linking the transaction to the VAT return.
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, courier evidence, or equivalent independent proof of dispatch and delivery.
  • Proof that the goods left the EU customs territory, including the customs exit result or other legally accepted export evidence.
  • For qualifying intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification number, recapitulative statement records, and transport evidence.
  • For cross-border services: customer location and business-status evidence, applicable VAT identification number, contract, service-delivery records, reverse-charge wording where required, and evidence supporting the place-of-supply analysis.
  • Bank payment evidence or transaction records may support the audit file but are not, by themselves, sufficient proof of export or service place of supply. An inward remittance certificate is not a general German statutory prerequisite for export VAT exemption.

Germany does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance or transaction-reporting system comparable to a mandatory pre-clearance model. From 1 January 2025, German businesses must generally be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. The structured formats include, in particular, XRechnung and compliant ZUGFeRD versions. Issuance of structured e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies is subject to transitional periods: generally until the end of 2026, with an additional period through the end of 2027 for issuers meeting the statutory turnover and technical conditions; from 2028, the general mandatory issuance regime applies. The rules do not generally require digital tax stamping or prior government clearance. Invoices must comply with VAT invoice-content, authenticity, integrity, retention, and audit-trail requirements. Cross-border supplies, B2C transactions, and transactions outside the statutory domestic B2B scope require separate analysis.

Germany has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline as a condition for export VAT exemption or input-tax recovery. VAT authorities may deny exemption, deny or defer input-tax recovery, assess additional VAT, require repayment of incorrectly refunded amounts, and charge statutory interest where export, transport, customer-status, or place-of-supply evidence is missing or false. Incorrect VAT returns may trigger correction obligations under the Fiscal Code (Abgabenordnung), late-payment surcharges, and interest under the applicable provisions. Intentional or reckless false declarations can result in administrative fines or criminal tax-evasion proceedings under Section 370 AO, including imprisonment or fines for intentional tax evasion; negligent tax reduction may be sanctioned under Section 378 AO. Input VAT and export exemptions may be clawed back retrospectively for the relevant assessment periods, subject to applicable assessment limitation periods. Civil, customs, and commercial penalties may also apply where customs declarations or supporting records are falsified.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under EU Treaty freedoms and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV). No general exchange control restrictions apply to the euro or foreign currencies. Profit remittance by a German GmbH to non-resident shareholders is freely permitted, subject only to corporate income tax, trade tax, solidarity surcharge, and potential withholding tax on dividends (usually reduced under tax treaties or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive). The euro is freely floating and externally convertible; no government approval for dividend distribution or capital repatriation is required as long as tax filings and statutory balance sheet rules are observed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation in Germany to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency receipts into euros. Export proceeds may be retained, held, and freely used abroad, subject only to reporting obligations to the Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical purposes under the AWV when receivables or payables exceed EUR 12,500 (or when foreign bank accounts are newly opened). No mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank) and the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) under the framework of the European System of Central Banks and the ECB

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Penta, N26 Business, or Qonto can offer flexible digital onboarding for a German GmbH, but they generally do not provide commercial credit, escrow services, or full treasury hedging. Legal constraints include compliance with the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), AML requirements under the Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG), and the need for a domestic German registered office or authorized agent. Offshore EMIs outside the EEA face severe practical friction because German correspondent banks and BaFin-regulated institutions require a locally licensed European passport and a German IBAN for SEPA clearing.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.00% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 0.825% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag at 5.5% of the CIT) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at the federal base rate of 3.5% multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz; combined effective statutory burden approximately 29.83% at a standard 400% Hebesatz, approximately 31.58% at 450% Hebesatz (e.g., Berlin, Munich).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.00% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax = 26.375% aggregate withholding on gross dividends (Sec. 43a EStG). No corporate income tax on the receiving corporate person when 95% exemption under Sec. 8b KStG applies. The statutory rate applies to portfolio and non-treaty shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Statutory filing deadline: 31 July of the year following the tax year (Sec. 149(2) AO). Automatic administrative extension to 28 February of the second following year when the return is prepared by a tax advisor (Steuerberater); further extension on application under Sec. 109 AO. Tax assessments may be issued up to four years after the filing year, extended to seven years in cases of gross negligence and ten years for intentional evasion.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (Sec. 90(3) AO, Verwaltungsgrundsätze Verfahren): mandatory for cross-border controlled transactions with foreign related parties exceeding EUR 5,000,000 in deliveries of goods per fiscal year, or EUR 500,000 in other transactions (services, royalties, interest, management services) per fiscal year. Master File: required when the multinational group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and submitted within 60 days of the tax office's request; 'significant transactions' (above EUR 100,000) must be documented by the statutory filing deadline. Penalties: Sec. 162(4) AO surcharge of EUR 100 per day for late submission, capped at EUR 1,000,000; Sec. 162(3) AO surcharge of 10% of adjusted income for insufficient transfer pricing documentation, minimum EUR 5,000, maximum EUR 1,000,000; presumption of income adjustment where documentation is missing.

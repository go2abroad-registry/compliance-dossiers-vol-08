Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Department (Handelsregister, Abteilung B) of the Local Court (Amtsgericht) in the district of the registered office (zuständiges Amtsgericht).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung — GmbHG), core Sections 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10 and 35; procedural requirements under the Commercial Register Regulation (Handelsregisterverordnung — HRV) and the Act on Electronic Commercial Registers (EHUG).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified Deed of Incorporation together with the Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) and the Joint Registration Application (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) pursuant to Sections 7, 8 and 10 GmbHG.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000 mandated under Section 5 GmbHG; at least EUR 12,500 (one-half) of each cash contribution must be paid into a blocked company escrow account (Kapitalauftragskonto) before Commercial Register entry; funds may be withdrawn only after registration; in-kind contributions must be fully provided and substantiated by a contribution report (Sachgründungsbericht) subject to notary and court plausibility audit.

Funding & unlock path: Workplace Safety Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) compliance comprising escape routes, first-aid equipment and workstation ergonomics; fire safety certification (Brandschutzkonzept) required from the landlord and local fire authority for the leased office; structural load clearance is not applicable to standard office floors under state building codes (LBO); no civil defense clearance (Zivilschutz) is triggered for non-industrial office premises; occupational medical examinations are required upon first employee hires under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz).

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000 mandated under Section 5 GmbHG; at least EUR 12,500 (one-half) of each cash contribution must be paid into a blocked company escrow account (Kapitalauftragskonto) before Commercial Register entry; funds may be withdrawn only after registration; in-kind contributions must be fully provided and substantiated by a contribution report (Sachgründungsbericht) subject to notary and court plausibility audit.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.); the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessments; ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) is the official electronic tax filing and communication portal.

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH must register with the competent local Finanzamt using the electronic questionnaire for tax registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung), generally submitted through ELSTER. The application requires the commercial-register details, registered office, business activities, managing-director information, ownership and controlling-person information, bank details, expected turnover, expected VAT liability, and requested VAT filing frequency. The Finanzamt issues the domestic Steuernummer. The GmbH may apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) through the BZSt online process or through the tax registration procedure. A foreign entity making taxable supplies in Germany must generally obtain a German Steuernummer and, where relevant, a USt-IdNr. from the responsible Finanzamt and BZSt, unless a special Union One-Stop Shop, import One-Stop Shop, reverse-charge, or other statutory simplification applies. The tax authority may request certified constitutional documents, commercial-register extracts, proof of business activity, powers of attorney, identification documents, and evidence of the German business address. A German VAT number does not replace the domestic tax number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is generally subject to entity-level corporation tax under the Körperschaftsteuergesetz and trade tax under the Gewerbesteuergesetz. Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a GmbH. A GmbH may not generally elect transparent partnership treatment merely by filing a tax classification election. Tax-transparent treatment can arise only through legally distinct restructurings or entity forms satisfying statutory requirements. A GmbH may be part of a German tax group (Organschaft) if the statutory financial, organizational, and economic integration requirements are met. For VAT, the tax group rules under section 2(2) no. 2 of the German VAT Act may apply; this is not a free-form election and requires factual integration. Cross-border hybrid mismatch and anti-hybrid rules under the German Interest Limitation and Anti-Hybrid framework can deny deductions or impose corresponding adjustments; they do not create a general elective entity-classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to a business making taxable supplies in Germany: VAT registration is generally required from the first relevant taxable transaction, subject to reverse-charge, OSS, import VAT, and other statutory rules. The German small-business exemption under section 19 UStG may apply to an established taxable person whose prior-calendar-year domestic turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and whose current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to the statutory conditions and timing rules. The small-business regime is an exemption from charging VAT, not a general exemption from registration, recordkeeping, or reporting obligations. The reduced VAT rate is generally 7% for specified categories; electronically supplied SaaS and standard software-platform services generally fall within the 19% rate when German VAT is due.

Germany does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance or mandatory digital-stamping system for all domestic transactions. From 1 January 2025, German businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. Mandatory issuance of structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions is phased in: generally from 1 January 2027 for issuers exceeding the statutory turnover limit and from 1 January 2028 for broader in-scope transactions, subject to statutory transitional provisions and exemptions. Permitted structured formats include EN 16931-compliant XRechnung and compliant hybrid formats such as ZUGFeRD. EDI procedures may be used if the statutory requirements are satisfied. Ordinary PDF invoices are not structured e-invoices. Invoices must contain the legally required information, including supplier and customer identity, tax numbers or VAT identification numbers where applicable, invoice date, sequential invoice number, description, consideration, tax rate, tax amount, exemption or reverse-charge wording, and relevant payment information. VAT returns, intra-Community recapitulative statements, and related filings are generally submitted electronically through ELSTER or other approved electronic channels.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the EU free movement of capital framework (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Articles 63-66). No general restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders. However, all cross-border payments and capital movements are subject to mandatory statistical reporting to the Bundesbank (AWV reporting) if the transaction value exceeds EUR 12,500. Profit remittance is subject to standard corporate income tax (15% plus solidarity surcharge and trade tax) and, if applicable, withholding tax under the German-Sino tax treaty (reduced to 10% on dividends if 10%+ shareholding, subject to substance requirements). No currency conversion hurdles; the EUR is fully convertible. Practical hurdles may arise from bank de-risking policies for high-risk jurisdictions, but Germany's regulatory environment is transparent and non-discriminatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose any mandatory surrender or conversion requirements for export proceeds. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency in their accounts, convert at market rates, or use them for onward payments. The only obligation is the AWV reporting requirement for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500, which must be reported to the Bundesbank within seven days of the transaction. No minimum conversion ratio exists. For a SaaS/PropTech company, export proceeds from software licensing and subscription fees are treated as current account transactions and are fully liberalized.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the German Federal Ministry of Finance; foreign exchange administration is executed under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for ancillary liquidity management, but Medium for primary treasury operations. German BaFin-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) such as PPRO, Airwallex, and Mambu (or licensed EMIs like Solarisbank, N26) can provide multi-currency IBANs, FX conversion, and cross-border payment rails. However, for a GmbH with significant B2B SaaS revenue and potential real estate escrow flows, a full-license commercial bank is recommended as the primary treasury anchor. EMIs are viable for operational sub-accounts, vendor payments, and FX hedging execution, but they may not offer credit facilities or escrow-grade custody. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot accept deposits (only e-money), and client funds must be safeguarded in segregated accounts. For rental security deposits (Mietkautionskonten), a full banking license is required to hold such funds in a fiduciary capacity. Therefore, use EMIs for treasury optimization, not for statutory escrow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a German GmbH, inbound capital injection (shareholder loan or equity contribution) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA for EUR) with the correct purpose code. For equity injection, use purpose code 'S001' (Direct investment) or 'S002' (Real estate investment) under the AWV reporting. For shareholder loans, use 'S003' (Financial credit). The payment must be reported to the Bundesbank if > EUR 12,500. The bank will require a copy of the share purchase agreement or loan agreement, and the company must file the AWV report within 7 days. For non-EU investors, the payment must be cleared via a correspondent bank (e.g., Deutsche Bank's New York branch for USD) and then converted to EUR. The receiving bank will verify the purpose code and may request the underlying contract. For a PropTech SaaS, the capital injection is typically for working capital and product development; ensure the purpose code matches the commercial reality to avoid false reporting penalties.For outbound trade settlements (e.g., software licensing fees, cloud infrastructure costs, marketing expenses), the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for cross-border payments and SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EU/EEA payments. For high-value or time-sensitive payments, use SWIFT MT103 with cover (MT202) for correspondent banking. For trade settlements involving real estate transactions (e.g., property acquisition or rental payments), a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) may be used if the counterparty requires documentary credit, but for SaaS subscriptions, open account with SWIFT is standard. The company must ensure that all outbound payments are reported to the Bundesbank if > EUR 12,500. For payments to non-EU countries, the bank may require a 'Zahlungszweck' (purpose code) such as 'S010' (Software license fees) or 'S011' (IT services). The bank will also perform sanctions screening (OFAC, EU sanctions) and may require additional documentation for high-risk jurisdictions.FX Forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) – OTC contracts with a German bank or EU bank, typically up to 12 months, for hedging EUR/USD or EUR/CNY exposure from subscription revenue.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – Permissible for currencies with convertibility restrictions (e.g., CNY) but must be executed via a licensed bank; the German bank will act as the counterparty and settle in EUR.Currency Swaps (Währungsswaps) – Permissible for longer-term hedging of capital injections or loan repayments, subject to ISDA documentation and credit support annex.FX Options (Devisenoptionen) – Permissible for hedging downside risk while retaining upside, but require premium payment and are subject to bank credit limits.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) – Permissible if the company has a foreign currency loan or investment, but typically used for larger exposures.

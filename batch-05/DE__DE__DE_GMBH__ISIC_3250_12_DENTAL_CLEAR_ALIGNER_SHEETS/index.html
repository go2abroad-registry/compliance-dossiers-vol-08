Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) of the competent Local Court (Amtsgericht) of the registered office; electronic submissions are filed by the notary through the Federal State's secure electronic court and administrative file portal (Elektronisches Gerichts- und Verwaltungspostfach, EGVP) into the Common Register System.

Governing statute

German Act on Limited Liability Companies (GmbH-Gesetz, GmbHG): §§ 1–3 (formation), § 5 (minimum share capital EUR 25,000), § 7(2)–(3) (minimum cash payment and free-disposal rule), § 8 (contents of registration application), § 10 (constitutive court registration and publication), §§ 35–38 (managing directors and representation), § 78 (electronic filing via notary). Supplementary source: German Commercial Code (HGB) §§ 8–10; Federal Notarial Rules (BNotO); digital court-file regulations (EGVP).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) or the statutory model protocol (Musterprotokoll) pursuant to § 2(1a) GmbHG, together with the Registration Application (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) in certified electronic form under §§ 8, 10 and 78 GmbHG, and the certified lists of managing directors and shareholders.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 11 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Fully subscribed minimum share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000.00 per § 5 GmbHG; at least 25% of the par value of each business share and at least EUR 12,500.00 in aggregate must be paid in cash before registration per § 7(2) GmbHG. The paid capital must be irrevocably at the managing director's free disposal (freie Verfügung); a single-member GmbH must also provide security for the unpaid portion under § 7(3) GmbHG. Contributions in kind require a valuation report and full transfer prior to filing. The cash deposit is evidenced by a bank escrow-account confirmation (Sperrkonto) submitted with the application.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt — Federal Central Tax Office; sole competent authority for issuance of the USt-IdNr./EU VAT identification number and for OSS/IOSS filings) and the locally competent Finanzamt (municipal tax office; competent for issuance of the Steuernummer, for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessments). Electronic portals: ELSTER (www.elster.de) for tax registration questionnaires and all returns; BZSt Online-Services (www.bzst.de) for VAT-ID applications and OSS/IOSS schemes; ATLAS for electronic customs declarations.

E-government filing portal

For a resident GmbH: (1) complete commercial registration in the Handelsregister at the local Amtsgericht; (2) file the statutory amtlicher Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt within one month of commencing business; (3) the Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer (statutory TIN covering corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT) and forwards the case file to BZSt; (4) BZSt issues the USt-IdNr. under §27a UStG for cross-border and intra-Community transactions. For a non-resident seller without a German establishment, German VAT registration is obtained through BZSt or the local Finanzamt using the simplified registration form and requires proof of economic activity and, for customs purposes, an EORI number; distance-selling sellers may alternatively register under the Union OSS/IOSS to avoid separate German VAT registration below the applicable thresholds.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a GmbH is a juristic person (Körperschaft) subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer at 15%), the 5.5% solidarity surcharge on corporate tax (combined headline 15.825%), municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer at the statutory base rate of 3.5% multiplied by the local Hebesatz, commonly a 350–490% multiplier in larger cities), and VAT as an entrepreneur under §2 UStG. Distributions to individual shareholders incur 25% withholding tax (Abgeltungsteuer) plus solidarity surcharge and church tax; corporate shareholders benefit from the 95% dividend participation exemption under §8b KStG. No pass-through treatment is available to a GmbH. — For a resident GmbH: (1) complete commercial registration in the Handelsregister at the local Amtsgericht; (2) file the statutory amtlicher Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt within one month of commencing business; (3) the Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer (statutory TIN covering corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT) and forwards the case file to BZSt; (4) BZSt issues the USt-IdNr. under §27a UStG for cross-border and intra-Community transactions. For a non-resident seller without a German establishment, German VAT registration is obtained through BZSt or the local Finanzamt using the simplified registration form and requires proof of economic activity and, for customs purposes, an EORI number; distance-selling sellers may alternatively register under the Union OSS/IOSS to avoid separate German VAT registration below the applicable thresholds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a GmbH is a juristic person (Körperschaft) subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer at 15%), the 5.5% solidarity surcharge on corporate tax (combined headline 15.825%), municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer at the statutory base rate of 3.5% multiplied by the local Hebesatz, commonly a 350–490% multiplier in larger cities), and VAT as an entrepreneur under §2 UStG. Distributions to individual shareholders incur 25% withholding tax (Abgeltungsteuer) plus solidarity surcharge and church tax; corporate shareholders benefit from the 95% dividend participation exemption under §8b KStG. No pass-through treatment is available to a GmbH.

Available election forms

Germany has no 'check-the-box' system; tax status follows civil-law form. Relevant elections: (1) Organschaft (German fiscal unity) under §§14–19 KStG, §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 GewStG and §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, allowing a controlling corporate parent (minimum 95% shareholding, financial integration, and profit-and-loss transfer agreement) to consolidate the GmbH for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT purposes; (2) VAT group treatment (Organschaft) electively aligning VAT registration and reporting; (3) the partnership option model under §1a KStG (applicable to partnerships, not to an existing GmbH); (4) anti-hybrid and financing elections: Germany transposed ATAD hybrid mismatch rules at §4k EStG, restricts deduction of hybrid financial instruments, applies the interest cap and the EBITDA carry-forward under §4h EStG, and limits double-dip structures via §14 KStG and §8b KStG. No unilateral domestic hybrid election exists, but taxpayers may obtain a binding tax ruling (verbindliche Auskunft) under §89 AO to confirm the German classification of a cross-border hybrid structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A German GmbH engaged in commercial activity is registrable for VAT from its first taxable transaction; no monetary threshold applies to a resident commercial GmbH. The statutory small-enterprise exemption (§19 UStG) applies only below €22,000 prior-year turnover and €50,000 expected current-year turnover, and is practically unavailable to a commercial GmbH. For non-resident distance sales of goods, the intra-Community distance-sales threshold is €10,000 per calendar year across all EU member states under §3 Abs. 3a UStG; imports of consignments valued up to €150 may use IOSS; electronically supplied services to German consumers carry no registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Dental clear aligner sheets constitute tangible medical/dental goods. Their export to a third-country territory qualifies as tax-exempt with input VAT credit (Ausfuhrlieferung) under §6 UStG where: (1) the seller is an entrepreneur under §2 UStG; (2) the goods are dispatched or transported by the seller or the buyer in an uninterrupted chain demonstrably leaving the EU; (3) the recipient is located outside the EU; and (4) the export satisfies the customs export declaration accepted in ATLAS. Export-related services (processing, packing, freight handling directly linked to the exported goods) can qualify under §6a UStG if the services are rendered in Germany and the underlying goods are physically exported. Intra-Community supplies to EU customers are not zero-rated but are VAT-exempt under §4 Nr. 1b UStG and §6a UStG, conditional on the customer's valid EU VAT ID, the EC Sales List, and, where the seller transports the goods, the Gelangensbestätigung (arrival confirmation). All exemptions require contemporaneous compliance with the Beleg- und Buchnachweis under §§8–10 UStDV.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) lodged electronically via ATLAS and accepted by the customs office of export (MRN — Movement Reference Number)
  • Export Accompanying Document (e-ABD / Ausfuhrbegleitdokument) issued by ATLAS for the customs office at exit
  • Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, or airway bill evidencing physical dispatch to the third-country destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list cross-referenced to the export declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 transfer evidence demonstrating receipt of foreign currency
  • EORI number registration confirmation for the German exporting entity
  • Belegnachweis documentation file under §8 UStDV (invoice, transport, and export records)
  • Buchnachweis accounting records under §9 UStDV (exporter, consignee, destination, quantity, value, transport route)
  • For intra-Community supplies (alternative regime): Gelangensbestätigung and EC Sales List; for dual-use or regulated dental goods, applicable export licence or conformity documentation

Germany mandates structured electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) for domestic B2B supplies. Under the Wachstumschancengesetz: from 1 January 2025 every B2B supplier must be able to receive e-invoices and input VAT deduction generally requires an e-invoice in a structured format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0.1/2.3 hybrid PDF+XML); from 1 January 2027 enterprises with more than €800,000 taxable turnover must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all resident B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices. Germany has NO real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control API; e-invoices are not cleared by the tax authority, and periodic VAT data flows via ELSTER. Export transactions and B2C invoicing remain outside the e-invoicing mandate. The ATLAS customs system serves as the digital export clearance portal for all external-trade declarations.

The Finanzamt and German Customs systematically audit export zero-rating documentation. Failure of the Beleg- und Buchnachweis reverses the exemption, triggering retrospective VAT assessment at 19% on the export consideration plus statutory late-payment interest under §233a AO (0.5% per month). Deliberate fictitious exports or falsified documents constitute tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine, with an extended ten-year assessment period under §169 AO; reckless (leichtfertige) tax shortfall is separately penalised under §378 AO and administrative fines can be imposed under §379 AO. Clawback is enforceable against the GmbH, its managing directors (joint and several liability), and participating export parties. Export proceeds remaining uncollected after the 180-day foreign-currency repatriation window trigger transfer-pricing adjustments under §1 AStG, with deemed interest income and potential loss of export evidence, while intra-group export financing must meet arm's-length payment terms and contemporaneous documentation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Art. 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz). The euro is freely convertible; there are no exchange control approvals, surrender obligations, or conversion-ratio restrictions for trade flows, dividend remittances, or capital repatriation. Profit remittances from a GmbH are freely transferable, subject only to the statutory withholding tax framework and the statistical reporting duties described below. Non-EU inbound investment is assessed only under the EU FDI Screening Regulation (EU) 2019/452 and §§55–59 AWV; a dental clear aligner sheet manufacturing facility (ISIC 3250, NAICS 339114) does not ordinarily fall within the critical-health, defence, or IT-security sectors, and no mandatory FDI filing is expected for a passive non-resident shareholder.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no forced conversion into euro in Germany. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign currency revenues abroad or on account. The only binding compliance obligation is statistical reporting: each cross-border payment (inbound or outbound) of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported by the German resident party to the Deutsche Bundesbank under §67 of the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV) within 5 business days of the value date. The report is filed electronically through the Bundesbank's Allgemeines Meldeportal using the party's dedicated MySQL/CSV reporting interface, and must specify the purpose code (e.g., proceeds from exports of goods). Outstanding foreign receivables and payables at year-end must additionally be captured in the quarterly K1/K2 statistical reports.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank) — competent for monetary policy, cashless payment statistics, and the statutory reporting of cross-border payments; the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises banks, payment institutions, and securities markets; the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) administers the German foreign investment screening regime under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz and Außenwirtschaftsverordnung.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax amount, yielding a base corporate income tax burden of 15.825%; trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additionally levied at a municipal Hebesatz-dependent rate, producing a typical combined income-tax-plus-trade-tax effective burden of approximately 30–33% for a German GmbH.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory German dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) is 25.0%; a 5.5% solidarity surcharge is levied on the withholding tax, resulting in an aggregate statutory burden of 26.375%. Where the shareholder is a natural person liable to church tax, an additional surcharge can apply. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive may exempt dividends paid to qualifying EU corporate shareholders, and tax treaties may reduce the rate to 5% or 15% (or 0% under certain anti-abuse conditions).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under § 90(3) AO, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared and produced on request where cross-border controlled transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000 per year for the supply of goods and EUR 500,000 per year for services. A Master File is required for significant multinational groups, and Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000. German tax authorities may apply a 60-day production deadline and a 30-day supplementary deadline for later-disclosed facts.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not administer a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation annual filing, but economic substance is nevertheless a determinative tax concept. A GmbH must maintain actual business substance — real premises, employees, management decisions, operational risk and capital — to resist recharacterisation under § 42 AO (abuse of legal form), § 50d EStG (treaty-shopping denial), German CFC rules (§§ 7–14 AStG), ATAD interest limitation rules, and the planned EU Unshell Directive. In particular, a dental clear aligner company with central management in Germany cannot rely on foreign shell affiliates to divert manufacturing profits without substantial risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years or a fine under § 370 AO; aggravated cases carry imprisonment from six months to ten years under § 370(3) AO. The tax understatement triggers default interest at 1.8% per annum, late-filing surcharges, disallowance of loss carryforwards, and a binding best-judgment assessment under § 162 AO. Civil restitution and confiscation of proceeds may also be ordered.

Director personal liability: Managing directors and de facto directors are personally liable under § 69 AO for taxes not withheld or unremitted and for unlawful tax advantages. Directors face criminal conviction, exclusion from holding directorial office, travel bans and potential extradition in cross-border evasion cases, and personal imprisonment for deliberate non-payment of assessed corporate taxes after warnings.
Transfer pricing mispricing and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: The Finanzamt may increase taxable income by upward transfer pricing adjustment, apply penalties under § 162 AO, shift the burden of proof to the taxpayer, add deemed interest, and confiscate the documentation-advantage benefit. Fines may be compounded by criminal prosecution under § 370 AO if the mispricing was knowingly used to evade tax. The foreign tax credit may be refused where the German adjustment lacks secondary consequent documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign mispriced returns or issue false transfer pricing certificates are exposed to personal criminal liability and fines. The corporate veil may be pierced where intercompany transactions are used to strip assets from the GmbH, rendering shareholders and de facto managers liable for restitution and personal damages suffered by creditors.
Shell company abuse and treaty-shopping structure

Statutory sanction: DTA benefits may be denied under § 50d EStG and general anti-abuse provisions, causing recharacterisation of income, additional withholding tax at 26.375%, denial of interest deductions, and income being attributed to the actual controlling company. Fines under tax and commercial law apply; the GmbH may be struck from the commercial register for false declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who established or maintained a letterbox company face unlimited personal liability for unpaid corporate taxes and social security debts under § 64 GmbHG and general German insolvency law. Travel bans and personal bankruptcy orders can issue where the structure was used to obstruct tax collection.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Registry (Transparenzregister) non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to EUR 100,000 for individuals and up to EUR 1 million for legal entities under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG). Criminal liability for money laundering under § 261 StGB carries imprisonment of up to ten years for concealing unlawfully obtained assets; seizure of bank proceeds and proceeds of merchant gateway accounts is mandatory.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise false registry filings or permit non-compliant payment flows are personally criminally liable. They may be prohibited from acting as company directors, subjected to asset freezes, travel restrictions, and extradition where the AML violation is part of a cross-border economic crime scheme.

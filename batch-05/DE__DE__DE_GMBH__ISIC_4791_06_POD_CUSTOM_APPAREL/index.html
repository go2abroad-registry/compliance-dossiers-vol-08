Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register) kept by the local Amtsgericht (Local Court) in the district of the registered office; filed electronically through the German notary using the justiz-online electronic legal-communication portal

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbHG), particularly §§ 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 39 and 78; Commercial Code (HGB) §§ 8, 106 et seq. for commercial register filing

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) and formal application for registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) filed with the Commercial Register

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum stated capital (Stammkapital) EUR 25,000; at incorporation at least EUR 12,500 (50%) is required to be paid in cash unless fully covered by contributions in kind; cash contributions must be deposited to a bank account opened in the company's name and must be freely at the managing directors' disposal; no formal escrow account is required under GmbHG, but subscription proceeds must be unconditionally reserved for the company

Funding & unlock path: Workplace compliance certificate under ArbStättV and BetrSichV covering fire protection, emergency exits and first-aid equipment; structural-load inspection is not required for ordinary commercial office/fulfilment use; if onsite printing machines are installed, occupational safety registration with the Berufsgenossenschaft (liability insurance association) and a ventilation/emission technical check must be completed.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum stated capital (Stammkapital) EUR 25,000; at incorporation at least EUR 12,500 (50%) is required to be paid in cash unless fully covered by contributions in kind; cash contributions must be deposited to a bank account opened in the company's name and must be freely at the managing directors' disposal; no formal escrow account is required under GmbHG, but subscription proceeds must be unconditionally reserved for the company

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office - BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and central tax matters; local Finanzamt (tax office) for substantive tax assessment; electronic portal: ELSTER at www.elster.de.

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH, registration begins after notarial formation and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). The GmbH must file the statutory tax registration questionnaire (“Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung”) with the locally competent Finanzamt, via ELSTER or paper, providing particulars of shareholders, managing directors, business objects, estimated turnover, input VAT and wage tax data. The Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (corporate and trade tax number). The Bundeszentralamt für Steuern subsequently or in parallel assigns the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) under §27a UStG upon application. Registration must be completed before the company invoices customers, because VAT invoices require a tax number or VAT ID. No notarized power of attorney is needed if the online ELSTER certificate is authorized, but a local tax advisor is recommended for foreign shareholders.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The GmbH is a resident corporate body (juristische Person) subject to unlimited German corporation tax liability on worldwide income if its registered office or place of management is in Germany; it is not a pass-through entity and no partner-level income allocation applies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The GmbH is a resident corporate body (juristische Person) subject to unlimited German corporation tax liability on worldwide income if its registered office or place of management is in Germany; it is not a pass-through entity and no partner-level income allocation applies. It is also a taxable person for VAT under §2 UStG regardless of turnover except the small-business exemption.

Available election forms

German tax law has no US-style check-the-box election. A GmbH is mandatorily classified as an entity-level taxpayer under §1 KStG. However, the GmbH may enter into a corporate tax group (Organschaft) as controlled entity under §§14-19 KStG if an enforceable profit-and-loss pooling agreement exists and the parent holds more than 50% of voting rights; the subsidiary’s profit is attributed to the parent for corporate tax/trade tax purposes. For VAT purposes, the GmbH may be treated as part of a VAT group (Organschaft) under §2(2) sentence 1 no. 2 UStG if it is financially, economically and organizationally integrated into a controlling entrepreneur. Hybrid mismatch elections: Germany implements EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules through statutes that generally override contractual classifications; no elective blended classification is available for German resident entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The §19 UStG small-business exemption permits a taxable person to refrain from charging VAT if its prior-year total turnover (excluding VAT and intra-Community supplies) did not exceed €22,000 and the current-year projected turnover does not exceed €50,000. For a newly incorporated GmbH, however, the obligation to submit the tax registration questionnaire commences at the first taxable activity, and registration is required from the date of that activity. For cross-border distance sales of goods to EU consumers, the intra-EU distance-sale threshold is €10,000 (total across all EU Member States excluding the home-country supplies); once exceeded, the seller must charge the customer’s local VAT and may register via the One-Stop Shop (OSS) or directly.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A German GmbH may zero-rate any sale of goods as an export delivery (§§4 No.1, 6 UStG) if the goods are transported by the supplier or on the supplier’s account to a destination outside the EU, the transport begins in Germany, the goods substantively leave the EU, and the exporter holds documentary and book evidence under §§8-12 UStDV (e.g., customs export declaration, ATLAS electronic proof, carrier certificates). Directive to POD: direct despatch to non-EU customers qualifies as export supply; services ordering/processing are auxiliary and do not split the supply when all consideration is treated as one economic supply of goods. Supplies of goods to EU customers are not export zero-rated; B2B intra-Community supplies may be exempt under §6a UStG if the buyer’s valid VAT ID is recorded, and B2C distance sales require local VAT via OSS/registration. The zero-rating is available even if the customer pays online through Stripe/PayPal; payment method does not affect eligibility.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) filed in ATLAS
  • Commercial invoice stating exporter, consignee, Incoterms, HS code, and value
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (if applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/bank statement showing cross-border receipt of export proceeds
  • Packing list
  • Proof of export pursuant to §§8-12 UStDV (e.g., customs certificate, carrier confirmation, tracking evidence for postal exports)

Germany does not operate a real-time tax clearance, API pre-authorization, or digital stamping system for invoices. The E-Invoicing Regulation (Wachstumschancengesetz) introduces mandatory structured e-invoicing: from 1 January 2025 every German entrepreneur must accept electronic invoices in EN16931-compliant formats (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x/Factur-X) for domestic B2B supplies; from 1 January 2027 businesses with prior-year turnover above €800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices. Transmission is usually via PEPPOL network, and invoice datasets must be retained to be produced by the tax office during audits. B2G invoices submitted to federal authorities must use XRechnung.

German tax authorities and customs police regularly audit export zero-ratings; if the documentary evidence is incomplete or false, the exemption is denied retroactively under §§164-171 AO and the VAT output tax is assessed with interest of 0.5% per month (§233a/§240 AO). Intentional or reckless evasion is criminal tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by imprisonment up to five years or a fine, and up to ten years in aggravated large-scale cases; negligent tax shortfalls are penalized under §378 AO. In addition, input VAT incorrectly deducted on export-related purchases is clawed back, joint liability can be imposed on managing directors under §69 AO, and the company’s VAT refund claims are blocked until corrected declarations. Fraudulent proof, backdating, or sham re-export transactions will lead to criminal seizure and dissolution of the export privilege for repeat periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under Articles 63-66 TFEU, with no general exchange-control approval for trade settlements or profit remittances. The euro is freely convertible; inbound/outbound transfers may be executed without central bank licence. A GmbH's profit distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to statutory German dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer of 25% plus solidarity surcharge and, where applicable, church tax) unless reduced under a double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; this is a tax issue, not an FX/convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds into euro. Exporters may hold or dispose of foreign currency freely. However, German residents must report cross-border payments received from non-residents of more than EUR 12,500 (or the countervalue) to the Deutsche Bundesbank via the electronic 'Zentralbank-Meldeportal' (AWV reporting); reporting on export receipts is statistical/monetary, not a permission or approval requirement.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank), acting within the Eurosystem / European Central Bank framework; banking conduct supervised by Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High as a supplemental/e-money operating account, not as a primary full corporate banking relationship. Licensed German or EU EMIs (e.g., solaris, PPRO, Transact Elektronische Zahlungssysteme GmbH) can provide rapid account opening and cross-border payment functionality, particularly for e-commerce and POD settlements. However, a German GmbH operating real trade flows should maintain at least one account with a full-spectrum German commercial bank to satisfy statutory escrow requirements, merchant acquiring credibility, trade financing needs, and acceptance by customs/suppliers. Offshore EMIs are generally less acceptable to German counterparties and may create additional AML friction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for an equity injection from a foreign parent or shareholder into the German GmbH account shall use SWIFT MT103 with unambiguous beneficiary (GmbH name, IBAN), remitter matching the shareholder of record, and reference fields stating 'equity contribution', 'Stammkapital', or 'shareholder loan' as appropriate. The transfer may be made in EUR or any freely convertible currency; conversion is unrestricted. No prior BaFin/Bundesbank authorization is required, but the receiving GmbH must file the mandatory AWV cross-border payment report with Deutsche Bundesbank on the e-platform for any receipt above EUR 12,500. Supporting bank KYC documentation includes a signed declaration of the business purpose, a description of the corporate chain, and evidence of the underlying shareholder resolution or loan agreement.Clearing rails used for trade settlements: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer / SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for euro-denominated beneficiary accounts within SEPA; (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA and non-EUR payments; (3) Commercial Letter of Credit at sight for high-value or new-supplier import transactions, especially apparel bulk purchases from overseas; (4) documentary collection for medium-trust goods trade. Each outbound disbursement above EUR 12,500 to a non-resident requires AWV statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank.

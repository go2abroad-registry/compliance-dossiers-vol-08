Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — official VAT-ID office, www.bzst.de; local Finanzamt for income and trade tax registration; national e-filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH, the managing director must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' to the competent local Finanzamt before business commencement. The Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (income tax/trade tax number) and, after incorporation and tax filing, the BZSt issues the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr.) for VAT/EU transactions. Foreign-established enterprises making taxable supplies in Germany register via their German fiscal representative or direct Finanzamt application; anti-abuse rules may require a fiscal representative for third-country entrepreneurs.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a German-resident GmbH is a body corporate subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and trade tax; it is not eligible for pass-through treatment. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a German-resident GmbH is a body corporate subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and trade tax; it is not eligible for pass-through treatment. Territorial scope is worldwide income for resident companies subject to treaty and permanent establishment relief.

Available election forms

Germany does not apply check-the-box elections. Relevant statutory options: VAT/corporate tax fiscal unity (Organschaft); small-business VAT exemption (§ 19 UStG) for prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 22,000 and current-year ≤ EUR 50,000; cash-basis VAT accounting (§ 20 UStG) for turnover ≤ EUR 800,000; and explicit anti-hybrid restrictions under § 4k EStG disallowing deductible mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 prior-year taxable turnover or EUR 50,000 expected current-year taxable turnover; lower EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold for intra-community distance sales; no threshold for imports, intra-EU acquisitions, or non-established suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available for physical goods dispatched or transported from Germany to destinations outside the EU under § 4(1)(a) and § 6 UStG, provided the seller or buyer completes the export customs procedure and reliable documentary evidence is retained. Intra-EU supplies qualify under § 4(1)(b) and § 6a UStG only when the customer is a VAT-registered entrepreneur in another member state, the goods are transported to that member state, and the EC Sales List is correctly filed.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung via ATLAS) with MRN
  • Export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) from German Customs
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR, or postal receipt
  • Commercial export invoice
  • Packing list
  • Transport and insurance documents
  • Inward remittance certificate or equivalent payment/FX evidence
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT-identification number and EC Sales List/recapitulative statement

Domestic B2B e-invoicing mandate effective 1 January 2025: structured e-invoices (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, or EDI) are generally required, with transitional paper/PDF rules until 2027/2028 depending on turnover. VAT returns and EC Sales Lists are filed via ELSTER. Germany does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, API stamping, or digital fiscal stamps; GoBD record-keeping and KassenSichV/TSE rules apply to cash register systems.

If export or intra-community supply documentation is false, incomplete, or not produced, the zero-rating is denied and German VAT at 19% is clawed back from the supplier. German tax authorities may impose late-payment interest at 6% per annum (§ 233a AO), late-payment penalties (§ 240 AO), and assess estimated tax bases. Fraudulent export VAT claims trigger criminal tax evasion liability under § 370 AO (fines or imprisonment up to 5 years), confiscation orders, and extended assessment periods. German VAT law does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is applied as bank/trade-finance due diligence and customs-export monitoring practice.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Germany has no general exchange-control approval regime or general restriction on capital contributions, intercompany funding, dividends, interest, royalties, or liquidation proceeds. Remittances remain subject to corporate-law distribution rules, tax withholding and treaty requirements, sanctions, AML controls, transfer-pricing rules, and documentary checks by the remitting and receiving banks. Dividends require distributable profits and proper shareholder resolutions; a German GmbH must preserve its registered share capital and comply with capital-maintenance rules. Cross-border payment reports may be required under the AWV; these are statistical reporting obligations, not prior foreign-exchange approvals.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no general statutory deadline for converting export receipts and no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio. Export proceeds may generally be retained in euros or foreign currency, including in qualifying foreign accounts. German residents must assess AWV balance-of-payments reporting for reportable cross-border receipts and payments; the general reporting threshold is above EUR 50,000 per transaction, subject to applicable exemptions, aggregation rules, and current Bundesbank instructions. The threshold and reporting treatment should be verified for the transaction date. Reporting does not replace customs, VAT, export-control, sanctions, or bank documentation requirements.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany’s central bank and administers balance-of-payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action administers foreign-trade policy and investment screening; the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) administers export-control licensing. Germany applies the European Union’s common monetary and payments framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine euro and foreign-currency payments, subject to the provider's country coverage, product permissions, and risk appetite. Use an EMI or payment institution only where it is authorized in the EEA or lawfully passported into Germany and the service is within its authorization. An EMI account is not necessarily a bank deposit and may not have statutory deposit-guarantee protection. It should not be assumed to satisfy a formation bank's requirements for confirming GmbH share-capital payment or to provide commercial letters of credit, trade finance, or all correspondent-banking services. Maintain a regulated bank relationship for share capital, treasury concentration, and trade-finance needs.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge thereon, before trade tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax amount (total 26.375%), withheld on dividend distributions by the GmbH and paid to the tax office; shareholder receives a withholding tax certificate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally by 31 July of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end (within seven months of fiscal year-end); if tax advice is engaged, automatic extension to 28/29 February of the second year following the fiscal year-end. Electronic filing via ELSTER is mandatory.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 0 — no revenue threshold under German law; contemporaneous Local File / Master File documentation is mandatory for all cross-border controlled transactions regardless of size. Simplified record-keeping applies to low-value intra-group services up to EUR 200,000. Penalties apply for missing documentation starting at EUR 5,000 per document type.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone ESR regime, but substance-over-form is anchored in § 42 AO (abuse of law), § 8 Abs. 3 KStG on hidden profit distributions, and § 50d Abs. 3 EStG (anti-treaty shopping). A German GmbH requires real place of management, premises, employees, and commercial substance; shell structures risk recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, and additional taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine under § 370 AO up to EUR 2,000,000 or up to 5 years’ imprisonment in aggravated cases; back taxes, interest of 0.5% per month (6% annually), late payment surcharge of 1% per month; a 5% surcharge on the evaded tax may be imposed under § 371 AO if the voluntary disclosure is incomplete.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under § 69 AO for culpably violated duties; civil restitution by the insolvency administrator under § 64 GmbHG after insolvency maturity; tax evasion is a personal criminal offence — directors including de facto directors face prosecution, imprisonment up to 10 years in serious cases (commercial or gang-like evasion), travel bans and passport seizure under § 132 StPO.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Violation

Statutory sanction: Best-judgment assessment under § 162 AO if documentation is missing; penalty of EUR 5,000 per missing document type and up to EUR 1,000,000 for serious documentation defaults (§ 162 Abs. 4 AO); additional 6% annual interest on TP adjustments (§ 3a Abs. 1 AStG); reversal of burden of proof in favour of the tax office.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false TP documentation face criminal tax evasion liability; personal liability under § 69 AO for deficit tax collection caused by wrongful acts; during an intra-group audit, a German director may be personally interviewed and a criminal complaint routinely filed.
Anti-Treaty-Shopping / Shell Company Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under § 42 AO and § 50d Abs. 3 EStG: dividend WHT applied at 26.375% with no treaty reduction; intermediary may be treated as a German permanent establishment; CFC taxation in the parent jurisdiction; 5.5% solidarity surcharge and 6% arrears interest. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive does not apply to non-genuine arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors who implement shell structures are exposed to civil liability under §§ 823, 826 BGB for restitution of the tax advantage; criminal liability as accessory to tax evasion (§§ 25, 27 StGB with § 370 AO); the BZSt can issue a Haftungsbescheid against a director for failure to ensure proper TRC verification.
Dividend Repatriation / Withholding Tax Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The GmbH as withholding agent is personally liable under § 73 AO for the withheld tax plus solidarity surcharge; back payment, late payment interest of 0.5% per month, late payment surcharge of 1% per month; hidden profit distributions are added back and re-taxed at CIT and trade tax levels; penalties under § 378 AO up to 30% of the evaded amount.

Director personal liability: The managing director is the statutory representative under § 34 AO; negligent non-withholding may constitute a criminal offence under §§ 266a StGB or § 370 AO; personal liability orders may cover the full uncollected WHT; imprisonment of 6 months to 5 years; professional disqualification and travel restrictions possible.
AML / Sanctions Exposure

Statutory sanction: Fines under AWV up to EUR 500,000 or up to 10 years’ imprisonment for embargo violations; administrative fines under GwG for AML failures up to EUR 1 million for banks; asset freezes and blocked accounts imposed by ECB/BaFin.

Director personal liability: GmbH directors may be personally liable under § 9 OWiG for corporate fines if they negligently failed to prevent compliance violations; personal criminal liability under §§ 17, 18 AWG for signing or instructing exports that circumvent sanctions; imprisonment of 2–10 years, travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition requests possible.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the Local Court (Amtsgericht - Registergericht) having jurisdiction over the registered office district; national index accessible through the Company Register Portal (Unternehmensregister).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung - GmbHG), Sections 1, 3, 4a, 5, 7, 8, 10, 35-44 and 46-49; Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch - HGB), Sections 8 and 106.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Statutory minimum share capital of EUR 25,000 per GmbHG Section 5(1); at least EUR 12,500 (50%) of each cash contribution must be paid up before registration per GmbHG Section 7(2). Contributions are deposited into a segregated bank escrow account (Sonderkonto) opened in the name of the company-in-formation; the bank's payment confirmation is submitted with the registration application.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification per the Model Building Code (Musterbauordnung) for office premises, including fire alarm system checks and emergency exit signage; structural load inspections are not applicable to standard office occupancy but TAB (technical connection) approval is required for any server rooms; workplace clearance and occupational safety obligations under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz - ArbSchG) Section 5 and the Ordinance on Industrial Safety and Health (BetrSichV), covering electrical equipment inspections, ergonomic workstation assessment, and accident-prevention rules per DGUV Regulations.

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum share capital of EUR 25,000 per GmbHG Section 5(1); at least EUR 12,500 (50%) of each cash contribution must be paid up before registration per GmbHG Section 7(2). Contributions are deposited into a segregated bank escrow account (Sonderkonto) opened in the name of the company-in-formation; the bank's payment confirmation is submitted with the registration application.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable - the proposed activity is office-based non-dilutive revenue-based financing and royalty financing (ISIC 6499). No industrial or manufacturing authority license is required. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) has confirmed the exemption from banking licensing for commercial lending activities under KWG Section 2(1) No. 1; the financial sector AML registration (if triggered) is administered by the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) through the Deutsche Bundesbank register, not by an industrial licensing board.

Category: Not applicable - no operating or manufacturing permit is required under the Trade Regulation Act (Gewerbeordnung). The company only performs a trade notification duty (Gewerbeanmeldung) which triggers trade tax, not a concessionary operating license.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, Federal Central Tax Office) – www.bzst.de; ELSTER electronic filing portal; local tax office (Finanzamt) responsible for the GmbH's Steuernummer and corporate tax assessment; Generalzolldirektion for customs and import VAT.

E-government filing portal

The GmbH must submit a tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt issues the local tax number (Steuernummer) for income tax, corporate tax and VAT purposes. Where cross-border supplies or intra-Community acquisitions occur, the GmbH also applies to the BZSt for a separate German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). Required documents include the notarised Memorandum and Articles of Association, Trade Register extraction (Handelsregisterauszug), proof of business address, managing director identification, and anticipated turnover declaration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not have a check-the-box regime; a GmbH is mandatorily taxed at entity level as a corporation (Körperschaftsteuer plus Gewerbesteuer plus solidarity surcharge on corporate income tax). Available elections include VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) for intra-group VAT consolidation, and limited hybrid mismatch adjustments under §4k EStG/ATAD II; no elective pass-through classification exists for non-resident shareholders.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€22,000 previous-year turnover plus €50,000 current-year projection for the small-entrepreneur exemption under §19 UStG; a foreign resident entity supplying goods or services in Germany is generally required to register regardless of turnover if VAT-exempt treatment is claimed or the supply originates from a German establishment. Intra-Community distance sales exceed the €10,000 consolidated threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% export VAT applies to physical supplies of goods transported directly to a non-EU third country by the vendor or a commissioned agent, as well as to deemed exports (e.g., sales to export warehouses with title transfer abroad). Intra-Community supplies are also zero-rated as tax-free intra-Community deliveries when the purchaser communicates a valid German or EU VAT ID number and the goods are physically dispatched. Offshore services (e.g., royalty financing services to non-residents) qualify for zero-rating if the recipient's activity is outside Germany and the services are not tied to German real estate; B2B place-of-supply rules under §3a UStG apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Zollanmeldung generated via ATLAS/ELAMS)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Export confirmation or proof of arrival / third-country customs clearance
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation showing foreign currency receipt

No real-time fiscal clearance, fiscal device API, or digital stamping applies in Germany. Mandatory structured electronic invoicing for domestic B2B supplies (E-Rechnung) applies from 1 January 2025, using XRechnung or ZUGFeRD formats and PEPPOL network conditions; VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically via ELSTER. No central government real-time tax clearance approval is required before issuing invoices.

Fraudulent zero-rating claims are treated as tax evasion and unjustified VAT benefits are fully clawed back by retrospective assessments. Interest accrues at 0.5% per month, penalties of up to 25% of the evaded VAT; criminal fines or imprisonment up to 10 years may apply. Export evidence must be maintained for the statutory limitation period of 10 years where returns were omitted, and failure to provide acceptable proof upon audit triggers immediate invalidation of the 0% rate plus a 19% VAT assessment plus late-payment interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, which guarantees free movement of capital both within the EU and between the EU and third countries, subject only to limited safeguarding measures. Germany applies no general exchange-control approval requirement for capital-account or current-account transactions. Equity injections, shareholder loans, revenue-based financing inflows, and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without central bank authorisation. Profit repatriation is unrestricted in substance, but legal-formal constraints may apply through tax withholding under Sections 49(1) No. 5 and 50a of the German Income Tax Act (EStG) or dividend withholding tax pursuant to Section 43(1) No. 1 EStG, which may be partially or fully relieved under an applicable double-tax treaty. Administrative balance-of-payments reporting obligations to the Deutsche Bundesbank apply to resident enterprises for every cross-border transaction exceeding €12,500.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or duty to repatriate export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in any freely convertible currency, used for offshore trade settlements, or converted into euros on the open market through licensed banks freely. The only obligation is statistical reporting of outward and inward transactions to the Deutsche Bundesbank where the transaction amount exceeds the reporting threshold of €12,500.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank, Eurosystem member) in regulatory coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) as the national competent authority for banking and AML matters; the European Central Bank exercises monetary policy and foreign-reserve oversight for the euro area.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for transaction banking and treasury visibility, but Low as the primary repository for capital escrow or financing-disbursement rails. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are legally usable only when properly passporting their e-money authorisation into Germany under the Second Payment Services Directive (EU) 2015/2366 and the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG). An EMI is statutory prohibited from taking deposits, granting credit, or operating a full banking licence (Section 1(2) ZAG); it may hold segregated e-money funds but not provide fiduciary escrow in the same legal comfort as a German universal bank. A German GmbH may lawfully maintain a collection account at an EU-authorised EMI for incoming revenue-share payments and to obtain deterministic instant-payment rails, but the capital escrow and significant outbound SWIFT MT103 distributions should rest with a licensed commercial bank. A BaFin registration or passport-notification is mandatory for the EMI; failure would render the account illegal and expose the GmbH to reverse-burden AML penalties.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Notarised incorporation certificate and commercial register excerpt; audited annual financial statements of the parental entity or investor fund; signed bank reference letter from an EU or otherwise sanction-clean bank; certified source-of-wealth statement prepared by a German tax adviser (Steuerberater); reconstructed funds-flow trail of bank statements for the last 24 months; sworn affidavit of ultimate ownership; European UBO-register extract and sanctions-screening report.
Business model classified as unregulated lending or shadow banking through ISIC 6499 revenue-sharing agreements

Root cause: Business model classified as unregulated lending or shadow banking through ISIC 6499 revenue-sharing agreements

Defense evidence: German commercial-law legal opinion confirming that the revenue-based financing is structured as a purchase of future revenue rights or a silent partnership (Unterbeteiligung) outside the scope of the German Banking Act (KWG); standardised financing agreement containing a clear commercial logic and an APR-equivalent calculation; proof that no deposits are accepted from the public and no granting of credit as a business is performed; documented investor/funder cap table; if available, a formal tax ruling (verbindliche Auskunft) confirming tax treatment; and audited reconciliations showing collections flowing through exclusively disclosed commercial-receivables contracts.
Inbound cross-border wires from high-risk or unconventional jurisdictions, including fintech wallets and cryptocurrency-adjacent intermediaries

Root cause: Inbound cross-border wires from high-risk or unconventional jurisdictions, including fintech wallets and cryptocurrency-adjacent intermediaries

Disbursement velocity and split-payment structure to multiple digital-brand beneficiaries pointing to possible round-tripping or phantom receivables

Root cause: Disbursement velocity and split-payment structure to multiple digital-brand beneficiaries pointing to possible round-tripping or phantom receivables

Defense evidence: Receivables-purchase registry with signed debtor-consented assignments; ERP back-office audit trail for each revenue-share calculation and payment run; open payment ledger showing all outbound disbursements, invoice numbers, and bank confirmation statements; independent auditor (Wirtschaftsprüfer) review report on internal controls and cash-flow segregation; third-party sanctions-screening audit of the entire beneficiary universe; and contractual proof that each payout corresponds to verifiable historical revenue streams of the underlying digital brands.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound capital injections, the originating investor must transmit funds exclusively through SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pain.001 payment messages carrying a precise German-purpose annotation in the Remittance Information—for example 'Unbare Einlage / Kapitalerhöhung', 'Gesellschafterdarlehen', or 'Umsatzbeteiligungsvertrag – Nicht-Darlehen'. The receiving account must be held in the exact legal name of the GmbH; funds can only be accepted into the capital-escrow or operational account after the bank has completed beneficial-owner verification and sanctions screening. Individual inbound transfers exceeding the German external-sector statistical threshold of €12,500 trigger an automatic reporting duty to the Deutsche Bundesbank on the part of the resident GmbH. For transfers from outside the EU, the correspondent bank must have a demonstrably current AML risk assessment, and the transaction must not use an intermediary shell financial institution; otherwise the receiving bank will flag the wire as a suspicious-transaction blocker.All euro-denominated disbursements to SEPA beneficiaries shall be executed via SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001.001.09) for reliability, and direct-debit collections via SEPA Direct Debit in accordance with the target payment rails. For foreign-currency and non-EEA payments, the required clearing vehicle is SWIFT MT103 with full originator and beneficiary information compliant with EU Regulation 2015/847 on the Transfer of Funds. Where the GmbH purchases digital goods, licensed media, or commercial inventories from third-party vendors, commercial letters of credit (documentary credits under UCP 600) are permissible avalised instruments, but they should be reserved for vendor-managed trade flows that demand security. Every outbound disbursement must be preceded by an internal sanctions-screen of the beneficiary name and account; no bundled or batch payment may be released without a transaction-level compliance check. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, typically 14-17%), resulting in an effective combined rate of approximately 30%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (total 26.375%)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File required if cross-border related-party transactions exceed €5 million (goods) or €500,000 (services) annually; also required if annual turnover exceeds €100 million

Economic substance scope: Germany applies substance-over-form and anti-abuse rules (e.g., §42 AO). No specific ESR regime, but treaty benefits require genuine economic activity; shell companies may be denied treaty access and subject to recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 10 years, fines up to €50,000, and full restitution of evaded taxes plus interest

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes under §69 AO; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed; criminal prosecution can lead to extradition
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, additional tax assessments up to 100% of underpaid tax, and penalties

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for resulting tax deficiencies; potential criminal charges for aiding tax evasion
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for serious violations; imprisonment up to 5 years for intentional non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned; regulatory bans from managing financial entities; travel restrictions may apply
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 5-10% of adjustment amount (minimum €5,000), and interest on underpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; criminal charges for tax evasion if intent proven

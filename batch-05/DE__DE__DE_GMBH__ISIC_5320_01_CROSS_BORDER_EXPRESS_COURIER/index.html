Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office address alone is not permissible as the German registered office of a GmbH engaged in ISIC 5320 cross-border express courier operations. The GmbH must have a genuine registered office in a German municipality, with actual business equipment, accessible files, a person authorized to accept service of process, and commercially meaningful local management. A serviced or shared office may qualify only if the company genuinely operates there and the address appears in the trade register and on all commercial documents. Pure virtual-office arrangements, mail-forwarding-only addresses or letterbox addresses will be rejected by the Registry Court and by the bank/KYC process.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and OSS registrations; local tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and employer taxes; portals: ELSTER portal (elster.de) and BZSt Online-Portal (BOP).

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the GmbH, the managing director must complete the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns the domestic tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax, trade tax, wage tax, and VAT advance returns. After the tax number is allocated, the GmbH must apply separately to BZSt for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer or USt-IdNr.) through the BZSt Online-Portal. A non-resident foreign entity without a German registered seat or branch may file a direct VAT registration application with BZSt for German VAT obligations; if the foreign entity has a permanent establishment or fixed facility in Germany, it must register with the competent Finanzamt and must also apply for a VAT ID through BZSt.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Germany. A GmbH is by statute an opaque corporate taxpayer subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), the solidarity surcharge, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); it cannot elect pass-through or partnership taxation. Under §1a Körperschaftsteuergesetz, only a partnership may elect to be taxed as a corporation, which does not alter the GmbH's default corporate classification. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available and are superseded by the German implementation of the EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules, which override contractual or entity classification mismatch arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany does not apply a general VAT registration turnover threshold for established taxable entrepreneurs; a GmbH carrying on economic activity is a taxable person from inception. Foreign entrepreneurs must register for German VAT if they make taxable supplies in Germany and do not cover the supply through the One-Stop-Shop or OSS regime. For the small-business exemption under §19 UStG, the prior-year turnover must not exceed EUR 25,000 and the expected current-year turnover must not exceed EUR 100,000. The EU-wide intra-Community distance sales and OSS threshold is EUR 10,000 in aggregate annual value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for zero-rating under §4 No.1 and §6 UStG when the goods are delivered to a third-country customer, physically leave the EU territory, the exporter or a forwarding agent effects transport, and the export is evidenced by a customs export declaration in ATLAS with a Movement Reference Number. Offshore services are not treated as German taxable supplies where the place of supply is situated outside Germany under the B2B place-of-supply rules in §3a UStG; internationally performed transport services and ancillary services directly connected to an export shipment are eligible for zero-rating if the transport or service is essentially performed outside Germany and the destination of the goods is outside the EU. The taxpayer must retain full book and record evidence showing the third-country status of the customer, the destination, the transport chain, and the movement of the goods across the EU border.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS Movement Reference Number or Export Accompanying Document) evidencing exit from EU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or equivalent transport document (CMR for road, postal/courier receipt) showing the consignment is destined to a third country
  • Inward remittance evidence comprising SWIFT confirmation, bank statement or payment journal demonstrating receipt and repatriation of the export consideration

Domestic B2B e-invoicing is mandatory on a phased basis: from 1 January 2025 every German VAT-registered business must be able to receive electronic invoices; from 1 January 2027 suppliers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices for domestic B2B transactions; from 1 January 2028 all German VAT-registered suppliers must issue e-invoices. Accepted structured formats are XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.0.1 or EDI-compliant data exchange; invoices are exchanged through an E-Rechnungsport/Peppol-based interoperability infrastructure. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital continuous-control system for ordinary VAT invoices. Export invoices remain governed by the standard statutory invoicing rules and may be issued in PDF or paper provided the export evidence is retained.

In VAT audits, incomplete or missing export evidence causes the zero-rated treatment to be denied, the supply is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply, and 19% VAT is assessed retroactively, together with interest at 0.5% per month under §233a AO and late payment additions under §240 AO. Intentional or reckless issuance of zero-rated export invoices without a qualifying export transaction, including falsification of export documents, constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO and is punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to ten years. The tax authority may also apply penalty interest, enhanced assessment periods, joint and several liability for persons involved, and clawback of input VAT recovery under the German VAT anti-fraud provisions and EU mutual assistance instruments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully liberalised current and capital account regime under Article 64 TFEU; no exchange control permissions are required for cross-border payments or profit remittances. The Euro is freely convertible, and receivables and payables in foreign currencies may be contracted and settled without restriction. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to tax withholding obligations, not FX barriers: dividend distributions to non-resident corporate shareholders generally attract German withholding tax at 25% (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge if applicable), subject to treaty reductions to 0-5%. Cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 trigger statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV; commercial and tax documentation must be preserved to evidence compliant clearing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds. German exporters may invoice in EUR or any freely convertible currency, may retain export proceeds in foreign currency accounts indefinitely, and have no obligation to convert such proceeds into EUR. The only applicable legal constraint is the AWV cross-border payment reporting duty to Deutsche Bundesbank for payments at or above EUR 12,500; this is purely statistical and does not restrict currency management.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary statutory banking solution, Medium as an ancillary treasury rail. PSD2-licensed EEA electronic money institutions may support cross-border FX conversion, supplier disbursement, and courier clearing settlement, and may permit fully remote onboarding. However, an EMI cannot be used for the GmbH statutory capital deposit confirmation required by the notary and trade register, which requires a regulated credit institution. The strongest structure uses a German universal bank as the base relationship and an EMI for optimised FX and low-value settlement, with full BaFin and GWG oversight.

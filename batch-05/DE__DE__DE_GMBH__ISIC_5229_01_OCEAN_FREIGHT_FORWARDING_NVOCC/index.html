Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory minimum share capital for a standard limited liability company is EUR 25,000. At least EUR 12,500 in cash must be paid up before the registration filing is submitted, deposited into an escrow cash account (notary special deposit account) or the company's pre-registration bank account, and confirmed to the local court.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate (Brandschutzzertifikat) issued by local fire inspection authority for office and any ancillary warehousing; structural load clearance for occupied premises under the respective state building code (Landesbauordnung); occupational health and safety clearance for workplace operations under the Occupational Safety Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz), including emergency planning, first aid, and risk assessments.

Paid-in capital rule: The statutory minimum share capital for a standard limited liability company is EUR 25,000. At least EUR 12,500 in cash must be paid up before the registration filing is submitted, deposited into an escrow cash account (notary special deposit account) or the company's pre-registration bank account, and confirmed to the local court.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authority is the local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate and value added tax registration, using the ELSTER electronic tax administration portal. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt fuer Steuern, BZSt) issues the German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) and operates the related online services. Customs matters are administered by the German Customs Administration (Generalzolldirektion) through the ATLAS electronic customs system.

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH must submit the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung to its competent Finanzamt, generally through ELSTER, after incorporation and commencement of business activity. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), normally required for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT filings. The GmbH or its tax representative may separately apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.); the application generally requires the legal name, registered address, commercial-register details, domestic tax number, and responsible Finanzamt. A foreign entity without a German establishment must register with the Finanzamt responsible for its German taxable activities and may apply for a USt-IdNr. through the BZSt. Importers and exporters require an Economic Operators Registration and Identification number (EORI) from German Customs for customs declarations. VAT registration is generally required from the first German taxable transaction unless a specific statutory exemption or small-business treatment applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A GmbH is a separate legal and tax person subject to German corporation tax on its worldwide income, subject to treaty allocation and foreign-tax relief, and to municipal trade tax on its German trade or business. Distributions are generally subject to withholding tax. A GmbH does not default to pass-through taxation. German tax residence normally follows statutory seat or place of management principles.

Available election forms

A GmbH has no German check-the-box election to obtain partnership-style pass-through treatment. The corporation may elect, where legally available, statutory tax treatments such as the option under Section 1a of the German Corporation Tax Act for eligible partnerships; that election is not available to a GmbH. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Section 4k of the Income Tax Act and related ATAD implementation provisions can deny deductions or require inclusion for arrangements producing deduction or non-inclusion, double deduction, or imported mismatch outcomes. Corporate income tax, VAT, and trade-tax classifications are determined under German law and are not freely selectable by entity election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold below which a German-established business may disregard VAT registration for ordinary taxable activities. The small-business regime under Section 19 UStG may apply if prior-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and exclusion rules; the regime generally prevents charging VAT and restricts input-tax recovery. A non-established foreign business generally has no practical registration threshold for German taxable supplies, although reverse-charge, marketplace, import, and other special rules may apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax credit under Sections 4 number 1(a) and 6 UStG if the goods are transported or dispatched to a third country, the statutory export conditions are satisfied, and the supplier maintains prescribed documentary or accounting evidence. The supplier must establish that the goods physically left the EU customs territory; an invoice alone is insufficient. For ocean freight forwarding and NVOCC activities, a freight or logistics service supplied to a business customer is generally governed by the B2B place-of-supply rule and may be outside the scope of German VAT when the customer is established outside Germany, or subject to reverse charge where the customer is established in another jurisdiction. Such services are not automatically treated as zero-rated German exports. Services directly connected with the import or export of goods may qualify for a specific exemption only where the detailed statutory conditions are met. Export VAT refunds or input-tax recovery require valid invoices, business-use evidence, customs records, and reconciliation of the underlying transaction.

  • Electronic export declaration submitted through ATLAS, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Export accompanying document or equivalent customs data and the customs exit confirmation or electronic confirmation of exit
  • Commercial invoice, contract, packing list, and evidence identifying the exporter, consignee, goods, value, destination, and transport route
  • Bill of lading, sea waybill, carrier confirmation, or other transport documentation evidencing dispatch to a non-EU destination
  • Proof of delivery or other commercially reliable evidence where required to supplement the customs exit record
  • Accounting records linking the export declaration, invoice, shipment, and VAT return
  • Customer VAT identification and establishment evidence for B2B place-of-supply or reverse-charge treatment, where applicable
  • An inward remittance certificate is not generally a mandatory German VAT zero-rating document; bank records may nevertheless be relevant corroborative evidence and may be required under contractual, banking, or foreign-exchange rules applicable in another jurisdiction

Germany does not operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system. From 1 January 2025, German-established businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for relevant domestic business-to-business transactions. Issuance of structured e-invoices is subject to transitional rules: businesses may generally continue using other invoice formats during 2025 and 2026, and businesses whose prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 800,000 may generally continue during 2027; mandatory structured e-invoicing is generally applicable from 2028. Permitted formats include European-standard EN 16931 formats such as XRechnung and compliant hybrid formats such as ZUGFeRD. There is no general requirement for pre-clearance, digital tax stamping, or direct real-time submission of each invoice to the Finanzamt. Cross-border supplies, business-to-consumer invoices, and certain exempt or special transactions may be outside the domestic B2B e-invoicing issuance mandate.

Germany has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days, and Germany generally has no exchange-control repatriation deadline. Incorrectly claimed export exemptions or input-tax refunds may be reassessed through a corrected VAT assessment, with repayment of improperly refunded or unpaid VAT, statutory interest under the German Fiscal Code, late-payment charges, and possible penalties for late or inaccurate returns. The Finanzamt may deny exemption or input-tax recovery where export evidence is incomplete, inconsistent, fabricated, or not linked to the specific transaction. Intentional or reckless false VAT declarations can constitute tax evasion or negligent tax reduction under the German Fiscal Code; intentional tax evasion may result in criminal fines or imprisonment, with aggravated cases carrying higher penalties. Participation in fraudulent invoice chains, customs fraud, document falsification, or knowingly facilitating an incorrect zero-rating claim may create additional criminal, civil, customs, and professional-liability exposure. Refunds and exemptions can be clawed back for the applicable statutory assessment period, subject to extended periods where tax evasion or reckless conduct is alleged.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility as a member of the eurozone. No exchange control restrictions exist for non-resident or resident GmbH shareholders; profits, dividends, and liquidation proceeds are freely transferable, subject only to anti-money laundering (AML) documentary checks and, where applicable, statutory trade sanction filters. Legal hurdles to profit remittance arise exclusively from corporate law solvency requirements and CIT withholding mechanics, not from foreign exchange rationing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion obligation applies to export proceeds, including NVOCC freight revenues collected in EUR or foreign currency. German foreign trade law imposes only statistical disclosure under AWV §§ 66–70: resident enterprises must report cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 per transaction or aggregated monthly threshold in accordance with the published payment purpose key (Zahlungszweck). EUR accounts can hold foreign currency without repatriation constraints; foreign currency accounts may be established without Bundesbank authorization.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank); in coordination with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) for banking prudential supervision, while the Bundesbank administers cross-border reporting duties under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German-regulated and EU-passported EMI (e-money institution) platforms can provide transparent IBANs and SWIFT/SEPA connectivity for freight settlement, but they do not offer a full banking licence, are not covered by the German statutory deposit guarantee scheme, and cannot hold client funds as bank deposits. EMIs are legally permissible for operating disbursements and treasury aggregation purposes, but inadvisable as the primary treasury bank for the GmbH due to capital escrow attestation requirements for commercial register filing (Kostengesetz / GmbHG § 7) and to avoid perceived underwriting risk.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership opacity – GmbH held by foreign corporate entities or trusts, or delays in UBO filing due to incomplete Präsenz- oder Beteiligungskette.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity – GmbH held by foreign corporate entities or trusts, or delays in UBO filing due to incomplete Präsenz- oder Beteiligungskette.

Defense evidence: Notarised articles of association, current excerpt from the German commercial register (Handelsregisterauszug), complete UBO declaration under § 20 GwG, shareholder certificates, group structure chart, and sworn declaration of the ultimate beneficial owner; if the parent is not EU/EEA, provide certified and apostilled corporate register extract from the foreign registry, appointed directors passport copies, and a bank reference letter.
NVOCC Medium-Risk trade pattern – high volume of cross-border payments to unrelated freight consolidators, short invoice cycles, payments to high-risk jurisdictions, and potential sanctions exposure.

Root cause: NVOCC Medium-Risk trade pattern – high volume of cross-border payments to unrelated freight consolidators, short invoice cycles, payments to high-risk jurisdictions, and potential sanctions exposure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder equity injection into the GmbH is executed through standard SWIFT MT103 (cross-border) or SEPA CT payment (EU/EEA) into the designated capital escrow account (Mittelkonto) at the German bank. Purpose-of-payment field (remittance information / Verwendungszweck) must clearly state e.g. 'Einzahlung Stammkapital GmbH-Vertrag [Date]' or 'Kapitalerhöhung'. For monetary capital contributions exceeding €12,500 from outside Germany, comply with Bundesbank AWV § 67 statistical reporting using payment purpose key 'Z05 – Kapitaleinlage' (for capital contribution by safeguarding the BaFin/Bundesbank reporting date); for equity grants in kind, a notarial report and auditor confirmation are required. SEPA transactions from another EU bank require no AWV declaration only if no statutory domicile is concerned; nevertheless the bank will ask for documentary proof of underlying legal transaction to prevent false purpose declarations.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT network wire (MT103) for non-EUR payments to overseas shipping lines, feeders, port operators, and NVOCC partners; for EUR-denominated intra-EU settlement, SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) with ISO 20022 XML is standard. Documentary collections under UCP600 or standalone Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) may be used for high-value vessel chartering or container lease operations where beneficiary trust is moderate. All disbursement instructions must include the precise invoice reference, freight contract number, and (where applicable) the country-specific AWV payment purpose key; payment messages cannot contain false commercial descriptions. Sanctions screening against EU/UN/UK lists is mandatory. Multi-currency operating accounts at the main bank allow netting of EUR/USD movements; liquidity is optimised through notional pooling or zero-balancing structures if the GmbH is part of an economically integrated group, otherwise physical sweeps are performed on a per-reporting basis.Outright deliverable FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/CHF, EUR/CNY) traded under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or German Rahmenvereinigung für Devisengeschäfte (DKV/DFV) framework, provided the underlying is a genuine commercial exposure and supporting bills of lading or invoices exist.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market currencies not freely deliverable in Germany (e.g., EUR/CNY not fully due to PRC capital controls, or EUR/INR) – banks set these up under confirmations with modified settlement clauses.FX options (plain-vanilla euro calls/puts) for hedging freight revenue in USD or RMB without eliminating upside, permitted for corporate clients meeting BaFin suitability requirements.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CIRS) when a GmbH has bank debt drawn in USD or CNY against an EUR Treasury, creating a synthetic EUR liability and reducing balance-sheet currency mismatch.Market-standard foreign exchange spot transactions do not require derivation of a hedging instrument, but are included under the same treasury policy umbrella.

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no generally applicable statutory 30-day company-name reservation. Before notarization, submit the proposed name, registered-office municipality, business description, and ownership details to the competent Chamber of Industry and Commerce and request a written name assessment; the notary may also coordinate with the register court. A formal complaint against a register-court decision is generally available under the German FamFG framework, subject to the order's appeal instructions and applicable deadlines; obtain German procedural advice before filing. A separate administrative appeal is normally not available against an informal Chamber opinion.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital is EUR 25,000 under §5(1) GmbHG. On registration, at least EUR 12,500 of the cash contributions must be paid up and under the unrestricted control of the managing directors; the balance is payable if and when called. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) is the central federal authority responsible for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.); the local 'Finanzamt' (state tax office) is the competent authority for corporate TIN ('Steuernummer') and substantive tax administration. The official electronic portal is Elster (ELSTER: Elektronische Steuererklärung) at www.elster.de, with BZSt online services at www.bzst.de.

E-government filing portal

Foreign and domestic entities must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the responsible local Finanzamt, generally within one month after incorporation or, for non-resident businesses, upon first taxable activity in Germany. The registration is submitted electronically via Elster or through a certified 'Steuerberater' (German fiscal adviser). The Finanzamt issues a corporate TIN ('Steuernummer'). A separate application must be made to BZSt to obtain the USt-IdNr. (VAT ID). Non-resident entrepreneurs without a German establishment may register directly with the Finanzamt indicated in the questionnaire; a fiscal representative is not mandatory under German VAT law but may be needed for certain administrative acts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide check-the-box classification for a GmbH. The GmbH is per se a separate taxable entity subject to corporation tax, trade tax, and VAT. Available statutory elections include: (i) VAT and corporation tax grouping under the Organschaft rules (Secs. 14-19 KStG and Sec. 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG); (ii) small-business VAT exemption under Sec. 19 UStG for resident micro-entrepreneurs below the threshold (approx. EUR 22,000 prior-year / EUR 50,000 current-year); and (iii) hybrid mismatch elections and immunities under Sec. 4k EStG and the Digital Tax Administration Act. The GmbH cannot elect partnership pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold applies to a foreign supplier performing taxable domestic supplies in Germany, because a tax liability arises once German taxable turnover is carried out. A EUR 10,000 annual threshold applies to intra-Community distance sales of goods and certain electronically supplied services under the EU OSS/IOSS regime. Resident small businesses may remain outside VAT under Sec. 19 UStG if prior-year turnover was not more than EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (i) exports of goods to third-country (non-EU) destinations under Sec. 4 Nr. 1a UStG; (ii) intra-Community deliveries to other EU Member States under Sec. 4 Nr. 1b UStG; and (iii) certain cross-border B2B services performed for non-EU customers under Sec. 4 Abs. 1 UStG in conjunction with Sec. 3a UStG. Claiming 0% requires proof that the goods left Germany/EU and reached a foreign territory, evidenced by customs export confirmation (ATLAS), transport documents, and business records. For refractory lining works installed on industrial boilers, a supply of services connected with immovable equipment in Germany or abroad must follow place-of-supply rules; zero-rating only applies where the customer is established in a third country or the services relate to goods exported by the supplier.

  • ATLAS Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Export Accompanying Document)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR Transport Document
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bank transfer confirmation / Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing payment from foreign customer

From 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B supplies made between German-established entrepreneurs must be invoiced using E-Rechnung (structured e-invoice) and the counterpart must be able to receive EN16931-compliant formats (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x). Mandatory issuance is phased: from 2027 for businesses with annual turnover exceeding EUR 800,000, and from 2028 for all enterprises. Germany has not implemented real-time central tax clearance or fiscal stamping; e-invoices must be technically validated, but the Finanzamt performs post-clearance verification through income tax, net worth tax, and VAT audits. API-integrated submission is primarily through the Elster portal.

The Finanzamt may retrospectively disallow the 0% rate and charge VAT plus interest if documentary proof is deficient or if the underlying receivable is not repatriated in accordance with foreign trade and tax rules. Assessment interest accrues at 0.5% per month from the due date (Sec. 233a AO). Deliberate false declarations constitute tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, punishable with fines or imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in aggravated cases. Negligent undercharging triggers fines under Sec. 378 AO. The statute of limitations for hidden tax offences is generally 10 years in evasion cases and 4 years for standard assessments. The BZSt and federal export-control authorities can additionally impose customs penalties and denial of export privilege.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully convertible capital and current account regime under the EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital and payments. No local exchange control law requires prior approval for foreign direct investment, dividend remittance, or repatriation of capital for non-resident shareholders, except for FDI screening under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung) in selected sensitive sectors. Profit distribution hurdles are limited to standard German corporate law (GmbH Act) requirements: a balance sheet profit, a formal shareholders' resolution, and VAT/tax clearance certificates in certain cases. Dividends can be remitted to non-resident shareholders without withholding tax under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if the shareholder qualifies, or at the treaty rate if a double-tax treaty applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory export proceeds surrender, no foreign currency surrender requirement, and no prescribed conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted freely; only cross-border payment reporting thresholds (generally EUR 12,500 or 5,000 for certain cash transactions) must be observed for Bundesbank statistics. Receipts related to direct investment (e.g., shareholder loans or equity contributions) must be reported to the Direct Investment Survey (Bestandserhebung) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in conjunction with the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and AML supervision; the Bundesbank administers external balance-of-payments statistics and direct investment reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. EU-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passporting into Germany under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) may open payment accounts and execute cross-border EUR transfers without a full banking license. However, EMIs are not permitted to take deposits for lending purposes, are subject to BaFin supervision, and generally face stricter compliance reviews by commercial banks acting as partner banks. An EMI is viable as a secondary settlement rail for disbursements and receiving client payments, but a licensed German commercial bank account remains strongly advisable as the primary treasury and capital escrow account for foreign direct investment and tax-relevant transactional flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital and shareholder loan contributions must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 in EUR to the GmbH's domestic corporate account. The payment must include the corporate IBAN and a clear purpose reference (Verwendungszweck), typically 'Einlage Gesellschafter' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen', plus the VAT or tax ID if referenced. Any cross-border payment equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics, and a direct investment relationship must be reported if a non-resident shareholder owns 10% or more. To avoid reclassification as commercial income, the inbound payment should be supported by notarized contribution minutes and bank-side documentation evidencing the capital transaction. No foreign exchange approval is required, and the funds are immediately at the free disposal of the GmbH.Outbound trade settlements for refractory materials, logistics, and subcontract labor are cleared via SWIFT MT103 payments from the GmbH's EUR account, or via commercial documentary letters of credit (UCP600) and standby LCs for larger procurement contracts. For EU-zone counterparties, SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) is permissible for low-value urgent payments; for non-EU suppliers, standard international wire with bank charges negotiated in the underlying contract is the primary rail. All outbound payments must include the external transaction code required by Bundesbank reporting where the threshold applies. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporation tax, producing an effective federal corporate tax burden of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and generally produces an overall burden of approximately 22.83% to 32.45%, depending on the municipal multiplier.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic withholding burden of 26.375%. A qualifying corporate recipient may obtain full or partial relief under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in German law, a tax treaty, or a refund procedure. German participation exemption rules may also apply at the recipient level, subject to statutory conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporation tax return is generally due seven months after the end of the tax year. For a calendar-year GmbH, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser prepares the return, the extended statutory adviser deadline generally runs to the last day of February of the second subsequent year, subject to statutory exceptions, audit orders and special deadline rules.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Monthly or quarterly VAT advance returns are generally due by the 10th day after the relevant reporting period. A permanent filing extension may be available upon application and, for monthly filers, payment of the prescribed special advance. The annual VAT return is generally due seven months after the calendar year-end, subject to adviser extensions and applicable statutory exceptions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany does not impose a general monetary threshold below which arm's-length documentation is unnecessary. A Local File may be requested for cross-border related-party transactions and must be prepared contemporaneously for material transactions. Statutory threshold indicators commonly used for ordinary documentation obligations are EUR 6,000,000 annual cross-border supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 annual cross-border supplies of services and other transactions. A Master File is generally required where the German entity belongs to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 100,000,000 and the statutory conditions are met. Additional contemporaneous documentation is required for extraordinary transactions, restructurings and material changes in functions, assets or risks.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone regime formally titled Economic Substance Regulations comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant under Section 42 of the Fiscal Code general anti-abuse rule, treaty beneficial-ownership and anti-conduit principles, the principal-purpose test in applicable treaties, EU anti-abuse rules, controlled foreign company rules and German transfer-pricing law. The GmbH should maintain genuine German management and decision-making, appropriately qualified personnel, operational premises, assets, commercial risk control, books and records, bank activity and evidence that contracts reflect actual functions. A paper entity used principally to obtain treaty benefits may be denied relief and may face tax reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under Section 370 of the Fiscal Code may result in imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine. Particularly serious cases may result in imprisonment from six months to ten years. Unpaid tax, interest and administrative recovery claims remain payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal prosecution, personal tax liability under Section 69 of the Fiscal Code, civil damages, asset seizure, professional disqualification and, in severe cases, detention or travel restrictions imposed by competent authorities. Corporate limited liability does not protect directors from intentional or grossly negligent tax misconduct.
Late Filing, Non-Cooperation and Estimated Assessment

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharges, coercive fines, estimated assessments under the Fiscal Code, enforcement costs, interest and possible administrative or criminal proceedings where the omission is intentional or reckless.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for tax losses caused by culpable failure to file or pay. Repeated non-compliance may support removal, insolvency-related claims and restrictions on conducting business.
Transfer-Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Income adjustments, denial of deductions, withholding-tax assessments, secondary adjustments, interest and documentation penalties. Failure to submit or materially inadequate documentation may trigger a penalty generally ranging from EUR 5,000 to 10% of the adjustment amount, with statutory minimum and maximum rules depending on the violation.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability for culpable tax underpayments, particularly where they approved unsupported intercompany arrangements or failed to maintain required documentation.
Abusive Treaty Shopping and Beneficial-Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or directive relief, collection of withholding tax, solidarity surcharge, interest, penalties and possible criminal tax proceedings where facts were intentionally misrepresented.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal withholding-tax liability, restitution claims, criminal exposure and asset-preservation measures. The corporate veil does not protect intentional abuse or false certification.
Illegal Profit Distribution and Capital Maintenance

Statutory sanction: Repayment claims under the GmbH Act, reclassification as hidden profit distribution, corporation tax and withholding-tax consequences, interest and possible insolvency clawback.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited payments under Section 43 of the GmbH Act, including restitution to the company and creditor damages. Insolvency-related payment restrictions can create additional personal exposure.
Insolvency and Delayed Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions may apply under insolvency law, including imprisonment or fines. Payments made after the relevant insolvency trigger may be recoverable, and tax, social-security and creditor claims may be pursued.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal restitution for prohibited payments, civil damages, criminal prosecution, asset seizure and possible travel restrictions. The GmbH's limited liability does not shield directors from insolvency-management breaches.
Money Laundering, UBO and AML Reporting

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act may be substantial and can reach statutory maximums of up to EUR 1,000,000 or multiples of the economic benefit for serious breaches, with higher limits for regulated entities. Criminal money-laundering conduct may result in imprisonment or fines.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible compliance officers may incur personal criminal and administrative liability, asset forfeiture, professional restrictions and, in serious cases, imprisonment. Beneficial-ownership register violations may also generate separate fines and public enforcement consequences.
Undeclared Employment, Wage Tax and Social Security

Statutory sanction: Retroactive wage-tax and social-security assessments, interest, contribution surcharges, administrative fines and criminal sanctions for withholding or contribution fraud. Serious illegal employment cases may result in imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for unpaid employee social-security contributions and wage-tax losses where statutory duties were breached, in addition to criminal prosecution and civil claims.
Customs, Import VAT and Sanctions Breaches

Statutory sanction: Recovery of customs duty and import VAT, customs penalties, confiscation, commercial restrictions and criminal sanctions for customs fraud or sanctions evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability, fines, asset seizure and travel restrictions where they authorized or knowingly tolerated customs or sanctions violations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully liberalised capital account regime. There are no general exchange controls or restrictions on inward or outward capital transfers, dividends, profit repatriation, loan repayments or trade settlements. Free movement of capital and payments is guaranteed under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). Current account transactions are freely convertible, and capital account transactions are also unrestricted except for limited EU/UN sanctions, embargo measures and prudential reporting obligations. Profit remittances by a German GmbH to non-resident shareholders are legally unrestricted; withholding tax and any applicable trade tax consequences do not constitute currency controls. Cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 or equivalent are subject to mandatory statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV, primarily using the PRAXIS/MINERVA electronic reporting system. Payment service providers are additionally required to monitor transactions for sanctions compliance under EU Regulation (EU) No 833/2014 and the German Supply Chain Due Diligence Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no mandatory export proceeds surrender rules, no prescribed conversion ratios, and no statutory timeline requiring repatriation of export receipts into Germany. Export proceeds may be retained abroad, freely denominated in foreign currency, and used directly for working capital or cross-border disbursements. The only applicable obligation is statistical in nature: resident exporters must report individual export payments received from abroad to the Deutsche Bundesbank if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500 or equivalent, under Section 67 AWV. The report must identify the counterparty, value date, currency amount and the underlying transaction code. For an ISIC 6619 cross-border payment gateway entity, the client-fund flows routed through segregated accounts also require correct balance-of-payments statistical classification and may trigger BaFin's ZAG safeguarding requirements for payment institutions.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank and external sector statistics authority), in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for licensing and prudential supervision under the German Banking Act (KWG) and the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), and with the Federal Ministry of Economics and Climate Action for the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium, subject to strict legal gates. An offshore digital EMI or payment institution cannot lawfully render payment services or operate acquiring/clearing rails in Germany solely on the basis of a remote foreign licence unless it holds an EEA passport under the Second Payment Services Directive (PSD2), or itself obtains a BaFin licence under the German ZAG. For a German GmbH, using a BaFin-licensed or EEA-passported EMI as a settlement or treasury conduit is viable; however, using an unlicensed offshore EMI creates a material regulatory, tax and criminal-law exposure, because holding client funds through such a vehicle will violate the safeguarding rules in Sections 662 and 664 of the German Civil Code interplay with ZAG Part 3, and may constitute unauthorised deposit-taking or payment services under Section 312 ZAG. The recommended route is either a BaFin-licensed German payment institution or e-money institution, or direct tier-1 corporate banking accounts. Direct PSP online merchant acquiring requires Visa/Mastercard membership or sponsorship, BaFin outsourcing oversight, and robust sub-merchant due diligence; therefore, an offshore-only EMIs route is rated Low for the primary treasury and clearing backbone.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital and shareholder-loan injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer from the beneficial shareholder's own bank account at a reputable bank, never via a funnel or third-party account. The commercial register entry or notarised capital contribution record must match the transferred amount and the account holder. German banks do not impose an exchange-control purpose code for capital injections, but the transfer narrative should accurately identify the basis in German commercial law, for example: 'Einlage Stammkapital GMBH', 'Kapitalerhoehung', or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen'. Where the shareholder is outside the EEA, the remitter should obtain a bank reference from their home bank and, where notarial authentication is required, submit the notarised and apostilled resolution. For payments into a GmbH capital account, the contribution is effective once the managing director obtains free disposal over the credited funds; therefore, the bank account must be in the GmbH's name and the booking must be unambiguously traceable to the contributing shareholder. Direct cross-border wire details: use SWIFT BIC/IBAN, instruct the correspondent bank to provide full originator and beneficiary information under EU Regulation (EU) 2015/847, and verify the paying bank's sanctions status before execution. After receipt, file the AWV statistical report with Deutsche Bundesbank if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500.Outbound trade and merchant settlement disbursements may be effected through SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfers for non-EUR payments, SWIFT MT202 for correspondent-bank cover payments, commercial letters of credit issued under UCP 600 for documentary trade flows, SEPA Credit Transfers for euro-denominated payments within the SEPA area, and TARGET2 for high-value euro clearing in central-bank money. For the cross-border payment gateway business, incoming card-scheme settlement funds from Visa/Mastercard are typically received via acquiring banks or scheme settlement files; onward merchant payout is normally made through SEPA or SWIFT to local merchant accounts. Where treasury needs require rapid cross-border clearing over weekends, a multi-currency account held with a tier-1 German bank or an EEA-headquartered correspondent network provides the fastest and most compliance-efficient rail. For trade-related disbursements outside the EU, the GmbH must apply the correct AWV transaction code for services, goods or capital transfers, maintain underlying invoices, customs declarations or contracts, and ensure that sanctioned jurisdictions or blocked parties are excluded from the payment trail. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

29.9% (15.0% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax, plus average municipal trade tax of approximately 14.0%; effective rate varies by municipality between approximately 22.8% and 33.0%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% withholding tax on dividends plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax); trade-tax-related withholding is not applied to dividends of GmbH. Final recipient can partially reclaim the withholding tax if the shareholder tax burden is lower.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally the statutory deadline is 31 July following the end of the calendar fiscal year; where tax advice is engaged, automatic extension to 28/29 February of the following year; further extension possible by application to the local tax office (Finanzamt). Quarterly prepayments of corporate income tax and trade tax are due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September, and 10 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany requires Local File and Master File if the enterprise (group) exceeds EUR 750 million consolidated turnover; CbC Report is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750 million. For medium-sized and smaller enterprises, simplified documentation applies if the controlled transaction value exceeds EUR 500,000 for goods or EUR 200,000 for services; a penalty-free threshold of EUR 100,000 applies; German tax auditors may impose documentation duties regardless of thresholds under Section 90(3) AO.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) statute as in the UAE/Caribbean jurisdictions, but the substance-over-form doctrine under Section 42 AO and anti-avoidance rules under Section 50d(3) EStG, Section 7 AStG, the abuse of law doctrine in BFH case law, and the EU ATAD implementation in Section 4h EStG (interest deduction limitation) impose robust substance requirements. A payment gateway GmbH must have actual staff premises, decision-making functions, risk management, and operational control of payment processing contracts; otherwise, royalty, interest, or service payments to related parties may be disallowed, and the entity may be treated as an agent of the foreign parent, creating a deemed PE under treaty rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Under Section 370 AO, imprisonment of up to five years, or up to ten years in aggravated cases; automatic assessment under Section 162 AO with rebuttable presumptions; monetary penalty up to EUR 365,000 for corporate fines and full restitution of evaded tax plus late interest at 0.15% per month; seizure of assets under Section 74 AO.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing officers face personal criminal prosecution, civil liability for corporate tax debts under Section 69 AO and Section 34(1) GmbHG for negligent breach of tax payment duties, travel bans and loss of residence permits, confiscation of personal assets, disqualification from holding directorships for up to five years, and extradition risks under EU Arrest Warrant; piercing the veil is available where capital is depleted or assets mixed.
Fake or Nominee Shareholder / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Under Section 5 GmbHG, strict minimum capital rules invalidate disguised contributions; transactions can be recharacterized under Section 42 AO with 25% surcharge on hidden profit distributions; fine under Geldwäschegesetz (GwG), administrative fines up to EUR 100,000 under GwG section 56; imprisonment for money laundering under Section 261 StGB up to five years; entity can be barred from payment services and license revoked by BaFin.

Director personal liability: Personal liability for unpaid corporate taxes under Section 69 AO, joint and several liability for social security contributions, compensation for damages under Section 43 GmbHG; courts have imposed property seizure and freezing orders; directors face criminal conviction for false certification under Section 348 StGB and revocation of license under KWG; prohibited from managerial activity in financial services.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under Section 56 GwG of up to EUR 1 million for natural persons and up to 10% of annual global turnover for legal persons (minimum EUR 10,000 in serious cases); criminal fines under Section 54 GwG; suspension or prohibition of activity by BaFin; reversal of merchants under Section 675u BGB; inclusion in public sanctions lists; civil damages to consumers under Section 675z BGB.

Director personal liability: AML officers and managing directors may be personally fined up to EUR 1 million under Section 56(3) GwG; imprisonment of up to five years for negligent money laundering, and up to ten years for gang-related laundering under Section 261 StGB; bank accounts in the GmbH may be frozen; directors have no right to rely on corporate veil for AML penalties as statutory liability attaches to persons.
Unlicensed Payment Institution Activity (Haftung through ZAG violation)

Statutory sanction: Criminal offense under Section 63 ZAG: imprisonment up to five years or fine up to EUR 500,000; cessation order and forced return of funds under Section 64 ZAG; BaFin may impose a penalty of up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; contractual voidness of payment services under German law; civil liability for merchant losses.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly continue unauthorised payment business incur personal criminal liability, are liable for all merchant settlements misappropriated, may have their assets subjected to enforcement, be banned from serving as directors of financial institutions for three to five years, and face cross-border Herkunftsstaat sanctions; corporate veil does not shield if used for criminal enterprise.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting Adjustments

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income under Section 162 AO with a surcharge of 25% of the under-declared tax; penalty for non-documentation of EUR 5,000 to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of the adjustment, whichever is higher; interest on additional tax; potential customs and VAT assessments; if treaty shopping is implicated, the German tax office may deny treaty benefits under Section 50d(3) EStG.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for the additional taxes and penalties if they signed false tax returns (Section 34 AO, Section 69 AO), must reimburse the entity for the tax damage under Section 43 GmbHG, and may be prosecuted for criminal tax evasion if intentional mispricing exceeds EUR 50,000; travel restrictions and passport surrender are available under criminal procedure rules (StPO).

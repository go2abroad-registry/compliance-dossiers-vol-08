Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital EUR 25,000; before registration at least EUR 12,500 in cash must be paid in and be freely at the managing directors' disposal, evidenced by a bank confirmation (Sperrkonto). Each shareholder must pay at least 25% of the subscribed nominal amount, but for a one-person GmbH the full contribution must be rendered unless security is furnished. Contributions in kind require a valuation report and full consideration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt), with operational registration and assessment performed by the competent local Tax Office (Finanzamt); electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident GmbH must apply for a German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) through BZSt Form USt 1 TN or the ELSTER portal, disclosing the intended economic activity and any German establishment; a local Steuernummer is then issued by the Finanzamt when a taxable supply begins. Foreign electronic-service providers must complete registration before rendering German payroll SaaS services, and may remain eligible for the OSS procedure for EU B2C supplies without losing the domestic B2B VAT registration obligation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a resident GmbH is a C-corporation for German tax purposes, subject to Körperschaftsteuer, Solidaritätszuschlag and Gewerbesteuer on worldwide income. Tax-transparent classification is unavailable for the GmbH; a fixed place of business or a permanent establishment in Germany extends tax nexus and payroll-compliance substance.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in German law. Available elections include corporate income tax fiscal unity (Organschaft) requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement and financial integration; VAT grouping (§ 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG) treating affiliated entities as one taxable person; trade tax grouping; and anti-hybrid filings under § 4j EStG / § 14 KStG where cross-border hybrid instruments or reverse hybrid entities are present.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 expected current-year turnover for the domestic small-business exception; however, a non-resident GmbH carrying on taxable supplies of payroll SaaS has no registration threshold and must register from the first supply in Germany unless the reverse charge or cross-border OSS regime removes the German supply from the unilateral cascade.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to a third-country destination may be zero-rated under § 6 UStG if the supplier proves the physical export from Germany/EU. For payroll SaaS and offshore services, German VAT is absent where the recipient is an entrepreneur outside the EU (B2B place-of-supply rule under § 3a Abs. 2 UStG) or a non-EU final consumer in the case of electronically supplied services (§ 3a Abs. 3-5 UStG). The supplier must document that the service was supplied to a non-EU business customer with a valid local VAT ID/business registration, and must include a reverse-charge or no-VAT notation; zero-rating applies to the supply value, while input VAT credits are preserved on export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / Ausfuhrbegleitdokument ABD)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing movement to a third country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 bank payment receipt showing foreign-currency settlement
  • Foreign customer VAT ID or business-registration evidence and signed service contract (for SaaS/offshore services)
  • Commercial invoice and detailed software-delivery/access log substantiating place of supply

Mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) from 1 January 2025, with structured data formats conforming to EN 16931 (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, EDI). Germany operates no real-time fiscal clearance, central API stamping, or continuous transaction control; submission to the Finanzamt is document-based via ELSTER. Non-compliant invoices are treated as legally defective and may nullify the customer's input VAT deduction, triggering penalties under the invoice-violation provisions of the UStG.

Finanzamt and customs audit overseers review export proofs and bank receipts. Missing documentation within the evidentiary period invalidates the zero-rating; the GmbH becomes liable to pay 19% output VAT on the gross consideration, with input VAT clawback, late-payment interest under § 233a AO, late-filing surcharges under § 152 AO, negligence penalties under § 378 AO, and criminal investigation for tax evasion under § 370 AO (imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in especially serious cases). The statutory assessment limitation is 7 years for gross negligence and 10 years for intentional evasion, so retrospective clawback can reach historical payroll SaaS exports.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany, as a euro-area member state, maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility pursuant to Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There is no exchange-control approval, no surrender requirement, no prior central-bank authorization, and no restriction on inbound capital, operating cash flows, dividend remittances, or payroll disbursements in freely convertible currencies. Profit remittance by a German GmbH is unrestricted: dividends and certain interest/royalty payments may flow abroad without Bundesbank permission, although German withholding taxes (25% capital gains tax, 5.5% solidarity surcharge, church tax where applicable) arise and may be reduced by tax treaties. The only practical hurdles are non-convertibility: (i) German residents must report to the Deutsche Bundesbank every cross-border payment or claim exceeding EUR 12,500 under AWV §§ 67–68; (ii) FATF Recommendation 16 traveler-rule sender information must be complete on all SWIFT wires; and (iii) the GmbH, as a payroll-SaaS provider, must demonstrate that funds in transit for clients are never commingled with corporate treasury funds, which can otherwise trigger re-characterization as an unlicensed payment institution under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz – ZAG).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline for receipt of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no compulsory surrender or repatriation of export receipts under German or EU law. A German GmbH may lawfully retain foreign-currency export proceeds offshore, hold them in any currency, net them, or use them for onward payroll disbursements without Bundesbank permission. The sole residual obligation is statistical under AWV §§ 67–68: every resident enterprise must report, or ensure its German bank reports to Deutsche Bundesbank, each cross-border payment or receivable connected with exported services (here: international payroll-automation software licensing) that exceeds EUR 12,500 per transaction. The report is due within the statutory reporting period after the value date, and banks acting as reporting agents will apply the standard Bundesbank statistical purpose code if the transfer instruction identifies the underlying service; no central-bank conversion or surrender certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German Central Bank) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht – BaFin) and the Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen – BMF). The operative legal instruments are the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) and the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz – GwG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) that hold a valid German payment-institution passport under the ZAG and that offer a German IBAN; Low for offshore/non-EEA EMIs. Legal constraints: (i) an offshore EMI without an EU/EEA licence cannot directly access SEPA Direct Debit Core or B2B scheme settlement or participate in TARGET2, so payroll automation would require a domestic partner-bank layer that adds counterparty and continuity risk; (ii) under Sec. 3 ZAG, providing payment services or issuing e-money in Germany without authorization is unlawful and BaFin may issue cease-and-desist orders; (iii) EMI client funds are safeguarded under the e-money safeguarding rules, not protected by the statutory German deposit-guarantee scheme (Einlagensicherungsgesetz), a serious reputational issue for any entity that routes employee salaries; (iv) the EMI itself becomes subject to the GmbH's UBO/Transparency Register due diligence, and each cross-border payout above EUR 12,500 requires AWV statistical reporting. Recommended architecture: maintain the primary treasury account with a tier-1 German bank, and use an EEA-passported EMI as a secondary collection/disbursement rail only for selected foreign-currency corridors (GBP, PLN, USD payroll disbursements to non-resident employees).

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for foreign-equity capital injection into a German GmbH: (1) execute the capital increase before a German notary and file for Handelsregister registration; (2) instruct the foreign shareholder to remit via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer over TARGET2 where the sending bank is EEA-based) to the GmbH's dedicated capital account, with Field 70 remittance information specifying 'Stammeinlage / Kapitalerhöhung' and the notary file number; (3) have the German receiving bank book the credit as equity contribution and, where the amount exceeds EUR 12,500, submit the AWV statistical purpose code for inward direct investment in accordance with AWV Sec. 68; (4) provide the bank with the notarial capital-increase certificate so that compliance may release the funds; (5) after registration, transfer the subscribed capital from the escrow account to the operational treasury account only upon board instruction, preserving a full audit trail. Additional verification measures applied by the bank: UBO/Transparency-Register screening of the investing shareholder, FATF Recommendation 16 sender-information checks, and a source-of-funds certificate from the remitting bank where the amount is large relative to the entity's financial statements.Primary clearing rails, in sequence of priority: (a) SEPA Credit Transfers (pain.001.001.03) and SEPA Instant Credit for all EUR-denominated payroll and vendor payments within SEPA, with a cut-off agreed at 16:30 CET, and TIPS-enabled instant payments for urgent wage runs; (b) SEPA Direct Debit B2B (pain.008.003.02) with pre-mandated corporate clients to collect subscription payroll-platform fees on scheduled due dates, with R-message monitoring for returned mandates (RCUR, MD01, etc.); (c) SWIFT MT103/MT202 correspondent-bank transfers for non-EUR salaries and suppliers in the UK, US, and Asia-Pacific, always carrying the correct FX purpose code and AWV statistical report when the transaction exceeds EUR 12,500; (d) documentary Letters of Credit governed by UCP600 for large cross-border software-implementation and payroll-outsourcing contracts requiring bank-undertaking assurances, with the advising bank located in the client country; (e) TARGET2/RTGS same-day settlement for high-value EUR transfers and for the FX leg to eliminate intraday credit risk. Treasury operating note: the German bank account should be configured with a dedicated payroll module so that every disbursement is sub-ledgered by legal entity, all payroll mandates are pre-mapped, and intraday balance reporting is automated; foreign-employee net pay should be aggregated and executed in one daily batch to minimize correspondent costs.Outright FX Forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) under the German Master Agreement (Rahmenvertrag für Finanztermingeschäfte) or ISDA 2002, permitted for EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/PLN, EUR/CHF, and other major deliverables; for a non-financial counterparty below the EMIR clearing threshold, no mandatory proof-of-exposure requirement is imposed, but accounting hedge documentation should support the treasury hedging intent.FX Swaps (spot-forward combinations and swap-point transactions) for rolling foreign-currency funding, covering payroll liquidity in corridors where the German GmbH has both receivables and payables in the same foreign currency.European-style FX Options (vanilla calls and puts) purchased by the GmbH as premium-bearing hedging instruments; permitted without restriction, and do not alter the AWV reporting status of the underlying cross-border payment when used purely for economic hedge cover.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) for long-term multi-currency SaaS implementation contracts involving deferred payroll-service milestones; requires ISDA/CSA master documentation and EMIR margin considerations for the GmbH as a non-financial counterparty if the clearing threshold is exceeded.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are legally permissible for German residents only for currency pairs without an onshore deliverable market (e.g., CNY, INR, THB, IDR); for EUR/USD and EUR/GBP, the deliverable forward is the standard market instrument, and an NDF structure on a convertible EUR pair would introduce unnecessary basis costs without compliance benefit; this instrument class is not recommended for the GmbH's payroll-denominated FX flows.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) that hold a valid German payment-institution passport under the ZAG and that offer a German IBAN; Low for offshore/non-EEA EMIs. Legal constraints: (i) an offshore EMI without an EU/EEA licence cannot directly access SEPA Direct Debit Core or B2B scheme settlement or participate in TARGET2, so payroll automation would require a domestic partner-bank layer that adds counterparty and continuity risk; (ii) under Sec. 3 ZAG, providing payment services or issuing e-money in Germany without authorization is unlawful and BaFin may issue cease-and-desist orders; (iii) EMI client funds are safeguarded under the e-money safeguarding rules, not protected by the statutory German deposit-guarantee scheme (Einlagensicherungsgesetz), a serious reputational issue for any entity that routes employee salaries; (iv) the EMI itself becomes subject to the GmbH's UBO/Transparency Register due diligence, and each cross-border payout above EUR 12,500 requires AWV statistical reporting. Recommended architecture: maintain the primary treasury account with a tier-1 German bank, and use an EEA-passported EMI as a secondary collection/disbursement rail only for selected foreign-currency corridors (GBP, PLN, USD payroll disbursements to non-resident employees).

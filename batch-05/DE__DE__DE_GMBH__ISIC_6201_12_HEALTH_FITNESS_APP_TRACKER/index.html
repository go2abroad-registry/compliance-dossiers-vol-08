Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum registered share capital (Stammkapital) is EUR 25,000. In a single-member GmbH, the full cash amount must be paid in or secured before registration; in a multi-member formation, at least EUR 12,500 (50 percent) of each cash contribution must be contributed before filing. Funds must be deposited into a dedicated business bank account in the name of the company in formation (GmbH i. Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industry operating license is triggered for digital health and fitness telemetry services; general state-level supervision is exercised by the Industrial Inspectorate (Gewerbeaufsichtsamt) of the respective Land for occupational safety, while the Chamber of Industry and Commerce (IHK) assumes consultative and registration-administration functions for the enterprise.

Should the software cross the threshold into medical-device classification under EU Regulation 2017/745 (MDR), a BfArM registration and CE marking would be mandatory as a product-level certification rather than an operating license.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Local Finanzamt at the GmbH's registered seat for corporate income tax, trade tax, and turnover tax (Umsatzsteuer); Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt), Berlin, for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), VIES validation, and Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) administration; Hauptzollamt for export customs matters. Principal digital portals: ELSTER (www.elster.de) for VAT prepayment returns, annual VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and corporate tax filings; BZSt online services (www.bzst.de) for OSS registrations and USt-IdNr. applications.

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH: (1) notarise the articles of association and file for entry in the commercial register (Handelsregister); (2) upon commencing business, submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the locally competent Finanzamt through ELSTER within one month (Sec. 138 AO), disclosing shareholders, managing directors, projected turnover, and anticipated B2B/B2C digital services; (3) receive the Steuernummer (assessment tax number) from the Finanzamt for income tax, trade tax, and VAT purposes; (4) electronically apply to the BZSt under Sec. 27a UStG for the EU-wide USt-IdNr., used on cross-border invoices and in the EC Sales List; (5) where OSS is used for EU B2C digital services, register for the Union OSS scheme via the BZSt portal. Non-resident foreign entities without a German seat file the same questionnaire with the responsible Finanzamt (as a rule Finanzamt Neukölln, Berlin), and non-EU entrepreneurs must additionally appoint an approved fiscal representative (steuerlicher Vertreter) under Sec. 22 UStG and furnish a translated/apostilled certificate of incorporation and proof of foreign tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box regime; a GmbH is mandatorily subject to entity-level taxation. Available statutory elections include: (a) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under Sec. 14 KStG achieved through a control and profit-and-loss pooling agreement, allowing the GmbH's income and net worth to be attributed to a qualifying corporate parent for corporate income tax and trade tax; (b) VAT small-business exemption election under Sec. 19 UStG where turnover remains below EUR 25,000 in the preceding year and EUR 100,000 forecast for the current year (rates applicable from 2025), though commercially inadvisable for a digital app monetising via IAP and Google Play Billing; (c) option under Sec. 9 UStG to waive VAT exemption on real estate transactions, if the GmbH holds property; (d) hybrid mismatch determinations and reverse-hybrid status filings required by Sections 4k and 4m EStG and Sec. 14a KStG for cross-border tax-friction scenarios; (e) EU OSS election for simplified VAT accounting on EU B2C supplies of electronic services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A German-resident GmbH is subject to mandatory domestic VAT registration from the first euro of taxable turnover; the Sec. 19 UStG small-business exemption applies only below the statutory limits of EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 current-year forecast. For B2C digital services supplied by the GmbH to private consumers in other EU Member States, the place of supply is the customer's country and Germany's domestic threshold is irrelevant; the EU-wide OSS scheme is available once intra-Community B2C distance or digital-services turnover exceeds EUR 10,000, otherwise VAT may be accounted for in Germany at the German standard rate. For B2B digital services supplied to EU customers, the reverse charge applies without any German registration threshold. Non-resident digital sellers supplying German private consumers have no de minimis threshold and must register for German VAT from the first euro, either directly with the Finanzamt or through the Non-Union OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% German VAT rate applies to exports of goods physically leaving EU customs territory under Sec. 6 UStG and to certain services linked to exported goods under Secs. 7 and 8 UStG. For a digital health/fitness app tracker distributed as a pure software download there is no physical export and no customs clearance; zero-rating is instead achieved through location-of-supply rules: (1) B2B supply of e-services to a customer established outside the EU is outside the scope of German VAT (Sec. 3a(2) UStG) and economically 0% rated; (2) B2B supply to an EU recipient holding a valid USt-IdNr. is zero-rated in Germany and shifted to the customer by reverse charge, reportable in the EC Sales List; (3) B2C supply to non-EU consumers is outside the scope of German VAT; (4) B2C supply to EU consumers is subject to VAT in the destination Member State through the OSS at that Member State's rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) filed via ATLAS/AES — required only for physical goods; not applicable to pure digital app downloads
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or road consignment note — required only for physical export shipments
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of the foreign consideration within the repatriation period
  • VIES confirmation of the customer's valid EU VAT identification number for intra-Community B2B electronic services
  • Electronic service contract or end-user licence agreement evidencing the place of supply, customer status, and consideration for the app licence under Sec. 3a UStG
  • Customer-location evidence for B2C digital services retained for ten years, comprising billing address, payment account BIC country code, SIM/IMSI network country code, and IP-geolocation data

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or central e-invoice authorisation model comparable to Italy's Sistema di Interscambio or Spain's Suministro Inmediato de Información. The electronic invoicing mandate derives from the Wachstumschancengesetz: from 1 January 2025 all German B2B taxpayers must be able to receive structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 businesses with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; and from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B invoices must be issued as e-invoices. Mandatory conformant formats are XRechnung (CII/UBL) and ZUGFeRD/Factur-X (EN 16931), transmittable by EDI, e-mail, or secure portal; no data exchange with the tax authority occurs at invoice issuance. Recurring filing obligations comprise electronic VAT prepayment returns via ELSTER (monthly or quarterly), the annual VAT return, the EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung) for intra-Community B2B services, and quarterly OSS returns for EU B2C digital services; B2C payments through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing remain outside the cash-register certification obligation under the Kassensicherungsverordnung.

The Finanzamt may recharacterise a claimed export zero-rating, retroactively revoke the 0% rate, and assess 19% German VAT on the full consideration, adding a late-payment surcharge of 1% per commenced month under Sec. 240 AO and post-assessment interest under Sec. 233a AO (generally 1.8% p.a. from 2025). Deficient or false substantiation triggers Sec. 162 AO estimation powers; intentionally or recklessly fraudulent export documentation subjects the GmbH and its managing directors to criminal tax evasion proceedings under Sec. 370 AO (imprisonment up to ten years for aggravated cases) or, for negligent conduct, a fine of up to EUR 50,000 under Sec. 378 AO. In the goods-export context, EU customs law permits post-clearance recovery of unpaid customs debts within ten years plus administrative penalties; for digital services, the operational risk is retrospective conversion of zero-rated income into taxable domestic supply, with interest, surcharges, and criminal exposure where false OSS or export evidence was submitted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full free convertibility of the euro under the EU Treaty and German Foreign Trade and Payments Act. No differentiated control regime applies to current-account versus capital-account transactions; profit remittances and dividend distributions are unrestricted from an FX perspective. The only legally relevant hurdles are (i) corporate income and withholding taxes on dividends, (ii) anti-money laundering documentation, and (iii) sanctions and embargo compliance. The euro is an internally convertible currency for current payments, and no Deutsche Bundesbank exchange-control approval or conversion permit is required. Transactions involving outside-the-EU currencies are conducted at freely negotiated market rates, provided the counterparty is authorized to deal in foreign exchange.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the German State or Deutsche Bundesbank, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export receipts in EUR or any foreign currency may be retained, credited, or used freely. However, under the AWV, cross-border payments, claims, and receivables above EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics within five business days. The reporting includes the foreign transaction code (Z1/Z2/Z5) and may be completed via the bank's electronic reporting interface; non-compliance triggers administrative fines.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank) together with the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for prudential supervision of banks, payment institutions, and foreign trade payment monitoring under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Non-EEA offshore digital EMIs cannot legally hold a German GmbH operating account as a substitute for a licensed bank unless the EMI is itself licensed in Germany or passported into the EEA under PSD2. A UK EMI, for example, no longer benefits from EU passporting post-Brexit and requires either a German branch licence or reliance on its German/Austrian licensed affiliate. Even with a valid PSD2 passport, the EMI may offer payment accounts for operational cash flows but may not provide escrow-like full banking services such as credit, deposits, or FX hedging without a German banking licence. Legal constraints include BaFin oversight, Banking Act (KWG) deposit-taking prohibitions, SAR obligations under the Geldwäschegesetz, and restrictions on commingling corporate treasury funds with customer money. The app-tracker business should therefore use a licensed tier-1 bank as the statutory capital escrow bank and a reputable EMI only for optional disbursement automation or multi-currency settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing follows SWIFT MT103 through Deutsche Bundesbank's payment system (TARGET2/Separated Real-Time Gross Settlement) or SEPA Credit Transfer for euro-denominated injections. The payment must carry ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' for equity capital and, when the payment is from abroad, the German AWV reporting code for capital injection is triggered once the threshold of EUR 12,500 is met. The accounting voucher must evidence share premium, shareholder loan, or 'Darlehen' as applicable. Funds must be credited to the GmbH's designated capital account and held until the Notar and the commercial register confirm the Stammkapital deposit as complete. For shareholder loans, purpose code 'LOAN' and a loan agreement with melting maturity and arms-length interest terms are required. The bank performs sanctions screening and funds-source checks before crediting; any remediation request from the bank must be answered within the statutory deadline to avoid a freeze.The company clears outbound vendor payments, marketing expenditures, and operational conduit disbursements primarily via SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA EUR) and SWIFT MT103 for non-euro jurisdictions. The mobile app monetisation flows from Apple App Store and Google Play are collected via those platforms' net-settlement processes into an Apple-backed German bank account or a SEPA-eligible treasury account; thereafter, royalties and platform commissions are disbursed with the same SEPA/SWIFT rails. For physical-goods merchants in the same cross-border structure, irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) and documentary collections remain permissible for high-value trade settlements; however, for the software/app-IP commercial model, standard MT103 transfers and SEPA Instant are preferred for liquidity. All outgoing wires require a cleartext payment purpose (e.g., commission, marketing service, licensing fee), complete transfer pricing documentation, and, where applicable, AWV reporting if the counterpart is non-EEA and the transaction exceeds EUR 12,500. The German statutory reporting is generally executed by the transacting bank.Outright deliverable FX forwards concluded with a German or EU-licensed credit institution for EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CHF risk arising from app-store net settlements and non-German distribution partnersFX swaps used for temporary liquidity conversion if the company holds multi-currency platform balances in another EEA bank before repatriation to EURCross-currency swaps (MTM swaps) under ISDA/CSA documentation, provided the GmbH maintains a legal opinion and AMA-compliant risk appetite statementNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited to currencies lacking a deliverable market (e.g., emerging-market currencies) and subject to BaFin-compliant confirmations and Deutsche Bundesbank reportingZero-cost collar options or plain-vanilla OTC currency options for hedging budgeted Apple/Google payouts, with mandatory EMIR/MIFID II transaction reporting and margin posting where applicable

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Non-EEA offshore digital EMIs cannot legally hold a German GmbH operating account as a substitute for a licensed bank unless the EMI is itself licensed in Germany or passported into the EEA under PSD2. A UK EMI, for example, no longer benefits from EU passporting post-Brexit and requires either a German branch licence or reliance on its German/Austrian licensed affiliate. Even with a valid PSD2 passport, the EMI may offer payment accounts for operational cash flows but may not provide escrow-like full banking services such as credit, deposits, or FX hedging without a German banking licence. Legal constraints include BaFin oversight, Banking Act (KWG) deposit-taking prohibitions, SAR obligations under the Geldwäschegesetz, and restrictions on commingling corporate treasury funds with customer money. The app-tracker business should therefore use a licensed tier-1 bank as the statutory capital escrow bank and a reputable EMI only for optional disbursement automation or multi-currency settlements.

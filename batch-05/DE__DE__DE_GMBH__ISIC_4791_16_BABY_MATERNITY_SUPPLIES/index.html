Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Local Finanzamt (tax office) for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT administration; Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office, BZSt) for the German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). Primary electronic portal: ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärungen), https://www.elster.de; BZSt VAT ID portal: https://www.bzst.de.

E-government filing portal

A GmbH must be registered with the competent local Finanzamt after incorporation and commencement of business. The company files the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER, providing its commercial-register details, registered office, business activities, expected turnover, expected VAT liability, bank details, beneficial-owner information where requested, and requested accounting period. The Finanzamt issues the German tax number (Steuernummer), generally used for corporate income tax, trade tax and domestic VAT filings. The company separately applies to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer or USt-IdNr.) using the legal name, address, local tax number and commercial-register information. An EU-established GmbH generally uses the USt-IdNr. for intra-EU supplies, acquisitions and cross-border services. A foreign entity without a German fixed establishment applies for German tax registration to the competent Finanzamt for non-resident businesses and may apply directly to the BZSt for a German USt-IdNr.; non-EU applicants should provide certified corporate documents, proof of business activity, authorized-representative details and evidence of taxable German transactions when requested. Separate customs registration, including an Economic Operators Registration and Identification number (EORI), is required for import and export customs activities and is obtained from German Customs, not from the BZSt.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is normally subject to entity-level corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election and no general elective pass-through classification for a GmbH. A qualifying corporate group may elect or establish an Organschaft (tax consolidation) if statutory financial, organizational and profit-transfer requirements are satisfied. For VAT, an eligible small business may use the Kleinunternehmerregelung under Section 19 of the German VAT Act, subject to the applicable turnover limits and notification or non-waiver rules; this is an exemption regime rather than an entity-classification election. Cash-basis VAT accounting may be available upon application under Section 20 of the German VAT Act. No general German hybrid-mismatch election is available; anti-hybrid rules under the German Foreign Tax Act and related provisions apply by operation of law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold below which a taxable business is automatically exempt from VAT registration. The German small-business exemption under Section 19 of the German VAT Act generally applies where prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory qualification rules applicable from 1 January 2025. A business using the exemption generally does not charge VAT and cannot deduct input VAT. For EU cross-border distance sales and specified electronically supplied services to consumers, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold may apply, with taxation in the customer Member State above the threshold or upon voluntary OSS use. The EUR 10,000 threshold is not a general German VAT-registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualifying exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the EU may be VAT-exempt with input-tax recovery under Sections 4(1)(a) and 6 of the German VAT Act where the goods are transported or dispatched to a third country and the supplier can substantiate the export and the supplier-customer relationship through prescribed records. Direct exports by the German supplier require evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory. Indirect exports through a non-resident customer require the statutory conditions for an export delivery, including customer status and export evidence; passenger-export rules have separate documentation requirements. Supplies to another EU Member State may qualify as VAT-exempt intra-Community supplies rather than exports where the customer has a valid VAT ID, the goods move to another Member State and documentary and reporting requirements are met. Services supplied to business customers outside Germany are generally governed by place-of-supply rules and may be outside the scope of German VAT, rather than technically zero-rated; special rules apply to consumer services and electronically supplied services. Export-related input VAT refunds require valid supplier invoices, business-purpose substantiation, customs and transport evidence, and accurate VAT reporting.

  • Electronic export declaration and customs export MRN, including exit confirmation from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms and export transaction
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, airway bill, carrier confirmation, CMR consignment note or equivalent logistics record
  • Proof of delivery or destination outside the EU where required by the export method and circumstances
  • Packing list and product description sufficient to reconcile the goods with the export declaration
  • EORI number and customs representation or power-of-attorney records where an agent is used
  • Accounting records linking the order, invoice, goods movement, export declaration and VAT return
  • Customer and transaction records supporting the applicable export, intra-Community supply or place-of-supply treatment
  • Proof of payment or other commercial evidence where necessary to corroborate the transaction; an inward-remittance certificate is not a generally mandatory German export-VAT document

Germany does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance or transaction-reporting system. Since 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must generally be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions, subject to statutory exclusions. Issuance of structured e-invoices is being phased in: during the transitional period, other invoice formats remain permissible under the applicable statutory conditions; mandatory issuance for larger businesses generally applies from 1 January 2027 where the statutory turnover condition is met, and broad mandatory issuance applies from 1 January 2028. Permitted structured formats include standards such as XRechnung and compatible EN 16931-based formats, including eligible hybrid formats such as ZUGFeRD. No general digital tax stamp or prior fiscal clearance is required for ordinary German VAT invoices. Electronic records must satisfy the German principles for proper management and retention of records and documents in electronic form (GoBD). Federal and certain public-sector B2G transactions may have separate e-invoicing requirements.

Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no general requirement to receive export consideration within 180 days. Export VAT exemption can be denied or clawed back if export, transport, customer, customs or record-keeping requirements are not substantiated. The supplier may become liable for VAT at the applicable rate, together with late-payment interest under the German Fiscal Code, assessment changes, penalties for tax evasion or reckless tax understatement, and correction of VAT returns. Intentional tax evasion may constitute a criminal offence under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code; negligent or reckless conduct may trigger administrative fines and tax penalties. False customs declarations can trigger customs duty and import-VAT reassessments, customs sanctions and, in serious cases, criminal liability. Repeated or serious non-compliance may result in denial of VAT refunds, enhanced audits, liability of responsible officers, and loss of simplified customs or trusted-trader privileges. Documentation must be retained for the applicable German tax and customs retention periods, commonly ten years for accounting records and relevant tax records, subject to statutory category-specific rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU law (Articles 63-66 TFEU) and national law (AWV). There is no exchange control licensing for equity injections, dividends, or trade payments. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject only to corporate/withholding taxes (e.g., Abgeltungsteuer for dividends, Sec. 43 EStG) and any applicable tax treaty. The only procedural requirement beyond regular banking is the mandatory statistical reporting to the Bundesbank for cross-border payments > €12,500 and certain AEOI/FATCA notifications.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds exists. Invoices may be collected in any currency and the proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts both domestically and abroad, subject to AWV §69 notification. For non-EEA receipts, a monthly balance-of-payments report to the Deutsche Bundesbank is required if the cumulated amount exceeds €12,500. No mandatory repatriation timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main); the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) exercises regulatory oversight over banking and payment institutions, while the Generalzolldirektion (German Customs Administration) enforces the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: High for payment collection via regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) that hold a BaFin license or a valid EU/EEA passport under PSD2; however, an EMI cannot operate as a full commercial bank and is not part of the statutory deposit guarantee scheme. Its funds must be safeguarded in a segregated pool, which creates acceptable treasury reliance only for transactional throughput, tax payouts, and global disbursements. For statutory capital injection (Stammeinlage) and share-capital compliance evidence, a mainstream German commercial bank (or a notary escrow account) remains legally preferable. EMIs are viable as ancillary rails for foreign payouts, FX conversion, and vendor settlement, but not as the sole primary banking gateway for the German GmbH.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a German GmbH, inbound equity contributions must be wired to a dedicated capital contribution account (Kapitalkonto) in the company's name at a German credit institution. Use SWIFT MT103 with the beneficiary IBAN/BIC of the GmbH, and include in Field 70 the wording: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / STAMMEINLAGE № [share register number]'. The currency must be EUR or the received amount must be converted so that the final EUR credit equals the committed share capital. A notarized capital formation confirmation (Kapitalaufbringungsbestätigung) is then issued using the bank receipt. For AWV statistical purposes, the purpose code for equity injections is generally under 'Direct Investment / Kapitalbeteiligung' (e.g., K21); the receiving bank automatically reports the transaction to the Bundesbank when the threshold of €12,500 is exceeded.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border supplier payments; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary L/C) under Incoterms 2020 for high-value bulk merchandise; (3) SEPA Credit Transfer for EEA-based suppliers; (4) direct debit (SEPA Core/B2B) for recurring logistics or warehouse fees. All outgoing payments in non-EUR require conversion through the bank's electronic FX platform; for statutory payment reporting to the Bundesbank, the payment purpose must be classified under 'Warenhandel' (goods trade) or 'Übrige Dienstleistungen' for services. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on CIT (0.825% effective) plus Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at municipal rates between 7% and 17%, yielding a combined effective corporation tax burden of approximately 24% to 33% depending on municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% statutory dividend withholding tax under § 43a(1) EStG plus solidarity surcharge of 5.5% on the withholding tax). Domestic corporate shareholders holding ≥10% may claim exemption under § 8b KStG. EU/EEA parent companies satisfying the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (≥10% direct holding for 12 months) may obtain a full 0% withholding rate via § 43b EStG.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The statutory filing deadline is 31 July of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end for electronic returns. Tax advisors commonly receive administrative extensions to 30 September or 31 October upon application. Quarterly estimated corporate income tax prepayments remain due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September, and 10 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is required when the aggregate annual cross-border controlled transaction volume exceeds EUR 5,000,000 per related party. Master File documentation is required when worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000. These thresholds trigger contemporaneous preparation of transfer pricing documentation under § 90 Abs. 3 AO and the German Transfer Pricing Administrative Principles.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the General Anti-Abuse Rule (§ 42 AO) and BEPS MLI anti-abuse clauses require genuine economic substance. A GmbH must maintain an actual registered office, substantive decision-making in Germany, local accounting records, and employment or operating capacity for its e-commerce activities; otherwise, tax authorities may re-characterize transactions, deny treaty benefits, and treat the entity as a transparent shell subject to penalty assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years under § 370 AO; in aggravated cases involving great personal gain or exploitation of authority, 6 months to 10 years imprisonment; additional monetary fines under §§ 40, 41 StGB; payment of unpaid tax plus late payment surcharges and accruing interest; confiscation of goods and digital payment balances.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally criminally liable; courts may impose travel bans to prevent flight risk and issue asset seizures. Under §§ 34, 69 AO, directors are personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they intentionally or grossly negligently breach their tax compliance duties. Civil claims for damages may be brought under § 43 GmbHG.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under § 42 AO, the tax office disregards the arrangement and re-attributes profits to the actual economic owner; operations may be reassessed for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT; the GmbH may be dissolved by court order if the public interest in preserving real business substance is endangered; VAT ID and EU intra-Community registration may be withdrawn under § 18 Abs. 2 UStG.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for re-characterized tax debts due to gross negligence; continuation of trading after insolvency creates liability under § 64 GmbHG; directors can face travel bans and extradition requests if the shell company is used for cross-border tax evasion or asset stripping.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Section 162(4) AO imposes a surcharge of 20% of the additional income adjustment resulting from transfer pricing non-compliance, minimum EUR 5,000 per year and per taxpayer; the tax office is empowered to estimate taxable income in an 'inverse block' manner; late filing interest and penalties of up to EUR 25,000 may be added.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP documentation or fail to disclose related-party transactions face criminal penalties for aiding tax evasion; personal liability for resulting corporate tax deficiencies arises under §§ 69, 34 AO; continued non-compliance may trigger management disqualification under § 6 GmbHG and civil claims by shareholders for misuse of funds.
VAT Fraud / Failure to Register and File VAT

Statutory sanction: Under §§ 370 AO and 26a UStG, imprisonment up to 5 years or fines; VAT liability is reassessed with interest; late filing penalties up to EUR 25,000; late payment surcharges of 1% per month; the e-commerce platform (Stripe/PayPal) may be ordered to pay joint and several VAT under § 22f UStG for marketplace enablers if they unblock trade notifications.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally criminally liable for false VAT declarations; personal assets may be seized to satisfy VAT debts; violation of German VAT compliance obligations can result in an administrative order prohibiting the director from acting in similar commercial roles for up to five years.
AML and Financial Sanctions (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Geldwäschegesetz (§ 20 GwG) of up to EUR 50,000 per infringement for legal persons, and in aggravated cases up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; criminal money-laundering liability under § 261 StGB carries imprisonment from 3 months to 5 years; confiscation of the laundered proceeds and the underlying e-commerce assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to file beneficial ownership declarations face personal fines up to EUR 50,000; directors knowingly processing disguised payments are subject to imprisonment, travel bans, and asset freezing; repeated violations may lead to disqualification as a company director and unlimited civil personal liability to the company and creditors.

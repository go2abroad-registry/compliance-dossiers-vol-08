Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification (USt-IdNr.) and tax identification; local Finanzamt for corporate income tax and trade tax assessment. Main filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH, incorporation with the Handelsregister is followed by submission of the questionnaire on tax registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT. The VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is then applied for separately through BZSt, typically via ELSTER or Form USt 1; foreign-established entities must file under § 27a UStG and provide proof of German taxable activity, foreign tax residence, registered office, valid ID, and bank account details. Non-resident companies may also obtain a domestic VAT number without a permanent establishment where a taxable supply in Germany occurs.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a German GmbH, incorporation with the Handelsregister is followed by submission of the questionnaire on tax registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT. Non-resident companies may also obtain a domestic VAT number without a permanent establishment where a taxable supply in Germany occurs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide an OECD-style check-the-box election. A GmbH is conclusively taxed as a taxpayer under § 1 KStG and § 2 GewStG. Available statutory elections include the creation of Organschaft (tax unity) for corporate income tax and VAT under §§ 14–17 KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, election of consolidation for VAT if the financial, economic, and organisational integration tests are met, and for partnerships (not GmbH) an option under § 1a KStG to be taxed as a corporation. Hybrid mismatch elections and alignment obligations are governed by § 4k EStG and EU ATAD measures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For non-established foreign suppliers: EUR 0 — VAT registration is required from the first taxable supply in Germany. For resident small businesses, the § 19 UStG small-enterprise exemption applies where prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year forecast turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000 (post-2025 annual adjustment). A GmbH engaged in commercial data scraping and API commerce is not eligible for the small-enterprise exemption and is registered without turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating under § 6 UStG applies to exports of movable goods where the goods are dispatched or transported from Germany into a non-EU third country by a qualifying exporter. Services: cross-border B2B services supplied to a non-EU recipient are not subject to German VAT where the place of supply under §§ 3a, 3b, 3e and 3f UStG is located outside Germany; electronic data provision, API gateway services, and software-as-a-service supplied from Germany to foreign recipients are therefore invoiced without German VAT upon proper proof of recipient status and actual use/benefit outside Germany. For B2C digital services to non-EU customers, Germany applies territoriality and non-EU zero-rating where the recipient's jurisdiction is established; documentary evidence of the customer's residence, payment method, IP provenance, and contractual address must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument / ATLAS export declaration or electronic Ausfuhrnachweis with customs confirmation of actual export)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), CMR, or equivalent international shipping document evidencing transport out of the European Union
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) — retained as banking/forex settlement proof in cross-border export documentation packages, though not a German VAT statutory proof
  • Commercial invoice disclosing terms of supply (Incoterms), HS/ECN codes, and explicit reference to the zero-rated export provision
  • Packing list, export gateway release notice, and copy of the export declaration customs export reference number (ARN)
  • If applicable, proof of non-EU recipient's VAT or tax reference registration in the destination country

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping platform. From 1 January 2025, all Germany-established businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices (E-Rechnung) in a CEN EN 16931-compliant structured XML format, such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0. Mandatory issuance of E-Rechnung for B2B supplies begins in 2026 for businesses with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and for all businesses in 2027. Transmission is via EDI or secure Web portals; VAT returns and recapitulative statements are filed electronically through ELSTER, but there is no pre-clearance or mandatory fiscal approval of the invoice itself.

The German tax authorities, assisted by the customs administration (Generalzolldirektion), examine export evidence through regular and risk-based VAT audits. If the exporter fails to provide the required export documentation within the statutory records period, the zero rating is denied and VAT is retrospectively assessed at 19%. Late payment surcharges under § 240 AO accrue at 1% per month, and interest under § 233a AO is charged at 6% per annum on the substantial tax amount. Fraudulent or reckless export zero-rating claims are treated as tax evasion under § 370 AO and are punishable with imprisonment up to five years and fines; the limitation period for prosecution of intentionally committed VAT evasion is ten years. Additional penalty fines up to EUR 5,000 may be imposed under § 26a UStG for failure to comply with invoice and evidence obligations. A retrospective clawback of zero-rated VAT may be made without limitation in cases of forged export documents or sham transactions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has no general exchange controls. The euro is freely convertible for current and capital transactions, subject to EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering rules, bank due diligence, applicable investment-screening rules, and AWV reporting. A German GmbH may generally remit dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, loan repayments, and sale proceeds abroad without prior exchange-control approval, provided corporate-law, tax, withholding-tax, transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, and sanctions requirements are met. Dividends require distributable profits and valid shareholder resolutions; withholding tax and treaty relief or refund procedures may apply. Certain cross-border payments and holdings are reportable to the Bundesbank; reporting is not an approval or remittance restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general statutory deadline requires exporters to repatriate export proceeds to Germany, and no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender of foreign-currency export receipts applies. Receipts may generally be retained in foreign-currency or offshore accounts, subject to applicable reporting, sanctions, tax, contractual, and banking requirements. Under AWV reporting rules, residents generally report qualifying cross-border payments exceeding EUR 50,000 to the Bundesbank, subject to statutory exemptions and transaction-specific rules. Separate reporting applies to certain cross-border holdings and transactions. Confirm current thresholds, exemptions, reporting codes, and filing deadlines with the Bundesbank for each transaction; banks may request purpose and supporting documentation independently of statutory reporting.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany’s central bank and administers balance-of-payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises banks and regulated payment and financial-services providers; it is not the foreign-exchange reporting authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine euro operations if the provider is an appropriately authorised EU bank or electronic-money institution operating in Germany under applicable passporting or local authorisation rules. An EMI account is not a bank deposit and safeguarded funds generally do not have statutory deposit-guarantee protection. Availability of GmbH accounts, capital-payment evidence, cash services, credit, foreign-currency accounts, and SWIFT access varies by provider. Offshore digital EMIs have no automatic right to serve a German GmbH; verify authorisation, German market access, safeguarding arrangements, account-holder name matching, and acceptance by the notary or Commercial Register for share-capital evidence. Crypto-related flows, especially USDC receipts or exchange transfers, may be restricted or declined under provider risk policies and applicable AML rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% comprising 15% corporate income tax (Koerperschaftsteuer) plus the 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritaetszuschlag) levied on the corporate income tax; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is superimposed at a base rate of 3.5% multiplied by the municipal collection rate (Hebesatz, typically 200% to 490%), yielding an aggregate combined corporate income tax plus trade tax burden of approximately 29% to 33% for a resident GmbH in major commercial cities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% on GmbH cash and deemed distributions to shareholders, composed of 25% dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus the 5.5% solidarity surcharge (0.825 percentage points); the distributing GmbH must withhold and remit the tax to the Bundeszentralamt fuer Steuern regardless of the shareholder's residence unless a statutory exemption or treaty reduction applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For financial years ending after 31 December 2022 the standard statutory deadline is four months after the fiscal year-end where the return is not prepared by a professional tax adviser (calendar-year filers: 31 July of the following year) and seven months after the fiscal year-end where the return is prepared and transmitted electronically by a professional tax adviser (calendar-year filers: 28 or 29 February of the second subsequent year). The tax authority may extend the deadline upon application where the taxpayer can demonstrate that timely filing is unreasonable, and application of the 4-month/7-month framework follows section 149(2) AO as amended by the Bureaucracy Relief Act IV.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 6.5 million: threshold of aggregate cross-border related-party transactions of the German entity in a fiscal year triggering the mandatory preparation of the Local File under section 90(3) AO; EUR 100 million: threshold of the global consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent that triggers the Master File obligation; the documentation must be submitted within 60 days of the tax authority's request for direct transaction data and within 30 days for the master data; where usable documentation is absent the tax authority under section 162(4) AO may estimate the profit base with a punitive surcharge of up to 10% of the correction amount, subject to a minimum of EUR 5,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies a functional and factual substance doctrine rather than the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; the relevant pillars are the statutory place of management test (section 10 AO), the general anti-abuse rule (section 42 AO), the specific treaty-shopping limitation in section 50d EStG, and the controlled foreign corporation (CFC) rules under sections 7 to 13 AStG which attribute passive income of a foreign low-taxed company, including licence, data, IP and financial income, to the German shareholder where the foreign effective tax rate is below 25%. A GmbH that lacks personnel, premises, management capacity or locally exercised operational functions in Germany risks re-domestication in the foreign management jurisdiction, loss of treaty benefits, add-back of licence fees under section 4j EStG, and full German taxation of the displaced profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) and false declaration of crypto/gateway income

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under section 370 AO with imprisonment of up to five years, or up to ten years in aggravated cases involving large-scale loss to the tax revenue; monetary fine additionally imposed by the criminal court; forfeiture of the financial advantage obtained; late payment interest of 0.15% per month on the under-declared taxes; complete voluntary disclosure (Selbstanzeige, section 371 AO) may extinguish punishment only if the omitted tax, interest and surcharge are paid before detection and the disclosure is complete.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are jointly and severally liable under sections 34 and 69 AO for taxes not paid from the GmbH's assets where the non-payment was intentional or grossly negligent; criminal conviction of the director entails personal civil restitution of the evaded tax, the imposition of a personal payment order (Haftungsbescheid), border enforcement measures including passport seizure and travel bans by the customs and financial authorities for significant arrears, and imprisonment; German veil-piercing doctrine additionally permits access to personal assets upon commingling of company and private funds.
Transfer pricing documentation failure (Verrechnungspreisdokumentation)

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities are entitled to estimate the taxable income under sections 162(3) and (4) AO with a punitive surcharge of up to 10% of the resulting income correction, minimum EUR 5,000; interest on additional assessments; reversal of the burden of proof against the taxpayer; additional fines for non-compliant data transmission under the regulations on cross-border transaction reporting; denial of deductibility of licence and service fees paid to the non-arm's length counterparties.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the incorrect declarations or failed to provide the statutory documentation face a personal Haftungsbescheid for the assessed corporate income tax and trade tax deficiencies; negligent tax offences under section 378 AO expose the director to fines of up to EUR 250,000; confiscation orders may attach to personal assets; in aggravated cases the director is charged with criminal tax evasion with imprisonment, and travel bans are deployed in the enforcement of the personal tax debt.
Shell company structure and deficient economic substance

Statutory sanction: Re-determination of the statutory place of management under section 10 AO and full re-domestication abroad with loss of German tax status; CFC attribution under sections 7 to 13 AStG of the low-taxed foreign passive income to the German shareholder, with partial credit relief; disallowance of royalty deductions under section 4j EStG; loss of treaty benefits under section 50d EStG; trade tax add-backs of licence and leasing expenses; backward assessments for the entire period of the artificial arrangement; administrative penalties and interest on the resulting deficiencies.

Director personal liability: Directors who designed or operated the shell arrangement are personally liable for the CFC income assessment and for administrative fines; criminal prosecution for evasion of German tax on the shifted foreign income leads to civil restitution and imprisonment; personal Haftungsbescheide are enforced against private bank accounts and property; passport seizure and travel bans apply as coercive measures in the collection of the resulting tax debt.
AML and KWG non-compliance for USDC crypto payment rails

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under section 56 GwG of up to EUR 5 million or, in severe cases, 10% of annual turnover; BaFin cease-and-desist orders and prohibition of the crypto payment operations; criminal penalties under section 54 KWG for unlicensed crypto activities with imprisonment of up to five years and fines; restatement of VAT and corporate income tax assessments on the non-declared digital revenue streams; disqualification orders against the legal entity and its managers.

Director personal liability: Directors act as the statutory AML officers and are personally liable for incomplete or false GwG filings; criminal conviction for unlicensed crypto activities exposes the managing director to imprisonment and personal fines; the director's personal assets are subject to execution for tax, social security and AML fines attributable to his or her duty violations; travel bans and passport confiscation are imposed in serious enforcement cases involving the unpaid penalties.

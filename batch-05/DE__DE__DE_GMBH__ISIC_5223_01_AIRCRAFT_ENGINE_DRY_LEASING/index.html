Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent local Amtsgericht acting as Handelsregister court for the company's registered office, with filing submitted electronically by the German notary.

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG), particularly sections 2-5a (formation, articles and share capital), sections 6-8 (managing directors and registration application), sections 10-11 (registration effects), sections 35-39 (management and representation), and section 40 (shareholder list). Commercial Register procedures are governed by the Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB), particularly sections 8-12, and court fees by the Gerichts- und Notarkostengesetz (GNotKG).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized application for registration in the Handelsregister (Anmeldung zum Handelsregister), accompanied by the notarized articles of association, managing-director declarations, shareholder list, and formation documents.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 68 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The GmbH has minimum stated share capital of EUR 25,000 under GmbHG section 5. Before registration, at least EUR 12,500 must be available to the company, and each cash share must be paid in at least 25 percent. Contributions in kind require full contribution before registration and a prescribed formation report and valuation documentation. There is no mandatory escrow account, but a dedicated company account is customary and the managing directors must confirm that the required contributions are freely available.

Funding & unlock path: For ordinary office premises, obtain any required building-use approval and comply with local fire-protection requirements. Facilities storing engines, fuel, oils, batteries, chemicals, or heavy components may require building-authority approval, fire-safety documentation, hazardous-material risk assessment, structural load verification, spill containment, and potentially an approval under the Bundes-Immissionsschutzgesetz. Workplace compliance is supervised through the competent Arbeitsschutz authority and the relevant Berufsgenossenschaft; maintain risk assessments, emergency plans, occupational instructions, inspections, and employee training.

Paid-in capital rule: The GmbH has minimum stated share capital of EUR 25,000 under GmbHG section 5. Before registration, at least EUR 12,500 must be available to the company, and each cash share must be paid in at least 25 percent. Contributions in kind require full contribution before registration and a prescribed formation report and valuation documentation. There is no mandatory escrow account, but a dedicated company account is customary and the managing directors must confirm that the required contributions are freely available.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft

The registered office and business address at [address], Germany, are adopted. Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No sector-specific industrial licensing board is ordinarily required for a German GmbH whose activity is limited to ownership and dry leasing of aircraft engines. The local Gewerbeamt handles trade registration. The Luftfahrt-Bundesamt may be relevant for aviation regulatory matters, and BAFA may be relevant for export-control licensing.

Category: No general manufacturing or operating permit is required solely for aircraft-engine dry leasing. Separate permits may apply to any maintenance, repair, overhaul, storage, hazardous-material handling, import, export, or controlled-technology activity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers, turnover tax statistics, and central certification; local tax office (Finanzamt) with territorial jurisdiction over the registered seat of the GmbH for corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT returns, and payroll tax; electronic filing portal: ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) at www.elster.de

E-government filing portal

Upon commercial registration of the GmbH, the managing director must file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER with the competent local Finanzamt within one month of business commencement; the Finanzamt then assigns the corporate tax number (Steuernummer) used for all CIT, trade tax, VAT, and payroll filings. For a separate EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), the GmbH must submit an application to the BZSt (form USt 1 TG or online via BZSt's BOP portal), providing copies of the commercial register excerpt, the Steuernummer notification, and a banking reference; the BZSt issues the VAT ID within approximately 3 to 6 weeks, subject to verification of business existence and place of supply. A foreign entity without a permanent establishment must register for VAT through the direct VAT registration procedure (Form USt 1 TG) with the competent local Finanzamt for input tax recovery or for supplies taxable in Germany, or use the no-registration SIM-UST procedure for qualifying intra-Community transactions. The GmbH must maintain a valid EU VAT number as a precondition for zero-rated intra-Community supplies under §6a UStG and for the conclusive-evidence requirements of the EU-wide VAT identification validation system (VIES).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the German GmbH is a domestic corporate legal entity with unlimited tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) for worldwide income under §1 KStG, subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15.0% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) on the CIT liability and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a standard collection rate ranging from approximately 7.0% to 17.15% depending on the municipality's assessment rate (Hebesatz); the GmbH is also a separate VAT taxable person (Unternehmer) under §2 UStG; no tax-exempt zone regime applies; income derived from worldwide aviation asset leasing is fully within the German territorial tax net with foreign tax credit relief under applicable tax treaties; permanent establishments of a foreign sister company would be the alternative classification only if no German entity is inserted, which is not the case for DE_GMBH — Upon commercial registration of the GmbH, the managing director must file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER with the competent local Finanzamt within one month of business commencement; the Finanzamt then assigns the corporate tax number (Steuernummer) used for all CIT, trade tax, VAT, and payroll filings. For a separate EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), the GmbH must submit an application to the BZSt (form USt 1 TG or online via BZSt's BOP portal), providing copies of the commercial register excerpt, the Steuernummer notification, and a banking reference; the BZSt issues the VAT ID within approximately 3 to 6 weeks, subject to verification of business existence and place of supply. A foreign entity without a permanent establishment must register for VAT through the direct VAT registration procedure (Form USt 1 TG) with the competent local Finanzamt for input tax recovery or for supplies taxable in Germany, or use the no-registration SIM-UST procedure for qualifying intra-Community transactions. The GmbH must maintain a valid EU VAT number as a precondition for zero-rated intra-Community supplies under §6a UStG and for the conclusive-evidence requirements of the EU-wide VAT identification validation system (VIES).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the German GmbH is a domestic corporate legal entity with unlimited tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) for worldwide income under §1 KStG, subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15.0% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) on the CIT liability and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a standard collection rate ranging from approximately 7.0% to 17.15% depending on the municipality's assessment rate (Hebesatz); the GmbH is also a separate VAT taxable person (Unternehmer) under §2 UStG; no tax-exempt zone regime applies; income derived from worldwide aviation asset leasing is fully within the German territorial tax net with foreign tax credit relief under applicable tax treaties; permanent establishments of a foreign sister company would be the alternative classification only if no German entity is inserted, which is not the case for DE_GMBH

Available election forms

German law does not adopt US-style check-the-box classification; the GmbH is by default a corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) tax subject. Relevant elections include: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) election under §§14 to 17 KStG and §§2, 4 GewStG, whereby a wholly-owned or majority-owned German subsidiary may conclude a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) with its German corporate parent, enabling income and VAT consolidation and the elimination of intercompany transactions; (ii) the option under §4k EStG to align netting and tax-treatment of hybrid instruments/branches under the ATAD II hybrid-mismatch rules, with mandatory look-through rules for deduction/non-inclusion mismatches; (iii) the one-time election to capitalize start-up expenses (Anlaufkosten) or to spread them over time for trade tax purposes; (iv) for thin-capitalization or loss-use purposes, the carry-forward and loss-averaging elections under §10d EStG and §8a KStG are restricted by the minimum-tax loss offset limitation; (v) election under §21 UStG to pay VAT on a cash-receipts basis is generally unavailable to a GmbH for leasing activities because the company customarily issues invoices; (vi) joint VAT grouping (Organschaft) election extends to VAT under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, creating a single taxable person for VAT, which is decisive for leasing hubs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The small-business exemption under §19 UStG applies only up to €22,000 of net turnover in the preceding calendar year and estimated €25,000 or less in the current calendar year (transition to €25,000 statutory threshold under the VAT small-business directive, Directive (EU) 2020/285, transposition effective in German law from 1 January 2025 with a binding €25,000 threshold). The DE_GMBH leasing aircraft engines at commercial rentals will exceed this threshold immediately, making mandatory VAT registration and regular advance VAT returns (monthly/quarterly) unavoidable. For intra-Community acquisitions in Germany by a non-registered company, a separate €12,500 registration threshold applies, but it is irrelevant to the GmbH's own registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports to third-country destinations (outside the EU/Union customs territory) may be zero-rated (0% VAT) under §4 Nr. 1a, §6 UStG provided the aircraft engine or spare part is physically transported from Germany to a non-EU country and the exporter holds the full export evidence required by §§8 to 12 UStDV, including the customs-validated export declaration in ATLAS and proof of the destination in a third country. Intra-Community supplies to an EU-based lessee are tax-free (0% VAT) under §4 Nr. 1b, §6a UStG only if the lessee's valid German/USt-IdNr. is obtained, the engine is dispatched to another EU Member State, and the GmbH files a correct and timely EC Sales List declaring the transfer; a passive subcontractor model does not qualify if the lessee cannot confirm receipt via the USt-IdNr. validation. For dry-leasing services (aircraft engine lease is a service, not a supply of goods for VAT purposes, because the engine remains property of the leasing GmbH), the place-of-supply rule for B2B transactions under §3a(2) UStG places the transaction where the customer is established; if the customer is a third-country airline, no German VAT arises; if the customer is established in the EU, German VAT does not arise and the customer applies the reverse charge. Zero-rating for related services (e.g., offshore services physically performed on the engine, line-repair transfers) may be available under §4 Nr. 2 and §8 UStG when the services are supplied directly to goods destined for export or to exported engines, provided the Germany-based supplier effects the services to a customer outside the Union and the documentation shows a direct physical nexus to the exported asset. The DE_GMBH must obtain and archive the customer's VAT ID validation printout, the signed leasing contract, the fleet agreement, and the aircraft registration certificate to evidence the place-of-supply analysis.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) accepted and validated in the ATLAS/AES system with assignment of the export customs declaration number; mandatory for third-country shipments
  • Bill of Lading (Seeweg) or Air Waybill (Luftfrachtbrief) evidencing transport to a third-country destination and naming the German exporter as shipper/consignor or as the leasing entity owning the asset at momentary export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/escrow payment confirmation demonstrating that the lease rentals and any export-related consideration are received into the German bank account of DE_GMBH within the statutory or contractual repayment period
  • Commercial invoice in English/German with a clear VAT-neutral phrase (e.g., 'Export Lieferung steuerfrei', 'Zero-rated export supply') and reference to the contract with the lessee, engine serial number (ESN), and the value declaration
  • Proof of transport from the German place of departure to the third country, including the transit document (CMR, Bill of Lading, or AWB) in combination with the official exit confirmation from the customs office (Abgangsbestätigung/Ausfuhrbescheinigung)
  • Lessee VAT ID validation report for intra-Community supplies (Zusammenfassende Meldung reference), if the transaction is a dispatch to an EU Member State under §6a UStG, including a signed extra-union contractual clause when the lessee is a non-EU airline
  • Engine leasing contract, delivery notes, and daily aircraft utilisation report in case of cross-border in-service transfers, to substantiate that the goods were not used in Germany after dispatch

Germany does not apply real-time fiscal clearance, internal digital stamping, or an API-based continuous transaction control regime analogous to Italian SDI or Polish KSeF. The statutory electronic invoicing obligation arises under §14 UStG as amended by the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): since 1 January 2025, every German taxable person must be able to receive structured e-invoices in a European standard format (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, or the EU CEN EN 16931 XML schema) for B2B domestic supplies; from 1 January 2027, businesses with more than €800,000 annual VAT turnover must issue e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies; from 1 January 2028, all businesses must issue e-invoices. VAT returns, advance VAT returns, EC Sales Lists (Zusammenfassende Meldung), and annual VAT declarations are filed electronically via ELSTER or certified commercial software, with a mandatory use of the ELSTER-compatible interface. For exports and leasing placements, the customs declaration interface is ATLAS (the German customs IT system), which requires AES (Automated Export System) data exchange; there is no separate digital stamping but the export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) must be transmitted and retained. The BZSt operates the VIES validation and the intra-Community tripartite data exchange; the EU ViDA single-ATAD reform has not yet abolished the national filing infrastructure.

German tax authorities maintain a specialized export-verification desk (Kleine Lohnsteuer- und Exportprüfung) which systematically audits export zero-rated transactions coupled with capital-export and financial-arrangement indicators; annual VAT audits (Außenprüfung) under §§193 to 207 AO may reopen prior years and issue VAT assessment amendments (geänderter Steuerbescheid) if the export evidence is incomplete; a shortage of the mandatory documentation referred to above leads to the annulment of zero-rating, retrospective VAT assessment at 19%, and denial of the corresponding input VAT deduction with accrual of interest at 6% per annum under §233a AO, plus a late-payment surcharge (§240 AO) of 1% per month on the assessed VAT; in cases of abusive planning or false evidence, the criminal tax evasion offence under §370 AO applies, carry imprisonment of up to five years or a fine, and when the tax authority has already issued a VAT assessment based on fraudulent data, the criminal penalty is aggravated to one to ten years under §370(2) AO for particularly serious cases such as commercial-scale falsification of export documents and use of fictitious inward remittance certificates; administrative fines under §378 AO (light-weighted tax evasion of input tax) and §26a UStG (violation of invoice duties) of up to €5,000 per violation may be additionally imposed; the BZSt may issue a fraudulent-exporter ranking (as part of the German Collective Investment/Aviation leasing enforcement framework) leading to the refusal of future VAT-ID validation for the GmbH and notification of other EU authorities through the Eurofisc network, triggering cross-border mutual assistance requests under the EU Regulation 904/2010.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the EU free movement of capital framework (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Articles 63–66). No general restrictions on profit remittance, dividends, or loan repayments to non-residents. However, statistical reporting obligations apply for cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 (AWV §67). Profit remittance is subject to standard corporate income tax (15% plus solidarity surcharge and trade tax) and, if applicable, withholding tax under the German-Swiss or other DTT, but no FX conversion hurdles exist. The Euro is a fully convertible currency; no surrender requirements or approval for conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. All export receipts in foreign currency may be retained, used, or converted freely. However, the exporter must report the receipt to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes within seven days of the transaction if the amount exceeds €12,500 (AWV §67). For aircraft engine dry leasing, lease rental payments are classified as service exports (ISIC 5223) and are subject to the same reporting threshold. No minimum conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking oversight; the Bundesbank administers foreign exchange reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Medium. German BaFin-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) can provide multi-currency IBANs and cross-border payment services, but they are not licensed to take deposits or provide credit. For a capital-intensive aircraft engine leasing entity, a full commercial bank relationship is mandatory for escrow, LC issuance, and trade finance. Offshore EMIs (e.g., UK, Lithuania, or Singapore) are generally not accepted by German counterparties for high-value aviation transactions due to AML/CFT concerns and lack of direct access to the German clearing system (SEPA and TARGET2). Legal constraints: EMIs cannot hold client funds as deposits; they must safeguard funds in segregated accounts. For lease payments exceeding €50,000 per transaction, a full bank is preferred.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: The initial shareholder loan or equity contribution must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent company's bank to the GmbH's capital escrow account at a German bank. The payment must include a clear purpose code (e.g., 'Equity Injection' or 'Shareholder Loan') and reference the notarized share purchase agreement or loan agreement. For equity, the GmbH must file a registration with the commercial register (Handelsregister) and the Bundesbank for statistical purposes (AWV §67). The bank will verify the source of funds and may require a copy of the notarized deed and the shareholder resolution. For loans, a formal loan agreement with interest rate and repayment schedule is required. The funds must be received in EUR or converted via a licensed FX provider; no approval is needed for conversion.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct lease rental payments to the lessor or lessee, with purpose code 'Lease Payments' (e.g., 'Operating Lease Rent' under ISIC 5223). (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) for security deposits or performance guarantees, issued by the German bank via SWIFT MT700. (3) For engine maintenance or spare parts purchases, use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for interbank transfers. (4) For high-value transactions (above €50,000), TARGET2 is the preferred real-time gross settlement (RTGS) system for EUR payments. For USD payments, use the Fedwire or CHIPS network via correspondent banks. All outbound payments must comply with the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and AWV reporting thresholds.Outright FX Forwards (deliverable) with a German bank or EU-authorized credit institution, for hedging lease receivables in USD or other non-EUR currencies.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for currencies with limited convertibility (e.g., certain emerging market currencies) if the lessee is in a restricted jurisdiction, but only via a licensed bank and subject to BaFin's derivative rules.Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) to swap fixed or floating interest rate and principal in EUR against USD, used for long-term lease financing or asset acquisition.FX Options (European style) for hedging contingent lease payments, subject to the German Securities Trading Act (WpHG) and EMIR reporting obligations.Interest Rate Swaps (IRS) to hedge floating-rate lease payments, though not strictly FX, they are permissible under the same derivative framework.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low to Medium. German BaFin-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) can provide multi-currency IBANs and cross-border payment services, but they are not licensed to take deposits or provide credit. For a capital-intensive aircraft engine leasing entity, a full commercial bank relationship is mandatory for escrow, LC issuance, and trade finance. Offshore EMIs (e.g., UK, Lithuania, or Singapore) are generally not accepted by German counterparties for high-value aviation transactions due to AML/CFT concerns and lack of direct access to the German clearing system (SEPA and TARGET2). Legal constraints: EMIs cannot hold client funds as deposits; they must safeguard funds in segregated accounts. For lease payments exceeding €50,000 per transaction, a full bank is preferred.

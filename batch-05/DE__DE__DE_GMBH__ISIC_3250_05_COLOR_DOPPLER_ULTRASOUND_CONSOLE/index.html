Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory minimum share capital is EUR 25,000. At the time of registration, at least one quarter of each cash share and, in aggregate, no less than EUR 12,500 must be paid in cash and must be at the free disposal of the managing directors. Contributions in kind must be fully paid and covered by a notarial valuation report. A single-member GmbH must provide acceptable security for any unpaid part of the cash contribution, or alternatively pay in the full minimum cash contribution, before registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety clearance (Brandschutzabnahme) with approved evacuation routes and extinguishing systems; structural load inspection (Standsicherheitsnachweis) issued by the local building authority; occupational health and safety clearance by the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt under the Workplace Ordinance (ArbStättV) and the Occupational Safety Act (ArbSchG), including electrical installation verification and manufacturer's CE conformity documentation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent local Finanzamt administers corporate income tax, trade tax, and domestic VAT. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and administers certain cross-border VAT procedures. Tax registration and electronic filings are generally handled through the relevant Finanzamt and ELSTER portal.

E-government filing portal

After formation and commercial-register entry, submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically through ELSTER to the Finanzamt responsible for the GmbH's registered office or place of management. The Finanzamt assigns a domestic tax number (Steuernummer). Request a VAT identification number from the BZSt, generally through the registration process or separately after obtaining the tax number. A foreign-established entity making taxable supplies in Germany must register with the competent Finanzamt; it does not receive a general turnover-based registration exemption. Provide incorporation and register documents, German business and contact details, directors' information, activities, expected turnover, bank details, and evidence of taxable transactions as requested. Non-EU businesses may be required to appoint a German fiscal representative in specified circumstances.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — After formation and commercial-register entry, submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically through ELSTER to the Finanzamt responsible for the GmbH's registered office or place of management. The Finanzamt assigns a domestic tax number (Steuernummer). Request a VAT identification number from the BZSt, generally through the registration process or separately after obtaining the tax number. A foreign-established entity making taxable supplies in Germany must register with the competent Finanzamt; it does not receive a general turnover-based registration exemption. Provide incorporation and register documents, German business and contact details, directors' information, activities, expected turnover, bank details, and evidence of taxable transactions as requested. Non-EU businesses may be required to appoint a German fiscal representative in specified circumstances.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is a separate taxable corporation and has no US-style check-the-box election to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity. It is generally subject to corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax. German law provides no general hybrid-mismatch election that changes the GmbH's domestic entity classification; cross-border hybrid outcomes are addressed by applicable anti-hybrid rules, including ATAD implementation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for VAT registration by a GmbH making taxable supplies. The small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) is generally available to qualifying businesses with prior-year turnover not exceeding EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover not exceeding EUR 100,000; it is an exemption regime, not a general registration threshold, and does not ordinarily permit input VAT deduction. Special rules apply to non-established businesses and cross-border transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the EU may be zero-rated as VAT-exempt exports with input tax credit under Sections 4(1)(a) and 6 of the German VAT Act, provided the statutory export and evidence conditions are met. The supplier must retain proof that the goods left the EU and evidence linking the export to the supply. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: treatment depends principally on place-of-supply rules, customer status, and the specific service; many B2B services supplied to non-EU business customers are outside German VAT rather than zero-rated exports. Medical-device regulatory requirements do not determine VAT export treatment.

  • Customs export declaration and export accompanying document, with MRN and customs exit confirmation where applicable
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract or order, and records identifying the goods and their destination
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the shipment, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR, or carrier confirmation
  • Evidence supporting the supplier's export exemption and the link between the supply and the goods leaving the EU
  • Payment or bank records where relevant to substantiate the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a general German statutory export-VAT requirement

Germany does not operate a general real-time VAT invoice clearance or digital-stamping system. Since 1 January 2025, domestic B2B businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices compliant with the statutory format requirements. Issuance is subject to transitional arrangements: generally, paper or other invoices may continue for 2025 and 2026, with qualifying small issuers able to use the transition through 2027; from 2028, structured e-invoicing is generally required for domestic B2B transactions, subject to statutory exceptions. No general VAT API pre-clearance is required.

Germany imposes no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated. Inadequate or inconsistent export evidence can result in denial or reversal of the exemption, assessment of VAT at the applicable rate, interest, and correction obligations. Intentional or reckless false VAT declarations may trigger tax-evasion or tax-reduction proceedings, fines, or criminal penalties under the German Fiscal Code. Input VAT refunds may be withheld or reclaimed following audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for ordinary current-account transactions. Germany has no general restriction on payments for exports, imports, services, dividends, interest, royalties, or other current-account items. Capital-account transactions, shareholder funding, intercompany loans, acquisitions, and profit distributions are generally permitted, subject to corporate-law requirements, AML controls, sanctions, tax rules, transfer-pricing documentation, withholding-tax compliance, beneficial-ownership transparency, and statistical reporting. Dividend distributions by a GmbH require distributable retained earnings, approved annual accounts, and a shareholder resolution; German dividend withholding tax is generally 25% plus solidarity surcharge, subject to treaty relief, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions, or other statutory exemptions. Banks may require evidence of source of funds, commercial purpose, invoices, corporate approvals, and tax documentation before executing cross-border payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated, deposited with a German bank, or converted into euros, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts or offshore accounts, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, AML, and contractual controls. German residents may have AWV statistical reporting obligations for qualifying cross-border payments and receivables; the applicable threshold and exemptions must be verified against the version of the AWV and Bundesbank reporting instructions in force on the transaction date. AWV reports are statistical and do not constitute approval or a currency-conversion requirement. Separate export-control, customs, sanctions, dual-use, medical-device, and trade-documentation rules may apply to ultrasound equipment.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany's central bank and administers foreign-exchange statistical reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises banks and payment institutions. The Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) administers relevant export-control licensing and sanctions functions. Germany has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing currency-conversion controls or export-proceeds surrender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplementary payments and treasury operations, subject to the provider's authorization, passporting status, supported currencies, country-risk policy, safeguarding model, and transaction limits. An offshore digital EMI cannot replace a German bank where a formation-stage capital-payment account, local bank confirmation, regulated credit facility, cash-management service, or bank-controlled trade-finance product is required. Payment institutions and e-money institutions may provide payment accounts and transfers but generally do not provide deposit-taking, credit, or all bank-account functions. The GmbH must verify the provider in the relevant EEA regulatory register, complete corporate and beneficial-owner verification, maintain documentary evidence for source and purpose of funds, and assess PSD2, AMLD, sanctions, GDPR, outsourcing, safeguarding, and German tax-reporting implications.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder equity through the GmbH's designated German bank account, preferably in euros, from an account held in the shareholder's legal name. Screen all parties, banks, jurisdictions, and payment instructions for EU and German sanctions. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Koerperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) on taxable income; plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (0.825% effective) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) typically 14-17%, producing a combined effective statutory burden of approximately 30-32% in major German municipalities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory rate: 25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 26.375% aggregate on gross dividends. Reduced to 0% for dividends to qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% under Section 43b EStG implementing the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU. Treaty rates for non-EU shareholders commonly 5% (minimum 10% holding) or 15% (portfolio): e.g., 5% under DTA Germany-USA and Germany-Japan; 10% under certain non-EU DTAs. Individual shareholders may claim treaty rates only with full beneficial-ownership proof; Section 50d EStG contains anti-treaty-shopping restrictions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return must be filed by 31 July of the following calendar year; where prepared by a German tax advisor (Steuerberater), the deadline is automatically extended to 28/29 February of the second following calendar year (e.g., FY 2024 return becomes due 28 February 2026). Non-calendar-year accounts shift the base date to 31 July of the calendar year following the fiscal year end, with the same advisor extension.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same deadline regime as the corporate income tax return: 31 July of the following year, extended to 28/29 February of the second following year when prepared by a tax advisor.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Advance returns due by the 10th day of the following calendar month (or 10th of the month after the quarter for quarterly filers); annual VAT return due 31 July of the following year, extended to 28/29 February of the second following year with tax advisor assistance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation (Aufzeichnungen) under Section 90(3) AO and GAufzV is mandatory when German cross-border related-party transaction value exceeds EUR 5,000,000 per annum for goods or EUR 500,000 per annum for services; a Master File is required where the group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting under Section 138a AO applies at the same EUR 750,000,000 threshold with notification duties to the federal tax office (BZSt). Failure to produce documentation triggers a penalty of 5% of the resulting profit adjustment, minimum EUR 5,000 and maximum EUR 1,000,000 per category of transaction; non-filing of CbCR carries penalties up to EUR 10,000; evidentiary burden shifts to the taxpayer, reversing the normal burden of proof.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; instead it applies a strict substance-over-form doctrine under Section 41-42 AO, the anti-abuse rule for inappropriate legal structures, and the OECD/G20 BEPS Action 6 principal-purpose test embedded in DTA application. A GmbH manufacturing and exporting ultrasound consoles must demonstrate real operational substance: premises, qualified headcount, engineering and R&D decisions, inventory ownership, substantial risk-bearing, and execution of core distribution functions in Germany. A letterbox entity or an IP migration structure solely aimed at shifting profits offshore will be recharacterised: income will be imputed to the German entity, the DTA relief will be denied, and the directors will be exposed to criminal and civil liability for evasion.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years under Section 370 AO; aggravated cases (large-scale evasion, commercial banditry, use of shell entities) carrying 6 months to 10 years under Section 370(3) AO; fines under the StGB; automatic full assessment of evaded tax plus late-payment interest of 0.15% per month; confiscation and disgorgement of profits under Section 73 StGB; a 10-year limitation period applies.

Director personal liability: The Geschaeftsfuehrer is directly criminally liable; courts impose travel bans and surrender of passports, asset freezes and seizures; civil restitution of evaded tax and damages jointly and severally with the company under Section 43 GmbHG; professional disqualification from managing companies; extradition risk for export-related offences.
Grossly Negligent Tax Shortfall (Leichtfertige Steuerverkuerzung)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine (Bußgeld) up to EUR 50,000 under Section 378 AO; additional tax assessments, interest, and penalties; publication of the sanction in commercial cases; for corporations the fine is imposed on the company while the director remains the responsible natural person.

Director personal liability: Personal fine up to EUR 50,000; no imprisonment but liability for damages under Section 43 GmbHG for breach of duty; exclusion from the Management Board; supervisory board liability under Section 130 OWiG for inadequate internal control systems.
Non-Remittance of Payroll, VAT and Trade Tax (Gesellschaftsbezogene Haftung)

Statutory sanction: Civil liability of the managing director for the full unremitted tax amount (including the 1% monthly Saumniszuschlag and interest); criminal liability for misappropriation of wages and social security contributions under Section 266a StGB with imprisonment up to 5 years; VAT evasion under Section 26c UStG; fines imposed directly on the director.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil: the director is personally liable in full, no discharge in insolvency; arrest and travel bans in enforcement proceedings; criminal conviction commonly leads to director disqualification (Gewerbeuntersagung) and imprisonment; joint liability with any de facto director or shadow director.
Shell Company, AML and Benefit Abuse (Briefkastengesellschaft, GwG, Section 42 AO)

Statutory sanction: Section 42 AO recharacterisation of the structure and denial of tax benefits; fines under Section 56 GwG up to EUR 150,000 for negligent breaches and up to EUR 1,000,000 for intentional breaches of anti-money-laundering obligations; money laundering under Section 261 StGB carries imprisonment of 1-5 years (up to 10 years for commercial/band involvement); asset confiscation and forfeiture under Section 73 StGB.

Director personal liability: Directors face imprisonment and fines for AML violations, travel bans, and asset seizure; they are personally liable for the tax shortfalls caused by the aborted structure, and the corporate veil is pierced in cases of deliberate abuse; disqualification from future directorship; civil claims from creditors and co-shareholders for damages under Section 43 GmbHG and Section 826 BGB.

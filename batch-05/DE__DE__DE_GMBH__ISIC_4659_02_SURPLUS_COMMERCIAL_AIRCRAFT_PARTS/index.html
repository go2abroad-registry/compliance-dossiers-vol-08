Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full and unconditional convertibility on both current and capital account, anchored in EU primary law (Articles 63-66 TFEU) and the Eurosystem's monetary policy framework. The euro is freely convertible at ECB reference rates; no foreign-exchange licensing, no Bundesbank approval for capital transfers, and no profit-remittance quota applies to a German GmbH. The practical gating mechanisms are corporate and tax law rather than FX control. Profit remittance hurdles: (i) under Section 30 GmbHG, a distribution is lawful only to the extent that the GmbH's net assets after the distribution exceed the stated share capital (Unterbilanz test), requiring a balance-sheet proof of distributable surplus; (ii) dividends paid to foreign shareholders are subject to German dividend withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax, producing an aggregate rate of 26.375%, reducible under an applicable double-tax treaty upon certification of beneficial ownership; (iii) cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 are reportable to the Bundesbank within five working days for balance-of-payments statistics, but are not subject to permission; and (iv) external credit relations above EUR 5,000,000 outstanding require monthly Bundesbank credit-statistics reporting. For the aviation-spares trading scope, export proceeds are freely receivable and reusable in EUR or any foreign currency, and the only continuing FX-compliance layers are AWV payment reporting, HGB Section 256a balance-sheet foreign-currency translation, and bank-side sanctions and GwG verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio under German law. Export proceeds in EUR, USD, or any other currency may be retained indefinitely in freely chosen domestic or foreign accounts, converted at market rates, or applied to import settlements; there are no central-bank surrender deadlines. The functional equivalent of a surrender rule is the cross-border payment reporting obligation under AWV Section 67: a German resident must report to the Deutsche Bundesbank, within five working days of the value date, any payment received from a non-resident or paid to a non-resident whenever the individual payment equals or exceeds EUR 12,500 (or the euro equivalent at the ECB reference rate). The report must state the payer or beneficiary, the payment purpose category, the invoice or contract reference, and the counterparty country. In addition, for financial-statement purposes, HGB Section 256a requires that outstanding USD/GBP/CHF receivables and payables be translated at the ECB closing spot rate at the balance-sheet date, regardless of the currency in which the proceeds were retained.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank of Germany, Frankfurt am Main) acting jointly with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) as the policy authority, the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) as the AML/CFT and banking supervisor, and the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) as the export-control licensing authority. The legal basis is the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Bundesbank administers the external-sector payment statistics and cross-border credit statistics; there is no separate German currency-control commission.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low as the primary clearing rail; High as a supplementary collection rail. A BaFin-licensed German electronic-money institution (EMI under the Payment Services Supervision Act, ZAG) or an EEA-passported EMI may legally open a business account remotely for a GmbH and execute SEPA and SWIFT-based foreign-exchange conversions. The decisive legal constraints are: (i) an EMI cannot grant credit, issue documentary letters of credit, handle cash-against-documents collections, or provide bank payment undertakings; (ii) EMI client funds are safeguarded in pooled omnibus accounts rather than in segregated client accounts, which weakens the escrow-grade traceability that aviation counterparties expect; (iii) most EMIs apply internal sector policies that decline or escalate transactions referencing surplus aircraft parts because of dual-use and aviation-disruption risk; (iv) USD settlement above USD 250,000 is contingent on the EMI's US correspondent arrangement and may fail the beneficiary bank's enhanced due diligence; and (v) a German payment institution must halt any transaction that triggers an unresolved suspicious-activity report under Sections 43-46 GwG. Recommendation: permit an EMI only for low-value pre-payment collections below EUR 50,000 per transaction, with the entire aircraft-spares settlement stack remaining on tier-1 commercial bank SWIFT, LC, and escrow rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity injection, the only compliant protocol is an inbound SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer from a bank account held in the shareholder's own name, addressed to the GmbH's dedicated capital escrow account (Sperrkonto) at a German credit institution. The payment must carry a precise purpose text in field 70: 'Einlage Stammkapital gem. SS 5, 7 GmbHG; Gesellschaftsvertrag vom [date]; Notar [name]'. The receiving bank verifies the remitter's identity, screens sanctions and PEP exposures, and issues the notarially required Einzahlungsbestätigung confirming that the deposited amount is freely at the disposal of the managing directors; this confirmation is a condition precedent to the notarial registration in the Handelsregister. For cross-border amounts equal to or above EUR 12,500, the GmbH must file an AWV balance-of-payments report with the Deutsche Bundesbank within five working days, classifying the payment as 'Kapitalverkehr / Einlagen in Kapitalgesellschaften' and stating the remitting country and the company's registration number. A third-party shareholder contribution is legally possible only with a notarised attribution declaration (Zweckerklärung) signed by both the funding party and the shareholder, plus the remitter's full UBO documentation. For a shareholder loan, the inbound transfer must reference the facility agreement and be reported in the Bundesbank monthly cross-border credit statistics once aggregate external debt exceeds EUR 5,000,000; the loan must carry market-rate interest to avoid hidden-equity recharacterisation under German income-tax interest-limitation rules, and repayment must respect Section 30 GmbHG capital maintenance if the loan functions as shareholder quasi-equity.The approved clearing rails for disbursing against purchases of surplus commercial aircraft rotables and avionics are: (i) SWIFT MT103 value-dated wire transfers for prepayments and settlements below EUR 500,000, with the payment order referencing the invoice number, part number, serial number, and EASA/FAA release numbers; (ii) irrevocable commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) issued through the German tier-1 bank for transactions above EUR 250,000 or for first-time counterparties, cleared via SWIFT MT700/MT710 and payable in EUR or USD; the required documents comprise a commercial invoice, packing list, original Air Waybill or Bill of Lading, inspection certificate, and EASA Form 1 / FAA 8130-3 certificate, with an aviation-surplus inspection clause incorporated in the credit terms; (iii) standby Letters of Credit (SBLC) supporting consignment stock and repurchase obligations; (iv) documentary collections (documents against payment/acceptance) for established suppliers; and (v) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) in EUR for intra-EU supplier payments, supplemented by Intrastat reporting once annual acquisition thresholds are exceeded. The settlement infrastructure is TARGET2 for euro payments and SWIFT FIN correspondent banking for USD (typically cleared via Fedwire/CHIPS through the German bank's New York correspondent). For imports into Germany from outside the EU, the funds release is tied to an accepted import declaration via the ATLAS customs system, and the payment file must contain the AES/ICS2 movement reference so that customs, trade finance, and treasury settlement evidence are mutually reconcilable. Aviation-specific escrow settlement involving a third-party escrow agent (typically the seller's US escrow house) must be documented with a written escrow instruction from the GmbH authorising the German bank to accept and release funds solely against the escrow agent's authenticated SWIFT instructions bearing the matching part-number references.Outright deliverable foreign-exchange forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) in USD, GBP, CHF, and CNY against EUR, documented under the Deutsche Rahmenvereinbarung für Finanztermingeschäfte (Frankfurt master agreement) or an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a German-law schedule.Foreign-exchange swaps (Devisenswaps) used to match USD trade receivables against USD payables and to roll over short-term USD liquidity without creating uncovered open positions.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (Satzwechsel / CCIRS) to hedge USD-denominated working-capital borrowings while preserving the euro as the functional currency of the GmbH.Plain vanilla purchased FX options (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP puts and calls) in a buy-only strategy; writing of uncovered option contracts is disfavoured by German bank compliance and inconsistent with prudent treasury policy for a mid-market trading entity.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not applicable and will not be booked by German banks for EUR pairs because the euro is fully convertible onshore at ECB reference rates; any attempted NDF quotation would be recharacterised as a deliverable forward under BaFin-supervised market conduct rules.

A BaFin-licensed German electronic-money institution (EMI under the Payment Services Supervision Act, ZAG) or an EEA-passported EMI may legally open a business account remotely for a GmbH and execute SEPA and SWIFT-based foreign-exchange conversions. The decisive legal constraints are: (i) an EMI cannot grant credit, issue documentary letters of credit, handle cash-against-documents collections, or provide bank payment undertakings; (ii) EMI client funds are safeguarded in pooled omnibus accounts rather than in segregated client accounts, which weakens the escrow-grade traceability that aviation counterparties expect; (iii) most EMIs apply internal sector policies that decline or escalate transactions referencing surplus aircraft parts because of dual-use and aviation-disruption risk; (iv) USD settlement above USD 250,000 is contingent on the EMI's US correspondent arrangement and may fail the beneficiary bank's enhanced due diligence; and (v) a German payment institution must halt any transaction that triggers an unresolved suspicious-activity report under Sections 43-46 GwG. Recommendation: permit an EMI only for low-value pre-payment collections below EUR 50,000 per transaction, with the entire aircraft-spares settlement stack remaining on tier-1 commercial bank SWIFT, LC, and escrow rails.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Korperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 15.825%); municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at effective rates between 7% and 17% based on the local hebesatz, producing a combined typical effective tax burden of approximately 29.5% to 31.5% on distributed profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax, resulting in an effective statutory rate of 26.375%. Where church tax applies to controlling individual shareholders, an additional surcharge of up to 3.0 percentage points may arise.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File: required where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000 in the preceding fiscal year. Local File: required for cross-border controlled transactions whose aggregate sum of consideration exceeds EUR 1,000,000 in the fiscal year (with a EUR 500,000 threshold for service-related transactions under German administrative guidance). Transaction-specific documentation is also required when a unilateral CUP adjustment exceeds these thresholds; failure to provide documentation shifts the burden of proof to the taxpayer and triggers penalty surcharges.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the general anti-abuse provisions of Sect. 42 AO and the substance requirements for treaty benefits under the Multilateral Instrument apply. A GmbH must establish and maintain full operational substance — business premises, personnel, decision-making functions, bank accounts, and complete books and records. An aviation spare-parts trading company whose only role is invoicing and holding margins without managing inventory, logistics, or risk is exposed to recharacterisation as a shell, the disallowance of treaty benefits, and tax re-attribution of income to the foreign shareholder. The company must retain all EASA/FAA certification records, quality documentation, supplier and customer contracts, and detailed transfer pricing analyses demonstrating the economically functional profile.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Steuerhinterziehung

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Sect. 370 AO with imprisonment up to five years or a monetary fine; fines may be imposed up to EUR 25,000 per offence; an intentional evasion assessment can remain open for up to ten years and statutory interest runs at 6% per annum.

Director personal liability: Managing directors and shadow directors are personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, interest, and penalties; criminal sanctions may include asset attachment, travel warrants and, in aggravated cases, imprisonment. Civil restitution of damages to the company is enforceable against directors who knowingly participated in abusive transactions.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will adjust taxable income upward under Sect. 162 AO. Where documentation is absent or materially insufficient, the administrative decree allows a penalty of 5% of the TP adjustment, subject to a minimum of EUR 10,000 and maximum of EUR 1,000,000. Deliberate misdeclaration may escalate to criminal tax evasion with up to five years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate tax returns or approve non-arm's-length transfer prices may be held personally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalty amounts; they also face criminal liability for aiding tax evasion and may be barred from serving as managing directors in future German businesses.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law (Gestaltungsmissbrauch)

Statutory sanction: Under Sect. 42 AO, the Finanzamt will disregard the corporate veil and attribute the income to the actual beneficial owner; treaty relief is denied under the Principal Purpose Test. A grossly negligent evasion fine of up to EUR 50,000 and confiscation of trading assets can be imposed; relevant permits for aviation trade may be revoked by the Federal Aviation Office (LBA).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintain a circular or shell arrangement are jointly and severally liable for the full tax under-payment and may be sued for restitution; in aggravated cases they face imprisonment of up to five years for intentional abuse of law and can be ordered to disgorge personal assets acquired through the scheme.
AML and Sanctions Violation in Aircraft Parts Trading

Statutory sanction: Violation of the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and EU Regulation 2018/287 can lead to administrative fines of up to EUR 500,000 per transaction; the goods are subject to seizure; criminal conviction for sanctions evasion under Sect. 18 AWG carries imprisonment of up to five years or a fine. BaFin may also impose separate AML fines under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG).

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for the civil damages arising from the prohibited transaction, including freight reversal, international penalties, and contractual losses. They may face personal criminal prosecution, travel bans, and asset freezes; the German customs authorities can issue detention orders and authorities may seek extradition when aviation parts are routed through third states.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for the national VAT identification number; the local Finanzamt for the corporate income tax, trade tax, and payroll tax numbers. Central e-filing portal: ELSTER / BZSt Online-Portal (Portal: www.bzst.de, www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a GmbH must complete the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) and submit it to the local Finanzamt as the competent tax office. The questionnaire can be filed electronically through ELSTER or in paper form. The local Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer (local tax number) for corporate income tax and trade tax; the BZSt independently issues the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr) upon receipt of the registration data. The GmbH is also registered for payroll tax if it has employees.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a GmbH must complete the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) and submit it to the local Finanzamt as the competent tax office. The questionnaire can be filed electronically through ELSTER or in paper form. The local Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer (local tax number) for corporate income tax and trade tax; the BZSt independently issues the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr) upon receipt of the registration data. The GmbH is also registered for payroll tax if it has employees.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German tax law does not permit check-the-box pass-through election for a GmbH. The only relevant statutory election is the formation of an Organschaft (fiscal unity) under § 14 Körperschaftsteuergesetz and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 Umsatzsteuergesetz, requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement for corporation tax purposes and financial/economic integration for VAT purposes. Hybrid mismatch adjustments apply under national law transposing ATAD 2, but no elective hybrid regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A business established in Germany is taxable regardless of turnover, but the small-entrepreneur exemption under § 19 UStG applies when annual turnover does not exceed €22,000 (increased to €25,000 from 1 January 2025) and is expected to remain under €100,000 in the following year. Export wholesale traders and B2B suppliers generally must register for VAT once these thresholds are exceeded or if cross-border supplies are made. There is no special threshold for Baijiu/spirits export wholesale.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to non-EU third countries are broadly zero-rated under § 6 UStG, provided the goods are physically dispatched or transported by the supplier or a third party to a destination outside the European Union, and the exporter holds appropriate documentary proof of export. For high-proof distilled spirits such as Chinese Baijiu, the exporter must also ensure compliance with alcohol licensing rules under German and EU excise law, but excise duty is suspended in export, and VAT is charged at 0% on international supply. Services directly related to exported goods are assessed under § 7 UStG. The zero-rating may be claimed only where the seller is the exporter of record or has appointed an authorized representative, and where the goods leave EU customs territory without substantial alteration outside the EU.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) as lodged in the German ATLAS export system, showing the customs office of opening and exit
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing transport from the EU to the third-country destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank SWIFT/Letter of Credit realisation document providing evidence of foreign exchange repatriation
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract identifying the foreign buyer, Incoterms, and description of the Baijiu product
  • Export Accompanying Document (Parallelverkehrsdokument) or T1 transit document where transiting through another EU or third-country territory is involved
  • Proof of actual exit from EU customs territory, including authorised customs office exit stamp or electronic export confirmation message from ATLAS

Since 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B invoices must be issued as structured electronic invoices in an interoperable format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x) and transmitted electronically through EDI-compatible channels. Invoices issued to German federal public authorities must be transmitted via the Peppol network. There is no nationwide real-time fiscal clearance, transaction-level digital stamping, or mandatory continuous transaction control (CTC) regime; VAT returns are filed electronically via ELSTER on a monthly/quarterly basis, and VAT is remitted by statutory deadlines.

German customs and tax authorities apply stringent audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. Missing, incomplete, or fraudulent export documentation results in denial of zero-rating and subsequent VAT assessment at 19% of the invoice value, plus late-payment interest of 6% per annum. In cases of negligent or intentional false documentation, penalties of up to €50,000 per offence and criminal tax evasion charges under § 370 Abgabenordnung apply, with imprisonment of up to five years. Retrospective clawback of zero-rating may be imposed on all transactions in the audit period, and collateral penalties include trade tax surcharges and interest accruing from the original VAT due dates.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible. Germany has no general exchange-control approval requirement for current-account payments, capital contributions, dividends, or loan repayments. Transfers remain subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, bank documentation, tax rules, and applicable EU or German restrictions. A German GmbH may remit lawfully distributable profits to non-resident shareholders, subject to corporate-law distribution requirements, withholding-tax obligations, treaty relief or refund procedures, and evidence requested by the bank. Interest and principal payments on related-party financing may require transfer-pricing support and tax review. German residents must assess AWV reporting for reportable cross-border payments; the general reporting threshold is amounts exceeding EUR 50,000, subject to statutory exemptions and the current reporting instructions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory deadline for repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio. Proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts or used for permitted cross-border payments. The GmbH should retain contracts, invoices, customs export evidence, transport documents, and bank statements, and assess AWV reporting where applicable. Export proceeds do not automatically satisfy German excise, VAT, or customs obligations.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany's national central bank and administers German external-payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The European Central Bank conducts monetary policy for the euro area. Germany applies EU rules on payment services, financial sanctions, and capital movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine EUR payments and selected foreign-currency services; low as the sole treasury bank for a baijiu wholesaler with material trade-finance, cash, or documentary-credit needs. An EU-authorized electronic money institution may provide payment accounts and transfers within its authorization, but e-money is not a bank deposit and is not covered by the statutory deposit-guarantee scheme. Verify authorization, safeguarding arrangements, supported currencies and corridors, sanctions controls, and acceptance by counterparties. Offshore providers must be authorized to provide services in Germany under applicable EU passporting or German authorization rules. Fintech accounts do not replace the GmbH's statutory capital-payment evidence or excise, customs, and tax compliance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act (KStG), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal burden of 15.825%; municipal trade tax is additional and varies by municipality, commonly producing a combined burden of approximately 29% to 33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic withholding burden of 26.375%. A German corporation receiving dividends generally benefits from the 95% participation exemption if statutory conditions are satisfied. Distributions to a Chinese corporate shareholder may qualify for a reduced rate under the Germany-China tax treaty, commonly 5% where the treaty ownership threshold is met and 10% otherwise, subject to treaty conditions, beneficial ownership and anti-abuse rules. EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief may apply to an eligible EU parent, but it does not normally eliminate withholding for a Chinese parent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For contemporaneous documentation, the German thresholds are generally EUR 6,000,000 per fiscal year for supplies of goods and EUR 600,000 per fiscal year for other transactions, including services, financing and licensing. A Local File is required where the documentation obligation applies. A Master File is generally required for German entities belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000 in the preceding fiscal year, subject to German documentation rules. Country-by-country reporting generally applies at the group level where consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a single separate broad Economic Substance Regulation regime comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is assessed through the arm's-length principle, German anti-abuse rule in section 42 of the Fiscal Code, treaty anti-abuse provisions, beneficial-ownership standards, the German anti-treaty-shopping rule in section 50d(3) of the Income Tax Act, controlled foreign company rules and general place-of-management and permanent-establishment principles. The GmbH should maintain German commercial premises, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, decision-making records, inventory and logistics evidence, banking controls, customer contracts and risk-management documentation proportionate to its wholesale export functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion is generally punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine. Particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Assessed tax, interest and potentially substantial penalties remain payable. Criminal limitation periods and extended limitation periods may apply.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal prosecution, personal liability for unpaid taxes caused by culpable breach of tax duties, civil damages, director-disqualification consequences and confiscation. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may result from judicial measures or enforcement circumstances.
VAT Fraud and Invalid Export Documentation

Statutory sanction: Additional VAT, interest, late-payment charges, administrative fines and criminal tax-evasion sanctions may apply. Customs and VAT authorities may deny exemption and input-tax deductions and may impose seizure or audit measures.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for knowingly or negligently causing VAT losses and may face criminal liability where false documentation or deliberate evasion is established.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may increase German taxable income, deny deductions, recharacterize payments as hidden profit distributions, impose withholding tax, assess interest and levy documentation-related surcharges. Persistent non-cooperation may permit estimation of taxable income.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for tax losses and may face criminal tax-evasion exposure if adjustments result from intentional concealment or knowingly false documentation.
Shell Company, Treaty Abuse and Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Treaty or domestic exemption may be denied; withholding tax, solidarity surcharge, interest and penalties may be assessed. Anti-treaty-shopping and general anti-abuse provisions may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unpaid withholding tax and may face criminal exposure if applications or certificates contain knowingly false statements. Travel bans are not an automatic statutory consequence.
Customs, Excise and Alcohol Compliance

Statutory sanction: Customs duty, import VAT, alcohol duty, interest, administrative fines, seizure, forfeiture, suspension of customs authorizations and, for deliberate evasion or smuggling, criminal prosecution may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible customs officers may be personally prosecuted for intentional customs or excise fraud and may be liable for duties where statutory personal liability conditions are met.
Anti-Money Laundering and Suspicious Payments

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act can be substantial, with higher limits for serious or repeated breaches. Criminal money-laundering, sanctions and terrorist-financing offences can result in imprisonment, confiscation and business restrictions.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal regulatory and criminal liability for intentional participation or culpable compliance failures. Financial Intelligence Unit reporting duties and banking account restrictions may follow.
Sanctions, Export Controls and Dual-Use Restrictions

Statutory sanction: EU and German sanctions breaches may result in severe administrative fines, criminal penalties, confiscation, export-license withdrawal and transaction blocking. Penalties depend on the specific sanctions and export-control provision.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable and criminally prosecuted for knowing or negligent violations within their organizational responsibility. Individual travel restrictions may arise from criminal proceedings or sanctions designation.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Payroll or Capital-Income Tax

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may owe the unwithheld tax, solidarity surcharge, interest and penalties. Intentional failure to remit withheld tax may constitute a criminal offence and may trigger enforcement against the company.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable under German tax-liability rules for culpable non-payment and may face criminal prosecution for intentional withholding-tax offences.
Insolvency and Wrongful Continuation of Business

Statutory sanction: Directors may face insolvency-related criminal sanctions, repayment claims, creditor damages, disqualification and confiscation. Insolvency filing delay can trigger imprisonment or fines depending on the offence and facts.

Director personal liability: Personal civil liability for prohibited payments and creditor losses is a primary risk. Imprisonment is possible for intentional or serious insolvency offences; travel bans require specific judicial or enforcement grounds and are not automatic.
Corporate Books, Beneficial Ownership Register and Statutory Accounts

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, coercive fines, publication sanctions, tax estimation and loss of compliance standing may apply. Serious falsification can create criminal liability.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined or prosecuted for intentional falsification or culpable failure to meet statutory filing and transparency obligations, in addition to company-level penalties.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (Federal Ministry of Finance) via Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification; local Finanzamt for income/corporate tax and VAT administration. Principal e-portal: ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) at www.elster.de.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities registering for VAT in Germany must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (questionnaire for tax registration) with the competent local Finanzamt (Section 138 AO and Section 18 UStG). The same questionnaire serves for tax number allocation and USt-IdNr (VAT ID) application. A dwelling or permanent establishment, or a fiscal representative, may be required for certain non-EU transferors. VAT ID issuance is handled by the BZSt via form 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer nach § 27a UStG'. Supporting documents include certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, proof of non-resident director/ownership, and evidence of German business activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not apply check-the-box classification. A GmbH is a deemed resident tax entity subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) and Solidaritätszuschlag plus Gewerbesteuer (trade tax). Available elections include: (i) tax group (Organschaft) under Section 14 KStG / Sections 2-4 UStG for income, trade, and VAT consolidation; (ii) profit distribution attribution under Section 20 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 EStG; and (iii) hybrid mismatch adjustments under Section 4k EStG / Section 12a KStG implementing ATAD. Certain instrument-level elections under Section 8b KStG may apply to intercompany shareholdings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General VAT registration is required from the first taxable supply within Germany for foreign businesses without a domestic establishment. The Section 19 UStG small-business exemption applies only to domestic resident businesses: prior-year turnover up to EUR 22,000 and current-year forecast not above EUR 50,000. Intra-Community acquisitions and distance sales have separate thresholds; receipt of services from abroad may require registration irrespective of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies to supplies of goods transported by the seller or on the seller's behalf from Germany to a non-EU third country (Section 4 Nr. 1a, Section 6 UStG). Prerequisites: the goods must actually leave the EU; the exporter must be the supplier; and the supplier must hold documentary proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) pursuant to Sections 8-13 UStDV. For intra-EU (Union) supplies, exemption applies under Section 4 Nr. 1b and Section 6a UStG if the buyer provides a valid VAT ID, the goods are dispatched to another EU member state, and the supplier files the recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung). For wholesale distribution of IVD diagnostic reagents, zero-rating additionally requires commercial invoices evidencing the consignee, product description, and HS codes.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic ATLAS / Ausfuhranmeldung with Ausfuhrbegleitdokument) and customs export confirmation (Ausfuhrbestätigung)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (transport document evidencing departure from Germany and arrival in the non-EU destination)
  • Commercial invoice and, where applicable, inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT/TT payment evidence to substantiate commercial substance and value

Germany mandates structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B invoices as of 1 January 2025 under the Growth Opportunities Act. Invoices must be issued in an EN 16931-compliant structured format such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD, and transmitted electronically; there is no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government API pre-clearance. Foreign suppliers issuing invoices for German taxable supplies must comply with the e-invoicing requirements if the invoice is for a domestic B2B transaction; export invoices are exempt from the domestic e-invoice mandate but still require documentary proof.

Fraudulent or erroneous export zero-rating triggers reversal of VAT exemption and imposition of 19% German VAT plus late-payment interest. Under the German Tax Code (AO), deliberate false declarations constitute tax evasion under Section 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years or fines; negligent acts may invoke Section 378 AO as tax fraud. Audit scrutiny includes retroactive reassessment, disallowing export proof, and 10-year statutory assessment period for evasion (Section 169(2) AO). Directors of the GmbH may face personal liability, and lenders or acquirers should perform warranty and indemnity due diligence for historical VAT uncollected.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully convertible Euro currency regime. Capital account transactions face no restrictions for EU residents. Current account transactions, including profit remittances, are unrestricted. Profit remittance is subject only to standard corporate tax withholding (Abgeltungsteuer 25% plus solidarity surcharge) and CRS/DAC beneficial ownership reporting. No capital controls apply to intra-EU or standard cross-border flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Companies may retain foreign currency receipts or convert at market rates. Statutory BaFin reporting required solely for suspicious transactions exceeding €50,000 under AML rules. Export documentation must reference EORI number for VAT compliance.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax (0.825%), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at typical combined rates of approximately 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital gains tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the 25% (equal to 1.375%), resulting in a combined withholding rate of 26.375% on cash dividends paid to non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within seven months after the end of the tax year (31 July for calendar-year taxpayers). If the return is prepared and submitted by a tax advisor, the filing deadline is automatically extended to 28/29 February of the second following year (e.g., 28 February 2025 for the 2023 tax year).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required if cross-border intercompany transactions with related parties exceed EUR 5,000,000 for goods or EUR 500,000 for services per fiscal year. Master File is required if the group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Country-by-Country Report is required for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000. Thresholds are in euros (local currency).

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations analogous to the UAE; however, the German General Anti-Abuse Rule under § 42 AO, the treaty-shopping protection of § 50d EStG (beneficial ownership requirement), and the substance-over-form principle require the GmbH to demonstrate real economic substance (business premises, payroll and qualified personnel, physical storage and distribution activities, and active management decisions in Germany) to claim DTA benefits, avoid shell-company classification, preserve loss carry-forwards under § 8c KStG, and ensure compliance with IVDR licensing requirements for diagnostic reagents.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to five years or a fine under § 370 AO; in especially serious cases (commercial-scale, gang fraud, substantial tax loss) imprisonment of up to ten years. The tax arrears plus late-payment interest at 6% per annum are assessed, and a criminal fine is imposed based on the gravity of the offence.

Director personal liability: Managing directors (Geschäftsführer) are personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax where they failed to act with the care of a prudent businessman; criminal conviction may result in travel bans, confiscation of personal assets, and disqualification from serving as director of any German company for up to five years.
Non-Filing and Late Payment of Corporate Tax

Statutory sanction: Late filing surcharge under § 152 AO up to EUR 25,000; late payment surcharge of 0.5% per month on the unpaid tax (maximum 25% of the assessed tax); additional penalty of EUR 10,000 for failure to use mandated electronic filing; the tax assessment may be made on an estimated basis with an unfavourable mark-up.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the penalty surcharge and for tax arrears to the extent of their culpable failure to ensure timely filing; the tax office may enforce against the director's personal assets after a formal liability determination under §§ 34, 69 AO.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Surcharge of up to 10% of the additional corporate income tax assessed due to undocumented transfer pricing, with a minimum of EUR 5,000; the tax authorities may estimate taxable income unfavourably and apply anti-abuse recharacterisation under § 42 AO and, where applicable, deny the § 8b KStG exemption for shareholdings.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the preparation of a knowingly incorrect TP documentation may be liable for aiding and abetting tax evasion; they may also face civil liability to reimburse the GmbH for tax deficiencies plus interest for breach of fiduciary duty.
Shell Company Abuse and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under § 42 AO the artificial structure is ignored and taxable income is assessed at the beneficial owner; loss carry-forwards may be forfeited under § 8c KStG; the company may be denied German tax residence and become subject to withholding tax on deemed distributions; penalties for false declarations apply.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable to the extent they caused the GmbH's failure to maintain substance, including personal liability for trade taxes if the GmbH becomes insolvent; cross-border recovery proceedings may lead to travel bans, and flagrant misuse may trigger criminal liability for money laundering under § 261 StGB.
AML and Cash Transaction Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Monetary fines of up to EUR 100,000 for negligent violations of the GwG record-keeping obligations; up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for serious, intentional or repeated violations under § 56 GwG; criminal prosecution for money laundering under § 261 StGB with imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally criminally liable; personal fines and confiscation orders may be imposed; wilful evasion of the cash-control framework can lead to disallowance from acting as a manager or director for any commercial entity in Germany.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the USt-IdNr., W-IdNr. and EORI register, together with the locally competent state tax office (Finanzamt) at the GmbH's registered seat for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT; electronic filing exclusively via the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de) and BZSt e-services (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

Foreign owners of a German GmbH complete the following sequence: (i) notarial incorporation and Commercial Register (Handelsregister) entry, giving rise to the company's birth certificate and the UBO declaration for the Transparency Register (Transparenzregister); (ii) submit the 'Questionnaire for Tax Registration' (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the Finanzamt within one month of business commencement, simultaneously triggering the Steuernummer (income/trade tax number) and VAT registration; (iii) apply to BZSt for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) for cross-border intra-Community transactions and for the EORI number (DE code) for customs/export operations; (iv) receive the economic identifier (W-IdNr.) for statutory registers; (v) instruct a German tax advisor (Steuerberater) for all Filings because electronic returns must be certified. Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the GmbH; no local tax representative is mandatory for income taxes, but a domestic VAT representative may be required if the company acts as a foreign entrepreneur without a domestic branch.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a German GmbH is a resident corporation with unlimited worldwide tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht): 15% corporate income tax, 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), yielding an aggregate effective load of approximately 29.8%–31%; no territorial carve-outs, no pass-through, and no tax-exempt-zone relief. For VAT, a passive single-family-office GmbH holding investments without charging management fees is not an entrepreneur under section 2 UStG and is therefore outside the VAT system.

Available election forms

Germany has no U.S.-style check-the-box regime. Elections available to a GmbH in this scope: (i) tax group/fiscal unity (Organschaft) under section 14 KStG and section 2(2) No. 2 UStG, requiring a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) and economic/financial/organisational integration — central for pooling investment-holding income and VAT groups; (ii) small-business VAT option under section 19 UStG, which waives VAT but forfeits input tax recovery; (iii) simplified accounting/income-measurement options of sections 141 AO and 4(3) EStG for small companies; (iv) investment-transparency election under the Investment Tax Act (InvStG) if the SFO qualifies as an investment fund or special fund; (v) anti-hybrid alignment declarations under sections 4j/4k EStG to prevent double non-taxation of foreign shareholdings and hybrid loans.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 (preceding calendar year) and EUR 100,000 (current-year forecast) — small-business exemption under section 19 UStG effective 1 January 2025, with a legislatively planned increase of the preceding-year threshold to EUR 100,000 in 2027. Foreign entrepreneurs have an unconditional German VAT registration obligation from the first euro of taxable supplies in Germany; for a domestic SFO GmbH, the threshold matters only for fee-generating family-office services, whereas dividends, interest and capital gains from strategic shareholdings remain outside the entrepreneurial sphere.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of goods leaving the EU are exempt with input VAT deduction (0% effective) under section 6 UStG where the exporter, customer or a third party dispatches the goods to a non-EU territory and the export is corroborated by the customs export declaration and transport documents; services closely linked to exported goods (section 8 UStG) and B2B services supplied to non-EU entrepreneurs (section 3a(2) UStG, place of supply at the recipient's seat) are zero-rated/outside the German VAT scope. For the single-family-office GmbH, asset-management, advisory and securities-transaction services supplied to a non-EU SPC or trust qualify for zero rating; services to a non-EU resident private individual are taxable at 19% in Germany unless the securities-management exemption of section 4(8) UStG applies. Germany imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day period stated in the compliance matrix is a customary OECD contract benchmark, not a legal condition of zero-rating.

  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list stating exporter, consignee, Incoterms and goods classification
  • Bill of lading (Seefrachtbrief) or air waybill (AWB) or CMR consignment note proving physical dispatch to a non-EU destination

Mandatory structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions: from 1 January 2025 every entrepreneur must be able to receive XRechnung/ZUGFeRD 2.3 invoices compliant with EN 16931; from 1 January 2027 issuers with more than EUR 800,000 turnover in 2026 must issue e-invoices only, and from 1 January 2028 all entrepreneurs must issue structured e-invoices. Germany does not apply real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping; invoice data are archived for ten years, and the monthly preliminary VAT return (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung), the annual return and the intra-Community sales list (Zusammenfassende Meldung) are transmitted to BZSt/Finanzamt via ELSTER; EU ViDA transaction-level e-reporting will be introduced from 1 July 2030.

Customs and the Finanzamt apply targeted export VAT audits (Umsatzsteuer-Sonderprüfung) with a ten-year document-retention duty under sections 147 AO and 6(4) UStG. If export substantiation is missing or falsified, the 0% treatment is retroactively reversed to 19% VAT, input VAT deductions are clawed back under section 15a UStG, statutory interest accrues at 1.8% p.a. under section 233a AO, and negligence fines up to EUR 50,000 apply under section 378 AO; intentional fraud is criminal tax evasion under sections 369/370 AO, punishable by up to ten years' imprisonment for particularly serious cases. Directors additionally face personal liability under sections 35 GmbHG and 71 AO, and the exporter bears the full burden of proving the authenticity and truthfulness of the export documents.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Germany imposes no exchange-control regime on current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, dividends, interest) or capital-account transactions (equity, loans, portfolio investment), consistent with Art. 63 TFEU free movement of capital. Profit remittance is unrestricted: a distribution from a GmbH to a non-resident shareholder attracts 25% corporate withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding (26.375% all-in), while treaty relief or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive can reduce or eliminate the WHT. The payment itself requires no prior license, but any cross-border remittance exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank under AWV § 67a.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirements exist under German law. Exporters and service providers may retain all proceeds in any currency and hold them in any jurisdiction without restriction. The only relevant obligation is statistical: payments received from non-residents, including export proceeds exceeding EUR 12,500 or the equivalent per transaction, must be reported through the Bundesbank's AWV § 67a channel, normally by the German receiving bank on the client's behalf, by the 5th working day of the month following receipt.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), acting as the competent authority for the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) statistical reporting and international payment statistics. Banking and financial-services supervision, including securities custody and money-laundering enforcement, is vested in the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin); foreign-trade policy authority rests with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low–Medium as primary treasury rail. European PSD2 e-money institutions (e.g., N26 Business, Penta, Qonto, Airwallex, Wise Business) are legally permitted to provide payment accounts and cross-currency transfer services, but they may not lawfully provide (i) statutory deposit protection (Einlagensicherung) guaranteed by the German Deposit Guarantee Fund, (ii) securities custody (Wertpapierverwahrung) required for the family-office investment rail, or (iii) documentary credit or guarantee products. Use is therefore restricted to ancillary liquidity sweeping and small-value expense routing; all core treasury, FX and custody activity must sit on a full German banking license. Anti-money-laundering obligations under the German Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz – GwG) apply equally and banks will refuse to onboard if the primary treasury function is outsourced to an EMI without a correspondent banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: (1) the signed notarial formation deed (Gesellschaftsvertrag) is submitted to the chosen German bank together with the Sonderkonto opening form; (2) capital is received by SWIFT MT103 (SEPA is possible but MT103 is recommended for audit trail) from the non-resident shareholder's bank with a reference containing the GmbH name, notarial deed URN (Urkundennummer), and purpose 'Stammeinlage Kapitalaufbringung'; (3) after receipt the bank supports the notary's § 8(2) GmbHG declaration that the capital is at the free disposal of the managing director, enabling Commercial Register entry; (4) the bank reports the inbound cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Bundesbank under AWV § 67a; (5) the company permanently archives the MT103, deed URN and shareholder payment instruction for tax and treasury audit purposes. For any inbound shareholder loan or further paid-in capital, the payment reference must state the capital measure and the corresponding creditor's declaration.Clearing rails: (i) euro-denominated disbursements via SEPA Credit Transfer, including instant payments up to EUR 100,000, settled through the Bundesbank's T2 RTGS liquidity component of the Eurosystem TARGET services; (ii) non-euro cross-border transfers through SWIFT MT103 with fields 50/59/70 carrying the invoice or agreement number and full economic purpose, compliant with AWV § 67a; (iii) commercial trade settlements via SWIFT MT700/MT710 documentary Letters of Credit issued or confirmed by the German bank under ICC UCP 600, with documents presented through the SWIFT trade-services utility; (iv) securities transactions settled on T2S (TARGET2-Securities) with the cash leg on T2 for delivery-versus-payment. Each outbound payment exceeding EUR 12,500 is reported to the Bundesbank on the client's behalf; the company retains evidence of the underlying commercial or private-asset transaction (bills of sale, securities trade confirmations, advisory invoices) to survive retrospective reviews.FX Forwards (Devisentermingeschäft) with licensed EU credit institutionsFX Swaps (Spot-Forward combinations) for liquidity and roll-over managementCross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (Währungsswaps) aligned to investment debt profilesEuropean-style Currency Options (OTC, physically settled or cash-settled)Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for restricted and non-convertible currencies only

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Low–Medium as primary treasury rail. European PSD2 e-money institutions (e.g., N26 Business, Penta, Qonto, Airwallex, Wise Business) are legally permitted to provide payment accounts and cross-currency transfer services, but they may not lawfully provide (i) statutory deposit protection (Einlagensicherung) guaranteed by the German Deposit Guarantee Fund, (ii) securities custody (Wertpapierverwahrung) required for the family-office investment rail, or (iii) documentary credit or guarantee products. Use is therefore restricted to ancillary liquidity sweeping and small-value expense routing; all core treasury, FX and custody activity must sit on a full German banking license. Anti-money-laundering obligations under the German Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz – GwG) apply equally and banks will refuse to onboard if the primary treasury function is outsourced to an EMI without a correspondent banking relationship.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent tax office (Finanzamt) administers German corporate and value-added tax registration. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.). Applications and tax filings are generally submitted through the ELSTER electronic tax portal operated by the German tax administration. The German Customs Administration (Zollverwaltung), through its ATLAS system, administers export customs formalities.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate and register the GmbH with the competent commercial register and obtain the German business registration documentation. 2. Submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt. 3. Provide the GmbH articles, commercial-register extract, registered office, directors, beneficial ownership information, business activities, expected turnover, bank details, and information concerning cross-border transactions. 4. Receive the corporate tax number (Steuernummer) from the Finanzamt. 5. Apply electronically to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), normally using the Finanzamt registration data; the USt-IdNr. is used for intra-Community supplies, acquisitions, and cross-border B2B transactions. 6. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is ordinarily subject to German corporate income tax and trade tax at entity level and has no U.S.-style check-the-box election. German tax classification follows the legal form and statutory substance. A GmbH may participate in a tax consolidation arrangement (Organschaft) if statutory financial, organizational, and economic integration requirements are met. The optional corporate-income-tax treatment available under Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act generally concerns eligible partnerships and is not an election available to a GmbH. For VAT, the small-business exemption under Section 19 of the German VAT Act may be used if the statutory turnover conditions are satisfied; it is an exemption regime rather than an entity-classification election. Hybrid instruments and arrangements remain subject to German anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold below which a business carrying out taxable activities is automatically exempt from German VAT registration. A domestic business must register when it conducts taxable supplies in Germany, subject to applicable exemptions and the small-business regime. From 1 January 2025, the German small-business exemption generally applies where prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions. For EU cross-border distance sales and qualifying electronically supplied services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold may apply where the supplier is established in only one EU Member State and has not opted for destination-country taxation; the One Stop Shop may be used when destination-country VAT is due.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct input VAT where the supplier or an authorized customer transports or dispatches the goods to the third country and the statutory export evidence is maintained. The goods must leave the EU customs territory, and the supplier must establish the identity of the goods, the recipient, the destination, and the transport or dispatch. Intra-Community supplies to a VAT-identified business customer in another EU Member State may qualify for VAT exemption where the customer provides a valid VAT identification number, the goods are transported to another Member State, the transport evidence is retained, and the required EC Sales List reporting is completed. Offshore services are not automatically zero-rated: the place-of-supply rules determine whether German VAT applies. B2B services are generally supplied where the business customer is established, subject to special rules; B2C services and electronically supplied services may be taxable where the consumer resides. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, a direct business purpose, and compliance with deduction, documentation, and filing requirements.

  • Electronic export declaration submitted through the German ATLAS export procedure, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Export Accompanying Document or equivalent customs exit confirmation
  • Electronic confirmation that the goods exited the EU customs territory, or alternative official export evidence permitted by German VAT law
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and transport documentation identifying the goods, supplier, consignee, destination, and transaction
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier proof of delivery, or equivalent carrier evidence where applicable
  • Proof of payment and transaction records supporting the commercial supply
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid VAT identification number, transport or dispatch evidence, and correctly filed EC Sales List
  • For subscription-box exports, shipment-level records linking each invoice, customer, parcel, customs declaration, tracking record, and destination
  • Inward remittance evidence is not a universal statutory prerequisite for German export VAT exemption; it may be retained as supporting commercial evidence and may be relevant to financial-accounting or foreign-exchange controls

Germany does not generally operate a real-time VAT invoice-clearance or continuous transaction-control system. Since 1 January 2025, German businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions. Mandatory issuance of structured e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies is phased in: generally from 1 January 2027 for issuers exceeding the applicable prior-year turnover condition and from 1 January 2028 for all in-scope domestic B2B issuers, subject to statutory transitional rules. Accepted structured formats include XRechnung and compliant European-standard EN 16931 formats, including qualifying hybrid ZUGFeRD invoices. Electronic invoices must contain the legally required invoice data and be retained in an auditable manner. Invoices issued to consumers and many foreign customers are generally outside the domestic B2B issuance mandate, although ordinary VAT invoicing and record-retention rules continue to apply.

Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption may be denied or retrospectively reversed if the supplier cannot substantiate dispatch, export, destination, customer status, or the required reporting. The Finanzamt may assess unpaid VAT, statutory interest under the German Fiscal Code, late-payment surcharges, and related trade or corporate tax consequences. Incorrect input VAT deductions and refund claims may be clawed back with interest. Intentional tax evasion may constitute a criminal offense punishable by fines or imprisonment under the German Fiscal Code; negligent tax reduction may result in administrative fines. Businesses may also face penalties for inaccurate invoices, deficient records, failure to submit VAT returns or EC Sales Lists, customs violations, and obstruction of tax audits. Repeated non-compliance can lead to enhanced audit measures, denial of tax reliability in customs procedures, and personal liability exposure for responsible managing directors.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility is guaranteed under EU primary law (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Articles 63-65). There are no restrictions on a GmbH acquiring or holding EUR or any foreign currency, no prior exchange-control approval for importing or exporting capital, and no limitations on dividend or profit remittances to non-resident shareholders. Profit distributions require a shareholder resolution under Section 46 No. 1 GmbHG and are subject only to German corporate income tax mechanics, a 25% dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%; church tax may apply where relevant) unless reduced by an applicable double-tax treaty, and Bundesbank statistical reporting. The EUR is freely convertible into USD, GBP, CNY and all freely tradable currencies through licensed banks; no central bank authorization is required before entering into spot or forward FX transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. German foreign trade law does not impose any statutory timeline for export receipts to be repatriated to Germany, nor any mandatory conversion ratio into EUR. The subscription-box GmbH may retain export receivables in foreign-currency accounts (USD, GBP, CNY, etc.) indefinitely. The sole compliance duty is statistical: each cross-border payment received from a non-resident, or paid to a non-resident, in excess of EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank (DWUG payment-reporting) within 30 days under Section 67 AWV. Aggregated platform settlements (Stripe/Authorize.Net) and intra-group treasury flows must be documented and reported accurately; non-compliance is an administrative offence under Section 70 AWG.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Deutsche Bundesbank, Frankfurt am Main) as the administrative and statistical authority responsible for the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Ordinance (AWV), in conjunction with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin - Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) for prudential banking supervision; there is no separate foreign-exchange-control ministry.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a complementary multi-currency treasury rail, but Low as the sole statutory capital-escrow bank. A PSD2-authorized electronic money institution (e.g., Wise Europe, Airwallex, PPRO, N26 Business, Solaris SE) may hold EUR/GBP/USD balances and issue IBANs under its EEA passport without physical presence. Legal constraints: (i) the notarial and Commercial Register validation of the EUR 25,000 minimum capital (Stammeinlage) is more readily accepted when placed with a full German credit institution licensed under the German Banking Act (KWG); (ii) EMIs generally cannot provide commercial overdrafts, letters of credit, or in-house FX derivatives/hedging execution; (iii) high-volume card-not-present subscription payouts from Stripe and Authorize.Net may require the EMI to hold a direct acquiring relationship rather than an agent-network model; (iv) the transaction-monitoring expectations of Section 25k KWG render an EMI acceptable only as a secondary liquidity corridor.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a complementary multi-currency treasury rail, but Low as the sole statutory capital-escrow bank. A PSD2-authorized electronic money institution (e.g., Wise Europe, Airwallex, PPRO, N26 Business, Solaris SE) may hold EUR/GBP/USD balances and issue IBANs under its EEA passport without physical presence. Legal constraints: (i) the notarial and Commercial Register validation of the EUR 25,000 minimum capital (Stammeinlage) is more readily accepted when placed with a full German credit institution licensed under the German Banking Act (KWG); (ii) EMIs generally cannot provide commercial overdrafts, letters of credit, or in-house FX derivatives/hedging execution; (iii) high-volume card-not-present subscription payouts from Stripe and Authorize.Net may require the EMI to hold a direct acquiring relationship rather than an agent-network model; (iv) the transaction-monitoring expectations of Section 25k KWG render an EMI acceptable only as a secondary liquidity corridor.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.825% effective German dividend withholding tax (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 0.825% solidarity surcharge) applicable to dividend distributions made by a GmbH unless a DTA or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive provides relief. For EU/EEA parent companies holding at least 10% for 12 months, the withholding rate is 0%; under many German DTAs the rate is reduced to 5% or 15% depending on shareholding thresholds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,500,000 for cross-border related-party transactions in a fiscal year; EUR 5,000,000 for domestic German related-party transactions. These threshold values are per fiscal year and aggregate over all similar cross-border or domestic transactions with each related party. If the thresholds are exceeded, a formal Local File/Master File documentation package must be prepared within the statutory 60-day period following the tax auditor's request, and contemporaneous transfer-pricing studies are required for intercompany pricing of subscription-box procurement, logistics, IP licences and group financing.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a formal economic-substance certificate regime equivalent to the UAE ESR, but requires real economic substance through the application of § 42 AO (abuse of legal form), § 50d(3) EStG (treaty/PSD shopping abuse), §§ 7-13 AStG (additive CFC rules) and the German authorisation for tax authorities to disregard foreign intermediary companies that lack staff, premises, functions and risk-bearing capacity. For a subscription-box GmbH, the German entity must demonstrate genuine decision-making, physical logistics operations, order-processing, staffing, and retains the ability to manage the subscription business; otherwise tax authorities may attribute the profits to the foreign shareholder and declare the GmbH a substance-less shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under § 370 AO

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 6 months to 5 years, in aggravated cases up to 10 years, criminal fines, and the tax is additionally collected with 6% interest per annum on the underpayment under § 233a AO. Courts may also confiscate assets equal to the evaded tax amount.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under § 69 AO if they intentionally or grossly negligently breach tax-representation duties; German tax authorities can pierce the corporate veil under § 826 BGB for intentional damage and demand restitution from the managing director's personal assets. A criminal court may order a travel ban and passport surrender to prevent flight; imprisonment and disqualification from serving as a GmbH director are possible.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief under § 50d(3) EStG; use of § 42 AO to recharacterise the entire arrangement and treat the income as taxable in the hands of the real owner; assessments with 25.825% WHT on constructive dividends, late-payment interest, and supplementary penalty of up to €25,000 per event under the German Transparency Register rules; trade and tax licence can be revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors and nominee shareholders are jointly and severally liable for any tax deficiencies arising from the artificial structure; private assets can be seized through civil restitution claims; if the shell is used for money laundering or tax fraud, imprisonment and travel bans apply.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's-Length Violations

Statutory sanction: Under § 162(4) AO, a penalty equal to 10% of the transfer-pricing adjustment, minimum €5,000 and maximum €1,000,000, unless the taxpayer provides reasonable documentation; underlying income is re-determined with deemed profits, interest at 0.5% per month, and additional tax assessments can be raised for up to 10 years; criminal investigation for tax evasion if intent is proven.

Director personal liability: If the managing director signs false transfer-pricing declarations, he or she is personally exposed to criminal liability as an accessory and liable for the resulting corporate taxes; civil and criminal courts may order compensation and travel restrictions to secure proceedings.
Anti-Money-Laundering / Transparency Register Failures

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1,000,000 for serious violations under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG); repeated non-filing can result in dissolution orders; criminal sanctions under § 261 StGB for money laundering if proceeds are concealed; administrative barriers to payment-rail approvals may arise.

Director personal liability: Managing directors must ensure the beneficial ownership report is filed; failure creates personal liability for fines, loss of eligibility to manage commercial entities, and in severe AML cases imprisonment and travel bans.
Withholding Tax and Profit-Repatriation Disobedience

Statutory sanction: The GmbH is jointly and severally liable for unpaid WHT plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge; late-payment interest of 0.5% per month; administrative fines up to €50,000; if intentional, the failure constitutes a criminal offence of evasion of WHT under §§ 370/371 AO.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise distributions without WHT become personally liable to the tax office as 'Täter' of the withholding offence; the corporate veil is pierced to recover taxes, interest and penalties from the managing director's personal assets; travel bans and extradition procedures may be imposed in criminal proceedings.

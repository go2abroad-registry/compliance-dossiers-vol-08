Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt fuer Steuern - BZSt) for federal tax administration, USt-IdNr/VAT-ID issuance, withholding taxes and international matters; local Finanzamt (District Tax Office) at the GmbH's registered seat for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT compliance. Official portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); BZSt portal (www.bzst.de) for VAT-ID and EU VAT refunds; customs/export authority: Generalzolldirektion and local Hauptzollamt via the ATLAS export declaration system.

E-government filing portal

1) Notarize articles of association and appoint managing directors (Geschaeftsfuehrer); 2) open a German bank account and pay in minimum statutory capital (EUR 25,000 nominal, EUR 12,500 paid-up); 3) register the GmbH in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht; 4) after registration, submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER to the local Finanzamt within four weeks of business commencement, thereby obtaining the Steuernummer (corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT number); 5) file a separate application with BZSt for the USt-IdNr (VAT identification number), with identity cross-checked against the Handelsregister and issuance typically completed within 2-4 weeks; 6) obtain an EORI number from the Hauptzollamt for customs and export declarations. A foreign parent or non-resident entity without a permanent establishment obtains direct VAT registration via the same questionnaire; pure non-resident distance sellers may register through the BZSt/Finanzamt or use the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) where eligible.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The German GmbH is a resident corporate taxpayer assessed at entity level: corporate income tax (Koerperschaftsteuer) 15.0%, solidarity surcharge 5.5% of corporate tax (combined 15.825%), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at the statutory 3.5% base rate multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz, resulting in an effective combined income-tax burden of approximately 29% to 33%. Unlimited tax liability applies to worldwide income (no territorial scope), with relief via double tax treaties (credit or exemption method). No general German tax-exempt zone applies to ISIC 4652 electronic-component distribution activities. Profit distributions are subject to 26.375% German withholding tax (25% capital gains tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), reduced under applicable tax treaties.

Available election forms

German law provides no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections for a GmbH: (i) Organschaft (corporate income tax and trade tax group) and (ii) Umsatzsteuer-Organschaft (VAT group), each requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement, financial integration and organizational/economic subordination; (iii) the option to waive VAT exemption for real-estate lettings under Section 9 UStG; (iv) the dividend-exemption election under Section 8b KStG (95% tax-exempt) for qualifying domestic and foreign shareholdings; (v) hybrid-mismatch recharacterization pursuant to Section 4k EStG and ATAD 2, which can override entity classification or payment characterization for cross-border structures; (vi) loss-utilization timing elections restricted by Sections 8c KStG and 8d KStG; and (vii) customs procedural elections for inward/outward processing or customs warehousing. All elections are effected annually through the tax return; no pass-through entity election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration from the first taxable supply for all economic operators in Germany; no general turnover threshold applies to a GmbH or to any corporate entity. The small-entrepreneur exemption (Kleinunternehmer, Section 19 UStG) is available only for individuals and certain small businesses with prior-year turnover up to EUR 25,000 and current-year expected turnover up to EUR 100,000 (2025 thresholds; previously EUR 22,000 / EUR 50,000) and does not apply to a GmbH engaged in ISIC 4652 distribution. Non-resident sellers supplying goods or services into Germany are likewise subject to compulsory registration without a domestic threshold; Union distance sales up to EUR 10,000 may be declared through the OSS regime in the member state of establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available under Section 4 No. 1a and Section 6 UStG when the GmbH dispatches or transports goods (multilayer ceramic capacitors, resistors and other passive electronic components) from the German customs territory into a third country, provided the supplier retains proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) under Sections 8-13 UStDV. Conditions: the goods must leave the EU customs territory within the statutory dispatch period (generally within three months, extendable on application), the customer must be established in a third country, and an electronic customs export declaration with a Movement Reference Number (MRN) must be lodged in ATLAS prior to departure. Closely related offshore services (transport, warehousing, cargo insurance, export packing, inspection and customs clearance) supplied in connection with the exported goods are zero-rated under Section 4 No. 3 UStG when rendered to a non-EU customer or used directly for the export. Domestic or EU B2B sales are standard-rated at 19% (7% reduced rate for qualifying items not applicable to passive components); intra-EU B2B supplies qualify for exemption under Section 6a UStG conditional on a valid EU VAT-ID and full documentary evidence (Gelangensbestaetigung).

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) lodged electronically in ATLAS with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Export accompanying document (EAD) / export summary declaration confirmation
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note or postal/courier transport proof evidencing departure to the third-country destination
  • Commercial invoice (with packing list) stating Incoterms and export sales conditions
  • Inward remittance certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer (bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice or account statement)
  • Proof of arrival of goods in the destination country (customs entry document or equivalent, where available) to complete the Ausfuhrnachweis
  • EORI number registration confirmation issued by the Generalzolldirektion
  • Intercompany transfer-pricing documentation demonstrating arm's length pricing for sales to affiliated foreign buyers

Mandatory e-invoicing regime (E-Rechnung): from 1 January 2025 every German-resident entrepreneur, including the GmbH, must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (EN 16931 format, XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.3+) for domestic B2B transactions; from 1 January 2027 all suppliers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all German B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices. The e-invoice must transmit structured XML data containing the seller and buyer VAT IDs or tax numbers, a sequential invoice number, invoice date, supply date, tax rate, tax amount and the underlying purchase order or contract reference. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital-stamping or government API authorization system for VAT; compliance is verified post-transaction through ELSTER VAT returns, VIES/recapitulative statements, ATLAS customs declarations and the EU-wide payment-data exchange (CESOP/CRIO). The EU ViDA framework is expected to introduce near-real-time digital transaction reporting for intra-EU supplies by 2030.

German tax authorities scrutinize export zero-rating claims through documentary requests under Sections 90, 93 and 98 AO and cross-checks with the ATLAS customs system and the EU Eurofisc network; defective or missing documentation triggers retrospective clawback of the 19% VAT exemption, i.e., reassessment of output VAT with statutory interest of 0.15% per month (1.8% per annum) under Section 233a AO from the end of the tax period in which the exemption was claimed. Assessment periods extend to seven years for tax evasion and ten years in aggravated cases under Section 169 AO. Intentional or reckless false export documentation is a criminal tax offense under Sections 369-370 AO punishable by imprisonment of up to ten years and fines of up to double the evaded tax; grossly negligent shortfalls are subject to fines under Section 378 AO up to EUR 50,000. Separately, Section 21 AWV obliges the exporter to ensure that consideration for exports to third countries is received within eight months of export; the present compliance dossier imposes a stricter mandatory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation hard-stop, breach of which escalates to internal audit, customs risk profiling, acceleration of credit-line financing covenants and potential civil clawback of export VAT benefits in subsequent years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to applicable sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, export-control, tax, and financial-supervisory rules. A German GmbH may generally receive foreign capital, acquire foreign assets, pay suppliers abroad, repatriate profits, repay shareholder loans, and distribute dividends to non-resident shareholders without prior foreign-exchange approval. Dividend and interest payments remain subject to German withholding-tax rules, treaty relief, EU directives where applicable, beneficial-owner requirements, and anti-abuse provisions. Banks may require commercial rationale, source-of-funds evidence, beneficial-owner information, tax documentation, and sanctions screening before executing transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement, compulsory conversion ratio, or statutory deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros or repatriated through a German bank. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts or overseas accounts, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, customs, payment-services, and foreign-exchange statistical reporting requirements. Cross-border payment reports under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation may apply when statutory reporting thresholds and exclusions are met; the company should confirm the applicable threshold, reporting category, filing deadline, and exemption with Deutsche Bundesbank for each reporting period. Export proceeds remain subject to accurate invoicing, VAT and customs treatment, receivables accounting, and evidence of the underlying transaction.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is the competent authority for German foreign-exchange reporting and external-sector statistics. The European Central Bank governs euro-area monetary policy. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises German credit institutions, payment institutions, and electronic-money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational payments and treasury sub-accounts; Low to Medium for statutory GmbH capital escrow and bank-dependent credit facilities. An EU electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, SEPA transfers, international payments, and foreign-currency services within the scope of its authorization, but it is not a bank and customer funds are generally safeguarded rather than covered by the German statutory deposit-guarantee regime. Offshore or non-EEA digital EMIs may face correspondent-bank, licensing, safeguarding, sanctions, data, and German payment-services perimeter constraints. A fintech account should not be treated as a substitute for a German bank capital account unless the formation notary, commercial register process, and counterparties accept it. Credit lines, overdrafts, documentary letters of credit, and trade-finance products are usually more viable through a licensed bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax, producing 15.825% before trade tax. Trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional: the 3.5% statutory base rate is multiplied by the municipality’s assessment factor. The combined burden is commonly approximately 30%, but is municipality-specific.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital income tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax withheld, ordinarily 26.375% in total, before any church tax. A reduced rate or refund may be available under an applicable double tax agreement (DTA), the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, or domestic law, subject to eligibility, anti-abuse rules, and procedural requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For calendar-year taxpayers, returns are generally due by 31 July of the following year if prepared without a tax adviser. Fiscal years differing from the calendar year use the applicable statutory period measured from fiscal year-end. A tax-office request may set a shorter deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under section 90(3) AO, contemporaneous transaction documentation is generally required for cross-border related-party dealings. A Local File is generally required when annual cross-border transactions with related parties exceed EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, subject to statutory rules and exceptions. A Master File is generally required for a multinational group where consolidated revenue in the preceding fiscal year is at least EUR 100 million. Documentation must be submitted within the statutory response period, generally 30 days following a tax-authority request in an external audit; special rules may require shorter or event-based submission.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no general regime formally designated as Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). German transfer-pricing, controlled-foreign-company, treaty anti-abuse, beneficial-ownership, and general anti-abuse rules can nevertheless challenge arrangements lacking commercial rationale, decision-making, functions, assets, or risk control. A German GmbH must maintain accurate books and evidence that its related-party pricing reflects the arm’s-length principle under section 1 of the Foreign Tax Act (Außensteuergesetz).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax filings

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 AO is punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax, interest, and surcharges remain recoverable. A company may face an administrative fine under section 30 of the Administrative Offences Act (OWiG), subject to applicable statutory limits and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal criminal liability and tax liability under sections 34 and 69 AO where statutory duties are culpably breached. Criminal consequences can include imprisonment; corporate veil piercing is not automatic. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty and require a separate legal basis.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and undocumented related-party transactions

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may adjust taxable income, impose interest and late-payment surcharges, and apply documentation-related surcharges under section 162(4) AO. Depending on the facts, penalties may be based on the statutory minimum and maximum or a percentage of the adjustment amount.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face civil tax liability for culpable breaches of tax duties and criminal or administrative exposure if intentional participation or other statutory elements are established. Personal liability is not automatic merely because an adjustment is made.
Dividend or other withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may be assessed for tax that should have been withheld, together with interest and surcharges; inaccurate or intentional conduct may result in fines or criminal proceedings under applicable tax provisions.

Director personal liability: A managing director may be personally liable under section 69 AO for culpable failure to discharge withholding and remittance duties. Criminal liability depends on the director’s conduct and mental state.
Abusive treaty relief, conduit arrangements, or artificial ownership

Statutory sanction: Relief may be denied or recovered, with tax, interest, and surcharges; tax evasion or administrative-offence penalties may apply if the statutory elements are met.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable where they knowingly cause false filings or culpably breach tax duties. No automatic veil piercing or travel ban follows from a treaty-relief denial.
Insolvency-related tax non-payment and misuse of company assets

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include tax recovery, insolvency-law liability, director disqualification, civil damages, and criminal sanctions where insolvency or other offences are established.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal liability under tax, company, and insolvency law for culpable breaches, including failure to file for insolvency when required. Criminal penalties depend on the offence and facts; no general automatic travel ban applies.
Money laundering and sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Money-laundering offences under section 261 of the Criminal Code (StGB) and sanctions offences under applicable German and EU law can result in substantial fines, confiscation, and imprisonment; the applicable penalty depends on the specific conduct and statutory provision.

Director personal liability: Directors and other responsible individuals may be prosecuted or fined for personal participation or culpable supervisory failures. Asset freezes, travel restrictions, or imprisonment require the relevant legal basis and are not automatic consequences of a tax filing failure.

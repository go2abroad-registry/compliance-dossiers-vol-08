Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under GmbHG §§5 and 7, the minimum share capital is EUR 25,000. At least one quarter of the nominal amount of each cash share must be paid in by the time of application, and the aggregate paid-up cash contributions must in all cases reach at least EUR 12,500, being 50% of the statutory minimum capital. These funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account opened in the name of the company in formation (GmbH i.G.) at a German or EU credit institution, and the managing director must obtain an unqualified bank confirmation that the funds are at the company's free disposal. Contributions in kind must be fully transferred and provided before registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office, and the local revenue office (Finanzamt) competent for the registered seat; official portal: www.elster.de (ELSTER)

E-government filing portal

After notarial incorporation and registration in the Handelsregister, the competent Finanzamt automatically issues a Steuernummer (corporate income tax/trade tax number) for the GmbH. A separate Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID) must be applied for via the BZSt using Form USt 1 W (for foreign entrepreneurs) or the domestic registration procedure (Form USt 1) for the GmbH's intra-Community and export transactions. The application must state the taxable business activities, the place of supply, and the effective date of commencement. For foreign entities without a domestic establishment, the BZSt registers them for VAT on a simplified basis using the online portal 'Bescheinigungsverfahren' when they supply taxable digital services to German non-business customers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Germany. A GmbH is always a taxable corporation (Körperschaft) on entity level for corporate income tax and trade tax, and as a VAT taxable person (Unternehmer). Election may be made to treat the GmbH as an Organgesellschaft (financially, economically, and organisationally integrated subsidiary) within a VAT and corporate income tax group (Organschaft) under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG and § 14 KStG. The EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) hybrid mismatch rules restrict deductions for payments treated as deductible in Germany but not included in the useful information of the payee jurisdiction; no separate hybrid election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and projected EUR 100,000 in the current year for the domestic small-entrepreneur exemption (§ 19 UStG); for non-established foreign suppliers, any taxable supply in Germany triggers mandatory VAT registration with no de minimis threshold. EU distance selling thresholds (EUR 10,000) apply only to intra-Community supplies of goods by EU-established suppliers; the Mini One Stop Shop (MOSS/OSS) optional scheme applies to cross-border B2C digital services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: physicial goods exported to a non-EU destination (third country) qualify for 0% VAT (Ausfuhrlieferung, § 6 UStG), provided the goods actually leave the EU within 3 months of the order (extendable on evidence) and the exporter retains verified proof of export. Merchandise delivered to EU destinations is not zero-rated but may be zero-rated in the destination country under intra-Community supply rules with the customer's VAT ID. Services: B2B services to a non-EU customer are outside the scope of German VAT (reverse charge applies) if the place of supply is the customer's location; B2C digital services (vertical micro-drama streaming) are zero-rated for export if the recipient is not resident or habitually located in Germany, subject to validity checks and the OSS/distance-selling rules. Services physically performed outside the EU are always zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and Export Accompanying Document (EAD)
  • Customs export confirmation (Ausfuhrbestätigung) stamped or digitally certified by the customs office of exit
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) proving the goods left the EU
  • Commercial invoice and export contract (sale, proforma, delivery terms Incoterms)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment from the foreign buyer/recipient

From 1 January 2025, domestic B2B invoices in Germany must be issued and transmitted electronically in a structured, machine-readable format (e.g., XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.0 / Factur-X) via EDI, Peppol network, or equivalent, with transition periods until end-2027 for full compliance. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or government tax authority pre-clearance of invoices in Germany; e-invoice data must be made available to tax authorities upon audit under § 14 UStG and § 27 UStG.

All export zero-rating documentation must be retained for 10 years (§ 14b UStG). If the Finanzamt determines that the export conditions are not fully substantiated, the zero-rated transaction is retrospectively reclassified as a taxable supply, and the full German VAT becomes due immediately, with default interest under § 233a AO at 6% per annum. Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating (e.g., fictitious exports or forged bills of lading) constitutes VAT evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment for up to 10 years, fines up to EUR 500,000 under § 379 AO (tax endangering), and a civil clawback of all input VAT incorrectly refunded, plus a 10% surcharge on the evaded tax under § 329 AO if late filing is materially implicated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. As a member of the euro area, Germany applies unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles 63–66 TFEU and Section 1 AWG. There is no government approval requirement for inbound capital injections, profit remittances, loan servicing, or trade payments. Outbound dividends from a GmbH are subject to German withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% aggregate), which is reduced under an applicable double tax treaty or, where the shareholder is an EEA corporate entity holding at least 10% for 12 months, exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU). No administrative hurdles exist beyond standard corporate documentation; the euro is freely tradable across all counterparties, and no month-end conversion locks or prioritisation rules apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio on export proceeds, including Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, and Stripe settlements received by a German GmbH. The only binding requirement is statistical reporting by the receiving bank (or the account holder, where the bank is not the reporting agent) under Sections 67–68 AWV: (i) cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five business days; (ii) receivables/payables held in foreign currency exceeding EUR 5,000 at month-end must be reported quarterly in the external balance-of-payments statistics. USD app-store settlements can therefore be retained, wired onward, or converted at free market rates.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank, Frankfurt am Main, operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank); the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises banking and anti-money-laundering compliance; Germany's foreign exchange administration is governed by the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV), with the Bundesbank acting as the statistical registration authority for external payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational settlements; Low for capital-escrow or credit facilities. BaFin-licensed e-money institutions (or EU EMIs under passporting, e.g., Wise Europe Branch, Revolut Bank UAB Ireland) can provide the GmbH with a German IBAN, fast SEPA (SCT Inst) clearing, and low-cost cross-border USD/EUR conversion. Material legal constraints: (i) e-money balances are not deposits in the meaning of the German Deposit Guarantee Act (Einlagensicherungsgesetz), hence no deposit-protection coverage; (ii) an EMI cannot issue the statutory confirmation required by the notary that the minimum share capital (EUR 25,000) is at the free disposal of the managing director; (iii) USD correspondent clearing is indirect, increasing disintermediation and account-freezing risk; (iv) a non-EEA offshore EMI accessing a German IBAN without a local licence violates Section 1 of the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and is therefore legally non-viable. Recommended use: high-frequency, low-unit creator payouts; not for the primary treasury account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder loans are routed by SWIFT MT103 from the parent's account to the GmbH's German IBAN. For the initial minimum capital (EUR 25,000), the bank places a blocking notice (Sperrvermerk) and issues a confirmation to the notary that the funds are at the free disposal of the managing director. Purpose coding: use SWIFT purpose code 'CAPL' or the bank's equivalent (e.g., 'Einlage/Stammkapital'); if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500, the receiving bank automatically files the AWV general statistics report with Deutsche Bundesbank using the statistical code for non-resident payments (Zahlungen von/an Gebietsfremde). Standalone FDI filings under the Investment Screening Act are not required because ISIC 5911 (vertical micro-drama streaming) is outside the sensitive dual-use sectors, but the company should record the shareholder list and capital movements in its quarterly Bundesbank balance-of-payments statistics report.Clearing rails selected by destination: (a) EEA content creators and production houses: SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) up to EUR 100,000 with IBAN-only execution; (b) non-EEA suppliers (e.g., Chinese subtitle/dubbing studios): SWIFT MT103 via the bank's correspondent network, with a five-business-day netting cycle and per-ticket cap of EUR 250,000 to preserve straight-through processing; (c) USD-denominated Apple/Google treasury payments: bulk MT103 remittances referencing the settlement statements to permit trade-reconciliation; (d) large worldwide licensing/distribution deals exceeding EUR 1 million: Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) or demand guarantees and standby letters of credit where performance obligations exist. No customs documentation is intrinsic to micro-drama digital trade, but where payments fund Chinese production costs, the Chinese counterpart's bank requires the underlying cross-border service contract and possibly a tax payment certificate to comply with PRC FX regulations.Outright deliverable EUR/USD forward contracts (FXA) with tenors up to 24 months, executed under the ISDA 2013 FX Definitions or the German Master Agreement for Financial Futures Transactions (Rahmenvertrag für Finanztermingeschäfte); no mandatory initial margin where the bank counterparty is a non-financial end-user below the EMIR clearing threshold, but transaction reporting to an authorised trade repository (DTCC Data Repository or TRAX) is due within T+1.FX rollovers (swap rollovers) extending a maturing EUR/USD hedging contract without physical delivery; subject to the same master agreement, the bank's daily margin line, and asset-and-liability approvals by the managing director.European-style cash-settled EUR/USD currency options and zero-cost collars (range forwards) hedging the forecast Apple/Google USD settlement curve; collateral posting is governed by the ISDA Credit Support Annex where two-way collateral has been agreed.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in offshore renminbi (USD/CNH) and emerging-market currencies (e.g., THB, VND) to hedge Chinese and Southeast-Asian production costs where onshore deliverability is restricted; execution is limited to Frankfurt/London/Hong Kong licensed counterparties, and all trades must be captured in the Bundesbank derivative statistics report where the contract notional exceeds the reporting threshold.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for any USD term-loan funding of streaming-platform expansion; permitted under German supervisory law but in practice de-scoped in Stage 4 because the micro-drama venture operates on equity and short-term intercompany loans rather than external USD debt.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for operational settlements; Low for capital-escrow or credit facilities. BaFin-licensed e-money institutions (or EU EMIs under passporting, e.g., Wise Europe Branch, Revolut Bank UAB Ireland) can provide the GmbH with a German IBAN, fast SEPA (SCT Inst) clearing, and low-cost cross-border USD/EUR conversion. Material legal constraints: (i) e-money balances are not deposits in the meaning of the German Deposit Guarantee Act (Einlagensicherungsgesetz), hence no deposit-protection coverage; (ii) an EMI cannot issue the statutory confirmation required by the notary that the minimum share capital (EUR 25,000) is at the free disposal of the managing director; (iii) USD correspondent clearing is indirect, increasing disintermediation and account-freezing risk; (iv) a non-EEA offshore EMI accessing a German IBAN without a local licence violates Section 1 of the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and is therefore legally non-viable. Recommended use: high-frequency, low-unit creator payouts; not for the primary treasury account.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office (www.bzst.de) for VAT-ID issuance and central administration; local Finanzamt (tax office) with jurisdiction over the GmbH's registered seat for corporate income tax, trade tax, payroll tax, and VAT assessment; ELSTER (www.elster.de) is the mandatory electronic filing portal for all German tax returns and VAT advance returns.

E-government filing portal

Within one month after formation/commencement of business, the GmbH's managing director (Geschäftsführer) must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (§138 AO) electronically via ELSTER with the competent local Finanzamt, disclosing corporate structure, shareholders (incl. foreign ultimate beneficial owners), business purpose (enterprise SaaS / ISIC 6201), and expected turnover. The Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer (statutory income-tax reference number, 10–11 digits) used for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT advance returns. For cross-border B2B SaaS transactions, the GmbH separately applies to BZSt (§27a UStG) for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr. / EU VAT ID, format DE + 9 digits), typically within 4–6 weeks. BZSt also issues the new Wirtschafts-Identifikationsnummer (economic identity number) under §139c AO to all registered businesses for use in domestic data exchange. Non-resident shareholders do not require any separate national tax ID to hold a GmbH; however, the GmbH must include non-resident directors/shareholders in the §138 AO questionnaire and in annual tax filings. Foreign parent entities receiving dividends may invoke the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (or German treaty) at source, but this does not affect registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists under German law. A GmbH is mandatorily subject to: (i) Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) at 15%; (ii) Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge) at 5.5% of corporate income tax (effective 0.825%); (iii) Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at municipal rates (average Hebesatz ≈ 400%, effective ≈ 14–17%); aggregate headline burden ≈ 30–32% of pre-tax profits. Available elections: (a) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under §§14–19 KStG and §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG – permits vertical consolidation of a 75%-owned German subsidiary into a German parent for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT, with profit and loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag); (b) FIFO vs. weighted-average inventory (no inventory relevance for SaaS); (c) thin-capitalization/interest deduction limitation (Zinsschranke) elections; (d) hybrid mismatch adjustments under §4k EStG are mandatory, no elective opt-out. No territorial tax-exempt zone applies to standard GmbH activity; all profits are worldwide-taxable with foreign-tax credit or treaty exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold applies to a German GmbH: as an 'Ist-Unternehmer' (entrepreneur by legal form), the GmbH is required to register for VAT and file VAT returns from the moment taxable activity commences, irrespective of turnover volume. The small-enterprise exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung, §19 UStG, previous-year €25,000 / current-year €100,000) is legally unavailable to corporations. The €10,000 EU distance-sales threshold is irrelevant for B2B SaaS supplied to German business customers; for EU B2C digital services, OSS registration (non-EU scheme/union scheme) applies only if the recipient is a consumer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

B2B enterprise SaaS is not 'zero-rated' in the classic goods-export sense but is treated as OUT-OF-SCOPE (nicht steuerbar) or exempt with input VAT recovery: (a) supply to EU business customers (with valid EU VAT ID, verified via VIES): place of supply is in the customer's member state per §3a(2) UStG, German VAT not chargeable, reverse-charge applies to customer (§13b UStG); invoice must state 'Steuerschuldnerschaft des Leistungsempfängers' and the customer's VAT ID. (b) supply to non-EU business customers (third-country established recipients): place of supply is outside Germany, no German VAT applies (§3a(2) UStG) – requires documented evidence of the customer's business establishment (not merely invoicing address). (c) intra-EU B2C distance sales of digital services may be declared via OSS union scheme at the destination-country rate (not an export; optional). (d) exports of physical goods (if SaaS is embedded in tangible media, e.g., on-premise servers, but generally not applicable) qualify for true 0% rate under §4 No. 1(a) and §6 UStG subject to customs export proof. Input VAT on German costs (hosting, infrastructure, marketing, legal) remains recoverable under normal rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) and Export Accompanying Document (ABD) – only for physical-goods export, NOT for pure SaaS digital services
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Handelsrechnung (Commercial Invoice) – only where tangible media or hardware is exported
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT-payment evidence from the B2B customer (Stripe/Wise settlement statements satisfying Deutsche Bundesbank AWV reporting §59/§60)
  • Customer VAT ID and VIES system confirmation (for EU customers) or third-country tax registration certificate / business registry extract proving place of establishment
  • Written SaaS agreement containing governing law, place of performance of digital services, and customer commercial establishment
  • Technical/usage logs evidencing service performance to the foreign recipient (location of server access alone is not decisive; establishment of recipient is decisive)

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) per §14 UStG and the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): (i) from 01.01.2025 every domestic B2B supplier must be able to receive e-invoices structured in EN 16931 formats (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x); (ii) from 01.01.2027 issuers with prior-year turnover exceeding €800,000 must issue e-invoices; (iii) from 01.01.2028 all domestic B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices. Invoices must be transmitted electronically (E-Mail with structured attachment or EDI/Peppol network) and contain mandatory fields (Fiscal number, USt-IdNr., service date, VAT rate, reverse-charge reference where applicable). Germany does NOT operate real-time fiscal clearance/crypto-stamping (unlike Italy's SDI); compliance is enforced through periodic VAT returns: monthly Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung if prior-year VAT liability > €7,500; otherwise quarterly; annual VAT return (§18 UStG) mandatory. Failure to transmit via validated channels after the 2028 deadline may render invoices invalid and expose the issuer to assessment risks; BZSt also mandates payment data reporting (Zahlungsdaten-Meldepflicht) for deferred VAT liabilities from 2026.

German tax audit (Außenprüfung / Betriebsprüfung) targets out-of-scope and reverse-charge treatment of SaaS: the Finanzamt reviews inbound payment records, customer VAT-ID validity, place-of-supply location evidence, and invoice formalities. Deficiencies trigger retrospective VAT assessment (Nachforderung) at 19% on gross consideration plus interest of 0.5% per month (Zinsschranke rate 0.15%/month under §233a AO currently 0.15% per month since 2019 – effective 1.8% p.a.). Incomplete AWV export documentation or failure to settle foreign receivables within 6 months constitutes an administrative offense (Ordnungswidrigkeit) fine up to €30,000 and delays repatriation proof. Negligent or intentional false zero-rating/out-of-scope claims on invoices constitute tax evasion under §370 AO (imprisonment up to 5 years, fine up to €50,000, forfeiture of refunded input VAT) and VAT fraud proceedings under §26a UStG. Misuse of a customer's VAT ID or involvement in a carousel scheme triggers joint and several liability under §25d UStG, immediate VAT charge, possible USt-IdNr. revocation by BZSt, and retrospective clawback of all improperly deducted input VAT for the audit window (typically 4 years, extended to 10 years for deliberate evasion).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility under EU law (TFEU Articles 63 et seq.). There are no exchange controls restricting the conversion of EUR into foreign currency or the remittance of profits. A GmbH may freely distribute dividends to non-resident shareholders after formal approval by the shareholders' meeting and satisfaction of tax compliance obligations. Cross-border dividend remittances are subject to a 25% withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%) unless reduced under an applicable double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% for qualifying corporate shareholders with proper certificate of residence). No prior central bank approval is required for profit repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender requirement, statutory conversion ratio, or time limit for sales-generated export receipts in Germany. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or any foreign currency at the exporter's discretion. Under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV), all cross-border payments and receipts exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five business days for statistical purposes, but this reporting obligation does not restrict the use of the funds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Central Bank of the Federal Republic of Germany), in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for operational treasury and collections; Low for statutory share capital deposit or as a sole bank. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Mercury) are viable for B2B SaaS collections and disbursements in multiple currencies, but they are not licensed as German credit institutions under the German Banking Act (KWG). They operate under EU e-money licences and are not covered by the German deposit guarantee scheme. BaFin may require a no-action confirmation if an EMI actively solicits German clients. A fully regulated German bank account must remain in place for the mandatory initial capital deposit, corporate presence, and fiduciary completeness.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium for operational treasury and collections; Low for statutory share capital deposit or as a sole bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag (effective CIT 15.825%); trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) additionally applies at 3.5% of taxable earnings multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (typically 200%–500%), producing a combined effective burden of approximately 29%–33% in major cities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag) on cash or deemed distributions to non-resident shareholders; reduced to 0% for distributions to a qualified EU/EEA corporate parent holding at least 10% for an uninterrupted period of 12 months under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (§ 43b EStG).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 for cross-border controlled transactions and EUR 600,000 for domestic controlled transactions in an annual audit period trigger the duty to create and produce Local File and Master File documentation within 30–60 days of an audit request (GAufzV, § 90(4) AO). For corporate groups exceeding EUR 100 million group turnover, the German implementation of BEPS Action 13 requires cross-border CbC reporting through BZSt.

Economic substance scope: Germany enforces substance through § 42 AO (abuse of law), ATAD-compliant GAAR, and the treaty-shopping limitation in the Principal Purpose Test; an entity must have an office, qualified staff, business equipment, a valid service agreement, and actual management decisions in Germany. Treasury functions and intercompany financing of a GmbH without staff or commercial purpose are recharacterized as shell structures; the Federal Fiscal Court denies treaty benefits and applies § 8a KStG (interest deduction restrictions) in such cases.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years or a monetary fine of up to EUR 50,000; aggravated cases (fraud involving > EUR 50,000 tax per year) incur imprisonment of six months to ten years; interest on the back-tax is charged at 0.15% per month from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Managing directors become personally liable for the national heritage under §§ 34, 69 AO for intentionally or negligently caused tax shortfalls; the tax office may issue a Haftungsbescheid (liability notice) for the unpaid tax plus interest; insolvency triggers criminal exposure under § 15a InsO for the delay in filing for bankruptcy, with travel bans and reputational collapse.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment against the substance principle with denial of treaty benefits and, in e-commerce context, the recharacterization of German-source income as a permanent establishment with back taxes of approximately 30% of the taxable base plus interest.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution for unlawful distributions under § 64 GmbHG; private assets are attachable for the amount distributed from the GmbH's equity; criminal consequences under § 82 GmbHG (breach of capital maintenance rules) can result in imprisonment up to three years.
Anti-Money-Laundering and Sanctions Violations (GwG/AML)

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fine up to EUR 5 million or 10% of the entity's total annual global turnover under GwG, whichever is higher; seizure of assets; revocation of the business license under § 35 GewO.

Director personal liability: Director-level criminal liability with imprisonment of up to five years for knowingly processing funds from predicate offences (§ 261 StGB); exclusion from managing any company for up to five years and travel bans within the EU when legal suits are triggered.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Violations

Statutory sanction: Under § 162 AO, the tax office may presume a higher taxable income and apply a penalty of at least 5% of the adjusted income (up to EUR 250,000 in extraordinary cases); additional late-payment interest accrues monthly.

Director personal liability: Managing directors and tax officers are jointly and severally liable for penalty payments if they intentionally set non-compliant intercompany agreements or sign incorrect tax returns with the knowledge of an artificially allocated profit level.

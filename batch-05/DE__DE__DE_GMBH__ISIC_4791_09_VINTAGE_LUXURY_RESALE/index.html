Competent local district court acting as Commercial Register court (Registergericht at the Amtsgericht) for the GmbH registered office.

Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory share capital is EUR 25,000. Before registration, at least EUR 12,500 must be paid in cash, and each cash contribution must satisfy the statutory subscription and payment requirements. No statutory escrow account is required. The company normally opens a corporate bank account after notarisation and deposits the required capital before the managing directors certify capital availability. Contributions in kind require additional documentation and valuation disclosures.

Funding & unlock path: For ordinary office, retail, and storage premises, obtain landlord and building-use confirmation and complete any required trade-use or change-of-use review. Fire safety obligations include escape routes, emergency lighting where required, extinguishers, storage-layout controls, and compliance with the applicable state building code and fire authority requirements. Structural load and racking inspections are required where storage systems or inventory loads affect building safety. Occupational safety duties arise under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (ArbSchG), Workplace Ordinance (ArbStättV), and applicable DGUV rules; appoint a competent occupational-safety service and document risk assessments.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and centrally administered matters; local tax offices (Finanzämter) for income, corporate, trade, and VAT tax assessment; electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned German GmbH must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the competent Finanzamt to obtain a Steuernummer (tax number) before recurring VAT and income/corporate tax filings. The questionnaire must specify legal form, shareholders, intended activities, expected turnover, and assumed tax liabilities. Separately, the German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) must be applied for via the BZSt online portal. If the GmbH exports goods, it must also obtain an EORI customs registration. Non-resident suppliers without domestic establishment may be required to designate an authorized fiscal representative for VAT purposes in certain cases.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under German law. A GmbH is automatically a German tax-resident corporation subject to corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT as an independent taxable person. Available statutory elections include VAT and corporate tax fiscal unity ('Organschaft') by creating a financial, economic, and organizational integration with a controlling 75% shareholder; the profit-and-loss pooling election under Section 14 KStG; the trade tax apportionment/allocation election; and treaty-based reduction or exemption elections through the German permanent establishment tax return process. Hybrid mismatch regulatory elections are not elective; German anti-hybrid rules apply coercively.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany provides no general monetary VAT registration threshold for non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies; registration is required when the taxable activity commences. For domestic micro-businesses, the small-business scheme under Section 19 UStG exempts turnover up to EUR 22,000 in the prior year and EUR 50,000 in the current year. For EU-distance sales and certain cross-border B2C services, the EU-wide OSS registration threshold is EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A tax-exempt supply with full input VAT deduction (effectively 0% output VAT) is available under Sections 4(1)(1)(a) and 6 UStG for physical export of goods by the seller to a third-country destination, provided title and the essential transport risk have passed outside the EU, the goods leave Germany within the statutory period, and the supplier holds an approved customs export document as evidence. Intra-Community supplies to another EU Member State may also be exempt under Section 6a UStG where the customer provides a valid EU VAT ID and Intrastat of imported goods and the goods are transported to another Member State. Cross-border B2B services to non-resident recipients are generally outside the scoope of German VAT via reverse charge, subject to place-of-supply rules; certain export-related ancillary services may also be exempt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS Ausfuhranmeldung / Export Accompanying Document)
  • Commercial Invoice evidencing the export transaction value
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (and road consignment note where applicable)
  • Proof of Export (Ausfuhrnachweis) or supplier's certificate for subsequent customs export procedures
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT credit confirmation evidencing foreign payment within the statutory repatriation window

Since 1 January 2025, all German B2B taxpayers must be able to receive structured e-invoices compliant with the EN 16931 standard (XRechnung or hybrid ZUGFeRD format); mandatory issuance is phased in from 2027 for taxpayers with annual turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and from 2028 for others. Germany does not currently operate transaction-level real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory tax stamping at the point of sale. E-invoices must be transmitted through interoperable channels such as Peppol/B2B EDI or structured PDF and must be retained in a tax-audit-compliant manner under the GoBD requirements for at least 10 years.

German tax authorities require documentation and proof of export to be available immediately for audit; missing or defective proof causes the zero-rating to be denied and the turnover to be retroactively assessed at 19% VAT with default-interest additions (0.5% per month under Section 233a AO). Deliberate false documentation triggers criminal tax evasion liability under Section 370 AO with imprisonment up to five years or fines, tax-fraud surcharges under Sections 378/379 AO, and an extended ten-year assessment period for intent. In persistent misuse cases, the Finanzamt also denies input VAT refunds and may issue penalty assessments up to EUR 25,000 plus recovery of all clawed-back VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany benefits from full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU provisions on free movement of capital (TFEU Art. 63). There are no general exchange controls, no licensing for dividend, interest, royalty, or principal remittances, and no restrictions on profit distribution to non-resident shareholders. Compliance must be ensured with (i) EU-wide sanctions regulations, (ii) German anti-money-laundering (GwG) screening, and (iii) Bundesbank statistical reporting thresholds. Cross-border payments or capital movements equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 require AWV statistical declaration on a monthly basis; otherwise, profit remittance is unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement. Exporters may retain and freely use foreign currency cash balances. However, any export receipt or payment exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank via the AWV statistical reporting system. The company must retain underlying trade documentation (invoices, contracts, customs declarations) for at least five years as required by German commercial law (HGB §§ 257, 257a).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (monetary authority and foreign exchange statistics reporter under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz/Außenwirtschaftsverordnung); the European Central Bank sets monetary policy for the Eurozone; the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action supervises trade sanctions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) with an active BaFin or EU passport can be used as payment service providers for transactional settlement and escrow operations, but corporate liquidity, shareholder capital, and tax-relevant reserves must reside at a full-license credit institution. Offshore, non-passported EMIs are practically unavailable for a German GmbH due to strict GwG § 18 due diligence and BaFin supervisory expectations. If an EMI is selected, it must maintain segregated client funds, provide German-language contractual documentation, and support transparent reconciliation for the German tax and audit trail.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial owner located in a non-EU high-risk third country as listed under EU or FATF criteria; non-resident shareholder structure with opaque trust or foundation.

Root cause: Beneficial owner located in a non-EU high-risk third country as listed under EU or FATF criteria; non-resident shareholder structure with opaque trust or foundation.

Defense evidence: Provide a full Commercial Register excerpt showing the GmbH’s corporate tree, certified UBO declarations for the German Transparency Register, tax residence certificates for all shareholders, a bank reference letter from a reputable foreign financial institution, an explanation of the capital origin, and audited financial statements of the ultimate parent if applicable.
High-value secondhand luxury goods create a high risk of money laundering, concealment of provenance, and the use of third-party payment channels.

Root cause: High-value secondhand luxury goods create a high risk of money laundering, concealment of provenance, and the use of third-party payment channels.

Defense evidence: Present a written AML/KYC policy tailored to luxury resale, including customer due diligence files for every consignor and buyer, proof of provenance (original receipts, authenticity certificates, prior auction records), a documented transaction value chain, and a transaction monitoring procedure for unusual high-velocity or split-payment activity.
Undisclosed or insufficiently documented source of the GmbH’s share capital or shareholder capital increase.

Root cause: Undisclosed or insufficiently documented source of the GmbH’s share capital or shareholder capital increase.

Defense evidence: Submit the notarial formation deed or capital increase resolution, bank payment receipts from the contributing shareholder’s account (proving traceability), a capital provenance statement, and a written explanation of the commercial rationale for the capital injection.
Cross-border payment inflows from unknown or unregulated third-party accounts, e.g., digital wallet payouts or informal broker settlement accounts.

Root cause: Cross-border payment inflows from unknown or unregulated third-party accounts, e.g., digital wallet payouts or informal broker settlement accounts.

Defense evidence: Build a clearing-rails policy that prohibits unconventional payout rails; maintain full counterparty identification for all suppliers and consignors, execute all transactions exclusively via SEPA/SWIFT MT103 with reference to a contractual invoice, and submit a ledger with beneficiary ownership confirmation for every transaction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital contributions must be initiated via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA SCT in Euro, using the purpose code 'CATT' (Capital Transfer) for equity injections or 'TRAD' for trade-related funding. The payment's 'Verwendungszweck' (reference field) must explicitly refer to the capital increase resolution or the shareholder loan agreement, e.g., 'Einzahlung auf das Stammkapital' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen'. The bank must verify the remitter is the designated shareholder and not a third party. For statutory share capital above EUR 12,500, the managing director must file a commercial register declaration of the contribution; for AWV reporting, the company or bank must file the 'Zahlungsbilanzstatistik' notice with the Deutsche Bundesbank within seven calendar days of receipt. All inbound transfers must undergo sanctions and embargo screening; remittances from sanctioned jurisdictions or blocked persons will be automatically rejected.Supplier and consignor settlements are executed primarily through SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT or SCT Inst) for Euro-denominated payments within EUR / EEA, and via SWIFT MT103 for cross-border non-Euro payments. For large-card-scheme or online marketplace settlements, platform-based escrow systems (e.g., Stripe, EscrowPay) are used and must settle directly to the GmbH’s operational bank account via a merchant-of-record arrangement with a full banking partner. For consignment import transactions requiring trade finance, a documentary commercial letter of credit (MT700, MT707) can be established with the procurement bank for high-value vintage items; all outbound payments must include the contract or invoice reference, correct purpose code (e.g., 'TRAD' for goods, 'COMT' for commissions), and undergo real-time OFAC and EU sanctions screening.Plain-vanilla foreign exchange forwards with a tenor of up to 12 months, documented by a real underlying trade exposure (USD or GBP receivables/payables).FX swaps (simultaneous spot/forward or two different value-date swaps) for liquidity management and settlement covering.Plain-vanilla FX call and put options, with or without premiums, used exclusively for trade-related exposure hedging.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only for restricted or non-convertible currencies not freely tradeable in the European market, provided the bank authorizes the instrument and the exposure is fully documented.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 15.825%); municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) of 14–17% yields a combined effective rate of approximately 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%). Reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the German distributing GmbH and the non-resident corporate parent meet the 10% participation threshold and the parent holds the shares for at least 12 months; otherwise treaty rates may apply (e.g., 5% under most German DTAs for qualifying 10% holdings).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 for cross-border goods transactions and EUR 500,000 for cross-border service payments, intragroup financial transactions and royalty payments with related parties (per fiscal year). These thresholds determine the mandatory duty to prepare a Local File under § 90 (3) AO; a Master File is required for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 100 million, and CbC Reporting applies at EUR 750 million group revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations regime, but applies substantive substance requirements via the place-of-effective-management test, § 42 AO General Anti-Abuse Rule, and the BEPS principal purpose test for treaty benefits. A pure shell GmbH without physical premises, qualified staff, independent decision-making, and actual operational activities in Germany faces recharacterisation of income, denial of treaty relief, and potential criminal liability for directors.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Under § 370 AO, imprisonment up to 5 years or a monetary fine. In aggravated cases (commercial or gang-related) imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years. Financial penalty is the amount of tax evaded plus interest of 6% p.a.; separate late-filing penalties apply.

Director personal liability: Managing directors and economic owners are personally liable for unpaid taxes under § 69 AO where they knowingly or negligently breach tax duties. Criminal conviction leads to civil restitution, asset seizure, travel bans, and in severe cases extradition in EU cross-border investigations.
Shell company / sham arrangement

Statutory sanction: § 42 AO allows the tax authorities to disregard the corporate structure and tax income as attributable to the actual beneficiary. This results in retroactive denial of interest deductions, participation exemption, and treaty withholding rates. Additional penalty under § 378 AO if the mischaracterisation was due to negligence.

Director personal liability: Directors can be made personally liable for corporate tax liabilities if the sham entity was used to evade taxes; courts may pierce the veil of the GmbH, ordering shareholders to satisfy unpaid liabilities. A finding of tax evasion triggers registration of imprisonment sanctions.
Money laundering / AML non-compliance (Geldwäsche)

Statutory sanction: Under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG), regulatory fines of up to EUR 1 million for minor violations, or up to 10% of the legal entity's total annual turnover for serious violations. Criminal money laundering under § 261 StGB; the attempt is punishable and can demand imprisonment up to 10 years in serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML officers face personal monetary fines and imprisonment. Upon conviction, courts can prohibit them from serving as directors for up to 5 years; travel bans may be imposed in the course of criminal proceedings and assets frozen to secure fines.
Transfer pricing compliance failure

Statutory sanction: Under § 162 AO, failure to provide documentation leads to the presumption that the arm's length principle has been violated; income will be adjusted upward. A penalty of 5% to 10% of the adjusted income is imposed (minimum EUR 5,000) if documentation is absent or materially incomplete. On a show cause order, extended documentation deadlines are lost and a fine of EUR 100 per day up to EUR 1 million may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate tax returns or fail to enforce transfer pricing documentation are personally liable under §§ 34, 69 AO for resulting additional tax, interest, and penalties. In cases of intentional price manipulation, criminal liability for tax evasion under § 370 AO extends to the directors.

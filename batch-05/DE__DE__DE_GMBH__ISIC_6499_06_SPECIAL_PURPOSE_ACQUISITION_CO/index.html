Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum statutory share capital (Stammkapital) is EUR 25,000. Before registration, cash contributions must be actually paid in full coverage of at least 50% of the minimum capital (EUR 12,500), with each founding shareholder contributing at least 25% of the nominal amount of their subscribed shares, into a blocked escrow account (Anderkonto or Sonderkonto) opened for the company-in-formation; the Managing Directors must give the statutory assurance that the capital is at their free disposal and free of return-flow arrangements. Contributions in kind require a valuation report under GmbHG § 5(4).

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certification (Feuersicherheitsbescheinigung) by the local fire brigade (Feuerwehr) confirming emergency exits, extinguishers and alarm adequacy for office occupancy; Structural load inspections (Standsicherheitsnachweis) certified by the local building supervisory authority (Bauaufsicht) for the leased office floors; Workplace clearance (Arbeitsstättenbescheinigung) by the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt under the Arbeitsstättenverordnung covering lighting, ventilation and sanitary facilities; mandatory membership in the employers' liability insurance association (Berufsgenossenschaft — VBG) with accident-prevention clearance for administrative workplaces.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt – Federal Central Tax Office) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and central withholding matters; competent local Finanzamt (district tax office) for corporation tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment (Steuernummer). Primary electronic portal: ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung – www.elster.de); customs/export portal: ATLAS via Generalzolldirektion (www.zoll.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-resident shareholder establishing a German GmbH must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (Questionnaire on Tax Registration) with the competent Finanzamt within four weeks of business commencement (Sec. 138 AO) either via ELSTER or in paper form, attaching a certified Handelsregister excerpt, apostilled constitutional documents (Gesellschaftsvertrag), and proof of registered office. The Finanzamt assigns the corporate Steuernummer for Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer, and VAT assessment purposes. Separately, the GmbH must apply to BZSt for the VAT identification number (DE + 9 digits) using Form USt 1 TU through ELSTER or the BZSt portal; BZSt validates the applicant's business address and prints the USt-IdNr. after anti-abuse checks, typically within 8 to 12 weeks, with retroactive effect to the date of business commencement. A non-EU parent with no domestic fixed establishment must simultaneously appoint a fiscal representative for VAT purposes (Sec. 22a UStG) and register with the BZSt direct register.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The GmbH is a domestic corporation with unlimited German tax liability under Sec. 1(1) No. 1 KStG: corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (approx. 15.825% combined), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) with effective rates generally between 14% and 17% (standard municipal multiplier 400-475%; the 2025-2027 rate reduction applies gradually). The GmbH is not eligible for any pass-through or check-the-box treatment; profits, including gains from the sale of shares in a target SPAC, are taxed at entity level (Sec. 8b KStG variants apply subject to anti-hybrid rules). A non-EU parent with no domestic fixed establishment must simultaneously appoint a fiscal representative for VAT purposes (Sec. 22a UStG) and register with the BZSt direct register.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The GmbH is a domestic corporation with unlimited German tax liability under Sec. 1(1) No. 1 KStG: corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (approx. 15.825% combined), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) with effective rates generally between 14% and 17% (standard municipal multiplier 400-475%; the 2025-2027 rate reduction applies gradually). The GmbH is not eligible for any pass-through or check-the-box treatment; profits, including gains from the sale of shares in a target SPAC, are taxed at entity level (Sec. 8b KStG variants apply subject to anti-hybrid rules). Registration records the GmbH as a taxpayer under Sec. 123 AO (Steuernummer), with tax classification stamped as Kapitalgesellschaft / unbeschränkt steuerpflichtig.

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box regime. Relevant elective and hybrid-mismatch mechanisms include: (1) corporate Organschaft (fiscal unity) under Sec. 14 KStG, enabling consolidation of the GmbH's taxable income with the controlling group parent through profit-and-loss pooling agreements and a minimum five-year term; (2) VAT Organschaft under Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG, requiring financial, economic, and organizational integration, with the parent VAT group member becoming the sole taxable person; (3) trade tax loss-pooling election ancillary to a corporate Organschaft; (4) hybrid mismatch designations under the ATAD-implementing provisions of Sec. 4k EStG and Sec. 8b(1) KStG, including elective opt-in to dividends-taxation in specific hybrid structures, to be disclosed in the annual corporate tax return; (5) the option to waive the tax-free treatment of capital gains under Sec. 8b(2) and (3) KStG only in narrowly defined banking/trading scenarios, not applicable to a passive SPAC. No further entity-classification elections exist; the GmbH's status as a taxable corporation is fixed by private-law form regardless of Sec. 6(3) KStG fiscal-transparency limitations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a domestically formed GmbH: mandatory VAT registration from the earlier of entity formation or first taxable supply; there is no economic-turnover registration threshold for resident corporations (Sec. 20 UStG). For EU/non-EU distance sellers and cross-border B2C online supplies: EUR 10,000 annual EU-wide distance-sales threshold (Art. 28 UStG / Sec. 3(3a) UStG). A SPAC holding, financing, and M&A advisory operation making exempt or outside-of-scope supplies is still obliged to register and file nil/periodic returns; any volume of taxable transaction triggered by management fees, break-up fees, or financing arrangements would require full VAT registration. Non-established foreign businesses making German taxable supplies must register at the FZA (Finanzamt für Körperschaften Berlin) under the Schlussnummer process and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Two statutory bases apply to a trading German GmbH: (1) export of goods to third-country territories (Sec. 4 Nr. 1(a), Sec. 6 UStG): the supplier must be the exporter, the goods must physically leave the EU through an officially approved customs procedure (ATLAS export declaration), and dispatch/transport must commence from the supplier's German or EU business seat; no taxable B2B export turnover is assessed to German VAT. (2) intra-Community supply (Sec. 4 Nr. 1(b), Sec. 6a UStG): requires the acquirer to be an entrepreneur holding a valid VAT ID of another member state, verified in VIES, with transport to the destination member state proven by a Gelangensbestätigung or equivalent (e.g., CMR with signature, warehouse-keeper confirmation, or electronic freight data). For a SPAC, zero-rating is claimable only for proven physical export of tangible assets (e.g., sale of acquired business assets); intangible services, management fees, M&A breakthrough fees, and financial services are not 'export goods' and their place-of-supply is governed by Sec. 3a(2) UStG (business-to-business place-of-supply in the recipient's territory), permitting 0% only where the recipient is established outside the EU/Germany as an entrepreneur and the charge is genuinely for an offshore service; mere zero-rating of intercompany service invoices without substance, transport documentation, and VIES alignment is a classic abuse risk. The SPAC must hold a valid outward-processing or customs-principal authorisation, or operate through a licensed customs representative, so that the ATLAS MRN references the GmbH itself.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) filed electronically via ATLAS, including the assigned MRN (Movement Reference Number) and the prior export licence/waste-disposal certification where applicable
  • Commercial Invoice with full exporter/importer particulars, Incoterms 2020 clause, HS/TARIC codes, and VAT zero-rating wording
  • Bill of Lading (for sea freight), Air Waybill (for air freight), or CMR/consignment note (for road/rail freight) evidencing physical dispatch and cross-border transport from Germany to the third country
  • Packing List and Lieferschein (delivery note) plus continuous transport record (Beförderungsbelege) demonstrating an uninterrupted goods movement
  • Gelangensbestätigung (Proof of Arrival) for intra-Community supplies or the customs-issued Ausfuhrbescheinigung (Export Certificate) for third-country exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank account confirmation showing receipt of hard-currency consideration within the statutory repatriation benchmark

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing for domestic supplies commenced 1 January 2025 under Sec. 14(1) UStG as amended by the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): all resident recipient businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices in XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x, EDI, or PEPPOL formats. Sending obligations are phased: from 1 January 2026 all taxpayers with turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 in the preceding year must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 all B2B senders must comply; a SPAC with listing-related cost allocations and advisory invoices should adopt e-invoicing immediately to avoid the EUR 5,000 penalty regime and invoice-validation sanctions. Germany operates a post-audit (retrospective) fiscal-control model, not a real-time clearance system: there is no mandatory tax-authority API pre-clearance, digital stamping, or instant-return filing akin to Italy's SDI or Hungary's NAV. B2G invoices to the German federal administration must use XRechnung through the OZG/ZRE portal in a mandated format. The SPAC's share-issuance and escrow-funded acquisition activities are generally outside the scope of VAT (Sec. 1(1a) UStG), while fee-based financing or asset-management supplies qualify as standard-rated unless exempt under Sec. 4 Nr. 8 (credit and securities dealing) with proper documentation.

The Finanzamt and BZSt conduct systemic audit by cross-matching the German zero-rated turnover register, VIES (ZMR) data, and ATLAS customs export records; any claimed export supply that lacks the mandatory MRN, proof of departure, or counterparty VAT ID is automatically re-characterized as domestic taxable at 19% with an assessment notice under Sec. 27 UStG. Retrospective clawbacks carry late-payment interest at 6% per annum (Sec. 233a AO) from the original return due date, penalty surcharges (Verspätungszuschlag) of up to EUR 25,000 plus 1.5% per commenced month of under-declared VAT, and, in the case of fraudulent misstatement or documentary falsification, criminal penalties under Sec. 370 AO of up to five years' imprisonment or monetary fine; corporate and management joint-and-several liability extends under Sec. 14(3), Sec. 31b AO and Sec. 71 AO. For a SPAC issuing zero-rated invoices without underlying physical export or qualifying offshore services, additionally: revocation of the USt-IdNr. with publication in the BZSt abuse file, denial of input-VAT deduction, security commitment orders (Sicherheitsleistung) under Sec. 9 UStG, and a 10-year statutory assessment period for tax evasion (Sec. 169(2) AO), five years for gross negligence; the criminal Statute of Limitations under Sec. 78(3) Nr. 4 StGB extends to ten years in aggravated cross-border export-fraud cases. The tax authorities may further disallow the trade-tax deduction for the related penalty payments and order the company's management to make personal restitution.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is a fully convertible hard currency in both the current and capital accounts. Germany imposes no exchange-control restrictions on current-account transactions or capital movements, consistent with EU Treaty free movement of capital and the IMF Article VIII obligations. Profit and dividend remittances by a German GmbH to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted and require no Bundesbank or BaFin authorization. The only material frictions are documentary: German dividend withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge applies, subject to reduction to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the shareholder holds at least 10%, or to reduced rates under applicable double-tax treaties, in each case requiring a German residence certificate (Ansässigkeitsbescheinigung) and a bank KYC dossier. Inbound equity injections, outbound acquisition payments, and treasury transactions are subject solely to AWV statistical reporting thresholds, not to prior licensing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not maintain any statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or forced repatriation regime. Under Sections 59-71 of the AWV, resident companies must file statistical balance-of-payments reports with the Deutsche Bundesbank for eligible cross-border payments and receivables generally above EUR 12,500 per transaction, or cumulative foreign receivables/payables above EUR 50,000; export proceeds are reported under the AWV goods-export code class and remain fully disposable in any currency of the exporter's choosing. There is no requirement to convert foreign currency into euro, hold proceeds for a minimum period, or remit a percentage to the central bank. For the SPAC vehicle itself, export-trade obligations are not applicable; the relevant reporting duties arise on investment-related capital flows, which likewise carry no surrender or conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and payment-services supervision. Germany maintains no foreign-exchange control authority as such: the Bundesbank is the designated national authority for balance-of-payments statistics, cross-border payment reporting, and the administration of the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for SPAC trust-escrow functions; Medium for ancillary operational disbursements. A non-EEA digital e-money institution (EMI) cannot lawfully provide payment accounts to a German GmbH without a BaFin licence under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) or a valid EEA passport under PSD2. An EMI is not a credit institution within Section 1 KWG, cannot accept deposits (Einlagengeschäft), and cannot replicate the investor-protection side of a regulated SPAC trust account; BaFin requires EMI client funds to be safeguarded in segregated accounts at a licensed credit institution. Therefore IPO proceeds and acquisition escrow must remain with a full commercial bank connected to TARGET2 and SWIFT. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a German GmbH receiving equity capital from non-resident sponsors or IPO subscription funds: (1) transfer via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's own fully-KYC-verified account; within the EU, TARGET2 RTGS or SEPA instant payment using a TARGET2 participant bank; (2) the payment mandate must carry the Bundesbank AWV statistical purpose code for a direct investment contribution, code class 'DI — Direktinvestitionen', with explicit beneficiary text 'Kapitalmassnahme GmbH — Einlage Stammkapital/Kapitalruecklage'; (3) the receiving German bank automatically reports the transaction to the Deutsche Bundesbank if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500, as mandated by Section 67 AWV, with no approval requirement; (4) before notarial registration of a capital increase, the bank issues a funds-confirmation certificate (Geldbestätigung) evidencing full payment of the contribution; (5) if received in a foreign currency such as USD, conversion to EUR is executed at market rate by the house bank with no central-bank conversion quota or licence; (6) to prevent AML rejection, the wire must cite the exact capital-increase resolution and the beneficial-owner fields must reconcile perfectly with the Transparenzregister filings of the GmbH.Clearing rails for a German SPAC making trade and acquisition payments: (1) standard cross-border settlement via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer with full beneficiary legal name, international bank account number or account number, and reference to the underlying purchase agreement; (2) where an intermediary cover payment is needed, SWIFT MT202 COV is used by the sending institution; (3) documentary trade instruments include irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued under UCP 600 via the SWIFT MT700 series, and standby letters of credit under ISP98 via SWIFT MT760 for acquisition guarantees, escrow releases, and earn-out obligations; (4) euro-denominated payments within the Eurozone settle through TARGET2 or SEPA Instant, while USD and other foreign-currency payments settle through correspondent accounts at SWIFT-connected institutions; (5) every outbound transfer above EUR 12,500 is AWV-reportable to the Bundesbank; cross-border acquisition payments are reported under the direct-investment code class, with no prior approval or conversion requirement; (6) for the SPAC business combination, the trustee releases trust proceeds only upon a Condition Satisfaction Date evidenced in the escrow agreement, and the bank executes the MT103 directly to the vendor's account with simultaneous sanctions and export-control screening.FX Forwards (Devisentermingeschaefte) under ISDA 2002 and a Credit Support Annex, used to fix EUR/USD or EUR/GBP conversion rates on acquisition proceeds and contingent consideration.FX Swaps and Rollover FX Swaps for temporary exchange of USD trust proceeds into EUR while preserving the ability to re-exchange at maturity for the acquisition currency.OTC Currency Options (call and put structures) to protect minimum conversion thresholds without eliminating upside in the base currency.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (EUR/USD) for hedging USD-denominated target-company debt assumed at closing, including periodic principal and interest exchanges.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) restricted to exotic or restricted emerging-market currencies where the underlying pair is non-deliverable; EUR/USD and EUR/GBP are deliverable pairs, so NDFs are not needed for the core SPAC treasury and are permissible only on a back-to-back transaction basis.

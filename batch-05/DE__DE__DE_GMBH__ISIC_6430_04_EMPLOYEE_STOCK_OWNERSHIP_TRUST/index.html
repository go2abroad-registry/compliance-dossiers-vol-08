Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authority is the local German tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax, wage tax, and domestic VAT administration. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and administers related cross-border VAT functions. Tax registration filings are generally submitted electronically through the ELSTER portal (Elektronische Steuererklärung).

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH must be registered with the competent local tax office after incorporation and commencement of business. The company submits the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER, including its commercial-register details, registered office, directors, beneficial ownership information, expected turnover, activities, bank details, corporate income tax status, trade tax information, payroll obligations, and VAT position. The Finanzamt then issues the German tax number (Steuernummer). A VAT identification number is separately requested from the BZSt through its electronic application process or through the relevant tax registration procedures. A foreign entity with German taxable activities must identify the competent Finanzamt, file the applicable tax registration questionnaire, obtain a German tax number where required, and separately request a VAT identification number if it makes or receives transactions requiring EU VAT identification. A German establishment, taxable supply, import, intra-EU acquisition, payroll obligation, or other German tax nexus may trigger registration even where the entity has no German commercial-register presence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is treated as a corporation subject to German corporate income tax and does not have a US-style check-the-box election. German partnerships may elect corporate-income-tax treatment under Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act, but that election is not available to a GmbH. A GmbH may participate in a tax group (Organschaft) if the statutory financial, organizational, and profit-transfer requirements are satisfied. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement ATAD requirements and generally deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences; they do not provide a general elective hybrid classification. Shareholder-level distributions are generally subject to dividend withholding tax, subject to statutory exemptions, treaty relief, or refund procedures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany has no general turnover threshold below which a taxable business may freely avoid VAT registration. A business must register when it carries out taxable transactions requiring German VAT reporting, unless a specific reverse-charge, exemption, or special scheme applies. The German small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) generally applies from 2025 where prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000. The exemption is not a general exemption from registration, recordkeeping, invoicing, or VAT obligations and may be unavailable or inappropriate for certain foreign-established businesses and cross-border transactions. The EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold may apply to qualifying intra-EU distance sales and telecommunications, broadcasting, and electronically supplied services where the supplier is established in only one EU Member State and does not use the One-Stop Shop to tax supplies in the customer Member State.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Germany generally treats qualifying exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union as VAT-exempt with input-tax deduction, commonly described commercially as zero-rated export supplies. The supplier must establish that the goods physically leave the EU customs territory, that the statutory export conditions are satisfied, and that the required documentary evidence is retained. For intra-EU supplies of goods, the relevant treatment is generally VAT exemption rather than export zero-rating, requiring valid customer VAT identification, transport evidence, and accurate recapitulative reporting. Services supplied to a business customer outside Germany are generally governed by place-of-supply rules and may be outside the scope of German VAT rather than zero-rated; exceptions apply to services connected with German real estate, events, transport, and other special categories. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, a direct business connection, and compliance with the applicable German or EU refund procedure.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and export Movement Reference Number (MRN), where required
  • Customs confirmation of exit or equivalent electronic exit evidence
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and goods description identifying the supplier, consignee, value, and destination
  • Transport documentation, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, courier proof, or equivalent evidence
  • Purchase order, sales contract, delivery terms, and proof of delivery where necessary to substantiate the transaction
  • Evidence linking the goods, invoice, customs declaration, and transport documents
  • For qualifying intra-EU supplies, the customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required recapitulative statement
  • Proof of payment or commercial settlement where relevant to audit substantiation; Germany does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a statutory condition for export VAT exemption

Germany does not operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or mandatory digital-stamping system. For domestic business-to-business transactions, a structured electronic invoice is being phased in under the German Growth Opportunities Act. Since 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must generally be able to receive compliant e-invoices. Issuance transition periods generally permit other invoice formats through 31 December 2026, and through 31 December 2027 for issuers whose preceding-year turnover did not exceed EUR 800,000, subject to the statutory conditions. From 1 January 2028, compliant e-invoicing is generally required for in-scope domestic B2B transactions. Acceptable structured formats include XRechnung and compliant ZUGFeRD versions using EN 16931. The rules generally do not cover exempt transactions, certain small-value invoices, passenger transport tickets, or transactions outside the domestic B2B scope. Public-sector invoicing has separate federal and state requirements, and invoice retention and authenticity controls remain mandatory.

Germany has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days. Export VAT exemption is denied or reversed where the goods did not leave the EU, documentary evidence is incomplete, the transaction is fictitious, the supplier knowingly participated in fraud, or the statutory conditions were otherwise not met. The tax office may assess output VAT retrospectively, deny related input-tax deductions, impose late-payment interest under the German Fiscal Code, and require correction of VAT returns. Incorrect VAT claims may result in administrative penalties and, where intentional or reckless conduct is established, tax-evasion or tax-fraud proceedings under the German Fiscal Code, including fines and imprisonment for intentional tax evasion, with substantially higher exposure in aggravated cases. Customs violations may create separate import or export duty liabilities, administrative fines, and criminal liability. Recovery actions can include tax assessments for prior periods, interest, penalties, repayment of improperly refunded VAT, and audit-based adjustments to related transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a full current-account and capital-account convertibility jurisdiction within the Eurozone. The euro is freely convertible; there are no exchange controls, no licensing of FX transactions and no quantitative limits on capital movements, guaranteed by Article 63 TFEU and Sections 1 and 4 of the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG). Dividend distributions, profit remittances, share capital injections, ESOP trust settlements and financing flows from or to non-residents require no FX approval from the Bundesbank or other state authority. The only fiscal hurdles are withholding taxes on distributions from a GmbH: 25% capital yield tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 26.375%), reducible under tax treaties, plus CRS/FATCA reporting formalities. Legal formality: single cross-border payments of euro 12,500 or more (cumulative monthly basis per counterparty for some categories) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank by the resident payer/payee under AWV § 67, using the bank's electronic statistics forms (Z1–Z5); this is statistical, not regulatory approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds, no surrender ratio, no mandatory conversion into euro and no foreign-currency account restrictions. Export receivables may be collected, held or netted offshore in any currency. The only compliance duty is the Bundesbank balance-of-payments statistics report for each invoice payment exceeding euro 12,500, coded by economic nature; retention of the underlying sales/equity transfer documentation for five years is required.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank and external statistics authority) together with Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin, Federal Financial Supervisory Authority). The Bundesbank administers cross-border payment reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV), while BaFin supervises banking, payment and securities conduct of German credit institutions and EMIs.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for escrow and trust funds; Medium for low-value operational payroll rails. German and EU-passported e-money institutions (EMIs) may lawfully provide payment accounts, card issuing and SEPA/SEPA Instant initiation, but they may not accept deposits repayable on demand for trust/escrow purposes without a full banking licence under Section 1 KWG. Holding ESOP participant funds on an EMI balance violates BaFin's regulatory perimeter and German escrow principles (Fremdgeldverbot) and exceeds the scope of the E-Money Directive. Unregulated fintech front-ends (corporate cards, BaaS platforms) may be used only as disbursement interfaces funded in real time from a tier-1 bank account; the statutory trust ledger and employee entitlement balances must remain on segregated accounts at a licensed credit institution covered by the German deposit guarantee scheme (up to euro 100,000 per depositor under CSD).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital and ESOP contributions are routed exclusively over the SWIFT network: SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR for EU originators (IBAN-to-IBAN, using the TARGET2 settlement layer) or SWIFT MT103 for all non-EUR and non-EEA originations. Payment instructions must carry an explicit purpose text, e.g., 'ESOP Kapitalerhöhung GmbH HRB [number]' or 'Shareholder loan ESOP trust', and the Bundesbank statistical nature code for equity participation. The Deutsche Bundesbank requires the resident beneficiary to identify the economic substance (direct investment in nominal capital) in the monthly Z-report when the transfer equals or exceeds euro 12,500; the commercial bank will reject or defer the credit if the originator omits the purpose code. Verification steps: (i) confirm MT103 field 70 against the capital increase resolution; (ii) reconcile the received amount with the notarial deed; (iii) retain the bank acknowledgement and Bundesbank report reference for the tax and audit file; (iv) for USD/GBP inbound, use the correspondent bank's intermediary details from the receiving German bank to avoid routing delays.Liquidity is pushed over four rails depending on destination and instrument: (a) SEPA Instant Credit (SCT Inst, up to euro 100,000) for same-second payroll wires to Eurozone employee bank accounts; (b) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA salary and bonus payments, free-format field 70 referencing the employee award/liquidated share unit; (c) commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) via MT700/MT707 for cross-border share purchase settlements where the seller requires documentary security, with the German bank issuing or confirming the LC against the escrow balance; (d) TARGET2 (T2) transfers for concentration of liquidity among the GmbH's own accounts. For equity-transfer settlements, delivery-versus-payment is achieved by coupling an SWIFT MT202 cover payment with the securities transfer booking in the respective CSD (Clearstream or foreign depositary); the treasury policy mandates that no outgoing payment is released without matching the two-factor authorisation matrix and the Bundesbank statistics reporting duty.Deliverable outright FX forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) in EUR/USD and EUR/GBP with maturities up to 12 months, executed under ISDA/CMS master agreements with a German credit institution; no BaFin product approval is required for a corporate end-user.FX swaps (swap of principal and interest flows) for temporary conversion of ESOP receivables from USD to EUR without balance-sheet currency risk.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for EUR/CHF and EUR/USD to hedge multi-year deferred-compensation liabilities where the ESOP plan is denominated in a foreign currency.Vanilla FX options and zero-cost collars on EUR/USD for hedging the equity-purchase price or dividend remittance exposure with verified underlying exposure; option writing is prohibited absent a banking licence.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are legally permissible but only economically justified for non-convertible emerging-market currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, BRL); they must not be used for freely convertible EUR pairs because no deliverability restriction exists in Germany.

German and EU-passported e-money institutions (EMIs) may lawfully provide payment accounts, card issuing and SEPA/SEPA Instant initiation, but they may not accept deposits repayable on demand for trust/escrow purposes without a full banking licence under Section 1 KWG. Holding ESOP participant funds on an EMI balance violates BaFin's regulatory perimeter and German escrow principles (Fremdgeldverbot) and exceeds the scope of the E-Money Directive. Unregulated fintech front-ends (corporate cards, BaaS platforms) may be used only as disbursement interfaces funded in real time from a tier-1 bank account; the statutory trust ledger and employee entitlement balances must remain on segregated accounts at a licensed credit institution covered by the German deposit guarantee scheme (up to euro 100,000 per depositor under CSD).

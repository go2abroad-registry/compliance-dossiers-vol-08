Applicable AML statute: German Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz, GwG), in particular Sections 1(13) and 3 defining the beneficial owner, Section 10 customer due diligence, Section 11 identification and verification, Section 12 verification documents, Section 15 enhanced due diligence, Section 19 Transparency Register information, Section 20 registration obligations for legal entities, Section 23 access rules, and Section 43 suspicious-activity reporting. Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A GmbH must have a domestic registered office and a domestic business address capable of service under Sections 4a and 8 GmbHG. A serviced or virtual office may be acceptable for the registered business address if the company has genuine access to the premises, reliable receipt of legal documents, and an appropriate operating arrangement. A mailbox-only address is insufficient. For cross-border commercial litigation, asset tracing, client funds, and legal services, a virtual office alone presents substantial regulatory, professional-conduct, banking, tax, and substance risks. If the entity provides legal services, the structure must comply with the Federal Lawyers' Act (BRAO), the Legal Services Act (RDG), professional rules, admission requirements, and client-money controls. A German GmbH is not automatically entitled to operate as a law firm merely because it is incorporated.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

German Commercial Register (Handelsregister) via the Länder joint register portal at handelsregister.de; the Unternehmensregister at unternehmensregister.de provides company information and filings. The notary submits the GmbH registration electronically to the competent local register court. The competent local Chamber of Industry and Commerce (Industrie- und Handelskammer, IHK) may provide a non-binding name assessment.

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no generally available statutory company-name reservation period. Obtain written pre-clearance from the competent IHK, instruct the notary to submit the formation filing promptly, and avoid public use before registration. If the register court rejects or objects to the name, obtain the written order and legal grounds, amend the name or submit substantiating evidence within the stated deadline, and coordinate with the notary. A judicial complaint against a register-court decision may generally be filed under the FamFG framework, normally within one month where the decision specifies that remedy; obtain German procedural advice because admissibility and deadlines depend on the order. If the objection concerns regulated-profession terminology, obtain confirmation from the competent professional body or restructure the name to avoid implying authorization.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister des Amtsgerichts (Commercial Register of the Local Court) at the registered seat of the GmbH, operated through the joint Federal commercial-register portal (www.handelsregister.de) with electronic transmission by the certifying notary; professional approval is issued in parallel by the regional Rechtsanwaltskammer (Chamber of Lawyers).

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG — German Limited Liability Company Act), with direct application of §§ 1 (formation), 2-2a (standard form contract), 3 (articles), 5 (minimum share capital), 7(2) (capital contribution prior to registration), 8 (commercial register application), 10 (legal personality upon registration), 35-39 (managing directors), 41 (shareholder list), 82 (false statements); registration and publicity framework under §§ 14, 39(1) of the Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB); professional-services company admission and insurance under §§ 59c, 59k and 59m of the Bundesrechtsanwaltsordnung (BRAO).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag / Musterprotokoll) authenticated under § 2 GmbHG, together with the notarized registration application (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) under § 8 GmbHG including the managing directors' declaration, the list of shareholders, the bank certificate of paid-in capital, and the pre-registration approval of the Rechtsanwaltskammer (§ 59k BRAO).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum nominal share capital of EUR 25,000 (§ 5(1) GmbHG). Before registration, at least 25% of each cash contribution — aggregating to at least EUR 12,500 — must be paid into the GmbH-in-formation account and left at the free disposal of the managing director, verified by an unconditional bank certificate (§ 7(2), § 8(2) GmbHG). In a single-member GmbH the full amount of the cash contribution must be paid up unless security is furnished; no statutory escrow account is imposed by German law. In-kind contributions require fully paid-up transfer of the asset and a statutory valuation and plausibility report (§§ 5(4), 7(3) GmbHG).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt, www.bzst.de) — allocates the German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, DE-format); local tax office (Finanzamt, determined by registered seat or permanent establishment, fallback for non-established entities: Finanzamt Neukölln) — assesses corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), VAT (Umsatzsteuer) and payroll tax (Lohnsteuer); national electronic filing and clearance portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); customs export control authority: Generalzolldirektion / main customs office (Hauptzollamt) operating the ATLAS export declaration system.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned GmbH: (1) file the digital Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung via ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt to obtain the corporation-tax/trade-tax/VAT assessment number (Steuernummer) and the tax account key (Steuerkonto); (2) apply separately to the BZSt for the German VAT ID using Form UST 1 A where the GmbH supplies cross-border services or exports goods; (3) register the Gewerbesteuer liability with the municipality of the registered office or permanent establishment; (4) appoint a fiscal/tax agent with a written power of attorney (Vollmacht) in the ELSTER-certified workflow — mandatory for non-resident managers; (5) finalize bar-authority admission (Zulassung als Rechtsanwaltsgesellschaft mbH under the German legal-services licensing regime) before taxable rendering of commercial litigation and asset-tracing services; statutory processing time: 3–8 weeks from complete submission; late registration triggers a delay surcharge of EUR 25 per commenced month under §152a AO up to EUR 25,000 plus 0.5% monthly late-payment interest; retroactive VAT registration is not available.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a GmbH is a resident corporate taxpayer under §1 KStG and §11 AO (seat or place of management in Germany), subject to unlimited worldwide taxation: corporation tax 15.0%, solidarity surcharge 5.5% on the corporation tax (effective 0.825%), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at the statutory 3.5% base rate multiplied by a municipal leverage factor of 200–490% (commonly ~400% => ~14%); combined effective corporate tax burden approx. 30–33%; no territorial exemption, no pass-through/transparent status; dividends received are 95% exempt under §8b KStG; profit distributions carry 25% German withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) reduced under tax treaties; foreign tax credits available under §26 KStG / §34c EStG for non-treaty income. — Foreign-owned GmbH: (1) file the digital Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung via ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt to obtain the corporation-tax/trade-tax/VAT assessment number (Steuernummer) and the tax account key (Steuerkonto); (2) apply separately to the BZSt for the German VAT ID using Form UST 1 A where the GmbH supplies cross-border services or exports goods; (3) register the Gewerbesteuer liability with the municipality of the registered office or permanent establishment; (4) appoint a fiscal/tax agent with a written power of attorney (Vollmacht) in the ELSTER-certified workflow — mandatory for non-resident managers; (5) finalize bar-authority admission (Zulassung als Rechtsanwaltsgesellschaft mbH under the German legal-services licensing regime) before taxable rendering of commercial litigation and asset-tracing services; statutory processing time: 3–8 weeks from complete submission; late registration triggers a delay surcharge of EUR 25 per commenced month under §152a AO up to EUR 25,000 plus 0.5% monthly late-payment interest; retroactive VAT registration is not available.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a GmbH is a resident corporate taxpayer under §1 KStG and §11 AO (seat or place of management in Germany), subject to unlimited worldwide taxation: corporation tax 15.0%, solidarity surcharge 5.5% on the corporation tax (effective 0.825%), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at the statutory 3.5% base rate multiplied by a municipal leverage factor of 200–490% (commonly ~400% => ~14%); combined effective corporate tax burden approx. 30–33%; no territorial exemption, no pass-through/transparent status; dividends received are 95% exempt under §8b KStG; profit distributions carry 25% German withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) reduced under tax treaties; foreign tax credits available under §26 KStG / §34c EStG for non-treaty income.

Available election forms

German law has no US-style check-the-box election; tax classification is mandatory and non-elective. Available statutory elections relevant to a cross-border litigation GmbH: (a) Organschaft (fiscal unity) election under §§14–17 KStG, §2 Abs.2 Nr.2 GewStG and §2 Abs.2 Nr.2 UStG — requires a profit-and-loss transfer agreement for a minimum of 5 years and financial integration, allowing profit/loss and VAT consolidation with the shareholder; (b) participation-exemption mechanics under §8b KStG and treaty election for third-country dividends; (c) §4k EStG hybrid-mismatch election to align German deduction treatment with the foreign investor jurisdiction; (d) §9 UStG option to waive VAT exemption for real estate (opting into taxable leasing); (e) administrative relief election for double taxation of non-EU permanent establishments under §34c EStG (credit versus exemption, annual election); (f) CSRD/ESEF-related group-reporting elections are separate from tax classification and do not alter the German entity-level tax pool.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business exemption: EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 current-year projection under §19 UStG; non-resident suppliers of services, distance sales or e-services have no general threshold and must register from the first euro of German taxable supply; the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold for cross-border tele/digital/online services applies only to micro-businesses established in the EU; a GmbH rendering B2B legal services to German clients has mandatory VAT registration regardless of turnover; B2B services to non-German clients are outside German VAT scope via reverse charge (§3a Abs.2 UStG) and do not trigger registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% German VAT applies under §4 Nr.1(a) UStG read with §6 UStG where goods physically leave the EU customs territory and the exporter holds reliable proof of removal (ATLAS electronic export declaration with MRN and transport documentation), recording the supply as an export sale in the VAT return. Services: cross-border B2B supplies of commercial litigation, arbitration, and asset-tracing services to a customer established in another EU member state or a third country are excluded from German VAT under §3a Abs.2 UStG (place of supply = the customer's establishment), effectively applying 0% German VAT with the supplier documenting the reverse-charge notice and the client's VAT ID; B2C legal services to foreign non-business clients remain German-taxable at 19% under the general rule (§3a Abs.1 UStG). Escrow amounts held in a German attorney trust account (Anderkonto) are outside VAT consideration until earned as fees. Substantiation requires contemporaneous retainer/engagement letters, fee notes, client identification, bank records, and, for goods, customs evidence; German audited practice (UStAE 6.1) requires export proof within 60 days of shipment for goods, and the 180-day benchmark reflected in this dossier is the customary payment-turnover audit window for cross-border litigation receivables, award-recovery flows, and insurer-funded fee claims.

  • Customs Export Declaration: ATLAS electronic export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with MRN (Movement Reference Number) and the export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument) confirming physical exit from EU customs territory — applicable to goods; for pure service supplies, the equivalent is the reverse-charge notice and the client's foreign VAT ID recorded in the VAT return.
  • Bill of Lading: ocean B/L, airway bill, CMR rail/road consignment note, or combined transport document naming the German GmbH as exporter and evidencing the goods leaving the EU territory; for litigation services, the substitute evidence is the executed engagement letter, the client's jurisdictional seat certificate, and the case-file records.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank SWIFT/SEPA credit advice, debit/credit notes, or the client's payment instruction evidencing actual collection of the export or cross-border-service consideration in the GmbH's German bank account — the German tax audit practice treats this as standard proof of the foreign-receipt chain, although it is not a statutory customs document under the Union Customs Code.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or SDI-type digital stamp exists in Germany as of the current phase (CTR/continuous-transaction-controls regime is planned under EU ViDA, with Germany targeting transaction-based reporting for intra-EU supplies from 1 January 2030). Mandatory structured e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) for domestic B2B since 1 January 2025: every supplier must receive structured e-invoices (EN 16931-compliant XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X XML); mandatory issuance is phased — from 1 January 2027 for issuers with prior-year taxable turnover above EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all other issuers; e-invoices must preserve authenticity of origin and content integrity; transmission is any-electronic-channel (e-mail, portal, PEPPOL); simultaneous hybrid-format transmission by mutual agreement remains possible only until the mandatory issuance dates. The official tax-declaration portal ELSTER remains the sole channel for VAT return filing and the future CTR reporting pipeline.

Failure to substantiate zero-rating triggers retrospective clawback: the Finanzamt assesses the full 19% VAT on the previously zero-rated supply (§§1, 13b UStG), reverses input-VAT deductions, and charges late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (§233a AO) from the original due date; the limitation period extends from 4 to 5 years for simple underassessment and to 10 years where tax evasion is established (§169 Abs.2 AO). Criminal and administrative penalties: fraudulent tax evasion under §370 AO is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine (aggravated commercial/gang fraud: 6 months to 10 years); negligent misdeclaration is a regulatory fine of up to EUR 50,000 under §378 AO; the BZSt additionally applies a fraudulent-behavior surcharge of up to 10% of the evaded amount. Since 2024, the BZSt operates a risk-analytics platform cross-matching ATLAS customs-export records with VAT return data and bank receipts in real time, with automatic invoice-and-bank red-flag generation for invoices outstanding beyond 180 days or inconsistent with foreign receipts.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: A GmbH generally requires a statutory audit if it is medium-sized or large under HGB. A company is medium-sized when it exceeds at least two of these thresholds for two consecutive financial years: EUR 7.5 million balance-sheet total, EUR 15 million net turnover, and 50 average employees. Large-company thresholds are EUR 25 million, EUR 50 million, and 250 employees. Small GmbHs are generally exempt, subject to statutory exceptions.

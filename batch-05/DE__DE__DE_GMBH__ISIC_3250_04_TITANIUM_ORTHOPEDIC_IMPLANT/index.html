Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt – Federal Central Tax Office) for VAT identification numbers and cross-border tax matters; local Finanzamt (district Tax Office) for corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT prepayments and audits; mandatory electronic filing exclusively via ELSTER portal (www.elster.de); Generalzolldirektion (Federal Customs Administration) for EORI registration and export control; medical device conformity supervised by BfArM under MDR 2017/745

E-government filing portal

Step 2: File the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER within four weeks of business commencement with the competent local Finanzamt; obtain the local Tax Number (Steuernummer, 13 digits). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a GmbH is a non-transparent corporation subject to unlimited corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) in Germany on worldwide income when its statutory seat or place of effective management is in Germany; profits bear corporate income tax at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax (effective 15.825%) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at effective rates of approximately 7% to 17.15% depending on the municipality’s collection rate (Hebesatz, 200%–490%); combined effective tax burden approximates 30%–33%; dividend income from qualifying domestic/EU shareholdings is 95% exempt under § 8b KStG; no pass-through taxation is available for a GmbH; territorial scope is unlimited resident taxation and limited-source taxation for non-residents.

Available election forms

Corporate income tax and trade tax fiscal unity election (Organschaft) requires a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Gewinnabführungsvertrag) effective for at least five years and a majority voting interest held by the parent (§§ 14, 17 KStG); VAT fiscal unity election (Umsatzsteuer-Organschaft, § 2(2) No. 2 UStG) available when the controlled entity is financially, economically and organizationally integrated into the controlling company; German Anti-Tax-Avoidance-Directive Act (ATAD-Umsetzungsgesetz, 25 June 2021) denies hybrid mismatch benefits and there is no check-the-box or unilateral hybrid election in German domestic law; trade tax aggregation and permanent-establishment allocation elections are available under §§ 2, 9 GewStG; small-entrepreneur VAT relief (§ 19 UStG) is elective but inadvisable for an export-oriented manufacturer because input VAT recovery is forfeited.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis VAT registration threshold for incorporated manufacturing entities – a GmbH qualifies as a taxable person (Unternehmer) with compulsory VAT registration from the first taxable transaction; the small-entrepreneur relief (Kleinunternehmer, § 19 UStG) is limited to prior-year gross turnover not exceeding EUR 22,000 and a current-year ceiling of EUR 50,000, but election forfeits input VAT recovery and is generally disadvantageous for an exporter; monthly or quarterly VAT prepayment returns and an annual VAT return are obligatory; the EUR 12,500 intra-Community acquisition registration threshold concerns only non-established traders and does not apply to a domestic GmbH; a USt-IdNr. is mandatory for any cross-border EU dispatch of goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% export VAT applies under § 4(1)(a) and § 6 UStG to finished titanium orthopedic implants physically consigned to a non-EU buyer where the goods are dispatched or transported out of EU territory by the exporter or on its behalf; intra-Community B2B supplies to VAT-registered EU customers are exempt with input VAT recovery under § 4(1)(b) and § 6a UStG, conditional on verification of the customer’s valid EU VAT identification number and listing in the EC Sales List; B2B services supplied to non-EU principals (offshore engineering, R&D, design validation, or quality certifications) are zero-rated under § 3a(2) together with § 4(1)(b) UStG; the export must be completed within three months of the supply date, and documentary proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) must be available before the VAT return for the relevant period is finalized; CE marking and MDR 2017/745 technical documentation must be retained as supplementary evidence of the medical-device nature of the goods for rate-verification purposes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has full convertibility in line with EU free movement of capital under TFEU Articles 63-66. There is no exchange-control licensing requirement for inbound investment, profit remittance, dividend distribution, or trade settlement. Payments may be screened for sanctions, AML, and fraud but no Bundesbank conversion approval is required. Profit remittance is subject to German dividend withholding tax at 26.375% including solidarity surcharge, unless reduced by an applicable double-tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation ratios on export proceeds. The only binding requirement is balance-of-payments statistical reporting: any payment received from or made to a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 or its equivalent must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank within seven days under Sections 67 and 69 AWV. Exporters may retain and convert export proceeds freely.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (statistical reporting authority under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance, Außenwirtschaftsverordnung-AWV); prudential banking supervision and anti-money-laundering enforcement are administered by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. BaFin-licensed or EU-passported Electonic Money Institutions can provide IBANs and process low-value FX, supplier payouts, and collections. They are unsuitable as escrow accounts for statutory GmbH capital because they are not licensed credit institutions under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz), cannot accept deposits, and provide limited sanctions traceability for high-value trade and LC transactions. Use EMIs only for minor operational payments, not for capital injections or core trade settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be routed exclusively through a German commercial bank account. The foreign shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 transfer from its own bank account to the GmbH's capital escrow account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.00% German corporate income tax, plus a solidarity surcharge of 5.50% of corporate income tax, producing an effective federal burden of 15.825%. Municipal trade tax is additional and varies by municipality; the combined corporate and trade tax burden is commonly approximately 23% to 33%, with an approximate national average near 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic burden of 26.375%, before church tax. A qualifying EU parent company may obtain full exemption under the German implementation of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, subject to minimum participation, holding-period, legal-form, subject-to-tax, anti-abuse, and substance requirements. Treaties commonly reduce German dividend withholding tax to 5% for qualifying corporate participation or 15% for portfolio holdings, but the applicable treaty must be checked.

For a calendar-year taxpayer without a tax adviser, the annual return is generally due by 31 July of the following year. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany generally requires contemporaneous documentation for cross-border related-party transactions under Section 90 of the German Fiscal Code and the German Transfer Pricing Documentation Ordinance. Detailed documentation is generally required where annual cross-border transactions exceed EUR 6,000,000 for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing, and royalties. A master file is generally required for a German entity belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 100,000,000 in the preceding financial year. Documentation must generally be produced within 60 days of request; extraordinary transactions generally require documentation within 30 days.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no single standalone economic substance regulation equivalent to a separate ESR regime. Substance is nevertheless relevant under the general anti-abuse rule in Section 42 of the German Fiscal Code, treaty beneficial-ownership and anti-abuse provisions, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive anti-abuse rule, interest and royalty withholding rules, controlled foreign company provisions, hybrid mismatch rules, and transfer-pricing requirements. A German GmbH manufacturing orthopedic implants should maintain genuine personnel, premises, production capability, decision-making, risk control, technical functions, inventory or supply-chain capability, and commercially documented financing. Conduit ownership, circular flows, nominal directors, unsupported royalty charges, and artificial interposition create substantial denial and penalty risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion is generally punishable by a criminal fine or imprisonment of up to five years. Particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. The tax authority may assess the avoided tax, late-payment surcharges, interest, and confiscation or recovery of economic benefits.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal tax liability for culpable breach of tax duties under the German Fiscal Code, civil liability under Section 43 GmbHG, criminal liability for their own conduct or participation, and insolvency-related liability. Courts may impose asset seizure, confiscation, and, in appropriate criminal or insolvency proceedings, restrictions affecting travel or freedom. There is no automatic travel ban merely because a tax return is late.
Late filing, late payment, and withholding failure

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharges may generally reach EUR 25 per month and up to EUR 25,000. Late-payment surcharges are generally 1% per commenced month of delay on the rounded arrears. Withholding-tax failures can result in assessment against the GmbH, interest, administrative fines, and criminal prosecution where intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable where tax losses result from culpable failure to file, withhold, or pay, particularly during financial distress. Preferential payment of other creditors while withholding tax remains unpaid can create significant personal exposure.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make an adjustment to the arm's-length result, impose additional corporate and trade tax, deny deductions, apply withholding tax, and impose documentation-related penalties. Failure to provide adequate documentation may permit estimation and a surcharge generally ranging from EUR 5,000 to EUR 1,000,000, subject to statutory rules and the size of the adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal civil and tax liability for deliberate or grossly negligent approval of manipulated pricing, and criminal liability if the conduct constitutes intentional tax evasion.
Shell company, conduit, and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Treaty or EU relief may be denied under anti-abuse rules, withholding tax may be assessed at the domestic rate, deductions may be rejected, and tax evasion or aiding tax evasion proceedings may follow where facts were concealed or misrepresented.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal tax and civil liability and criminal exposure for knowingly implementing an artificial structure. A court may order confiscation and other criminal measures; travel restrictions are case-specific and not automatic.
VAT fraud and carousel transactions

Statutory sanction: Input VAT may be denied, VAT and interest may be assessed, administrative penalties may apply, and intentional VAT evasion may carry criminal fines or imprisonment up to five years, or six months to ten years in particularly serious cases.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for VAT under the German Fiscal Code and may face criminal prosecution for participation, aiding, or abetting.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial-ownership violations

Statutory sanction: Applicable obligations depend on whether the GmbH is an obligated entity under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act or operates through an obligated bank, payment institution, customs intermediary, or professional adviser. Breaches can attract administrative fines, transaction restrictions, reporting duties, and criminal sanctions for money laundering or participation in predicate offences. For certain obligated entities, fines may reach the higher of statutory amounts, EUR 1,000,000 or a multiple of the economic benefit, with higher turnover-based ceilings in specified cases.

Director personal liability: Directors may face administrative fines, criminal prosecution for intentional money laundering or aiding, confiscation, and civil liability. Failure to maintain beneficial-owner information can also create commercial-register and compliance consequences.
Customs, export-control, and sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Customs duties, import VAT, interest, administrative fines, seizure, license withdrawal, and criminal penalties may apply. Export-control and sanctions violations can carry substantial fines and imprisonment depending on intent, goods, destination, and applicable EU or German restrictions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally prosecuted for authorization or negligent supervision failures, and may face fines, imprisonment, confiscation, and professional or commercial restrictions.
Medical-device regulatory non-compliance with tax consequences

Statutory sanction: Authorities may impose product recalls, sales prohibitions, administrative fines, withdrawal of market access, and criminal sanctions for serious breaches. Tax deductions and R&D claims may be challenged where expenditures or commercial supplies are fictitious or unlawfully documented.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil product liability, regulatory fines, criminal liability for intentional or negligent violations, and insolvency-related claims where non-compliance causes creditor or customer losses.
Insolvency filing and creditor-preference violations

Statutory sanction: A managing director must generally file for insolvency without undue delay and no later than three weeks after illiquidity or six weeks after over-indebtedness, provided that a credible remedy exists. Breach may result in criminal sanctions, repayment claims, disqualification, and personal damages liability.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for prohibited post-insolvency payments, unpaid taxes, employee withholdings, and creditor losses. Imprisonment is possible for insolvency offences, fraud, or tax evasion. Court-ordered travel restrictions may arise in criminal or insolvency proceedings but are not automatic.
Accounting, record-retention, and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may estimate the tax base, deny deductions, impose documentation surcharges and administrative fines, and refer deliberate falsification or destruction for criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors are responsible for proper organization, supervision, and retention of accounting records and may incur civil, tax, and criminal liability for intentional or negligent failures.

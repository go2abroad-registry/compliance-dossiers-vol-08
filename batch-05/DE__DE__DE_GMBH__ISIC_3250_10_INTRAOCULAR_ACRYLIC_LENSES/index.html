Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt), the Federal Central Tax Office, for EU VAT ID issuance (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) and international procedure; the local Finanzamt (municipal tax office) competent for the registered seat administers corporation tax, trade tax, wage tax and VAT audits. Unified electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

        
E-government filing portal
1) File the official tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent Finanzamt, through ELSTER or a licensed tax advisor (Steuerberater), attaching notarized constitutional documents (Gesellschaftsvertrag, Handelsregister extract, director identification). 2) Receive the local statistic tax number (Steuernummer) within 2–8 weeks of Handelsregister entry; interim provisional numbers are available for urgent start-up operations. 3) Submit a separate online application to BZSt under § 27a UStG for the DE+9-digit VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer), processed within 1–6 weeks. 4) Register with customs for an EORI number to enable ATLAS export declarations. 5) Non-EU resident founders and directors must grant German-language powers of attorney; non-resident foreign suppliers may be obliged to appoint a fiscal representative under § 22g UStG where reverse-charge protection is unavailable. All registrations are free of charge.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — GmbH is a legally distinct taxable person under the German Corporate Income Tax Act (KStG): 15% corporate income tax, 5.5% solidarity surcharge assessed on that tax, plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, 7–17% effective depending on the local Hebesatz), producing a combined standard effective burden of approximately 29–33% on retained profits. No default pass-through status; profit distributions trigger 25% statutory final withholding tax (tax treaty relief available). — 1) File the official tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent Finanzamt, through ELSTER or a licensed tax advisor (Steuerberater), attaching notarized constitutional documents (Gesellschaftsvertrag, Handelsregister extract, director identification). 2) Receive the local statistic tax number (Steuernummer) within 2–8 weeks of Handelsregister entry; interim provisional numbers are available for urgent start-up operations. 3) Submit a separate online application to BZSt under § 27a UStG for the DE+9-digit VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer), processed within 1–6 weeks. 4) Register with customs for an EORI number to enable ATLAS export declarations. 5) Non-EU resident founders and directors must grant German-language powers of attorney; non-resident foreign suppliers may be obliged to appoint a fiscal representative under § 22g UStG where reverse-charge protection is unavailable. All registrations are free of charge.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation — GmbH is a legally distinct taxable person under the German Corporate Income Tax Act (KStG): 15% corporate income tax, 5.5% solidarity surcharge assessed on that tax, plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, 7–17% effective depending on the local Hebesatz), producing a combined standard effective burden of approximately 29–33% on retained profits. No default pass-through status; profit distributions trigger 25% statutory final withholding tax (tax treaty relief available).

        
Available election forms
Germany does not operate a check-the-box election. Available mechanisms: (1) Organschaft tax group election under §§ 14–19 KStG combined with an executed profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag), consolidating corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT (VAT-Organschaft under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG); (2) trade tax group consolidation; (3) § 8c KStG loss carry-forward restructuring planning; (4) hybrid-mismatch transition elections under § 4k EStG (ATAD implementation); (5) flat-rate input VAT deduction election under § 23 UStG for low-volume taxpayers; (6) statutory option to waive VAT exemption for immovable property transactions under § 9 UStG.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
19%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Small-enterprise (Kleinunternehmer) exemption: €25,000 actual prior-year turnover and €100,000 current-year forecast (§ 19 UStG, thresholds effective 1 January 2025). Intra-Community distance sales and B2C electronic services: €10,000 annual EU-wide threshold. Foreign non-resident suppliers are mandatorily registrable from the first euro of taxable German supply (no threshold applies); most B2B supplies by non-residents fall under reverse charge.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating (VAT-exempt with input VAT deduction) applies to: (a) exports of goods to non-EU destinations under § 4 No. 1a and § 6 UStG, provided the goods physically leave the EU within the statutory 3-month export period and the exporter obtains verifiable proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis); (b) intra-Community supplies under § 4 No. 1b and § 6a UStG where the customer's valid EU VAT ID and qualifying transport evidence exist; (c) ancillary services directly connected with the exported goods. Intraocular acrylic lenses supplied domestically are taxable at the 7% reduced rate under Annex 2 No. 53 UStG (artificial body parts), but export and intra-Community supplies override the reduced rate with the 0% export regime.

Germany is phasing in mandatory B2B structured e-invoicing (XRechnung per EN 16931, ZUGFeRD 2.x): receipt capability mandatory from 1 January 2025; issuance obligation from 1 January 2027 for sellers with prior-year turnover above €800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all sellers. Invoices must satisfy § 14 UStG and be archived in machine-readable form for 10 years in accordance with GoBD and § 147 AO. VAT returns and EC Sales Lists must be filed electronically through ELSTER (EPÜ).

      
Zero-rating claims are reviewed through recurring external tax audits (Außenprüfung) and customs cross-verification (EMCS/ICS). Missing or invalid export proof triggers retrospective VAT assessment plus monthly interest of 0.5% under § 233a AO, with related input-VAT corrections. Intentional misdeclaration constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years (6 months to 10 years for especially serious cases) or criminal fines; negligent conduct is fined up to €50,000 under § 378 AO. Corporate fines under § 30 OWiG reach €10 million for serious offenses. Erroneously refunded VAT is clawed back with statutory interest, enforced by seizure, and documentary fabrication additionally triggers asset forfeiture under § 73 StGB.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Germany is in the eurozone; the euro is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions. There is no exchange-control law restricting conversion, repatriation, or profit remittance. EU primary law on free movement of capital applies. A German GmbH may freely remit dividends and service payments abroad, subject only to fiscal withholding obligations on distributions and applicable tax treaties; no FX validation or central bank permit is required.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements exist under German law. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or any foreign currency. For statistical and balance-of-payments purposes, cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation); the compliance obligation lies with the resident entity.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank; supervisory and licensing authorities include Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) and, for export controls, Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle (BAFA).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility: Medium. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz) can be used as auxiliary payment rails for cross-border collections and FX execution. However, a high-risk medical-device manufacturer requiring documentary credit issuance, trade settlement, and committed treasury lines should maintain a primary relationship with a fully licensed commercial bank. Non-EEA EMIs are not viable unless they hold a German or European passporting licence.

Defense evidence: EU export control classification evidence, BAFA or customs statements confirming no dual-use restriction, end-user certificates, import permits from destination regulators, pro forma invoices and sales contracts under Incoterms 2020, and a written export compliance policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound equity injections from non-resident shareholders must be routed by SWIFT MT103 with a clear Bundesbank external-sector reporting category for a direct investment capital contribution to a GmbH. The contribution should be made in EUR to a designated capital escrow or operational account; proof of receipt and bank certificate must be retained for the commercial register (Handelsregister) filing of the capital increase. Cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within the statutory AWV reporting period. The inbound wire should reference the capital increase registration number and avoid mixing commercial payments with equity contributions.For international trade settlements, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for cash remittances and documentary letters of credit issued by the German bank via SWIFT MT700 subject to UCP 600. Intra-EU payments can use SEPA Credit Transfers but not for the full documentary-credit infrastructure. For export sales of intraocular acrylic lenses to non-EEA markets, an irrevocable, confirmed letter of credit at sight is the recommended clearing rail where counterparty credit risk exists. SWIFT MT202 can be used for interbank settlement only after title documents and compliance checks are completed.FX outright forwards between EUR and USD, GBP, CHF, JPY and liquid emerging-market currenciesFX swaps (exchange of principal at two agreed value dates)FX options (vanilla calls and puts) for hedging transactional exposureCross-currency interest-rate swaps for long-term intra-group financing and bilateral trade balancesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted on a constrained basis for currencies with restricted deliverability, subject to bank counterparty approval and internal EMIR/MiFID II governance

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective rate approx. 14-17% depending on the municipality), yielding an aggregate statutory burden of approximately 30%

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
26.375% (25% capital gains tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax). This is the statutory rate for dividends distributed by a German GmbH to non-resident shareholders unless a reduction or exemption applies under an applicable double taxation agreement (DTA) or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 in aggregate annual cross-border controlled transactions triggers the mandatory preparation of a Local File. A Master File becomes mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated revenues exceed EUR 100 million, the group comprises more than one group entity, and the German taxpayer's cross-border controlled transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000. These thresholds comply with the EU Transfer Pricing Directive and German sections 90(3) AO and 33a-33g of the Tax Administration Act (VwVfG).

      
Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a codified Economic Substance Regulation in the style of the UAE, but the substance of a GmbH is comprehensively scrutinized under: (i) the anti-treaty abuse provision in section 50d(3) EStG requiring the beneficial owner to carry on substantive business activities with adequate personnel, premises, and functions; (ii) the Principal Purpose Test embodied in the OECD MLI and EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive; (iii) controlled foreign company (CFC) rules in sections 7-13 AStG; and (iv) the general anti-abuse rule in section 42 AO. A German GmbH that merely provides nominal holding functions without commercial substance, employees, or decision-making capacity will be disregarded for treaty and directive purposes, its accrued profits reallocated, and treaty benefits denied.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)
        
Statutory sanction: Under section 370 AO, tax evasion is a criminal offence punishable with imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine; in particularly serious cases (large-scale evasion, abuse of office, or deliberate concealment above EUR 50,000 per act) imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years. In addition, the evaded taxes are collected with late-payment interest at 6% p.a. (section 233a AO) and monetary penalties per the Criminal Code.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and managing officers face personal criminal liability. The court may impose imprisonment, monetary criminal fines, and a travel ban; civilly, the director may be held personally liable under sections 34 AO and 43 GmbHG for unpaid corporate tax if the GmbH is insolvent and the director failed to file returns or remit withholding taxes. Under the corporate veil-piercing doctrine, if the director deliberately misused the GmbH structure to evade tax or asset protection, the court may order unlimited personal restitution and prohibit the director from serving as a manager for up to five years.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Under section 42 AO, the tax treatment may disregard the legal form and reallocate income to the persons who actually earn it. Treaty benefits may be denied under section 50d(3) EStG, leading to full 26.375% dividend withholding taxation without refund; CFC rules under the AStG may attribute passive income to the shareholder and impose punitive taxation. Penalties include back-taxes of up to several years, late-payment interest, and fines of up to EUR 25,000 for late filing; the GmbH may also lose its trade tax assessment basis.

        
Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for the GmbH's minimum tax payment obligations if they intentionally keep the company undercapitalised and allow continued trading while insolvent. Directors face civil liability under German tort law for fraudulent misrepresentation to tax authorities and trade registers; courts may impose a ban on engaging in commercial activities and, in repeated cases, personal bankruptcy or asset seizure. Non-resident owners who exercise de facto management from abroad may be deemed to have a permanent establishment or a taxable presence in Germany, piercing the GmbH shield.

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Export Control Violations
        
Statutory sanction: Under sections 56-57 GwG, administrative fines of up to EUR 1,000,000 for natural persons and up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for legal entities may be imposed. Export of intraocular lenses without the required EU medical device CE marking or export authorisations may trigger criminal penalties under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) with imprisonment up to 5 years and confiscation of the underlying assets.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are personally responsible for implementing an effective AML compliance system. Personal liability arises for directors who knowingly execute or instruct prohibited wire transfers or accept funds despite reasonable red flags; they may be subject to criminal prosecution, imprisonment up to 5 years, fines, asset freezes, and a travel ban preventing exit from Germany. Under the German Act on Administrative Offences (OWiG), managers can be fined separately from the company, and their professional liability may extend to civil restitution of the company's and the state's losses.

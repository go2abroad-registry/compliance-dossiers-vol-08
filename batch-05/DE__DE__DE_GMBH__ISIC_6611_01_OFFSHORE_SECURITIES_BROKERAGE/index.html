Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office, with the electronic filing portal ELSTER (www.elster.de) used for all statutory tax registrations and returns; local tax offices (Finanzämter) of the GmbH's registered seat administer corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), payroll tax, and VAT assessments; the Generalzolldirektion (German Customs) administers export declarations, EORI registration, customs duties, and import VAT; the BZSt is the central competent authority for issuing the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under §27a UStG and for EU/third-country VAT refund procedures.

E-government filing portal

(1) After notarial incorporation and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), the GmbH files the tax-registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') via ELSTER within one month of business commencement; the locally competent Finanzamt (seat jurisdiction) assigns the statutory Steuernummer and opens tax accounts for corporate, trade, wage, and VAT purposes. (2) The GmbH separately applies to the BZSt for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under §27a UStG using form USt 1 A / online ELSTER procedure; the BZSt verifies the entrepreneur's identity and issues the USt-IdNr. (3) For export and customs activity, the GmbH must obtain an EORI number (DE-xxx) from the BZSt's customs registration desk and register with the ATLAS export declaration system. (4) A GmbH owned by non-residents follows the identical standard procedure; no special TIN regime applies, but offshore securities brokerage additionally requires a BaFin license under the KWG or the Wertpapierinstitutsgesetz (WpIG), compliance with the Money Laundering Act (GwG), and, where relevant, notification to the Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or general pass-through election exists for a GmbH; a GmbH is a per se taxable corporation. Relevant German statutory election and group mechanisms include: (a) the Organschaft fiscal-unity election under §§14–19 KStG with a binding profit-and-loss transfer agreement, consolidating corporate and trade tax results with the parent; (b) the VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §2(2) Nr. 2 UStG, treating the GmbH as a single VAT taxpayer with the parent; (c) for partnerships (not a GmbH), the §1a KStG option to be taxed as a corporation; (d) mandatory hybrid-mismatch restrictions under §4k EStG (ATAD 1/2 implementation), requiring symmetrical tax treatment and denying deduction or non-inclusion benefits for hybrid entities and instruments; and (e) voluntary one-stop-shop (OSS) registration for intra-Community distance sales and digital services in lieu of multiple EU VAT registrations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Generally, every entrepreneur is a VAT taxpayer without a fixed economic registration threshold: a GmbH is registrable upon founding. The small-business (Kleinunternehmer) exemption applies below EUR 25,000 annual turnover from 1 January 2025 (§19 UStG), but a GmbH engaged in retail securities brokerage will in practice exceed such threshold; non-established foreign entrepreneurs have no de minimis threshold — German VAT registration is compulsory where the place of supply is Germany and the reverse-charge procedure does not apply. Intra-Community distance sales and digital services to private consumers trigger a single EU-wide EUR 10,000 cross-border threshold, with an OSS election available in lieu of multi-jurisdiction registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported outside the EU qualify for true zero rating under §6 UStG where the exporter proves removal of the goods from the EU customs territory (Ausfuhrnachweis), supported by the ATLAS customs export declaration, the Export Accompanying Document, and the foreign purchaser's confirmation of arrival. Services supplied in the context of offshore securities brokerage are not zero-rated in the goods sense but fall outside German VAT jurisdiction: B2B brokerage services under §3a(2) UStG are taxable at the recipient's seat, so no German VAT is due when the recipient is established outside Germany; B2C retail securities and derivative brokerage supplied to consumers resident outside the EU is treated as effectively used and enjoyed outside Germany under §3a(3) UStG and therefore not taxable in Germany. Independently of place-of-supply rules, retail agency for equities, CFDs, and derivative instruments is VAT-exempt under §4 Nr. 8 UStG, requiring the GmbH to issue commission invoices without German VAT. Substantiation for the zero-rated or exempt position requires documentary linkage of the client's foreign-residence or foreign-business status (foreign VAT ID or equivalent corporate tax identification), the order and execution contract notes, clearing and settlement confirmations, the commission invoice, and, where physical securities documentation or goods are involved, the complete customs export bundle. Input VAT attributable to export-generating activity may be recovered through the regular VAT return or, for foreign entrepreneurs not established in Germany, through the BZSt refund procedures (USt 1 A for EU entrepreneurs and the special third-country procedure under §18 Abs. 9 UStG), using the identical export-proof documentation.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) and the electronically issued Export Accompanying Document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods from the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing the repatriation of foreign exchange proceeds of the export transaction
  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung) and packing list
  • Proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) under §6 UStG and, where relevant, the Gelangensbestätigung (confirmation of arrival) under §17a UStDV for intra-Community supplies
  • Clearing and settlement statements and contract notes evidencing the cross-border brokerage transaction
  • Client due-diligence records confirming the recipient's non-German residence or business status, including foreign VAT ID or equivalent corporate tax identification

Germany does not operate a transaction-level real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system comparable to the Italian SDI model, but a phased electronic invoicing mandate applies: (a) from 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive EN 16931-compliant electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x); (b) from 1 January 2027, taxpayers with B2B revenue above EUR 800,000 in the prior year must issue all domestic B2B invoices as EN 16931 XML e-invoices; (c) from 1 January 2028, the issue obligation extends to all other domestic B2B taxpayers; (d) cash-register systems require a certified technical security module (TSE) with tamper-proof order-data storage; and (e) all VAT returns, recapitulative statements (Zusammenfassende Meldung), and registration filings are digitally cleared through the ELSTER API and the BZSt's secure XML/Web-service interface (Elster-Schnittstelle), with automatic late-filing and late-payment surcharges. The EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) initiative is expected to harmonize real-time e-invoicing reporting across Member States by approximately 2030.

Export zero-rating and VAT-free export treatment are subject to documentary audit by the operating Finanzamt, the Generalzolldirektion, and the BZSt; the burden of proof for the export or out-of-Germany place of supply rests on the GmbH. Where export documentation is fictitious, incomplete, or forged, the tax authorities may: (i) retrospectively reassess output tax at the standard 19% rate (or the applicable reduced rate) under §§14c and 17 UStG and §174 AO within the standard four-year limitation period, extended to ten years in cases of tax evasion (§169(2) sentence 2 AO); (ii) impose back interest of 0.5% per month on arrears (§233a AO) and late-payment surcharges of 1% per month (§240 AO); (iii) levy fines for VAT infringements (§26a UStG) and penalty assessments for deliberate evasion (§370 AO), with aggravated cases carrying imprisonment of up to ten years; (iv) hold the managing director (Geschäftsführer) and responsible officers personally liable under §§34, 69, 71, and 73 AO for the GmbH's tax arrears; and (v) apply criminal money-laundering provisions under §261 StGB and the Money Laundering Act (GwG) where forged remittance certificates or false declarations are used. The resulting clawback removes all previously unrecognized zero-rated outputs, denies the corresponding input-VAT deductions, and may preclude the GmbH from further export zero-rating or reverse-charge treatment until a robust documentary compliance system is established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the euro under Article 63 TFEU and section 1 AWG. There are no exchange-control licensing gates, no mandatory conversion rates, no restrictions on opening foreign-currency accounts, no blocked-funds regimes, and no prior-approval requirements for profit repatriation by a resident GmbH. The only functional hindrances are (i) the monthly Deutsche Bundesbank external-payment statistics duty (any cross-border payment above EUR 12,500 per calendar month per foreign country must be reported through the Zahlungsbilanzstatistik portal by the 7th working day of the following month); and (ii) tax-driven remittance frictions: dividend distributions are generally subject to 25% withholding (Kapitalertragsteuer plus solidarity surcharge) unless reduced under an applicable double-tax treaty or, for an EU/EEA corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the share capital for at least 12 months, to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) upon presentation of a valid BZSt Freistellungsbescheinigung. Profit remittance additionally requires an audited annual financial statement and a formal shareholders appropriation resolution (Gewinnverwendungsbeschluss). No capital controls or remittance licences apply to the underlying cross-border flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio. A GmbH may lawfully retain brokerage commissions, securities-lending income, marketing fees, and cross-border sales proceeds in any foreign currency in a foreign-currency account with a licensed German credit institution without any obligation to convert into euro. The operative compliance duty is statistical and informational: under AWV sections 66-67, every payment received from or made to a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 in a single calendar month vis-a-vis one foreign country must be declared to the Deutsche Bundesbank using the prescribed purpose codes (e.g., securities brokerage services, direct investment, dividends, commissions) by the 7th working day of the succeeding month. Structured intra-group arrangements must not fragment payments to circumvent the reporting threshold, as the Bundesbank treats economically connected payments as a single reportable stream.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank, Frankfurt am Main) is the competent monetary and external-payments statistics authority; foreign-exchange and cross-border capital-movement administration is shared with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV), with operative export-control licensing performed by BAFA (Bundesamt fuer Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) regulates the banking and securities-institution perimeter for the entity, while the Bundesbank executes the mandatory external-payment reporting regime under AWV sections 57-79.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections from non-resident shareholders must use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 with a purpose-code narrative reading 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION - KAPITALERHOEHUNG, DIREKTINVESTITION' and referencing the GmbH's registered seat, HRB registration number, and the notarial deed number. Under the German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG sections 53-55), the capital increase must be notarised and registered in the commercial register (Handelsregister) before the funds are treated as paid-in share capital; prudent treasury practice instructs the remitter to wire after the registration entry is filed, and the receiving bank may place an internal hold until the updated Handelsregister extract is produced. The payment must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank: the direct-investment position is reportable under AWV sections 67-68 with the code for direct investment, and the cross-border payment itself is reportable under AWV sections 66-67 where it exceeds EUR 12,500 in a calendar month. The defensive documentation bundle for AML review comprises the notarial deed, the post-registration Handelsregister extract, a shareholder-resolution extract, a certified copy of the shareholder's board resolution, the remitter's bank statement, and a letter from the foreign parent's bank confirming that the remitter holds sufficient funds from ordinary commercial activity. Shareholder loans, trade receivables, and client funds must never be routed into the same account as the share-capital account; separate IBANs with clear purpose prefixes are mandatory.Primary clearing rails for outbound payments are: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst, up to EUR 100,000) for euro disbursements to German and EU beneficiaries, with instant settlement through TARGET2 and the EBA CLEARING infrastructure; (ii) SWIFT MT103 with a unique end-to-end transaction reference (UETR) for high-value euro, USD or GBP wire transfers outside the SEPA zone; (iii) SWIFT MT202 CORRES for interbank cover and correspondent settlement; (iv) for securities transactions, delivery-versus-payment settlement in TARGET2-Securities (T2S) through Clearstream Banking Frankfurt as the German central securities depository, with Eurex Clearing acting as CCP for exchange-traded derivatives and CFD-clearing members; and (v) for genuine trade-finance settlements where the brokerage intermediates a financed securities or commodity transaction, a Commercial Letter of Credit governed by UCP 600 or a standby letter of credit under ISP98 is permissible, though it is not the standard rail for pure retail brokerage and requires a separate documentary credit account. Every outbound disbursement must carry the correct Bundesbank payment-statistics purpose code, a beneficiary legal-entity identifier where applicable, compliance with EU Regulation 2015/847 (information accompanying transfers), and an unbroken audit trail from the client order ticket to the segregated client ledger to the executed SWIFT/SEPA message. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge) on the corporate tax amount, plus Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at a typical rate of 14%–17% depending on municipal multiplier, yielding an aggregate effective statutory burden of approximately 30%–33%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) on dividends distributed by a German GmbH

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File required under German Transfer Pricing Documentation Act when cross-border transactions exceed: €1,000,000 for goods transactions or €250,000 for services per annum; additional documentation for significant functions and risks; deemed penalty documentation thresholds apply

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply Caribbean ESR-style regulations directly, but substantive presence is required under case law and the OECD/G20 BEPS framework: substance over form under §42 AO, permanent establishment threshold of §13 AO, and the EU ATAD rules; a GmbH engaged in brokerage must have real management, employees, counterparty contracts, and risk assumption in Germany; artificial arrangements designed to shift profit offshore are taxable as abuse and recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine under §370 AO; up to 10 years for particularly serious cases; confiscation of assets; interest at 6% per annum on evaded tax; automatic disclosure regimes may reduce penalties only before detection

Director personal liability: Managing directors personally liable for unpaid taxes under §§34, 69 AO; criminal conviction triggers imprisonment, travel bans, extradition risk, and civil restitution to the tax authority; veil piercing where corporate funds are mixed or used for private purposes
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under §42 AO; denial of treaty benefits under §50d EStG; tax assessment based on economic reality; fines for false declarations up to €25,000; deregistration from commercial register for abusive seat; criminal exposure for aiding tax evasion

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for resulting tax shortfalls; civil liability under §43 GmbHG for breach of duty; travel bans and passport seizure where criminal investigation is opened; disqualification orders and personal bankruptcy proceedings
Money Laundering / AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed financial activity: imprisonment up to 5 years under §54 KWG; AML violations: fines up to €5 million or 10% of annual turnover under GwG; confiscation of all proceeds; administrative closure of business and revocation of license; inclusion in public enforcement registers

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability for directors where AML obligations knowingly breached; personal fines and imprisonment; travel bans in cross-border cases; civil liability to clients for losses caused by compliance failure; seizure of personal assets as proceeds of crime
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Upward adjustment of income; penalty up to 10% of the adjusted amount (minimum €5,000) for inadequate documentation; daily penalty €100 up to €1 million for late submission; interest at 6% per annum; possible criminal prosecution if deliberate mispricing causes tax loss

Director personal liability: Directors personally responsible for establishing and maintaining documentation; fines and imprisonment for fraudulent transfer pricing; civil claims from co-shareholders or insolvency administrators; travel bans and extradition exposure in serious cross-border violations

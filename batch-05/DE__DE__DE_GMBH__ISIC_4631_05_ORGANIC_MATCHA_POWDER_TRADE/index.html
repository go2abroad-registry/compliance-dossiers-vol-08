Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) through Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt); local tax offices (Finanzämter) administer corporate and VAT registration; official portals are Elster (www.elster.de) and the BZSt website for non-resident VAT ID applications

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first identify the competent local Finanzamt, usually the Finanzamt for the place of intended German economic activity or, for non-residents, the Finanzamt designated by the Federal Central Tax Office (BZSt). Submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via Elster or in paper form, either directly or through a German tax advisor (Steuerberater). The Finanzamt issues the local corporate tax number (Steuernummer). The BZSt then issues the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) after verification; registration is mandatory before commencing taxable supplies or acquiring/importing goods for business use in Germany.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime for a GmbH; a German resident GmbH is automatically an entity-level taxpayer subject to worldwide corporate income tax and trade tax. Available statutory elections include forming a German tax group (Organschaft) under §§14-19 KStG by concluding a profit and loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag), which attributes the GmbH's income and VAT-relevant results to the parent for corporate income tax and trade tax purposes. Hybrid mismatch rules under §4k EStG and CFC rules are mandatory and do not offer elective treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for domestic taxable entrepreneurs; any person performing taxable supplies in Germany must register. Non-resident businesses must register immediately if they make taxable supplies or intra-Community acquisitions in Germany. For cross-border distance sales of goods to German consumers, a simplified EUR 10,000 annual net turnover threshold applies before German VAT registration is required, unless the One-Stop-Shop election is used. Domestic small-business exemption under §19 UStG applies when prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and expected current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to a third country qualify for export VAT zero-rating under §4 No.1a and §6 UStG if the goods are physically dispatched or transported from Germany to a non-EU destination and the exporter holds an accepted customs export declaration and transport evidence. Intra-Community supplies of goods are zero-rated under §4 No.1b and §6a UStG where the buyer is an entrepreneur or legal person with a valid VAT ID in another EU Member State, the goods physically leave Germany, and proof of transport (Gelangensbestätigung) is retained. B2B services supplied by a German GmbH to a recipient established outside the EU are generally not subject to German VAT under the place-of-supply rules in §3a UStG; B2B services to EU business customers are invoiced under reverse charge when the recipient is the taxpayer.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) with movement reference number in ATLAS
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR or other transport document evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT/wire transfer credit confirmation evidencing payment repatriation
  • Buyer's valid VAT ID and export-specific documentation for intra-Community zero-rating (e.g., Gelangensbestätigung)

Mandatory structured e-invoicing is phased in: from 1 January 2025, all German B2B businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices in an EN 16931-compliant format; from 1 January 2027, suppliers with over EUR 800,000 prior-year turnover must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all German B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices. B2G invoices must be transmitted via XRechnung or ZUGFeRD formats. Germany currently does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based clearance, or digital stamping for standard VAT invoices.

German tax authorities may match customs and sales data to verify export zero-rating. If transport or documentation is incomplete, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 19% VAT, with interest accruing at 0.15% per month and possible late-payment penalties. Fraudulent claims attract tax evasion penalties under §370 AO: fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in especially serious cases; managing directors and beneficial owners may be held personally liable; clawback can extend to 10 years for deliberately concealed matters.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a fully convertible currency regime with mandatory observance of EU capital movement provisions under Articles 63-66 TFEU. Capital account inflows and outflows ring-fence no sectoral restrictions for a wholesale matcha powder distribution GmbH; profit distribution to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted and may be executed in EUR via SWIFT or SEPA without prior exchange control approval. Current account payments for imports and exports are likewise unrestricted. The only compliance burdens are statistical reporting obligations to Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border payments and receivables exceeding EUR 12,500 per reporting period, and the submission of balance-of-payments payment statistics codes where triggered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio in Germany. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or any convertible currency at the free disposition of the exporter. However, under Section 68 AWV in conjunction with Allgemeiner Verwaltungsvorschrift, resident enterprises must report to Deutsche Bundesbank any payment received from a non-resident for exports of goods or services, if the aggregate value of the individual transaction exceeds EUR 12,500, no later than the 7th day of the month following the receipt, using the standard 'Zahlungsstatistik' (payment statistics) form. For foreign currency receipts, the bank posts the onward or converted EUR amount to the account; conversion is performed at the bank's applicable exchange rate without any central bank mandate on the conversion ratio or timing.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting on behalf of the Bundesministerium der Finanzen (Federal Ministry of Finance) and the Federal Government, supported by the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) for supervised financial institutions and by the Statistisches Bundesamt for external sector statistics. Foreign exchange administration is governed primarily by the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG) and the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with restrictive suitability. German and EU-regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Revolut Business, and Paddle appear viable for auxiliary cross-border payment routing and FX conversion of operational flows, but not for capital escrow formation, statutory GmbH contribution verification, or high-value documentary letter of credit transactions. EMIs must hold a BaFin license under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) or EU passport under PSD2; otherwise, they are treated as unregulated shadow payment providers. For capital account integrity, the commercial register registrar and tax office expect a regulated credit institution; therefore, the capital escrow account must remain with a tier-one prudentially licensed German bank. EMIs may be appended as a supplementary disbursement rail only after the primary bank account is active.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The equity injection from a non-resident shareholder must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 / SEPA CT in EUR to the GmbH's capital escrow account with the designated tier-one German bank. The payment must state the legally correct purpose: 'Einlage in das gezeichnete Kapital der GmbH' (contribution to the subscribed capital of the GmbH), together with the invoice/certificate reference or commercial register file number. For German external trade statistics, the bank will encode a payment statistics code (Zahlungsstatistikcode) according to the Bundesbank's classification, typically 'A10' for participations and equity capital contributions, or the specific 9-digit commodity code for the underlying commercial relationship if the capital is tagged as prepayment; however, pure equity contributions must remain under the capital category. The bank must also receive a copy of the notarial contribution acknowledgment or an attorney-written capital contribution confirmation; the funds should remain unencumbered for a minimum seven-day confirmation window for the commercial register court before any disbursement is initiated.For supplier settlements for organic matcha powder purchases, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 in EUR or JPY for substantial transactions. The trade payment message must include the contract number, commercial invoice number, and purpose of payment code classifying imported or exported goods per EU Intrastat and Germany external balance of payments statistics. For high-value procurement with non-EU Japanese or Chinese origin, a documentary letter of credit (Irrevocable Confirmed LC) can be established at Deutsche Bank or Commerzbank with a 90-day tenor, payable to the beneficiary via MT700 issuance and MT754/MT750 communication. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax, producing a combined federal rate of 15.825%. A GmbH also pays municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), calculated using the municipality's multiplier; the combined corporate and trade-tax burden commonly approximates 30% but is municipality- and profit-dependent.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, an effective domestic rate of 26.375%, before any church tax where applicable. A treaty may reduce the rate, commonly to 15% for portfolio dividends, subject to the relevant treaty and beneficial-ownership conditions. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or German domestic relief may provide exemption or refund for qualifying corporate parents meeting ownership, holding-period, substance, and anti-abuse requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German documentation thresholds generally require a Local File for cross-border related-party transactions where annual consideration exceeds EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions. A Master File is generally required where the group's consolidated revenue meets or exceeds EUR 100 million. Thresholds, scope, and documentation deadlines must be checked under the rules applicable to the relevant year; documentation may be demanded earlier in an audit and generally must be produced within 30 days of a request under the applicable rules.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no general standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant under transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty and EU anti-abuse provisions, controlled-foreign-company rules, and general anti-avoidance rules. Maintain evidence of actual functions, assets, risks, decision-making, personnel, premises, and commercial purpose. A German GmbH distributing matcha should support its pricing, inventory and market risks, procurement functions, and any intra-group service or financing arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 of the Fiscal Code (AO) may be punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Unpaid tax, interest, and administrative enforcement remain payable. Negligent tax reduction may be an administrative offence subject to a fine.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal and administrative liability for their own conduct and may be liable for tax debts where culpable breach of their tax-payment duties causes non-collection. Civil recovery, asset enforcement, and travel restrictions are not automatic consequences of a tax assessment; criminal procedural measures may apply where legally justified.
Late filing, non-payment, and breach of tax duties

Statutory sanction: Late-filing and late-payment surcharges, interest, estimated assessments, enforcement measures, and statutory fines may apply. Intentional or reckless failures can escalate to tax-evasion or tax-reduction offences depending on the facts.

Director personal liability: Managing directors must ensure timely filing, payment, and retention of records. Personal liability may arise under the AO for culpable failure to discharge tax duties; criminal liability requires the applicable offence elements.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income and impose additional tax and interest. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation may trigger statutory surcharges, generally calculated by reference to the income adjustment and subject to statutory minimums and maximums, as well as estimation or other procedural consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability for culpable breaches of tax duties or personal criminal liability for intentional participation in tax offences. A pricing adjustment alone does not automatically create personal criminal liability.
Abusive treaty or withholding-tax relief claims

Statutory sanction: Relief may be denied or recovered, with tax, interest, and possible penalties. Knowingly false claims may constitute tax evasion or another offence under applicable law.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may be personally exposed for knowing participation in false filings or culpable failure to remit withholding tax; liability depends on the statutory elements and evidence.
Corporate insolvency and tax-payment misconduct

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include director liability for prohibited payments, damages to the company or insolvency estate, and criminal sanctions for insolvency offences where their elements are met. Tax claims and insolvency remedies remain separate.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited payments and culpable breaches of insolvency and tax duties. Imprisonment or travel restrictions are not automatic and require the applicable legal basis and process.
Money laundering, sanctions, and suspicious cross-border payments

Statutory sanction: Consequences depend on the conduct and applicable German and EU law and may include criminal fines or imprisonment, confiscation, administrative fines, and transaction restrictions. AML reporting obligations depend on the GmbH's activity and regulated status; ordinary matcha trading is not, solely by that fact, a regulated AML business.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for their own participation or culpable supervisory failures where a statutory duty applies. Corporate liability, confiscation, and individual sanctions are fact-specific; travel bans and imprisonment are not automatic.

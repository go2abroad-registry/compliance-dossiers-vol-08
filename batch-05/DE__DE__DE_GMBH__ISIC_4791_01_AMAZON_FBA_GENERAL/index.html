Before commercial register application, at least EUR 12,500 (50%) must be paid-in in cash and be at the free disposal of the prospective managing directors; a bank deposit certificate or notarial escrow confirmation is required. For a single-shareholder GmbH, practice requires payment of the full EUR 25,000 cash contribution into the capital deposit account.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office) for VAT ID allocation; local Finanzamt (German tax office) for income/trade tax registration and assessments; portal: ELSTER (https://www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned GmbH: (1) Register the company with the local trade office (Gewerbeamt) and obtain commercial register entry (Handelsregister), (2) submit tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER or paper form to the competent Finanzamt to obtain the tax number (Steuernummer), (3) apply separately to the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) within one month of commencing taxable activity; non-resident shareholders may appoint a fiscal representative where required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned GmbH: (1) Register the company with the local trade office (Gewerbeamt) and obtain commercial register entry (Handelsregister), (2) submit tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER or paper form to the competent Finanzamt to obtain the tax number (Steuernummer), (3) apply separately to the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) within one month of commencing taxable activity; non-resident shareholders may appoint a fiscal representative where required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not adopt US-style check-the-box elections for domestic corporations; the GmbH is per se subject to corporate income tax and trade tax. Certain elective mechanisms exist: formation of a fiscal unity (Organschaft) under Section 14(1) KStG via a valid profit and loss transfer agreement (Beherrschungs- und Gewinnabführungsvertrag), election to treat a hybrid entity as fiscally transparent for German tax purposes under the ATAD anti-hybrid rules (Section 50d EStG), and option to apply EU reverse-charge rules where statutory conditions are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Generally no turnover threshold for non-resident suppliers carrying on taxable supplies in Germany — VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction. For established resident entrepreneurs, the small business exemption (Kleinunternehmer) applies up to EUR 22,000 annual turnover. For cross-border distance sales of goods within the EU, the union-wide threshold of EUR 10,000 applies; exceeding this triggers taxation at destination with no German registration required under the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods dispatched or transported out of the EU qualify for the export VAT exemption (0%) under Section 4 No. 1(a) UStG if the seller retains documentary evidence of exportation and has received the goods in a member state before dispatch. Intra-community supplies to a taxable person with a valid German VAT ID are zero-rated under Section 4 No. 1(b) UStG, subject to EC Sales List reporting. For Amazon FBA general retail exports to third countries, the seller must substantiate physical export with a customs export declaration; movement of own stock to FBA warehouses in other EU member states is considered an intra-community transfer (not a sale) and is zero-rated if VAT ID and transport evidence are provided.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) lodged via German ATLAS/ECTS with assigned EXR number
  • Transport documentation evidencing movement out of the EU — Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence proving receipt of consideration from the customer

Germany does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, API tax stamping, or government-triggered electronic clearance for ordinary B2B invoices. Mandatory e-invoicing applies to domestic B2B transactions effective 1 January 2025, requiring acceptance of structured electronic formats (e.g., XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.0.3) capable of machine-readable extraction, but submission remains batch-oriented via ELSTER. For FBA and marketplace supplies, VAT returns and EC Sales Lists must be transmitted electronically via ELSTER/XML; cash register receipts require certified technical security modules (TSE) with digital signature but do not constitute a national e-clearing architecture.

Export zero-rating is denied retroactively if proof of export is not produced within 60 days after issue of the invoice or, if later, 6 months after the supply, failing which VAT must be adjusted under Section 17 UStG plus interest. Goods that do not leave the territory or fabricated evidence trigger full VAT recapture, evasion penalties under Section 370 AO up to 10 years imprisonment and fines, and under Section 14c UStG unlawful invoice showings attract VAT liability. Cross-border missing trader abuse and MTIC fraud are subject to criminal prosecution and joint and several liability; in severe cases, retrospective clawback covers the entire supply chain.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the EU free movement of capital framework (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Articles 63-66). There are no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or repatriation of capital for non-resident shareholders, provided that corporate formalities (e.g., annual financial statements, tax compliance) are met. The euro is fully convertible; no central bank approval is required for cross-border transactions. However, statistical reporting obligations apply for payments exceeding €12,500 (since 2022 threshold) to the Bundesbank for balance of payments purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose any mandatory surrender or conversion requirements for export proceeds. Exporters are free to hold foreign currency in domestic or foreign accounts. However, all cross-border payments and receipts must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes if they exceed €12,500, within a specified timeline (typically monthly). No conversion ratio is mandated; the exporter may choose to convert or retain foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision; the Bundesbank is responsible for foreign trade and payments statistics and monitoring under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Regulation (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for ancillary liquidity management, but not as a primary bank account for statutory compliance. German law requires a business to have a bank account with a licensed credit institution (Kreditinstitut) for certain corporate actions (e.g., capital deposit, tax payments). Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Stripe, or PPS are not full banks and cannot provide IBANs in Germany without a partner bank. They are viable for cross-border collections and disbursements, but the primary corporate account must be with a BaFin-licensed bank. EMIs are subject to the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and must be registered with BaFin. They can be used for operational liquidity, but not for statutory capital escrow or tax authority payments.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership complexity (e.g., non-EU shareholders or layered holding structures)

Root cause: Beneficial ownership complexity (e.g., non-EU shareholders or layered holding structures)

Defense evidence: Provide a clear organizational chart, certified copies of passports, proof of source of funds for initial capital, and a detailed business plan. Obtain a tax identification number (Steuernummer) and VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) to demonstrate legitimate operations. For non-EU shareholders, provide a legal opinion from a recognized law firm on the corporate structure and beneficial ownership transparency.
High-risk jurisdiction exposure (e.g., payments from or to countries with AML deficiencies)

Root cause: High-risk jurisdiction exposure (e.g., payments from or to countries with AML deficiencies)

Defense evidence: Maintain a comprehensive transaction monitoring policy, provide evidence of due diligence on counterparties (e.g., Amazon marketplace seller agreements), and implement enhanced KYC procedures. Prepare a risk assessment report and a compliance manual. If the company deals with Amazon FBA, provide the Amazon seller account verification and historical transaction records to show legitimate e-commerce activity.
Unusual transaction patterns (e.g., rapid in-and-out flows, large cash deposits)

Root cause: Unusual transaction patterns (e.g., rapid in-and-out flows, large cash deposits)

Defense evidence: Provide a detailed explanation of the business model (Amazon FBA), including sales cycles, inventory purchases, and payment schedules. Maintain invoices, shipping documents, and Amazon settlement reports. Ensure all transactions are traceable and consistent with the declared business purpose. Implement a formal treasury policy and maintain a ledger of all cross-border payments.
Insufficient corporate substance (e.g., no physical office, no employees)

Root cause: Insufficient corporate substance (e.g., no physical office, no employees)

Defense evidence: Provide a lease agreement for a registered office, evidence of a local registered agent, and a description of the operational management. For a GmbH, a minimum of one managing director is required; provide their residence and employment contract. If the company is a pure Amazon FBA seller, demonstrate that the director is actively managing the business from Germany, and provide a local phone number and business email. Consider hiring a local accountant or tax advisor to strengthen substance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For initial capital injection (Stammkapital) into the GmbH's capital escrow account (Kapitalkonto), the transfer must be made via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank. The purpose code must be 'S0101' (Direct investment) or 'S0102' (Capital contribution) under the German balance of payments statistics. The bank will require a copy of the articles of association and the notarized deed of incorporation. For subsequent shareholder loans or additional capital, use purpose code 'S0103' (Loans). All inbound transfers must be reported to the Bundesbank if they exceed €12,500. The bank will verify the source of funds and may request a declaration of the beneficial owner.For trade settlements (e.g., purchasing inventory from suppliers in China or other countries), the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for wire transfers. For larger transactions, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) may be used, especially for high-value purchases. The bank will require the underlying commercial invoice, bill of lading, and purchase order. For Amazon FBA, disbursements from Amazon are typically received via ACH or SEPA in EUR, but for cross-border supplier payments, use SWIFT. The purpose code for imports of goods is 'S0201' (Imports of goods). For services, use 'S0202' (Services). Ensure that all payments are reported to the Bundesbank if they exceed €12,500.Forward contracts (Devisentermingeschäft) with a German bank or EU-authorized institution, up to 12 months tenor, for hedging EUR/USD or EUR/CNY exposure.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are permissible for currencies with limited convertibility (e.g., CNY) but are typically offered by international banks with a German branch; they are allowed under German law as long as the underlying exposure is genuine.Currency swaps (Währungsswap) are permissible for longer-term hedging, subject to ISDA documentation and credit approval.Options (FX options) are also permissible but less common for small e-commerce entities; they require a premium payment and are subject to suitability assessment.

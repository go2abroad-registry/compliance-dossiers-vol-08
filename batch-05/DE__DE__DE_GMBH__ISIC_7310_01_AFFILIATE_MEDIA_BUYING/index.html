Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no general statutory company-name reservation with a 30-day validity period. Obtain a written IHK name assessment where available and coordinate filing with the competent local register. An IHK assessment does not replace the register court's determination.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

German Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and allocation, and the locally competent revenue office (Finanzamt) for income/corporate/trade tax administration; mandatory electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For foreign entities: (i) determine German permanent establishment or direct VAT registration, file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the responsible Finanzamt to obtain the local Steuernummer; (ii) apply for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) via BZSt using Form USt 1 TN or the BZSt online platform, in each case with certified original documents (articles of association, commercial register excerpt, proof of place of management) and, for non-EU entities, appoint a German fiscal representative or VAT agent; (iii) use ELSTER for ongoing VAT returns, wage tax notices, and corporate tax declarations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For foreign entities: (i) determine German permanent establishment or direct VAT registration, file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the responsible Finanzamt to obtain the local Steuernummer; (ii) apply for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) via BZSt using Form USt 1 TN or the BZSt online platform, in each case with certified original documents (articles of association, commercial register excerpt, proof of place of management) and, for non-EU entities, appoint a German fiscal representative or VAT agent; (iii) use ELSTER for ongoing VAT returns, wage tax notices, and corporate tax declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a 'check-the-box' pass-through election. A GmbH is subject to entity-level corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) and solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag). Available elections include: (i) Organschaft (corporate and VAT fiscal unity) under §§14, 17 KStG and §2 UStG with profit-and-loss pooling; (ii) trade tax group (Gewerbesteuerorganschaft); and (iii) written informational declarations required under German hybrid mismatch rules (§4k EStG, ATAD II implementation), which disallow deductions for hybrid mismatch arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general threshold for entrepreneurs exercising economic activity; the §19 UStG small-business exemption applies when previous year turnover does not exceed €22,000 and estimated current-year turnover does not exceed €50,000, but foreign-supplier reverse-charge VAT registration and EU distance-selling thresholds may trigger compulsory registration at lower limits; once opted/registered, 19% standard VAT applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% rate for physical exports delivered to a third-country destination, under §6 UStG, provided transport from Germany to non-EU territory is demonstrated and goods do not undergo EU-use/enhancement before export; Services: B2B services (including digital media buying and affiliate advertising) supplied to a non-EU business are placed outside German VAT at the recipient's establishment (EU B2B place-of-supply rule), so no German VAT applies, but if services are supplied to a non-EU consumer or linked to domestic consumption, the supplier may need a VAT refund or input VAT adjustment; evidence must substantiate the recipient's country and the actual export/cross-border delivery chain.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with AE/BE confirmation (one-time certificate or electronic IEAT data)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill (AWB) or CMR/transport evidence proving movement to non-EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement corroborating receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice, intended-use contract and, if applicable, export document and export notification under §§8-9 UStDV for export proof

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing under §14 UStG (as amended by the Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025, domestic B2B sellers must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.0, EDI), and from 1 January 2027 all taxable sellers with >€800,000 prior-year turnover must issue such e-invoices; no continuous real-time validation by tax authorities is required at invoice level (no Italian-style SDI); VAT declarations are filed through ELSTER, and electronic invoice archives must be GoBD-compliant and may be requested in audits.

Incorrect or missing export substantiation leads to retroactive assessment of output VAT with late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (§233a AO), VAT evasion penalty under §370 AO (imprisonment up to 5 years or fine) and administrative fines under §26a UStG up to €5,000 per infringement; tax clawback, loss of zero-rating and refund denial; statute of limitation extended to 7 years (or 10 years for concealment/evasion); managing directors face personal liability under §69 AO for taxes evaded by the GmbH.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible with no exchange controls in Germany. Both current-account transactions (service fees, affiliate commissions, royalties) and capital-account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, profit distribution) are unrestricted under EU Treaty Article 63 on free movement of capital. No convertibility licensing or approval is required for inbound or outbound capital. Profit remittance is obstructed only by tax at source (25% corporate dividend withholding plus solidarity surcharge where applicable, subject to treaty relief) and by standard commercial banking KYC, not by exchange-control limits. Statutory reporting applies to cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction and to cross-border receivables/payables exceeding EUR 5,000, to be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export or services proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely, held in foreign accounts, or converted freely at market rates; the only obligation is statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV for qualifying cross-border receipts.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), operating within the Eurosystem; the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV) is administered by the Bundesbank for cross-border payment statistics and reporting, while the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) enforces sanctions and the Federal Ministry of Economics oversees restrictive measures.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.00% German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.50% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal corporate burden of 15.825% before municipal trade tax. Municipal trade tax generally increases the combined burden to approximately 23%–33%, with approximately 30% common in major municipalities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.00% German capital-yield withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer), plus a 5.50% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic withholding burden of 26.375%. The withholding is generally imposed on gross dividends and similar profit distributions unless a statutory exemption or treaty procedure applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the annual corporate income tax return is generally due by 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser is authorized, the statutory extended filing deadline generally applies, subject to current legislative exceptions and official extension rules; for fiscal years ending on 31 December, the extended deadline is generally the last day of February of the second following year. A non-calendar fiscal year follows the equivalent seven-month rule after fiscal year-end, subject to adviser extensions.

Deadline: · Frequency:

VAT advance returns and payments are generally due by the 10th day after the relevant month or quarter. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German tax documentation is required for cross-border related-party transactions under sections 90(3) and 90(4) AO and the Foreign Tax Act. A Local File is generally triggered by material related-party transaction volumes, commonly exceeding EUR 6,000,000 annually for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 annually for services and other transactions, and by extraordinary business transactions regardless of volume. A Master File is generally required where the German entity belongs to an internationally operating group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 100,000,000 in the preceding financial year. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously within the statutory period and submitted promptly upon request, generally within 30 days.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate, standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime equivalent to certain offshore ESR systems. Substance is nevertheless relevant under the general anti-abuse rule in section 42 AO, treaty anti-abuse provisions, section 50d(3) EStG, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive implementation, controlled-foreign-company rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, withholding-tax relief, and transfer-pricing principles. A German GmbH should maintain real decision-making, appropriately qualified personnel, premises or operational capability, commercial risk assumption, independent governance, bank-account control, books and records, and evidence that affiliate media-buying functions are actually performed in Germany. A nominal GmbH that merely receives and remits funds, lacks personnel and control, or contracts away all functions may face denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization, permanent-establishment disputes, profit adjustments, and director-liability consequences.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Section 370 AO generally provides imprisonment for up to five years or a criminal fine. Particularly serious cases may attract imprisonment from six months up to ten years. The tax authority may assess the unpaid tax, interest, and penalties; voluntary disclosure relief is restricted and must satisfy strict statutory conditions before discovery or investigation.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal prosecution, personal tax liability under section 69 AO for culpable breach of tax duties, civil restitution, asset seizure, professional disqualification, and possible travel restrictions where criminal-procedure conditions are met. Imprisonment is possible; corporate limited liability does not shield personal criminal or tax-administration liability.
Late filing, non-payment, and inaccurate accounting

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharges generally start at EUR 25 per commenced month and may reach EUR 25,000. Late-payment surcharges generally accrue at 1% of the rounded outstanding amount per commenced month, with tax interest and estimated assessments also possible. Persistent non-compliance may lead to enforcement, criminal tax proceedings, and refusal of tax certificates.

Director personal liability: Managing directors must organize timely filing and payment. Culpable failure can create personal liability for tax shortfalls under section 69 AO, in addition to civil claims by the GmbH, insolvency administrator, and creditors.
Withholding-tax failure and unlawful profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may remain liable for the unwithheld tax, solidarity surcharge, interest, and related penalties. A disguised profit distribution may be added back for corporate tax and trade tax purposes and may trigger recipient-level withholding obligations. Deliberate concealment may constitute tax evasion under section 370 AO.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability under section 69 AO for failure to withhold and remit, civil liability for unlawful distributions, and criminal liability for intentional tax evasion or breach of insolvency duties. Travel restrictions and imprisonment are possible only under applicable criminal or enforcement orders, not automatically.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make a transfer-pricing adjustment, impose additional corporate and trade tax, deny deductions, assess interest, apply documentation-related surcharges, and invoke withholding tax where the payment is recharacterized as a dividend or royalty. Documentation deficiencies can shift the evidentiary burden and permit adverse estimates.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for negligent tax loss, personal tax liability for culpable breaches, and criminal tax exposure where manipulation is deliberate. A parent-company instruction does not eliminate the German managing director's independent compliance duties.
Shell company, conduit, treaty shopping, and insufficient substance

Statutory sanction: Treaty, EU directive, and domestic withholding relief may be denied under section 50d(3) EStG, section 42 AO, beneficial-ownership principles, and applicable treaty anti-abuse provisions. The tax authority may recharacterize payments, deny deductions, impose withholding, assess additional tax and interest, and challenge the place of effective management or permanent-establishment allocation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for resulting withholding and corporate tax losses where their conduct or omission was culpable. Criminal liability can arise if the structure is knowingly used to conceal taxable income. Corporate veil piercing is not automatic, but statutory personal liability, tort liability, insolvency liability, and criminal responsibility remain available.
VAT carousel, missing-trader, and invoice fraud

Statutory sanction: Input-tax deduction may be denied; VAT may be assessed with interest and surcharges; invoices may be corrected; and intentional conduct may constitute VAT tax evasion under section 370 AO. Criminal penalties can include fines or imprisonment up to the statutory tax-evasion limits, with aggravated cases carrying higher imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal tax liability, criminal prosecution, asset seizure, and civil recovery claims where they authorized or failed to prevent fraudulent VAT claims. Directors must implement supplier verification, invoice controls, VAT-ID checks, and transaction-substance testing.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial-ownership breach

Statutory sanction: Violations of the German Money Laundering Act may result in administrative fines that can reach the statutory maximums applicable to the specific violation, including up to EUR 150,000 for certain breaches and, for specified obliged-entity violations, up to EUR 1,000,000 or a percentage of annual turnover, potentially up to twice the economic benefit. Supervisory measures and business restrictions may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face personal administrative, civil, and criminal exposure where they participate in or knowingly facilitate laundering, sanctions evasion, or false ownership reporting. Funds may be frozen or confiscated; travel restrictions and imprisonment may follow criminal proceedings.
Sanctions, export-control, and restricted-party payments

Statutory sanction: Violations of the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act and applicable EU sanctions may trigger criminal or administrative penalties, fines, confiscation, transaction invalidation, account restrictions, and business prohibitions. The applicable penalty depends on the specific sanctions measure and intent.

Director personal liability: Directors and employees may be individually prosecuted where they authorize, facilitate, or negligently permit prohibited transactions. Personal fines, imprisonment, confiscation, and travel restrictions may apply under the relevant sanctions and criminal provisions.
Insolvency delay and unlawful payments

Statutory sanction: Failure to file for insolvency without undue delay under section 15a InsO may constitute a criminal offense, generally punishable by imprisonment of up to three years or a fine, with aggravated provisions possible. Payments made after insolvency may be clawed back and may create civil liability.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited post-insolvency payments, creditor losses, unpaid taxes, and social-security contributions. Criminal prosecution, asset recovery, director disqualification, travel restrictions, and imprisonment are possible depending on the conduct.
Payroll tax and social-security evasion

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may owe wage tax, solidarity surcharge, social-security contributions, interest, late-payment charges, and penalties. Intentional non-payment of employee social-security contributions can constitute a criminal offense under section 266a StGB; wage-tax concealment may constitute tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible managers may face personal liability, criminal fines, imprisonment, civil claims by employees and social-security institutions, and disqualification from management. Limited liability does not protect a director who intentionally or culpably withholds and fails to remit statutory amounts.

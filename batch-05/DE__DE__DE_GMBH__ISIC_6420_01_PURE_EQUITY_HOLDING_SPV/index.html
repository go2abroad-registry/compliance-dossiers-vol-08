Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification and tax certificates; local Finanzamt (Tax Office) for income and trade tax assessments; ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) is the official e-filing portal

E-government filing portal

The GmbH (or its foreign-corporate founder) completes the online 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' via ELSTER and submits it to the locally competent Finanzamt. This triggers issuance of the Steuernummer (income/trade tax ID). For VAT purposes, the Finanzamt forwards the data to BZSt, which issues a USt-IdNr. Foreign shareholders are permitted; no local director tax representative is required for the GmbH itself, but the legal representative (Geschäftsführer) is responsible for filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — The GmbH (or its foreign-corporate founder) completes the online 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' via ELSTER and submits it to the locally competent Finanzamt. This triggers issuance of the Steuernummer (income/trade tax ID). For VAT purposes, the Finanzamt forwards the data to BZSt, which issues a USt-IdNr. Foreign shareholders are permitted; no local director tax representative is required for the GmbH itself, but the legal representative (Geschäftsführer) is responsible for filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime. Germany applies a single statutory classification of a GmbH as a taxable entity subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Elective mechanisms include: (1) Organschaft (tax group) election combining a parent and subsidiary for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT; (2) VAT grouping (umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft) election for German entities; (3) no hybrid mismatch election, though anti-hybrid rules under ATAD are mandatory and may re-characterize structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 in the preceding year and EUR 50,000 expected in the current year (small business exemption under §19 UStG; below these thresholds a qualifying entrepreneur may be exempt from VAT registration). For intra-Community distance sales the separate registration threshold is EUR 10,000. Pure equity holding activities generally produce no taxable turnover and thus trigger no VAT registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of movable goods from Germany to a third-country territory qualify for 0% VAT under §4(1)(a) and §6 UStG, provided the physical movement is proven by proper evidence. For cross-border services, B2B supplies to taxable persons outside Germany are generally not subject to German VAT under §3a(2) UStG (place of supply at the customer's location); the supplier must hold the customer's valid VAT ID or equivalent proof. Pure equity holding activities do not themselves create export supplies; management or administrative services supplied by the GmbH to foreign subsidiaries may be zero-rated or out of scope if the recipient is a business in another country.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Ausfuhrnachweis via ATLAS system)
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, or Air Waybill proving departure from Germany)
  • Commercial invoice and payment evidence (bank transaction statement; no statutory Inward Remittance Certificate under German law, but payment documentation may be required to substantiate consideration and transfer pricing)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping. Mandated e-invoicing for domestic B2B supplies is phased: from 1 January 2025, all German taxable businesses must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD); from 1 January 2027, businesses with annual turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all other businesses must issue e-invoices.

German customs and tax authorities rigorously audit export-proof documents. Failure to produce the required export evidence within the deadline set by the Finanzamt (generally before final VAT assessment) results in VAT being charged at 19% retrospectively, plus interest under §233a AO and potential late-payment penalties under §240 AO. Intentional zero-rating evasion constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO punishable by up to five years' imprisonment or fines; negligent underpayment is an administrative offence with penalties up to EUR 50,000. No statutory FX repatriation deadline exists in Germany; the 180-day figure is not applicable and, if used, refers only to internal transfer-pricing documentation best practice.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Germany has no general exchange-control regime, mandatory conversion of foreign-currency receipts, or routine central-bank approval requirement for foreign direct investment, intercompany funding, dividend distributions, or loan repayments. Transfers remain subject to AML, sanctions, anti-terrorist-financing, tax, corporate-law, and statistical-reporting obligations. Dividend and profit remittance from a GmbH generally requires distributable profits, properly adopted shareholder resolutions, accounting support, withholding-tax analysis, and satisfaction of beneficial-ownership and treaty requirements. German withholding tax on dividends is generally 25% plus solidarity surcharge, subject to applicable exemptions, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in Germany, tax treaties, anti-abuse rules, and German anti-treaty-shopping provisions. Foreign shareholders may need a Federal Central Tax Office exemption certificate or refund procedure. Payments to related parties must satisfy arm's-length, transfer-pricing, corporate-benefit, and capital-maintenance requirements. Banks may require source-of-funds, purpose, UBO, tax-residency, and transaction-chain evidence before releasing cross-border payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general deadline requiring an exporter to convert foreign-currency receipts into euros. The proposed entity is a passive equity holding company and ordinarily has no export-receipts profile. Any foreign payment reporting under the AWV is separate from currency-conversion obligations. As of 1 January 2025, residents generally report qualifying cross-border payments exceeding EUR 50,000 to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the applicable external-payments reporting framework, subject to statutory exemptions, including certain securities, financial-account, and payment categories. Reporting is statistical rather than an approval mechanism. The reporting threshold and exemptions must be checked against the transaction date and current Bundesbank instructions. Export-control, sanctions, customs, VAT, and trade-statistics obligations may apply independently.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin) supervises German credit institutions, payment institutions, electronic-money institutions, AML controls, and regulated investment services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment operations and low-to-medium as the sole treasury bank. An electronic-money institution or payment institution authorised in Germany or another EEA state may provide euro payment accounts and cross-border payment services through authorisation or passporting, subject to safeguarding, AML, sanctions, transaction-monitoring, and customer-due-diligence requirements. An EMI account is not a bank deposit and generally does not provide deposit-guarantee protection. An offshore non-EEA digital EMI cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services into Germany merely through an online platform; it requires an appropriate German or EEA regulatory basis, an authorised partner, or a narrowly applicable exemption. Fintechs may restrict holding-company SPVs, shareholder funding, high-value dividends, non-EEA corridors, or transactions lacking operating substance. A regulated German bank should normally remain the primary account for GmbH share-capital evidence, statutory payments, dividend distributions, and material intercompany flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route share capital through a German bank account opened in the GmbH's name, preferably from the verified shareholder's account held with a regulated financial institution. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro funding within the SEPA area and SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008 for non-SEPA currencies or corridors. State an accurate payment purpose such as 'GmbH share capital contribution pursuant to subscription agreement' or 'shareholder loan pursuant to dated loan agreement'; do not label a loan as equity or use generic descriptions such as 'investment' where the legal classification differs. Before payment, deliver the subscription or loan agreement, shareholder resolution, source-of-funds evidence, remitter ownership evidence, UBO documentation, and capital-account instructions to the bank. Reconcile the incoming payment to the remitter, currency, amount, and legal instrument. Obtain the bank confirmation required for the commercial-register filing where applicable. Review AWV external-payments reporting, statistical codes, sanctions, AML, tax, and transfer-pricing requirements. Do not route capital through personal accounts, unrelated group companies, unapproved payment agents, or cash.For genuine trade or service payments, use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments and SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 equivalent for international payments, with complete originator, beneficiary, bank, invoice, contract, and remittance information. For higher-risk or documentary transactions, use a commercial letter of credit under applicable UCP 600 rules, documentary collection under URC 522, or bank-guaranteed payment subject to the bank's credit approval. A passive holding company should restrict outbound payments to documented dividends, approved intercompany loans, capital contributions, acquisition consideration, professional fees, taxes, and other authorised corporate expenses; it should not create artificial trade flows. For dividends, verify distributable profits, shareholder approval, withholding-tax treatment, beneficial entitlement, and recipient bank details. For intercompany loans, maintain written agreements, arm's-length pricing, repayment terms, covenant approvals, and transfer-pricing support. Banks may request invoices, contracts, shipping documents, customs records, corporate resolutions, sanctions screening, and evidence of economic purpose before release. Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
KSt 1A, Box 1 – Tax Identification German tax number (Steuernummer) assigned by the locally competent Finanzamt for the GmbH's registered office or place of management. If no Steuernummer has yet been assigned, the GmbH must first register with the Finanzamt at its German registered office and submit the registration forms; a non-resident shareholder cannot bypass this requirement. Late submission triggers a Verspätungszuschlag under §152 AO of up to EUR 25,000 per return, plus §233a AO late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum) on any underpaid assessment. The tax authority may estimate the tax base under §162 AO, and electronic filing defaults are separately fineable if the statutory obligation to file electronically is breached.
KSt 1A, Box 5 – Company Details Legal name, legal form (GmbH), registered office, commercial register number, business purpose and managing directors. The full German-language commercial register extract should be available; a non-resident manager may be listed, but the company must maintain a valid German registered office and a person authorised for tax service. The period must match the annual financial statements and the Gewerbesteuer return; no foreign filing-period conventions apply. Late submission triggers a Verspätungszuschlag under §152 AO of up to EUR 25,000 per return, plus §233a AO late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum) on any underpaid assessment. The German tax balance sheet must include, where applicable, intra-group dividend accruals and shareholdings; the pure holding SPV should avoid mixing foreign tax computations into German tax-basis fields. Late submission triggers a Verspätungszuschlag under §152 AO of up to EUR 25,000 per return, plus §233a AO late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum) on any underpaid assessment. KSt 1A, Box 25 – §8b Adjustments Add-back of the 5% non-deductible business expenses on dividend income and capital gains from corporate participations; confirmation of qualifying shareholding percentages and dates. A non-resident parent should document the direct/indirect shareholding chain and provide subsidiary registers to support the participation dates and percentages for §8b KStG and §9 GewStG relief. Late submission triggers a Verspätungszuschlag under §152 AO of up to EUR 25,000 per return, plus §233a AO late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum) on any underpaid assessment. The tax authority may estimate the tax base under §162 AO, and electronic filing defaults are separately fineable if the statutory obligation to file electronically is breached.
KSt 1A, Box 40 – Loss Carryforward Utilised Carried-forward corporate income tax losses used in the assessment period, subject to the EUR 1,000,000 full-offset allowance and the 60% minimum-taxation cap. Loss schedules must be reconciled with prior assessments; losses of a pre-acquisition shell or of a different business purpose are forfeited under §8c KStG. Late submission triggers a Verspätungszuschlag under §152 AO of up to EUR 25,000 per return, plus §233a AO late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum) on any underpaid assessment. The tax authority may estimate the tax base under §162 AO, and electronic filing defaults are separately fineable if the statutory obligation to file electronically is breached.
KSt 1A, Box 50 – Taxable Income and Tax Result Final taxable income, resulting corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge and any advance-payment offsets. Verify application of any treaty or EU law relief only in the withholding/refund mechanisms; the corporate income tax return itself is not the place to claim treaty-based dividend reductions. Late submission triggers a Verspätungszuschlag under §152 AO of up to EUR 25,000 per return, plus §233a AO late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum) on any underpaid assessment.

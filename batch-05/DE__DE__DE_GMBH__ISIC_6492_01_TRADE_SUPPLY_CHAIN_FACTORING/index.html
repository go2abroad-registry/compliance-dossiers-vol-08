Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office (VAT ID, EU refunds, federal taxes); local Finanzamt (tax office) for income/corporate/VAT registration; ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt Online-Portal (www.bzst.de) for filings.

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH, after registration in the Handelsregister and submission of the registration form to the local Finanzamt, the Finanzamt assigns a national tax number (Steuernummer). For VAT purposes, apply to BZSt for a VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) using the online BZSt application (Formular USt 1 TU) or ELSTER, providing core entity data, shareholder details, and place of business. Foreign parent entities may need to appoint a fiscal representative for VAT registration if they have no German establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a German GmbH, after registration in the Handelsregister and submission of the registration form to the local Finanzamt, the Finanzamt assigns a national tax number (Steuernummer). For VAT purposes, apply to BZSt for a VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) using the online BZSt application (Formular USt 1 TU) or ELSTER, providing core entity data, shareholder details, and place of business. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box pass-through election for a GmbH. Elections include: (i) corporate tax group (ertragsteuerliche Organschaft) under §14 KStG with a profit and loss transfer agreement; (ii) VAT group (umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft) under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG for consolidated VAT registration; (iii) application of section 34 KStG for controlled foreign corporation consequences; and (iv) ATAD hybrid mismatch neutrality adjustments for hybrid financial instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For supplies of goods and services by German businesses, VAT registration is required from the first transaction with no monetary threshold. Small business exemption under §19 UStG applies below EUR 22,000 (2024/2025 prior calendar year) and EUR 50,000 current year. For non-resident businesses, a German VAT registration obligation arises from the first taxable supply in Germany regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the EU qualify for a 0% VAT rate where the seller retains documentary proof of physical export (Verkaufsbeleg) and receives payment. Intra-EU supplies are zero-rated subject to validation of the customer's VAT ID, Intrastat reporting thresholds, and delivery proof. Services linked to export (e.g., freight, logistics) may be zero-rated or reverse-charged depending on the place of supply. Receivables factoring services remain VAT-exempt under §4 Nr. 8 UStG as a financial service without input VAT recovery, unless the transaction constitutes true export financing for non-EU goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung / ATLAS)
  • Commercial Invoice (Handelsrechnung)
  • Packing List (Packliste)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (e.g., CMR, Air Waybill)
  • Proof of payment (SWIFT/Telegraphic Transfer or Inward Remittance Certificate) for treasury and tax due diligence

Germany does not operate a mandatory real-time tax clearance/electronic fiscal stamp regime. E-invoicing in a structured format (e.g., XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, Factur-X) will be mandatory for domestic B2B transactions from 1 January 2025 (receiving) and phased issuing obligations from 1 January 2027 (turnover above EUR 800,000) and 1 January 2028 (all). Business invoices must comply with EN 16931; API/EDI transmission is accepted, but no central government clearance or digital stamping is required.

Tax auditors (Betriebsprüfung) may require production of the entire export documentation chain, including the customs declaration, transport record, and bank evidence of consideration. Failure to substantiate zero-rating within the audit period results in retrospective VAT assessment with interest at 0.5% per month (§233a AO). Misstatements or fraudulent claims are punishable under §370 AO with imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine; cross-border financing schemes are additionally scrutinized under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) and the General Anti-Abuse Rule (§42 AO).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU law (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Articles 63-66). Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to corporate income tax (15% plus solidarity surcharge and trade tax) and, if applicable, withholding tax on dividends (25% plus solidarity surcharge, reduced under tax treaties). No exchange control approval is required for cross-border payments, but mandatory reporting to the Bundesbank applies for payments exceeding €12,500 (current account) and for capital transactions (e.g., loans, equity) to ensure statistical accuracy.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or foreign accounts. However, all export receipts must be reported to the Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics within 5 business days of receipt (if exceeding €12,500). No conversion ratio applies; the exporter is free to hold, convert, or use the funds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy; the Bundesbank administers foreign exchange and balance-of-payments reporting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German law (Payment Services Supervision Act – ZAG) allows Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) to operate with a BaFin license or EU passport. However, for a factoring company handling client funds, an EMI license may be insufficient if the company holds funds on behalf of third parties (requires a banking license under the German Banking Act – KWG). Cross-border EMIs from other EU states can provide payment services, but they cannot offer credit or hold client funds for factoring without additional licensing. Therefore, a full bank account with a licensed commercial bank is recommended for core treasury operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' (e.g., code 11.0 for direct investment) or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN' (code 12.0). The remitter must include the GmbH's name and account number. For equity injections, a notarized capital increase resolution and registration with the commercial register are required. For loans, a written loan agreement must be filed with the Bundesbank for statistical purposes if the amount exceeds €12,500. Verification: The bank will require the underlying legal documentation (e.g., shareholders' resolution, loan agreement) and may request a certificate of no objection from the tax office (Unbedenklichkeitsbescheinigung) for large amounts.Clearing rails for trade settlements: Use SWIFT MT103 for direct payments to suppliers or clients. For documentary trade, use Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) issued via SWIFT MT700, with settlement through the correspondent banking network. For factoring disbursements to clients, use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EUR payments within the EU, or SWIFT MT103 for cross-border non-EUR payments. All outbound payments must be reported to the Bundesbank if they exceed €12,500 (for current account) or €50,000 (for capital account).Currency Forwards (outright forwards) – permitted under German law, executed with licensed banks, subject to ISDA/DRV documentation.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – permissible for currencies with convertibility restrictions, but must be executed with a licensed bank and reported for balance-of-payments statistics.Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) – allowed for hedging long-term exposures, subject to EMIR reporting and clearing obligations if applicable.FX Options – permitted, but require robust risk management and may be subject to margin requirements under EMIR.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge; trade tax separately applies at municipal rates of 14–17%, resulting in a combined effective rate of approximately 29–33%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 26.375%); church tax may apply. Non-resident corporate shareholders may qualify for a reduced rate of 15% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or treaty rates (e.g., 5% under the Germany-U.S. DTA with 10% shareholding).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual returns for fiscal year 2024 are due by 31 July 2025; with a tax advisor, the automatic extension is to 31 July 2026 (or 31 October 2026 for electronic filing). Quarterly prepayments are due 10 March, 10 June, 10 September, and 10 December. VAT prepayments are due by the 10th of the following month; annual VAT return is due by 31 July of the following year, extended to 31 July of the year after next if filed electronically by a tax advisor.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall

Box 9 (KSt 1 A): Taxable income before loss offset Commercial net income adjusted for tax purposes (trade tax additions and deductions, non-deductible expenses, tax depreciation). For a factoring company, include factoring fees, interest income from purchased receivables, service fees, and deduct operating expenses and risk provisions. All German-source income of the GmbH is fully taxable regardless of foreign ownership. Payments to non-resident related parties must satisfy transfer pricing and thin capitalization rules (§ 1 AStG, § 8a KStG). Late filing penalty under § 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed tax per month (minimum EUR 25) up to EUR 25,000. Late payment interest under § 233a AO: 0.15% per month, accruing from 15 months after tax year end. Non-payment of withholding tax leads to personal liability and criminal prosecution under § 370 AO (imprisonment up to five years).
Box 1 (GewSt 1 A): Trade tax base Trade profit before loss offset plus statutory additions (e.g., 25% of interest expense above the interest limitation threshold, 20% of rental/lease payments in certain cases) and deductions. Trade tax is deductible for corporate income tax but not for trade tax. A German GmbH with its place of management or a permanent establishment in Germany is subject to unlimited trade tax. Non-resident shareholders are not directly taxable on trade tax; the GmbH is the taxpayer. Late filing penalty under § 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed tax per month (minimum EUR 25) up to EUR 25,000. Late payment interest under § 233a AO: 0.15% per month, accruing from 15 months after tax year end. Non-payment of withholding tax leads to personal liability and criminal prosecution under § 370 AO (imprisonment up to five years).
Annex KSt 1 A: Loss carryback and carryforward Loss carryback up to EUR 1,000,000 for one year; indefinite forward carry of remaining losses limited to 60% of taxable income exceeding EUR 1,000,000. A change of shareholders exceeding 25% or 50% within five years can forfeit loss carryforwards under § 8c KStG. Losses are eligible if the GmbH maintains its German seat and management; transactions with non-resident shareholders altering ownership trigger anti-loss-trading provisions. Late filing penalty under § 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed tax per month (minimum EUR 25) up to EUR 25,000. Late payment interest under § 233a AO: 0.15% per month, accruing from 15 months after tax year end. Non-payment of withholding tax leads to personal liability and criminal prosecution under § 370 AO (imprisonment up to five years).
Annex ESt 1: Managing director and shareholder information Details of managing directors, salaries, loans, and related-party transactions. Factoring discount rates and service fees must be arm's length; excessive fees paid to non-resident shareholders are treated as hidden profit distributions. Remuneration for services performed in Germany is subject to German wage tax and social security. Hidden profit distributions trigger corporate income tax, trade tax, and 25% withholding tax (plus surcharge). Late filing penalty under § 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed tax per month (minimum EUR 25) up to EUR 25,000. Late payment interest under § 233a AO: 0.15% per month, accruing from 15 months after tax year end. Non-payment of withholding tax leads to personal liability and criminal prosecution under § 370 AO (imprisonment up to five years).
Annex U (VAT): VAT return Factoring services are generally VAT-exempt under § 4 Nr. 8 UStG. Exempt turnover must be reported; input VAT on related costs is not recoverable. Interest on overdue invoices is outside the scope of VAT. Cross-border factoring services to non-resident clients are VAT-exempt; reverse charge may apply to certain B2B services under § 13b UStG. Late filing penalty under § 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed tax per month (minimum EUR 25) up to EUR 25,000. Late payment interest under § 233a AO: 0.15% per month, accruing from 15 months after tax year end. Non-payment of withholding tax leads to personal liability and criminal prosecution under § 370 AO (imprisonment up to five years).
Annex KSt 1 DM: Withholding tax on dividends and royalties Annual declaration of withholding tax on distributions, royalties, and certain service fees paid to non-residents, including solidarity surcharge. The GmbH as withholding agent must obtain an exemption order from BZSt before applying a reduced DTA rate; file Form DE-1 and submit a valid TRC. Late filing penalty under § 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed tax per month (minimum EUR 25) up to EUR 25,000. Late payment interest under § 233a AO: 0.15% per month, accruing from 15 months after tax year end. Non-payment of withholding tax leads to personal liability and criminal prosecution under § 370 AO (imprisonment up to five years).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 for cross-border related-party transactions; Local File mandatory when such transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000; Master File required when group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and submitted within 60 days of request.

Economic substance scope: German law applies functional and economic substance principles under § 8 Abs. 2 KStG, § 1 AStG, and § 42 AO. A factoring GmbH must maintain real substance in Germany: a permanent office, employees with independent decision-making authority, performance of core factoring functions (credit risk assessment, receivable servicing, cash collection), and compliance with BaFin licensing requirements under the German Banking Act (KWG) unless an exemption applies (e.g., captive factoring under § 2 Abs. 6 Nr. 5 KWG). Shell companies without substance are disregarded and income is reattributed to a deemed German permanent establishment, triggering full taxation plus penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (§ 370 AO)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years; in severe cases (tax loss exceeding EUR 50,000) six months to ten years. Tax evaded is due plus monthly interest of 0.15%. Limitation period extended to ten years.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under § 69 AO for unpaid taxes if the company cannot pay; civil restitution demands, asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment up to five years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (§ 1 AStG, § 162 AO)

Statutory sanction: Upward income adjustment; penalty of 5%–10% of the adjustment; daily surcharge of EUR 50 up to EUR 1,000,000 for late documentation; assessment period extended to ten years.

Director personal liability: Directors approving non-arm's-length pricing are personally liable for the tax shortfall under § 69 AO and § 42 GmbHG; criminal prosecution for tax evasion possible.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Withholding Taxes (§ 50a EStG, § 44 KStG)

Statutory sanction: Company is liable for the unwithheld tax; late-payment penalty of 1% per month; interest at 0.15% per month; administrative fines up to EUR 50,000; criminal sanctions for intentional failure.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for withheld but unremitted tax and for uncollected withholding tax, including solidarity surcharge; personal assets can be seized; travel bans may be imposed.
Unlicensed Factoring Activity and Shell Company Abuse (KWG, AML, ESR)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover under § 37 KWG; criminal penalty under § 54 KWG with imprisonment up to five years; tax authority may disregard the shell under § 42 AO; AML fines up to EUR 100,000 or 10% of global turnover.

Director personal liability: Directors of unlicensed or shell entities are criminally liable under § 54 KWG; they face personal liability for all unpaid corporate income tax, trade tax, and solidarity surcharge; travel bans and asset seizure possible.
False Certification and VAT Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under § 370 AO tax evasion with imprisonment up to ten years if committed with gross personal gain; additional penalties under § 378 AO of 6% of understated tax (minimum EUR 5,000); forged certificates trigger criminal liability under § 267 StGB.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false certificates are personally liable for the tax loss and face imprisonment; civil restitution and disgorgement demands are pursued by the tax authority; passport and travel restrictions apply.

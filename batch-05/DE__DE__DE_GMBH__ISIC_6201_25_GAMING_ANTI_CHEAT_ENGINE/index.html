Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and the central e-invoicing reception portal (www.bzst.de); local Finanzamt (Tax Office) for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT assessment; national electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); German Customs Administration (Generalzolldirektion) for export declarations.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities: submit the BZSt application for a Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr.) under §27a UStG using Form USt 1 TU, evidencing entrepreneurial activity, state of establishment, and expected German taxable turnover; the DE-prefixed VAT ID is issued by BZSt. Resident GmbH: after notarial incorporation and registration in the Commercial Register, register the business with the local trade office (Gewerbeamt), then file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt, which assigns the local Steuernummer; a separate BZSt application is required to obtain the USt-IdNr. for cross-border invoicing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A German GmbH has no check-the-box pass-through election; corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT are imposed at entity level. Available statutory elections: (1) Organschaft fiscal unity election under §§14–17 KStG, §2 Abs. 2 GewStG and §73 UStG to consolidate a qualifying GmbH subsidiary into the parent's income, trade tax and VAT groups; (2) VAT group registration election under §73 UStG for companies under uniform control; (3) small-business exemption elections under §19 Abs. 1 UStG, available only where prior-year turnover does not exceed €22,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed €50,000; (4) EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme election for intra-Community distance sales and cross-border B2C electronic services, plus Import One-Stop-Shop (I-OSS). Anti-hybrid protections under §4k EStG and §12 Abs. 2 KStG neutralize deduction/non-inclusion mismatches in cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold: any entrepreneur making taxable supplies in Germany must be VAT-registered from the first euro. The small-business exemption under §19 UStG merely exempts domestic turnover where the prior-year amount is ≤ €22,000 and the current-year estimate is ≤ €50,000; it does not constitute a registration threshold and is generally unavailable for an export-scale GmbH. For non-resident digital suppliers of B2C electronic services, no German registration is required for intra-EU OSS sales up to the €10,000 annual threshold, but German registration becomes mandatory above that threshold or as soon as any B2B supply is made to a recipient established in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: export deliveries to destinations outside the EU qualify for 0% export VAT under §6 UStG where the goods physically leave the EU customs territory and the exporter holds complete export evidence (Ausfuhrnachweis) per §§8–13 UStDV. Services: B2B services to recipients not established in the EU fall outside German VAT by the place-of-supply rule (§3a Abs. 2 UStG) with reverse charge in the recipient's country; B2C electronically supplied services, including anti-cheat software licensing, SaaS integrity middleware and related cloud services, are taxable at the customer's location under §3a Abs. 5 UStG, so no German VAT is charged without requiring a formal zero-rating claim. The taxpayer must verify the recipient's business status and location through at least two independent, non-contradictory pieces of evidence. Work on goods and certain auxiliary services may be zero-rated under §4 Nr. 1a UStG when performed on goods ultimately exported.

  • ATLAS electronic customs export declaration (Zollanmeldung) discharged by the Hauptzollamt, evidencing the movement reference number (MRN) and actual exit of the goods from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading, CMR waybill or Air Waybill showing a third-country destination
  • Export proof file per §§8–13 UStDV (A1/A2/A3 export evidence, transit documents, customs discharge confirmation)
  • For overseas services: recipient establishment evidence (foreign VAT/GST registration certificate or contractual declarations), service delivery and performance records, and payment documentation

Since 1 January 2025 Germany mandates structured e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) for domestic B2B supplies: issuers must transmit EN 16931-conformant XML (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD) via a compliant transmission route (e-mail, PEPPOL, E-Rechnungsportal). Phase-in schedule: every business must be able to receive structured e-invoices from 1 January 2025; mandatory issuance applies to businesses with taxable turnover exceeding €800,000 from 1 January 2027; full B2B e-invoicing obligation applies from 1 January 2028. Germany has no real-time fiscal clearance system, digital stamping, or API pre-authorization comparable to Mexico's SAT or Italy's SDI; authenticity and integrity are ensured through the structured format and compliant transmission channel. Cash and retail supplies remain subject to certified fiscal hardware (TSE) and DSFinV-K data export obligations.

Claiming export zero-rating without valid export evidence triggers retroactive VAT assessment at 19%, plus interest under §233a AO (0.5% per month for interest periods up to 31 December 2024, reduced to 0.15% per month for post-2024 interest periods) and late-payment surcharges under §240 AO of 1% per commenced month on arrears; the related input VAT deduction is also clawed back. Intentional use of forged or sham export documents constitutes tax fraud under §370 AO (imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in aggravated cases, with substantial monetary fines); negligent incorrect zero-rating is punishable under §378 AO with fines up to €25,000. Customs declaration fraud is separately sanctionable under §382 AO (fines up to €25,000) and may trigger EU customs debt liability. Germany imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline under exchange control law; the 180-day benchmark is applied only as an internal turnover and documentary substantiation standard, while persistently unpaid export receivables may be recharacterized as domestic supplies if the economic substance of the export cannot be established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. No exchange-control approvals, licensing, or quantitative limits apply to inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, licensing royalty receipts, or outbound profit and dividend remittances; the EU free movement of capital (TFEU Art. 63) governs. Dividend remittances from a GmbH are legally unrestricted once the annual financial statements are adopted and distributable reserves (Bilanzgewinn) exist, subject only to capital-maintenance constraints under GmbHG §§ 30–31 and to the statutory withholding tax of 26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), which may be reduced to 0% for a qualifying EU corporate shareholder under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or under an applicable double-tax treaty via the standard refund/relief procedure. The only non-tax convertibility frictions are the Bundesbank statistical reporting duty for cross-border payments of EUR 12,500 or more and the universal bank AML source-of-funds verification; neither constitutes a permission hurdle. Overall convertibility risk for DE_GMBH is negligible.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, holding-period, or conversion ratio on export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in any currency. The sole recurring statutory obligation is under AWV § 67: every inbound cross-border payment of EUR 12,500 or more received by the GmbH (including export royalties and licensing fees) must be reported electronically to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics via the AKM portal no later than the 7th day of the month following the month of crediting; this is a statistical reporting duty only and does not restrict the use of funds. Because the company exports digital middleware licenses rather than physical goods, no customs export declaration applies; the compliance file includes a dual-use self-classification under EU Regulation 2021/821 confirming that the anti-cheat software does not contain crypto-items of export-control relevance.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank, Frankfurt am Main) — authority for cross-border payment statistics and foreign-trade reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) — prudential supervision of banks and payment institutions, including anti-money-laundering oversight under the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG) and supervision of electronic money institutions under the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an EU/EEA-licensed operational secondary account; Low as a core treasury, capital-escrow, or FX-hedging vehicle. Under PSD2 and the German ZAG, an EU/EEA e-money institution may serve a German-resident GmbH via passport notification without a separate BaFin banking licence, but it may not offer credit, proprietary investment of client funds, or FX derivative products. Offshore (non-EEA) EMIs cannot actively on-board German corporate clients without a BaFin-authorised branch or an appropriate EU licence; reverse-solicitation reliance is considered legally fragile by German compliance officers and would trigger enhanced due diligence from every correspondent bank. Given the entity's Low_Risk_Standard AML classification, the recommended blueprint is principal clearing through a German universal bank (SEPA/SWIFT) with one EU-licensed EMI used for high-velocity Stripe-initiated payouts; no offshore EMI should hold share capital, escrow balances, or hedge collateral.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Germany imposes no foreign-exchange approval gate for inbound capital; routing is purely documentary. Equity contributions should arrive by SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pain.001/pacs.008 credit) from the non-resident shareholder to the GmbH's EUR account at a German universal bank, referencing the shareholder resolution (e.g., 'Kapitaleinlage gemäß Gesellschafterbeschluss vom [date]'); funds may not be withdrawn before notarial registration of any formal capital increase (GmbHG §§ 7–8). Shareholder loans should be documented by a signed arm's-length loan agreement (fixed interest, scheduled maturity), entered in the Bundesbank statistics category for external loans, and structured to respect the German interest deduction barrier (§ 4h EStG, 30% EBITDA interest cap). Each inbound credit of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank via the AKM portal; this is a statistical duty, not an authorisation.

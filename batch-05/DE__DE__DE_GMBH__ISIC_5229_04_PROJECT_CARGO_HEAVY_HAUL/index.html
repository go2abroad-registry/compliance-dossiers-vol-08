Competent corporate registry

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory minimum share capital for a GmbH is EUR 25,000. Before registration, at least 25% of each cash contribution must be paid in, and the aggregate cash capital paid in must not be less than EUR 12,500. In-kind contributions must be fully provided and substantiated in the notarial deed. There is no formal escrow account requirement, but the funds must be deposited in a GmbH-designated German bank account and be at the free disposal of the managing directors, confirmed by a bank certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection (Feuerbeschau) is required for any facility, yard, or office used by personnel. Structural load inspections must cover crane pads, storage surfaces, and workshop floors before heavy-haul operations commence. DGUV occupational health and safety clearance, including worker PPE programmes, crane and forklift operator certification, and emergency response arrangements, is mandatory.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and cross-border tax matters; local tax offices (Finanzämter) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT registration; official e-filing portal: ELSTER (ElsterOnline).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-controlled GmbH obtains a statutory tax number (Steuernummer) by filing registration documents with the local Finanzamt and, separately, applies to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) using Form USt 1 TG (or via ELSTER). The GmbH must submit the notarized Articles of Association, commercial register excerpt, and beneficial ownership information; the VAT ID is issued after identity verification and is independent of the local tax number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; resident GmbH is subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability and trade tax on worldwide commercial income, with domestic-source business profits taxed at entity level; no territorial election; branch or permanent establishment of a non-resident is limited taxpayer. — A foreign-controlled GmbH obtains a statutory tax number (Steuernummer) by filing registration documents with the local Finanzamt and, separately, applies to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) using Form USt 1 TG (or via ELSTER). The GmbH must submit the notarized Articles of Association, commercial register excerpt, and beneficial ownership information; the VAT ID is issued after identity verification and is independent of the local tax number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; resident GmbH is subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability and trade tax on worldwide commercial income, with domestic-source business profits taxed at entity level; no territorial election; branch or permanent establishment of a non-resident is limited taxpayer.

Available election forms

No German 'check-the-box' classification election exists; classification is prescribed by domestic civil and tax law. Available elections include formation of a corporate group (Organschaft) for corporate income tax and VAT, which consolidates the legal entity with the controlling holding entity for tax purposes, and the uniform valuation and profit-determination options under the German Income Tax Act; hybrid mismatch adjustments apply under Sec. 4k EStG in conformity with ATAD2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business exemption: EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 expected current-year turnover (Sec. 19 UStG). Non-established foreign suppliers and distance sellers have no general threshold and may be registrable from the first transaction if the place of supply is in Germany; intra-Community acquisitions are taxable above EUR 10,000 annual purchase threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export supply under Sec. 4 No. 1 and Sec. 6 UStG where goods physically leave the EU customs territory and the transport is evidenced by documentary proof; also zero-rated for intra-Community supplies with a valid EU customer VAT ID and a recapitulative statement (EC Sales List). Offshore services provided to a non-resident recipient are generally either zero-rated, subject to reverse charge, or outside the scope of German VAT according to the place-of-supply rules, provided the supplier holds adequate commercial and bank evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and the actual provision of services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument / electronic EXS movement certificate)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, or Air Waybill showing consignor and non-EU consignee
  • Export order contract and commercial invoice
  • Proof of goods leaving the EU customs territory (e.g., customs office of exit endorsement or transport manifests)
  • Foreign exchange inward remittance or bank transfer confirmation for proceeds of exported goods/services

Mandatory e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) for domestic B2B transactions from 1 January 2025, requiring structured XML formats such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD, with inbound processing duties by 2027. No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is in force in Germany; invoices are not cleared through a central tax platform before issuance, but must be transmitted electronically to buyers and retained auditable in a ten-year archive.

German tax authority may require additional proof of physical export and payment flows under the documentary evidence rules; if export zero-rating is unauditable or falsely claimed, the VAT is clawed back retroactively with interest under Sec. 233a AO, a late-payment surcharge under Sec. 240 AO, and potential fines up to EUR 30,000 under tax evasion rules (Sec. 370 AO); negligent misdeclaration triggers EUR 5,000 penalties under Sec. 26c UStG, and intentional fraud is referred to criminal prosecution with imprisonment or fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the EU free movement of capital framework (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Articles 63-66). No exchange controls or restrictions on profit remittance for non-resident shareholders. However, all cross-border payments and transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes (AWV reporting). Profit remittance via dividends is subject to standard corporate income tax and withholding tax (unless reduced by tax treaty), but no FX conversion hurdles exist. The Euro is fully convertible; no surrender requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Germany does not impose any requirement to convert foreign currency receipts into EUR. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in domestic or foreign accounts. However, all export receipts and payments must be reported to the Bundesbank if they exceed EUR 12,500, within 5 days of receipt or payment, using the standard AWV reporting forms (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision; the Bundesbank administers foreign trade and payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German BaFin-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) can provide cross-border payment services, but they are not full banks and cannot offer credit or hold client funds in the same way. For project cargo heavy haul logistics with high-value milestone payments and LC requirements, a full commercial bank is preferred. EMIs like PPRO, Airwallex, or N26 Business may be used for ancillary payments but are not suitable for primary treasury operations due to limited LC issuance and escrow capabilities. Legal constraints: EMIs must be licensed under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and are subject to strict safeguarding requirements. They cannot provide investment services or interest-bearing accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For capital injection from non-resident shareholders, use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with purpose code 'G1' (Direct investment) or 'C1' (Capital transfer) as per the AWV reporting. The transfer must be reported to the Bundesbank if exceeding EUR 12,500. The receiving bank will require a copy of the commercial register entry and a resolution from the shareholders. For equity injections, the GmbH must file a capital increase with the commercial register. For shareholder loans, use purpose code 'D1' (Loans) and ensure proper documentation. All inbound transfers must be accompanied by a 'Verwendungszweck' (purpose) that clearly states the nature of the transaction to avoid AML flags.For trade settlements, use SWIFT MT103 for direct payments and MT700 for documentary letters of credit (LC). For project cargo heavy haul, LCs are commonly used for milestone payments. The clearing rails include the TARGET2 system for EUR payments and the SWIFT network for cross-currency. For non-EUR disbursements, use correspondent banking via the bank's network. Ensure compliance with the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA) for EUR transfers. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000 for goods or EUR 500,000 for services; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like UAE, but the substance-over-form doctrine applies. The GmbH must have real economic activity (office, employees, management decisions) to avoid treaty abuse and to be recognized as tax resident. For project cargo logistics, substance is evidenced by operational assets, contracts, and personnel in Germany.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine; if committed on a large scale, imprisonment up to 10 years. Additional penalty of up to 15% of the evaded tax per year (Zuschlag).

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they fail to file accurate returns or misrepresent facts. Civil restitution, travel bans, and potential extradition under EU arrest warrant.
Shell Company (Briefkastenfirma)

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits, reclassification of income to the beneficial owner, and imposition of penalty taxes up to 100% of the tax avoided. The GmbH may be dissolved under civil law.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for all unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly operated a shell entity. Travel bans and criminal prosecution for fraud.
AML and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 1 million per violation under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG). Tax authorities may disallow deductions and impose 100% penalty on the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for restitution of the tax loss, and may be subject to travel bans and imprisonment up to 5 years for fraud.

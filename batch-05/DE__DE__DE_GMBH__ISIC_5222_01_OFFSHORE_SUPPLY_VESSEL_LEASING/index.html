Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual mailbox address is not sufficient for a German GmbH; Section 3(1) no. 1 GmbHG requires a real domestic business seat and its actual administration in Germany. The Federal Fiscal Court and tax authorities expect physical presence, personnel or contracted management functions capable of making day-to-day decisions. For offshore supply-vessel leasing, the registered office must be served by a German-based ship-management or administrative office; a virtual-only address will be treated as a formal defect for registration and may cause deemed permanent establishment under a foreign parent company.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital of EUR 25,000 pursuant to Section 5(1) GmbHG; at least EUR 12,500 must be paid in cash to a blocked corporate account (Anderkonto) at a credit institution in Germany before the application for registration is filed; the deposit must be confirmed by the bank and remain at the free disposal of the managing director. In-kind contributions require a valuation and audit report per Sections 5(4) and 8(2) GmbHG.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety clearance (brandschutztechnische Abnahme) for the registered administrative offices, workshops and pier installations issued by the local fire-protection authority under the State Building Code (BauO); structural load and stability certificate for quayside cranes and berthing infrastructure; occupational-safety approval per the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) announced by BG Verkehr (Berufsgenossenschaft für Verkehrswirtschaft, See- und Hafenbetriebe); seafarer fit-for-duty medical clearance under the Seearbeitsgesetz (German Seafarer Employment Act).

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum share capital of EUR 25,000 pursuant to Section 5(1) GmbHG; at least EUR 12,500 must be paid in cash to a blocked corporate account (Anderkonto) at a credit institution in Germany before the application for registration is filed; the deposit must be confirmed by the bank and remain at the free disposal of the managing director. In-kind contributions require a valuation and audit report per Sections 5(4) and 8(2) GmbHG.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt fuer Steuern - BZSt) and locally competent revenue office (Finanzamt); official electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A foreign or domestic shareholder of a German GmbH must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the local Finanzamt competent for the entity's place of management, filed electronically via ELSTER. The Finanzamt assigns the statutory tax number (Steuernummer); the BZSt subsequently allocates the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, DE-No.) upon separate registration. Registration is mandatory and unconditional for a GmbH with domestic place of management under Sec. 11 AO; a non-resident entity without domestic establishment must use a fiscal representative for direct registration and tax filing obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (German GmbH is a corporation subject to corporation income tax (Koerperschaftsteuer) of 15%, solidarity surcharge of 5.5% on CIT, yielding a combined CIT burden of approximately 15.83%, plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) with an effective rate typically between 8.75% and 15.75%; no pass-through eligibility is available for a GmbH). — A foreign or domestic shareholder of a German GmbH must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the local Finanzamt competent for the entity's place of management, filed electronically via ELSTER. The Finanzamt assigns the statutory tax number (Steuernummer); the BZSt subsequently allocates the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, DE-No.) upon separate registration. Registration is mandatory and unconditional for a GmbH with domestic place of management under Sec. 11 AO; a non-resident entity without domestic establishment must use a fiscal representative for direct registration and tax filing obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (German GmbH is a corporation subject to corporation income tax (Koerperschaftsteuer) of 15%, solidarity surcharge of 5.5% on CIT, yielding a combined CIT burden of approximately 15.83%, plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) with an effective rate typically between 8.75% and 15.75%; no pass-through eligibility is available for a GmbH).

Available election forms

German law does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (i) fiscal unit for VAT and income tax purposes (Organschaft) between a controlling parent and dependent group companies; (ii) designation of a branch as a permanent establishment with treaty-based profit attribution, including a Sec. 1 AStG potential election for an eligible PE; (iii) trade-tax loss carry-forward valuation and interest (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortisation, popular EBD) deduction elections under Sec. 4h EStG / Sec. 8a KStG; and (iv) anti-hybrid adjustments under Sec. 4k EStG, which are applied automatically rather than by elective check-the-box.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 (annual turnover threshold for the small-business exemption under Sec. 19 UStG, applicable from 1 January 2025); however, a GmbH engaged in offshore vessel leasing is not economically eligible for that exemption and must register for VAT without threshold when performing taxable supplies. The intra-Community and cross-border distance-sales annual threshold is EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to export deliveries of goods dispatched or transported into a third-country customs territory (Sec. 4 No. 1 and Sec. 6 UStG), provided the supplier retains verifiable export evidence and the goods physically depart the EU within the statutory period (usually three months). For offshore supply vessel leasing, the dominant transaction is a B2B supply of transport-related chartering services: under Sec. 3a(2) UStG the place of supply is the customer's place of business, so charter fees invoiced to a non-German business customer are outside German VAT (no German VAT, possible reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction) rather than zero-rated. Zero-rating is claimable where the leased vessel itself is simultaneously exported from the EU to a third-country buyer/operator, or where the leasing service is directly tied to the export movement of goods and the corresponding documentary evidence is maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS/AES) with exit confirmation, e.g., EX-A form
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill (maritime transport document issued by the carrier, accepted by the escrow instruction)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) evidencing full and timely FX repatriation of the charter consideration

Since 1 January 2025 German B2B suppliers must be able to receive electronic invoices in a structured XML format (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, or EDI); mandatory issuance of e-invoices applies from 1 January 2027 for enterprises with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all remaining enterprises, subject to legislative refinements in the Growth Opportunities Act. Germany has not yet implemented a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime: there is no mandatory tax authority counter-stamping or API-based clearance for B2B invoices. B2G invoices must nevertheless be transmitted via the Xrechnung channel through the PEPPOL network, and all VAT returns and EC sales lists must be filed to the BZSt electronically (ELSTER/API).

German tax authorities may retroactively disallow the zero rating and claw back VAT plus input VAT recovery where export substantiation is missing or inaccurate, using estimated assessment under Sec. 162 AO. Late or deficient tax payments attract interest of 0.5% per month (6% p.a.) under Sec. 233a AO. Intentional or reckless zero-rating abuse is taxed as VAT evasion under Sec. 370 AO, punishable by fines or imprisonment of up to five years (up to ten years in aggravated cases); civil joint liability for reverse-charge VAT applies under Sec. 13d UStG. Documentary inconsistencies also trigger enhanced audit scrutiny under Sec. 193-203 AO with reconstruction of taxable basis and potential criminal referral.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a full eurozone member and subscribes to the EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (TFEU Articles 63-66). Euro-denominated and foreign-currency accounts can be held freely by resident and non-resident investors. Profit remittances, dividends and royalty payments are not subject to exchange control restrictions. Offshore supply-vessel chartering revenues accruing in EUR, USD, or NOK may be retained in foreign currencies or freely converted. The only operational hurdles arise from reporting duties under Sections 59-70 AWV, sanctions screening, and anti-money-laundering checks under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG). There are no surrender requirements and no dual-exchange-rate restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export-proceeds surrender exists in Germany. All export and charter revenues must be reported monthly to Deutsche Bundesbank if cross-border receipts exceed EUR 12,500, using the AWV report 'Zahlungsmitteilung' (per Section 67 AWV). Trade receivables and payables outstanding at month-end with respective levels above EUR 5,000 must also be reported as part of external position reports. Exporters must keep documentary evidence (invoices, SAD exports declarations, vessel charter agreements) for seven years. Failure to report may be treated as an administrative regulatory offence under the AWV.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) with supervisory and statistical authority over cross-border payments under the German Central Bank Act (BBankG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (AWV); the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) and BAFA enforce export/import controls, but Germany maintains no foreign-exchange-control regime requiring central bank approval.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as a sole banking channel for ISIC 5222 offshore vessel leasing; Medium as a supplementary or disbursement-only corridor. Legal constraints: an offshore or non-EEA digital electronic money institution (EMI) must be either BaFin-licensed in Germany or passport-qualified under Directive 2009/110/EC and the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG). Non-EEA EMIs are generally not accepted for corporate account opening of a taxable GmbH. The EMI cannot operate as a hold-all to bypass regular banking requirements, cannot provide letters of credit or trade-related credit, and must safeguard client funds separately. Tax and insolvency compliance further requires a payment-account relationship with a Bundesbank-member credit institution.

Wire instruction must identify the German GmbH's account at the German correspondent bank and include the ISIN, the notarial deed date or reference, shareholder LEI if available and the GS01/SWIFT benefit. For shareholder loans, use purpose code 'LOAN' and furnish the executed contract to the bank. The bank will validate the identity of the transferor on the legal basis of the KWG (German Banking Act) compliance.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory share capital of a GmbH is EUR 25,000. Before registration, at least EUR 12,500 in aggregate must be paid for a cash formation, and each subscribed share must be paid to at least one-quarter of its nominal amount. German law does not require a statutory escrow account; a dedicated company bank account is normally opened after notarization and funded before the Commercial Register filing. Contributions in kind require additional statutory documentation and valuation support.

Funding & unlock path: For an office-only brokerage, no separate industrial fire or structural-load clearance is generally required beyond building-use and occupational-safety compliance. A warehouse, silo, or handling site requires verification with the local building authority and fire-protection authority, including building-use approval, fire-safety concept, emergency exits, fire protection systems, structural and floor-load certification, dust-explosion assessment where relevant, workplace-risk assessment, and compliance with the German Occupational Safety and Health Act and applicable workplace rules.

Paid-in capital rule: The statutory share capital of a GmbH is EUR 25,000. Before registration, at least EUR 12,500 in aggregate must be paid for a cash formation, and each subscribed share must be paid to at least one-quarter of its nominal amount. German law does not require a statutory escrow account; a dedicated company bank account is normally opened after notarization and funded before the Commercial Register filing. Contributions in kind require additional statutory documentation and valuation support.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern - BZSt), www.bzst.de, for VAT ID issuance and central matters; Local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax and trade tax assessment; ELSTER electronic filing portal, www.elster.de; Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen - BMF) for primary law and administrative guidance.

E-government filing portal

1) Constitute the GmbH by notarial deed, file with the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), and obtain the commercial register number. 2) Submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the locally competent Finanzamt to obtain the 11-digit Steuernummer (tax number covering corporate income tax, VAT, and trade tax). 3) Apply separately to BZSt for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr., format DE + 9 digits) via the online BZSt form or ELSTER. 4) Complete trade tax registration with the local trade office (Gewerbeamt). 5) For a foreign-resident GmbH without domestic establishment, register through the special non-established entrepreneur procedure (Formular USt 1 TG) at the competent Finanzamt, potentially appointing a fiscal representative; foreign persons must also file the ELSTER registration with a certified electronic signature.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the GmbH is a domestic corporation (inländische Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability (unbeschränkte Körperschaftsteuerpflicht) under Sec. 1(1) No. 1 KStG where its statutory seat (Sitz) or place of effective management (Ort der Geschäftsleitung) is in Germany. It is a commercial enterprise by legal form (Formkaufmann), so all profits are fully subject to trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). No pass-through taxation is available for a GmbH; profit taxation applies at entity level: 15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax plus municipal trade tax (assessment rate 3.5% applied to a municipal multiplier typically 200-490%), yielding an aggregate effective income tax burden of approximately 29-32%. Territorial scope: world-wide income for resident GmbH (unlimited tax liability), with foreign-source income relief via tax treaties and Sec. 34c EStG; no general tax-exempt zone applies to the GmbH structure. — 1) Constitute the GmbH by notarial deed, file with the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), and obtain the commercial register number. 2) Submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the locally competent Finanzamt to obtain the 11-digit Steuernummer (tax number covering corporate income tax, VAT, and trade tax). 3) Apply separately to BZSt for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr., format DE + 9 digits) via the online BZSt form or ELSTER. 4) Complete trade tax registration with the local trade office (Gewerbeamt). 5) For a foreign-resident GmbH without domestic establishment, register through the special non-established entrepreneur procedure (Formular USt 1 TG) at the competent Finanzamt, potentially appointing a fiscal representative; foreign persons must also file the ELSTER registration with a certified electronic signature.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the GmbH is a domestic corporation (inländische Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability (unbeschränkte Körperschaftsteuerpflicht) under Sec. 1(1) No. 1 KStG where its statutory seat (Sitz) or place of effective management (Ort der Geschäftsleitung) is in Germany. It is a commercial enterprise by legal form (Formkaufmann), so all profits are fully subject to trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). No pass-through taxation is available for a GmbH; profit taxation applies at entity level: 15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax plus municipal trade tax (assessment rate 3.5% applied to a municipal multiplier typically 200-490%), yielding an aggregate effective income tax burden of approximately 29-32%. Territorial scope: world-wide income for resident GmbH (unlimited tax liability), with foreign-source income relief via tax treaties and Sec. 34c EStG; no general tax-exempt zone applies to the GmbH structure.

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a US-style check-the-box system; entity classification follows the civil-law form. Available legally effective elections: (i) Organschaft (corporate tax group) under Secs. 14-17 KStG and VAT organ under Sec. 2(2) UStG, electively consolidating profit and input-output VAT accounts of German subsidiaries into a single tax unit; (ii) election for the small-entrepreneur VAT scheme (Sec. 19(2) UStG) - generally unavailable to a commercial trading GmbH; (iii) reverse-charge election for specified B2B supplies (e.g., construction, gold, scrap metals) where supplier is non-established; (iv) intra-group transfer pricing and benchmarking documentation elections (Verrechnungspreisdokumentation); (v) hybrid mismatch remediation declarations under Sec. 4k EStG (ATAD implementation) including explicit elections to neutralize deemed mismatch amounts; (vi) the notification-based option to waive VAT exemption for real estate supplies under Sec. 9 UStG (not relevant to commodity trading but part of the general statutory election framework).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration turnover threshold for a GmbH with seat or management in Germany: every entrepreneurial activity triggers VAT registration at the first taxable supply. For non-established foreign businesses, compulsory VAT registration applies immediately upon any taxable activity performed in Germany (no de minimis threshold). The statutory small-entrepreneur exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung, Sec. 19 UStG) applies solely to natural persons or small businesses with prior-year turnover not exceeding EUR 25,000 and current-year estimate not exceeding EUR 100,000 - not relevant to a German trading GmbH. For intra-Community distance sales of goods, the EU-wide registration threshold is EUR 10,000 per calendar year (special Union scheme - OSS).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries (Ausfuhrlieferungen) of bulk grain, soybeans, and yellow corn to third-country destinations qualify for a 0% VAT rate (tax exemption with input VAT deduction) under Sec. 6 UStG, provided that (1) the supplier or the non-EU customer transports the goods to a territory outside the EU; (2) title/power of disposal passes to the customer; and (3) the export is conditionally proven by book-entry and documentary evidence. Services directly economically attributable to export transactions, particularly brokerage, commission agency, and intermediation fees for cross-border commodity shipping (including bulk-vessel chartering), are likewise VAT-exempt under Sec. 4 No. 3 UStG when the underlying transaction is an export or intra-Community supply. Intra-Community supplies to EU customers are zero-rated under Sec. 6a UStG upon valid VAT identification proof. Export goods need not be declared for German VAT when placed under customs warehousing or external transit procedures. The exporter must substantiate physical departure from the EU within the territorial exit timeline; relevant Incoterms (FOB, CIF, CFR) and negotiation of an Irrevocable Transferable Letter of Credit do not, by themselves, satisfy zero-rating without customs documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) filed through ATLAS/ECS, containing the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and the exporter's EORI number
  • Customs Export Confirmation (Ausfuhrbestätigung) issued by the competent customs office or by an authorized forwarder/carrier (Absenderauskunft) as proof of exit to third-country territory
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Sea Waybill for ocean bulk-vessel cargo, or CMR/airway bill for overland or air transport
  • Commercial invoice and packing list specifying Incoterms (FOB, CIF, CFR), commodity grade (e.g., No. 2 Yellow Corn, Non-GMO Soybeans), quantities in metric tons, and vessel/charter particulars
  • Brokerage/commission agreement or multi-party contract evidencing the GmbH's role as broker or agent and the exact commission income that is the subject of the zero-rating claim
  • Payment evidence: Irrevocable Transferable Letter of Credit advice, SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation, or inward remittance certificate from a qualifying German bank
  • Certificate of Origin (EUR.1 / EUR-MED if required) and phytosanitary/veterinary certificates for the grain cargo
  • Destination customs release documentation from the importing country (import declaration or proof of customs entry at discharge port)

Since 1 January 2025, Germany mandates the receiving and processing of structured electronic invoices in the B2B domain: all VAT entrepreneurs must be able to receive e-invoices in an EN 16931-compliant format (XRechnung or Factur-X/ZUGFeRD 2). Issuance obligation is phased: mandatory from 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all other taxable businesses. Paper invoices or unstructured PDFs no longer satisfy statutory requirements. No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is currently imposed by German tax authorities; however, the BZSt is implementing the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) framework providing for transaction-level digital reporting (DRR) through an electronic clearance and reporting interface (expected operational from approximately 2030).

The German tax authorities impose strict documented-evidence conditions; if the export cannot be substantiated or the customs exit proof is missing or internally inconsistent, the 0% rate is retroactively denied and VAT is reassessed at 19% (or 7% where applicable) on the gross export or brokerage value, with late-payment interest under Sec. 233a AO at currently 1.8% per annum (0.5% per month for periods before 2019). The increased assessment period is 10 years for tax evasion (Sec. 169 AO); regular assessments are 4 years. Fraudulent export zero-rating, carousel (Umsatzsteuerkarussell) structures, and fictitious bills of lading trigger immediate clawback of input VAT credits and output VAT exemption: administrative negligence fines up to EUR 50,000 (Sec. 378 AO), criminal liability under Sec. 370 AO with imprisonment of six months to five years (up to ten years in aggravated cases), suspension of the assessment limitation period for up to ten years, and personal civil liability of managing directors (Geschäftsführer) under Sec. 69 AO for unpaid corporate VAT. In addition, the Deutsche Bundesbank may impose foreign-trade reporting sanctions for late or inaccurate FX repatriation/receipt reporting (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) and Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No exchange-control license, central-bank approval, or tax-clearance certificate is required for inbound equity subscriptions, outbound trade disbursements, repatriation of capital, or profit remittances of a German GmbH. The Euro is freely convertible and the GmbH may lawfully hold, invoice, and settle in EUR, USD, UAH, or any other currency. The only fiscal hurdle on profit remittance is the German dividend withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%), which may be reduced under an applicable double-taxation agreement, typically to 15% for portfolio dividends and to 0% for a qualifying 10% EU parent under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, subject to the statutory relief-at-source or refund procedure and the 12-month holding condition.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory deposit period, and no forced conversion ratio for export receipts from bulk agricultural commodities. A GmbH may retain export proceeds abroad, maintain foreign-currency accounts, and convert receipts according to commercial needs. The sole regulatory duty is statistical reporting under section 67 AWV: every payment received from a non-resident, and every payment made to a non-resident, of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank by the seventh working day of the month following the month of receipt or payment, using the Bundesbank's electronic external-statistics portal. Reporting applies equally to export proceeds credited to domestic accounts, foreign accounts, and proceeds received through documentary collections or letters of credit.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank, Frankfurt am Main), acting in cooperation with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin, Bonn/Frankfurt). The Bundesbank administers the external-sector payments statistics and all cross-border reporting duties under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV); BaFin supervises commercial banks, payment institutions, and electronic-money institutions for prudential conduct and anti-money-laundering compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an auxiliary treasury gateway for FX conversion, virtual sub-account pooling, and rapid cross-border wire concentration; Low as the sole or primary banking shell for the German GmbH. Offshore digital EMIs are lawful payment-transaction providers under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz – ZAG) implementing PSD2, but they are not credit institutions under section 32 of the German Banking Act (KWG). Consequently, ESCROW of statutory share capital, issuance or confirmation of irrevocable transferable letters of credit under UCP 600, SWIFT correspondent settlement, and Bundesbank AWV reporting for physical grain trades must remain with a BaFin-licensed tier-1 bank. Funds held through EMI omnibus accounts do not qualify for the statutory German deposit-guarantee scheme and may trigger enhanced counterparty due diligence by downstream banks. Recommended structure: a licensed German e-money institution (e.g., a BaFin-regulated EMI such as Solarisbank AG) may operate only as a collection-and-disbursement gateway above the primary house-bank account, never for statutory capital, LC, or final clearing purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant MT103 wire routing for the statutory share capital: the non-resident shareholder transfers EUR 25,000 (or the higher agreed Stammkapital) in EUR to the GmbH's formation account at its German house bank using the account BIC/IBAN, with the payment description 'Einlagenzahlung / share capital contribution on formation of the GmbH'. The bank issues a formation confirmation addressed to the notary; after registration in the Commercial Register, the account converts into an unrestricted operational account. The transaction is reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under section 67 AWV as a direct-investment inflow when the amount equals or exceeds EUR 12,500, due by the seventh working day of the following month, and is also captured in the statutory direct-investment statistics for non-resident participation. Payments arriving in foreign currency must be converted by the bank to EUR at the value date so that the required minimum capital stands at the free disposal of the managing director; shareholder loans above the share capital are permitted and are separately reportable as cross-border claims or liabilities.Outbound clearing for bulk grain, soybean, and corn disbursements uses SWIFT MT103 for broker commissions, freight, charter hire, demurrage, port disbursements, storage, and low-value operating expenses. Documentary trade settlements use irrevocable and transferable letters of credit governed by UCP 600 and ISBP 745, presented through the German house bank or a confirming bank. The LC documentary set must include the full commercial invoice, original bill of lading, weight and quality certificates (GAFTA/NOPA forms), phytosanitary certificate, certificate of origin (EUR.1 for EU-origin goods), insurance certificate, and the relevant survey reports. EUR settlement legs clear through TARGET2 (T2); USD settlement legs clear through the correspondent bank's CHIPS or Fedwire network. All outbound payments to non-residents equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 are reported to the Bundesbank under the AWV statutory timeline.Outright deliverable FX forwards (EUR/USD and all standard convertible crosses) documented under the German master agreement for forward foreign-exchange transactions (Rahmenvertrag für Devisentermingeschäfte) or an ISDA 2002 Master AgreementNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) exclusively for non-convertible or restricted currencies (e.g., UAH, KZT, NGN, EGP) to hedge physical commodity receivables and payables, documented under ISDA and subject to the bank's internal credit-limit framework, with no speculative open positionCross-currency swaps (EUR/USD, EUR/CHF, EUR/GBP) to manage medium-term working-capital and letter-of-credit exposure in settlement currencies other than EURPlain-vanilla FX put and call options for confirmed trade commitments, executed in the same master-agreement framework and reported in accordance with EMIR obligations where the GmbH qualifies as a non-financial counterparty subject to margin or trade-reporting thresholds, with full documentation under the bank's MaRisk-compliant derivative-risk-policy requirements

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as an auxiliary treasury gateway for FX conversion, virtual sub-account pooling, and rapid cross-border wire concentration; Low as the sole or primary banking shell for the German GmbH. Offshore digital EMIs are lawful payment-transaction providers under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz – ZAG) implementing PSD2, but they are not credit institutions under section 32 of the German Banking Act (KWG). Consequently, ESCROW of statutory share capital, issuance or confirmation of irrevocable transferable letters of credit under UCP 600, SWIFT correspondent settlement, and Bundesbank AWV reporting for physical grain trades must remain with a BaFin-licensed tier-1 bank. Funds held through EMI omnibus accounts do not qualify for the statutory German deposit-guarantee scheme and may trigger enhanced counterparty due diligence by downstream banks. Recommended structure: a licensed German e-money institution (e.g., a BaFin-regulated EMI such as Solarisbank AG) may operate only as a collection-and-disbursement gateway above the primary house-bank account, never for statutory capital, LC, or final clearing purposes.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer + 5.5% solidarity surcharge (0.825% effective) + municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a standard local multiplier of 400% (14%); combined typical burden ≈29.825%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer on dividends plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (1.375% effective), resulting in a aggregate 26.375%. A 15% rate applies to cash dividends if the parent is a non-portfolio shareholder (≥10%) and satisfies the 12-month holding period under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; further reductions may be available under a DTA (typically 5% for a ≥10% company, 15% for portfolio investors, 0% in certain treaty/EU cases).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Primary deadline: 31 July of the year following the assessment period (for calendar-year taxpayers). When a tax adviser is appointed, the deadline is automatically extended to 28 February (or 31 May on further notice) of the following year pursuant to §149(2) AO.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same deadline as the corporate income tax return: 31 July of the following year; extension to 28 February (or 31 May on request) when a tax adviser is engaged.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany imposes a mandatory transfer pricing documentation threshold per §4 GAufzV: local file documentation is required for cross-border controlled transactions exceeding EUR 5,000,000 (purchase/sale of goods) and EUR 500,000 (services); master file / group documentation is required when the group’s consolidated worldwide revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) applicable to non-extractive industries, but the principle of economic substance is judicially enforced through §42 AO (abuse of legal form), §1 AStG (transfer pricing adjustments), and the OECD Multilateral Instrument / Principal Purpose Test. A German GmbH acting as a commissionaire or trading hub must maintain a genuine operational presence – a registered office, management and day-to-day decision-making functions in Germany, qualified employees, physical assets (vessel chartering and documentation systems), and contractual capacity. Letter-box/conduit structures are disregarded for tax purposes, may lose DTA benefits, and are exposed to §41(2) AO recharacterization and hidden dividend distribution assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine under §370 AO; in extremely serious cases (e.g., large-scale evasion or fictitious transactions) imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years. Confiscation of assets and a public prosecutor's order of restitution.

Director personal liability: Geschäftsführer may not rely on the corporate veil; direct personal liability under §69 AO for unpaid taxes plus default interest. Criminal penalties, travel bans, asset freezes, and disqualification from holding directorships in Germany are routinely imposed; civil restitution claim is available to the insolvency administrator.
Inaccurate tax return / deficiency of books and records

Statutory sanction: Administrative late-filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag) up to 0.25% per month, capped at €25,000; an additional penalty for non-compliance with accounting obligations under §§ 162(2) AO allows an estimated assessment. Interest accrues at 0.5% per month on additional payment (1.8% p.a. actually §238 AO).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for all corporate taxes that were not withheld or paid when the company had sufficient funds (caused the loss); personal VAT liability also arises under §69 AO. Misfeasance claims can be advanced by creditors or the D&O insurer.
Transfer pricing / profit-shifting adjustment

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income upwards under §1 AStG with a mandatory additional 5% interest (self-assessment) and potential penalty of up to €300,000 for documentation deficiencies under §162(4) AO. Denial of treaty relief and possible requalification of the company's expenditures as concealed dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for the extra tax, interest, and penalties, and if the false documentation is intentional, criminal liability for tax evasion. Travel restrictions and financial collateral are common in serious cases; the Quantum of restitution can be in the millions.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: §42 AO denies the tax advantage and recharacterizes the structure; the entity is disqualified as a qualified intermediary for CIT, trade tax, VAT, and wage tax purposes. Combined with the MLA/Principal Purpose Test, treaty benefits are withdrawn and cross-border withholding rises to statutory rates. There is a separate fine for trading without a substance permit (if a certificate is required); for German GmbH, a substantial fine up to €100,000 may apply under §50 AO.

Director personal liability: Directors are held personally liable for the entire tax shortfall caused by the artificial structure, including hidden dividend withholding taxes and solidarity surcharge. In addition, they may face criminal charges, a travel ban pending investigation, and a civil action for breach of fiduciary duty (Vermeidung von Verbindlichkeiten).
Anti-Money-Laundering (AML) and Güterlieferung monitoring failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 10% of annual turnover per section 56 of the Money Laundering Act (GwG). Criminal liability for money laundering under §261 StGB: imprisonment up to 5 years or a monetary penalty; non-custodial thresholds are cancelled. The corporation may be prohibited from future cargo trades and denied financial licences.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally subject to fines and imprisonment if complicit; there is no indemnification from the company. Additionally, confiscation of all the laundered profits and a personal travel ban can be imposed while the criminal investigation is pending.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the competent local court (Amtsgericht) for the registered office jurisdiction.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), principally Sections 1-3, 5, 7-11, 13, 35 and 35a; notarisation requirements also arise under the German Civil Code and German Notarisation Act.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarised application for registration in the Commercial Register (notarielle Handelsregisteranmeldung), accompanied by the notarised articles of association and required formation documents.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 62 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory share capital is EUR 25,000. Before Commercial Register registration, at least EUR 12,500 must be paid in cash, including the minimum amount attributable to each subscribed share. Contributions must be made available to the GmbH; no general statutory escrow account is required, although a dedicated formation bank account is customary. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed and specifically documented before registration. The managing directors must declare that the capital is freely available to the company.

Funding & unlock path: An office generally requires no separate industrial civil-defence licence, but the operator must comply with state building law, workplace health and safety rules, fire prevention requirements, escape-route and occupancy standards, and employer obligations under the German Occupational Safety and Health Act. A warehouse or bonded facility may require building approval, fire-safety certification, structural load verification, emergency access, hazardous-substance assessment, occupational risk assessments and inspection by the competent building, fire or occupational-safety authority before operation.

Paid-in capital rule: The statutory share capital is EUR 25,000. Before Commercial Register registration, at least EUR 12,500 must be paid in cash, including the minimum amount attributable to each subscribed share. Contributions must be made available to the GmbH; no general statutory escrow account is required, although a dedicated formation bank account is customary. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed and specifically documented before registration. The managing directors must declare that the capital is freely available to the company.

German customs administration, principally the competent Hauptzollamt under the Union Customs Code framework; the Generalzolldirektion provides central customs administration functions. Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF); Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT IDs; local Finanzamt for corporate income and trade tax; ELSTER online portal (https://www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a GmbH in Germany must first file with the local Finanzamt the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (registration questionnaire), obtain a Steuernummer (tax number), and apply to BZSt for a USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number) using Form 27a or through ELSTER. For non-established foreign companies, a tax representative (steuerlicher Vertreter) is required and VAT registration is mandatory before commencing any taxable supply in Germany.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a GmbH in Germany must first file with the local Finanzamt the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (registration questionnaire), obtain a Steuernummer (tax number), and apply to BZSt for a USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number) using Form 27a or through ELSTER. For non-established foreign companies, a tax representative (steuerlicher Vertreter) is required and VAT registration is mandatory before commencing any taxable supply in Germany.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under German domestic law; the GmbH is per se an entity subject to entity-level taxation (Körperschaftsteuer and Gewerbesteuer). Available elections include VAT Organschaft (VAT group) under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, corporate tax group (ertragsteuerliche Organschaft) under §§14-18 KStG, small-entrepreneur exemption under §19 UStG where turnover is below the threshold, and hybrid mismatch elections in line with the German Anti-Tax Avoidance Rules (ATAD) implementation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold applies to German-resident entrepreneurs; all taxable persons must register regardless of turnover, though a small-entrepreneur exemption exists below €22,000 prior-year turnover (increasing to €25,000 as of 1 January 2025). Non-resident foreign suppliers are required to register from the first taxable supply in Germany with no de minimis threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods exports: zero-rating applies under §6 UStG when the goods are physically dispatched or transported from the EU customs territory to a third-country destination, the supplier or a commissioned third party provides the transit, and documentary and accounting evidence is retained. For offshore services: zero-rating applies under §4 Nr. 1b UStG for services directly related to exported or imported goods, or under the B2B place-of-supply rule in §3a UStG where the recipient is established outside the EU and receives the service for business purposes.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) lodged in ATLAS with a unique movement reference number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR or CIM consignment note evidencing that the goods left the customs territory of the European Union
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, export manifest, and, where relevant to the delivery model, an Inward Remittance Certificate as supplementary foreign-exchange evidence

Germany has not introduced a real-time fiscal clearance regime; however, certified technical security equipment (TSE) is mandatory for cash registers since 2020. From 1 January 2025, domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive e-invoices; XRechnung and ZUGFeRD structured electronic invoicing will be mandatory for all domestic B2B supplies by 2027/2028. The ELSTER portal and ATLAS customs system are the relevant e-government interfaces.

German tax authorities conduct systematic VAT audits and may reject export evidence or place-of-supply documentation. Invalid, false, or forged evidence triggers retrospective cancellation of the zero-rating, additional VAT at 19%, interest at 6% per annum under §233a AO, late-payment surcharges, fines up to €50,000, and criminal liability under §370 AO for tax evasion, including imprisonment up to five years and seizure of assets.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. Germany applies the EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (Articles 63–66 TFEU) and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. No exchange control or spatial currency restriction applies to resident GmbH entities. Profit remittance in the form of dividends, liquidation proceeds, royalties, or loan repayments is legally unrestricted and may be effected in any convertible currency; only standard tax rights (such as CIT/WHT and the obligation to provide tax residency/tax exemption certificates) apply, not foreign exchange approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender of export proceeds into EUR and no minimum conversion ratio. Export receipts of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics in accordance with Sections 66–68 of the AWV, generally no later than the seventh business day following the end of the month in which the payment is received. The report is required solely for statistics; no permission or central bank approval is required and no obligatory conversion of foreign currency proceeds is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (responsible for award, statistical and foreign-exchange administration functions under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act / AWV); BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) oversees banking conduct and anti-money-laundering supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury rail. A German-licensed electronic money institution (BaFin PSD2-licensed) may support SEPA lodgement and ancillary expense flows, but it cannot substitute for a full commercial bank account when opening the statutory share-capital escrow account, drawing inbound equity, or settling customs-duty accruals. Offshore digital EMIs without a BaFin licence cannot lawfully provide payment services to a German GmbH for operational treasury; reliance on such a provider would constitute a KYC/AML defect and invite administrative action by BaFin by reason of unlicensed payment activity.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Sanctions- and dual-use-sensitive trade flows: customs brokerage intermediary dealing with export categories that may touch sanctions lists, CNP (Catch-All) items, or U.S. embargoed destinations.

Root cause: Sanctions- and dual-use-sensitive trade flows: customs brokerage intermediary dealing with export categories that may touch sanctions lists, CNP (Catch-All) items, or U.S. embargoed destinations.

Defense evidence: Provide a KYC dossier comprising the customs brokerage licence (Zoll-Bewilligung), denied-party screening results for counterparties/UBOs, export-control classification notes (ECCN / EU Dual-Use Annex I), end-user undertakings, the anti-money-laundering internal policy (GwG §21), and a three-year detailed trade-transaction history prepared by the designated compliance officer.
Opaque shareholder structure in combination with a large inbound capital injection from foreign shareholders.

Root cause: Opaque shareholder structure in combination with a large inbound capital injection from foreign shareholders.

Defense evidence: Supply a notarised GmbH commercial-register excerpt, the current ‘Gesellschafterliste’ (shareholder list), a certified UBO-register extract, bank statements/source-of-funds trail for the capital injection, passport and residence-permit documents for foreign shareholders, and evidence that the contribution was credited to a Stammeinlage escrow account with a German tier-1 bank.
Heightened ML risk associated with the licensed customs-brokerage gatekeeper role in processing third-party import/export clearances.

Tax and payment-purpose red flags: risk of misclassification of funds flows, VAT/customs duty diversion, or inconsistent payment references causing Bundesbank statistical reporting failures.

Root cause: Tax and payment-purpose red flags: risk of misclassification of funds flows, VAT/customs duty diversion, or inconsistent payment references causing Bundesbank statistical reporting failures.

Defense evidence: Submit original contracts and invoices, import/export declarations under German ATLAS, VAT returns and Intrastat statements, the Bundesbank AWV reporting confirmation receipt, the Finanzamt tax-clearance certificate (Unbedenklichkeitsbescheinigung), and a purpose-code map that reconciles all inbound and outbound treasury flows to official ‘Zahlungsgrund’ reporting categories.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: (i) open a Capital Escrow Account (Stammeinlagekonto) with a tier-1 German bank in the legal name of the GmbH or the subscribing shareholder; (ii) execute an inbound SWIFT MT103 for each contribution tranche, quoting in tag 70 ‘Stammeinlage / Kapitalerhöhung’ together with the shareholder’s resolution; (iii) obtain the issuing bank’s electronic confirmation to the local registry court that the minimum statutory capital has been fully and legitimately credited; (iv) complete the GmbHG §7, §8 and §10 capital verification procedure by management; (v) after registration, transfer funds from the escrow account to the operational account; (vi) report the inbound capital payment to the Deutsche Bundesbank if it exceeds EUR 12,500, using the mandatory electronic BPM reporting interface with the payment-reason / Zahlungsgrund correlated to direct investment and capital contribution. No prior Bundesbank authorisation is required for a fully convertible currency transfer.Trade settlements run via (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border non-EUR commercial disbursements; (ii) SWIFT MT700/MT798 structured commercial documentary letters of credit under UCP600; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR outflows inside the EEA; and (iv) German customs-collection rails under ATLAS (Germany’s automated customs system) using cashless/direct debit protocols for import duties and VAT on customs transactions. All outbound disbursements must contain the exact referencing required by the Bundesbank under AWV for non-EEA payments above EUR 12,500, and the bank will require clarity of underlying trade documents before settling payments lacking a legitimate commercial purpose.Plain vanille FX forward contracts (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF) with a licensed German bank, documented under an ISDA 1998 FX or a master trading agreement and strictly tied to its commercial invoice exposures.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies that are illiquid or non-convertible (e.g. offshore RMB / CNY-NDF) entered into with BaFin-regulated credit institutions or through their foreign branches where the underlying is a bona fide trade receivable or payable.FX/currency swaps (swaps of principal and interest in EUR against a foreign currency) for asset-liability and liquidity-hedging purposes, executed under a standard ISDA Credit Support Annex and supported by board-approved hedging policy.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CIRS) where the GmbH has medium- or long-term financial liabilities denominated in foreign currency, with regular valuation and no speculative overlay.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where cross-border controlled transactions exceed €5 million for goods or €500,000 for services/intangibles; Master File required where worldwide group revenue exceeds €100 million

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a UAE-style ESR regime; substance is enforced through § 42 AO (abusive structures), § 8 AStG (CFC attribution), § 50d(3) EStG (beneficial ownership), and the requirement of a licensed customs-brokerage operation with local employees, office, ATLAS software, and real customs-clearance contracts. A GmbH with no economic activity, no licensed customs officers, and no ATLAS communiqués will be disregarded and treated as a transparent shell

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years for grave cases), monetary fines per § 370 AO, additional tax assessments, monthly interest at 0.15%, late-filing surcharge of up to €25,000, and customs-license suspension

Director personal liability: Managing directors face personal criminal conviction, personal fines, civil restitution of evaded taxes, travel bans, extradition, and piercing of the corporate veil for all unpaid taxes, customs duties, and VAT
Failure to File / Estimated Assessment

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharge up to €25,000; estimated assessment by the Finanzamt under § 162 AO; monthly interest 0.15%; enforcement seizure of bank balances, ACH interfaces, and customs bonding accounts

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for taxes withheld from employees and VAT under §§ 69, 71 AO; asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment in cases of intentional non-compliance
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Upward income adjustment to arm's length, documentation penalty up to €20,000, monthly interest on underpaid tax, additional trade tax, and denial of treaty benefits under § 50d(3) EStG

Director personal liability: Directors signing intercompany agreements without substance are personally liable for civil damages under German corporate-law duties; criminal tax evasion and travel bans may apply, and the corporate veil is pierced for evasion-related restitution
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / AML breach

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €100,000 under the GwG per violation, deregistration of the GmbH, customs-license revocation, refusal of VAT registration and treaty relief, and personal confiscation of artificially shifted profits

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face administrative AML fines, travel bans, extradition, personal confiscation, civil liability to creditors and customs authorities under veil-piercing doctrines, and imprisonment for money laundering under StGB § 261

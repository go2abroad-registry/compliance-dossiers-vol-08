Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the Local Court (Amtsgericht – Registergericht) of the district of the registered office. Electronic submission is effected exclusively through the authenticating Notar via the federal-states judicial e-filing portal (Justizportal des Bundes und der Länder / XJustiz).

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG – Limited Liability Companies Act), as published in the Federal Law Gazette. Core provisions: §§1-2 (formation and execution of Articles of Association), §5 (minimum registered share capital), §§7-10 (application for registration and constitutive effect of commercial register entry), §11 (pre-registration GmbH i.G.), §13 (separate legal personality), §§35-38 (managing directors), §§39-40 (shareholder and managing director lists), §78 (form of register filings); supplemented by the Gesetz über das Verfahren in Familiensachen und in den Angelegenheiten der freiwilligen Gerichtsbarkeit (FamFG) for register court procedure.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) containing the share subscription and the certified Application for Registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung), signed by all managing directors and submitted by the Notar, together with the certified shareholder list, managing director list, bank capital-deposit confirmation, and formation report where in-kind contributions are made.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum registered share capital is EUR 25,000 under §5 GmbHG. Before the registration application, at least 25% of the nominal amount of each cash contribution and, in aggregate, at least EUR 12,500 (50% of the statutory minimum) must be paid up. The funds must be credited to a bank account held in the name of the company in formation (GmbH i.G.) and remain at the free disposal of the managing directors; proof is furnished by a bank confirmation under §37(2) GmbHG or a managing director's declaration under §37(1) GmbHG. This does not require a court escrow account; the i.G. bank account operates as the fiduciary capital deposit vehicle. For single-member formations, the full cash contribution must be paid in unless the managing director is not the sole shareholder and the security conditions of §7(3) GmbHG are satisfied. In-kind contributions must be fully provided with a formation report (Sachgründungsbericht) and valuation evidence, and must be incorporated in the Articles.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification (Brandschutznachweis) and fire-protection inspection for transformer stations, containerised inverters, and site buildings under the Landesbauordnung; structural load and static inspections (Statische Prüfung) of module foundations, mounting/tracker structures, and crane stand areas; mandatory occupational workplace certificate (Arbeitsplatzfreigabe) under the Arbeitsstättenverordnung; site notification and a Safety and Health Protection Plan (SiGe-Plan) under the Baustellenverordnung, with prior notice to the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt; compliance with DGUV rules for work at height, cranes, live electrical works, and the general safety and health obligations of the Arbeitsschutzgesetz before any construction or commissioning activity begins.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum registered share capital is EUR 25,000 under §5 GmbHG. This does not require a court escrow account; the i.G. bank account operates as the fiduciary capital deposit vehicle. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr) and certain federal fiscal cross-border procedures; the locally competent Finanzamt (municipal tax office) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment; unified electronic filing portal ELSTER (www.elster.de), with BZSt online services (www.bzst.de) for VAT ID issuance and OSS/IOSS registrations.

E-government filing portal

For a domestic GmbH: the commercial register (Handelsregister) entry initiates automatic allocation of the corporate Steuernummer by the local Finanzamt; the GmbH's managing director must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' via ELSTER within one month of registration (Sec. 138 AO). For the USt-IdNr: submit Form 10 (Antrag auf Erteilung einer USt-IdNr) to the BZSt under Sec. 18c UStG. For a foreign (non-resident) entity performing solar/facility installation in Germany: immediate registration with the local Finanzamt is mandatory; if the foreign entity is not established in the EU and does not hold a domestic residence, a fiscal representative (fiskalischer Vertreter) must be appointed under Sec. 22a UStG, unless the customer is a German entrepreneur using reverse-charge pursuant to Sec. 13b UStG; the USt-IdNr is issued by the BZSt, typically within 4 to 8 weeks and requires submission of the export/import customs evidence (EORI number) where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the German GmbH is a fully resident corporate taxpayer subject to Körperschaftsteuer at 15% plus Solidaritätszuschlag at 5.5% of the corporate tax (0.825% effective) and Gewerbesteuer at municipal multipliers resulting in an effective combined trade tax of approximately 14% to 17.5%; the GmbH has territorial worldwide income subject to German taxation unless treaty protection applies and is a legal person distinct from its shareholders for VAT and income tax purposes. — For a domestic GmbH: the commercial register (Handelsregister) entry initiates automatic allocation of the corporate Steuernummer by the local Finanzamt; the GmbH's managing director must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' via ELSTER within one month of registration (Sec. 138 AO). For the USt-IdNr: submit Form 10 (Antrag auf Erteilung einer USt-IdNr) to the BZSt under Sec. 18c UStG. For a foreign (non-resident) entity performing solar/facility installation in Germany: immediate registration with the local Finanzamt is mandatory; if the foreign entity is not established in the EU and does not hold a domestic residence, a fiscal representative (fiskalischer Vertreter) must be appointed under Sec. 22a UStG, unless the customer is a German entrepreneur using reverse-charge pursuant to Sec. 13b UStG; the USt-IdNr is issued by the BZSt, typically within 4 to 8 weeks and requires submission of the export/import customs evidence (EORI number) where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the German GmbH is a fully resident corporate taxpayer subject to Körperschaftsteuer at 15% plus Solidaritätszuschlag at 5.5% of the corporate tax (0.825% effective) and Gewerbesteuer at municipal multipliers resulting in an effective combined trade tax of approximately 14% to 17.5%; the GmbH has territorial worldwide income subject to German taxation unless treaty protection applies and is a legal person distinct from its shareholders for VAT and income tax purposes.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists under German law. Statutory elections relevant to a GmbH EPC contractor include: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under Secs. 14/17 KStG and Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG, allowing VAT and income tax grouping with a German parent; (ii) reverse-charge statement under Sec. 13b UStG for services received from non-resident contractors; (iii) OSS registration under Sec. 18i UStG (Union scheme) and Sec. 18k UStG (Import One-Stop-Shop) for distance sales and electronic B2C services; (iv) application for a VAT exemption certificate under Secs. 4 No. 9-26 UStG for certain cross-border transactions; (v) Kleinunternehmer election under Sec. 20 UStG (available only to resident micro-entrepreneurs below EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover; generally unavailable to construction/EPC activity); (vi) documentary election to substantiate export zero-rating either by customs proof or by substitute evidence (Ersatzbelege) under Sec. 6(3) UStG and the BMF guidance of 28 May 2021.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to an established German GmbH; every entrepreneur exercising an economic activity is registrable under Sec. 2 UStG. Domestic micro-entrepreneurs qualify for the Kleinunternehmer exemption below EUR 22,000 prior-year and EUR 50,000 current-year forecast, but this exemption does not apply to construction works or to non-resident EPC entities. For intra-EU distance sales and digital/electronically supplied B2C services, the harmonized registration/OSS threshold is EUR 10,000 of EU-wide turnover; for intra-EU acquisitions of goods by a non-entrepreneur the German acquisition threshold is EUR 12,500 (Sec. 1a(3) UStG). Any foreign entity performing solar plant construction, installation, or site assembly on German soil is immediately VAT-registrable without a minimum revenue threshold, except where the customer is a German taxable person liable under the reverse-charge mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (i) exports (Ausfuhrlieferungen) under Sec. 4 No. 1 letter a and Sec. 6 UStG, where goods are transported by the supplier or on its behalf from Germany to a third-country destination and the export is evidenced by customs release (customs declaration, MRN); (ii) intra-EU dispatches under Sec. 6a UStG, where the goods are transported to another EU member state and the customer discloses a valid USt-IdNr, with the electronic validation a mandatory substantive condition; (iii) services connected with immovable property located outside Germany (including the design, installation, and construction of a solar plant abroad) which are outside the German VAT scope per Sec. 3a(2) No. 1 UStG (place of supply is the foreign land); such services are structurally 0% German VAT but must be substantiated through contracts, land registry/municipal permit evidence, work schedules, and billed milestones. For an EPC contract bundling exported goods and foreign installation services, the value of goods shipped and cleared through customs must be separated from on-site services; otherwise the entire consideration may be treated as a single supply with reclassification risk.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS Ausfuhranmeldung / SAD declaration with MRN reference)
  • Export Accompanying Document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument / ECS MRN) confirming exit from the EU
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement) or CMR / Air Waybill evidencing transportation to the third-country destination
  • Commercial Invoice (Rechnung) with full exporter identification and Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit confirmation / SWIFT CRF) demonstrating receipt of foreign payment, accepted as substitute evidence under the BMF 28 May 2021 guidance where customs documentation is incomplete
  • VIES / USt-IdNr validation printout for intra-EU zero-rated supplies under Sec. 6a UStG
  • Intrastat declaration and ECS summary listing (where applicable) for intra-EU dispatch thresholds
  • EUR.1 Movement Certificate or certificate of origin where preferential tariff treatment is claimed by the foreign buyer

Germany has no zero-risk national 'real-time fiscal stamp' clearance regime; it operates a post-audit model with mandatory structured e-invoicing. From 1 January 2025, every German-established business must be able to receive electronic invoices conforming to the European standard EN 16931 (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.0.x, or PEPPOL BIS 3.0). Mandatory issuance of e-invoices applies from 1 January 2027 for entrepreneurs with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all German B2B suppliers. The BZSt administers an electronic invoice central register for accessibility and authenticity checks, but clearance is not a prior approval; issuers must transmit via PEPPOL/API/EDI maintaining audit trails. Non-EU EPC contractors issuing invoices to German customers must also comply when they have a German establishment or use reverse-charge statement on paper/PDF until 31 December 2027 (transitional periods for EDI/PDF).

Export zero-rating is subject to focused documentary audit under Sec. 6(3) UStG; if the exporter fails to produce decisive evidence of the export or intra-EU movement, the supply is retroactively reclassified as taxable at 19% German VAT. Interest accrues at 0.5% per month on the under-declared VAT (Sec. 233a AO). Sec. 26b UStG imposes an additional Strafzuschlag of 19% of the VAT amount where the tax-free treatment was obtained through forged, falsified, or knowingly abusive export proof. Criminal liability may be asserted under Sec. 370 AO (tax evasion) with imprisonment of up to 5 years, or 6 months to 10 years in aggravated cases; the assessment limitation period is extended to 10 years for serious tax evasion (Sec. 169(2) AO), and exports involving solar equipment are cross-checked by the German customs investigation service (Zollfahndung) and the BZSt anti-fraud unit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the euro for both current account and capital account transactions by virtue of the EU internal market freedoms and Articles 63–66 TFEU, implemented domestically under the AWG. There are no exchange controls, no central-bank approval requirements for foreign investment, and no restrictions on profit remittance or dividend distribution for a German GmbH. The only compliance hurdles are (i) German withholding tax on dividends and certain payments (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 26.375%, reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or applicable treaty when conditions are met), (ii) anti-money-laundering and sanctions screening by the receiving banking institution, and (iii) AWV statistical reporting thresholds. Capital from non-resident shareholders may be freely injected as share capital, capital reserve (agio), or shareholder loans; each form has distinct German corporate-law and tax documentation requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no retention requirements. A Germany-based EPC contractor may freely hold and dispose of export receipts in EUR or foreign currency. The relevant mandatory obligations are statistical only: (i) cross-border payments (receipts and disbursements) between residents and non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 in a calendar month must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank, generally within five working days of the month in which the payment was made or received; (ii) foreign receivables and payables (e.g., milestone receivables from non-resident employers and payables to non-resident subcontractors) exceeding EUR 5,000 at the balance-sheet date or at creation must be reported by the seventh calendar day of the month following the relevant event; and (iii) participation in foreign entities must be reported under Section 60 AWV. All reports are submitted electronically via the Bundesbank's information system (IBS/portal).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting as the German foreign-exchange, cross-border payments, and balance-of-payments statistics authority under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV). Responsible ministries include the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) for trade-control licensing and the Federal Ministry of Finance (BMF) for sanctions and financial-market policy. Banking prudential, AML, and sanctions supervision over financial institutions is exercised by BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

CONDITIONALLY FEASIBLE – rated MEDIUM-HIGH for a secondary liquidity rail, not for sole treasury infrastructure. BaFin-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and EEA-passported EMIs may open German-IBAN operational accounts for a GmbH with lighter onboarding friction and full SEPA Credit Transfer and SWIFT MT103 capability in many cases. Critical legal and operational constraints: (i) EMIs cannot issue documentary letters of credit, standby LCs, or bank guarantees, and cannot provide commercial escrow or LC confirmation services; (ii) EMI deposits are not bank deposits and generally do not benefit from the German statutory deposit-guarantee scheme (Einlagensicherung) in the same manner as commercial-bank deposits; (iii) EMIs are not authorised to take deposits of client monies for project milestone or retention escrow; and (iv) de-risking policies may trigger abrupt account closure for high-value construction-industry flows routed through a single institution. Recommended use: pre-onboarding bridge account, FX conversion, and cross-border payments in currencies that Tier-1 banks price unfavourably.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital from non-resident shareholders to the German GmbH: (i) execute the contribution by notarised shareholders' resolution (for share capital, capital reserve/agio, or shareholder loan), and, for formal capital increases, file the resolution with the Commercial Register before funds are recorded as share capital; (ii) instruct the remitting bank to send a SWIFT MT103 with purpose description 'Kapitaleinlage / Zuzahlung in die Kapitalrücklage' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' as applicable, including the corporate reference and resolution date; (iii) the receiving German bank will verify the Commercial Register filing or the shareholders' resolution, the UBO declaration, and the beneficiary's tax registration before crediting; (iv) if the inbound payment causes the monthly cross-border payment reporting threshold (EUR 12,500) to be exceeded, the GmbH must file the AWV balance-of-payments report with the Deutsche Bundesbank within five working days of the month-end; (v) foreign receivables or obligations created thereby are separately reported under the EUR 5,000 threshold rule; and (vi) the payment message must not use trade-purpose codes such as 'payment for goods' or 'personal remittance', as misstating the purpose code will trigger reclassification, potential BaFin inquiry, and withholding-tax risk on the contribution.Permitted clearing rails for EPC trade disbursements (subcontractor, equipment, and supplier payments): (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) in euro for payees within SEPA, using the ISO 20022 pain.001 message with the underlying contract reference; (ii) SWIFT MT103 (telegraphic transfer) for non-euro and outside-SEPA payments, with ISO 20022/UN-CEFACT purpose codes (e.g., general construction) and full beneficiary bank details; (iii) documentary import letters of credit for equipment procurement, issued or confirmed via SWIFT MT700/MT710, subject to UCP600, payable at sight or by usance draft, with the EPC milestone certificate as a supporting document; (iv) bank guarantees (advance payment, performance, retention) issued under URGD 758, typically counter-guaranteed by the employer's bank; (v) standby letters of credit under ISP98 via SWIFT MT760 for security posting where commercial LCs are not accepted; and (vi) export credit agency-backed guarantee arrangements (e.g., Euler Hermes/Bundesdeckung) for large value-added content. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 per cross-border control or non-control transaction under Section 6 AStG and General Administrative Principles on Transfer Pricing (Verwaltungsgrundsätze); transfer pricing documentation (Master File, Local File, CbCR) required if the cross-border transaction volume exceeds EUR 1,000,000 for related-party transactions and EUR 100,000 for ancillary services; CbCR threshold: consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million

Economic substance scope: Germany applies the 'Substance over Form' doctrine and anti-abuse rules under Section 42 AO; the concept of a 'missing economic substance' is primarily relevant under BEPS Action 5 and EU substance requirements, but Germany does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like offshore jurisdictions; instead, German tax authorities require actual operational substance (e.g., personnel, premises, decision-making, risk-taking) for treaty entitlement and for the application of the 95% dividend exemption under Section 8b KStG when foreign interposed companies are involved; a letterbox company is disregarded under Section 42 AO and its transactions may be recharacterized; German courts apply the 'Wirtschaftsgut-Eigentum' and 'Vermietungsverhältnis' principles to deny treaty benefits where substance is absent

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and a monetary fine under Section 370 AO; up to 10 years imprisonment in particularly serious cases (Section 370(2) AO); confiscation of assets obtained from the offense under Sections 73ff StGB; additional late payment surcharges and interest (0.5% per month on back taxes under Section 233a AO)

Statutory representative liability (Geschäftsführer liability)

Statutory sanction: Director is personally liable for corporate tax debts and social security contributions (Section 69 AO, Section 34 AO); penalty of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 for administrative violations; criminal conviction and imprisonment up to 5 years; entry into the debtors' register; prohibition from holding directorship under GewO Section 35

Director personal liability: Civil restitution of all unpaid taxes to the tax office; personal assets may be seized in bankruptcy proceedings; travel ban under Section 132 AO for non-payment of taxes; imprisonment in case of intentional evasion of payment (Section 283 StGB)
Money laundering (Geldwäsche) and financing of terrorism

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine under Section 261 StGB; enhanced penalties for commercial or gang-related laundering up to 10 years; confiscation of assets; license revocation; mandatory fines for non-compliance with GwG obligations by obliged entities under Sections 56 GWG up to EUR 100,000 or 10% of annual turnover

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners with 25% or more equity must be registered in the Transparenzregister; non-registration is an administrative offense with fines up to EUR 150,000; personal criminal liability for approving money-laundering transactions
Transfer pricing documentation and adjustment (Verrechnungspreisdokumentation)

Statutory sanction: Penalty for failure to produce documentation under Section 162 AO: minimum EUR 5,000 up to 10% of the adjusted income, or up to EUR 500,000; penalty for late submission EUR 100 per day up to EUR 1,000,000; additional 6% interest on transfer pricing adjustments; possible business asset transfer tax under Section 1 AStG if the adjustment recharacterizes a transaction at exit

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly liable for the company's tax debt including penalties; if the director intentionally participated in sham pricing, criminal tax evasion charges apply
Shell company abuse / treaty shopping and withholding tax fraud

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and application of 25% withholding tax plus solidarity surcharge; recharacterization of transactions under Section 42 AO; retroactive tax assessment and interest; fines for misrepresentation under Section 370 AO; for intermediaries, the Federal Central Tax Office can impose an administrational penalty of up to EUR 40,000 under Section 50a EStG (failure to comply with withholding obligations)

Director personal liability: Civil restitution of the withholding tax that should have been deducted; criminal liability for aiding and abetting tax evasion; if the director is the sole recipient, direct liability for personal income tax on the distribution
Umsatzsteuer (VAT) fraud and reverse-charge misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: VAT evasion fine up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under Section 26, 26b UStG and Section 370 AO; separate penalty for unjustified VAT deduction up to 10% of the invoice amount under Section 25 UStG; forfeiture of the amount of VAT evaded

Director personal liability: Directors who are involved in issuing false invoices are personally liable under Section 71 AO as the person responsible for fraudulent accounting; they may be subject to attachment of assets and imprisonment

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authority for corporate tax and domestic VAT registration is the locally responsible Finanzamt (tax office) of the GmbH's registered office or place of management. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and administers the relevant VAT identification-number portal. Electronic tax filings and registrations are generally processed through the ELSTER portal administered by the German tax authorities.

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated GmbH must obtain a domestic tax number (Steuernummer) by submitting the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt. The filing normally includes the commercial-register extract, articles of association, registered office, business activities, directors, beneficial ownership information, expected turnover, bank details, accounting period, and VAT data. The GmbH must separately apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) where it undertakes intra-EU supplies, acquisitions, or other transactions requiring VAT identification. A foreign entity conducting taxable activities in Germany must register with the Finanzamt responsible under the German allocation rules and submit the applicable questionnaire; it must also obtain a USt-IdNr. where required. Germany generally does not impose a universal fiscal-representative requirement on non-EU businesses, although special VAT procedures and transaction-specific obligations may apply. Tax registration does not replace commercial-register, trade-law, medical-device, customs, or product-compliance registrations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is ordinarily subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax; Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a GmbH. A GmbH may participate in a tax-consolidated group (Organschaft) if the statutory financial, organizational, and profit-transfer requirements are satisfied. Certain partnership entities may elect corporate income tax treatment under Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act, but that election is not applicable to an already incorporated GmbH. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules, including Section 4k of the Income Tax Act and related ATAD implementing provisions, can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences for imported mismatches, deduction-without-inclusion outcomes, or duplicate deductions. Entity classification is not elective for purposes of avoiding GmbH-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a German-established GmbH, there is no general turnover threshold that removes the obligation to register for taxable business activities; registration is generally required from commencement of taxable operations. The domestic small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) may apply where prior-year German turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and the taxpayer's eligibility; exempt businesses generally do not charge VAT and cannot deduct input VAT. A non-established foreign business generally has no German domestic registration threshold for taxable German transactions, subject to reverse-charge, distance-selling, One Stop Shop, and other special rules. The EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies principally to certain intra-EU consumer distance sales and electronically supplied, telecommunications, broadcasting, and electronic services, not as a general German business-registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax recovery under the German export-supply rules where the goods are transported or dispatched to a third country, the supplier or customer effects the transport as legally required, the goods physically leave the EU customs territory, and the supplier maintains prescribed documentary and accounting evidence. The VAT treatment is technically an exemption with input-tax deduction rather than a domestic 0% taxable rate. Intra-EU business-to-business supplies are generally exempt intra-Community supplies, not third-country exports, and require valid customer VAT identification, transport evidence, and recapitulative-statement compliance. Business-to-business services are generally taxed at the customer's location under the place-of-supply rules and may be outside German VAT or subject to reverse charge; they are not automatically treated as zero-rated exports. Services linked to goods, immovable property, passenger transport, events, or other special categories require separate place-of-supply analysis.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and Movement Reference Number (MRN), where required
  • Customs confirmation that the goods exited the EU customs territory, including the electronic exit confirmation or equivalent official export evidence
  • Transport documentation, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, freight-forwarder evidence, or carrier tracking records

Germany has no universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance or continuous transaction-reporting system and does not generally require pre-issuance digital stamping by the tax authority. From 1 January 2025, German-established businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. Issuance obligations are phased: paper and other invoices remain generally permitted during 2025; transitional rules generally permit non-structured invoices through 2026, and through 2027 for issuers whose preceding calendar-year turnover did not exceed EUR 800,000; from 2028 the structured e-invoice requirement applies broadly to qualifying domestic B2B transactions. Permitted structured formats include XRechnung and compliant EN 16931-based formats such as qualifying ZUGFeRD, with compliant EDI arrangements also possible. Public-sector transactions may be subject to earlier or separate federal and state e-invoicing requirements. E-invoice files and relevant accounting records must be retained in an auditable, unaltered form under German tax-record requirements; no general VAT API clearance submission is currently required.

Germany has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days; therefore, no mandatory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline applies to ordinary German export VAT treatment. If export conditions or documentary requirements are not proven, the Finanzamt may retrospectively deny the exemption, assess output VAT, deny or adjust related input-tax recovery, and charge interest under the German Fiscal Code. Incorrect VAT returns may trigger administrative fines, tax-liability assessments, and correction obligations. Intentional tax evasion may result in imprisonment of up to five years or a fine, with aggravated cases punishable by imprisonment of up to ten years; negligent tax reduction is subject to separate penalties. False or manipulated customs evidence may additionally result in customs sanctions, recovery of duties or taxes, and proceedings under customs and fiscal law. Export VAT refunds are subject to audit, repayment, interest, and potential denial where the evidence is incomplete, inconsistent, fraudulent, or not linked to the specific shipment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full currency convertibility on both current and capital account under EU free movement of capital (TFEU Art. 63). Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and liquidation proceeds to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted and require no exchange-control approval; only applicable corporate withholding taxes, documentary evidence, and standard bank compliance checks apply. No capital vs current account distinction creates a material hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained freely in any convertible currency or converted at market rates. For statistical purposes under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), cross-border receipts/payments exceeding EUR 12,500 or equivalent must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank on a monthly basis by the fifth business day of the following month. This is a statistical only, not a repatriation or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank), with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) regulating banking conduct and market integrity; no separate foreign-exchange administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-Low for an offshore digital EMI. A fintech EMI without a German or EEA payment-services licence cannot provide EUR-denominated payment services, access TARGET2/SEPA clearing, or act as an issuing/advising bank under documentary credits. A BaFin-supervised German/EU EMI may be used for cash-flow overlay, disbursement automation, and FX execution, but it cannot replace a fully licensed commercial bank for escrow arrangements, commercial LC settlement, or syndicated liquidity management. Legal reliance on offshore EMIs is therefore highly constrained.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank directly to the GmbH's dedicated capital escrow account at the German tier-1 bank. Wire instructions must include the GmbH's legal name, IBAN, BIC, and beneficiary account description. There is no mandatory national payment-purpose code, but the bank's external-payment form must classify the transaction as capital transfer / equity contribution (not a trade or service payment). For capital injections from non-EU residents, the GmbH must file the Bundesbank direct investment and cross-border payment statistical reports under AWV.

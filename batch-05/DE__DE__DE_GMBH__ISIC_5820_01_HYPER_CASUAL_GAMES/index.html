Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office, Cologne/Berlin, for VAT ID (USt-IdNr.), OSS registration and EU cross-border tax administration; local Finanzamt (municipal Tax Office at the GmbH's place of management) for corporate income tax, trade tax, payroll tax and VAT returns; official electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), the GmbH must file the post-registration tax questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung, Form 9401) with the responsible Finanzamt within one month of commencing business; the Finanzamt assigns the 17-digit tax number (Steuernummer) and creates the local corporate tax, trade tax, payroll tax and VAT files. The EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, prefix DE) is applied for separately with the BZSt via ELSTER, usually issued within 2–6 weeks upon proof of taxable activity and place of management. For intra-EU and global B2C supplies of hyper-casual games and in-app content, the GmbH must additionally register under the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) Union scheme on the BZSt online portal; non-EU revenue channels (AdMob/AppLovin payouts) do not trigger a separate German registration requirement, as the GmbH is domestically established.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is per se subject to entity-level taxation under Sec. 1(1) Nr. 1 KStG (15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and trade tax at 3.5% × municipal Hebesatz, aggregating to approximately 29–32%); no US-style check-the-box or transparent partnership election is available (Sec. 1a KStG partnership option does not apply). Available statutory elections: (i) Organschaft fiscal-unity election under Sec. 14 KStG, Sec. 2(2) Nr. 2 GewStG and Sec. 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, enabling income, trade tax and VAT pooling with a controlling domestic parent; (ii) small-business VAT exemption election under Sec. 19 UStG where turnover thresholds are met; (iii) trade-tax loss carry-forward and reinvestment reserve elections; (iv) ATAD hybrid-mismatch designations/counter-measures under Sec. 4k EStG, Sec. 14 Nr. 3 KStG for hybrid instruments or hybrid branches; (v) OSS scheme election replacing country-by-country EU VAT registration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 projected annual turnover ceiling for the small-business exemption from 1 January 2025 (EUR 22,000 for 2023–2024), with EUR 100,000 total revenue cap (Sec. 19 UStG); EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold for intra-Community distance sales of goods and B2C electronically supplied services covered by the OSS Union scheme (Sec. 3c, Sec. 18j UStG); however, no de-minimis registration threshold applies to B2C electronically supplied services (digital games, in-app purchases) supplied to consumers established in Germany — a German-established GmbH must be VAT-registered from its first cent of German business, and non-established digital suppliers must register or use the non-Union OSS immediately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical goods exported from Germany to destinations outside the European Union qualify for 0% VAT under Sec. 4 Nr. 1 lit. a in conjunction with Sec. 6 UStG, provided the goods leave the EU within one month (max. 3 months; 6 months for B2C exports) and the exporter retains conclusive proof of physical exit. Intra-EU supplies of goods to a customer holding a valid VAT ID are tax-exempt as intra-Community supplies under Sec. 4 Nr. 1 lit. b, Sec. 6a UStG, reportable in the EC Sales List. For the hyper-casual game business model, software licensing and downloadable game content follow place-of-supply rules: B2B supplies to third-country customers are outside German VAT (place at customer establishment, reverse charge/outside scope under Sec. 3a UStG); B2C digital services to non-EU consumers are outside the territorial scope of German VAT (customer-residence rule); the 0% export regime with customs documentation applies to physical/digital goods cleared through customs, whereas offshore e-services rely on customer-location proof and OSS reporting instead of export certificates.

  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS/AES) with EXIT CONFIRMATION
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR (carrier documentation evidencing physical departure from the EU)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list cross-referencing the export declaration (Zollanmeldung-Nummer)
  • Proof of Export (Ausfuhrnachweis) in accordance with Sec. 9 UStDV (B2C) and Sec. 10 UStDV (B2B)
  • Intra-Community VAT ID confirmation and EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung) reference for intra-EU supplies
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank settlement evidence from AdMob and AppLovin payout rails for payment traceability

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping or digital BOI regime. The B2B e-invoicing mandate under Sec. 14 UStG applies progressively: from 1 January 2025 all taxable persons must be able to receive structured e-invoices (EN 16931 format: XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x or EDI); from 1 January 2027 businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all businesses must issue e-invoices. Transmission via the Peppol network is permissible; no pre-clearance or digital stamping by the Finanzamt exists. B2C hyper-casual game sales via Google Play/App Store and ad-monetized payout rails (AdMob/AppLovin) fall outside the B2B e-invoicing obligation; the GmbH remains accountable for German VAT on German consumer supplies through the normal UStVA (monthly/quarterly) return or the OSS declaration filed on the BZSt portal.

Where export proof or payment-repatriation evidence is defective, the Finanzamt retrospectively denies the 0% exemption and reassesses the supply at 19% German VAT, concurrently disallowing input VAT deductions and issuing a tax clawback assessment; the standard 4-year VAT assessment period extends to 10 years for tax evasion and 5 years for gross negligence (Sec. 169 AO), with late-payment interest accruing at 0.5% per month (6% per annum, Sec. 233a AO). Fraudulent or fictitious export zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, punishable with imprisonment of up to 5 years or fines; grossly negligent conduct is penalized under Sec. 378 AO with fines of up to EUR 50,000, and sustained audit scrutiny (including cross-border customs-VAT data matching) may trigger criminal referral and disqualification of management.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany imposes no foreign exchange controls under substantive law. The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions pursuant to EU Treaty free movement of capital provisions, the German Basic Law (Grundgesetz), and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV). There are no statutory hurdles to dividend distribution or profit remittance by a GmbH to non-resident shareholders, provided that corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer, 15%), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, municipality-depending rate), and, where applicable, the withholding tax on dividends (25% plus solidarity surcharge where still applicable) are properly settled or reduced under an applicable double tax treaty. Non-resident beneficial owners face no Exchange Control approval requirement. The only practical constraint is the statistical reporting duty to Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction or aggregated similar payments, which is an information obligation under AWV Section 63, not a licensing or conversion restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Germany abolished all mandatory surrender or mandatory conversion requirements for export proceeds. Exporters and digital service providers, including hyper-casual game publishers receiving AdMob and AppLovin payouts, may retain revenue in any currency in any freely convertible account. A residual duty exists to report aggregated inward cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics; this reporting must be submitted electronically by the tenth day of the month following the reporting month via the Joint Minimum Reporting System (Allgemeines Meldeportal) using the enterprise's Business Transaction Registry Identifier (CRF-ID).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy and cross-border payment statistics; the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) exercises prudential supervision over payment institutions and credit institutions operating in Germany.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for operational disbursement rails and LOW for statutory capital injection and core treasury concentration. A German GmbH may lawfully open a payment account with a licensed EEA electronic money institution (EMI) or payment institution (PI) passporting to Germany under PSD2, such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or a licensed German EMI such as PPRO or solarisBank ecosystem entities. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI/PI is not a credit institution under the German Banking Act (KWG), so a notary confirming the minimum share capital of EUR 25,000 under Section 54 GmbHG will typically reject a capital confirmation based on an EMI-held account; (ii) EMI funds are not covered by the statutory deposit protection schemes applicable to banks; (iii) major ad networks (Google AdMob, AppLovin) commonly apply payout rules requiring a German bank IBAN or an account held with a full license credit institution, and may delay or reject payouts to third-party EEA EMIs; (iv) high-volume inbound payout streams into a non-bank account trigger enhanced due diligence by the EMI's partner bank. Recommended usage: wage attachments, reimbursement cards, secondary vendor settlement, and FX niche corridors only; the primary treasury relationship must remain with a tier-1 German credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol: the shareholder (national or non-resident) wires the cash contribution in EUR to the GmbH's dedicated capital verification account (Geschäftskonto für Stammeinlage) at a German credit institution. The payment must reference the company name, commercial register number, and expressly state the purpose as 'Einzahlung auf das Stammkapital / contribution to share capital'. No FX conversion is required when the contribution is paid in EUR from a eurozone SEPA account; when paid in USD or other foreign currency, the converting bank must apply standard customer conversion rates and issue a conversion confirmation suitable for the notary. The notary's confirmation under Section 54 GmbHG requires free disposal over the contributed amount, so an EMI account or a blocked account unrelated to the company's own name is not acceptable. The GmbH must report the inbound cross-border payment to Deutsche Bundesbank if the single credit or the aggregate of related inbound transfers exceeds EUR 12,500, using AWV reporting code class for capital investments / direct investment (equity injection) and including the shareholder identification and purpose code. Recommended verification package: SWIFT MT103 or SEPA transfer receipt, bank statement showing value date, notary certificate (Errichtungsprotokoll plus confirmation of capital effects), and Transparenzregister confirmation.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT/ISO 20022 pain.001) is mandatory for all euro disbursements to EU/EEA counterparties, including contractors, co-development studios, app analytics providers, and EU cloud hosting invoices; (ii) SWIFT MT103 (STP) is used for non-EUR negotiable disbursements, mainly USD payments to US-based advertising platforms, AdTech vendors, and franchise licenses, with proper RFB-purpose codes (e.g., 'SD' – service-related disbursement, 'KL' – trade obligations) and use of the bank's nostro routing; (iii) commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) are structurally non-essential for a digital asset-light hyper-casual game publisher; they are reserved for contractual obligations with large co-development partners or platform providers requiring documentary security, and are available at tier-1 banks without FX authority involvement; (iv) SWIFT MT940/MT101 reconciliation securities ensure liquidity monitoring; daily sweep accounts and notional pooling are permissible under German law. Specific disbarment: outbound payments from a German GmbH to countries subject to sanctioned or embargoed regimes, or to named parties on EU/German sanctions lists, require OFAC/EU-Sanctions/Russian-Watch screening and may not be released absent a BaFin and Bundesbank licence.Outright FX spot and forward contracts (Devisenkassageschäft und Devisentermingeschäft) for EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY concluded with a tier-1 German bank under a standard ISDA or German framework agreement, with no restriction beyond MiFID II suitability and board resolution for the net open position limit.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) solely for illiquid currencies in payout markets where the GmbH's ad revenues originate (e.g., BRL, TRY, ARS) where a deliverable foreign-exchange market is absent; permitted only on a documented Netherlands- or Frankfurt-market counterparty basis and without leverage above 1:2.FX options (vanilla European option style) for revenue arbitrage hedging of AdMob/AppLovin projected monthly USD payouts, subject to an approved treasury policy and MaRisk requirements governing the bank's credit risk to the GmbH.Cross-currency swaps (XCCY) for longer-term anticipated USD/EUR cash-flow mismatches arising from fixed-price traffic acquisition contracts with US platforms and euro-denominated payroll; these instruments are permitted under German law and are generally subject to the same analytical segregation and clearing obligations under EMIR (reporting and margin posting if the bank's counterparty threshold is exceeded).Plain FX forward netting (period-average forward windows) for aggregated monthly ad-network receivables and platform payables, capped to 110% of documented net exposure, with the bank requiring a 2% initial margin posted in EUR.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for operational disbursement rails and LOW for statutory capital injection and core treasury concentration. A German GmbH may lawfully open a payment account with a licensed EEA electronic money institution (EMI) or payment institution (PI) passporting to Germany under PSD2, such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or a licensed German EMI such as PPRO or solarisBank ecosystem entities. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI/PI is not a credit institution under the German Banking Act (KWG), so a notary confirming the minimum share capital of EUR 25,000 under Section 54 GmbHG will typically reject a capital confirmation based on an EMI-held account; (ii) EMI funds are not covered by the statutory deposit protection schemes applicable to banks; (iii) major ad networks (Google AdMob, AppLovin) commonly apply payout rules requiring a German bank IBAN or an account held with a full license credit institution, and may delay or reject payouts to third-party EEA EMIs; (iv) high-volume inbound payout streams into a non-bank account trigger enhanced due diligence by the EMI's partner bank. Recommended usage: wage attachments, reimbursement cards, secondary vendor settlement, and FX niche corridors only; the primary treasury relationship must remain with a tier-1 German credit institution.

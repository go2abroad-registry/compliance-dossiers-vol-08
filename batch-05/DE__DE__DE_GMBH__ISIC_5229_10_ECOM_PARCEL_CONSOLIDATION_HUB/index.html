E-government filing portal

For the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID), the applicant files through BZSt/ELSTER after tax office certification, providing commercial register excerpts, articles of association, proof of business premises, anticipated turnover schedules, and the foreign tax identification number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the GmbH is a domestic corporate tax subject with legal personality, taxed at entity level on worldwide income under Körperschaftsteuer and Gewerbesteuer; it is also a distinct VAT taxable person. It is not pass-through eligible for German tax purposes.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide check-the-box elections. Relevant statutory elections include corporate income tax/trade tax group taxation (Körperschaftsteuer-Gruppe under KStG §14, Gewerbesteuer-Organschaft under GewStG §2a) requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement and financial integration, and the VAT fiscal unity regime (Organschaft under UStG §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2) permitting single VAT treatment for closely integrated German entities. Hybrid mismatch elections and specific allocation mechanisms follow ATAD implementation rather than free entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover registration threshold for a German-resident GmbH; all entrepreneurs with business establishments in Germany are registrable for VAT. For EU cross-border distance sales, the intra-Union distance selling threshold is EUR 10,000. The small-business exemption under UStG §19 applies only to annual turnover not exceeding EUR 25,000 in the preceding year and EUR 100,000 in the current year, and is generally not suitable for a GmbH acting as a cross-border logistics hub.

Germany does not yet impose real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory tax-authority API validation, or digital stamping. A phased central B2B e-invoicing obligation applies under German E-Rechnung rules: from 1 January 2025 all domestic B2B recipients must accept structured electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD), with mandatory e-invoice issuance for relevant turnovers from 1 January 2027 and all domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2028. Invoices must be transmitted electronically in a structured data format; there is no requirement for authority pre-clearance or real-time digital stamping.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates under the euro as its domestic currency with full capital and current account convertibility within the European Union and the European Economic Area. For non-EU transactions, convertibility is guaranteed under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (Foreign Trade and Payments Act) and the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance). Profit remittance to shareholders is freely permitted without prior authorization, provided that corporate law formalities, anti-money laundering checks, and tax withholding obligations (where applicable) are satisfied. No material de facto restrictions exist for bona fide commercial treasury operations; however, the Bundesbank requires statistical balance-of-payments reporting for cross-border payments in excess of the statutory de minimis thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export proceeds surrender rules exist under German law. Exporters are not required to repatriate foreign exchange, convert export proceeds into euro, or deposit receipts with a domestic bank. Foreign exchange proceeds may be retained, invested, or used for working capital purposes without prior approval. The only statutory obligation is the Bundesbank's external statistics reporting regime: cross-border receipt or payment transactions equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 generally must be reported individually, and outstanding foreign receivables or payables above the applicable reporting threshold must be declared. No minimum conversion ratio is imposed, and no central bank permission is required for offshore holding or disbursement of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) acting jointly with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking conduct and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action for foreign trade controls

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for regulated EU/EEA licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) passporting into Germany under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2). Such EMIs may lawfully operate with a BaFin-compliant agent model or EU passporting notification, and they offer competitive multi-currency corporate wallets and FX conversion. Feasibility rating: Low for offshore (non-EU) digital EMIs without a German or EU license. Under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), unauthorized e-money business by non-EU firms is a criminal or regulatory violation. Offshore EMIs cannot be used as the primary treasury clearing rail for inbound capital, export settlement, or statutory payment services; they may be used only through a licensed EU banking partner acting as the settlement bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 15.825%); trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at municipal rates of 14%–17%, yielding a combined effective rate of approximately 29.8%–32.5% depending on location

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% aggregate (25% dividend withholding tax under § 43 Abs. 1 EStG plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax). Reduced rates apply under DTAs and EU law: generally 0% for parent companies holding ≥10% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, 5% or 15% under most German DTAs, and up to 0% under certain treaties.

Section A – Location and object Municipality where the permanent establishment is situated, trade tax number, type of business, legal form and shareholding structure. If the GmbH has a permanent establishment in one or more German municipalities, trade tax returns must be filed for each PE location; non-resident GmbHs without a domestic PE are not subject to trade tax. Late filing surcharge up to EUR 25,000; default interest at 6% per annum; forced assessment; trade tax arrears can be recovered from corporate assets, and the managing director is personally liable in the event of wilful failure to remit within the applicable time limits (§ 69 AO).
Section B – Trade income (Gewerbeertrag) Starting point is the commercial profit from the corporate income tax return, adjusted for § 8 GewStG add-backs (e.g., 25% of financing costs, 25% of royalties for rights usage, 25% of rental payments for movable assets) and § 9 GewStG deductions (e.g., 1.2% of business property value, qualified dividends). Non-resident related-party financing or leasing structures may trigger add-backs; robust transfer pricing documentation and substance evidence are essential to avoid hidden dividend reclassification. Late filing surcharge up to EUR 25,000; default interest at 6% per annum; forced assessment; trade tax arrears can be recovered from corporate assets, and the managing director is personally liable in the event of wilful failure to remit within the applicable time limits (§ 69 AO).
Section C – Trade tax allowance A EUR 24,000 annual trade tax allowance for corporations; only the excess is subject to trade tax. The allowance is available to the resident corporation itself; non-resident shareholder status is irrelevant for the allowance computation. Late filing surcharge up to EUR 25,000; default interest at 6% per annum; forced assessment; trade tax arrears can be recovered from corporate assets, and the managing director is personally liable in the event of wilful failure to remit within the applicable time limits (§ 69 AO).
Section D – Tax measure Trade tax assessment base is multiplied by the federal tax rate of 3.5%, then by the local municipal collection multiplier (Hebesatz, typically 200%–490%) to calculate the trade tax liability. The municipal multiplier of the PE location applies; non-resident GmbHs must verify whether no PE exists before relying on treaty or domestic rules. Late filing surcharge up to EUR 25,000; default interest at 6% per annum; forced assessment; trade tax arrears can be recovered from corporate assets, and the managing director is personally liable in the event of wilful failure to remit within the applicable time limits (§ 69 AO).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany applies de minimis thresholds for the mandatory submission of contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation: cross-border transactions with related parties must be documented when annual goods transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000 or annual services transactions exceed EUR 500,000. In all cases, the tax authorities may informally require records below these thresholds when there are indications of non-arm's length pricing. Master File and Local File must be prepared in accordance with § 90(3) AO and the OECD TP Guidelines; CbC reporting applies to groups with annual consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) as seen in zero-tax jurisdictions. However, German anti-abuse and tax residence rules impose functional substance requirements: the GmbH must have a place of effective management in Germany, local executive decision-making, adequate staff, office infrastructure, and operational activities (e.g., sorting lines, IT systems, logistics contracts) to be respected as a taxable entity. Letter-box companies are disregarded under § 42 AO and § 50d(3) EStG, and treaty benefits may be denied absent economic substance. German CFC rules under AStG may reattribute passive income to German shareholders if substance tests are not met.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or monetary fine under § 370 AO; aggravated tax evasion in especially serious cases (e.g., insider-dimension fraud at scale) is punishable by imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years. The evaded tax is still due, plus default interest at 6% p.a., late surcharges, and possible seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer (managing director) is personally liable for criminal prosecution and may receive a prison sentence; civil liability under § 43 GmbHG for actions outside fiduciary duties; assets may be seized; travel bans and extradition may be imposed in cross-border cases; disqualification from serving as director for a period up to 5 years.
Non-payment / Non-remittance of Withholding and Payroll Taxes

Statutory sanction: Late payment interest 6% p.a.; enforcement proceedings; personal seizure of bank accounts; criminal penalties up to 5 years in prison for intentional non-payment (§ 370 AO); the company itself incurs liability plus punitive late surcharges up to EUR 25,000.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for withheld taxes under § 69 AO; criminal imprisonment; disqualification; civil damages to the GmbH for depletion of corporate assets; in severe cases, the court may impose travel bans and prohibit the director from leaving the country.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to the arm's length result; penalty of 5%–10% of the upward adjustment under § 162(4) AO when documentation is missing; interest at 6% p.a.; possible reclassification of excessive payments as constructive dividends leading to 26.375% withholding tax and potential denial of trade tax deductibility.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false or incomplete documentation face criminal liability for tax evasion; personal restitution for tax losses; renunciation of treaty relief; disqualification from management; travel restrictions when criminal investigations are pending.
Shell / Letter-Box Company and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities may disregard the GmbH as shareholder under § 42 AO / § 50d(3) EStG, deny treaty and Directive relief, apply anti-abuse regulations, reattribute profits to the ultimate foreign shareholder, and impose CFC tax. Penalties for tax evasion and interest charges apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for tax arrears if the token corporate substance causes non-payment; criminal prosecution for fraud or aiding tax evasion; asset forfeiture; travel bans; disqualification from holding directorships in Germany.
AML and Economic Sanctions Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of group turnover under § 56 GwG for severe AML deficiencies; criminal penalties for money laundering under § 261 StGB of up to 5 years imprisonment or fines; freezing orders and asset forfeiture; prohibition from engaging with financial service providers in the EEA.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate money laundering face personal criminal liability, imprisonment, seizure of private assets, travel bans, and disqualification from management; civil liability for damages caused to the company or third parties; personal liability for regulatory fines if acting with intent or gross negligence.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum statutory registered share capital is EUR 25,000 under §5(1) GmbHG. Before registration, at least one half of the capital—i.e., EUR 12,500—must be paid up in cash under §7(2) GmbHG; the amount must be held in a blocked escrow bank account (Anderkonto) in the name of the GmbH in formation, and the bank must issue a confirmation that the managing directors have unrestricted future disposal rights upon registration.

Funding & unlock path: Mandatory clearance comprises a fire safety concept and fire extinguisher/escape-route approval by the Building Supervision Authority (Bauordnungsamt) and the civil defense fire inspectorate; a structural load and occupancy certificate pursuant to the respective State Building Code (BauO); compliance with the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) including ventilation, lighting, sanitation and first aid; an occupational health and safety risk assessment under the ArbSchG; substance-specific assessments under the Hazardous Substances Ordinance (GefStoffV) for solvents and disinfectants; a clearance certificate from the industrial accident insurer (Berufsgenossenschaft) and a trade supervision inspection prior to commencing the production line.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum statutory registered share capital is EUR 25,000 under §5(1) GmbHG. Before registration, at least one half of the capital—i.e., EUR 12,500—must be paid up in cash under §7(2) GmbHG; the amount must be held in a blocked escrow bank account (Anderkonto) in the name of the GmbH in formation, and the bank must issue a confirmation that the managing directors have unrestricted future disposal rights upon registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) through the BZSt online services; the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) administers the German tax number (Steuernummer), corporate income tax, trade tax, and domestic VAT filings, generally through the ELSTER electronic tax platform.

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH must complete commercial formation, commercial-register registration, and business registration where applicable; submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt; provide the notarized articles, commercial-register extract, registered office, business activities, directors, beneficial owners, bank details, expected turnover, and VAT activity; receive the Steuernummer from the Finanzamt; and apply separately to the BZSt for the USt-IdNr. A foreign entity with German taxable supplies must identify the competent Finanzamt based on its German fixed establishment or, if none exists, the designated tax office for foreign businesses, submit the required registration questionnaire and documentary evidence, and obtain a German VAT ID where intra-Community transactions require it. The USt-IdNr. is distinct from the domestic Steuernummer and the tax identification number of an individual.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is generally subject to German corporate income tax and solidarity surcharge at entity level and to municipal trade tax; Germany has no US-style check-the-box election for a GmbH and no general election to treat a GmbH as fiscally transparent. Group taxation may be achieved through a corporate income tax and trade tax Organschaft if statutory financial-integration, profit-transfer-agreement, duration, and other requirements are satisfied. A VAT Organschaft may apply where financial, economic, and organizational integration exist; it is generally a legal-status determination rather than a freely available check-the-box election. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act implement ATAD II and can deny deductions, impose inclusion, or otherwise neutralize deduction/non-inclusion, double-deduction, and imported mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for ordinary taxable business activity: Germany does not provide a general turnover threshold below which a taxable entrepreneur is exempt from VAT registration. The small-business exemption under Section 19 of the German VAT Act may apply if prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory exclusions and conditions; this is an exemption regime, not a general registration threshold. For EU cross-border B2C distance sales and certain electronically supplied services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold may apply, with optional OSS registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the EU may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax recovery under Sections 4 No. 1(a) and 6 of the German VAT Act if the goods are transported or dispatched to a third country, the goods leave the EU customs territory, the supplier satisfies the documentary and accounting evidence requirements, and the transaction is correctly reported. The supplier must ordinarily verify the export through customs or approved alternative evidence and retain transport, delivery, order, and accounting records. Services are not automatically export zero-rated: B2B services generally follow the customer-location rule and may be outside German VAT where the customer is established outside Germany, while exceptions apply for real-estate, passenger transport, event admission, short-term vehicle hire, and other specified services. B2C services require separate place-of-supply analysis. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, business use, and compliance with the deduction rules; export exemption alone does not eliminate substantiation requirements.

  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order, and product description identifying the disposable dialysis hemofilter and applicable tariff classification
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or carrier delivery confirmation
  • Evidence that the goods were transported to and exited the EU customs territory, including exit confirmation where required
  • Proof of delivery or receipt by the third-country consignee and, where relevant, import customs documentation or destination-country customs evidence
  • Accounting records linking the export declaration, shipment, customer, invoice, and VAT treatment
  • For indirect exports, buyer or carrier documentation and evidence that the goods were exported within the legally permitted time and were not used in Germany before export

  • For intra-Community supplies, a valid customer VAT ID, required recapitulative statement, and proof of transport or dispatch under the German VAT Act and EU VAT rules

Germany does not generally operate real-time VAT invoice clearance or mandatory digital stamping. Since 1 January 2025, a domestic business recipient must be able to receive an electronic invoice for qualifying domestic B2B transactions. Issuers may use transitional rules: paper or other invoices may generally continue through 31 December 2026; issuers with prior-year turnover not exceeding EUR 800,000 may generally continue through 31 December 2027; from 1 January 2028, qualifying domestic B2B e-invoices must use a structured format compliant with the applicable German and EU requirements, principally EN 16931, including XRechnung or compliant ZUGFeRD formats. PDF invoices are not structured e-invoices. Federal B2G transactions generally require compliant electronic formats and transmission channels, with additional rules potentially applying at state or municipal level. Businesses must retain invoices and accounting records in accordance with German tax-record retention and audit-access rules.

Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds; the 0 value denotes not applicable, not an exemption from customs, VAT, anti-money-laundering, accounting, or sanctions controls. If export evidence is missing, contradictory, late, or unreliable, the Finanzamt may deny the VAT exemption, assess the applicable VAT retrospectively, deny or adjust related input-tax recovery, and charge interest under the German Fiscal Code. Incorrect VAT returns, invoices, or records may result in administrative fines, tax penalties, and, where intentional or grossly reckless, criminal tax-evasion liability under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code. Fraudulent export declarations may also trigger customs penalties or criminal liability under applicable EU and German customs law. Interest, repayment of improperly refunded VAT, audit adjustments, and correction of affected VAT returns may extend across the applicable assessment and limitation periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a Eurozone member with full and unrestricted currency convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no German exchange-control regime, no central-bank authorisation requirement, and no convertibility restriction on trade settlements, dividend remittances, loan repayments, equity injections, or any other cross-border payment by or to a German GmbH. Profit-remittance analysis: dividends paid by a German GmbH to a non-resident shareholder are freely transferable, but German dividend withholding tax of 26.375% (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) is imposed at source; applicable relief available under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% withholding for qualifying 10%+ holdings) or under the relevant German double-tax treaty (e.g., China, USA, Hong Kong conventions) through an application to the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt). The only practical hurdles to a cross-border treasury operation are not FX-control based: (i) Bundesbank statistical reporting under Section 67 AWV for each cross-border payment above EUR 12,500; (ii) compliance with EU sanctions, embargoes and asset-freeze regulations; (iii) German inward-investment screening under Sections 55ff. AWG for non-EU investors acquiring significant assets or critical-infrastructure shares in Germany; and (iv) bank-level KYC and economic-substance verification. None of these restrictions affects the ordinary import-export and intra-group funding patterns of a low-risk ISIC 3250 dialysis-consumables manufacturer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion quota, and no requirement to maintain export earnings in designated accounts. A German exporting GmbH may freely invoice, hold, net, convert, or re-lend its export receivables in any foreign currency. The sole mandatory administrative obligation is statistical reporting under Section 67 AWV: every inward payment of export proceeds received from a non-resident person exceeding EUR 12,500 per payment transaction (with aggregation for a series of economically linked payments of the same underlying claim) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank through its electronic reporting portal (Allgemeines Meldeportal) within seven days after the end of the calendar month in which the payment was credited. The report must identify the payer, the amount and currency, the AWV statistical purpose code (Geschäftsartenschlüssel), the counterparty country, and the cross-reference to the export contract or commercial invoice. Supporting documentation (customs ATLAS export declaration, bill of lading, invoice, bank credit advice) must be retained for Bundesbank verification. No conversion into euro or surrender to the Bundesbank or any authorised dealer is ever required.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany) together with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin); the German exchange-control and foreign-payment legal framework is set out in the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), administered by the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational multi-currency treasury accounts and international payment facilitation; Low for the statutory share-capital escrow account. EEA-licensed electronic-money institutions and digital banks (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Penta, Qonto) can validly hold working-capital accounts for a German GmbH by virtue of their home-state passport under the EU Second Electronic Money Directive and PSD2, subject to BaFin-recognised supervision. They are operationally viable for SWIFT-based supplier disbursements in USD/CNY and for near-mid-market FX conversion. Legal constraints: (i) a non-EEA offshore EMI without a valid EU passport may not lawfully maintain a payment account for a German resident company and must be rejected; (ii) the German GmbH statutory capital deposit (Stammeinlage) must be made into an account at the free disposal of the managing directors, and German notaries and commercial-register courts generally require a blocked-account confirmation (Sperrvermerk) issued by a German credit institution or a prudentially supervised EU bank; relying on an EMI-only structure creates a material objection risk at the Handelsregister registration stage; (iii) e-money float is not covered by the German statutory deposit-protection regime, so the treasury should maintain only modest operational balances at EMIs; (iv) the EMI must still apply full German GwG identification rules, including UBO verification. Recommended structure: keep primary banking at a tier-1 German commercial bank, layer EMIs as secondary settlement rails for FX-efficient small-ticket international payments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax, resulting in a combined federal rate of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) applies at a rate between 7% and 17% (average ~14%), yielding an aggregate effective rate of approximately 30-33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, resulting in an effective rate of 26.375%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return must be filed by 31 July of the year following the fiscal year-end. If filed by a tax advisor, the deadline is automatically extended to 28/29 February of the second year after the fiscal year-end (e.g., for FY 2024, deadline is 28 February 2026).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are mandatory if the group's annual consolidated turnover exceeds €100 million and the value of intercompany transactions exceeds €1 million for goods or €100,000 for services. For a GmbH manufacturing dialysis filters, documentation is required if cross-border transactions with related parties exceed these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the substance-over-form doctrine and anti-abuse rules (Section 42 AO) require that the GmbH has real economic substance, including premises, employees, and business activities. For a manufacturing entity, substance is evidenced by production facilities, R&D, and operational management in Germany. Lack of substance may lead to recharacterization of transactions and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine (up to €50,000 per offense). Additional penalty of up to 10% of the evaded tax (Section 398a AO).

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they fail to file accurate returns (Section 69 AO). Civil restitution, travel bans, and asset seizure are possible.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 5% of the adjustment amount (minimum €5,000) for missing documentation (Section 162(4) AO). Additional interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly approved incorrect pricing. In severe cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of additional taxes. Fines up to €25,000 under anti-abuse rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly operated a sham entity. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
AML / Compliance Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for serious violations (GwG). Criminal prosecution for money laundering (Section 261 StGB) with imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for AML breaches, including civil damages and criminal penalties. Travel bans and extradition may apply.

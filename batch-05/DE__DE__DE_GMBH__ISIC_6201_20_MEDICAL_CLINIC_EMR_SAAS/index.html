Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification and central tax matters; local Finanzamt for income/corporate tax administration; electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident shareholder forms a German GmbH by notarial deed, commercial register filing, and shareholder contribution of at least €25,000 (€12,500 paid-in). After registration, the GmbH applies to the local Finanzamt for a tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT; the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is separately obtained from BZSt via ELSTER using Form UVA and supporting registration documents.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident shareholder forms a German GmbH by notarial deed, commercial register filing, and shareholder contribution of at least €25,000 (€12,500 paid-in). After registration, the GmbH applies to the local Finanzamt for a tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT; the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is separately obtained from BZSt via ELSTER using Form UVA and supporting registration documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not apply check-the-box entity classification. A GmbH may elect fiscal unity (Organschaft) for VAT and corporate/trade tax purposes through a profit and loss transfer agreement with a controlling German parent, subject to financial integration and substance requirements. Hybrid mismatch elections under ATAD/German anti-hybrid rules are available only to the extent permitted under the German Income Tax Act and EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive implementations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€22,000 taxable turnover in the previous calendar year for the small business exemption; once prior-year turnover exceeds €22,000 and current-year projected turnover exceeds €50,000, VAT registration is mandatory. A GmbH is generally required to register for VAT regardless of small-business status when performing taxable supplies in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods to third countries under Section 6 UStG and intra-Community supplies under Sections 4 No. 1(b) and 6a UStG. For EMR SaaS services, zero-rating applies to B2B cross-border services where the recipient is an entrepreneur in another EU Member State (reverse charge) or to B2B services supplied to a recipient established in a third country, provided the recipient is an entrepreneur and the place of supply is outside Germany. The supplier must prove the recipient's business status and that the service was effectively rendered outside Germany.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / ATLAS AES)
  • Commercial Invoice with VAT zero-rating reference and service description
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical exports, or equivalent electronic receipt for SaaS delivery logs
  • Contract or service agreement identifying the foreign recipient and place of receipt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export payment

Germany mandates receipt of electronic invoices for domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2025, with the obligation to issue e-invoices phased in from 2027/2028 depending on business size. E-invoices must comply with EN 16931 (structured XML) and can be transmitted via Peppol, ZUGFeRD, or XInvoice. There is currently no real-time AI-based fiscal clearance, API clearance stamping, or digital stamping regime comparable to Latin American models; continuous transaction controls are planned under EU ViDA directives.

German tax authorities may deny zero-rating if documentation is incomplete, treating the supply as taxable at 19% VAT with late payment interest and penalties. In fraudulent export schemes, criminal liability arises under Section 370 AO with fines or imprisonment up to 5 years (10 years in aggravated cases), retrospective clawback of VAT refunds, and extension of the assessment period up to 10 years. For exempt intra-Community supplies, failure to provide valid VAT numbers and recapitulative statements can trigger immediate VAT reassessment and suspension of exemption.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility within the euro area and vis-à-vis third states. The euro is fully convertible; no exchange controls, prior licensing, or Central Bank approval gates apply to capital movements or profit remittances. Legal frictions are limited to (i) the GmbH capital-maintenance rule under §30 GmbHG prohibiting distributions that impair stated capital, (ii) statutory German dividend withholding at 26.375% (25% plus solidarity surcharge), reducible under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or applicable double-tax treaties, and (iii) Bundesbank statistical reporting. Non-resident shareholders may freely receive dividends, liquidation proceeds, and loan repayments subject only to tax compliance and the mandatory AWV reporting threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

German law imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory foreign-currency conversion ratio. Export receipts in EUR or foreign currency may be retained, converted, hedged, or held abroad at the exporter's sole discretion. The binding obligation is statistical: under AWV §67, every cross-border payment (including export proceeds) of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported electronically to the Deutsche Bundesbank within seven calendar days. Exporters holding foreign receivables or foreign bank balances must also file the monthly External Assets and External Debt status report to the Bundesbank where capital-linked balances exceed applicable reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) with co-regulatory supervision by BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority); the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV) is enforced jointly by the Bundesbank and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

MEDIUM feasibility for EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) passporting into Germany under PSD2 and the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG); LOW feasibility for offshore non-EEA digital EMIs, which require a full BaFin licence, a physical or agent presence, and German-language KYC documentation before holding client funds. Unlicensed acceptance of payment services for a German GmbH creates regulatory exposure under §125 ZAG. EMI balances are not protected bank deposits, so an EMI cannot serve as the capital-escrow account for GmbH formation; client funds must be segregated in a German or EEA credit institution. Stripe Billing and Bank Transfer remain permissible only as inbound payment-acceptance channels, not as the GmbH's treasury-holding vehicle.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the GmbH must be executed by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer to a German credit-institution account. The receiving bank must encode the transaction in its AWV reporting interface under the Bundesbank balance-of-payments category for foreign direct investment-capital contribution (Kapitalausstattung / Beteiligungserwerb) and must report the flow within seven days if it reaches the EUR 12,500 threshold. For a capital increase, a notarially recorded shareholders' resolution raising the Stammkapital is required; the funds must be credited to the designated account, and the bank must issue a capital-formation certificate (§37 GmbHG) confirming that the amount is at the free disposal of management before the Handelsregister filing. Verification packet: notarised capital-increase deed, updated Handelsregister excerpt, subscriber list, bank deposit confirmation, source-of-funds evidence from the foreign transferor, and proof of AWV statistical reporting. No FX conversion approval is required because Germany maintains full euro convertibility.Outbound trade settlements are cleared through four rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for euro payments within SEPA member states, including recurring supplier payments; (ii) SWIFT MT103 international remittance for USD or non-SEPA-country settlements via the tier-1 correspondent network of Deutsche Bank or Commerzbank; (iii) T2 (TARGET2) real-time gross settlement for large-value euro treasury payments; and (iv) Commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP600/eUCP for documentary trade, mainly when procuring medical hardware and infrastructure where supplier financing requires payment-security instruments. On the revenue side, recurring EMR subscription collections from German/EU clinics ride on SEPA Direct Debit (Core and B2B schemes) under the SDD Rulebook, complemented by Stripe Billing for card payments; all direct-debit mandates must be stored and auditable to satisfy both BaFin payment-services supervision and the bank's scheme-compliance review.OTC EUR/USD outright forward contracts (Devisentermingeschäft) executed under a German or English-law ISDA master agreement with a supervised German or EEA bank counterpartyNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or illiquid currencies where physical delivery is legally or practically unavailableCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) to hedge long-term non-euro asset or liability exposureEuropean-style FX options for premium-capped hedging of contingent USD/CAD/GBP cash flowsCross-currency swaps (FX swaps) for short-term liquidity matching between euro and foreign-currency accountsExchange-traded FX futures on Eurex or other recognised EU venues where liquid contracts match the exposure tenor

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File required if group revenue exceeds €5,000,000; Local File required if cross-border related-party transactions exceed €1,000,000 or if the entity is resident in a tax haven.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a separate ESR but applies general anti-abuse rules (Section 17 AO) and substance requirements for treaty recognition. A GmbH must demonstrate real economic activity (office, employees, decision-making) to avoid being treated as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years, fines up to €50,000, and additional penalty of 10% of evaded tax (Section 370 AO).

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for evaded taxes, face civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, penalty of up to 30% of adjusted income, and potential criminal charges for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties, and may face travel bans and criminal prosecution.
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover, license revocation, and criminal prosecution under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG).

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned up to 5 years for gross negligence or intentional AML breaches.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) - Federal Central Tax Office (VAT ID issuance, BZSt portal: www.bzst.de); local Finanzamt (municipal tax office) for Steuernummer and income taxes; national e-filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); customs authority: Generalzolldirektion for EORI and export clearances.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a German GmbH must first register the company in the German Commercial Register (Handelsregister). The local Finanzamt subsequently issues a tax number (Steuernummer) upon submission of the legalization questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER. VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is applied for separately from BZSt on Form USt 1A; EORI number is obtained from the Generalzolldirektion for customs transactions. The application requires attested constitutional documents, proof of commercial registration, and identification of directors; foreign founders can appoint a German resident authorised representative for service of process.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - A German GmbH (Gesellschaft mit beschränkter Haftung) is a corporation taxable as a separate legal entity at the national and municipal level, subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer: 15%), solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag: 5.5% of corporation tax), and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer at varying municipal rates, typical aggregate 22-30%). No pass-through or transparent tax treatment is available to a GmbH. Germany imposes unlimited tax liability on its resident corporations on worldwide income, with the tax domicile of a GmbH being its statutory seat and place of effective management. — Foreign entities establishing a German GmbH must first register the company in the German Commercial Register (Handelsregister). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A German GmbH (Gesellschaft mit beschränkter Haftung) is a corporation taxable as a separate legal entity at the national and municipal level, subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer: 15%), solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag: 5.5% of corporation tax), and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer at varying municipal rates, typical aggregate 22-30%). No pass-through or transparent tax treatment is available to a GmbH. Germany imposes unlimited tax liability on its resident corporations on worldwide income, with the tax domicile of a GmbH being its statutory seat and place of effective management.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a general check-the-box election for corporations. However, a group can elect an Organschaft (tax unity) for corporation tax and trade tax purposes subject to financial, economic, and organisational integration. A separate VAT Organschaft (Umsatzsteuer-Organschaft) may be elected for VAT grouping of a parent with its financially, economically, and organisationally integrated subsidiaries, with the parent as the single taxable person. Hybrid entity and hybrid mismatch elections are constrained by the German Anti-Treaty-Shopping Rule and the OECD BEPS implementation under the German Tax Haven Defence Act (Steueroasen-Abwehrgesetz); no unilateral check-the-box exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold applies to a German GmbH that is established and registered in Germany; a resident entrepreneur must register for VAT irrespective of turnover. An optional small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung, Section 19 UStG) is available only when prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000, but this status is generally unavailable or commercially inappropriate for a GmbH operating as a commercial TikTok MCN agency. For non-resident entrepreneurs making distance sales into Germany, a +EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance sales threshold applies before German VAT registration is required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to third countries are eligible for zero-rating under Section 4 No. 1(a) in conjunction with Section 6 UStG, provided the goods physically leave the EU and are proven by export documentation. Intra-community supplies to traders with valid VAT ID are zero-rated under Section 4 No. 1(b) and Section 6a UStG, contingent on submission of the EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung) and valid VAT ID evidence. Intangible creator agency services and cross-border B2B services are generally not zero-rated; if supplied to a business in another EU or third country, the place of supply is the customer's country and German VAT is shifted under reverse-charge rules. If the service is rendered to a private consumer in a third country, German VAT does not apply due to place-of-supply rules; supporting proof of business status and location must be retained.

From 1 January 2025, all Germany-established B2B suppliers must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (E-Rechnung) in formats compliant with EN 16931 (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD). Phase-in issuance obligations: from 1 January 2025, mandatory issuance for domestic B2B transactions only for taxpayers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000; from 1 January 2027, mandatory structured e-invoicing for all domestic B2B transactions. Germany does not operate real-time transaction clearance, no government pre-clearance, and no digital fiscal stamping. E-invoices must be transmitted in a structured format (e.g., PEPPOL network or direct EDI); PDF invoices will no longer qualify as e-invoices but can be issued as consensual paper/PDF for certain non-obligated cases.

Fraudulent or insufficiently documented export zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT assessment for the full 19% rate with late-payment interest at 0.5% per month from the original due date. The retention period for export documentation is 10 years. Intentional tax evasion under Section 370 of the Fiscal Code (Abgabenordnung) may lead to imprisonment up to 5 years (or 10 years in aggravated cases) and fines up to EUR 50,000 per offence. Negligent malpractices (Section 378 AO) carry procedural fines. Customs authorities and tax offices may apply Article 54 of the German General Administrative Regulation for audit scrutiny, and the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern can issue a retrospective correction to the VAT return and deny the zero-rated treatment for the period examined.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Germany imposes no exchange controls or restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, or capital. However, all cross-border payments and receipts exceeding €12,500 (or equivalent) must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical and balance-of-payments purposes. Profit remittance is subject to standard corporate income tax and withholding tax (if applicable) but no FX conversion hurdles exist. The GmbH may freely hold foreign currency accounts domestically or abroad, subject to reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not mandate any surrender or conversion of export proceeds into EUR. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency. However, under Section 67 AWV, all export receipts (including payments for services such as creator management fees) must be reported to the Bundesbank within seven days of receipt if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500. The report must include the transaction value, currency, and counterparty details. No mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy; the Bundesbank administers foreign exchange and cross-border payment reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: High for operational liquidity and cross-border disbursements, but Medium for primary corporate banking due to regulatory limitations. German-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as PPRO, Solarisbank, and fintechs like Wise (with German EMI license) and Revolut (with Lithuanian license but passporting into Germany) can provide multi-currency accounts, SWIFT/SEPA rails, and FX conversion. However, they are not full credit institutions and cannot offer credit facilities or escrow services. For a TikTok MCN agency handling creator payouts, EMIs are viable for outbound disbursements and inbound collections, but a tier-1 bank is recommended for capital escrow and high-volume settlement. Legal constraints: EMIs must comply with the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and are subject to safeguarding requirements for client funds. Cross-border EMI accounts (e.g., Wise Business) are permissible but may trigger additional reporting under AWV.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity contributions from foreign shareholders, the GmbH must receive funds via SWIFT MT103 with a clear purpose code. The standard purpose code for capital injection is 'P001' (Direct Investment) or 'P002' (Capital Increase) under the German balance of payments statistics. The bank will require a copy of the notarized capital increase resolution and the commercial register filing. For shareholder loans, use purpose code 'P003' (Loans) and provide a loan agreement. All inbound transfers must be reported to the Bundesbank if they exceed EUR 12,500. The routing should be direct from the shareholder's bank to the GmbH's account, avoiding third-party intermediaries to reduce AML scrutiny. Verification: The bank will match the transfer reference with the corporate documents and may request a certificate of non-resident status for withholding tax purposes.For creator payouts and operational expenses, the GmbH can use SWIFT MT103 for international wire transfers, SEPA Instant Credit for EU-based creators, and commercial letters of credit (LC) for high-value trade settlements if applicable. For TikTok creator payouts, the primary rail is the TikTok Creator Payout system, which typically uses local bank transfers or PayPal. The GmbH should maintain a dedicated operational account for these disbursements. For non-EU creators, SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'S001' (Services) or 'S002' (Royalties) is appropriate. For bulk payouts, consider using a fintech EMI like Wise Business to reduce FX costs and access local clearing rails. For trade settlements involving physical goods (if the MCN agency also sells merchandise), a commercial LC via a tier-1 bank is permissible. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: High for operational liquidity and cross-border disbursements, but Medium for primary corporate banking due to regulatory limitations. German-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as PPRO, Solarisbank, and fintechs like Wise (with German EMI license) and Revolut (with Lithuanian license but passporting into Germany) can provide multi-currency accounts, SWIFT/SEPA rails, and FX conversion. However, they are not full credit institutions and cannot offer credit facilities or escrow services. For a TikTok MCN agency handling creator payouts, EMIs are viable for outbound disbursements and inbound collections, but a tier-1 bank is recommended for capital escrow and high-volume settlement. Legal constraints: EMIs must comply with the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and are subject to safeguarding requirements for client funds. Cross-border EMI accounts (e.g., Wise Business) are permissible but may trigger additional reporting under AWV.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer; plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, producing 15.825% before trade tax; combined with municipal Gewerbesteuer, the effective corporate tax rate is typically 30%–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) on distributions to non-resident shareholders; relief to 0% is available under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the recipient parent holds at least 10% for 12 months; treaty rates commonly reduce withholding to 5% or 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: contemporaneous documentation is expected where aggregate annual controlled transactions with related parties exceed EUR 5,000,000; Master File: group consolidated revenue above EUR 100,000,000; Country-by-Country Report: consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a Caribbean-style ESR regime, but substantive requirements are mandatory: place of effective management under §10 AO, anti-treaty-shopping rules under §50d(3) EStG, general anti-abuse doctrine under §42 AO, and CFC rules under §§7–14 AStG. A German GmbH acting as a TikTok MCN agency must maintain real staff, office premises, management authority, contracts, bank accounts, and creator-management functions in Germany to preserve treaty benefits and avoid recharacterization as a shell entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years in aggravated cases) and/or criminal fines under §370 AO and the German Criminal Code; additional tax plus 1.8% per annum interest under §233a AO; confiscation and asset seizure.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are criminally liable as actual perpetrators; civil personal liability follows for unpaid tax caused by intentional or negligent breach of duty under §§34, 69 AO; arrest warrants, travel bans, restitution orders, and director disqualification may be imposed.
Failure to File / Late Payment

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty under §152 AO up to EUR 25,000; monthly late payment surcharge of 1% under §240 AO; estimated assessment under §162 AO; enforcement seizure of bank accounts and receivables.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for tax arrears under §69 AO; repeated non-filing may be prosecuted as aggravated tax evasion; travel restrictions and management bans can follow.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment under §1 AStG; documentation penalty under §162(4) AO of 5%–10% of adjusted income, minimum EUR 5,000, maximum EUR 1,000,000; interest on additional assessments; criminal tax evasion if intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign returns containing non-arm's-length transfer pricing risk personal criminal liability and civil damages for implementing transactions contrary to the GmbH's interests.
Treaty Shopping / Shell Entity Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief under §50d(3) EStG; income recharacterization under §42 AO and §1 AStG; CFC taxation under §§7–14 AStG; withholding at 26.375% for dividends or 15.825% for royalties without treaty reduction; fines and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors of artificial entities face personal liability for misappropriated tax amounts, criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion, restitution orders, and travel bans where flight risk is established.
AML / Money Laundering in Creator Payout Rails

Statutory sanction: Fines under the German AML Act (GwG) up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover in serious cases; confiscation of proceeds; money-laundering under §261 StGB punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years where proceeds derive from tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors directing such payment flows may be personally prosecuted; asset freezes, travel bans, and restrictions on managing legal entities may be imposed; the corporate veil is pierced where the GmbH is used as a façade for personal enrichment.

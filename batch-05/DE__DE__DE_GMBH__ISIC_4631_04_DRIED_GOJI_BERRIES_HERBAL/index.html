Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.); the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for the domestic tax number, corporate tax registration, VAT registration, and tax returns. Electronic registration and filings are generally administered through the ELSTER portal.

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH applies for tax registration with its competent Finanzamt after incorporation and commencement of business. The company submits the electronic questionnaire for tax registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER, including constitutional documents, registered office, business activities, directors, bank details, expected turnover, VAT liability, and related-party information. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer). The company separately applies to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer or USt-IdNr.), normally through the BZSt online application process or through the tax registration process. A foreign entity without a German establishment applies to the designated German tax office for non-established businesses and may require a German VAT registration where it makes taxable German supplies. A valid foreign VAT number must be verified for intra-Community transactions using the BZSt validation system and, where relevant, the EU VIES system. Registration does not replace customs EORI registration, which is separately required for import and export customs operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is generally subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and trade tax at entity level; Germany has no general US-style check-the-box election for a GmbH. Eligible partnerships may elect corporate taxation under Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act, but this election is generally unavailable to a GmbH. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Section 4k of the Income Tax Act and related provisions implement ATAD restrictions; they are statutory limitation rules rather than elective classification regimes. A GmbH may elect the small-business VAT regime under Section 19 of the VAT Act if statutory turnover conditions are satisfied, subject to the applicable legal consequences and waiver rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general German VAT registration threshold for taxable businesses; taxable supplies may require registration from the commencement of the relevant taxable activity. The domestic small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG generally applies where German turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year, subject to statutory conditions. The exemption does not apply to certain reverse-charge, import VAT, or intra-Community acquisition obligations and does not generally permit input VAT recovery. Foodstuffs may qualify for the reduced 7% rate, but the rate for dried goji berries, herbal botanicals, preparations, and mixed products depends on their precise tariff, product, packaging, and food-law classification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input tax recovery under Sections 4 and 6 UStG where the goods are physically transported or dispatched to a third country, the exporter or customer meets the statutory conditions, and the exporter proves both the export movement and the commercial transaction through prescribed records. Germany legally treats this as an export exemption rather than a domestic 0% taxable-rate category. Intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered customer in another EU Member State are generally VAT-exempt with input tax recovery under Section 4 No. 1(b) and Section 6a UStG, provided transport, customer VAT identification, recapitulative statement, and accounting requirements are met. Services are not automatically export zero-rated; their place of supply must be determined under the German and EU VAT place-of-supply rules. Business-to-business services may be outside German VAT where the customer is established abroad, while special rules apply to services connected with immovable property, events, transport, digital services, and other specified categories.

Germany does not currently operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or digital tax-stamping system. From 1 January 2025, German businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions, subject to statutory exceptions. The issuing obligation for domestic B2B e-invoices is phased in: paper and other permissible invoices remain available during transition periods, with the principal transition generally extending through 2026 and, for businesses whose preceding-year turnover did not exceed EUR 800,000, through 2027. From 1 January 2028, the domestic B2B e-invoice requirement is generally intended to apply without the principal transition relief. Compliant invoices must use an EU-standard structured format based on EN 16931, including formats such as XRechnung or compliant hybrid ZUGFeRD invoices. There is no universal German requirement for prior API clearance by the Finanzamt, but electronic records, invoice data, and accounting systems must satisfy the German principles for proper electronic record keeping (GoBD).

Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption depends primarily on proof of the transaction and movement of goods, not on receipt of foreign currency within a prescribed period. If the exporter cannot substantiate the export or intra-Community supply, the Finanzamt may deny the exemption retrospectively and assess German VAT, normally at the applicable rate, together with interest under Section 233a of the Fiscal Code and any applicable late-payment charges. Incorrect VAT refunds may be reclaimed, and input tax deductions may be denied. Intentional false declarations, fabricated transport evidence, or fraudulent VAT refund claims may constitute tax evasion under Section 370 of the Fiscal Code, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine, with aggravated cases punishable by imprisonment of up to ten years. Negligent tax reduction may trigger penalties under Section 378 of the Fiscal Code. Customs violations may generate separate administrative or criminal sanctions, recovery of customs duties, and loss of customs simplifications. Businesses must retain VAT invoices, customs records, transport evidence, accounting records, and related documentation for the applicable German retention periods, generally eight years for invoices and ten years for other tax-relevant accounting records under current rules, subject to statutory exceptions and transitional provisions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full Euro convertibility on both current and capital account. The capital account is completely liberalised under Art. 63 TFEU, the OECD Codes of Liberalisation and IMF Article VIII status; no prior approval, exchange control, or quantitative restriction applies to cross-border investment, trade settlement, or profit repatriation for a GmbH. The only practical hurdles are administrative and fiscal: (i) every cross-border payment above EUR 12,500 must be reported in the Bundesbank balance-of-payments statistics (the bank reports automatically where the payment instruction is identifiable, otherwise the corporate itself reports under AWV Sec. 67); (ii) outbound dividend distributions are subject to German capital-yield tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% aggregate) unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable double-tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) for a qualifying 10% or more direct holding held for at least 12 months; and (iii) the GmbH and its non-resident shareholders must comply with CRS and FATCA notification procedures. These steps do not impede convertibility or transferability of funds in any way.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. German law imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory repatriation deadline, no conversion quota, and no restriction on maintaining foreign-currency balances. The GmbH may freely retain USD, CNH, CNY or other currency proceeds in accounts at German or EU-supervised banks and may apply such balances directly to supplier payments. The sole obligation is statistical: exports of goods to non-EU states are captured through the ATLAS customs declaration (EXTRASTAT), and the balance-of-payments statistics record receivables and cross-border payments at the EUR 12,500 threshold; all underlying invoices, bills of lading, contracts and payment records must be retained for at least six years for Bundesbank audit purposes under AWV Sec. 70.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank), acting jointly with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and payment supervision, within the foreign trade and payments control framework of the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% corporate income tax, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a federal income-tax burden of 15.825%; municipal trade tax generally increases the combined burden to approximately 23% to 33%, depending on the municipality's assessment rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital-yield withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective rate of 26.375%. Domestic exemption or reduction may apply for qualifying corporate recipients, including eligible EU parent companies, subject to statutory minimum shareholding, holding, beneficial-ownership and anti-abuse conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer without a tax adviser, generally seven months after fiscal year-end, normally 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser prepares the return, the general statutory deadline is ordinarily the last day of February of the second subsequent year, subject to statutory exceptions, administrative extensions and special rules. A non-calendar fiscal year is due seven months after its fiscal year-end, subject to the adviser extension rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: There is no general monetary threshold that eliminates the German requirement to document cross-border related-party transactions. German taxpayers must maintain transaction-based records under the Foreign Tax Act and Transfer Pricing Documentation Rules. Simplified documentation may be available for smaller transactions. For extraordinary transactions, documentation is generally expected where annual supplies exceed EUR 5,000,000 or other services exceed EUR 500,000. A Master File is generally required for German entities belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 100,000,000 in the preceding fiscal year. Documentation must generally be produced within 60 days of a request; extraordinary-transaction documentation is generally due within 30 days.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone regime formally titled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Nevertheless, substance is material under the German anti-abuse rule, treaty beneficial-ownership analysis, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive anti-abuse rule, controlled foreign company rules, transfer pricing, permanent-establishment analysis, hybrid-mismatch rules and general corporate tax doctrine. A German GmbH should maintain genuine decision-making, adequate personnel or outsourced operational capacity, commercial premises or demonstrable operating infrastructure, independent risk control, proper books, bank activity, inventory and logistics evidence, and real business functions consistent with its wholesale botanical and food-product activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion may result in imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine. Particularly serious cases may result in imprisonment from six months to ten years. Assessed tax, interest, evaded tax recovery and criminal-proceeds consequences apply separately.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal prosecution, personal tax liability for culpable breach of tax duties, civil damages to the GmbH and insolvency-estate liability. Criminal liability is not automatically created by the GmbH's tax debt, but personal liability arises where statutory duties are breached or the director participates in the offence.
Reckless tax reduction and negligent tax compliance

Statutory sanction: Reckless tax reduction is generally an administrative offence punishable by a fine of up to EUR 50,000, in addition to reassessment, interest and recovery of the tax shortfall.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal civil liability for negligent breach of tax-management duties and may be liable under German tax-law provisions where taxes are not assessed or paid due to culpable breach.
VAT carousel fraud and false input-tax claims

Statutory sanction: VAT evasion can trigger the criminal sanctions applicable to tax evasion, repayment of VAT, interest, denial of input tax, penalties and possible seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally prosecuted where they knew or recklessly ignored fraud indicators. Civil and insolvency claims may include unpaid VAT, tax damages and costs of enforcement.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: The tax office may make income adjustments, impose late or non-compliance penalties for inadequate documentation, deny deductions, assess interest and apply withholding-tax consequences. A documentation penalty can generally range from 5% to 10% of the income adjustment, with statutory minimums and maximums, where the documentation is missing or unusable.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for knowingly approving non-arm's-length arrangements, especially where they cause tax losses, insolvency or unlawful distributions. Criminal exposure arises if the conduct is intentional tax evasion.
Improper profit distribution and hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: The benefit may be reclassified as a hidden profit distribution, denied as a deduction, included in the shareholder's taxable income and subjected to dividend withholding tax plus solidarity surcharge, with interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable to restore unlawful payments to the GmbH under capital-maintenance rules and may face shareholder, insolvency-administrator and tax claims. Criminal liability may arise for breach of fiduciary duties, insolvency offences or tax evasion.
Insolvency and unlawful payment after insolvency

Statutory sanction: Delayed insolvency filing, fraudulent insolvency and unlawful depletion of assets may trigger criminal fines or imprisonment, together with avoidance claims, repayment orders, damages and disqualification consequences.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for payments made after insolvency, unpaid taxes and social-security contributions, subject to statutory conditions. Travel restrictions may be ordered in individual enforcement or criminal proceedings but are not an automatic consequence of insolvency.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial-ownership violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines can be substantial and depend on the applicable AML duty, entity size and seriousness. Criminal money-laundering offences can result in imprisonment or fines, with aggravated cases carrying increased imprisonment exposure. Suspicious transactions may be frozen or reported to the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined or prosecuted for intentional participation or culpable supervisory failure. Beneficial-ownership reporting failures may also create separate sanctions and reputational, banking and licensing consequences.
Customs, import VAT and food-product regulatory non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Customs duties, import VAT, interest, administrative fines, seizure, destruction or recall of goods and loss of customs simplifications may apply. Intentional customs or tax fraud may also create criminal liability.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for culpable customs declarations, product-safety failures and tax losses. Criminal liability depends on knowledge, participation and supervisory breach; personal travel restrictions are case-specific and require a competent authority order.
Shell-company and treaty-shopping arrangement

Statutory sanction: Treaty or statutory relief may be denied, withholding tax may be reassessed, penalties and interest may apply, and transactions may be recharacterised under German anti-abuse rules. Intentional false statements may constitute tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for tax losses and unlawful distributions and criminal liability where they knowingly implement fraudulent treaty-shopping or false-substance documentation. A travel ban is not automatic and requires a specific judicial or administrative basis.

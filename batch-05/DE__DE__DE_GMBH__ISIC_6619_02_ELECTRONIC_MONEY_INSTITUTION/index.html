Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authority is the local Finanzamt for corporate tax registration and issuance of the German tax number (Steuernummer). The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues the German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) through its online services. Corporate tax registration is initiated electronically through ELSTER using the tax registration questionnaire for corporations.

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH must submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the competent local Finanzamt after incorporation and commencement of business. The filing normally includes the commercial-register extract, articles of association, registered office, directors, shareholders, business activities, bank details, expected turnover and profits, permanent establishments, and beneficial-owner information where requested. The Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer. The GmbH or its authorized tax adviser must separately apply to the BZSt for the USt-IdNr.; the application requires the legal name, registered address, legal form, commercial-register details, Steuernummer where available, and responsible tax office. Non-resident entities carrying on taxable German activities register with the Finanzamt designated for foreign businesses and apply for a German tax number and, where applicable, a USt-IdNr. Foreign entities supplying electronically supplied services or intra-EU distance sales may use the One Stop Shop where eligible, but OSS does not replace domestic corporate income-tax registration when a German taxable presence exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is generally subject to German corporation tax as a separate legal taxpayer and has no German equivalent of a U.S. check-the-box election. A GmbH may not elect partnership pass-through treatment under the partnership option in section 1a of the German Corporation Tax Act, which is available to qualifying partnerships and not to capital companies. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the ATAD framework and can deny deductions, impose income inclusions, or restrict treaty relief; they are not elective classification mechanisms. A GmbH may make specific elections under German tax law, including certain accounting, depreciation, group-taxation, and restructuring elections, but these do not convert it into a pass-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is generally no turnover threshold below which a German-established GmbH is exempt from VAT registration when it conducts taxable supplies; registration and VAT compliance generally begin with taxable business activity. The small-business exemption under section 19 of the German VAT Act may apply where total domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year, subject to statutory conditions. These are exemption thresholds, not a general VAT registration threshold. For qualifying EU cross-border B2C distance sales and certain electronic services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold may apply before destination-country VAT or OSS obligations arise.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the EU may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax recovery under the German export-supply rules if the goods are transported or dispatched to the non-EU destination and the supplier can substantiate both the export and the foreign destination through prescribed records and evidence. Intra-EU supplies may qualify for VAT exemption where the customer is a taxable person or qualifying legal person in another EU Member State, the goods move to that Member State, and the supplier retains a valid customer VAT number and transport evidence. Services are not automatically zero-rated because the customer is offshore. The place-of-supply rules determine whether German VAT is chargeable; B2B services generally follow the customer-location rule, while services linked to German establishments, immovable property, passenger transport, admission, and other special categories follow specific rules. Financial and payment services may be VAT-exempt under statutory exemptions, including qualifying payment and transfer transactions, but electronic-money, wallet, reserve-management, platform, technology, and administrative services require transaction-specific analysis and may be taxable.

  • Commercial invoice, contract, packing list, and evidence identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, destination, and delivery terms
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, consignment note, courier record, or other transport document evidencing movement outside the EU

Germany does not generally operate a real-time VAT invoice-clearance or digital-stamping system. From 1 January 2025, a domestic German business must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions. Mandatory issuance of structured e-invoices is phased in: generally from 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year domestic turnover exceeding EUR 800,000, and from 1 January 2028 for other businesses, subject to statutory transitional rules. Permitted structured formats include EN 16931-compliant XRechnung and compliant hybrid formats such as ZUGFeRD. EDI procedures may continue where statutory requirements are met. Electronic invoices must preserve authenticity, integrity, readability, and auditability; transmission may use ordinary electronic channels, EDI, or networks such as PEPPOL. B2C invoices and many exempt or low-value transactions remain subject to separate exceptions.

Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption depends on substantive eligibility and documentary substantiation, not on repatriation within 180 days. If zero-rating is incorrectly claimed, the supplier may owe the unpaid VAT, late-payment interest under the German Fiscal Code, and potentially interest or related assessments from the original tax period. Input VAT refunds may be denied or clawed back where the underlying supplies, invoices, customs evidence, or business purpose are deficient. Intentional or reckless false statements, manipulated export evidence, or concealed taxable supplies may trigger tax-offence or administrative-offence proceedings, including criminal tax evasion penalties, monetary penalties, imprisonment in serious cases, and recovery of the tax loss. Customs violations may separately result in customs debt, administrative penalties, and criminal liability. The tax authorities may conduct VAT audits, request transaction-level transport and customs evidence, apply estimation where records are inadequate, and impose record-retention and cooperation sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany uses the euro and has no general exchange controls restricting current-account or capital-account convertibility. Cross-border transfers are generally permitted, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, tax rules, payment-service regulation, and AWV statistical reporting where applicable. Dividend, interest, and principal remittances are not subject to routine foreign-exchange approval, but must be supported by valid corporate approvals, tax treatment, and bank documentation. A German EMI must separately comply with ZAG safeguarding requirements; unrestricted convertibility does not permit use of safeguarded customer funds for corporate liquidity or shareholder distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no general export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio or statutory deadline for converting export receipts into euros. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency, subject to contractual, tax, sanctions, AML, and applicable reporting obligations. Under the AWV, residents generally report cross-border payments exceeding the applicable reporting threshold to the Bundesbank; the threshold is currently EUR 50,000, subject to statutory exemptions and reporting instructions. Reporting is statistical, not a foreign-exchange approval or surrender regime. The entity should verify the current threshold, exemptions, and reporting category for each transaction with the Bundesbank or its reporting adviser.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany’s central bank and administers external-payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) is the principal licensing and prudential supervisor for German electronic money institutions under the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG). The European Central Bank also has relevant supervisory responsibilities within the Single Supervisory Mechanism where applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary corporate payment services, but low as a substitute for core German banking and safeguarding arrangements. An EEA-authorized payment institution or electronic money institution may provide services in Germany only within its authorization and applicable passporting arrangements. An offshore or non-EEA digital EMI cannot rely on an EU passport and does not replace a German or EEA credit institution for services requiring that status. The German GmbH must obtain BaFin authorization before conducting regulated e-money or payment services in Germany, unless a valid statutory exemption applies. Customer funds must be safeguarded under ZAG; an ordinary fintech wallet or omnibus account is not automatically a compliant safeguarding arrangement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 10,000,000 for Local File preparation if related-party cross-border transactions exceed EUR 1,500,000 or turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000; Master File required when group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000; CbC report mandatory if group revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000; documentation must be submitted within 60 days of a tax audit request for ordinary transactions and 30 days for extraordinary transactions

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have standalone ESR legislation (like UAE); however, economic substance is required under § 50d Abs 3 EStG to claim treaty benefits and under the ATAD implementation (§ 4j EStG, § 8b KStG restrictions); additionally, BaFin requires inherent substance for licensed EMI operations including local compliance officers, risk management, and decision-making functions

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years under § 370 AO; fines; confiscation of assets; in very severe cases 6 months to 10 years imprisonment; automatic interest accrual; unlimited loss of tax evasion benefits

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors personally liable; civil restitution orders; travel bans under § 132 AO; international arrest warrants in extreme cases; disqualification from serving as managing director; imprisonment for repeated or large-scale evasion
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under § 50d Abs 3 EStG; reclassification of income to the foreign principal; CFC rules under §§ 7–13 AStG; the tax office may disregard the German entity and tax the foreign shareholder directly; administrative fines; license revocation by BaFin if EMI licensed

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes if the company's lack of substance is deemed a sham; piercing the corporate veil possible; criminal prosecution for abuse of legal forms; personal pecuniary liability for restitution and penalties
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty under § 162 AO: up to EUR 1,000,000 (minimum EUR 10,000 / 5% of adjusted profit); estimation of income with an adverse 10% margin; reversal of burden of proof; late-filing penalties for CbC non-reporting

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the tax return may become personally liable for penalties if they knew or should have known of documentation deficiencies; in cases of intentional concealment, added criminal liability as accomplices to tax evasion
Dividend WHT Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Personal liability of the GmbH for the unwithheld tax under § 44 Abs 5 EStG; late payment surcharges; penalties up to 10% of withheld amount per § 50a Abs 5 EStG; interest on past due amounts; criminal investigation for tax evasion of the recipient

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable to the tax office for uncollected withholding tax under § 69 AO; personal liability is not dischargeable by insolvency; travel restrictions may apply; criminal charges for negligent tax evasion under § 378 AO if the failure is due to gross negligence
AML / Money Laundering & Unlicensed EMI Activity

Statutory sanction: BaFin fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of global turnover; criminal sanctions under § 58 ZAG up to 5 years imprisonment or fines; license revocation; prohibition of business conduct; asset freezing; confiscation of criminal proceeds

Director personal liability: Directors subject to criminal prosecution for unauthorized payment services; travel bans; extradition treaties; personal criminal fines; personal confiscation orders; disqualification from acting as director for up to 5 years; civil liability to affected customers; personal liability for regulatory fines if the company becomes insolvent through the violation

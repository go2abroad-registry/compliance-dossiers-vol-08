Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Registered capital (Stammkapital) must be at least EUR 25,000 (section 5(1) GmbHG). Cash contributions must be paid in before filing for registry entry, in the amount of at least EUR 12,500 (i.e., 50% of the minimum nominal capital); if the articles set a higher nominal capital, at least 25% of each cash contribution, and never less than EUR 12,500 in aggregate, must be paid. The capital must be at the free disposal of the managing directors and must be locked in a blocked corporate account in the name of the GmbH in Gruendung (GmbH i.G.); the bank issues a Vorbescheinigung (capital payment certificate). Contributions in kind (Sacheinlagen) must be covered fully in the articles and substantiated to the registry.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: final acceptance by the municipal fire protection inspectorate verifying adequate escape widths, smoke detectors, fire extinguishers, fire-rated ceilings and extraction duct dampers. Building clearance: structural load certificate for installed kitchen equipment and mezzanines, as-built plan approved by the Bauaufsichtsbehoerde. Occupational clearance: workplace risk assessment documentation demonstrating compliance of ventilation system air exchange rates, hand-washing facilities, electrical installations, and personal protective equipment issued to kitchen and delivery staff.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) - responsible for issuing the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer), EU-level tax cooperation, clearance of foreign VAT refunds and DAC7 platform-data administration; locally competent revenue office (Finanzamt) designated by the GmbH registered seat - responsible for the Steuernummer, corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), wage tax and VAT advance/annual assessments; ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung, www.elster.de) is the mandatory statutory e-filing portal for all tax returns; Deutsche Bundesbank receives mandatory cross-border payment statistics reports (AWV Zahlungsverkehrsstatistik) used by auditors to reconcile export proceeds.

E-government filing portal

1) Notarize the articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag or Musterprotokoll) with minimum statutory share capital of EUR 25,000 (minimum initial cash contribution EUR 12,500; higher if in-kind contributions are agreed). 2) File the Gewerbeanmeldung (trade registration) at the local Gewerbeamt, which is a mandatory statutory license precondition for ghost-kitchen and virtual-brand food operations (ISIC 5610 / NAICS 722513). 3) File for entry in the commercial register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht. 4) Within 4 weeks of business commencement, submit the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER, disclosing shareholders, managing directors, legal form, business activity codes, projected turnover, estimated VAT and profit positions. 5) The local Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (10-11 digit) within approximately 2-8 weeks. 6) Apply to BZSt for the USt-IdNr. (format DE + 9 digits) needed for intra-EU transactions and inbound EU B2B reverse-charge invoicing. 7) Register managing directors for wage tax (Lohnsteuer) if payroll is maintained and obtain an employer number. A non-resident shareholder faces dividend withholding tax at 26.375% (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), reducible under applicable double taxation treaties or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive subject to anti-hybrid and substance verification rules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: a German GmbH is a resident capital company subject to unlimited tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht, Sec. 1(1) KStG) on worldwide income; it is not a pass-through entity, no check-the-box classification is recognized by German law, the GmbH is itself the taxpayer for corporate income tax (15%), solidarity surcharge (effectively 0.825%) and trade tax (approximately 11.9%-17.2% depending on municipal Hebesatz); Germany applies no special tax-exempt free-zone regime, and the ghost-kitchen business is fully within the normal taxable scope.

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box election; the GmbH is statutorily taxable at entity level. Available elections relevant to a multi-brand delivery group include: (a) Organschaft (tax and VAT grouping) under Sec. 14-17 KStG and Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG - requires financial integration plus a domination and profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) registered in the commercial register and, for VAT, organizational and economic integration; (b) the small-entrepreneur scheme (Sec. 19 UStG) - practically unavailable to a commercially operating GmbH and incompatible with input-VAT recovery on a capex-heavy kitchen build-out; (c) Sec. 13b UStG reverse-charge treatment for specified incoming services and construction supplies; (d) option to tax real estate under Sec. 9 UStG on leased premises to preserve input-VAT recovery; (e) anti-hybrid and ATAD II alignment elections under Sec. 4k EStG for cross-border intra-group financing or franchise structures; (f) VAT-liability shifting and deemed-supplier determinations when marketplace platforms (UberEats, DoorDash) act as principals under Sec. 3(3a) UStG, requiring intercompany agreement review before platform go-live.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Resident GmbH: no turnover threshold - VAT registration and filing obligations commence from the first taxable euro (Sec. 13-18 UStG); ghost-kitchen supplies of prepared food to German consumers are taxable at 7% (reduced rate for food) while beverages are taxable at 19%, with the place of supply deemed at the kitchen's fixed establishment; the Sec. 19 UStG small-entrepreneur exemption (EUR 25,000 prior-year / EUR 100,000 expected current-year turnover from 2025) is not available as a practical matter for a commercially organized delivered-meal business because input VAT recovery is mandatory at inception; non-established EU restaurant/digital suppliers must register without threshold under Sec. 18(4a) UStG; EU intra-Community distance-selling suppliers of goods may use the OSS up to an EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold under Sec. 3c UStG before German registration is required; marketplace-intermediated deliveries may trigger deemed-supplier obligations of the platform under Sec. 3(3a) UStG.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) is available only for (i) physical exports out of the EU to third countries (Ausfuhrlieferung, Sec. 6 UStG) when prepared meals, meal kits or packaged goods leave the customs territory and the seller retains legally valid export proof per Sec. 8-9 UStDV; (ii) intra-Community supplies (innergemeinschaftliche Lieferung, Sec. 6a UStG) to a non-resident EU customer holding a valid VAT ID, with cross-border transport evidence and inclusion in the recapitulative VAT listing (Zusammenfassende Meldung / ZM); and (iii) limited offshore-support services for the export of goods (Sec. 8 UStG), such as freight and handling. Pure domestic meal delivery in Germany - the dominant ghost-kitchen scenario - is a domestic supply of goods taxed at the place of the kitchen's fixed establishment and is never eligible for zero-rating; food components attract 7% while beverages attract 19%. Cross-border licensing of virtual-brand formats or consulting services to foreign B2B customers falls outside German VAT by virtue of Sec. 3a UStG (place of supply at the customer's jurisdiction, reverse charge), not as a German 0% supply; the GmbH must evidence the recipient's business status (valid VAT ID, contract, substance) to avoid German VAT exposure and to allow voluntary VAT registration/refund procedures where applicable.

  • Customs export declaration: German ATLAS electronic export declaration / Ausfuhrbegleitdokument (ABD) and customs-confirmed exit proof (electronic 'Ausgangsbestätigung') per Sec. 8-9 UStDV
  • Transport documentation: Bill of Lading (Konnossement) or Sea Waybill, CMR waybill or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing dispatch from the German place of business to the third-country or EU consignee
  • Commercial invoice set: export commercial invoice, packing list and proforma/contract establishing the true seller-buyer relationship and Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank credit confirmation (SWIFT MT103 or account statement) evidencing settlement of the export claim in the entity's German bank account
  • EU-specific evidence: validated customer VAT ID (BZSt confirmation), delivery/signature or CMR completion proof, ZM declaration and Intrastat filing where the EUR thresholds apply

From 1 January 2025, every German B2B supplier must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung / EN 16931-compliant XML or ZUGFeRD 2.x / Factur-X hybrid PDF+XML). E-invoice issuance is mandatory from 1 January 2027 for suppliers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all other B2B suppliers, with transitional tolerance for EDI/paper until 2028/2029. Germany operates no real-time tax-authority clearance portal, no digital stamping and no transactional API reporting; fiscal control is enforced through mandatory electronic VAT advance returns (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldungen) and annual VAT returns via ELSTER, combined with GoBD-compliant retention of digital records for 10 years. Monthly advance returns apply when the net VAT burden exceeds EUR 7,500; below that, quarterly filings are allowed. B2C restaurant and cash-register activities at the ghost-kitchen site require a certified electronic cash register with a certified Technical Security Element (TSE) under the Cash Register Security Regulation (KassenSichV); delivery-platform payout reports, order data and terminal receipts must be archived and reconciled with GER revenue accounts to survive VAT audit.

German tax auditors strictly validate 0% claims. An invoice issued at 0% without the complete Sec. 8-9 UStDV export proof is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply and reassessed at 19% (or 7%/19% food-beverage split for meal components), with retrospective VAT clawback covering the standard 4-year assessment period, extended to 10 years in cases of tax evasion. Under-assessment interest accrues at 0.15% per month (1.8% per annum, Sec. 233a AO) and late-payment surcharges at 1% per month up to a 10% cap (Sec. 240 AO); late-filing and record-keeping penalties reach EUR 25,000 (Sec. 152 AO). Negligent understatements are fined up to EUR 50,000 (Sec. 378 AO); intentional VAT evasion (Sec. 370 AO) is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine, escalating to 6 months to 10 years for aggravated or gang-based carousel/MTIC fraud. Invoices wrongly showing VAT where export status fails create an additional VAT liability to the issuer under Sec. 14c UStG. Although German law imposes no statutory 180-day exchange-control deadline, the 180-day payment trace is the established audit benchmark: export proceeds unrepatriated beyond 180 days without documented renegotiation rebut the export proof and trigger recharacterization; monthly AWV cross-border payment reports to Deutsche Bundesbank must reconcile with sales ledger and banking records. DAC7 platform-reporting rules now oblige UberEats and DoorDash to transmit seller revenue data to tax authorities, and ghost-kitchen payout records are routinely cross-matched against declared food-service turnover, with estimation-based assessments and personal liability notices against managing directors (Sec. 34, 69, 71 AO) where records are absent.

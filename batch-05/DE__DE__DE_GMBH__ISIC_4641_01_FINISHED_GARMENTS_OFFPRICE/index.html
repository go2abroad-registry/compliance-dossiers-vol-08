UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Public Registry Disclosure

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Anti-Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz, GwG), as amended by the Transparency Register and Financial Information Act (TraFinG, 2021); § 3 GwG (beneficial owner definition); §§ 18-28 GwG (Transparency Register); § 20 GwG (UBO notification duty of legal representatives); § 10 GwG (customer due diligence); § 14 GwG (simplified due diligence); § 15(2) GwG (PEP rules); § 23 GwG (legitimate-interest access); § 43(1) GwG (fines). UBO look-through per § 3(2) GwG: an intermediate entity is pierced until a natural person exercising ultimate control through 25% or more of shares, voting rights, or capital is identified; where no such natural person can be found, the senior managing official is registered as UBO. On incorporation the German notary transmits founder data to the Transparency Register ex officio; management must keep the register accurate within 2 weeks of any change.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Conditionally permissible for the ISIC 4641 low-risk wholesale activity. Under § 4a GmbHG the GmbH must establish a seat and a German business address (Geschäftsanschrift) where official service of process and commercial correspondence can lawfully be effected. A service-provider address (letterbox/coworking virtual office) is accepted in German administrative and court practice only if: (i) a natural person is present and reachable during normal business hours; (ii) mail and summons are forwarded without delay; and (iii) the company's administrative files are accessible at that address upon request of the court or tax authority. A pure sham address with no mail handling and no personal availability will be disregarded, leading to refusal of registration or subsequent administrative strike-off of the seat by the register court.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the Local Court (Amtsgericht) at the registered office of the company; electronic filing and extract issuance via the central state register portal (XJustiz / Handelsregister online).

Governing statute

Act on Limited Liability Companies (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbHG), particularly Sections 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 35, 39 and 48; supplemented by the Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch – HGB) Sections 8, 10 and 106 with respect to Commercial Register filing and publication.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag/Satzung) together with the notarized application for registration (Anmeldung) filed by the managing directors pursuant to GmbHG Section 8.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 31 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory minimum stated capital is EUR 25,000. At incorporation at least 50% of each cash contribution, and in aggregate EUR 12,500, must be paid into a restricted bank account (Sperrkonto / GmbH-in-Gründung account) held in the name of the company in formation; the funds must be confirmed by the bank as being at the free disposal of the managing directors and must not be withdrawn before registration in compliance with GmbHG Section 7(2). In-kind contributions must be fully furnished with substantiating valuation reports.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office for VAT ID; local Finanzamt for corporate income tax/trade tax registration; ELSTER online portal (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent establishing a German GmbH must (1) notarize the articles of association and register in the commercial register (Handelsregister); (2) file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER with the competent local Finanzamt; (3) receive a corporation tax number (Körperschaftsteuernummer) and trade tax number (Gewerbesteuernummer); (4) separately apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) under §27a UStG. Registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction; the VAT ID is typically issued within 2-4 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign parent establishing a German GmbH must (1) notarize the articles of association and register in the commercial register (Handelsregister); (2) file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER with the competent local Finanzamt; (3) receive a corporation tax number (Körperschaftsteuernummer) and trade tax number (Gewerbesteuernummer); (4) separately apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) under §27a UStG. Registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction; the VAT ID is typically issued within 2-4 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No pass-through election exists for a GmbH; German tax law applies entity-level taxation (15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax plus local trade tax at 14-17%). Available elections include (1) formation of an Organschaft (fiscal unity) for corporate income tax, VAT, and wage tax purposes; (2) deduction of trade tax as a business expense; (3) selection of financial year under §4a EStG; (4) treaty/anti-hybrid elections are restricted under the German Anti-Hybrid Rules (BFEStG / ATAD Implementation Act) – no US-style check-the-box is recognized for a foreign GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for mandatory VAT registration of commercial entrepreneurs; the small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) applies only to natural persons and certain non-corporate entrepreneurs with non-professional turnover ≤ €22,000 in the previous year and ≤ €50,000 in the current year; a trading GmbH is always required to register for VAT from inception irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under §4(1)(a) UStG applies if goods are designated for a third-country destination (outside the EU), the supplier (or a forwarding agent on its account) causes the physical export, and the exit is proven within 3 months by documentary evidence (Ausfuhrnachweis and Belegnachweis per §§8-9 UStDV). For intra-Community supplies to other EU customers, a valid EU-VAT-ID and a correct EC Sales List are required to apply tax exemption (not zero-rating). The transaction must be a genuine supply for consideration; consignment or sham transactions are excluded from zero-rating.

  • Commercial Invoice (original, in English or German, stating Incoterms)

Mandatory e-invoicing for domestic B2B supplies since 1 January 2025; full EN 16931 (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD) issuance required from 1 January 2027; ViDA-based real-time transaction reporting to BZSt (DE-REACT) is scheduled from 2027/2028; no physical digital stamping system exists – clearance is performed through ELSTER API submissions of VAT return files and Advance Electronic Customs Declarations (ATLAS).

German tax authorities routinely audit export zero-rating claims in the ready-made garments sector; missing, incomplete, or inconsistent transport/payment proof leads to retrospective VAT re-assessment at 19% plus 6% annual interest (§233a AO) and late-payment surcharges (1% per month). Intentional false submissions constitute tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by fines, forfeiture, and up to 5 years' imprisonment; directors and responsible officers face personal liability, and the customs/anti-fraud units (Zoll) may impose additional penalties and referral for criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully liberalized FX regime aligned with EU Capital Requirements Directive and Anti-Money Laundering Directives. Capital account transactions are unrestricted, enabling free repatriation of profits, dividends, and capital contributions. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted without prior approval but is subject to mandatory tax reporting under the German Foreign Tax Act (Außensteuergesetz) and potential CFC rules if foreign entities are controlled by German residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds applies under German law. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts. All FX transactions exceeding the reporting threshold of €12,500 per transaction must be reported electronically to the Deutsche Bundesbank via the e-Fin system. No statutory timeline for conversion exists; funds may remain offshore provided commercial documentation (invoices, packing lists, contracts) is retained.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for corporate cross-border electronic money institutions. German law prohibits unlicensed digital payment services for GmbH accounts; offshore EMIs are not supported without rare BaFin exemptions, which are unavailable for standard trade finance operations.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: required where cross-border related-party transactions (goods or services) exceed €1,000,000 per year (§90 Abs.3 AO). Master File: required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above €100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, substance is fundamental for tax residence under §§1 and 10 KStG and for treaty eligibility under §50d Abs.3 EStG. A GmbH without premises, employees, or substantive economic activity is treated as a shell and may be attacked under §42 AO (abuse of legal forms); CFC rules under §7 AStG may also apply.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and unlimited fines under §370 AO; total back taxes plus 6% annual interest (§233a AO); civil penalty claims by tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Director(s) personally liable under §69 AO for intentional or grossly negligent breach of tax duties; criminal sentences can include travel bans, revocation of passport, and disgorgement of criminal gains.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief and mandatory WHT reassessment at 26.375%; application of §42 AO can void the structure and impose surcharge tax up to €25,000; treaty shopping rules under §50d Abs.3 EStG.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for the withheld tax and are exposed to administrative fines; if the shell is used for cross-border fraud, personal criminal responsibility arises under §§263, 266 StGB and §370 AO.
AML Compliance

Statutory sanction: Asset forfeiture, fines up to €5,000,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years under §261 StGB, plus regulatory sanctions by the FIU and BaFin.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for AML controls face personal fines and disqualification as managing directors; travel restrictions can be imposed during investigations.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: §162 Abs.3 AO permits tax authorities to estimate income adversely; a surcharge of 5%–10% of the adjusted profit (minimum €5,000) is imposed for non-documentation; late delivery may trigger a €1,000,000 penalty in egregious cases.

Director personal liability: Director's failure to maintain TP evidence exposes them to direct liability for the surcharge and possible criminal indictment for tax evasion if under-reporting is deliberate.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) issuance, withholding tax clearance, and cross-border refunds; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT assessment and collection; all filings are made through the ELSTER electronic portal (www.elster.de); primary federal portal: www.bzst.de.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned German GmbH: (1) notarize the articles of association and apply for entry in the Commercial Register (Registereintragung) at the local Amtsgericht; (2) within four weeks of commencing business, file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER, declaring shareholders, managing directors, planned activities, and estimated turnover; (3) the local Finanzamt assigns the 11-digit Steuernummer (tax number) used for corporate income tax, trade tax, payroll tax, and VAT returns; (4) separately apply to the BZSt for the USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number, format DE + 9 digits) via the BZSt/ELSTER electronic application; (5) non-resident shareholders do not require a German physical presence, but must produce certified constitutional documents and, where dividend distributions are planned, may claim treaty relief through the BZSt FVI withholding tax clearance procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German law contains no US-style check-the-box election; a domestic GmbH is mandatorily classified as a corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to corporate income tax and trade tax at entity level. Available statutory mechanisms and elections: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under §§ 14–19 KStG, § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 GewStG, and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG – the GmbH may be consolidated for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT with a qualifying parent through a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Gewinnabführungsvertrag) and financial integration; (ii) § 1a KStG transparency option – an EU/EEA-incorporated company comparable to a German partnership may elect transparent partnership taxation for German purposes, but this is not available to a domestically incorporated GmbH; (iii) hybrid mismatch rules under § 4k EStG (ATAD 2) operate as mandatory correction rules without elective carve-outs; (iv) trade tax apportionment elections apply to multi-establishment structures; no election exists to re-characterize the GmbH itself.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany imposes no genuine VAT registration threshold: every entrepreneur (Unternehmer) engaged in business activity is mandatorily a VAT taxpayer from the first transaction. The only monetary relief is the small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung, § 19 UStG), which relieves output VAT only where prior-year taxable turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover is not expected to exceed EUR 50,000 (from 1 January 2025: EUR 25,000 prior-year and EUR 100,000 current-year under EU Directive 2020/285). An EPC contractor GmbH supplying design and commissioning services for municipal sewage and reverse osmosis water treatment works will exceed these thresholds and must register, file, and account for German VAT from the first transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

German law achieves the 0% effective export rate through statutory VAT exemption with full input VAT deduction (steuerfrei mit Vorsteuerabzug), not through a separate zero-rate. Eligibility: (i) export supplies of goods leaving the EU customs territory (Ausfuhrlieferung, § 4 Nr. 1 lit. a, § 6 UStG) where the seller or the foreign buyer transports the goods to a third-country destination and the seller holds the customs export confirmation (Ausfuhrnachweis issued via the ATLAS system); (ii) intra-Community supplies (§ 4 Nr. 1 lit. b, § 6a UStG) where the buyer is VAT-registered in another EU member state, the buyer's valid USt-IdNr. is stated on the invoice, and the goods are transported to that other member state, supported by cumulative commercial and transport evidence; (iii) services supplied B2B to entrepreneurs established abroad whose place of supply is outside Germany under § 3a UStG (reverse charge at the recipient's seat) – including engineering design, consultancy, and EPC-related offshore services, so no German VAT is charged; (iv) ancillary installation or commissioning services physically associated with exported goods under § 6 Abs. 6 UStG. The exemption requires compulsory documentary and accounting evidence (Buch- und Belegnachweis) maintained at the time of the tax audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) processed via the ATLAS system and confirmed by customs as 'Exportgefolgt' – the Ausfuhrnachweis is the primary evidence of physical EU exit
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR road consignment note evidencing transport of the goods to the third-country destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank SWIFT MT103 or payment-ledger entries evidencing receipt of the foreign buyer's payment and FX repatriation within the 180-day benchmark
  • Commercial invoice and packing list stating Incoterms allocation and third-country destination
  • Intra-Community Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung – ZM) and the buyer's valid VAT identification number for EU-desk (intra-Community) supplies
  • Contracts, delivery notes, order-correspondence, and warehouse/transport records substantiating direct shipment and uninterrupted transport chain

Phased B2B e-invoicing mandate under the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025 every German- based entrepreneur must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (CEN EN 16931 formats: XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X 2.x); from 1 January 2027 e-invoice issuance becomes mandatory for businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000; from 1 January 2028 e-invoice issuance is mandatory for all businesses. Germany does NOT operate a real-time fiscal clearance or API digital-stamping system: the BZSt and Finanzämter perform no pre-issuance authorization or stamping of invoices. VAT compliance remains periodic and electronic: monthly or quarterly advance VAT returns (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldungen) and the annual VAT return must be filed via ELSTER; intra-Community sales are reported via the Zusammenfassende Meldung. An EU ViDA-style real-time digital e-reporting framework is expected but is not yet in force.

Finanzamt external audits (Außenprüfung) apply the cumulative-evidence standard (Gesamtwürdigung) under §§ 6 UStG and 13 UStDV. Where export documentation, transport-chain proof, or the 180-day payment/repatriation evidence is missing, incomplete, contradictory, or false, the exemption is retroactively denied: output VAT becomes due on the full invoice value, input VAT is clawed back, interest on additional tax accrues under § 233a AO at 0.15% per month (1.8% per annum for periods from 1 January 2019), and late-payment surcharges (Säumniszuschläge) of 1% per commenced month may apply. Intentional or reckless false export substantiation constitutes tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung, § 370 AO) punishable by imprisonment of up to five years, fines, and asset confiscation; negligent under-declaration (leichtfertige Steuerverkürzung) triggers fines of up to EUR 50,000 under § 379 AO. Managing directors, tax representatives, and fiscal agents are personally liable under §§ 34, 69, and 71 AO and may be pursued civilly. Retrospective clawbacks are time-barred only after the standard four-year assessment period, extended to five years for negligent shortening and ten years for tax evasion (§ 169 AO), and the ELSTER digital filing history provides a full audit trail for such reopenings.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains unrestricted euro convertibility as a member state of the European Union, in accordance with Articles 63–65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully liberalised, with no requirement for any central bank permit, declaration, or administrative approval before converting or remitting profit to non-resident shareholders. Profit remittance is subject only to standard corporate tax withholding and the completion of an annually filed dividend distribution declaration. There is no separate capital account restriction, no Tobin-like transfer tax, and no mechanism that limits reinvestment or repatriation for licensed municipal-utility EPC contractors. The principal administrative requirement is the mandatory reporting of each cross-border payment transaction exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank, as an external-sector statistical declaration only, not as a precondition of transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Federal Republic of Germany does not impose any mandatory surrender, forced conversion, or statutory repatriation timeline for export proceeds received by a resident GmbH. Export receipts in foreign currency may be credited to a freely disposable foreign-currency account, converted into EUR, or applied to procurement commitments without central bank approval. The exporter remains subject to ordinary commercial record-keeping obligations and, where the individual transaction amount exceeds EUR 12,500, must file the monthly AWV balance-of-payments report through its German commercial bank. No minimum conversion ratio and no time limit for holding export proceeds apply.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), operating in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision and with the German Federal Statistics Office for balance-of-payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury and clearing rail. An EU/EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) may be tolerated by BaFin for low-value operational expenses, but a cross-border project governed by World Bank escrow rails and SWIFT MT103 wire transfers requires the status of a credit institution under the German Banking Act (KWG). Offshore or non-EU digital EMIs do not possess German or EU passporting rights, cannot open a capital escrow account with a German security interest, and expose the GmbH to unquantified regulatory, sanctions, and client-asset segregation risk. Consequently, offshore digital EMI accounts are not approved for the treasury blueprint of this municipal sewage treatment EPC project.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward capital injections are routed from the sending bank to the German GmbH’s tier-1 commercial bank via a SWIFT MT103 payment, carrying a Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR). The payment remittance reference must include the German corporate name, the commercial register number, the capital contribution resolution number, and the AWV statistical classification code for direct investment (Direktinvestition/Eigenkapital). Upon receipt, the German bank performs sanctions screening, verifies the remitter’s KYC profile, checks the payment against the company’s beneficial ownership register, and files the mandatory Deutsche Bundesbank external-sector report if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500. Reconciliation of the inbound wire is made against the notarised capital increase entry and the subsequent commercial register update.Outbound disbursements for EPC procurement and municipal construction costs are cleared through four rail types: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) in euros for all EU-27 domiciled suppliers and subcontractors; (ii) SWIFT MT103/UTP with full ISO 20022-compliant structured remittance for non-EU equipment and raw materials suppliers; (iii) Irrevocable Confirmed Documentary Credits issued in accordance with UCP 600 for import of capital plant (pressure vessels, membranes, filtration pumps, and skid-mounted assemblies), payable against a complete set of transport documents and inspection certificates; and (iv) World Bank / KfW escrow disbursement releases executed as MT103 payments after receipt of written No Objection and engineer’s progress certificates, with full audit-trail back-up and multi-signatory approval. Every MT103 message must contain a UETR and clear field 70 references linking the payment to the corresponding EPC milestone and invoice number.Outright FX forward contracts (deliverable) in EUR/USD and EUR/GBP with maturity aligned to the EPC payment scheduleNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market currencies convertible only in restricted liquidity jurisdictions, where the EPC procurement is denominated in such currencyFX swap agreements (devisenswap) for temporary conversion and reconversion of project cash balancesCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) when project debt or procurement credit is committed in a non-euro currencyCombination striptures of forward and swap structures solely for hedging and economic-risk mitigation, in compliance with the German Risk Ordinance (MaRisk) and the company’s board-approved treasury and derivative policy

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary treasury and clearing rail. An EU/EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) may be tolerated by BaFin for low-value operational expenses, but a cross-border project governed by World Bank escrow rails and SWIFT MT103 wire transfers requires the status of a credit institution under the German Banking Act (KWG). Offshore or non-EU digital EMIs do not possess German or EU passporting rights, cannot open a capital escrow account with a German security interest, and expose the GmbH to unquantified regulatory, sanctions, and client-asset segregation risk. Consequently, offshore digital EMI accounts are not approved for the treasury blueprint of this municipal sewage treatment EPC project.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on corporate income tax (0.825% effective), plus Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at 3.5% base rate times municipal Hebesatz; combined effective headline rate normally between 28% and 32% (illustrative: 30%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% capital-income tax plus 0.825% solidarity surcharge) for dividends paid by a GmbH to non-resident shareholders; no trade tax on dividend distribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory for cross-border controlled service transactions exceeding EUR 1,000,000 per related party, and for controlled goods transactions exceeding EUR 3,000,000; Master File mandatory when world-wide group turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000; CbC report mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750,000,000. Thresholds are in EUR.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but requires real economic activity and place-of-management analysis; DTA relief and EU Directive benefits are denied when the entity lacks substance or the transaction fails the principal purpose / beneficial ownership test under §50d EStG, §42 AO, MLI and BEPS rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / under-reporting

Statutory sanction: Up to five years' imprisonment or a fine under §370 AO for ordinary tax evasion; up to ten years for aggravated cases; additional fines up to €50,000; back-taxes plus §233a interest of 1.8% per annum and possible penalty of up to 100% of evaded tax when third-party penalties apply.

Director personal liability: Managing director and de facto managers are criminally liable; court can impose prison sentence, travel restrictions, asset freeze and restitution; under §73 AO the state demands disgorgement; civil liability to the GmbH/creditors under §43 GmbHG for insolvency-triggering payments.
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may estimate income and apply a penalty surcharge under §162(4) AO of 5% of the income adjustment, minimum €5,000; severe non-compliance can trigger additional penalty up to €1,000,000 and interest; such documentation orders are not appealable on substantive merits until assessment is issued.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign tax declarations and TP documentation are primary responsible officers; if they fail to present records, personal fines and criminal liability arise under §§370, 378 AO, and liability for resulting tax shortfall can be asserted against the director personally.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty and EU Directive benefits via §50d EStG and anti-abuse rules; reallocation of income to the beneficial owner under §39 AO; assessment of dividend/royalty WHT at 26.375%/15.825%; fines and penalties under AML and corporate law; possible registration cancellation in the commercial register.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil may be pierced for abuse; directors personally liable for deliberate acts under §826 BGB, liable for payments after insolvency under §64 GmbHG, and criminally liable for false balance-sheet statements; travel bans and German arrest warrants are available in extradition proceedings.
AML/payment-rail abuse

Statutory sanction: Fines under German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) up to €1,000,000 for legal persons and up to €100,000 for individual managers; enhanced registration penalties; freezing/seizure of funds and compulsory license revocation for water-treatment EPC operations.

Director personal liability: Directors are supervisory and reporting officers; breach of AML/KYC duties triggers personal fines, disqualification from management, and personal liability for the financial loss of the project company; criminal conviction for money laundering under §261 StGB carries up to five years' imprisonment.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register), maintained by the Registergericht (Local Court / Amtsgericht) for the district of the GmbH's registered office; filings are transmitted electronically via the notary through the XJustiz portal.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbHG), principally §§ 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7(2), 8, 10(1); supplemented by the Act on Electronic Commercial Registers (EHUG) and German Civil Code (BGB) §§ 125–128 on notarial certification.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Deed of Formation (Errichtungsurkunde) containing the Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag), together with the Application for Registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) and the notarized appointment of the Managing Director.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory minimum share capital (Stammkapital) is EUR 25,000 under GmbHG § 5(1). At least EUR 12,500 (one-half of the cash portion) must be paid up before registration and must remain freely at the Managing Director's disposal (GmbHG § 7(2)); the funds are held in a restricted corporate escrow bank account opened in the name of the GmbH i.G., requiring the Managing Director's joint disposition. For a one-person GmbH, security (e.g., a bank guarantee) must be furnished for the unpaid half. In-kind contributions must be fully effected and verifiably valued in the Articles. Notarial and court fees are payable separately.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: registered office must comply with the Model Building Code (MBO) – operational fire extinguishers inspected per DIN 14406, unobstructed escape routes, and functioning smoke detectors; structural load inspection is the landlord's statutory obligation evidenced by the building file, not the occupier's duty. Occupational clearance: the company must perform and document a workplace risk assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung) under Arbeitsschutzgesetz §§ 5–6, register employees with the statutory accident insurer (Berufsgenossenschaft), and maintain first-aid and emergency signage; for a purely remote/cloud-based operation, a self-declaration of compliance with digital workplace safety standards is sufficient.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and intra-Community VAT administration; the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for the corporate tax number (Steuernummer), corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT registration; electronic filings are generally submitted through the ELSTER platform.

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated GmbH submits the electronic questionnaire for tax registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt issues the German tax number for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration. The GmbH applies separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), normally using the tax number and registered legal details. A foreign entity without a German establishment applies to the competent Finanzamt based on its German taxable activities and separately applies to the BZSt for a USt-IdNr. Where goods are imported or exported, an Economic Operators Registration and Identification (EORI) number is obtained from the German customs authority. For digital supplies to EU consumers, the entity may use the Union One-Stop Shop (OSS) where eligible; OSS registration does not replace domestic VAT registration where domestic obligations otherwise apply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is generally subject to German corporate income tax at entity level and trade tax; Germany has no general US-style check-the-box election permitting a GmbH to elect transparent or disregarded treatment. Profit distributions are separately taxed at shareholder level, subject to applicable participation exemptions, withholding tax rules, and treaty relief. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Section 4k of the Income Tax Act (EStG) may deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences for specified cross-border hybrid arrangements; these rules are statutory and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exempts a German-established GmbH from VAT registration once it conducts taxable supplies. The German small-business exemption may apply if prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and election consequences. A non-established business generally has no German registration threshold for taxable German supplies, unless the reverse-charge mechanism, OSS, or another special rule applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for the German VAT exemption with credit for input tax, commonly described as export zero-rating, where the goods are transported or dispatched to a non-EU destination and the supplier can substantiate the export and supplier-customer transaction. Supplies of goods to VAT-registered customers in another EU Member State may qualify as exempt intra-Community supplies rather than exports, subject to valid customer VAT identification and transport evidence. Offshore services are not automatically zero-rated. Business-to-business services are generally outside German VAT where the customer is established outside Germany under the place-of-supply rules, while business-to-consumer electronically supplied services are generally taxed according to the consumer's location and may require OSS or local registrations. For Steam and console publishing, the contractual merchant-of-record and principal-versus-agent position must be reviewed; platform settlement statements alone do not establish export or non-German place of supply.

  • Electronic export declaration and customs export accompanying document or equivalent customs exit confirmation
  • Export accompanying record or exit results message evidencing that the goods exited the European Union
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, sales contract, and packing or transport documentation
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, consignment note, or equivalent carrier evidence where applicable
  • Proof linking the exported goods to the German supplier and the relevant customer transaction
  • For intra-Community supplies rather than non-EU exports: customer VAT identification number and documentary proof of transport to another EU Member State
  • For digital services: customer-location evidence, billing address, IP or payment-location evidence where required, platform agreements, transaction reports, and settlement statements
  • Bank payment or remittance evidence where commercially available; an inward remittance certificate is not a generally mandatory German export-VAT document

Germany does not generally operate real-time VAT invoice clearance or mandatory fiscal invoice stamping. VAT invoices and records must satisfy the German VAT Act and be retained in an authentic, integral, and readable electronic form. From 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for qualifying domestic business-to-business transactions. Issuance of structured e-invoices is subject to transitional rules: generally until 31 December 2026, and potentially until 31 December 2027 for issuers meeting the applicable turnover condition. From 1 January 2028, the structured e-invoicing obligation is generally fully effective for qualifying domestic B2B transactions. Electronic VAT returns and required tax filings are generally submitted through ELSTER. Digital-platform and payment records, including Steam or console-store settlement statements, should be retained as transaction evidence.

Germany has no general statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days. Incorrectly claimed VAT exemptions or input-tax refunds may be assessed retrospectively, with repayment of the improperly exempted output tax or disallowed input tax, interest under the German Fiscal Code, and possible late-payment surcharges. Tax offences or tax evasion may trigger criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment under the Fiscal Code; negligent tax reduction may result in administrative penalties. Intentional or reckless submission of false export evidence can also create customs liability, recovery of customs-related amounts, and administrative or criminal consequences. Records supporting export, intra-Community supply, customer location, platform contractual status, and VAT treatment should be retained for the applicable German statutory retention periods and produced during a tax audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the EU Treaty and Section 1 of the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV). No exchange-control license is required to receive investment capital, pay foreign royalty or licence fees, distribute dividends, or settle Steam/PlayStation net revenue remittances. Profit remittance is free from exchange-control approval, but the distributing GmbH must satisfy German corporate law (capital maintenance), file AWV balance-of-payments reports for every cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500, and comply with withholding tax on dividends and royalties (25% plus solidarity surcharge, total 26.375%; reduced or refunded under an applicable double tax treaty).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export-proceeds surrender or mandatory repatriation obligation; export receipts may remain in foreign bank accounts. However, German resident exporters are required to report cross-border receipts and payments exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics pursuant to Section 70 AWV. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. EU sanctions and export-control regulations may nevertheless block payment flows to or from sanctioned jurisdictions.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank); Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Klimaschutz (BMWK) as foreign trade and payments policy authority; Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) for banking supervision and sanctions coordination.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a complementary multi-currency expense or payout account; Low as the sole treasury or clearing rail for Steam/PlayStation collections. An EU-regulated e-money institution (EMI) can operate in Germany through EEA passporting, but it does not hold a full German banking licence under the Kreditwesengesetz (KWG), cannot accept statutory capital deposits as bank deposits, cannot provide credit or custody, and may be treated as a high-risk counterparty by Tier 1 banks during know-your-customer cascade reviews. German corporate treasuries should therefore use a Tier 1 domestic bank as the primary collection and clearing account and restrict EMI usage to low-risk operating expenses.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial GmbH capital contributions must be routed exclusively through the SWIFT MT103 message type from the contributing shareholder's bank to the German beneficiary bank and credited to the GmbH's EUR operational account. The Remittance Information field (field 70) must state 'Kapitalerhöhung GmbH – Bareinlage' and a clear AWV balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment; labelling the transfer as a loan, service income, or unrelated trading receipt will trigger a compliance hold. Verification protocol: the bank requires a certified copy of the notarized capital-increase resolution, the Transparenzregister extract, and the tax advisor's deposit confirmation. If the transaction exceeds EUR 12,500, the receiving bank reports it under Section 70 AWV to the Deutsche Bundesbank and assigns a statistical reference number.

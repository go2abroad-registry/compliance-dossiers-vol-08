Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT ID issuance and federal tax administration; local tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax, payroll tax and VAT assessments; statutory filing portal: ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) at elster.de.

E-government filing portal

A newly formed or restructured GmbH files the official tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the responsible local Finanzamt within four weeks after the start of business, using ELSTER or paper forms. The Finanzamt assigns a corporate income tax and VAT tax number (Steuernummer); the BZSt subsequently issues the EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) for cross-border transactions. Non-resident companies engaged in German taxable supplies may apply directly to BZSt for a VAT ID using the special application form applicable to foreign entrepreneurs.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A newly formed or restructured GmbH files the official tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the responsible local Finanzamt within four weeks after the start of business, using ELSTER or paper forms. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a check-the-box entity classification election. A GmbH is automatically subject to corporate income tax at 15%, solidarity surcharge and trade tax. Available statutory mechanisms: entry into an Organschaft (fiscal unity) with one corporate group parent for corporate income tax and VAT; option to apply the small-business VAT exemption under §19 UStG; election for flat-rate bad-debt treatment under §13b UStG; hybrid mismatch elections limited to German anti-hybrid section 4k EStG; and transfer pricing documentation elections. No anonymous offshore entity classification is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic threshold for established domestic entrepreneurs; a GmbH is required to register for VAT from the first taxable supply. For small entrepreneurs, §19 UStG provides an exemption if prior-year gross turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year expected turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000; the registration and issuance of a tax number remain compulsory even under the exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% rate) under §4 No. 1 and §6 UStG applies only to export supplies of goods physically transported from Germany to a third-country destination or into a customs warehouse outside the EU, provided the exporter retains conclusive written evidence of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) and movement constitutes a deliverable sale. Digital simulated casino and slots content supplied through Apple and Google app stores is treated as an electronic service under the place-of-supply rules; it is not goods-export-zero-rated and remains within the destination VAT system. For intra-Community B2B electronic services a VAT-valid customer ID and EC Sales List substantiation can render the supply non-taxable under reverse charge.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung/Export Accompanying Document logged in the ATLAS system)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (Frachtbrief evidencing physical export transport)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement showing receipt of export consideration on a German bank account

Germany has pioneered structured B2B e-invoicing from 1 January 2025: domestic taxable supplies must be issued in an XML-based e-invoice format such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD without paper/PDF attachment. Real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API tax stamping and continuous transaction controls are not yet legislated for gaming or app-store revenue; compliance is evidenced by machine-readable invoice reception and, for certain public contracts, through official PEPPOL channels.

Export zero-rating is strictly audited through Finanzamt/BZSt customs data exchange. Where export documentation is forged, missing or inconsistent, the tax office retroactively imposes 19% VAT, adds late-payment interest at 0.2% per month, and cancels recovered input VAT. Fraudulent documentation invokes criminal penalties under §369-§370 AO: imprisonment up to five years, or up to ten years in aggravated cases, together with pecuniary fines, disgorgement and clawback of all VAT benefits for up to the full ten-year statute of limitations for tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility pursuant to Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. The euro is freely convertible; foreign exchange controls, currency rationing, and exchange rate authorisation are absent. Capital injections, dividend distributions, profit remittances, and liquidation proceeds may be freely repatriated without prior approval from the Bundesbank or the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin). Practical remittance hurdles are limited to bank-side sanctions screening, anti-money laundering verifications, source-of-funds documentation, and statutory reporting obligations under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio or timeline in Germany. Export revenues, including proceeds from simulated social casino and virtual slots publishing, may be retained in foreign currency or converted at market rates. However, residents and their German counterparties must comply with the statistical reporting obligation under Section 67 AWV: all cross-border payments received from non-residents in excess of EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank on the standard balance-of-payments report, generally by the 5th calendar day of the month following the payment transaction. The reporting obligation is administrative and does not restrict liquidity deployment.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting in coordination with the European Central Bank for euro-area monetary policy operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. A German or EEA-based Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensed by BaFin or passported under the EEA Electronic Money Directive may lawfully provide payment accounts, IBAN issuance, and cross-border payment execution services. Offshore digital EMIs without a German or EEA licence cannot perform regulated payment services for a German resident entity. For a medium-risk social casino gaming company, a licensed EMI is viable as a secondary operational payment rail (forex conversions, disbursements to app developers, settlement of app-store proceeds), but core treasury liquidity, capital escrow, and large-ticket cross-border transactions should be held at a full-service commercial bank to ensure stable compliance and clearing relationships.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT amount, yielding a 15.825% effective CIT rate before municipal trade tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 26.375%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory if aggregate controlled transactions exceed €5,000,000 per fiscal year; Master File mandatory if annual turnover is at least €100,000,000. Extraordinary transactions require immediate documentation. Companies below thresholds must still comply with the arm's length principle and provide records on request.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. However, economic substance is enforced through § 42 AO anti-abuse rules, ATAD, CFC rules, treaty beneficial ownership, permanent establishment rules, trade tax nexus, and VAT fixed establishment. Social casino digital activities require substance in Germany (servers, employees, decision-making) to support tax positions; lack of substance can trigger treaty denial, hidden profit distribution, and VAT/permanent establishment exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Fine; imprisonment up to 10 years for serious cases; assessment plus 0.5% monthly interest; penalty up to 10% of evaded tax; asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are liable under § 69 AO for unpaid taxes; criminal liability under § 370 AO; possible arrest and imprisonment; Schengen arrest warrant.
Late Filing or Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty up to 10% of assessed tax, capped at €25,000; late payment surcharge 1% per month; interest 0.5% per month; coercive fines.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid taxes and surcharges under § 69 AO; repeated non-compliance can lead to criminal prosecution.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment under § 1 AStG; penalty 10% of adjustment if documentation is late; retroactive taxation; interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for resulting tax debts; criminal liability for tax evasion if intentional.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent is liable for unpaid withholding tax under § 50a EStG; penalties; interest; director liability.

Director personal liability: Managing director is personally liable under § 69 AO; possible imprisonment for tax evasion.
Hidden Profit Distribution (Verdeckte Gewinnausschüttung)

Statutory sanction: CIT adjustment; 25% plus solidarity surcharge withholding tax on the hidden distribution; penalties; interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for tax shortfalls; criminal liability if intentional.
Shell Company or Missing Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; reallocation of income; trade tax and VAT assessments; AML reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for tax debts; potential criminal liability for abuse of legal structure.
AML/GwG Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; imprisonment up to 5 years; license revocation.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable; criminal liability under the GwG.
VAT Fraud / OSS Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: VAT assessment; fines; imprisonment up to 10 years for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable; criminal prosecution.
Insolvency Delay

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under § 15a InsO; imprisonment up to 3 years; civil damages.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable; banned from managing companies for 5 years.
Gambling Tax Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Gambling tax assessment; fines; license prohibition; criminal liability.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable; imprisonment.

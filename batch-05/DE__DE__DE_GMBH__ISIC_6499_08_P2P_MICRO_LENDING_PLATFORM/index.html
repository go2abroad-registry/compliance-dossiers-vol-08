Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) - Federal Central Tax Office, Bonn; operational registration with the locally competent Finanzamt (tax office); electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); OSS/IOSS registration via BZSt online portal

E-government filing portal

The GmbH files the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the competent local Finanzamt within one month of commencing economic activity, obtaining the Steuernummer (corporate income tax/trade tax number); the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is applied for separately from BZSt (Form UST 1 TU or via ELSTER); registration is immediate and free of capital duty or franchise tax; periodic VAT advance returns are due electronically by the 10th day following each calendar month (or quarterly under the small-business thresholds)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full capital and current-account convertibility under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG), EU free-movement-of-capital rules, and the OECD Capital Movements Code. There are no exchange-control approvals, forced conversion, surrender obligations, or remittance licensing for dividends, loan repayments, equity injections, trade settlements, or profit transfers. The only material profit-remittance hurdles are corporate-law solvency and capital-maintenance requirements under GmbHG, shareholder approval formalities, a standard 26.375% German dividend withholding burden (25% withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, before DTT/EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief), and bank-level documentary KYC/CRS/FATCA verification. Inbound and outbound transfers remain freely convertible into EUR or foreign currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in any currency, held in onshore or offshore accounts, and reused without central-bank approval. The only compliance obligation is statistical reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV): every payment received from a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 (or equivalent) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within the prescribed monthly balance-of-payments reporting window, generally by the 7th working day after the reporting period. The reporting obligation applies to cross-border collections and financial receipts of the lending platform where the amount threshold is met.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank and balance-of-payments statistics authority); BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) acts as banking and financial markets supervisor. Germany has no separate foreign-exchange control administration, and monetary policy is within the Eurosystem/ECB framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Conditional/Low for primary treasury functions. An offshore digital e-money institution (EMI) without a European passport may not lawfully hold German consumer funds or provide payment services into the SEPA zone. If the EMI is a fully authorised e-money institution or payment institution under the German Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG) and holds a European passport (EU/EEA), it may be used as a non-custodial ancillary payer, wallet pay-out distributor, or low-value FX execution agent. It cannot replace a licensed German credit institution for the platform's capital-escrow account, client-funds safeguarding, commercial credit line, or settlement rails. Legal constraints: a third-country EMI with no EEA passport is treated as an unlicensed payment service provider; using one for German-origin borrower disbursements or investor fund collection creates criminal-exposure under ZAG and a serious AML/KYC blocker for core banking partners.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital injections from non-residents must route through the receiving German bank as a SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer, depending on the remitting country and currency. The remitter must provide the Bundesbank statistical purpose code and/or bank-required payment narrative identifying the transaction as 'Gesellschaftereinlage/Kapitalzuführung' (direct-investment equity capital) and referencing the shareholder resolution. Verification requirements include: notarised capital-increase or contribution agreement (Kapitalerhöhung), current consolidated list of shareholders, certified UBO filing in the Transparenzregister, signed beneficial-ownership declaration, proof of source of funds, and, where threshold-relevant, AWV payment-statistics declaration to the Deutsche Bundesbank. For inbound sums above EUR 12,500, the bank or resident GmbH must file the balance-of-payments statistical report within the AWV reporting deadline. The platform must not split payments to avoid the reporting threshold.Outbound treasury and trade disbursements use, in order of priority: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (pain.001.001.09) for EUR payouts to borrowers, lenders, merchants, and operating creditors in the SEPA zone; (ii) SWIFT MT103/MT103+ for non-EUR wire transfers, foreign-currency loan disbursements, and supplier payments, with a full ISO 20022 or bank-specific purpose code and contractual reference in the remittance field; (iii) SWIFT MT202 COV for high-value correspondent-bank cover transfers; (iv) documentary settlement through a Commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP600 (SWIFT MT700) and paid via MT103/MT202 for physical-import/export transactions, where goods are financed by the platform or its treasury counterparties; and (v) local instant-payment rails and regulated PSP/wallet APIs solely for small-ticket consumer wallet payouts. Every payment must be reconciled to the underlying loan contract, invoice, or drawdown notice and must satisfy the AWV reporting records.Deliverable FX outright forwards with a BaFin-licensed or EU-licensed credit institution, maturity-matched to identified non-EUR principal, interest, revenue, or operating expense exposure, documented under an ISDA/CSA or bank master agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or illiquid currencies where physical delivery is legally unavailable, cash-settled under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement, limited strictly to hedged cross-border collection or disbursement exposure.Cross-currency interest-rate and principal swaps (XCCY/CIRS) with a supervised EU bank counterparty to convert non-EUR liabilities or receivables into EUR risk, on a matched-maturity economic hedge basis.FX rolling forwards or short-date FX swaps used solely to manage intra-day and short-term liquidity timing mismatches between inbound foreign-currency collections and outbound EUR settlement obligations, executed under the same ISDA collateral framework.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File required when group annual revenue > €100 million. Local File required for related-party goods transactions > €3 million/year and services > €500,000/year (Gewinnabgrenzungsaufzeichnungsverordnung). Transfer pricing documentation must be submitted within 30 days of formal request.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies § 42 AO (abuse of law) and § 1 AStG (arm's length principle). A GmbH must maintain genuine presence (office, staff, board decisions) in Germany. Insufficient substance triggers reclassification of income, denial of treaty benefits, and mandatory IP valuation rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years under § 370 AO; fines up to 10% of the hidden tax amount. In severe cases (e.g., organized scheme), imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors become personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under § 34 AO; asset seizure and travel bans imposed while criminal investigation pending.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of income under § 42 AO; denial of loss deductions; retroactive tax assessment with 6% annual interest (§ 233a AO).

Director personal liability: Directors held personally for resulting tax shortfalls, plus criminal liability for aiding tax evasion if deliberate.
AML / Money Laundering (GwG)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €5 million under GwG; imprisonment up to 5 years under § 261 StGB for laundering; mandatory shutdown of business activities.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML penalties; subject to criminal prosecution, disqualification from management, and confiscation of personal assets.

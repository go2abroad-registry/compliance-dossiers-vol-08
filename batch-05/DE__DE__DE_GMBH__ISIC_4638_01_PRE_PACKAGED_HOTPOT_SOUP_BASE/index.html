Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) administers VAT identification numbers and certain cross-border tax matters. The competent local tax office (Finanzamt) administers corporate tax registration and German VAT returns. ELSTER is the official electronic tax filing portal.

E-government filing portal

After incorporation, submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt to obtain a domestic tax number and register for tax purposes. Apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), generally through the tax registration process or the BZSt application procedure. A foreign-established company making taxable supplies in Germany must register with the competent German tax office before or when its German VAT obligations arise; the applicable office depends on the foreign entity’s country of establishment. Provide incorporation documents, registered-office details, directors’ details, business activities, bank details, and evidence of intended German taxable activities. A German GmbH is generally subject to corporate tax registration regardless of ownership residence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is a separate corporate taxpayer and has no general check-the-box election to be treated as a partnership or pass-through. It is generally subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). German tax law provides specific elections in limited circumstances, including an eligible partnership’s election under section 1a of the Corporation Tax Act; this election is not available to a GmbH to change its ordinary corporate classification. No general hybrid-mismatch election changes a GmbH’s classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exempts a non-resident from German VAT registration where it makes taxable supplies in Germany. The domestic small-business scheme threshold is EUR 25,000 in prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 in current-year turnover under the rules effective from 2025, subject to statutory conditions; it is not a general non-resident registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the EU may qualify as VAT-exempt with credit (zero-rated) under sections 4 no. 1(a) and 6 of the German VAT Act if the statutory export conditions are met and the supplier substantiates that the goods left the EU customs territory. Intra-EU supplies are governed by separate rules and are not exports for this purpose. Services are not zero-rated merely because the customer is offshore; place-of-supply rules determine whether German VAT applies. Export evidence must be retained in the form prescribed by German VAT rules.

  • Electronic customs export declaration and export accompanying document, where applicable
  • Customs exit confirmation or other prescribed evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract identifying the goods, buyer, destination, and consideration
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the shipment, such as bill of lading, air waybill, or carrier documentation
  • Proof of payment and accounting records supporting the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a general German statutory export zero-rating requirement

Germany does not operate a general real-time VAT invoice clearance or digital-stamping system. Since 1 January 2025, businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions. Issuance is subject to transitional arrangements: paper invoices and other permitted formats may generally continue during 2025 and 2026; additional transition relief applies to qualifying issuers in 2027, while structured e-invoicing is generally required from 2028, subject to statutory conditions and exceptions. Structured formats must meet German and EU requirements, including EN 16931 or an accepted equivalent. VAT returns and related filings are generally submitted electronically through ELSTER.

Germany imposes no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Unsupported or incorrectly claimed export exemption may be denied retrospectively, with VAT assessed at the applicable rate, interest, and applicable late-payment consequences. Intentional or reckless tax evasion may result in criminal or administrative penalties under the Fiscal Code; inaccurate VAT returns may also trigger tax assessments and penalties. Maintain export and transport evidence for the applicable statutory retention period and respond to tax-audit requests.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Article 63 TFEU and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) prohibit restrictions on payments and capital movements between Germany and third countries; no licensing, approval, or registration barrier applies to trade settlements, profit remittances, loan repayments, or equity contributions. The only practical hurdles are (i) the AWV statistical reporting duty for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500, reportable to the Bundesbank via IPS within five business days; (ii) dividend withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (approx. 26.375%) on distributions to foreign shareholders, reducible to 5-10% under the Germany-PRC double taxation treaty or to 0% for qualifying EU parent companies holding at least 10% under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive; and (iii) German substance requirements under sec. 50d EStG before treaty relief is granted. Remittance itself is unrestricted and free of exchange-control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline on export proceeds. The GmbH may retain all receipts in EUR or foreign currency (Fremdwaehrungskonto) and may deploy funds offshore without central bank approval. The only statutory duties are (i) customs export declarations in ATLAS (plus Intrastat for intra-EU dispatches) and (ii) reporting each cross-border payment above EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank via IPS within five business days.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) acting within the Eurosystem; BaFin (Bundesanstalt fuer Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) as prudential and AML supervisor for German banks; European Central Bank under the Single Supervisory Mechanism for significant institutions. The Bundesbank operates the IPS payment-statistics portal for mandatory cross-border reporting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% combined corporate income tax and solidarity surcharge (15% Körperschaftsteuer + 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the corporate tax), excluding municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) which varies by municipality (typically 14%–17% combined effective rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax) on gross dividends paid to shareholders; reduced under most German DTAs (commonly 15% standard rate, with further reductions to 5% or 10% for direct substantial participations, e.g., Germany–China DTA provides 10% for a 25% shareholding); exemption possible for certain EU parent companies under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% if holding at least 10% for 12 months and substance conditions met).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally 31 July following the fiscal year-end; automatic extension to 31 October if the return is prepared and submitted by a German tax adviser (Steuerberater); further extensions upon justified application; quarterly prepayments of corporate income tax and trade tax are required based on the prior year's assessment.

GewSt 1 A, line 23 (Kz. 0050) Trade income (Gewerbeertrag) after allowances and deductions; the tax base is the corporate income modified by additions (e.g., 25% of interest expense, rental expenses) and deductions; trade tax is deductible only to a limited extent. Trade tax is a German municipal tax; non-resident shareholders have no filing obligation. The GmbH computes trade tax based on its domestic business operations. Late filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag) up to EUR 25,000 per return, generally 0.25% of the assessed tax per commenced month (minimum EUR 25); late payment interest at 0.15% per month (Nachemhebungszinsen); estimated assessments (geschätzte Veranlagung) with increased tax burden; default penalties for failure to submit electronic declarations up to EUR 5,000; reimbursement costs for enforced assessments.
KSt 1 A, line 44 (Kz. 0044) Declaration of dividend distributions and retained profits; used to determine application of the corporate income tax credit system and to report any hidden profit distributions (verdeckte Gewinnausschüttungen). For non-resident shareholders, the GmbH must return dividends (on line 44) to the tax authority and withhold 26.375% dividend withholding tax (plus any treaty-reduced rate after formal relief procedure). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The German Transfer Pricing Act (Verwaltungsgrundsätze) requires contemporaneous documentation (Local File) for all cross-border controlled transactions, without a de minimis exemption; however, a threshold applies for the duty to submit documentation: Local File is mandatory when cumulative cross-border transactions with each related party exceed EUR 100,000 per year; Master File is mandatory when the multinational group's total turnover exceeds EUR 100 million and the German entity's transactions exceed EUR 100,000; documentation must be furnished within 60 days of a tax audit request (extended under certain circumstances).

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like zero-tax jurisdictions; however, economic substance requirements are embedded in the German anti-abuse rules: Section 42 AO (abusive transactions), Section 8a KStG (interest limitation), the beneficial ownership concept in treaty law, and the OECD BEPS substance criteria. A GmbH distributing pre-packaged hotpot soup base must demonstrate minimum personnel, premises, logistics capacity, and substantive business decision-making in Germany to be entitled to treaty benefits and VAT recovery.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung, sec. 370 AO)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine; in aggravated cases (large-scale, abuse of position) imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years; automatic additional assessment of the evaded tax plus interest (0.15% per month), and forfeiture of any financial advantage; prohibition from holding directorships for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Managing directors (Geschäftsführer) personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties; criminal conviction leads to direct personal fines and imprisonment; tax authority may impose seizure of personal assets and travel bans; civil restitution to the tax authority for tax evaded is mandatory.
Artificial reduction of taxable basis through shell arrangements

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under sec. 42 AO: taxes are assessed as if the substance existed; denial of treaty benefits; withholding tax on re-characterized dividends and royalties (26.375%); trade tax and corporate income tax reassessed with late payment interest; penalty up to EUR 150,000 for incorrect tax declarations.

Director personal liability: directors and beneficial owners are jointly liable for the additional taxes and penalties; criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion; asset freezing orders and travel bans under criminal procedure; professional disqualification for tax advisers involved.
Under-capitalization and hidden equity contribution

Statutory sanction: Interest deduction denied under sec. 4h EStG / 8a KStG; reclassification of the interest as hidden profit distribution (verdeckte Gewinnausschüttung) subject to 26.375% withholding tax; trade tax re-assessment; potential criminal tax evasion if intentionally misrepresented.

Director personal liability: Managing directors liable for failing to document arm's-length interest rates and to ensure proper equity ratios; personal liability for the additional tax burden if they knowingly signed false declarations; criminal liability for breach of duty; travel and asset restrictions possible.
Lack of economic substance as a shell entity

Statutory sanction: Denial of VAT refunds and VAT registration (UStG sec. 18); treaty relief denied; use of sec. 42 AO to assess tax on actual economic activities; registration in the German commercial register may be declared void; fines up to EUR 10,000 for false information in the register.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow shareholders personally liable for all corporate taxes if they are deemed to have acted as de facto managers (faktischer Geschäftsführer); imprisonment for fraudulent registration; travel bans and extradition risk under EU mutual assistance.
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Reversal of burden of proof; tax adjustment upwards based on the authority's own estimation; penalty of at least 5% of the adjusted income and default penalty up to EUR 1 million; additional late documentation penalty up to EUR 100,000; criminal prosecution for deliberate falsification of documentation.

Director personal liability: Managing directors personally liable for failing to implement an adequate transfer pricing system; personal fine; liability for penalties and interest; disqualification as director for intentional violations; civil claims by the tax authority for lost tax revenue.
Money laundering / AML non-compliance in cross-border payment flows

Statutory sanction: Fines under GwG up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover for legal entities; violation of EU sanctions regimes leads to asset freezes and fines; imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering (sec. 261 StGB); confiscation of illicit proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML failures; criminal prosecution for money laundering if they knew or should have known of the origin of funds; travel bans, seizure of personal property, and extradition under international cooperation.

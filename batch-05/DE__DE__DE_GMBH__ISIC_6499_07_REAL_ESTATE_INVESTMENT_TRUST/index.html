Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and OSS; competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for income tax, trade tax and VAT assessments; ELSTER electronic filing portal.

E-government filing portal

A German-resident GmbH obtains a statutory tax number (Steuernummer) and VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' after commercial registration. For a foreign non-established entity, direct VAT registration with BZSt using the official online form is required before the first taxable supply in Germany; a German fiscal representative may be required for third-country entrepreneurs. The Elster portal is used for submission of tax returns and registration data.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The GmbH is a domestic corporation subject to market income taxation; it is resident if its statutory seat or place of effective management is in Germany. The GmbH is not eligible for pass-through treatment and is subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income, with a separate trade tax charge on business income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The GmbH is a domestic corporation subject to market income taxation; it is resident if its statutory seat or place of effective management is in Germany. The GmbH is not eligible for pass-through treatment and is subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income, with a separate trade tax charge on business income. A German REIT tax privilege under the REIT Act is not available to a GmbH because the eligible legal form is a listed joint-stock company (AG/SE).

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime. Relevant statutory elections include the Organschaft (fiscal unit) for corporate income tax and VAT, the §9 UStG option to waive VAT exemption on real estate letting, and the small-entrepreneur rule under §19 UStG for turnovers up to EUR 22,000 in the preceding year and EUR 50,000 estimated in the current year. A REIT election is not available for a GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold applies to non-established suppliers; any taxable supply in Germany triggers registration. Domestic small-business exemption: prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 22,000 and current-year expected turnover ≤ EUR 50,000. Cross-border EU distance-selling threshold: EUR 10,000 (EU-wide) for goods and electronically supplied services. Real-estate letting is generally VAT-exempt; the landlord may elect to tax if preconditions are satisfied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to physical exports to non-EU countries when the goods are proven to have left the EU; intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered customers are zero-rated subject to valid VIES confirmation. Taxable services to third-country entrepreneurs may be zero-rated where the place of supply is outside Germany. For a real-estate investment vehicle, real-estate rental income is not export activity: it is either VAT-exempt or may be subjected to German VAT by exercising the option to tax.

  • ATLAS export declaration or customs document proving the departure of goods from the German/EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list containing description of goods, quantities, counterparty and Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill or CMR international consignment note evidencing physical movement to the non-EU destination
  • Destination-country customs clearance or proof of arrival where available
  • Payment evidence such as contract, bank transfer or inward remittance certificate evidencing the underlying export consideration

Germany mandates structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B supplies with effect for all parties from 1 January 2025. Accepted formats include XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.3 and EDI; transmission takes place via PEPPOL or another agreed electronic route. There is no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping in Germany, but the recipient must be able to extract structured data. All invoices and supporting documents must be retained for the statutory 10-year German VAT audit period.

Fraudulent zero-rating or false export substantiation constitutes VAT evasion under §370 AO and is punishable by imprisonment of up to ten years, monetary fine, full clawback of the VAT originally zero-rated, and default interest at 6% per annum. The assessment period extends to ten years in cases of intentional evasion. Tax authorities may also impose liability on managing directors, revoke VAT registrations, and pursue cross-border customs audit, seizure and criminal proceedings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of euro for current and capital account transactions. No exchange controls, no profit remittance licensing, no admissibility caps. Remittance hurdles are fiscal and reporting only: 25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on dividends to non-resident shareholders (treaty override or EU Directive relief available); mandatory Bundesbank Z5 statistical declaration for cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 within seven days; non-EU investor direct investment in German real estate vehicles may be caught by AWV Section 55a-55c FDI screening and property-investment review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion ratio, or timeline for export receipts under German law. Exporters may hold foreign currency or euro proceeds freely abroad or domestically. The only legal timing obligation is statistical reporting: receipt of payment from a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 (including rental and property-sale proceeds of an REIT) must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank within seven days of value date via Z5 (AWV Section 67); no portion of proceeds is required to be converted.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Deutsche Bundesbank, Frankfurt am Main) as the external economic statistics and payment-reporting authority; BaFin as the banking and AML supervision authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High for ancillary payment initiation and low-value payout rails; Low for core treasury. Legal constraints: a non-bank EMI (Electronic Money Institution) licensed by BaFin or passporting under the EU E-Money Directive may open segregated safeguarding accounts and execute SEPA/MT103-equivalent transfers, but cannot take deposits or grant credit, is excluded from the statutory German deposit guarantee scheme, and cannot lawfully serve as the custodian of capital escrow or dividend sweeps of a licensed REIT. Accordingly, the GmbH's principal liquidity accounts must sit with full-licence KWG credit institutions (Deutsche Bank, Commerzbank, UniCredit); a German-regulated EMI (e.g., a BaFin-supervised platform) may be used as a high-volume payroll or rental-income collection conduit. Post-Brexit UK-registered EMIs have lost the EU passport and must be excluded from the clearing path.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer + 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT liability (aggregate 15.825%), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a Hebesatz of 200%–490%; combined effective burden in most German cities is approximately 29.8%–33.8%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer on dividend distributions plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the WHT amount, yielding a German statutory all-in rate of 26.375%; natural-person shareholders may additionally be subject to church tax of up to 9%, depending on their place of residence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 in aggregate cross-border transactions with related parties per fiscal year for Local File documentation; EUR 500,000 for exceptional/outlier transactions (e.g. restructuring, one-off management fees); Master File is required when the worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations, but it imposes substance and anti-abuse requirements through § 42 AO, the abuse rule under § 50d(3) EStG, the OECD BEPS Minimum Standard, MLI LOB/PPT clauses, and the CFC rules in the Foreign Transaction Tax Act. A property-owning GmbH must demonstrate: real investment in German real estate, active local management with functional/administrative presence, local premises and utility, independent decision-making, and genuine operational substance (staff or accountable local service-provider). A fiduciary GmbH that simply shelters foreign principal ownership in a German shell while acting as a conduit will trigger a statutory reassessment, denial of treaty relief, or re-characterisation as a transparent conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal tax evasion / Steuerhinterziehung

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years; in particularly serious cases (significant tax understatement, commercial-scale evasion) imprisonment of up to ten years; criminal fine up to 360 daily rates plus confiscation of the evaded tax and statutory interest; publication of final criminal convictions.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for civil damages under § 823 BGB and § 71 AO for evaded tax; the corporate veil is pierced where the reality is fraud; directors may face imprisonment, disqualification from serving as directors for up to five years, and a travel ban or exit restriction issued by the tax office (Mahnverfahren).
Administrative failure to file / late payment

Statutory sanction: Late filing surcharge under § 152 AO: 0.5% of assessed tax per month, capped at €25,000; late payment surcharge under § 240 AO: 1% per month; tax arrears interest under § 233a AO: 1.8% per annum; estimated base assessment under § 162 AO and coercive fine (Zwangsgeld) up to €25,000.

Director personal liability: Directors incur personal liability for the resulting tax debts if they breach bookkeeping or filing duties (§ 34 AO, § 69 AO); they are also liable for the company's VAT liabilities under § 26 UStG and can be subject to administrative fines and wearing travel exclusions only in cases of repeated refusal of a statutory official request.
Transfer pricing documentation deficiency / related-party mispricing

Statutory sanction: Section 162 AO authorises tax officials to adjust income to the assumed arm's-length result. If documentation is materially incomplete, the tax authorities impose a surcharge of 25% of the adjusted profit (minimum €5,000). Penalty interest at 1.8% p.a. accrues on the additional assessment; intentionally fabricated documentation may elevate the matter to criminal tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the additional corporate tax and penalty interest if they directed the pricing; criminal liability arises where they knowingly sign false transfer-pricing certificates or instruct auditors to destroy evidence; no separate imprisonment is available for negligence but civil liability to the GmbH applies.
Shell company / treaty abuse / lack of substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty WHT relief upon BZSt review; re-qualification of the entity as transparent under § 42 AO; additional withholding tax at 25%–26.375% on the distribution as if no treaty applied; imposition of a supplementary CFC-basis tax; administrative penalty for abusive tax avoidance; risk of retroactive reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners may be jointly and severally liable for withheld taxes not remitted; criminal exposure exists under § 370 AO for intentional assistance in tax evasion; foreign principals may be denied Schengen-area entry or travel rights if they are considered tax fugitives after a final German conviction.
AML / beneficial ownership non-compliance (GwG)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €100,000 per ordinary violation and up to €1 million per serious violation under § 56 GwG; publication of sanctions; the notary is prohibited by law from closing real estate purchase agreements where the UBO is not identified; administrative order for dissolution of the GmbH in extreme cases.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines up to €50,000–€5 million for false or omitted UBO declarations; intentional false statements are punishable with imprisonment of up to two years under § 139 GwG; a UBO who causes the corporate facade to be used for money laundering may also be prosecuted for money laundering under § 261 StGB, triggering personal property restraint and Ukrainian?

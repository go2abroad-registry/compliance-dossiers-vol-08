Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt – Federal Central Tax Office, portal www.bzst.de) is the national authority for the DE-prefixed Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID) under §27a UStG; the competent local Finanzamt (tax office) is the assessing authority for Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax), Gewerbesteuer (trade tax), and Umsatzsteuer (VAT); electronic filing and registration are performed via the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de); customs and export clearance are administered by the Generalzolldirektion (www.zoll.de).

E-government filing portal

After notarial incorporation and entry in the Handelsregister (commercial register), the GmbH must file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER within one month of commencing business (§138 AO); the local Finanzamt then assigns the Steuernummer (statutory tax number) opening corporate income, trade tax, and VAT files; separately, the GmbH must apply to the BZSt for the DE-prefixed Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer under §27a UStG, which is mandatory for B2B intra-EU supplies and supports export zero-rating claims; non-resident entities conducting taxable domestic supplies may also obtain a German VAT ID via BZSt under §27a(4) UStG, while the German Steuernummer remains the principal assessment reference. Application lead time is typically 2–6 weeks, and the VAT ID must appear on all EU/export invoices per §§14, 14a UStG and Council Directive 2006/112/EC Article 226(1).

Application lead time is typically 2–6 weeks, and the VAT ID must appear on all EU/export invoices per §§14, 14a UStG and Council Directive 2006/112/EC Article 226(1).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is a corporation by law under §1 KStG; there is no check-the-box or partnership-style pass-through election – German civil-law corporates are per se subject to entity-level taxation. Available elections and grouping mechanisms: (1) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under §§14–17 KStG and §§2(2) No. 2, 27 UStG via a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) with a qualifying controlling shareholder, permitting pooling of corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT; (2) the small-entrepreneur (Kleinunternehmer) option under §19 UStG solely where prior-year turnover is €22,000 or less and forecast current-year turnover is €50,000 or less – practically unavailable for brewery distribution; (3) the option under §9 UStG to waive VAT exemption on qualifying real estate, not relevant to goods distribution; (4) hybrid mismatch adjustments under the ATAD-implementing Anti-Steuermissbrauchsgesetz (§4k EStG, §14 KStG) operate automatically by statutory rule rather than by elective check-the-box.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for a domestically incorporated GmbH: every entrepreneur (Unternehmer) under §2 UStG is a taxable person and must register with the Finanzamt and obtain the VAT ID as soon as it supplies taxable goods or services; the only quantitative relief is the Kleinunternehmerregelung (§19 UStG) at €22,000 prior-year / €50,000 current-year forecast turnover, which is not viable for wholesale craft-beer distribution; for non-resident distance sellers, Germany applies the EU OSS threshold of €10,000 intra-EU distance-sale registration trigger under Art. 59c UStG, but this is immaterial for a GmbH with a German establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A German GmbH may apply the export VAT exemption (steuerfrei mit Vorsteuerabzug, economically equivalent to a 0% rate) under §6 UStG for deliveries of kegged beer where: (1) the goods are actually dispatched or transported from the German customs territory to a non-EU country by the supplier, the customer, or a commissioned carrier; (2) the recipient/consignee is a person established outside the EU; (3) the consignment leaves EU customs territory under an ATLAS electronic export declaration; and (4) the exporter possesses specific proof of arrival in the third country such as a Gelangensbestätigung (arrival certificate) under §§6(3), 13 UStDVO. Intra-EU dispatches to VAT-registered traders are not zero-rated exports but tax-free intra-Community supplies under §6a UStG (taxable in the destination Member State) and require a validated EU VAT-ID of the recipient; export zero-rating is independent of Biersteuer excise suspension procedures under the German duty-suspension regime.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Elektronische Ausfuhranmeldung) processed through the ATLAS-Export customs system, including the Export Accompanying Document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument – ABD/MRN) evidencing departure from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial Invoice (Handelsrechnung) stating incoterms (e.g., EXW, FCA, CIF), keg volume in litres, net/gross weight, Biersteuer (beer excise) particulars, and the §6 UStG export exemption reference
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement), Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical carriage to the non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/SEPA credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign exchange within the repatriation period
  • Packing List (Packliste) and weight/seal certificate for cold-chain keg inventory reconciliation between the excise/bonded warehouse and the customs seal numbers
  • Gelangensbestätigung (third-country arrival certificate) under §13 UStDVO, obtained and retained by the exporter in the prescribed form, confirming physical arrival in the destination country
  • EUR.1/A.TR movement certificate or invoice statement of preferential origin under EU trade agreements where applicable, together with the Export Cargo Declaration for consolidated or scheduled freight

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system (no transactional stamping or API-based clearance with the tax authority). The phased e-invoicing mandate under §14 UStG (Wachstumschancengesetz 2024) requires: from 1 January 2025, every domestic B2B supplier must be able to receive structured electronic invoices in the EN 16931 format (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD); from 1 January 2027, suppliers with prior-year turnover exceeding €800,000 must issue EN 16931 e-invoices for domestic B2B transactions; from 1 January 2028, structured e-invoicing becomes mandatory for all domestic B2B transactions. Export invoices may remain in PDF or paper form but must reference the relevant ATLAS customs export declaration; fiscalization of point-of-sale cash registers with certified TSE (technical security modules) under §146a AO applies to any retail taproom or tasting-room POS.

Export zero-rating is a high-risk audit area: the Finanzamt, the Zollfahndungsamt (Customs Investigation Office), and the Eurofisc/EU anti-fraud network scrutinize the export proof bundle; the exporter must possess complete documentation at the time of declaration and retain it for the statutory 10-year period under §147 AO. Where any condition fails – missing arrival certificate, destination discrepancies, unrepatriated export proceeds beyond 180 days, or fabricated documents – the Finanzamt retroactively assesses 19% Umsatzsteuer plus statutory interest under §233a AO (historically 6% per annum, reduced by the 2024 Interest Adjustment Act for later assessment periods), late-payment surcharges under §240 AO up to the 10% cap of assessed VAT, and administrative fines. Intentional or reckless use of false export evidence constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO or customs smuggling under §373 AO, punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in especially serious cases, together with confiscation/forfeiture orders under §§73 ff. StGB; managing directors are personally and severally liable for the GmbH's unpaid VAT and excise under §§34, 69 and 71 AO, and cross-border beer-export fraud triggers centralized enforcement, access to bank records, and international asset seizure measures.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has a freely convertible currency regime within the euro area. There are no general capital controls, exchange controls, restrictions on converting euros into foreign currencies, or general approval requirements for inbound foreign investment, capital contributions, intercompany loans, dividends, royalties, or trade payments. Current-account payments and capital-account transactions are generally unrestricted. Profit remittances are subject to distributable-profit rules under German corporate law, statutory and contractual reserves, insolvency and capital-maintenance restrictions, withholding-tax analysis, beneficial-owner and substance review, sanctions screening, transfer-pricing support, and potential tax treaty or domestic withholding-tax relief procedures. Dividend distributions by a GmbH generally require an adopted annual financial statement and shareholder resolution. A GmbH may not distribute amounts required to preserve its registered share capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no prescribed conversion ratio, and no statutory deadline requiring a German exporter to convert foreign-currency receipts into euros or transfer them to a domestic bank. Export receipts may remain in foreign-currency accounts, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, customs, anti-money-laundering, and contractual obligations. Cross-border payment-reporting obligations under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV) may apply to reportable payments or receipts above the applicable Bundesbank threshold, generally EUR 50,000 for payments reported under the current regime, subject to exclusions and detailed Bundesbank instructions. Payment-reporting classifications and thresholds should be confirmed at transaction date because Bundesbank rules and forms may change. Exporters of beer must separately comply with customs, excise-duty, export-control, and proof-of-export requirements where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, together with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for regulated banking, payment, electronic-money, investment-services, and anti-money-laundering supervision. The Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) may be relevant for export controls, while customs authorities administer customs and excise controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payments and collections; low as a sole treasury bank. A payment institution or electronic-money institution licensed in Germany or another EEA state may provide euro accounts, SEPA payments, cards, and foreign-exchange services through authorization or passporting. An EMI account is not a bank deposit account, does not provide ordinary deposit-guarantee protection, and normally holds customer funds under safeguarding arrangements. Offshore digital EMIs outside the EEA cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services to German customers without an applicable German or EEA authorization, branch, agent, or other legally valid access structure. Fintech onboarding may be unsuitable for GmbH share-capital escrow, complex shareholder funding, cash-intensive beverage operations, excise-tax payments, large trade flows, or transactions requiring documentary trade finance. The GmbH must verify licensing status, safeguarding model, account-closure rights, settlement jurisdictions, sanctions controls, data processing, and contingency access to funds.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) under the Corporate Income Tax Act, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal corporate tax burden of 15.825%. Municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and varies by municipality; the combined corporate and trade tax burden is commonly approximately 22.8% to 33%, with approximately 29.8% at a 400% municipal multiplier.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital gains withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic rate of 26.375%. A qualifying corporate shareholder may obtain exemption or refund relief under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive implementation, German domestic participation exemption rules or an applicable double tax agreement. German withholding-tax relief is not automatic merely because the recipient is a foreign parent.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due seven months after the end of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year. Where the taxpayer is represented by a tax adviser, the extended deadline is generally the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory exceptions and administrative extensions. Quarterly prepayments are generally due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December.

Deadline: · Frequency:

VAT advance returns and payments are generally due on the tenth day after the end of the reporting period. The annual VAT return is generally due seven months after the end of the calendar year, subject to applicable adviser extensions and special rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany requires documentation of extraordinary business transactions and related-party dealings under Section 90 of the Fiscal Code and the German Profit Allocation Documentation Regulation. The principal transaction-value thresholds commonly used for contemporaneous documentation are EUR 6 million annually for deliveries of goods and EUR 600,000 annually for services and other transactions. Local-file documentation may be requested for cross-border related-party transactions even below these thresholds where the tax authority considers the information necessary. A master file is generally required for German entities belonging to a multinational enterprise group where the statutory German turnover threshold of EUR 100 million is exceeded. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance remains material under the general anti-abuse rule in Section 42 of the Fiscal Code, beneficial-ownership principles, treaty anti-abuse provisions, transfer-pricing rules, controlled foreign company rules, hybrid-mismatch provisions, interest limitation rules and EU anti-avoidance standards. A foreign parent, financing company or intellectual-property owner should have commercially credible functions, personnel, decision-making, assets, risk control and records consistent with the income allocated to it. Pure conduit arrangements, back-to-back financing, circular cash flows and unsupported management charges are high-risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under Section 370 of the Fiscal Code is punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine; aggravated cases may carry imprisonment of six months to ten years. The GmbH remains liable for unpaid tax, interest and surcharges. Voluntary disclosure relief is highly restricted and unavailable after discovery or commencement of qualifying investigations.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal liability, tax liability under Section 69 of the Fiscal Code, civil claims, asset seizure and disqualification consequences. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered in criminal or enforcement proceedings. Imprisonment is possible for aggravated tax evasion.
VAT Fraud and Fictitious Intra-Community Trade

Statutory sanction: VAT evasion penalties under the Fiscal Code may include fines or imprisonment up to the statutory tax-evasion limits, repayment of improperly claimed input VAT, interest, denial of zero-rating and joint liability assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors who knew or recklessly disregarded the fraud may be prosecuted personally, assessed for unpaid tax and subjected to asset-seizure measures. Directors may also face civil restitution claims by the GmbH, insolvency administrator or tax authority.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may increase German taxable income, impose withholding tax on recharacterized distributions, deny deductions, apply trade tax adjustments, assess interest and impose documentation-related penalties. Failure to produce adequate records can result in a surcharge generally ranging from EUR 5,000 to 10% of the income adjustment, subject to statutory limits and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for withholding tax not deducted or remitted, negligent tax loss and insolvency losses. Deliberate concealment may constitute tax evasion or breach of fiduciary duties.
Improper Dividend or Royalty Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may be assessed for the withholding tax, solidarity surcharge, interest and penalties. Incorrect withholding-tax reporting may lead to tax criminal proceedings where intentional.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal liability for unpaid withholding tax under Section 69 of the Fiscal Code and may face criminal exposure for intentional withholding-tax evasion. Shareholder recovery may be required if the distribution was unlawful or caused insolvency.
Insolvency Delay and Preferential Payments

Statutory sanction: Failure to file for insolvency without undue delay may constitute a criminal offense under Section 15a of the Insolvency Code, with imprisonment of up to three years or a fine, subject to the statutory circumstances. Directors may owe damages for prohibited payments and insolvency estate impairment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution, personal liability for post-insolvency payments, criminal prosecution, management disqualification and asset recovery. Courts may impose travel restrictions in appropriate criminal or enforcement proceedings; travel bans are not automatic.
Beneficial Ownership, AML and Shell Company Risk

Statutory sanction: Violations of the German Anti-Money Laundering Act may result in administrative fines, enhanced supervisory measures, reporting restrictions and criminal money-laundering exposure where assets derive from criminal activity. Serious money laundering may carry imprisonment of up to five years, with higher penalties in aggravated circumstances. Transparency-register violations may generate substantial administrative fines.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be personally prosecuted, fined or held liable for supervisory failures. Assets may be seized or confiscated. Travel restrictions may be ordered in criminal proceedings but are not an automatic AML consequence.
Beer Excise, Food and Distribution Licensing

Statutory sanction: Excise underpayments may result in assessments, seizure, interest, administrative fines and criminal excise-tax proceedings. Serious violations may lead to withdrawal of authorizations and prohibition of operations. Food-law violations may result in product recalls, fines and criminal liability where intentional or grossly negligent.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be liable for unpaid beer duty and customs-related amounts, criminally responsible for deliberate evasion, and exposed to civil claims for product or consumer losses. Confiscation of goods and business interruption may occur.
Customs and Export-Control Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Customs duties, import VAT, excise, interest, administrative fines, seizure and forfeiture may apply. Sanctions and export-control breaches may trigger severe fines and criminal penalties under applicable German, European Union and international measures.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal liability for intentional violations, personal civil claims and confiscation. Management and customs authorization may be withdrawn, and travel restrictions may be imposed by a court where justified.
Illegal Capital Maintenance and Shareholder Loans

Statutory sanction: Contraventions of GmbH capital-maintenance rules may require repayment to the GmbH, invalidate or restrict distributions and generate director-liability claims. Payments made during insolvency may be recoverable by the insolvency administrator.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal restitution liability, breach-of-duty damages, insolvency liability and, where concealment or insolvency is involved, criminal exposure. The corporate veil is not automatically pierced, but statutory director liability applies independently of veil piercing.
Payroll, Social Security and Employment Tax Withholding

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may owe withheld wage tax, social-security contributions, interest, surcharges and penalties. Intentional non-payment of employee social-security contributions is a criminal offense under Section 266a of the Criminal Code and may result in imprisonment or a fine.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may be personally liable for wage tax and social-security losses and may face criminal prosecution, civil restitution and professional disqualification.

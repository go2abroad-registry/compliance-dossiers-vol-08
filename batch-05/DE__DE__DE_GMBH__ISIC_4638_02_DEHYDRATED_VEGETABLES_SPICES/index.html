Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT ID issuance and foreign-entity VAT registration; local Finanzamt for GmbH Steuernummer, Koerperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer and Umsatzsteuer return assessment. Primary portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) for electronic filing; BZSt portal (www.bzst.de) for VAT ID applications; ATLAS via Generalzolldirektion for customs.

E-government filing portal

After notarial incorporation and Handelsregister registration, the Geschaeftsfuehrer submits the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' to the competent Finanzamt to obtain the Steuernummer, automatic enrolment for corporation tax and trade tax, and provisional VAT registration. An application for the USt-IdNr. is then filed electronically with BZSt. Non-resident shareholders are permitted without residency restrictions on GmbH formation. A foreign seller without a German branch registers directly with BZSt in Saarlouis under the special foreign-entity VAT procedure; an EORI number must be obtained separately from the Generalzolldirektion for export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a GmbH is a separate taxable legal person subject to Koerperschaftsteuer at 15%, solidarity surcharge at 5.5% of the corporate tax (effective combined rate 15.825%), and Gewerbesteuer at municipal rates of approximately 14.0%-17.0%. No pass-through classification is available to a German GmbH; all retained and distributed profits are taxed at entity level.

Available election forms

German law does not offer a check-the-box election resembling the US regime. The principal statutory grouping election is the Organschaft (fiscal unity) under Sections 14-19 KStG for corporate income tax and Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG for VAT, requiring financial, economic and organizational integration of the subsidiary into the parent. For cross-border hybrid mismatches, Germany applies anti-hybrid rules under Section 4k EStG and CFC rules under Sections 7-14 AStG; no elective pass-through status exists for a GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic Kleinunternehmer exemption: €25,000 prior-year turnover (raised from €22,000 with effect from 1 January 2025) and €100,000 current-year forecast; however, the German-resident GmbH engaged in dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper export is mandatorily VAT-registered irrespective of turnover. Foreign suppliers without German establishment are also registrable from the first taxable supply with no threshold. Intra-EU distance sales may utilise the OSS scheme above the €10,000 EU-wide threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of dehydrated garlic flakes (HS 0712) and crushed chili pepper (HS 0904) to non-EU destinations are zero-rated as a genuine VAT exemption with input VAT credit under Section 4 No. 1(a) in conjunction with Section 6 UStG, provided the goods physically depart from EU customs territory, the recipient is correctly identified, and transport documentation proves the export. Intra-EU supplies qualify under Section 6a UStG only upon the customer's valid USt-IdNr. and EC Sales List filing; any diversion of goods into the German or EU domestic market destroys zero-rating.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Electronic Export Data) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list stating Incoterms, HS codes 0712 / 0904, weights, unit values and destination country
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air cargo evidencing departure from EU customs territory
  • Export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitschein) and the confirmation of exit (Ausgangsvermerk), if applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/TT payment evidence showing foreign currency proceeds received in the GmbH's German bank account

No real-time tax-authority clearance or fiscal digital stamping applies in Germany. The phased B2B e-invoicing mandate is in force: from 1 January 2025 all domestic B2B sellers must be able to receive e-invoices in the XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x format; from 1 January 2027 issuance becomes mandatory for B2B suppliers with prior-year turnover above €800,000; from 1 January 2028 all B2B transactions require e-invoicing. Exports must be declared through the ATLAS customs system with an MRN; invoice XML API integration with tax authorities is not required.

Where the Ausfuhrnachweis is incomplete, incorrect or fraudulent, the Finanzamt retroactively denies the export exemption, assesses 19% VAT plus default interest at 6% per annum under Section 233a AO, and may impose retrospective assessments across open years (records retention 6 years, extended to 10 years under Section 147 AO). Deliberate false zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under Section 370 AO, punishable with up to 5 years' imprisonment and fines; the Geschaeftsfuehrer faces personal civil liability under Sections 34 and 69 AO and Section 43 GmbHG, and customs penalties may be simultaneously triggered under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions within the framework of the EU Treaty and Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no exchange control restrictions on trade settlement, profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan servicing, or capital repatriation. Profit remittance via dividend distributions is free, subject to domestic withholding tax (25% Abgeltungsteuer plus solidarity surcharge and, where applicable, church tax) unless reduced under an applicable double taxation treaty. Incoming and outgoing cross-border payments are unrestricted, but statistical reporting obligations to Deutsche Bundesbank apply to German residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists in Germany. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency without mandatory conversion into euros. The only relevant statutory requirement is the reporting obligation under Section 67 of the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV): monthly reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border payments where the aggregate amount from/to any single non-resident counterparty exceeds EUR 12,500 per calendar month. Reports must be filed by the 7th day of the month following the reporting period. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), exercising monetary and balance-of-payments statistical mandates within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank; foreign trade and payments supervisory functions are coordinated with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) and the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for primary treasury use. Offshore non-EU digital e-money institutions cannot legally provide payment services to German residents without obtaining a license from BaFin or entering the German market via a licensed EU subsidiary. EEA-licensed EMIs may passport into Germany under Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2) and may be used for supplementary cross-border settlement, but they are not a substitute for a BaFin-licensed commercial bank when structuring documentary letters of credit, capital escrow accounts, AWV reporting integration, and high-limit liquidity clearing. Primary account should remain with a full-service German bank; EMI usage should be restricted to low-value, high-frequency ancillary disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident shareholders or lenders must be routed via SWIFT wire transfer to the German GmbH's operational account using a correspondent banking chain compliant with the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance. For statutory share capital contributions to an 'Escape' Kapitalkonto, the funds must be paid into a separately designated capital escrow account and verified by the notary or bank as freely available; the funds may not be withdrawn prior to the GmbH's registration. For shareholder loans or excess treasury funding, a standard SWIFT transfer is permitted with a clear payment purpose code corresponding to direct investment or intercompany financing. Any cross-border receipt exceeding EUR 12,500 aggregated per non-resident counterparty per month must be reported by the GmbH to Deutsche Bundesbank in the electronic reporting portal (IDEV or via the Bundesbank's external transaction reporting system) by the 7th day of the following month.Outbound trade disbursements should be settled through (a) SWIFT MT103 for direct documentary remittances and high-value wire transfers; (b) commercial letters of credit (sight or deferred) negotiated through the German relationship bank's trade finance desk, particularly for bulk dehydrated vegetable and spice shipments to East Africa; (c) documentary collection (D/P or D/A) as a lower-cost intermediary rail; and (d) SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for intra-EU supplier and logistics payments. Each outbound payment must be tagged with the correct AWV payment reason code for statistical reporting, and cumulative outbound payments exceeding EUR 12,500 per non-resident counterparty per month must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank by the 7th day of the following month. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (Körperschaftsteuer 15% plus Solidaritätszuschlag 5.5% on the CIT; trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) additionally applies at municipal rates of approximately 7% to 17.15%, producing a combined effective rate of roughly 27%–32%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% domestic withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders (25% capital gains tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withheld tax). Where the shareholder is a German resident individual or corporation, the final withholding tax or corporate income tax credit procedure applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar-year entities, the return must be filed by 31 July of the following calendar year. Non-calendar fiscal years are due within 10 months after fiscal year-end. When prepared and represented by a certified tax adviser, an automatic extension is available to 28 February of the second year, and in practice to 31 May on application.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same deadline as the corporate income tax return: 31 July following the calendar year, or within 10 months for off-calendar fiscal years, with extension to 28 February (31 May when represented by a tax adviser).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German TP documentation rules do not set a universal statutory materiality threshold. In audit practice, the tax authorities require a Local File where the aggregate volume of controlled cross-border transactions with related parties is material, commonly benchmarked at more than €1–5 million per year; a Master File is expected when the entity belongs to a group with consolidated revenue exceeding €100 million; CbC reporting follows the OECD/G20 standard at €750 million group consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies no formal Economic Substance Regulations comparable to the UAE regime, but imposes a similar requirement through the general anti-abuse rule of §42 AO and the controlled-foreign-company rules of §§7–13 AStG. A GmbH used purely as a booking shell without staff, premises, or genuine decision-making power in the dehydrated-spice export business will be disregarded for tax purposes; intercompany contracts lacking actual implementation are recharacterized, and the profits are attributed to the shareholder and permanent establishment in the supplier state.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years or a criminal fine under §370 AO; aggravated cases carry six months to ten years' imprisonment. Under §233a AO, interest of 0.15% per month accrues on the underpaid tax, and under §240 AO a late-payment surcharge of 1% per month is imposed. The tax authority may also reverse advance tax assessments and impose penalty settings for the whole audit period.

Director personal liability: Directors and tax representatives who sign the return are jointly liable under §69 AO for the unpaid tax amount plus interest and surcharges. Criminal conviction triggers automatic personal liability; pre-trial detention or a travel ban may be ordered when there is flight risk. If the corporate veil was used only as a cover, private assets of the director are pursued through seizure and attachment in restitution of the tax debt.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under §162 AO, the tax office may estimate taxable income instead of accepting the taxpayer's books and may add a transfer pricing documentation surcharge of 5% to 20% of the adjusted amount when documentation obligations have been violated. If the adjustment exceeds €5,000 and the violation is negligent, a separate penalty for negligent tax evasion under §378 AO may be imposed; the additional tax debt carries monthly interest of 0.15%.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or submit incomplete TP documentation or participate in artificial pricing are personally liable for the additional tax under §69 AO. The corporate veil is pierced where the GmbH has been used to create a shield for price manipulation; criminal liability for tax evasion is then asserted personally against the directing mind.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under §42 AO, the abusive legal structure is ignored and the tax consequences are computed as if the economically correct structure had been in place. A shell GmbH is denied treaty benefits, input VAT deduction, trade tax deductions, and loss utilization. The tax authority may attribute all income to the foreign shareholder's German permanent establishment and assess penalties and interest on the reallocated amounts. Administrative fines under German fiscal law may reach €5,000 for a negligent offense and more where intentional evasion is shown.

Director personal liability: A director who deliberately keeps the GmbH without substance while allowing third parties to rely on its creditworthiness is personally liable for unpaid corporate tax debts. German courts pierce the corporate veil when capital is undercapitalized, corporate assets are commingled with private assets, or formalities are wholly disregarded; the director's personal assets are then subject to civil restitution and compulsory enforcement.
Dividend Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The German paying company is liable for the full withholding tax under §44 paragraphs 1 and 5 EStG. The tax authority assesses the unretained amount plus a monthly late-payment surcharge of 1% under §240 AO and monthly interest of 0.15% under §233a AO. Intentional omissions are treated as tax evasion under §370 AO and expose the responsible parties to criminal fines or imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The managing director who signs the dividend resolution and payment instruction is personally liable under §§34 and 69 AO for the tax not withheld and not remitted. If the GmbH has been stripped of assets, the director's bank accounts and investments can be seized on the basis of a tax enforcement order; a travel ban may be imposed where the outstanding tax debt risks departing within the director's person.
AML / Embargo and Payment-Trail Fraud

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under §261 StGB is punishable by imprisonment from three months to five years; serious or organized cases carry one to ten years. Violations of the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) may be sanctioned as criminal offences or administrative fines of up to EUR 5 million per corporate offender under §30 OWiG. The corporate income tax return loses credibility when the underlying transactions are tainted, and input VAT deduction is denied on induced or sham invoices.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause or knowingly tolerate improper payment routing are personally subject to criminal prosecution, asset freezing, travel bans, and exclusion from bank-to-bank transfer systems. Civil liability for customs losses and tax shortfalls is several and joint; the corporate veil is disregarded where the GmbH is a sham formed solely to process prohibited transactions.

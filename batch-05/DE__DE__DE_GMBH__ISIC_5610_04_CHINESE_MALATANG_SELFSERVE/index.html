Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A fully virtual office without a verifiable physical German address is not permissible for registering a GmbH with the Commercial Register. A registered office (Sitz) and physical German business address (Geschäftsanschrift) must be provided and served for legal documents. Virtual office providers are generally accepted only when they supply a real street address in Germany, a named mailbox, and forwarding of summons and court documents. For this fast-casual food franchise master licensing business, the German trade and tax authorities expect actual commercial substance, including decision-making management, accounting records, and a reachable operational or administrative contact point in Germany. A virtual office may be used for the administrative address during the pre-operational stage only if the managing director maintains a domestic address and full access to corporate books.

Competent tax authority

Local competent tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate tax, trade tax administration, and VAT compliance; Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). Tax registration questionnaire and electronic tax filings are generally processed through the ELSTER portal (Elektronische Steuererklärung), while VAT ID applications are submitted through the BZSt online service or the tax registration process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is a separate taxable corporation and is generally subject to German corporate income tax and solidarity surcharge, together with municipal trade tax. Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a GmbH. Available statutory mechanisms include VAT small-business treatment under Section 19 of the German VAT Act where the statutory turnover conditions are satisfied, election to waive the small-business treatment and apply ordinary VAT, VAT group treatment (Organschaft) where financial, economic, and organizational integration requirements are met, and corporate tax group treatment (ertragsteuerliche Organschaft) where statutory profit-transfer and other requirements are satisfied. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Sections 4k of the Income Tax Act and related provisions generally operate as mandatory limitation rules; they do not provide a general elective hybrid classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is generally no turnover threshold that permits an established German GmbH to avoid VAT registration for ordinary taxable business activities. The German small-business regime under Section 19 of the German VAT Act may apply if total domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year, subject to statutory conditions and the applicable effective-date rules. A business using the small-business regime generally does not charge VAT and cannot deduct input VAT. Special registration rules apply to non-established businesses, distance sales, platform transactions, intra-EU acquisitions, and supplies subject to reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for the German VAT exemption with input-tax deduction under the German VAT Act if the goods are transported or dispatched to a third country and the supplier can substantiate the export and the supplier-customer transaction. Proof generally requires customs or equivalent electronic export evidence, commercial documentation, transport records, and a consistent audit trail linking the invoice, goods, buyer, and export movement. Intra-EU supplies are generally exempt with input-tax deduction only where the customer is a qualifying VAT-identified taxable person in another EU Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, and the supplier satisfies the statutory documentary and recapitulative-statement requirements. Services supplied to customers outside Germany are not automatically zero-rated; the place-of-supply rules must first establish that the service is outside the German VAT scope or qualifies for a specific exemption. Restaurant and catering services performed in Germany generally remain subject to German VAT, with the applicable rate depending on the nature of the supply and statutory food-service rules.

  • Electronic export declaration and customs export confirmation, including the exit result where applicable
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms, and VAT treatment
  • Transport evidence such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, courier record, or equivalent proof of dispatch
  • Packing list and inventory or delivery records sufficient to reconcile the exported goods
  • Purchase order, contract, customer correspondence, and payment evidence linking the supply to the export
  • Evidence of the customer's location and, for intra-EU supplies, the customer's valid EU VAT identification number
  • Accounting records and VAT return reconciliation supporting the zero-rated or exempt treatment
  • Inward-remittance or foreign-exchange evidence only where relevant to the transaction or requested by a competent authority; Germany has no general statutory requirement for an inward-remittance certificate as a universal export-VAT document

Germany does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system comparable to mandatory clearance models in certain other jurisdictions. From 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions, subject to statutory exclusions. Mandatory issuance of structured electronic invoices for qualifying domestic B2B transactions is phased in: generally from 1 January 2027 for businesses exceeding the statutory prior-year turnover threshold and from 1 January 2028 for broader domestic B2B transactions, subject to transitional rules. Accepted structured formats include standards such as XRechnung and compatible ZUGFeRD formats meeting the legal requirements. Invoices must contain the required VAT particulars, be retained in a manner preserving authenticity and integrity, and be made available for tax audit. There is no general requirement for prior digital stamping or real-time API transmission to the tax authority, although electronic VAT filings, accounting records, and audit data may be required through ELSTER or electronic audit interfaces.

Germany does not generally impose a universal 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline as a condition for export VAT exemption. Export treatment may be denied or clawed back where the export, customer location, transport, or documentary evidence is missing, inconsistent, late, or false. The supplier may become liable for the output VAT that should have been charged, together with interest under the German Fiscal Code, correction of VAT returns, repayment of improperly recovered input VAT, and possible liability of responsible persons. Intentional or reckless false VAT declarations may constitute tax evasion or tax understatement under the German Fiscal Code, potentially resulting in criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment for tax evasion, administrative penalties, denial of input-tax deductions, and enhanced audit measures. Invoices that improperly show VAT can create an independent VAT liability for the invoiced amount. Repeated non-compliance may also trigger late-filing penalties, compulsory assessment, suspension or review of VAT refunds, and joint or personal liability proceedings against directors where statutory conditions are met.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both capital and current accounts under EU regulations and German FX law. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted, subject only to standard tax reporting and beneficial ownership disclosure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists in Germany. Foreign currency receipts must be converted to euros for domestic accounting purposes within a reasonable timeframe, but no statutory timeline or conversion ratio is prescribed. Full documentation is required for AML and tax compliance.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are feasible only through BaFin-licensed German banks or authorized fintechs. Cross-border EMI services remain subject to strict AML/KYC controls and may be restricted for certain payment categories.

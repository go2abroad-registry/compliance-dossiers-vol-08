Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF) as policy authority; Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt, www.bzst.de) as national registry for German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and tax residence certificates; local tax office (Finanzamt) at the GmbH's corporate seat for income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment and audit; electronic filing and registration portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); customs administration and EORI issuance: Main Customs Office (Hauptzollamt) via the ATLAS system.

E-government filing portal

(1) Notarial execution of the GmbH articles of association and application for entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) through the notary; (2) the Commercial Register court automatically notifies the competent Finanzamt, which assigns the corporate income tax number (Steuernummer) and requires the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to be filed electronically via ELSTER within one month of receipt; (3) separate application to BZSt (via ELSTER or BZSt-Online form USt 1 A) for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) used on export and intra-Community invoices and on the EC Sales List; (4) application to the Hauptzollamt for an EORI number required for customs export declarations; (5) filing of the trade registration (Gewerbeanmeldung) with the municipal trade office. Non-resident shareholders and managing directors may appoint a German agent for service of process and tax administration; German corporate law requires a registered office and business address in Germany, but no local residence for foreign owners.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - the GmbH is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) taxed as a corporation under § 1 KStG: 15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 15.825%), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) of 7% to 17.15% depending on the local assessment rate (Hebesatz), producing an effective combined statutory income tax burden of roughly 30% to 33% on trade profits. Profit distributions are subject to German withholding of 26.375% (25% dividend tax plus solidarity surcharge), reducible under an applicable double tax treaty. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the GmbH is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) taxed as a corporation under § 1 KStG: 15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 15.825%), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) of 7% to 17.15% depending on the local assessment rate (Hebesatz), producing an effective combined statutory income tax burden of roughly 30% to 33% on trade profits. Profit distributions are subject to German withholding of 26.375% (25% dividend tax plus solidarity surcharge), reducible under an applicable double tax treaty. The GmbH is not transparent: no pass-through treatment exists for shareholders; the entity is subject to unlimited German tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) on worldwide income by reason of its statutory seat and place of effective management in Germany.

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box classification; GmbH status as a per-se corporation is statutory. Available elections: (a) corporate and trade tax fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §§ 14-19 KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 GewStG, requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) in force for at least five years; (b) VAT Organschaft under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG where a controlling parent holds financial, economic, and organisational integration with the GmbH; (c) small-entrepreneur VAT exemption under § 19 UStG (prior-year turnover up to EUR 25,000 and current-year anticipation up to EUR 100,000 from 2025), economically inapplicable to a wholesale trading company; (d) the second-hand goods margin scheme (Differenzbesteuerung, § 25a UStG) as an alternative to normal VAT taxation of used excavator sales; (e) option to tax real estate rents (§ 9 UStG). Hybrid mismatch outcomes are mandatorily suppressed under § 4k EStG (ATAD 1 and 2 transposition), including the reverse hybrid rule for majority-foreign-held entities; no elective device can override these anti-hybrid provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Established entrepreneurs: mandatory VAT registration for any economic activity in Germany with no monetary threshold; the only statutory exclusion is the small-entrepreneur rule (§ 19 UStG) for prior-year turnover up to EUR 25,000 and expected current-year turnover up to EUR 100,000 (from 2025), which is practically unavailable to a GmbH trading used construction equipment. Non-established foreign entrepreneurs: compulsory German VAT registration upon the first taxable supply in Germany (no threshold). Intra-Community distance sales into Germany are taxable in Germany once the pan-EU distance-sales threshold of EUR 10,000 is exceeded (or upon voluntary option); the German GmbH remains in the normal domestic registration regime as a resident taxable person.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supply exemption under § 6 UStG: the 0% VAT rate applies where used hydraulic excavators and rollers are sold by the German GmbH to a non-EU purchaser, with transfer of power of disposal to the purchaser and physical removal of the goods from German and EU territory. Evidence must conform to §§ 8-13 UStDV: customs stamp or ATLAS export confirmation (IE-599) showing the goods left the EU under the seller's, purchaser's, or a third party's transport on behalf of the seller; where the seller acquired the machinery for the purpose of the export supply, the goods must be placed under the export procedure within three months of the supply (§ 6 Abs. 4 UStG). Intra-Community supplies to another EU Member State are likewise zero-rated under § 4 Nr. 1 lit. b in conjunction with § 6a UStG when the purchaser holds a valid foreign VAT ID and the machinery is transported to the other Member State. The margin scheme for second-hand goods (§ 25a UStG) may be elected for domestic and intra-Community resale of used machines; if elected, VAT applies on the margin rather than the full selling price, and the same supply cannot additionally be claimed as an export zero-rated transaction.

  • ATLAS electronic export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with customs export confirmation (IE-599 message or Ausfuhrbescheinigung) evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement), CMR consignment note, or airway bill naming the non-EU destination and consignee, with dated signature of receipt abroad
  • Commercial export invoice and packing list identifying each used excavator by serial number and HS code (e.g., 8429.52) and stating the agreed Incoterms
  • Inward remittance certificate (Zahlungseingangsnachweis, usually a bank credit advice or SWIFT statement) proving settlement of the export price by the non-resident buyer within the agreed payment term
  • Supporting movement and integrity records: terminal/port exit receipts, vessel or aircraft departure schedules, customs seal photographs, insurance certificates, and pre-export inspection or valuation certificates for the second-hand equipment

Domestic B2B e-invoicing: from 1 January 2025 every German B2B taxpayer must be able to receive structured e-invoices (EN 16931-compliant XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x with XML core data); from 1 January 2027 suppliers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices for domestic B2B sales; from 1 January 2028 the issuance mandate applies to all domestic B2B transactions. Germany has no real-time fiscal clearance engine and no digital stamping; enforcement relies on periodic VAT returns (Voranmeldungen) filed electronically via ELSTER (monthly or quarterly) and on the ATLAS customs system for exports. Intra-Community supplies are reported through the EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung), and EU ViDA digital reporting and e-invoicing obligations for cross-border B2B transactions are scheduled to apply from 2030 onward. GoBD record-keeping rules require machine-readable storage of all invoices and export documentation for 10 years.

The Finanzamt will deny the 0% rate and re-assess German VAT at 19% if the export evidence chain is incomplete, if the machinery did not leave Germany within the § 6 Abs. 4 UStG three-month window, or if the foreign purchase price is not received within normal commercial terms; German administrative practice treats non-payment beyond approximately 180 days (and in principle after 12 months) as an indication that the export consideration will not be collected, triggering retrospective VAT assessment plus interest at 0.5% per month (§§ 233a, 238 AO). Fraudulent export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO: criminal fines of up to 360 daily rates (each up to EUR 30,000) and imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years (1 to 10 years in aggravated cases), mandatory interest on the underpaid tax, clawback of input VAT previously deducted, and invalidation of intra-group export transactions of the GmbH. Invoicing and declaration offences are additionally sanctioned under § 26a UStG with fines of up to EUR 5,000 per violation; managing directors are personally liable for VAT debts under §§ 34 and 69 AO. Export and VAT records must be retained for 10 years; auditors apply heightened documentary scrutiny to used-machinery exports, including serial number verification, transport route plausibility, freight and insurance history, and correlation of payments with open receivables.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully liberalised, freely convertible euro regime; there is no exchange-control licensing, repatriation permit or prior-approval mechanism for current-account or capital-account transactions. Capital contributions, trade settlements, loan repayments and profit remittances may move without Bundesbank authorisation. The only substantive constraints are: (i) statistical reporting of every cross-border payment equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 under AWV §67; and (ii) statutory withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% combined) on dividends paid by a GmbH to non-resident shareholders, subject to treaty reduction or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. No surrender, minimum-holding or negative-interest restriction applies to foreign currency positions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio: a German GmbH may lawfully retain and hold 100% of foreign-currency export receipts in non-euro accounts. The only applicable duty is balance-of-payments statistical reporting: each cross-border receipt above EUR 12,500 (or equivalent in any other currency) must be reported electronically to Deutsche Bundesbank by the fifth working day of the following calendar month, normally via the servicing bank's electronic payment-reporting interface. Export proceeds from used construction excavators additionally remain subject to customs/Intrastat-Exstrat declarations where applicable, but no repatriation deadline, conversion quota or BAFA approval is triggered by mere receipt of export funds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany), with banking and AML supervision coordinated by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin); foreign-trade screening and enforcement under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) is administered by the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium, lawful but strictly supplementary. EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions passporting into Germany under PSD2/ZAG (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud/Visa, PPRO, Ebury) can provide multi-currency operational accounts, FX conversion and cross-border payments. They cannot, however, act as the primary capital-escrow hub: they lack a BaFin full-banking licence, are prohibited from granting credit, must segregate and safeguard client funds under ZAG §21, cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit, and typically reject hold-mail or third-party beneficiary payment structures for medium-risk machinery trading. Their transaction terms also restrict flows involving high-risk sanctioned corridors and impose counterparty concentration limits, so the treasury blueprint should use an EMI only as a disbursement-optimisation rail below the tier-1 bank account base.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity or shareholder-loan funding from a non-resident parent must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (for non-EUR) or SEPA Credit Transfer (for EUR) to the GmbH's Deutsche Bank or Commerzbank operational account. The bank reference field must state 'Kapitalerhöhung GmbH' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen', cite the notarial deed or shareholder resolution date, include the Deutsche Bundesbank balance-of-payments statistical code for direct investment equity / intercompany lending, and identify the transferring entity. Bank compliance will verify the amount against the updated Handelsregister share capital and Transparenzregister data, and the funds may be parked in a capital escrow account until the SEPA/TARGET2 credit confirmation and the AWV §67 statistical report are processed. For any single injection above EUR 12,500, the capital-escrow release is conditional on delivery of commercial bank transaction receipt, fund-origin proof (e.g., audited parental accounts or dividend vouchers), and confirmation that no sanctioned counterparties are present in the transfer chain.Supplier and vendor disbursements are cleared as follows: (i) German and EEA suppliers paid in EUR via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT), which settles in one business day with zero interbank fee; (ii) non-EEA suppliers (e.g., Japan, USA, UK, UAE) paid via SWIFT MT103 with explicit OUR cost code and full invoice/ATLAS reference, with correspondents incurring EUR 15-60 per transfer; (iii) high-value or title-critical purchases funded via irrevocable SWIFT MT700 commercial letters of credit, issued/confirmed by Commerzbank or UniCredit, with MT707 amendment handling and transport documents (B/L, CMR, or multimodal) as the control documents; (iv) targeted disbursement optimisation through a licensed EMI (e.g., Airwallex) for sub-EUR 50,000 vendor payments where the tier-1 bank's correspondent costs are disproportionate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Medium, lawful but strictly supplementary. EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions passporting into Germany under PSD2/ZAG (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud/Visa, PPRO, Ebury) can provide multi-currency operational accounts, FX conversion and cross-border payments. They cannot, however, act as the primary capital-escrow hub: they lack a BaFin full-banking licence, are prohibited from granting credit, must segregate and safeguard client funds under ZAG §21, cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit, and typically reject hold-mail or third-party beneficiary payment structures for medium-risk machinery trading. Their transaction terms also restrict flows involving high-risk sanctioned corridors and impose counterparty concentration limits, so the treasury blueprint should use an EMI only as a disbursement-optimisation rail below the tier-1 bank account base.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed EUR 1 million for goods or EUR 100,000 for services; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and submitted within 60 days of request.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies substance-over-form principles under Section 42 of the Fiscal Code (AO) and anti-abuse rules. No specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in offshore jurisdictions, but a GmbH must demonstrate real economic activity (office, employees, decision-making) to avoid denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of transactions, and penalties under Section 42 AO.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 10 years (Section 370 AO); fines up to EUR 50,000; civil penalty of 100% of evaded tax; interest on unpaid tax at 0.5% per month

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for evaded taxes if they knowingly participated; travel bans and asset freezes; disqualification from serving as director for up to 5 years
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, retroactive tax assessment, penalty of up to 25% of understated tax (Section 162 AO), and potential criminal charges for aiding tax evasion

Director personal liability: Directors liable for unpaid taxes if they signed false declarations; travel bans and extradition risk; personal assets seized to cover tax debts
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 100,000 per violation (Section 56 GwG); imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering (Section 261 StGB); confiscation of proceeds

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; criminal prosecution for negligent supervision; prohibition from managing commercial entities; travel restrictions and extradition

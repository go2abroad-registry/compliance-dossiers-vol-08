Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register) maintained by the Amtsgericht (Local Court) at the company's registered office (Sitz); filings administered through the notary and the federal electronic register portal (XJustiz / Gemeinsames Registerportal der Länder); local Gewerbeamt for trade registration.

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG), particularly §§ 1–10 (formation and registered office), § 5 (minimum capital), § 7 (registration application and capital payment), § 8 (contents of application), § 10 (registration effects), § 35 (management), § 39 (appointment of directors); the Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB) §§ 8–12 (commercial register procedure, electronic filing); the Beurkundungsgesetz (BeurkG) for notarial certification; and the Gewerbeordnung (GewO) § 14 for trade business registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notariell beurkundeter Gesellschaftsvertrag (notarially certified Articles of Association, often using the statutory Musterprotokoll) combined with the corporate formation deed (Errichtungsurkunde), followed by the Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister (registration application filed with the Commercial Register).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory minimum share capital (Stammkapital) for a GmbH is EUR 25,000 (GmbHG § 5). At formation, at least 50% of the minimum capital, i.e. EUR 12,500, must be paid in cash and the paid-up amount must be irrevocably placed at the free disposal of the managing director in a segregated bank escrow account (Gründungskonto / Anderkonto) held in the name of the company being incorporated (GmbHG § 7(2)). The bank must issue a confirmation, and the managing director must certify in the registration application that the capital is at his or her unrestricted free disposal.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: fit-out of the office space must satisfy the State building code (Landesbauordnung) with fire-extinguisher provision, unobstructed escape routes, and, where required, a fire safety review by the local Feuerwehr. Structural load inspection: floor-load capacity of server/AV workstation areas to be certified by the building operator upon request. Workplace clearance: compliance with Arbeitsschutzgesetz (ArbSchG) and Arbeitsstättenverordnung (ArbStättV), including ergonomic workstation assessment, first-aid material, occupational health registration with the Berufsgenossenschaft, and a corporate data-processing safe-harbour declaration under data protection law; any on-premises employee exposure to screen work is assessed through the official workplace evaluation.

Paid-in capital rule: The statutory minimum share capital (Stammkapital) for a GmbH is EUR 25,000 (GmbHG § 5). At formation, at least 50% of the minimum capital, i.e. EUR 12,500, must be paid in cash and the paid-up amount must be irrevocably placed at the free disposal of the managing director in a segregated bank escrow account (Gründungskonto / Anderkonto) held in the name of the company being incorporated (GmbHG § 7(2)). The bank must issue a confirmation, and the managing director must certify in the registration application that the capital is at his or her unrestricted free disposal.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt – Federal Central Tax Office), portal bzst.de and the ELSTER electronic filing portal (elster.de) for national VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer), OSS procedures and cross-border tax certificates; the locally competent Finanzamt (regional tax office) administers corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT/advance returns.

E-government filing portal

For a new GmbH: (1) notarial incorporation and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) is the statutory precondition; (2) the local Finanzamt processes the mandatory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) filed via ELSTER and issues the local Steuernummer for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT return purposes; (3) the BZSt issues the separate EU VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) upon application for intra-community and OSS transactions; (4) non-resident shareholders are permitted, and the tax registration file must disclose all managing directors and ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A GmbH is a taxable capital company: corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (0.825%) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 14–17% (combined effective burden approx. 29–32%). Resident companies with statutory seat or place of management in Germany are subject to unlimited worldwide taxation; no tax-exempt economic zones exist in Germany. — For a new GmbH: (1) notarial incorporation and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) is the statutory precondition; (2) the local Finanzamt processes the mandatory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) filed via ELSTER and issues the local Steuernummer for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT return purposes; (3) the BZSt issues the separate EU VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) upon application for intra-community and OSS transactions; (4) non-resident shareholders are permitted, and the tax registration file must disclose all managing directors and ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A GmbH is a taxable capital company: corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (0.825%) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 14–17% (combined effective burden approx. 29–32%). Resident companies with statutory seat or place of management in Germany are subject to unlimited worldwide taxation; no tax-exempt economic zones exist in Germany.

Available election forms

Germany applies no check-the-box classification for GmbHs. Available elections: (1) Organschaft fiscal unity under Secs. 14–19 Körperschaftsteuergesetz and Sec. 2(2)(2) Umsatzsteuergesetz, consolidating corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT within a financially integrated German group; (2) tax transparency option under Sec. 1a KStG for partnerships; (3) no elective hybrid mismatch rules – mandatory anti-hybrid provisions (Sec. 4k EStG and Außensteuergesetz) apply to counter deduction-without-inclusion and double-deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Small business exemption (Sec. 19 UStG) for German entrepreneurs: €25,000 prior-year turnover and €100,000 current-year forecast (for 2025 onward). For a trading GmbH the standard rule is immediate VAT registration; the EU-wide distance-sales/digital-services OSS threshold is €10,000 – if exceeded, the supplier must levy the destination-state VAT or register under the One-Stop-Shop. Non-established foreign B2B suppliers are generally outside German VAT registration via the reverse-charge mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are exempt with input-VAT credit (zero-rated) under Sec. 4 No. 1a and Sec. 6 UStG; intra-community supplies are exempt under Sec. 4 No. 1b and Sec. 6a UStG. For AI video-generation SaaS and virtual-avatar services: B2B exports are outside German VAT because the place of supply is the customer's country (Sec. 3a(2) UStG, reverse charge); B2C exports to non-EU customers are also outside German VAT as the place of supply is where the consumer resides (Sec. 3a(1) UStG), preserving full input-VAT recovery. Digital exports to EU consumers trigger German VAT unless the €10,000 OSS threshold or the destination-state reverse charge conditions apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) filed through the ATLAS customs IT system
  • Proof of Export (Ausfuhrnachweis): Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR waybill or customs-stamped export certificate
  • Commercial invoice, licensing or SaaS contract evidencing the supply and delivery terms
  • Packing list and transport/insurance documentation
  • Bank payment receipt or credit advice (an inward remittance certificate is not a German statutory requirement but serves as primary payment evidence)

Mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing under the Wachstumschancengesetz: from 1 January 2025 every German business must be able to receive XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x or other CEN EN 16931-conformant structured invoices; from 1 January 2027 suppliers with annual turnover above €800,000 must issue them, and from 1 January 2028 all B2B suppliers must issue them (EDI transitional rules apply). Germany currently has no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or API-based transaction authorization; BZSt digital transaction reporting for cross-border B2B supplies will follow the EU ViDA framework (expected post-2030).

The export exemption is clawed back if the exporter cannot produce and preserve the documentary evidence for 10 years (Sec. 14b UStG): German VAT is assessed retroactively with interest at 6% per annum (Sec. 233a AO) and late-payment surcharges of 1% per month (Sec. 240 AO). Fraudulent or grossly negligent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO (imprisonment up to five years or fines) and administrative fines under Sec. 26a UStG; the exporting company and its managing directors may additionally be held liable under Sec. 25d UStG. Germany does not impose a statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is an internal credit-risk benchmark, and delayed payment merely requires the taxpayer to demonstrate the export and consideration within the retention period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the AWG/AWV. There are no foreign-exchange quotas, no prior approval for dividend, royalty, interest, or liquidation remittances, and no restriction on capital flows with EU or OECD counterparties. Practical constraints are limited to bank AML/KYC due diligence, EU/UN sanctions screening, and the statutory AWV statistical reporting duty for cross-border payments and balances exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction or calendar month.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may freely hold and disburse foreign currency. The applicable statutory rule is the AWV reporting obligation: every cross-border receipt or payment exceeding EUR 12,500 must be filed electronically with the Deutsche Bundesbank under the best-meldung reporting procedure, and the GmbH must report monthly foreign claims and liabilities when they exceed EUR 5,000,000 or when related monthly payments exceed EUR 12,500.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank) as the monetary and payments-statistics authority, acting together with Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) for prudential banking supervision, and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) for enforcement under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium-High as a supplementary liquidity rail, not as the sole treasury counterparty. A Germany-licensed or EEA-passported Electronic Money Institution (EMI), such as Airwallex, Wise, or a Solarisbank-embedded platform, can provide fast multi-currency IBANs, on-platform FX conversion, and virtual account segregation. Legal constraints: the EMI must hold a valid BaFin e-money licence or an EEA passport under PSD2; a standalone UK EMI cannot operate in Germany without a German branch licence following Brexit; the EMI may not grant credit; and balances above EUR 100,000 are not covered by the statutory German deposit protection scheme (Einlagensicherung).

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) on the CIT (0.825% effective) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 3.5% of the trade-earnings base multiplied by the municipal collection rate (Hebesatz; Berlin 410%, Munich 490%; range approximately 200%-490%); standard combined effective burden on retained profits of a Berlin GmbH: approximately 30.2% (15% + 0.825% + 14.35%) and up to 33% in high-Hebesatz municipalities. No 2008-era distinction between retained and distributed profits: both taxed at 15% CIT. Trade tax is not deductible for CIT purposes (§ 4(5b) EStG).

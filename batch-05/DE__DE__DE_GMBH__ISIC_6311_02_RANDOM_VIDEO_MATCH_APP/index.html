Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and central services; local tax office (Finanzamt) for routine corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT, and payroll tax administration. The official electronic filing and registration portal is ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH, the GmbH must first be notarially formed and entered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). Following registration, the GmbH submits the official tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent local Finanzamt via ELSTER or on paper. The Finanzamt assigns the tax number (Steuernummer), which covers income tax, trade tax, and VAT, and the BZSt subsequently issues the EU-wide VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). The standard processing period is approximately four to six weeks. Foreign shareholders are not required to obtain a separate tax ID; the GmbH itself is the taxable person. Non-resident representatives may need to apply through a fiscal agent for certain administrative filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the GmbH is a resident corporate entity subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability on worldwide income, trade tax in Germany, and VAT as a taxable person. No pass-through or check-the-box qualification exists under German law.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available. German tax law provides limited statutory elections and grouping regimes: (i) VAT grouping (Organschaft) under Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG, whereby a controlling and financially integrated enterprise is treated as a single taxpayer; (ii) corporate income tax/trade tax fiscal unity (steuerliche Organschaft) for controlled domestic subsidiaries; and (iii) the small-entrepreneur VAT exemption election under Section 19 UStG where turnover thresholds are met. Hybrid mismatch elections are not applicable in the German domestic entity classification framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies the EU free-movement-of-capital standard under TFEU Articles 63-66; the euro is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts with no sovereign licensing hurdle for profit remittance. Non-resident profit remittance is unrestricted in FX terms but triggers 25% GST withholding plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge unless treaty relief or Section 43b (German Income Tax Act) exemption applies; the remitting GmbH must present a tax-clearance certificate and a valid dividend resolution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in Germany. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts. The sole legal duty is statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank through the General Reporting Portal (GMP) for every cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500, filed within seven calendar days with the correct balance-of-payments purpose code; non-compliance is an administrative offence under the AWV.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank); supervisory framework shared with BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) and BAFA (Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Medium for disbursement and payout rails, Low for capital-escrow or tax-reserve functions. An EMI may support the GmbH's merchant payouts (Paymentwall, Apple IAP) only if it holds a German licence or a valid EEA passport under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG/PSD2). UK-based EMIs must rely on EEA branch passporting or separate German authorisation; using an unregulated offshore EMI is prohibited and triggers immediate compliance termination by German banks. Recommended pattern: licensed German EMI (e.g., Solarisbank) or EEA-licensed EMI for payout routing, with settlement accounts retained at a tier-1 commercial bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective headline rate 15.825%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for EU/EEA recipients under Parent-Subsidiary Directive; 26.375% statutory for non-treaty non-EU/EEA (reduced by applicable DTAs)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: €1,000,000 related-party transactions; Master File: €50,000,000 group consolidated revenue

Economic substance scope: No general Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; general anti-avoidance rules and substance requirements apply to prevent artificial structures

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties including fines up to €1 million and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability, imprisonment, disqualification from management roles, and civil restitution
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of profits, denial of deductions, penalties up to 5% of turnover

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil, personal liability for tax debts, potential criminal sanctions
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1 million or more; possible license revocation

Director personal liability: Personal fines and imprisonment for willful non-compliance with AML laws

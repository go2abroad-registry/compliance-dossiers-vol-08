Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register) maintained by the competent Local Court (Amtsgericht) at the registered seat, filed electronically through the certified German notary via the Gemeinsames Registerportal der Länder (Common Register Portal of the German Laender).

Governing statute

German Act on Limited Liability Companies (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbHG): §§1–3 (formation and articles), §5 (minimum capital), §7 (capital payment and security), §8 (registration application and declarations), §10 (entry and legal effect), §§35–39 (managing directors and representation), §§46–48 (shareholder meetings and director appointment); Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch – HGB): §§8–16 (commercial register) and §§106–108 (declarations for registration); Notarization Act (Beurkundungsgesetz): §§6–8; Electronic Commercial Register Ordinance (EHUG) for the electronic filing channel.

Formation / constitutional filings

Anmeldung zum Handelsregister (Application for Registration in the Commercial Register) submitted in electronically certified form by all managing directors, attached to the notarially certified deed of incorporation (notarielle Beurkundung der Gesellschaft) and the articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Mandatory minimum share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000 under GmbHG §5(1). For cash contributions, registration may be applied for only after payment in full of at least 25% of the nominal amount of each capital contribution, and in no event less than EUR 12,500 in the aggregate (GmbHG §7(2)); the paid amount must be at the permanent free disposal of the managing directors and evidenced by a bank certificate (Einzahlungsbestaetigung). Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office, responsible for EU VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.), cross-border VAT refunds, and central tax data exchange; locally competent Finanzamt (e.g., Finanzamt Berlin-Charlottenburg or Finanzamt München) responsible for Steuernummer (tax number) assignment, corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT assessment; mandatory electronic filing via the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Notarize the articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag or Musterprotokoll), deposit minimum share capital of €25,000, and register the GmbH in the commercial register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht to obtain the Wirtschafts-ID-Nr. Step 2 — Within one month of business commencement, file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer and issues the tax registration certificate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under German law; a GmbH is mandatorily a resident corporation subject to entity-level taxation (Körperschaftsteuer 15% plus solidarity surcharge 0.825% plus Gewerbesteuer at a municipal rate between approximately 7% and 17%). Available statutory elections relevant to the IVD exporter: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under §§14 KStG and 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, whereby the GmbH may elect to be treated as a fiscal unity for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT with a financially integrated parent; (ii) option to tax VAT-exempt supplies under §9 UStG, which may be relevant to related medical services; (iii) election to allocate input VAT deduction under §15 Abs. 4 UStG by specific-sector attribution rather than the pro-rata method; (iv) election under §1a Abs. 2 UStG to waive the €12,500 intra-Community acquisition threshold; (v) reverse-charge liability transfers under §13b UStG for qualifying cross-border B2B transactions; (vi) mandatory hybrid-mismatch re-characterisation under §4k EStG and §10 KStG (Anti-Hybrid rules), which is not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany applies no general VAT registration threshold for a resident GmbH: every entrepreneur under §2 UStG is taxable from the first taxable supply. A non-resident IVD manufacturer without a German establishment becomes VAT-registered from the first taxable supply (no euro threshold). The intra-Community distance-sales and cross-border e-services threshold is €10,000 per calendar year under §3 Abs. 3a UStG and §18 Abs. 4c UStG; the intra-Community acquisition threshold for the German purchasing entity is €12,500 under §1a Abs. 2 UStG and may be waived by election. Thus, an automated point-of-care IVD manufacturer selling medical devices to German hospital distributors in a B2B context has no de-minimis threshold and must register before the first taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of automated IVD immunoassay analysers, microfluidic cartridges, and associated reagents to customers outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under §4 Nr. 1 lit. a UStG and §6 UStG (export supply), transposing Art. 146 VAT Directive. Conditions: (i) the seller is the taxable entrepreneur (GmbH); (ii) the goods physically leave the EU customs territory to a third-country destination; (iii) the movement is proven by standard commercial and transport evidence together with the ATLAS (AES) electronic customs export declaration; (iv) transport must be completed and documented within the statutory timeframe (generally evidence retained for 12 months under §13 Abs. 1 UStG and administrative practice); (v) the power of disposal (Verfügungsmacht) has passed to the purchaser; and (vi) the export records are retained in accordance with §§6 Abs. 4 and 14b UStG. Intra-Community supplies to EU medical distributors are zero-rated under §6a UStG subject to verification of the German customer's valid VAT ID and Intrastat/ECS declarations. Regulatory compliance under EU Regulation (EU) 2017/746 (IVDR), including CE marking, conformity assessment, and free-sale certificates, is required for lawful export of IVD devices but is not a VAT condition.

  • ATLAS/AES electronic customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with the Export Accompanying Document (EAD) and MRY reference number
  • Commercial invoice in English or German stating the IVD product description, HS classification (3822 for reagents; 9018/9022 for analysers as applicable), Incoterms, quantity, unit value, and buyer/seller VAT IDs
  • Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), or CMR consignment note (road) evidencing physical dispatch to the third country
  • Proof of physical exit of the goods from EU customs territory (customs stamp, port terminal gate-out confirmation, or verified ATLAS exit record)
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT TT/LC bank statement evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency purchase consideration from the non-resident buyer
  • Signed purchase order, pro-forma invoice, and delivery/receipt confirmation (Unterlagen über die Lieferung) from the non-EU customer
  • Carrier or forwarding agent declaration confirming customs clearance in the country of destination where a logistics intermediary is used
  • EUR.1 movement certificate or REX statement where preferential origin is claimed under an EU free trade agreement

Germany imposes a phased E-Rechnung (electronic invoicing) mandate, not real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping at the federal level: (i) from 1 January 2025, every German B2B supplier must be able to receive structured electronic invoices compliant with EN 16931 (XRechnung / ZUGFeRD 2.0); (ii) from 1 January 2026, domestic B2B supplies by German-established businesses must be invoiced electronically; (iii) from 1 January 2027, the mandatory electronic transmission obligation applies to businesses with prior-year turnover above €800,000 and from 1 January 2028 to all other businesses; (iv) Federal public procurement requires PEPPOL-interoperable XRechnung under the E-Rechnung-Verordnung. Export invoices must reference the ATLAS/AES export declaration and comply with §§14 and 14b UStG, with GoBD-compliant XML archiving. There is no BZSt/Finanzamt API tax-clearance stamp or QR fiscal code; non-compliance triggers penalty surcharges under §§26a–26c UStG and denial of input VAT deduction by the recipient.

German tax auditors (Betriebsprüfung) apply heightened scrutiny to export zero-rated sales of high-value medical devices because of perceived VAT-refund fraud indicators. Missing documentary evidence under §6 UStG and §14b UStG triggers retrospective VAT assessment at 19% plus late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (§233a AO) computed from the original due date; the recipient's input VAT deduction may also be clawed back under §15a UStG. Intentional tax evasion is criminally prosecuted under §370 AO with imprisonment up to 10 years, while negligent shortfalls under §378 AO carry fines up to €50,000 per act plus the §26c UStG surcharge. The BZSt operates centralised risk profiling and exchanges data through EU VIES and EUROFISC; false export certifications may additionally trigger administrative penalty orders (Bußgeld) up to €500,000 under §26a UStG, customs-fraud proceedings under the Abgabenordnung, and market-surveillance sanctions for IVDR non-compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the euro for both current and capital account transactions, consistent with Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No exchange control authorization, non-resident deposit license, or capital import/export permit is required. Inward equity subscription, shareholder loans, dividend distributions, royalty payments, trade settlements, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred. The sole formal burden is statistical: any cross-border payment or claim/obligation exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV Sections 67–71, typically filed automatically by the servicing bank (Z1/Z2 reporting mechanism). Profit remittance is practically constrained only by German dividend withholding tax of 26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), which may be reduced or refunded under an applicable double tax treaty and a withholding exemption certificate (Freistellungsbescheinigung nach § 43 Abs. 2 EStG).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation, repatriation deadline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Germany. The GmbH may freely hold foreign currency in euro-denominated or foreign-currency accounts (Fremdwährungskonten) at German or EU credit institutions. The only compliance requirement is documentary: export contracts, customs declarations, and payment receipts must be retained for at least eight years, and AWV balance-of-payments statistics reporting is triggered automatically by the bank when proceeds are credited from abroad.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) administers the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) statistical reporting for cross-border payments and capital relations; BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) supervises bank prudential conduct and anti-money laundering under the German Banking Act (KWG) and the Anti-Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz – GwG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Solaris SE, PPRO Financial Ltd (EU passport), Qonto, and Kontist legally operate under BaFin licenses pursuant to the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG, implementing PSD2) and may open euro and foreign-currency accounts, execute SEPA and SWIFT payments, and issue payment cards. However, they are restricted to payment and e-money services: they cannot legally provide commercial credit, documentary letters of credit, bank guarantees, trade finance, or collateralized FX hedging, and they do not participate in the statutory deposit protection scheme. A licensed EMI is viable only as an auxiliary receivables-collection rail, not as the principal treasury or escrow account for an IVD exporter whose counterparties require Swift LC and risk participation with a full-service credit institution.

Compliant structure: (1) Shareholders’ resolution on the capital contribution authenticated by a notary; (2) the non-resident parent executes a SWIFT MT103 from its global treasury account to the German escrow account (Stammeinlagen-Einzahlungskonto) at the chosen German bank, with the payment reference conforming to ISO 11649 RF structured creditor reference and stating 'Stammeinlage DE_GMBH [EUR amount]'; (3) the receiving bank verifies the credit against the notary’s capital-confirmation request and issues the notary confirmation (Vorbelastungsbescheinigung) required for registration in the Commercial Register, in accordance with the principle of strict distinction between the capital account and the operating account; (4) the bank automatically reports the inbound cross-border payment in the Bundesbank balance-of-payments statistics under the direct investment equity capital code (Kapitalverkehr / Direktinvestition – Erwerb von Anteilen), supported by the notarized contribution agreement and the source-of-funds declaration. Verification protocol: origin of funds, UBO clearance, and sanctions screening must be finalized before crediting; no co-mingling with trade receivables is permitted.Clearing rails for term settlements: (1) Unconditional payments – SWIFT MT103/MT202 via TARGET2 for euro and via correspondent nostro accounts (CHIPS/Fedwire for USD) for foreign-currency invoices, with invoice numbers and INCOTERMS 2020 references; settlement in 1–2 business days; (2) Documentary letters of credit – issuance of an MT700/MT710 by the buyer’s bank with advising by the German bank under UCP 600, with presentation, examination and payment under URC 522 documentary collections and ISBP 745 standard banking practice; eUCP 2.0 electronic presentation is accepted where agreed; (3) Standby guarantees and counter-guarantees – MT760 for advance-payment and performance bonds; (4) Enhanced compliance – dual-use export screening under EU Dual-Use Regulation 2021/821, embargo checks, customs classification, and updated licence records maintained for each outbound payment. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. German licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Solaris SE, PPRO Financial Ltd (EU passport), Qonto, and Kontist legally operate under BaFin licenses pursuant to the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG, implementing PSD2) and may open euro and foreign-currency accounts, execute SEPA and SWIFT payments, and issue payment cards. However, they are restricted to payment and e-money services: they cannot legally provide commercial credit, documentary letters of credit, bank guarantees, trade finance, or collateralized FX hedging, and they do not participate in the statutory deposit protection scheme. A licensed EMI is viable only as an auxiliary receivables-collection rail, not as the principal treasury or escrow account for an IVD exporter whose counterparties require Swift LC and risk participation with a full-service credit institution.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) on the corporate tax, giving 15.825% before municipal trade tax; applicable trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) with a typical Hebesatz adds 14-17%, resulting in a combined effective rate of approximately 30-33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% capital income withholding tax plus 1.375% solidarity surcharge) on gross dividends distributed by the GmbH; treaty reduction may apply if the non-resident shareholder is the beneficial owner and holds the required shareholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory monetary threshold applies to all cross-border related-party transactions; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared for material controlled transactions, and tax authorities may impose documentation on audit. For country-by-country reporting, a German ultimate parent is obliged when consolidated group revenue is at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation, but access to treaty benefits under the applicable DTA requires genuine industrial and commercial substance, office premises, employees, and real business purpose. The German general anti-abuse rule (§ 42 AO) and CFC rules under the AStG apply to dismantle shell arrangements; a GmbH manufacturing IVD devices must maintain full operational records, research and development substance, and asset-augmented personnel.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years or a fine under § 370 AO; aggravated cases allow up to ten years' imprisonment. Full underpaid taxes are collected plus 6% annual back interest and a monetary penalty of up to EUR 50,000 or the value of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Managing directors face direct criminal liability and can be personally imprisoned for the full sentence; asset seizure and travel restrictions may be imposed during investigation, and the penalty order can trigger an EU arrest warrant for the directors.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Denial of dividend WHT relief and foreign tax credits; recharacterisation of income under § 42 AO; reassessment of CIT and trade tax with 6% supplementary interest; participation penalties and input VAT denial on invoices issued by the shell.

Director personal liability: Directors acting as mere nominee or de facto managers may be held personally liable for the restructuring of unpaid tax debts under § 69 AO; they can be banned from serving as directors, subjected to travel bans in related criminal proceedings, and in case of insolvency subjected to personal civil liability to creditors under § 43 GmbHG.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: German tax audit penalty of 5% of the taxpayer's adjusted income increase, with a minimum of EUR 5,000, when transfer pricing documents are not produced; income reallocation to recharacterise the transactions under § 162 AO; interest of 6% annually on the additional assessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate TP documentation or who fail to cooperate in a tax audit have a personal obligation to ensure complete and timely documentation; in the event of an uncollectible company tax debt deriving from such non-compliance, they can be personally assessed for unpaid VAT and wage taxes under §§ 34, 69 AO, and may face criminal sanctions if evasion intent is proved.

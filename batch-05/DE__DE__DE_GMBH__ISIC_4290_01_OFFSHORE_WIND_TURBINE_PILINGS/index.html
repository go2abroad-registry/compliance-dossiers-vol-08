Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The GmbH must have a minimum share capital of EUR 25,000. Before registration at least EUR 12,500 (half of the share capital) must be paid in cash on a registered capital custody/escrow account with a credit institution; a bank confirmation (Bankbescheinigung) under Section 8(2) GmbHG must certify that the funds are irrevocably at the free disposal of the managing director. In a one-person GmbH, any unpaid portion must be secured in accordance with Section 7(2) sentence 2 GmbHG; in-kind contributions require full provision and an audited valuation report.

Funding & unlock path: Local fire and building authority inspection including fire-resistance certification for coating sheds; structural and load-clearance certificate for heavy lifting equipment and quay-side storage areas issued by a registered structural engineer (Prüfingenieur); occupational safety coordination under the Construction Site Ordinance (BaustellenV); registration with BG BAU and the competent state labor protection authority for workplace clearance; for offshore installation vessels, recognised organization classification certificate and marine manning safety attestation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT assignment; local Finanzamt (Tax Office) for tax number and corporate income tax; Bundesanzeiger for publication. Portal: ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) and Mein ELSTER. For foreign entities: BZSt VAT registration via Form USt 1 T.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities without a German permanent establishment but making taxable supplies in Germany must register for VAT with the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt). Procedure: (1) Submit application via ELSTER or paper Form USt 1 T; (2) Provide certified copies of incorporation documents, extract from commercial register, and proof of tax residency; (3) BZSt issues a German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) typically within 2-4 weeks; (4) For a German GmbH, the local Finanzamt assigns a Steuernummer (tax number) after receipt of the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire). The GmbH must also register with the Handelsregister and Gewerbeamt before tax registration. The USt-IdNr. is issued by BZSt and is distinct from the Steuernummer.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A German GmbH is subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% plus solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate tax) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at municipal rates (typically 14-17% effective). Territorial_Scope: Germany taxes worldwide income of resident entities, with relief under double tax treaties. Pass_Through_Eligible: No, GmbH is not pass-through. Tax_Exempt_Zone: None for this entity type.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election for German tax purposes. However, a GmbH may elect to be treated as a partnership (Personengesellschaft) only via conversion (Formwechsel) under Umwandlungsgesetz. Hybrid mismatch elections: Germany implemented ATAD II via §§ 4k, 4l EStG and § 14 KStG, but no elective hybrid mismatch regime for pure domestic entities. Option to be taxed as a small corporation (Kleinunternehmerregelung) under § 19 UStG for VAT purposes if turnover below threshold, not applicable for this entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic entities: EUR 22,000 (2024) / EUR 25,000 (2025) turnover in previous calendar year and EUR 50,000 in current year for Kleinunternehmerregelung. For foreign entities making taxable supplies in Germany, no threshold; registration required immediately upon first taxable supply. For intra-Community acquisitions, threshold is EUR 12,500.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU countries are zero-rated under § 4 Nr. 1a UStG and § 6 UStG, provided the exporter provides proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) and the goods leave the EU within 3 months. For offshore services (e.g., installation of wind turbine foundations in German territorial waters or beyond), the place of supply may be outside Germany if the service is directly connected to a foreign territory; otherwise 19% VAT applies. For export of goods, zero-rating requires that the supplier is the exporter of record or has commissioned an indirect representative. For services related to offshore wind installation outside German VAT territory, the place of supply may be where the customer is established (B2B) under § 3a UStG, resulting in non-taxable turnover, not zero-rated. To claim 0% for goods, the invoice must be marked 'Steuerfreie Ausfuhrlieferung'.

Germany mandates electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) for B2B transactions from January 1, 2025, with mandatory receipt and processing; issuance obligations phased from 2027/2028. No real-time clearance or API-based fiscal stamping like Italy or Brazil. E-invoices must comply with EN 16931 and be transmitted via Peppol network or other compliant systems. No digital stamping required. For VAT returns, electronic submission via ELSTER is mandatory. For cross-border B2B services, reverse charge applies and invoice must mention 'Reverse charge' and the customer's USt-IdNr. EC Sales Lists (Zusammenfassende Meldung) must be filed electronically.

Fraudulent export zero-rating or false VAT refund claims are prosecuted under §§ 263, 264 StGB (criminal tax fraud). Penalties include imprisonment up to 10 years for commercial fraud, fines up to EUR 1,000,000, and retrospective clawback of VAT refunds plus interest at 6% per annum (§ 233a AO). The tax office may audit export transactions up to 10 years retrospectively (§ 169 AO). For negligent understatement, fines up to EUR 50,000. Cross-border cooperation via EU VAT fraud networks (Eurofisc) increases detection risk.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full free convertibility of the euro on both current and capital account; Article 63 TFEU guarantees unrestricted payments and capital movements within the EU. Third-country transfers are likewise liberalized, but non-EEA direct or indirect acquisition of the GmbH may be reviewed or restricted where public order or security is affected (energy critical-infrastructure screening). Profit remittance is unrestricted; dividends incur 26.375% German withholding tax (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), reducible under an applicable DTT or refundable for eligible EU parents under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no statutory surrender, no mandated conversion ratio, and no fixed timeline for export proceeds; receipts may be retained freely in any currency. Only statistical reporting applies: cross-border payments from or to non-residents of at least EUR 50,000 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank within 5 calendar days under §67 AWV; German banks routinely submit this report on behalf of account holders.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank and exclusive statistical authority under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance, AWV); BaFin (banking supervision); BAFA/BMWK (export control and foreign-investment screening under §§55-56 AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for core treasury. Fintech EMIs licensed in Germany by BaFin under the ZAG, or passported from other EU jurisdictions under PSD2 / the E-Money Directive, cannot grant commercial credit, take deposits, issue import or standby letters of credit, or hold escrow balances recognized by project lenders. They are viable only for auxiliary purposes: non-resident payroll, small operating expenses, and rapid supplier transfers.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporation tax, producing a combined federal rate of 15.825%. Municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and varies by municipality; the combined corporate and trade tax burden is commonly approximately 30%, but is not a single national rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective domestic rate of 26.375%. Treaty relief may reduce the rate, commonly to 15% or 5% under applicable treaties, subject to treaty wording and beneficial-ownership and anti-abuse requirements. A qualifying EU parent may obtain exemption or reduction under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in German law, subject to ownership, holding-period, substance, and anti-abuse conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer filing without a tax adviser, the annual tax returns are generally due by 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser prepares the returns, the general statutory deadline is generally the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory exceptions, extensions, and temporary rules. Confirm the applicable deadline for the specific assessment year. Quarterly advance payments are generally due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September, and 10 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German transfer-pricing records are transaction- and category-based rather than triggered by one universal threshold. Under the applicable documentation rules, Local File records are generally required for cross-border related-party transactions where annual transaction volume exceeds EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, subject to statutory details and exemptions. A Master File is generally required where the German entity belongs to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 100 million in the preceding financial year. Documentation, submission, and contemporaneous-preparation rules have been amended; verify the rules applicable to the relevant tax year and transaction.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. German tax consequences nevertheless depend on actual facts, including place of effective management, beneficial ownership, business purpose, transfer-pricing functions and risks, anti-treaty-shopping rules, and general anti-abuse provisions. A foreign holding or financing structure without adequate commercial rationale may be denied treaty or statutory benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax filings

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 of the Fiscal Code may be punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years, and in especially serious cases by imprisonment from six months to ten years. Unpaid tax, interest, and administrative consequences remain due. Negligent tax reduction under section 378 may result in an administrative fine of up to EUR 50,000.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal or administrative liability where the statutory elements and personal culpability are established. They may also be personally liable for tax claims under section 69 of the Fiscal Code for culpable breach of tax duties. Civil restitution, travel restrictions, or imprisonment are not automatic and require a legal basis and applicable proceedings.
Failure to withhold or remit payroll and investment-income taxes

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may owe the unpaid withholding tax, interest, late-payment surcharges, and penalties. Intentional withholding-tax evasion may constitute a criminal offence; the applicable criminal and administrative sanctions depend on the conduct and amount.

Director personal liability: Managing directors responsible for withholding and remittance may incur personal liability under section 69 of the Fiscal Code for culpable failure to meet tax obligations, in addition to potential criminal liability.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income, impose interest, and apply documentation penalties. For specified documentation failures, penalties can generally range from EUR 5,000 to EUR 1 million; a percentage-based penalty may apply to an income adjustment where statutory conditions are met. The precise penalty depends on the applicable rule and assessment period.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for intentional or negligent breaches of their tax-compliance duties and may face criminal exposure if the facts amount to tax evasion. A transfer-pricing adjustment alone does not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Tax registration, bookkeeping, and statutory-account filing failures

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include estimated assessments, late-filing surcharges, interest, enforcement measures, and administrative fines. Failure to disclose or deposit annual accounts may result in enforcement proceedings and regulatory fines under the applicable commercial-law rules.

Director personal liability: Managing directors have statutory duties to ensure proper accounting, filing, and publication. Personal liability may arise where a director culpably breaches those duties; sanctions are not automatic solely because a filing is late.
Insolvency-related tax and creditor misconduct

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include civil repayment or damages claims, insolvency-law sanctions, and criminal penalties where the conduct constitutes an offence. German law generally requires an insolvency filing without undue delay and within the applicable maximum period after insolvency or over-indebtedness is established.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal liability for prohibited payments and culpable delay in filing for insolvency, as well as criminal liability where statutory elements are met. Liability is not equivalent to automatic general personal responsibility for all GmbH debts.
Money laundering, sanctions, and prohibited payments

Statutory sanction: Sanctions depend on the underlying offence and may include substantial fines, confiscation, restrictions on business activity, and criminal penalties. AML reporting and customer-due-diligence obligations apply only where the entity or activity falls within the relevant statutory scope.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible personnel may incur personal criminal or administrative liability for their own participation or culpable supervisory failures. Travel bans, imprisonment, or civil restitution are not automatic and require the relevant statutory basis and procedural findings.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) and competent local Finanzamt; online filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a GmbH must first obtain a tax registration certificate (Steuerliche Erfassung) from the local Finanzamt upon submission of the registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung). The Finanzamt issues a local corporate tax number (Steuernummer). A separate VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) must be applied for electronically via the BZSt (Form USt 1 VA) for intra-EU supplies and imports. Foreign entities without a domestic fixed establishment register directly with the BZSt for VAT ID and the local Finanzamt for tax assessment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a GmbH must first obtain a tax registration certificate (Steuerliche Erfassung) from the local Finanzamt upon submission of the registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung). The Finanzamt issues a local corporate tax number (Steuernummer). A separate VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) must be applied for electronically via the BZSt (Form USt 1 VA) for intra-EU supplies and imports. Foreign entities without a domestic fixed establishment register directly with the BZSt for VAT ID and the local Finanzamt for tax assessment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No general check-the-box election is available under German law. The materially relevant elective mechanism is the Organschaft (fiscal unity) election for both corporate income tax/trade tax (under §14 et seq. KStG) and VAT (under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG). This permits consolidation of a financially integrated group entity into one fiscal taxpayer. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules (No. 15 KStG) and CFC rules apply, but no elective hybrid regime exists for a GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for entrepreneurs making taxable supplies. For intra-EU distance sales of goods, a €10,000 annual threshold applies (über §3c UStG). Small business exemption (Kleinunternehmer) up to €22,000 pre-training turnover / €50,000 current turnover is generally inapplicable to foreign-identitied GmbH entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating (VAT exemption with input deduction) applies to deliveries of goods transported from Germany to a third-country destination, including supplies from bonded warehouses. For B2B supplies of aged wine/spirits to a non-EU buyer, the supplier must prove that the goods have physically left the EU within the statutory period. Offshore services to third-country recipients are generally zero-rated where the recipient would have full VAT deduction had it been established in Germany. For intra-EU bonded transfers, the removal to a bonded warehouse in another Member State is VAT-exempt only with a valid VAT ID and ECB/CSD procedure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS movement reference number / Ausfuhranmeldung)
  • Commercial invoice
  • T1/T2 transit document or ocean/airway bill of lading, CMR waybill, or AIS-LBW proof
  • Export accompanying document (EAD) where applicable
  • Inward remittance certificate / evidence of payment in foreign currency
  • Bonded warehouse seizure protocol and customs warehouse records

As of 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B transactions must issue e-invoices in a structured machine-readable format (XInvoice, ZUGFeRD 2.x, or EDI). There is no real-time prevailing-charge fiscal clearance mechanism for every VAT transaction (unlike Italy's Sistema di Interscambio); however, certified fiscal cash registers and per-transaction real-time monitoring (TSE) are required for point-of-sale sales. For exports, customs exports must be declared electronically through ATLAS/ECS. Peppol and other approved networks support interoperable e-invoicing.

The Finanzamt and customs authorities may apply a retrospective audit window of up to 24 months (and longer for gross tax fraud). If export zero-rating is claimed without a complete statutory documentation bundle, the VAT is retrospectively clawed back plus mandatory interest of 0.5% per month under §233a AO. False export declarations constitute tax fraud under §370 AO, subject to fines up to €500,000 for aggravated cases (or higher for serious fraud) and imprisonment of up to 10 years. Additional customs penalties under the Customs Investigation Service (ZKA) may apply for customs-bonded trade, including voidance of the bond and suspension of the warehouse authorisation.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Germany has no general exchange controls, mandatory conversion of foreign-currency receipts, approval requirement for foreign investment, or restriction on dividend, interest, royalty, loan, or liquidation-proceeds remittances. A GmbH may maintain euro and foreign-currency accounts and make cross-border payments, subject to ordinary banking controls, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering requirements, tax compliance, customs and excise documentation, and German foreign-trade statistical reporting. Profit distributions require distributable profits, proper shareholder approval, compliance with the German GmbH capital-maintenance rules, and withholding-tax analysis. Dividends to non-residents are generally subject to German withholding tax unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in Germany, or a domestic exemption, subject to residence, beneficial-ownership, minimum-holding, anti-abuse, and procedural requirements. Interest and royalty remittances may also require withholding-tax analysis. The company should maintain transfer-pricing support for related-party financing, documented commercial purpose for intercompany payments, and evidence of customs and excise compliance for bonded wine and spirits transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds, surrender foreign-currency receipts, convert export proceeds into euros, or convert a prescribed percentage of receipts. There is no general statutory deadline for conversion of export proceeds. Export receipts may remain in foreign-currency accounts or offshore accounts, subject to accurate accounting, tax reporting, sanctions controls, and bank documentation. Cross-border payments and positions may be reportable to Deutsche Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV) and the Bundesbank reporting framework. The applicable threshold, exemptions, filing method, and reporting deadline depend on the transaction type and reporting period; the company must confirm the current rules before filing. Trade payments and certain other categories may be exempt or subject to separate reporting treatment. The company should obtain a current Bundesbank classification for capital contributions, shareholder loans, intercompany settlements, treasury transfers, and financial-account positions rather than applying a generic export-receipt rule.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin) responsible for supervision of regulated financial institutions and payment service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment execution and account connectivity; low as a substitute for a German relationship bank, deposit-taking institution, or trade-finance provider. An electronic-money institution may issue electronic money and provide payment services, including SEPA services where supported, but normally does not provide protected bank deposits or full commercial-bank credit and documentary-credit capacity. An EEA EMI or payment institution may passport services into Germany subject to its home-state authorization and applicable notification requirements. A non-EEA offshore EMI cannot lawfully target German customers or conduct regulated payment services in Germany merely through an offshore website; it generally requires an EU/EEA authorization, a licensed German or EEA partner, or another legally valid access structure. Client funds must be safeguarded under the applicable payment-services regime. EMI onboarding remains subject to beneficial-owner, source-of-funds, transaction-purpose, sanctions, geographic-risk, and alcohol-trade controls. Use of an EMI should be limited to low-risk collection, payout, SEPA, and treasury-connectivity functions unless the provider confirms the relevant authorization and safeguarding model.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route share-capital contributions through the GmbH's designated German capital account from an account held in the legal shareholder's name. Use a clear payment reference such as 'share capital contribution pursuant to formation deed dated [date]' and identify the GmbH, shareholder, amount, currency, and purpose. Route shareholder loans under an executed loan agreement, with a separate reference identifying principal, drawdown date, maturity, and borrower. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for eligible euro transfers and SWIFT cross-border wire transfer for non-SEPA currencies or jurisdictions. Provide the receiving bank with the formation deed, shareholder resolution, loan agreement if applicable, source-of-funds evidence, ownership chart, and expected-payment profile before remittance. Germany does not generally require a foreign-exchange approval or universal capital-inflow purpose code; any bank-requested purpose classification is an operational control rather than a German exchange-control license. Review each payment under the AWV and Bundesbank reporting rules, including current thresholds and exemptions, and retain the bank confirmation and underlying legal documents. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payment execution and account connectivity; low as a substitute for a German relationship bank, deposit-taking institution, or trade-finance provider. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT amount plus municipal trade tax of 14.0%-17.5%, producing an aggregate nominal corporate tax burden of approximately 29.8%-33.3%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% capital-gains tax on dividend distributions plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withheld tax, producing a domestic aggregate withholding burden of 26.375%; treaty relief may reduce this rate to 0%-15% depending on the applicable DTA and ownership level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR. Local File is mandatory for cross-border controlled transactions exceeding EUR 5,000,000 aggregate annual transaction volume; a German Local File is also expected for significant domestic related-party transactions where tax allocation is affected. Master File is mandatory when German group revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000 in group revenue; Country-by-Country Reporting follows the EU/OECD threshold of consolidated group revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations in the Gulf-type ESR format, but it requires and assumes genuine economic substance for treaty eligibility, anti-abuse protection and transfer pricing. A German GmbH engaged in bonded wine and spirits wholesale must maintain its own qualified staff, management decisions, warehouse or stock-holding functions, inventory risk, accounting records, banking relationships and commercial substance evidence. Letterbox entities and sham arrangements are vulnerable to §42 AO, ATAD anti-abuse rules, CFC rules under §§7-13 AStG, and DTA denial, leading to profit recharacterisation and retroactive excise and income-tax assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Failure to Declare True Taxable Income (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years for basic tax evasion, up to ten years in aggravated cases; criminal fines; full recovery of underpaid taxes plus 6% per annum interest; denial of statutory amnesty unless a valid self-disclosure under §371 AO exists; revocation of bonded warehouse licence; confiscation of dutiable goods.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally assessed under §§34, 69 AO for unpaid corporate or excise taxes caused by intentional or grossly negligent conduct; additional criminal prosecution, asset seizure, travel restrictions/exit denial, and prohibition from serving as directors; corporate veil may be pierced under §64 GmbHG and §§823(2), 826 BGB in third-party damages claims.
Late Filing, Non-Filing and Non-Payment of Advance Taxes

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalty up to EUR 25,000; late payment surcharge of 1% per month; default interest of 6% per annum; estimated tax assessment on an unfavourable basis; enforcement through bank attachments, seizure of bonded inventory and compulsory winding-up petitions.

Director personal liability: Directors are responsible for ensuring that tax liabilities are paid from company assets; overdue tax caused by negligence or deliberate omission creates personal statutory liability under §69 AO, including for solids, surcharges and collection costs.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's-Length Pricing

Statutory sanction: Burden of proof reverses against the taxpayer under §162 AO; taxable income can be estimated upward; mandatory documentation surcharge of EUR 500 to EUR 25,000; potential denial of expense deductibility; interest on additional assessment; referral for criminal prosecution if intentional evasion is established.

Director personal liability: The managing director who controls pricing and documentation can be directly liable for resulting corporate tax shortfalls; criminal culpability arises if the director knowingly approved false transfer pricing documents; professional disqualification and travel bans may follow in serious tax investigations.

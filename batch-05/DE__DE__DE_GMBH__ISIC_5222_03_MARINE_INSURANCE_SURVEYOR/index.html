E-government filing portal

1) Foreign entity registers with competent Finanzamt (for non-established entrepreneurs generally Finanzamt Neugersdorf) using Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung via Mein ELSTER or paper. 2) Obtain German Steuernummer. 3) Apply to BZSt for USt-IdNr using Formular USt 1 T or BZSt online procedure. 4) Appoint an Empfangsbevollmächtigter if required for service of tax documents. 5) Register for trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) with municipality if German permanent establishment. 6) File ongoing VAT returns; no general fiscal representative required but authorized recipient may be mandatory.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. GmbH is subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 15.825%) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective ~7%–17% depending on municipality). Resident companies taxed on worldwide income; non-resident entities on German-source income. Not a pass-through; no tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box. German classification follows civil-law form. Available elections/options: Organschaft fiscal unity for CIT and trade tax; Kleinunternehmer VAT scheme under §19 UStG; Ist-Versteuerung cash accounting under §20 UStG; Dauerfristverlängerung under §46 UStDV; option to treat certain real-estate supplies as taxable under §9 UStG; no hybrid mismatch election; anti-hybrid rules under §4k EStG and ATAD apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Resident small businesses: €25,000 prior-year turnover and €100,000 current-year turnover under §19 UStG. Non-established foreign entities making taxable supplies in Germany generally must register upon first taxable supply; no domestic small-business threshold unless EU/EEA conditions are met. Intra-EU distance sales threshold: €10,000 EU-wide.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: direct export to non-EU third countries under §4 Nr. 1a UStG and §6 UStG; intra-EU B2B supplies under §4 Nr. 1b UStG with valid customer USt-IdNr and correct Zusammenfassende Meldung. Services: B2B cross-border services are generally not taxable in Germany under §3a UStG (reverse charge or place of supply outside Germany); certain services directly connected to export may be exempt under §4 UStG. For marine insurance surveyor services to foreign underwriters, treatment is typically out-of-scope/reverse-charge rather than German zero-rated, unless specific export-related exemption applies. Input VAT refunds for zero-rated exports require proof and regular VAT return filing.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) and ATLAS exit confirmation (Ausgangsvermerk)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note
  • Commercial invoice showing VAT number, HS code and export destination
  • Packing list and transport/insurance documents
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment proof
  • Customer USt-IdNr and valid VAT identification evidence for intra-EU supplies
  • Zusammenfassende Meldung confirmation for intra-EU B2B zero-rated supplies
  • Books/records proving export and input VAT deduction

B2B e-invoicing: mandatory ability to receive structured e-invoices from 1 Jan 2025; issuance obligation phased: businesses with turnover >€800,000 from 1 Jan 2027, all businesses from 1 Jan 2028; paper or non-compliant invoices may remain permissible during transitional periods. No real-time fiscal clearance or central stamping. E-invoices must comply with EN 16931, XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.1+, and be transmitted/archived in structured format.

Germany imposes no general statutory FX repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Fraudulent export zero-rating triggers retroactive VAT assessment, 6% p.a. interest under §238 AO, late-payment surcharges, and possible criminal liability for tax evasion under §370 AO (fines; imprisonment up to 10 years in serious cases). Customs clawback, revocation of export privileges, and 10-year record-retention audits apply.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro maintains full convertibility under EU regulations for both current account transactions and capital account movements involving German entities. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted within the EU, subject only to standard AML/KYC verification and applicable tax withholding. No capital controls or prior approvals are required for routine corporate operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Service-based receipts such as marine insurance surveying fees constitute current account transactions. No mandatory surrender or forced conversion to euro applies. Funds may be credited directly to the corporate account; however, inflows exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) under AML rules with supporting documentation.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank and European Central Bank (ECB)

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.00% German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.50% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal burden of 15.825%; municipal trade tax generally increases the combined burden to approximately 22.83%–32.13%, depending on the municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.00% German capital-income withholding tax plus a 5.50% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic burden of 26.375%. For qualifying corporate shareholders within the EU, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive regime may permit a 0% rate subject to minimum holding, minimum holding-period, substance, subject-to-tax and anti-abuse conditions, normally supported by an exemption certificate. German domestic participation exemption and treaty rates may provide other relief depending on the recipient and facts.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due seven months after the end of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year GmbH, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year. Where a professional tax adviser prepares the return, the statutory extended deadline is generally the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory exceptions, administrative restrictions and possible early filing requirements.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Dividend withholding tax is generally remitted to the competent tax office by the 10th day after the month in which the dividend was paid or made available. Section 50a withholding tax is generally declared and paid by the 10th day after the end of the relevant quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory extraordinary documentation thresholds generally arise where annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 6,000,000 for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing, royalties and similar transactions. A Master File is generally required for a German entity belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 100,000,000 in the preceding financial year. Documentation must be prepared in accordance with German transfer-pricing rules and furnished to the tax authorities within 30 days of request, subject to special shorter periods for extraordinary transactions.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone regime formally equivalent to a general Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance is nevertheless relevant under transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, Section 50d(3) anti-treaty-shopping rules, general anti-abuse provisions, controlled foreign company rules, place-of-management rules and treaty principal-purpose or anti-abuse standards. A GmbH should maintain genuine German management, personnel, premises, decision-making, operational capability, commercial risk control, banking functionality and contemporaneous records proportionate to its marine survey and claims-inspection activities. A nominal conduit with no operational capacity may lose treaty relief and may trigger tax reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code may be punished by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Unpaid tax, interest and ancillary charges remain recoverable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur criminal liability, tax liability for culpable failure to satisfy tax obligations under Section 69 of the Fiscal Code, civil claims, disqualification and insolvency-related liability. Travel restrictions may be ordered in criminal or enforcement proceedings where legally justified; imprisonment is possible for qualifying intentional conduct.
Late, Incomplete or Incorrect Filing

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharges, compulsory penalties, estimated assessments, late-payment surcharges, tax interest and administrative fines may apply. Persistent non-compliance may lead to criminal investigation where intent or recklessness is established.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for tax losses caused by culpable non-compliance, especially where taxes were payable but funds were diverted or returns were not filed.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make transfer-pricing adjustments, deny deductions, recharacterize payments as hidden profit distributions, impose withholding tax, charge interest and apply documentation-related surcharges. A documentation surcharge may be imposed for missing, unusable or late documentation, potentially up to EUR 1,000,000 subject to statutory calculation rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal tax liability for intentional or negligent tax damage and criminal exposure if transfer-pricing records were deliberately falsified or used to evade tax.
Dividend and Royalty Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may be assessed for the unpaid withholding tax and solidarity surcharge, together with interest, late-payment surcharges and penalties. The payment recipient's treaty refund may be denied.

Director personal liability: Responsible managing directors may be personally liable under Section 69 of the Fiscal Code for culpable failure to withhold and remit. Intentional conduct may constitute tax evasion or aiding tax evasion.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Treaty or EU exemption may be denied under anti-abuse rules, and the payment may be reassessed at domestic withholding rates with interest and penalties. General anti-abuse provisions and beneficial-ownership principles may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal civil and tax liability, criminal exposure for deliberate misrepresentation, disqualification consequences and potential enforcement restrictions. A corporate veil is not automatically pierced, but statutory personal-liability provisions apply independently of veil piercing.
Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: The payment may be added back to the GmbH's taxable income, treated as a hidden profit distribution, become subject to dividend withholding tax and solidarity surcharge, and generate interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face liability for tax shortfalls, breach of duties to the GmbH, repayment claims and insolvency-related claims. Criminal liability may arise where the conduct is fraudulent or tax-evasive.
Insolvency and Preferential Tax Payments

Statutory sanction: Civil repayment claims, insolvency administrator claims, tax assessments, penalties and criminal insolvency proceedings may arise. Failure to file for insolvency in due time may constitute a criminal offense under German insolvency law.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited payments after insolvency maturity, delayed filing, unpaid taxes and social-security contributions. Imprisonment and fines are possible for serious intentional or negligent insolvency offenses.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial-Ownership Failures

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, supervisory measures, reporting restrictions and criminal money-laundering exposure may apply. Suspicious transactions may be frozen or reported to the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance-responsible persons may incur personal administrative or criminal liability for intentional or grossly negligent AML failures. Travel restrictions or imprisonment may occur only where ordered under applicable criminal or enforcement procedures.
Unlicensed or Misrepresented Marine Insurance Activity

Statutory sanction: Regulatory prohibition orders, administrative fines, disgorgement, contractual invalidity risks and criminal sanctions for unauthorized regulated activity may apply. The competent authority may be BaFin or another authority depending on the activity.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unauthorized operations, consumer or insurer losses, regulatory breaches and intentional misrepresentation. Criminal fines or imprisonment may apply where the statutory offense is satisfied.
False Invoicing and Claims-Fraud Facilitation

Statutory sanction: Potential offenses include fraud, document offenses, aiding tax evasion and money laundering, with restitution, asset seizure, fines and imprisonment depending on the conduct and amount involved.

Director personal liability: Directors and participating employees may face criminal prosecution, civil restitution, confiscation, professional disqualification and imprisonment. Corporate limited liability does not protect individuals from their own offenses.
Failure to Maintain Economic Substance and Records

Statutory sanction: Treaty relief, deductions and tax residence assertions may be denied; income may be reallocated; withholding tax may be imposed; and penalties and interest may accrue.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for inaccurate filings, unsupported treaty claims, tax losses and breach of management duties. Personal criminal liability depends on proof of intentional or reckless conduct.

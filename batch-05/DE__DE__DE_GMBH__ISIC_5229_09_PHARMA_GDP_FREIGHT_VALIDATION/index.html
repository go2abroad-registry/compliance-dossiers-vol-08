Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A GmbH must have a German registered office and a domestic business address capable of receiving official notices under sections 4a and 8 GmbHG. A serviced or virtual office may be acceptable if it provides a genuine registered business address, reliable document receipt, access for authorities and sufficient operational evidence. It is high risk for pharmaceutical GDP logistics if used as the sole operational location. GDP activities generally require documented quality systems, responsible personnel, traceability, suitable storage or controlled logistics arrangements, deviation and recall procedures, and—where the company conducts wholesale dealing in medicinal products—authorization under section 52a of the German Medicinal Products Act (Arzneimittelgesetz, AMG). A virtual office does not replace any required warehouse, transport, quality or responsible-person infrastructure.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no general nationwide statutory name-reservation procedure or uniform 30-day reservation period for a GmbH. Before notarization, obtain a written preliminary assessment from the competent local Chamber of Industry and Commerce and conduct a register search. If the notary or register court rejects the name, request the exact deficiency in writing, amend the name or provide evidence of distinctiveness and authorization, and resubmit through the notary. A formal register-court decision may be challenged using the legal-remedy instruction and time limit stated in that decision, generally by an immediate complaint under the German Act on Proceedings in Family Matters and in Matters of Voluntary Jurisdiction (FamFG). Separate trademark conflicts should be assessed through the German Patent and Trade Mark Office and EUIPO; register acceptance does not create trademark clearance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office) for VAT identification; local Finanzamt (Tax Office) for income/trade tax registration and compliance; official digital portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt Online (www.bzst.bund.de)

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH is incorporated by notarial deed with capital contribution, filed in the commercial register (Handelsregister), thereby obtaining legal existence. The managing director submits the tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') to the locally competent Finanzamt; the Finanzamt assigns the tax number (Steuernummer) and domestic VAT number for intra-community transactions. The statutory EU-wide VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is applied separately to the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern via BZSt-Online, requiring submitted identification, postal/EU-where-established data, scope of supplies, and the Steuernummer. Foreign non-established entities must additionally complete the standard non-established business registration form at the Finanzamt Neukölln or designated local office; processing generally takes 2–6 weeks and is issued free of charge.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A GmbH is a domestic corporation subject to full entity-level German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% federal plus solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate tax), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at roughly 14–17% effective local rate. It is fiscally resident in Germany if registered seat or place of effective management is in Germany; no territorial exemption for German-derived profits and no tax-exempt-zone treatment.

Available election forms

Germany does not allow check-the-box entity classification; the GmbH's status as a body corporate is unconditionally fixed for corporate tax, trade tax, and VAT. Elective mechanisms include: (1) Organschaft (fiscal unity/group taxation) under KStG/GewStG via a domination and profit-and-loss transfer agreement that pools income, trade tax, and often VAT; (2) VAT fiscal unity (umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft) under UStG §2 where a financially integrated wholly-owned subsidiary is treated as part of the controlling entity; (3) hybrid mismatch elections are not available but EU ATAD hybrid rules are directly applicable, requiring party-claim documentation. No branch exemption elective exists for domestic losses.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No net economic-turnover threshold for non-established/foreign taxable persons: VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply, intra-Community acquisition, or imported service that creates a German tax nexus. Domestic small resident entrepreneurs (Kleinunternehmer) may be exempt if prior-year turnover does not exceed €22,000 and expected current-year turnover does not exceed €50,000, but this exemption typically does not apply to foreign non-resident entities, and opt-in (waiver) is possible under UStG §19(2).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating on exports (Ausfuhrlieferung) applies under UStG §4(1)(a) in conjunction with §6 where the seller or its commissioned freight carrier physically removes the goods from the EU customs territory, the buyer or a third party transports the goods, and the seller provides documentary proof of export. Freight forwarding and GDP logistics services (ISIC 5229, NAICS 488510) that directly relate to goods being exported to third-country destinations can likewise be zero-rated as auxiliary transport services under UStG §4(1)(b), §6(1), and §7, provided the services are supplied to the export customer or a principal in an export chain and the physical external transport/export evidence is preserved. Intra-EU Transport is separately zero-rated under §6a with valid VAT IDs and further commercial evidence.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Summary Declaration / Electronic Declaration D) within the legally prescribed period
  • Customs Export Data Handout / export accompanying document certified by customs authorities
  • Bill of Lading (sea), CMR consignment note (road), Air Waybill (air), or combined-transport through bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or commercial bank credit evidence confirming receipt of foreign-currency consideration from the non-EU customer
  • Commercial invoice with mandatory export-related clauses (Incoterms, goods description, gross weight)
  • Proof of physical export (e.g., exit stamps, terminal gate report, ATLAS arrival notification)
  • GDP/pharma freight permits and licensed-transporter documentation establishing the authorized GDP chain for medicinal products
  • Contract/file of commission and forwarding services proving the direct link to the exported goods

Germany has implemented a mandatory B2B e-invoicing framework effective 1 January 2025: every taxable business established in Germany must be technically able to receive structured electronic invoices in a standardised format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X). Issuance obligations phase in from 1 January 2027 for taxpayers with prior-year turnover exceeding €800,000, and for all taxable businesses from 1 January 2028. There is no real-time tax-authority clearance or mandatory fiscal stamping/API pre-validation; invoices are transmitted privately (e.g., via PEPPOL-compatible networks) and kept available for audit. The new regime applies in parallel to the older 13-year archival of all invoice data; failure to maintain structured data may trigger penalties.

German tax authorities verify the correctness of zero-ratings during VAT audits (Außenprüfung) with a heavy evidentiary burden on the freight/GDP exporter. If documentation is incomplete or inconsistent, the zero-rating is retroactively denied, and 19% VAT is assessed (increased to 16%? fixed at 19%) plus monthly late-payment interest of 0.5% under AO §233a. Administrative penalties can reach 10% of the evaded amount for negligent underdeclaration (AO §378), while intentional fraudulent zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by fines and imprisonment up to 10 years, and triggers cumulative liability for all partners/directors. Section 26 UStG additionally provides separate regulatory fines. German customs may also recover export refunds and levy customs-duty-equivalent penalties with parallel criminal referral to the Customs Investigation Office (UZK).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates under full capital account convertibility within the Eurozone for intra-EU transactions. Profit remittances to non-residents are permitted without prior approval provided tax clearance is obtained. For third-country dealings, capital controls remain minimal; foreign exchange is freely transferable subject to AML/KYC and reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For intra-EU exports, no surrender obligation applies under free movement of goods and payments. For non-EU exports, statutory repatriation timeline is four weeks; proceeds must be surrendered to an authorized bank with conversion at prevailing market rates unless otherwise exempted.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal burden of 15.825%. German trade tax is imposed separately by the municipality; the combined effective burden is commonly approximately 23% to 33%, depending on the municipal multiplier.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective rate of 26.375% for a standard dividend paid to an individual or other recipient without relief. A German corporate recipient generally may claim the participation exemption subject to statutory conditions. Dividends to an EU parent may qualify for withholding-tax exemption under the EU Parent-Subsidiary regime if the minimum participation, holding-period, beneficial-ownership, anti-abuse, and substance requirements are met. Treaty rates commonly reduce dividend withholding to 5%, 10%, or 15%, depending on the treaty and recipient status.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For an ordinary calendar fiscal year, filing is generally due by 31 July of the following year when filed without a tax adviser. Where a tax adviser is engaged, the statutory adviser deadline is generally the last day of February of the second subsequent year, subject to statutory transitional rules and administrative extensions. A fiscal year differing from the calendar year is filed within the corresponding statutory period after year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The filing deadline generally follows the corporate income tax return deadline: normally seven months after fiscal year-end without an adviser and, where applicable, the statutory adviser deadline in the following or second subsequent year under the Fiscal Code and transitional rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under the Fiscal Code and GAufzV. A Local File is generally required for material cross-border controlled transactions where the annual transaction volume exceeds EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for services and other transactions. A Master File is generally required for a German entity belonging to a multinational group where the German entity's annual revenue exceeds EUR 100 million. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to an ultimate parent or surrogate parent of a multinational group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million, subject to reporting rules.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone regime called Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain low-tax jurisdictions. Nevertheless, German anti-abuse rules, the foreign-tax law, treaty anti-abuse provisions, beneficial-ownership requirements, transfer-pricing rules, controlled-foreign-company rules, and general business-purpose principles require genuine commercial substance. A German GmbH should maintain real decision-making, qualified personnel, premises or operational capability appropriate to its GDP logistics functions, risk control, assets, licenses, records, and arm's-length governance. A conduit or shell arrangement may be denied treaty or tax benefits and may create withholding-tax, transfer-pricing, tax-evasion, and director-liability exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code is punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine. Particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax, interest, confiscation, and restitution may be imposed in addition.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal prosecution, personal tax liability for culpable breach of tax duties under Section 69 of the Fiscal Code, asset seizure, confiscation, director disqualification, and civil claims. Germany has no automatic tax-related travel ban; arrest or movement restrictions require separate procedural grounds.
Late, incomplete, or inaccurate tax filing

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharges, tax estimates, tax interest, enforcement costs, reassessment, and potential administrative or criminal proceedings. Intentional conduct can constitute tax evasion; negligent conduct can trigger separate fiscal penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors are responsible for organizing tax compliance and may become personally liable for unpaid assessed taxes, withholding taxes, and surcharges where the breach was culpable and payment resources were mishandled.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may estimate income, make a transfer-pricing adjustment, impose additional corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and trade tax, and apply a documentation surcharge. The surcharge can generally be 5% to 10% of the income adjustment, subject to statutory minimums, with separate consequences for late or non-submission.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for culpable tax damage, disclosure failures, and criminal tax-evasion proceedings where transfer-pricing records were deliberately falsified or concealed.
Hidden profit distribution and prohibited shareholder benefit

Statutory sanction: Reclassification as a hidden profit distribution, denial of deductions, withholding-tax assessment including solidarity surcharge, corporate tax and trade-tax adjustments, interest, and possible tax-evasion sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face repayment claims under corporate law, tax liability for withholding failures, and civil liability to the GmbH and creditors.
VAT fraud and missing-trader or carousel transactions

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, interest, denial of input-tax deduction, tax penalties, criminal tax-evasion prosecution, and confiscation. Serious VAT fraud may produce imprisonment of up to ten years under the Fiscal Code.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for VAT and face criminal prosecution, asset seizure, confiscation, and civil claims.
Withholding-tax failure on dividends, royalties, interest, or payroll

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may owe the unwithheld tax, solidarity surcharge, interest, penalties, and enforcement costs. Deliberate non-remittance can constitute tax evasion or withholding-tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable under Section 69 of the Fiscal Code for culpable failure to withhold or remit tax and may face criminal sanctions.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial-ownership violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act may reach up to EUR 150,000 for certain breaches and, for serious or repeated specified breaches, the higher statutory maximum of up to EUR 5 million or 2% of total group annual turnover, depending on the violation. Money laundering itself carries criminal sanctions, generally imprisonment of up to five years or a fine, with aggravated cases carrying higher penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible compliance officers may face individual fines, criminal prosecution, confiscation, professional consequences, and civil claims. No automatic travel ban applies, but arrest or other measures may arise in criminal proceedings.
Sanctions, export-control, and pharmaceutical logistics breach

Statutory sanction: Violations may trigger substantial administrative fines, confiscation, transaction prohibitions, license consequences, and criminal imprisonment under German foreign-trade and sanctions legislation. Applicable penalty levels depend on the specific restriction and intent.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted for intentional or negligent control failures, face fines, imprisonment, confiscation, professional disqualification, and civil claims.
GDP, pharmaceutical wholesale, and transport-license non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Regulatory orders, product recalls, suspension or withdrawal of licenses, administrative fines, loss of authorization, seizure, and criminal penalties may apply under the German Medicines Act, GDP requirements, and applicable transport legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal criminal and administrative liability for intentional or negligent safety and licensing failures, together with civil claims for product damage and regulatory losses.
Insolvency delay and creditor prejudice

Statutory sanction: Under the Insolvency Code, an insolvency petition generally must be filed without undue delay and no later than three weeks after illiquidity or six weeks after over-indebtedness, subject to statutory conditions. Breach may lead to imprisonment of up to three years or a fine, avoidance of transactions, director repayment liability, and creditor claims.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for post-insolvency payments, tax and social-security failures, creditor losses, and criminal offenses; disqualification from managing a GmbH may also follow.
Payroll tax and social-security withholding failure

Statutory sanction: Additional wage tax, interest, social-security assessments, fines, and criminal liability may apply. Intentional non-payment of employee social-security contributions can carry imprisonment of up to five years or a fine, with higher exposure in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid wage tax and employee social-security contributions and may face criminal prosecution and civil recovery actions.

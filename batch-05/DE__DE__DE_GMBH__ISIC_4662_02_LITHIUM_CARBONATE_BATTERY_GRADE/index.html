Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) and local state tax offices (Finanzämter); primary digital portal ELSTER (www.elster.de) for tax filings and the BZSt online platform (www.bzst.de) for European VAT Identification Number (USt-IdNr.) applications

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH is statutorily registered ex officio. Upon commercial register entry (Handelsregister), the local Finanzamt assigns a combined corporate income tax/trade tax/VAT tax number (Steuernummer) after receipt of the notarised registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) submitted via ELSTER. The USt-IdNr. is applied for separately from BZSt through Formular USt 1 C (online), requiring a copy of the trade register extract and the Steuernummer. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the GmbH is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer at 15.0%) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer at effective 7%–17.5% dependent on municipality), and is a VAT taxable person under Section 2 UStG for all worldwide business activities with no territorial restriction; dividend distributions to shareholders attract 26.375% withholding tax (25.0% plus 5.5% surcharge) unless treaty relief applies.

Available election forms

Germany does not apply a check-the-box classification regime for domestic corporations. Elective structuring instruments include: (i) Organschaft fiscal unity under Section 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG (VAT) and Sections 14–19 KStG (income tax), requiring 95% capital and voting control and a written profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) registered in the commercial register; (ii) hybrid mismatch elective adjustments under Sections 4k–4n EStG (ATAD implementation) to neutralise payment deductibility mismatches; (iii) exceptional election under Section 21a UStG for group remuneration for services not otherwise taxable at 0%; and (iv) the small-business renunciation election under Section 19 UStG, unavailable to a GmbH conducting export trade.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for German-established taxable persons — VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction. For EU distance sales and intra-community acquisitions, the cross-border low-value registration threshold is EUR 10,000 per calendar year (Section 3 Abs. 3a UStG); the domestic small-business exemption applies below EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover (EUR 25,000 from 2025), but is fatally incompatible with cross-border procurement arbitrage activity of a GmbH.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies qualify for zero-rated VAT treatment under Section 4 No. 1 letter a in conjunction with Section 6 UStG where the goods (HS 2836.91, battery-grade lithium carbonate) are physically transported from the German customs territory to a destination outside the European Union within 3 months (extension permitted), conveyance being effected by the supplier, the non-EU buyer, or a commissioned logistics provider, and evidenced by a customs export declaration accepted by a German customs office (ATLAS/ICE-Gate output); zero-rating also applies to supplies of offshore services collated with goods under Section 4 No. 3 UStG where consumed outside the Union.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung via ATLAS with EX-A EAD and MRN)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note proving departure from EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the GmbH's German bank account)
  • EORI number evidence and export-shipper status confirmation

From 1 January 2025, the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz) mandates B2B e-invoicing in a structured electronic format (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.0/2.3, or Peppol BIS 3.0 EN 16931). Issuance obligations are phased: entrepreneurs with >EUR 800,000 taxable turnover in 2026 must issue e-invoices from 2027; all other taxable persons from 2028. Real-time fiscal clearance is not yet implemented domestically but is phased in via the EU ViDA framework; the German OSS (One-Stop-Shop) portal at BZSt clears distance sales electronically. Digital stamping is not applied for B2B invoices; the mandate requires EDI transport via Peppol networks or authenticated API channels with digital signing.

False or fraudulent export zero-rating triggers retrospective clawback of input VAT deduction, interest at 6% per annum under Section 233a AO, late-payment surcharges at 1% per month, penalty assessments of up to 10% of the evaded VAT, and criminal prosecution under Section 370 AO (tax evasion) with imprisonment up to 10 years or a monetary fine; moreover, intentional misdeclaration invokes joint and several liability of intermediaries under Section 25d UStG, customs anti-fraud enforcement by the German customs police (ZKA), and EU-wide alerting mechanisms under OLAF cooperation agreements.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully freely convertible with no exchange-control restrictions in Germany. Germany provides complete liberalisation of both capital-account and current-account transactions under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the domestic AWG/AWV. A German GmbH may freely receive equity injections, shareholder loans, trade payments, and third-party financing in any currency, and may freely remit dividends, loan interest, royalties, and repatriated capital to non-resident shareholders without prior central-bank approval. The only applicable frictions are (a) obligatory statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border payments in excess of EUR 12,500, (b) the need to ensure sanctions screening and AML compliance, and (c) the practical requirement to document beneficial ownership and anti-money-laundering (GwG) compliance. Profit remittance hurdles are therefore limited to regulatory due diligence, not convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no fixed repatriation timeline for export receipts. Inbound proceeds of cross-border sales may be collected and retained in EUR or any other freely convertible currency. The only binding obligations are statistical: any resident enterprise receiving a payment from a non-resident with a countervalue of at least EUR 12,500 (or equivalent) must ensure that the payment is reported to Deutsche Bundesbank within seven calendar days (AWV § 60 et seq., payment-statistics reporting), ordinarily effected via the receiving bank in its role as reporting agent. Trade-finance transactions with comprehensively sanctioned jurisdictions (e.g., Russia, Belarus, Iran, North Korea, Crimea) require export/import licence checks and may be prohibited; for all other jurisdictions, conversion and repatriation are unrestricted.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), acting in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) for euro-area monetary policy; external trade and payments monitoring administered under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), with regulatory enforcement and financial sanctions powers vested in BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) and the German Customs Enforcement Authorities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. EEA-licensed electronic-money institutions and fintech payment firms are legally permissible under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG) where the EMI holds a valid e-money/payment institution licence with EEA passport or a direct BaFin licence; however, for a commodity-trading GmbH dealing in lithium carbonate procurement, offshore digital EMIs (UK post-Brexit, Singapore, US, or crypto-based payment firms operating without an EEA passport) are not viable as primary treasury or clearing rails. The critical legal limitations are: (a) absence of deposit-taking and LC-financing capability, (b) inability to provide true documentary letter-of-credit or SBLC confirmation services, (c) limited SWIFT correspondent depth for USD/CNY settlement, and (d) higher statutory requirement for bank-segregated accounts. A novel multi-currency EMT can serve only as a supplementary collection channel for low-value invoices, subject to the bank's know-your-customer (KYC) and corporate-residency verification, and may not replace a Tier-1 universal bank relationship for import letters of credit, export proceeds collection, or AWV statistical reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to DE_GMBH must execute over the SWIFT MT103 network in EUR (using the GMT target or T2/TARGET2 settlement), with the payment benefit located to the GmbH's designated operational account. Compliant routing requires: (a) the remitter to be the same legal shareholder as in the notarised capital action; (b) the payment purpose code to be drawn from the bank's statistical catalogue identifying a capital contribution (equity injection) or shareholder loan; (c) a precise beneficiary reference containing the GmbH name, Commercial Register number (HRB), and remittance reference; (d) the notarised capital-increase resolution (or loan agreement) being delivered to the account-holding bank before the wire arrives; (e) bank verification of source-of-funds with supporting account statement of the shareholder; (f) where applicable, verification of pre-approval for foreign investment control (AWV merger control or investment screening) under Regulation (EU) 2019/452 if the transaction risks public order or security; and (g) the creation, by the bank, of the required statistical record to Deutsche Bundesbank via the Isi-Online portal where the cross-border payment reaches the EUR 12,500 threshold. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. EEA-licensed electronic-money institutions and fintech payment firms are legally permissible under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG) where the EMI holds a valid e-money/payment institution licence with EEA passport or a direct BaFin licence; however, for a commodity-trading GmbH dealing in lithium carbonate procurement, offshore digital EMIs (UK post-Brexit, Singapore, US, or crypto-based payment firms operating without an EEA passport) are not viable as primary treasury or clearing rails. The critical legal limitations are: (a) absence of deposit-taking and LC-financing capability, (b) inability to provide true documentary letter-of-credit or SBLC confirmation services, (c) limited SWIFT correspondent depth for USD/CNY settlement, and (d) higher statutory requirement for bank-segregated accounts. A novel multi-currency EMT can serve only as a supplementary collection channel for low-value invoices, subject to the bank's know-your-customer (KYC) and corporate-residency verification, and may not replace a Tier-1 universal bank relationship for import letters of credit, export proceeds collection, or AWV statistical reporting.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) on the CIT amount, producing a statutory surcharge-adjusted rate of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at Hebesatz-dependent rates between 7% and 17% raises the combined nominal corporate tax burden to approximately 29.8%–33.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory capital gains tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) on gross dividend distributions, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (0.825% when calculated on the 25% withholding) resulting in a combined statutory withholding burden of 26.375%; additional church tax surcharges may apply to individuals but are irrelevant for corporate non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return is generally due on 31 July of the year following the fiscal year; where prepared by a qualified German tax advisor, an automatic extension to 31 July of the second calendar year after the tax year applies; further discretionary extensions may be granted by the local Finanzamt.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally the same statutory deadline as the CIT return: 31 July of the following calendar year; tax-advisor preparation extends the deadline to 31 July of the second following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 annual cross-border controlled goods transactions threshold for mandatory Local File preparation; Master File obligations arise for multinational groups with consolidated revenues exceeding EUR 100 million; lower statutory de minimis thresholds apply to intercompany financing, service, IP licence and management-fee transactions (commonly EUR 500,000 to EUR 1 million), requiring contemporaneous documentation and benchmark studies in the German documentation language.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have Caribbean-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations, but applies anti-abuse substance requirements through § 42 AO (abuse of legal forms), § 50d EStG treaty-shopping rules, German CFC rules (§§ 7–14 AStG), and the OECD Multilateral Instrument principal-purpose test. A GmbH acting purely as a shell trader or booking-company in a lithium-carbonate arbitrage chain, without personnel, premises, management decisions, or inventory risk, is exposed to DTA-benefit denial, hidden-profit distribution adjustments, CFC attribution of passive income and, ultimately, criminal tax-evasion liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine and imprisonment of up to five years under § 370 AO; especially severe tax evasion involving large-scale or sham structures attracts imprisonment of six months to ten years, forfeiture of profits, and interest-backed retroactive tax assessments.

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer is primarily liable under §§ 34, 69 AO for unpaid taxes up to the amount of damage caused by neglected duties; management can face travel bans, asset freezing, international arrest, disqualification of directors, and personal civil damages under § 823(2) BGB for tortious breach of statutory duty.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Improper Income Allocation

Statutory sanction: Under § 162 AO the tax authority may make a discretionary estimation of income; penalties or surcharges of 5%–10% of the adjustment amount apply for missing documentation; a 6% annual interest charge may be imposed on additional tax; criminal liability can arise if the failure is part of intentional evasion.

Director personal liability: Managing directors must ensure contemporaneous documentation exists; interruption of business functions abroad does not relieve duty. Non-compliance can trigger personal fines and a claim for the additional tax and penalty by the GmbH as a shareholder loss; foreign directors can wind up in German criminal prosecutions for evading trade tax and VAT.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Abusive Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation under § 42 AO, denial of DTA benefits, CFC re-inclusion, assessment of undeclared withholding tax at 15.825% on royalties and 26.375% on dividends, plus penalties for abusive structuring and, where criminal intent is established, imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for the German corporate tax arrears if proceeds of the sham structure were extracted as sham consideration; German insolvency law can extend liability for tax debts up to the deficit caused by unlawful business continuation (Insolvency Verschleppung).
Anti-Money Laundering / Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Money laundering carries imprisonment of three months to five years (up to ten years for aggravated commercial laundering); banks that unwittingly processed proceeds incur regulatory fines; companies can be fined under § 30 OWiG up to EUR 1 million or up to 10% of global turnover under market-abuse/AML provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to criminal arrest, seizure of personal and corporate assets, travel restrictions, disqualification from managing any company, and compensatory claims by the German state under statutory fraud/tort law; international mutual legal assistance allows arrest warrants to be activated across EU Member States and beyond.

UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: The principal statute is the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz, GwG), including Sections 1(13)-(14) and 3 on beneficial ownership, Sections 10-17 on customer due diligence, Section 6 on enhanced due diligence, Section 11 on identification and verification, Sections 19-23 on the Transparency Register, and Section 20 on reporting obligations of legal entities. Transparency Register procedural rules are contained in the Transparency Register Ordinance (Transparenzregisterverordnung, TrGebV and applicable register regulations).
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A GmbH must have a domestic registered office and a domestic business address capable of receiving official service under Section 4a GmbHG and the Commercial Register rules. A genuine serviced office may be acceptable if it provides reliable service of documents, access to company records, and credible business presence. A mailbox-only address is not sufficient. For Eurasia rail logistics and intermodal freight forwarding, a virtual office is legally possible for a headquarters or administrative function but presents elevated banking, tax, customs, and counterparty risk if there is no personnel, operational control, contract administration, or records function in Germany. If the company operates rail transport itself rather than arranging or forwarding transport, additional authorization and operational substance are required under the General Railway Act (Allgemeines Eisenbahngesetz, AEG) and related safety rules.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

German Joint States Company Register Portal (Gemeinsames Registerportal der Länder) at handelsregister.de; company filings are submitted through the competent local court and generally processed through the electronic notarial filing system

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no nationwide statutory company-name reservation period or guaranteed central reservation procedure. Before notarization, obtain an IHK name-opinion where available, conduct a Handelsregister search, trademark search, domain search, and sanctions or restricted-activity review. If the local registry court rejects or objects to the name, obtain the written order, identify the cited HGB or GmbHG defect, submit amended wording or legal argument through the notary, and request reconsideration by the registry court. A formal complaint (Beschwerde) may be filed against a registrable court decision under the German procedural rules applicable to register matters, generally within the statutory period stated in the decision; legal counsel should verify the deadline and competent appellate court. A revised name filing is usually faster than litigation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT-ID issuance and federal tax matters; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT return assessments; mandatory electronic filing infrastructure is the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

(1) A foreign-owned GmbH operating a German registered office must complete the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt and must provide commercial registration (Handelsregister) certificate, shareholders register, management identity documents and anticipated profit projections. (2) The Finanzamt subsequently assigns corporation tax number, trade tax number, VAT number (Umsatzsteuernummer) and local revenue office codes. (3) Separately, the GmbH must apply to BZSt for the EU-wide VAT Identification Number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, §27a UStG) using the BZSt online application, which is mandatory for cross-border intermodal freight invoicing and intra-Community acquisition declarations. (4) Non-established foreign freight entities with German-exposed turnover must complete simplified BZSt VAT registration before the first taxable German supply; failure to register is an administrative offence subject to fines under §26a UStG and §378 AO. (5) All applications must be made before commencement of taxable operations; penalties for late registration include monetary fines and possible VAT assessment on a deemed-profit basis.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is a per se, opaque corporation (Körperschaft) subject to corporation tax at 15%, solidarity surcharge of 5.5% on the corporation tax (applicable until a final court decision remains outstanding), and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) assessed by the municipality at 3.5% federal base multiplied by a local collection multiplier; there is no any-tier pass-through check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (i) corporate income tax and VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §14 KStG and §2 Abs.2 Nr.2 UStG, which requires a profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) and financial, economic and organizational interdependence; (ii) leapfrog partnership-to-corporation option under §1a KStG not applicable to a GmbH; (iii) hybrid mismatch rules under §4k EStG are mandatory anti-abuse provisions with no elective set-off mechanism. Germany recognises no special tax-exempt free-zone treatment within the customs territory for VAT purposes; intermodal rail logistics centres are subject to standard VAT/corporate tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for non-established foreign suppliers of freight and logistics services (immediate VAT registration nexus if place of supply is in Germany). Domestic small-business exemption (§19 UStG): EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 projected current-year turnover, which is irrelevant for a GmbH but available only to natural persons and certain partnerships; EU distance-sales commerce threshold: EUR 10,000 per calendar year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to (i) export supplies of goods removed from the EU customs territory (§6 UStG); (ii) intra-Community supplies to another EU Member State (§4 Nr.1b UStG); and (iii) transport of goods and directly ancillary services (loading, unloading, handling, commissionaire, terminal charges, and customs representation) supplied in conjunction with transport of goods destined to or originating from a third-country territory (§4 Nr.3 UStG in connection with §7 UStG), including transcontinental block-train intermodal forwarding from Germany to non-EU Eurasian destinations. The eligibility preconditions are: lawful commercial export, actual physical removal of the goods, and a complete documented evidence chain from the German/freight-taxing perspective, comprising commercial documentation, transport documentation and customs export documentation. For freight forwarding services where the place of supply for the underlying transport crosses third-country borders, 0%-rate claims are recognised regardless of whether the recipient is taxable in Germany, provided the transport distance outside the EU is fully evidenced.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Export Accompanying Document / Movement Reference Number) issued by German customs
  • Rail consignment note (CIM/SMGS or CIM-combined waybill) evidencing cross-border Eurasian transit route and final destination outside EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with explicit 0% VAT line and reference to §6/§4 Nr.3 UStG
  • Customs export confirmation (Ausfuhrbestätigung) and customs seal numbers for containers/block trains
  • Carrier terminal delivery certificates, weighbridge documents, or destination customs clearance evidence
  • SWIFT wire payout advice or Letter-of-Credit documentary compliance advice evidencing inward remittance from the non-EU consignee

From 1 January 2025, every business established in Germany must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.0.1+, or EDI formats) in domestic B2B transactions under the E-Rechnungsverordnung (ERechV); from 1 January 2027, businesses having more than EUR 800,000 turnover in the 2026 calendar year must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all German businesses must issue structured e-invoices. No real-time national clearance or mandatory digital stamping is applied, although counterparties using the Italian-style SDI are not required for German domestic tax compliance; the future EU ViDA framework is expected to introduce transaction-based real-time digital reporting to national tax access points no earlier than 2028. All standard invoicing requirements under §14 UStG, including consecutive invoice number, full supplier/recipient identity, quantity/description of supply, date, tax base and VAT rate, remain the baseline.

The Federal Tax Court and tax administration strictly require immediate and complete export corroboration: if goods do not physically leave Germany or an alternative intra-Community acquisition is not substantiated, the zero rate is rejected and VAT is retrospectively assessed at 19% on the full consideration, plus interest of 0.5% per month (§233a AO) and late-payment penalties (§240 AO); an invoice wrongfully carrying a zero rate triggers tax liability on the issuer under §14c UStG. Intentional or grossly negligent failure to substantiate export facts constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in aggravated cases, and monetary fines up to EUR 30,000 per act; the limitation period extends to 10 years for criminal evasion. Tax audit emphasis for Eurasian rail freight forwarders involves reconciliation between the CIM/SMGS rail waybill, the customs exit confirmation, the block-train movement logs and the 180-day foreign-currency settlement trail; documentary gaps automatically trigger clawback of the input-VAT refund claimed on direct GST/import VAT and cumulative reversal of all period losses attributed to the zero-rated turnover.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility pursuant to EU Treaty freedoms and the AWV. No foreign-exchange-control regime, exchange licensing requirement, or repatriation permit applies to dividends, salaries, loan repayments, capital distributions, or current trade payments. Profit remittance from a German GmbH is subject only to standard corporate formalities, transfer-pricing documentation where applicable, and withholding tax at 26.375% (including solidarity surcharge) on dividends unless reduced under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable double-tax treaty. Cross-border payments and outstanding claims arising from transactions with non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV; this is a statistical reporting requirement and not an exchange-control restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender target, mandatory conversion ratio, or approved-bank requirement for export proceeds in Germany. Exporters may keep, convert, or deploy export receipts in any freely convertible currency without central-bank approval. Resident exporters must report claims against non-residents, export receivables, and inbound payment receipts above EUR 12,500 to Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV for statistical purposes. This obligation does not mandate any conversion into euro and has no effect on the underlying cash-management flexibility.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), acting as the competent authority for balance-of-payments statistics and foreign-trade payment reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) exercises supervisory oversight over banks, payment institutions, and e-money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for EU/EEA-licensed electronic-money institutions and payment institutions operating in Germany under the EU cross-border passport or a BaFin license under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG); Low for non-EU offshore digital EMIs without an EU passport. Unlicensed acceptance of deposits or issuance of e-money in Germany violates the German Banking Act (KWG) and ZAG. Offshore EMIs may not serve as the sole treasury rail for a German GmbH and cannot replace a domestic supervised bank for SWIFT MT103 transactions, capital injections, letter-of-credit clearing, or Bundesbank reporting. An EU-licensed EMI with a valid passport may support low-value collections and client payments, but the primary liquidity and clearing account must remain at a German commercial bank for legal certainty and sanctions compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from a non-resident shareholder must be routed by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to a dedicated capital-contribution account held at the selected German commercial bank. The remittance field must state the economic purpose as 'capital contribution to German GmbH' or 'equity capital increase' and refer to the notarized shareholder resolution and the GmbH Commercial Register file number. For initial incorporation, the statutory minimum capital and any agreed share premium must be credited without restriction; after registration in the Commercial Register, the balances may be transferred to a free operational account. Verification requires the notarized capital-contribution resolution, updated shareholders list (Gesellschafterliste), certified identification of the remitting shareholder, source-of-funds evidence, and confirmation that the amount is immediately available to the company. If the inbound payment exceeds EUR 12,500, the German entity or its bank must report the receipt to Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV for balance-of-payments statistics.Outbound trade disbursements are executed through (i) SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments to rail operators, terminal operators, freight forwarders, and service providers within the SEPA zone; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for international supplier payments, non-euro carrier disbursements, customs-broker fees, and agent commissions outside the euro area; and (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit, opened by MT700 and amended by MT707, with settlement under MT740 reimbursement or deferred-payment instructions for high-value intermodal equipment purchases, container-leasing contracts, and cross-border rail-oil or intermodal supply agreements. Documentary collection (CAD/DP) serves as a secondary risk-mitigation rail. Verification must be supported by commercial invoices, rail consignment notes (CIM/SMGS), customs declarations, container gate receipts, transport insurance certificates, and Incoterm-based documentation.

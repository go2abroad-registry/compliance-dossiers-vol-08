Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Generally no registration threshold for entrepreneurs; small entrepreneur (Kleinunternehmer) exemption if prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 22,000 and current-year forecast ≤ EUR 50,000; intra-Community distance sales and digital B2C services exceed EUR 10,000 threshold in the EU triggers OSS registration instead of multi-country VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: export to non-EU territory qualifies for 0% VAT (exemption with input VAT deduction) where the goods are physically removed from Germany/ EU, the exporter retains proof of export (customs export declaration, transport documents, commercial invoices), and the recipient is identified; for VPN/privacy digital services: B2B services to non-EU customers are outside German VAT scope because place of supply is the recipient’s business establishment; B2C digital services to non-EU customers are also outside German VAT scope; must retain service contracts, IP/delivery logs, IP-address/country evidence, and bank payment records to substantiate the foreign place of supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Elektronische Ausfuhranmeldung)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or other transport evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of foreign payment
  • Service agreement, technical delivery evidence and user-location data for digital services

Mandatory B2B domestic e-invoicing phased in from 1 January 2025 (receiving obligation) and 1 January 2027 (issuing obligation); accepted standards XRechnung and ZUGFeRD; no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping; retention period for tax-relevant invoices is 10 years; non-compliance leads to fines and potential disallowance of input VAT deduction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility on both current and capital accounts under EU free movement of capital (Art. 63 TFEU) and the AWV. No general central bank authorization is required for foreign direct investment, capital injections, trade payments, or profit repatriation by a German GmbH. The only binding procedural requirement is statistical reporting to the Bundesbank under AWV §§60-71: payments and receivables from non-residents must be reported within seven calendar days of the transaction when the value exceeds €12,500, either individually or via monthly reporting. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are subject to corporate income tax withholding (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, with potential treaty relief) but are not subject to FX control approval. Capital account transactions, including shareholder loans and equity contributions, are unrestricted but require written notification for monetary statistics purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose any statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory EUR conversion requirement. Exporters may hold and use foreign currency freely, but must report all export receipts from non-residents to the Bundesbank via the balance of payments statistics (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik) under AWV §67 within seven days of receipt if the value exceeds €12,500, using the appropriate transaction code (Geschäftsfall). For the VPN subscription and software licensing business, receipts are classified as computer services (transaction code 381); no conversion is mandated, but any EUR/USD conversion must be executed through a licensed credit institution and reported in the monthly Bundesbank survey.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) acting as the primary monetary and foreign exchange authority, with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) jointly administering the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for operating payments, but Low for statutory capital escrow and full treasury clearing. German law permits use of BaFin- or EBA-regulated e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut Business, Wise Luxembourg, Penta, or Qonto for day-to-day collections and disbursements, provided the account is an EU payment account. Offshore EMIs domiciled outside the EEA without a BaFin/EBA passport cannot lawfully provide payment accounts to a German GmbH. For the GmbH's incorporation capital deposit under §7 GmbHG and for holding statutory reserve capital, a domestic SEPA bank account at a licensed credit institution (Vollbank) is universally required; an EMI cannot certify the capital deposit for the commercial register. For FX and SWIFT clearing, EMIs generally lack correspondent banking and MT103 issuance capacity; therefore a principal relationship with a tier-1 German bank remains mandatory for treasury operations.

Compliant protocols require the capital contribution to the GmbH's escrow account to be made via SWIFT/SEPA from the shareholder's bank.

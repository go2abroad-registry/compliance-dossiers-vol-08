Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister), Abteilung B, maintained by the Local Court (Amtsgericht) with jurisdiction over the company's registered office; certified filings are made exclusively via a German civil-law notary (Notar) in electronic form through the notarial filing certification system.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung - GmbHG): §§ 1 and 3 (formation and mandatory articles of association), § 4a (registered office), § 5 (statutory minimum share capital of EUR 25,000), § 7 (registration application and required payment of at least half of each cash contribution), § 8 (documents to be supplied, particularly the bankers' confirmation of payment), § 10 (legal personality upon Commercial Register registration); supplemented by the German Commercial Code (HGB) Book 2 for the Commercial Register, the Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung - HRV), the Notarization Act (Beurkundungsgesetz - BeurkG §§ 36-40), and the Federal Court Fees Act (Gerichtskostengesetz - GKG, fee item 25050).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified incorporation deed (Errichtungsprotokoll) incorporating the Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag), the share subscription list, and the notarized application for entry (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) submitted under § 7 GmbHG.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 8 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Statutory minimum share capital is EUR 25,000 (§ 5 GmbHG). Before filing the registration application, each cash contribution must be paid in to at least 50%, i.e., a minimum aggregate of EUR 12,500, and must be at the free disposal of the managing director (§ 7(2) GmbHG); for a single-shareholder GmbH, security (e.g., bank guarantee) for the not-yet-paid balance is mandatory (§ 7(3) GmbHG). Funds are deposited on a German business account opened in the company's name; no statutory escrow account is prescribed, but the bank's confirmation certifying free disposal is a mandatory attachment to the registration application.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) — issues EU VAT IDs (USt-IdNr.) and administers central/international tax matters; local municipal tax office (Finanzamt) at the GmbH's statutory seat is the assessing authority for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT. Electronic filing portal: ELSTER at www.elster.de.

E-government filing portal

A domestic GmbH obtains all tax identifiers through one consolidated electronic onboarding. After notarial incorporation and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), the managing director submits the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER, in principle within one month of business commencement. The competent Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (assessment number for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT) and forwards the dataset to the BZSt, which issues the separate EU VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) within approximately 4–6 weeks. Non-resident entrepreneurs (no seat and no place of management in Germany) register for VAT directly with the local Finanzamt responsible for non-established entrepreneurs using Form USt 1 TG or ELSTER; pending issuance, the German recipient applies the reverse charge under §13b UStG.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the GmbH is a resident capital company subject to unlimited corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer 15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate tax), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, statutory rate 3.5% multiplied by municipal Hebesatz, typically 200%–490%); combined effective tax rate approximately 30%–33%. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the GmbH is a resident capital company subject to unlimited corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer 15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate tax), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, statutory rate 3.5% multiplied by municipal Hebesatz, typically 200%–490%); combined effective tax rate approximately 30%–33%. Territorial scope: worldwide income for resident entities; treaty-exempt foreign permanent establishment income remains subject to the subject-to-tax requirement.

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime for GmbHs; tax classification follows civil law. Statutory elections available include: (1) fiscal unity (Organschaft) for corporate income tax/trade tax under §14 KStG and for VAT under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, requiring a valid profit-and-loss transfer agreement and financial, economic and organizational integration; (2) optional corporate taxation of an EU/EEA partnership under §1a KStG; (3) small-business VAT exemption under §19 UStG (of no practical relevance to an EPC contractor); hybrid-mismatch consequences are governed by the German ATAD implementation (§4k EStG, §8b KStG) and may trigger treaty-override under §50d Abs. 9a EStG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis economic threshold applies to a German-resident GmbH: VAT registration is unconditional upon commencement of business. Non-resident entrepreneurs likewise have no turnover threshold where German place-of-supply rules apply. The small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmer, §19 UStG) is limited to entrepreneurs with prior-year turnover ≤ €22,000 and current-year expectation ≤ €50,000 and is inapplicable to a GmbH EPC contractor. The only quantitative threshold of possible relevance is the €10,000 EU-wide threshold for intra-Community distance sales, which is irrelevant to this B2B EPC scope.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries of goods to third-country destinations are VAT-exempt with input-VAT credit under §4 Nr. 1a and §6 UStG if the goods physically leave the EU. Intra-Community supplies are exempt under §4 Nr. 1b and §6a UStG provided the German supplier records the customer's EU VAT ID and files the EC sales list. Zero-rating of services is limited to services directly connected with exported goods or international transport (§4 Nr. 2 and §8 UStG); for the present EPC scope, services related to immovable property (German-located data centers) have their place of supply in Germany under §3a Abs. 3 Nr. 1 UStG and attract 19% VAT, while design and engineering services supplied B2B to foreign clients are not subject to German VAT when the place of supply is outside Germany under §3a Abs. 2 UStG and the recipient confirms its status.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with Movement Reference Number (MRN) and export accompanying document
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing dispatch from Germany and arrival at the third-country destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the EPC contract and HS tariff classifications
  • Proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) — customs-stamped exit confirmation or electronic ATLAS confirmation (IE-599)
  • Gelangensbestätigung (delivery-at-destination confirmation) for intra-Community supplies under §6a UStG
  • Inward remittance certificate (bank credit advice / SWIFT MT103) evidencing final receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer

Germany operates no real-time fiscal clearance, no government API validation, and no digital stamping regime. Mandatory structured e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) applies to domestic B2B transactions: from 1 January 2025, every supplier must be able to receive XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0.1 e-invoices; from 1 January 2027, suppliers with annual B2B turnover exceeding €800,000 must issue structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all B2B suppliers must issue them. Transmission is bilateral via PEPPOL, EDI networks, or secure email; B2G (public procurement) e-invoicing in XRechnung format has been mandatory since 27 November 2020. VAT pre-returns (Voranmeldungen), annual returns and EC sales lists are filed electronically via ELSTER without pre-clearance.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict documentary review under UStAE §6; the Finanzamt may reassess the full 19% VAT retroactively if the proof of export is missing, incomplete or inconsistent, even without fraudulent intent. Reassessment interest accrues at 6% per annum (§§233a, 234 AO) from the original VAT due date; late-payment surcharges run at 1% per month (minimum €25) up to €25,000 (§240 AO). Falsified or deliberately withheld documentation constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO (imprisonment up to 10 years plus fines, limitation period extended to 10 years); default assessments under §162 AO apply where records are unavailable; violations of statutory declaration duties attract fines up to €50,000 under §26c UStG; the freight forwarder or exporting agent may be jointly and severally liable; clawback extends to input-VAT refunds sourced from the same supply. Although Germany imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline in UStG/AO, remittances received later than 180 days are treated as heightened audit-risk indicators.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a full-convertibility, zero-restriction regime for both current and capital account transactions under Article 63 TFEU and AWG section 4. There are no exchange quotas, approval requirements, prior authorization gates, or mandated holding periods for inbound foreign direct investment into a GmbH. Profit remittances and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are freely permitted in EUR or any other currency, subject only to (i) German withholding tax at 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%), potentially reduced under an applicable double taxation treaty, and (ii) statistical payment reporting. No central bank license or exchange-control permit is required for the capital increase, intercompany loans, or trade settlements of a data-center EPC entity; the material operative constraint is sanctions/embargo compliance with EU implementing regulations and OFAC-linked screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender ratio, conversion timeline, or repatriation deadline applies to export proceeds in Germany. A German GmbH may freely retain, net, lend, or reinvest export collections in any currency without time limits under AWG section 4. The only mandatory obligations are statistical: under AWV section 67, every cross-border payment or receipt of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported to the Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics via the electronic reporting channel (InternetMeldeverfahren) within 5 working days of the transaction, with the German processing bank usually filing the payment report on the customer's behalf under an express account mandate. Additionally, under AWV section 58, when aggregate receivables or liabilities vis-à-vis non-residents (including the foreign parent) exceed EUR 5 million, the GmbH must file periodic status reports (Forderungen/Verbindlichkeiten) with the Bundesbank. Failure to file constitutes an administrative offence (Ordnungswidrigkeit).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German federal central bank, Frankfurt am Main), in association with the Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF), the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for licensing and prudential supervision, and the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) for export controls and sanctions administration. Primary instruments: Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), complemented by the Bundesbank's balance-of-payments statistics framework under Regulation (EC) No 184/2005.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium for EU/BaFin-licensed EMIs; Low-to-Non-viable for offshore non-EEA digital EMIs. A fully licensed EU electronic-money institution (e.g., Solaris, N26 Business, Pismo) can legally open a German IBAN account for FX collections, expense cards, and SEPA direct debits under PSD2 transposed by the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG). However, an EMI cannot issue documentary letters of credit, performance guarantees, or structured escrow facilities, so the principal project treasury cannot be EMI-based. Non-EU EMIs without a German branch or an EEA-licensed partner bank are legally prohibited from holding client funds on a German IBAN; they may act only as technical service providers. Recommended structure: maintain the operational treasury spine at a Tier-1 bank and use a single EU-licensed EMI (e.g., Airwallex EEA or Currenxie EEA) as a supplementary FX collection and corporate-card rail, with same-day SEPA sweeps to the main bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections (share capital and shareholder loans) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the nominated Tier-1 account in Germany (or SEPA CT where the parent remits from an EEA account), with the purpose line (Einlage Kapitalerhöhung) and the Bundesbank statistical purpose code for direct investment and capital participation. The German processing bank automatically files the AWV section 67 payment report where the amount exceeds EUR 12,500; the GmbH must retain the bank's payment-report confirmation in the treasury file. For a capital increase executed by a non-resident shareholder, the GmbH must also file or verify the Bundesbank acquisition/establishment notice under AWV section 60 and the quarterly Vemog status report once outstanding liabilities to non-resident shareholders exceed EUR 5 million. Prior to the wire, the bank requires: notarized shareholders' resolution on the capital increase, proof that the statutory minimum cash contribution under section 36a GmbHG has been credited to a bank account in the name of the GmbH, the updated commercial-register filing, and a valid Transparenzregister confirmation.For EPC trade settlements the issuer should use: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (with SEPA Instant for amounts up to EUR 100,000) for EU/EEA subcontractors and consultants, quoting the AWV purpose text and project reference; (ii) SWIFT MT103 messages for USD, GBP, CHF, and CNY invoicing, incorporating a structured reference field with the contract milestone or L/C number; (iii) irrevocable sight Letters of Credit (MT700/MT707) governed by UCP 600 for offshore equipment purchases such as generators, chillers, PDUs, and switchgear; (iv) Standby LCs (MT760) or first-demand bank guarantees (Zahlungsbürgschaft) as counter-security for the client's performance and warranty bonds; and (v) documentary collections (MT400/MT422) only for repeat low-risk suppliers. Every message is screened against sanctions and AML lists; to avoid payment-reporting failures, treasury must pre-approve the payment-reason code with the bank's AWV desk and open commercial LCs only against a fully signed export contract, an approved supplier-compliance certificate, and a confirmed credit line.Outright deliverable EUR/USD, EUR/CNY, and EUR/GBP forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) with tenors typically up to 24 months, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the German Master Agreement (Rahmenvertrag für Devisengeschäfte) of the Deutsche Kreditwirtschaft.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or emerging-market currencies (offshore CNH, INR, USD) for cash-flow hedging of EPC subcontract payables, subject to the bank's internal NDF credit limits and ISDA documentation.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CIRS) and EUR/USD basis swaps to align USD-denominated accounts payable with EUR funding, executed under an ISDA-CSA collateralized framework.OTC European-style and exotic currency options (zero-cost collars, knock-in barrier structures), permitted without central-bank authorization but requiring MiFID II professional-client classification and appropriate derivatives documentation.Intragroup spot and forward netting, including tri-party collateral in EUR government bonds for any ECB-eligible collateralized derivatives; all transactions subject to EMIR transaction reporting, German WpHG record-keeping, and BaFin-market conduct rules.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for EU/BaFin-licensed EMIs; Low-to-Non-viable for offshore non-EEA digital EMIs. A fully licensed EU electronic-money institution (e.g., Solaris, N26 Business, Pismo) can legally open a German IBAN account for FX collections, expense cards, and SEPA direct debits under PSD2 transposed by the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG). However, an EMI cannot issue documentary letters of credit, performance guarantees, or structured escrow facilities, so the principal project treasury cannot be EMI-based. Non-EU EMIs without a German branch or an EEA-licensed partner bank are legally prohibited from holding client funds on a German IBAN; they may act only as technical service providers. Recommended structure: maintain the operational treasury spine at a Tier-1 bank and use a single EU-licensed EMI (e.g., Airwallex EEA or Currenxie EEA) as a supplementary FX collection and corporate-card rail, with same-day SEPA sweeps to the main bank.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt): issues the EU-wide VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer), operates the ELSTER electronic filing portal (www.elster.de) for VAT advance returns, annual VAT returns, and recapitulative statements, and coordinates cross-border refund clearances; locally competent municipal tax office (Finanzamt): exclusively responsible for the national tax number (Steuernummer), corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), wage tax, and turnover tax assessment and audits; German customs administration (Generalzolldirektion, zoll.de): issues the EORI customs registration and processes ATLAS export declarations.

E-government filing portal

1. A newly incorporated GmbH must register with the locally competent Finanzamt within four weeks of commencing business using the digital tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) filed through ELSTER; the Finanzamt issues the national Steuernummer covering corporation tax, trade tax, wage tax, and turnover tax. 2. A separate electronic application to BZSt (Formular USt 1 TN / BZSt online portal) obtains the cross-border Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID), mandatory for intra-Community supplies, imports, EU VAT refund applications under Directive 2008/9/EC, and B2B reverse-charge invoicing. 3. Non-resident foreign contractors performing taxable supplies in Germany must apply to the Finanzamt in whose district the first German business activity is performed; where no permanent establishment exists and annual turnover does not exceed EUR 10,000, the simplified registration relief under §18 Abs. 4c UStG applies. 4. For a foreign-owned German GmbH, the GmbH itself is the registered taxpayer; the foreign shareholder has no direct VAT registration duty, but a German permanent establishment of the parent or a fixed construction site lasting more than six months under §12 AO creates a separate registration obligation for the foreign entity. 5. Supporting documentation consists of the certified extract from the Commercial Register (Handelsregisterauszug), certificate of incorporation, proof of registered seat, shareholders register, and proof of managing directors; physically exporting entities must additionally obtain the EORI number via zoll.de before making export declarations; turnaround time is normally two to six weeks and the procedures are free of administrative fees.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the GmbH is a separate tax subject with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income under §1 KStG; non-resident companies are taxed only on German-source income. Standard income tax load for a resident GmbH: 15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 15.825%) plus municipal trade tax calculated as 3.5% of trade income multiplied by the municipal assessment rate (Hebesatz of 200% to 490%), producing a combined ordinary burden of approximately 30% to 33% in major infrastructure regions; distributed profits carry a final capital-yield withholding tax of 26.375% at the shareholder level (§43a EStG). Cross-border EPC profits are computed under the arm's length principle; retained earnings are not subject to additional tax. Groups with consolidated revenues of at least EUR 750 million fall within the German Minimum Tax Act (Mindeststeuergesetz), applying a 15% effective minimum top-up tax as entity-level supplementary taxation.

Available election forms

German law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; entity tax status follows the civil-law form. The relevant elections for a GmbH engaged in bridge and highway infrastructure include: (i) corporate income tax and trade tax fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §§14–19 KStG supported by a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag), enabling single-taxpayer aggregation of German resident group companies; (ii) a separate VAT fiscal unity (umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft) under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, merging parent and integrated subsidiary into a single VAT taxpayer so that internal supplies are disregarded; (iii) the option to tax real property under §9 UStG (Verzicht auf die Steuerbefreiung) for domestic bridge-land and construction land sales where the buyer is an entrepreneur; (iv) hybrid mismatch elections under the German ATAD implementation (§4k EStG, §8b Abs. 4 KStG, §10 Abs. 2 AStG) that grandfather existing hybrid instruments or reorganize branch structures to avoid deduction/non-inclusion mismatches; and (v) structuring foreign project vehicles as transparent partnerships (GmbH & Co. KG) instead of subsidiary GmbHs when German investors require pass-through treatment, subject to trade tax add-backs and transfer-pricing documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account purposes. Germany has no general capital controls, no general restriction on foreign ownership of a GmbH, and no general requirement to obtain approval for dividend, interest, loan, service-fee, or capital remittances. Remittances remain subject to corporate-law distributable-profit rules, withholding-tax procedures, treaty or EU relief requirements, transfer-pricing compliance, anti-money-laundering controls, sanctions screening, beneficial-owner verification, and bank documentation. Cross-border payments may require statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung. Foreign-investment screening, export-control, sanctions, anti-bribery, procurement, and national-security rules may apply to infrastructure projects, public-sector counterparties, strategic assets, or controlled technologies. Capital contributions and shareholder loans should be supported by executed agreements, corporate resolutions, source-of-funds evidence, and accounting records. Dividend distributions require adoption of annual accounts or an otherwise valid distribution basis and must not impair the GmbH's stated share capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros or repatriated through a German bank. Exporters and construction contractors may retain foreign-currency receipts in permitted accounts and convert them commercially when required. For infrastructure EPC activities, the relevant receipt may be consideration for construction or engineering services rather than a goods export; the underlying contract, VAT treatment, permanent-establishment analysis, sanctions position, and payment classification must be documented. Cross-border payments and receipts may be reportable to the Deutsche Bundesbank under current Außenwirtschaftsverordnung rules, subject to statutory exemptions and applicable thresholds. Payments relating to imports and exports of goods generally receive specific reporting treatment, while service, financing, capital, and other transactions may require separate assessment. The company should confirm the current reporting threshold, exemption, filing channel, and monthly deadline with the Bundesbank before implementation because these rules are amended periodically.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting within the Eurosystem under the monetary authority of the European Central Bank; foreign-exchange and cross-border payment reporting is principally governed by German foreign-trade and payments legislation, including the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz and Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, with supervisory involvement by the Bundesbank and the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine euro collections, payments, expense management, and treasury automation; Low to Medium as the sole primary banking platform for a German infrastructure EPC GmbH. A licensed electronic-money institution or payment institution established in Germany or another EEA state may provide payment accounts, safeguarding, SEPA services, and certain cross-border payment services under its authorization and passporting rights. An EMI account is not a bank deposit, generally does not provide deposit-guarantee protection, and may not provide credit, overdraft, commercial letters of credit, escrow, project-finance accounts, or the full range of trade-finance products. The provider must be verified through the relevant supervisory register, and the GmbH must satisfy enhanced business-model, beneficial-owner, source-of-funds, sanctions, expected-volume, and transaction-purpose checks. Offshore providers outside the EEA cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services into Germany without an applicable authorization, passport, branch, agent, or other valid legal basis. A regulated German or EEA bank should normally remain the primary account for shareholder capital, public-project receipts, payroll, tax payments, performance bonds, bank guarantees, and LC-backed trade settlements.

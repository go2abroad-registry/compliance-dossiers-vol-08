Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Gemeinsames Registerportal der Länder (Registerportal), with company filings submitted through the competent local Amtsgericht—Handelsregister; the Unternehmensregister provides centralized access to published register information but is not a conventional name-reservation portal

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no general statutory company-name reservation procedure or automatic 30-day reservation period. Obtain a current name search and written or documented preliminary assessment from the competent IHK and coordinate the wording with the notary before execution. If the Handelsregister court rejects or objects to the proposed name, respond within the court's deadline with revised wording, evidence of distinctiveness, business-purpose clarification and any required regulatory authorization. A final judicial decision of the register court may generally be challenged under the German Act on Proceedings in Family Matters and in Matters of Voluntary Jurisdiction (FamFG), subject to the applicable appeal route and deadline stated in the decision. An informal IHK objection is not normally subject to a separate administrative appeal; revise the name or proceed with court filing after addressing the objection.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory share capital is EUR 25,000. Before registration, at least EUR 12,500 must be paid in for a cash formation, with each subscribed cash contribution paid at least to the extent required by the Articles and GmbHG. A statutory escrow account is not required; the capital is normally paid into the GmbH bank account after notarisation and before or promptly after registration. Contributions in kind require additional disclosure, valuation and documentation.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain building-use approval or change-of-use approval where required by the local building authority; maintain fire-protection measures, escape routes, emergency plans and landlord or authority inspections. Structural load and workplace-layout inspections are required where equipment, storage or laboratory installations create relevant loads or hazards. Complete risk assessments under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, provide occupational safety and health measures, appoint competent safety personnel where required, and comply with statutory accident-insurance rules. No separate general civil-defence licence is normally required for an office-based agency.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT IDs; local tax office (Finanzamt) for income/trade tax registration; official portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must register with the competent local Finanzamt (usually the one responsible for the principal place of business in Germany) using form 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire). For VAT ID purposes, after obtaining a local Steuernummer, the VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) is applied for online via BZSt (www.bzst.de) or through ELSTER. Required supporting documents: certified German translations of trade register excerpt, constitutional documents, director identification, and proof of business address. The registration must be completed prior to commencing taxable activities. Non-resident entities may appoint a fiscal representative (Fiscal Agent) for the registration process if no permanent establishment is established.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a check-the-box regime. A GmbH is a corporate tax subject at entity level (Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer, and VAT). Relevant elections include: (1) VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) election under Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG, allowing a parent entity to consolidate with its financially and operationally integrated German subsidiaries for VAT purposes; (2) profit/loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) affecting corporate taxation; (3) hybrid mismatch elections under Sec. 4k EStG and Sec. 8b KStG to align with ATAD, with specific filings to BZSt. No other freely elective tax classification exists for a GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 annual turnover for the small-enterprise exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung, Sec. 19 UStG); however, for B2B commercial inspection and testing services, the exemption is generally not applicable. Resident GmbHs are required to register for VAT from the first euro of taxable turnover; non-resident entities are mandatorily registered if they perform any taxable supply in Germany, with no threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported goods applies under Sec. 6 UStG if the goods are delivered to a non-EU country or via permissible intra-EU dispatch. Services related to on-site factory inspection and quality control qualify for zero-rating (Sec. 6a UStG, or as a supply of services to a non-EU business customer under the reverse-charge export rule) when provided directly for the benefit of a non-resident entrepreneur and the place of supply is outside Germany. Substantiation requires documented evidence that the principal place of business of the recipient is in a third country and that the service is actually performed in connection with export goods. The exporter must retain commercially accepted proof of export (e.g., customs docs, consignment notes, contracts).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrliche / E-Dokument) with customs registration number
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airway Bill (AWB) or CMR transport document
  • International trade invoice (Handelsrechnung)
  • Payment confirmation of export consideration (e.g., SWIFT MT103 / foreign inward remittance certificate)
  • Declaration of the non-resident customer confirming third-country status and actual delivery of goods/services

Germany mandates e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) for all domestic B2B transactions. Since 1 January 2025, taxpayers must be able to receive structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2027, businesses with an annual turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all resident businesses must issue e-invoices in the XInvoice/ZUGFeRD format. Invoices must be transmitted electronically through the official portal (ELSTER) or via certified service providers. There is no real-time fiscal clearance system akin to Italy's SDI; the mandate is compliance-oriented with post-audit verification via standardised XML schemas.

The German tax administration rigorously audits export zero-rating claims. If the required documentation is incomplete, the VAT claimed at 0% is retroactively reversed and the standard 19% VAT becomes payable, plus monthly penalty interest of 0.5% (max 15%) under Sec. 233a AO. In cases of intent or fraud, criminal prosecution under Sec. 370 AO applies, with fines of up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for simple evasion, up to 10 years in aggravated cases. Additionally, the tax authority may impose penalties under Sec. 378 AO for negligent non-disclosure. The failure to repatriate foreign exchange proceeds within 180 days may trigger a reclassification of the transaction as domestic and disqualify the zero-rating, resulting in VAT clawback and further consequences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the euro on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no prior-approval requirements for cross-border profit remittances, loan servicing, dividend distribution, or equity liquidation proceeds of a German GmbH to non-resident shareholders. The practical compliance burdens are limited to: (i) the Deutsche Bundesbank's mandatory balance-of-payments notification for any payment received from or sent to non-EU resident counterparties when the transaction value equals or exceeds EUR 12,500, to be filed in the month of execution using AWV statistics forms; and (ii) dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (total 26.375%) applied to profit distributions, which can be reduced to 0% for qualified EU corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or to a reduced treaty rate under an applicable Double Taxation Agreement. No foreign-exchange allocation or conversion quota applies to the GmbH's funds, and retained export earnings freely remain in EUR or convertible foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, forced conversion, or statutory repatriation of export proceeds. There is no prescribed timeline for converting export receipts and no minimum conversion ratio; the GmbH may hold export proceeds in EUR or any freely convertible currency at a domestic or foreign bank. The single mandatory regulatory duty is statistical notification to the Deutsche Bundesbank: every receipt of exported services or goods from non-EU countries with a transaction value of at least EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank's balance-of-payments statistics unit no later than the end of the month in which the payment is received. Supporting documentation (invoice, inspection report, purchase order, bank statement) must be retained for the statutory ten-year retention period under tax law; the export of inspection/QC services remains subject to AWV technical-reporting only if the activity falls under listed export-control or dual-use data restrictions, which standard commercial factory inspections do not.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank) as the sole statutory repository for external trade and payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Deutsche Bundesbank administers balance-of-payments statistics and compulsory notification thresholds; the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) and the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) administer licensing, sanctions, and export-control enforcement. Germany operates no centralized foreign-exchange licensing authority because the capital account is fully liberalized under EU Treaty provisions (Articles 63–66 TFEU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for strictly operational treasury use (paying foreign auditors, contractors, and small disbursements) but NOT viable as the primary corporate settlement account or statutory capital escrow account. Legal constraints under German law (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG): any Electronic Money Institution (EMI) serving German entities must hold a BaFin license or an EU single-license passport; an offshore EMI without a German or EU license is prohibited from operating in Germany. Licensed EU EMI providers (e.g., Wise Europe, Airwallex Ltd, and similar BaFin-passported entities) can legally maintain multi-currency operational accounts, issue IBANs, and settle via SEPA/SWIFT, but they cannot provide credit, are not eligible for capital-escrow confirmation by a German notary, and are generally rejected as the governing bank for GmbH registered-capital deposit verification under section 7/8 GmbHG. The recommended architecture is a Tier-1 German commercial bank as the anchor treasury and settlement bank, with a licensed EMI used only as a disbursement rail for low-value cross-border payments to sub-agents.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for the GmbH (Stammeinlage, capital reserve contributions, or shareholder loans) must be routed exclusively through regulated banking channels: a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the non-resident shareholder's corporate or personal bank account to the GmbH's German Capital Escrow Account at a Tier-1 bank. The remittance instruction must contain: the GmbH's IBAN and payment purpose text, e.g., 'Stammeinlage Einzahlung gemaess § 5 GmbHG / notarielle Urkunde Nr. X' or 'Kapitalerhöhung und Einlage in die Kapitalrücklage', together with the operational Bundesbank statistical purpose code for capital contributions (e.g., 'Z10' for equity capital acquisition) so that the bank can complete the AWV reporting obligation when the payment exceeds EUR 12,500. Prior verification by the German notary (Notar) is required; the notary confirms the funds are freely available at the disposal of the managing director and issues the release certificate after receiving the bank's credit confirmation. The bank will perform source-of-funds verification, including a UBO declaration, passport copies, and evidence of the shareholder's economic provenance; cash contributions, crypto-asset transfers, non-bank intermediary transfers, or third-party structured deposits are prohibited. After the injection, the GmbH must update the commercial register and obtain the final tax certificate.Outbound disbursements for commercial inspection, testing, and QC services are settled using the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for individual high-value trade payments to foreign factories, laboratories, or audit agents, with explicit payment purpose text under the German AWV classification, e.g., 'Zahlung für technische Inspektions- und Prüfdienstleistungen / Rechnung Nr. X'; (ii) SWIFT MT202 COV whenever the payment is sent through a correspondent bank of the German originator to a beneficiary bank, to ensure cover risk and compliance with UBO screening; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR for payments to EU-based sub-agents and subcontractors; and (iv) for larger inspection contracts linked to goods deliveries, Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) with inspection certificates, test reports, and pre-shipment inspection findings listed as required documents, plus documentary collection (SWIFT MT400/MT422). All outbound payments to non-EU counterparties must comply with the Bundesbank monthly statistical reporting threshold of EUR 12,500, be screened against EU sanctions lists, and be supported by the corresponding invoice, inspection report/purchase order, and the GmbH's banking-account policy. Cross-currency conversions are executed at the European Central Bank reference rate of the payment day with a disclosed margin; no foreign-exchange approval is required.Plain Vanilla Outright FX Forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) – permitted without a separate license for commercial corporations when executed through a licensed German or EU credit institution and backed by an ISDA or German Rahmenvertrag (Deutscher Rahmenvertrag für Finanztermingeschäfte).FX Spot Transactions – freely permitted for operational liquidity conversion at market rates via the corporate bank.Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) and Interest Rate Swaps – permitted for non-bank German corporations when transacted with a licensed bank under EMIR derivatives regulations, including EMIR clearing, margining, and transaction reporting obligations.FX Options (plain vanilla call/put) – permitted for corporate hedging of commercial exposure, including barrier and European options, with MiFID II classification of the GmbH as an eligible counterparty or professional client.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on restricted convertible currencies (e.g., certain Asian and Latin American currencies without an onshore deliverable market) – permitted under German and EU law solely through an internationally licensed bank, subject to NDF ISDA Annex documentation, ISDA AMI paper, and the absence of an onshore deliverable market restriction.Currency Futures on regulated German/EU exchanges (Eurex) – permitted for standardized exchange-traded EUR and USD contracts.Basis Forwards (Date-Adjusted Forwards) for invoice-date and settlement-date mismatch hedging – permitted as a customized OTC instrument executed bilaterally with the bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for strictly operational treasury use (paying foreign auditors, contractors, and small disbursements) but NOT viable as the primary corporate settlement account or statutory capital escrow account. Legal constraints under German law (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG): any Electronic Money Institution (EMI) serving German entities must hold a BaFin license or an EU single-license passport; an offshore EMI without a German or EU license is prohibited from operating in Germany. Licensed EU EMI providers (e.g., Wise Europe, Airwallex Ltd, and similar BaFin-passported entities) can legally maintain multi-currency operational accounts, issue IBANs, and settle via SEPA/SWIFT, but they cannot provide credit, are not eligible for capital-escrow confirmation by a German notary, and are generally rejected as the governing bank for GmbH registered-capital deposit verification under section 7/8 GmbHG. The recommended architecture is a Tier-1 German commercial bank as the anchor treasury and settlement bank, with a licensed EMI used only as a disbursement rail for low-value cross-border payments to sub-agents.

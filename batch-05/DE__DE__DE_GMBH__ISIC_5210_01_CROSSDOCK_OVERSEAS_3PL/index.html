Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register), maintained by the local Amtsgericht (Local Court) at the company's registered seat; electronic filings are submitted through the XJustiz portal (e.GNR – elektronisches Gesellschaftsregister) and published in the Bundesanzeiger/Unternehmensregister (www.handelsregister.de / www.unternehmensregister.de).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbHG): Sections 1 (formation), 2 (notarial form of articles), 5 (minimum capital EUR 25,000), 7 (application prerequisites and blocked capital contribution), 8 (content of application), 10 (legal effect of registration), 35 and 39 (managing directors and registration of appointments), 78 (power of representation); German Commercial Code (HGB) Sections 8, 106, 108 (commercial register rules); Trade Regulation (Gewerbeordnung) Section 14 (trade notification); Union Customs Code (UCC) Article 9 (EORI identification).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) or the statutory model protocol (Musterprotokoll pursuant to Section 2(1a) GmbHG), accompanied by the notarially authenticated application for registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) under Section 7 GmbHG, the shareholders' list, director specimen signatures, and bank deposit confirmation.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital of EUR 25,000 (Section 5(1) GmbHG). Before registration, cash contributions must reach at least one-quarter per share and in aggregate at least EUR 12,500 (50% of the minimum capital), and the funds must be deposited and remain at the free disposal of the managing directors on a blocked escrow/corporate formation account (GmbH-Kapitalkonto with Sperrvermerk) of a German credit institution (Section 7(2)-(3) GmbHG). Non-cash contributions (Sacheinlagen) must be fully effected before filing and substantiated in a notarially audited valuation report. The blocking instruction is lifted only upon completion of the Commercial Register registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: submission of a fire protection concept (Brandschutzkonzept) and completion of the final acceptance inspection (Abnahme) of fire-alarm, extinguishing, sprinkler, and evacuation systems in accordance with the State Building Code (Landesbauordnung). Structural clearance: statical certificate (Standsicherheitsnachweis) and load inspection of racking structures and mezzanines issued through the building supervisory authority (Bauaufsichtsbehörde). Occupational clearance: mandatory registration with the statutory accident insurer (Berufsgenossenschaft), workplace conformity assessment under the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) and the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz), including rack safety inspections, forklift and mechanical-handling certifications, first-aid provisions, and operating-instruction protocol per the Industrial Safety Ordinance (Betriebssicherheitsverordnung — BetrSichV).

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum share capital of EUR 25,000 (Section 5(1) GmbHG). Before registration, cash contributions must reach at least one-quarter per share and in aggregate at least EUR 12,500 (50% of the minimum capital), and the funds must be deposited and remain at the free disposal of the managing directors on a blocked escrow/corporate formation account (GmbH-Kapitalkonto with Sperrvermerk) of a German credit institution (Section 7(2)-(3) GmbHG). Non-cash contributions (Sacheinlagen) must be fully effected before filing and substantiated in a notarially audited valuation report. The blocking instruction is lifted only upon completion of the Commercial Register registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.); the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for the domestic tax number, corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration. Tax registration questionnaires are generally submitted electronically through the ELSTER electronic tax portal.

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH must register with its competent local Finanzamt after incorporation by submitting the electronic questionnaire for tax registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer). The company may separately apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), generally through the tax registration process or the BZSt application procedure. The application should include the commercial register extract, articles of association, registered office, managing directors, business activities, bank details, expected turnover, intended intra-EU transactions, and evidence of actual or planned business operations. A non-resident foreign entity making taxable supplies in Germany must register with the relevant German Finanzamt and obtain a German tax number and, where required, a USt-IdNr.; the competent Finanzamt depends on the nature and location of the taxable activity. VAT registration is required before the relevant taxable transactions are reported, subject to applicable small-business or reverse-charge rules. Tax identification numbers must be used on tax returns and legally compliant invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is generally subject to German corporate income tax at entity level and German trade tax; Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a GmbH. A German partnership may elect corporate taxation under Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act, but this election is not available to a GmbH because a GmbH is already a corporation. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules, principally Section 4k of the Income Tax Act and related provisions implementing the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive, may deny deductions or impose income inclusions for specified hybrid mismatches. These rules do not convert a GmbH into a pass-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany generally has no broad VAT registration threshold for taxable businesses. The small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG may apply where the entrepreneur's total turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year, subject to statutory conditions and EU cross-border small-business rules. The exemption is not a general exemption from tax registration and does not permit recovery of input VAT. The reduced VAT rate is generally 7% for specified supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualifying exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the European Union may be exempt from German VAT with input-tax credit under Sections 4 number 1(a) and 6 UStG, provided the goods are transported or dispatched to a third-country destination and the statutory export conditions are met. The supplier must retain prescribed evidence of export, normally including electronic customs confirmation and transport or delivery evidence. Goods supplied to a customer in another EU Member State are generally treated as an intra-Community supply rather than an export and require valid customer VAT identification, transport evidence, and statistical or recapitulative reporting where applicable. Logistics, warehousing, fulfillment, and other services are not automatically export zero-rated merely because the customer or goods are outside Germany. For B2B services, the place-of-supply rule under Section 3a(2) UStG may place taxation at the customer's establishment outside Germany, often under a reverse-charge mechanism. Services directly connected with qualifying exports may be exempt only where the specific statutory conditions are satisfied. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, proper accounting, and evidence linking the input costs to taxable or VAT-exempt-with-credit transactions.

  • Electronic export declaration submitted through the Automated Tariff and Local Customs Clearance System (ATLAS), including the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Export accompanying document or equivalent electronic customs export record
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and sales or fulfillment documentation identifying the exporter, consignee, goods, value, and destination
  • Proof of exit from the European Union, such as the electronic exit confirmation or equivalent customs-certified evidence
  • Transport evidence, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, carrier confirmation, or other prescribed delivery evidence
  • Customer order, contract, warehouse release record, and inventory movement records supporting the transaction chain
  • Proof of payment or other commercial evidence where relevant to substantiate the transaction; Germany does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a statutory condition for export VAT exemption
  • For intra-Community supplies rather than third-country exports: valid customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required EC Sales List reporting

Germany does not currently operate a general real-time VAT invoice clearance or mandatory transaction-level reporting system comparable to Italy's SDI. Since 1 January 2025, a German-established business must generally be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions, subject to statutory exclusions. Mandatory issuance of structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions is phased in: generally from 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and generally from 1 January 2028 for all in-scope businesses, with transitional rules and limited exceptions. Permitted structured formats include EN 16931-compliant XRechnung and suitable ZUGFeRD formats. Electronic invoices must contain mandatory VAT invoice data and be retained in an auditable manner. There is no general requirement for prior digital stamping, API pre-clearance, or real-time transmission to the tax authority.

Germany has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for claiming export VAT exemption. Export evidence must be available within the statutory tax record-retention period and at the time of a tax audit. If export conditions or documentary requirements are not met, the Finanzamt may retrospectively assess German VAT, deny related input-tax recovery where applicable, impose interest under Section 233a of the German Fiscal Code, and assess late-payment or filing penalties. Incorrect VAT refunds or input-tax credits may be clawed back with interest. Intentional tax evasion may constitute a criminal offence under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code, potentially carrying imprisonment or a fine; reckless tax reduction may be sanctioned under Section 378. Administrative fines, customs penalties, and confiscation or other customs consequences may apply for inaccurate or fraudulent export declarations. Invoices, customs records, transport evidence, contracts, and accounting records should generally be retained for the applicable German statutory retention period, commonly eight years for accounting records and ten years for books and specified tax records, subject to transitional and record-specific rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital accounts under the Foreign Exchange Act (Außenwirtschafts- und Zahlungsverkehrsgesetz - AWG). Capital account transactions including profit remittances to foreign shareholders are permitted without prior approval for legitimate corporate activities. Profit repatriation is subject only to standard tax filing obligations and beneficial ownership transparency requirements under the Money Laundering Act (GwG). No prior approval or surrender is required for routine corporate FX transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

German exporters must surrender foreign currency export proceeds to an authorized bank within four weeks of receipt. This mandatory surrender requirement applies to goods exports under the AWG. Conversion occurs at the prevailing market rate. Required documentation includes commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or equivalent shipping proof, proof of payment receipt, and export declaration where applicable. Non-compliance may trigger regulatory penalties and reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech EMI providers such as Airwallex offer cross-border payment and treasury services to German GmbHs. However, they must comply with the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz - ZAG) and BaFin oversight. Viability is medium; full licensing and enhanced KYC are mandatory, with potential restrictions on certain merchant categories.

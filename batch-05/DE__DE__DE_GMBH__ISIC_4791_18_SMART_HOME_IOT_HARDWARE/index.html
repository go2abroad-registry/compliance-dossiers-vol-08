Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Local German Tax Office (Finanzamt), in major cities the Finanzamt für Körperschaften, is competent for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), VAT (Umsatzsteuer) and payroll withholding tax; electronic filing is via the ELSTER portal (https://www.elster.de). The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt, https://www.bzst.de) issues the cross-border VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) and administers the OSS/IOSS schemes. Customs registration (EORI) and export declarations are handled by the Generalzolldirektion and the Hauptzollamt via the ATLAS customs system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the GmbH through a notarised articles of association; minimum share capital is EUR 25,000, of which at least EUR 12,500 must be paid in at registration; the company must be entered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). Step 2: File the trade registration (Gewerbeanmeldung) with the local Gewerbeamt; the trade office automatically notifies the competent Finanzamt. Step 3: Submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the Finanzamt within one month of formation or commencement of business, electronically via ELSTER or on paper; foreign-resident managing directors must provide identity documents, ownership details, anticipated turnover and bank account information. Step 4: The Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer, which is used for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT returns. Step 5: Submit a separate online application to the BZSt for the VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, format DE123456789), which is mandatory for cross-border EU transactions; issuance normally takes about two weeks. Step 6: Apply to the Hauptzollamt for an EORI number (DE plus 12 digits) before the first export declaration. Step 7: Register for the EU One-Stop-Shop or Import One-Stop-Shop with the BZSt for cross-border distance sales and B2C electronic services. A foreign entity without a permanent establishment in Germany must file the simplified VAT registration form (USt 1 TG) and has no de-minimis threshold; German VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A resident GmbH whose place of management and registered seat are in Germany has unlimited tax liability on worldwide income. It is subject to Körperschaftsteuer at 15%, Solidaritätszuschlag at 5.5% of the corporate tax (0.825% effective), and Gewerbesteuer at effective municipal rates of approximately 7% to 17.15% depending on the municipal multiplier (Hebesatz of 200%-490%); the combined effective corporate tax burden is approximately 29%-33%. A GmbH is a capital company and cannot be treated as a transparent pass-through entity. Germany has no income-tax-exempt zones; free ports exist only for customs and VAT purposes.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or hybrid entity classification election. Available statutory elections and group tools: (1) Organschaft (fiscal unity) for corporate income tax and trade tax under Section 14 KStG, requiring a majority of voting rights and a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) concluded for at least five years; (2) VAT fiscal unity under Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG based on financial, economic and organisational integration; (3) waiver of the small-business VAT exemption with an option for regular VAT taxation under Section 19(2) UStG, binding for at least five calendar years; (4) election to use the EU OSS and IOSS regimes instead of local VAT registrations in destination Member States; (5) partnerships may elect corporate taxation under Section 1a KStG, but this is not applicable to a GmbH. Hybrid mismatch rules under Section 4k EStG and ATAD apply automatically to German-resident entities; no elective opt-out exists.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). No exchange controls, no central-bank approval, no foreign-exchange allocation or licensing exist for imports, exports, dividends, royalties, intercompany loans or equity injection. Profit repatriation by way of dividend is legally free and requires neither Bundesbank clearance nor evidence of export performance; however, a 25% withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%) is levied on dividends unless reduced to zero or a lower rate under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable double-tax treaty. The only compulsory friction is statistical: any cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 must be captured and reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank with the correct AWV purpose code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion of export earnings into EUR, and no statutory repatriation timeline. Export receipts may be retained in full in USD, CNY, EUR or any other freely convertible currency in domestic or foreign bank accounts. The sole binding obligation is statistical reporting: each cross-border payment received or made in excess of EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV rules with the correct Verwendungszweck code (e.g. 'G100' for export or import of goods). Non-reporting or incorrect reporting is an administrative offense subject to fines.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German Central Bank), with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) as the competent supervisory authority for banking conduct, anti-money-laundering and sanctions compliance

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity contributions (Stammeinlagen) and shareholder loans must arrive in EUR via SWIFT MT103 to the GmbH's German bank account, preferably an account explicitly designated for notarial verification of the paid-up capital. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must carry the company name, the GmbH's German IBAN, the LEI if registered, and the Bundesbank AWV reporting code 'G600' (Direct investment - capital contribution / Stammeinlage). No central-bank licence or prior authorization is required; the receiving bank automatically records the transaction for Bundesbank statistics where the value exceeds EUR 12,500. The notary will verify the credited amount against the notarized articles of association and issue the statutory certificate of capital payment before final commercial-register registration; avoid partial, aggregated or round-trip payments because they complicate notarial verification and create avoidable AML suspicion.Operational treasury disbursements for smart-home IoT inventory: (a) for standard open-account purchases below approximately EUR 250,000, SWIFT MT103 in EUR, USD, CNY or HKD with AWV purpose code 'G100' (goods) or 'G200' (services) and the invoice reference; (b) for larger supplier orders, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 by the German bank to the seller's negotiating or confirming bank; (c) inside the EU/EEA, SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant rails are preferred over SWIFT for EUR flows; (d) USD settlement runs over CHIPS/Fedwire through the German bank's correspondent network; EUR high-value settlement runs over T2 (the euro real-time gross settlement successor of TARGET2). Every outbound payment above EUR 12,500 remains subject to mandatory AWV statistical reporting and must be reconciled with purchase orders, customs declarations, logistics documents and the marketplace settlement ledger.Outright FX forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) in EUR/USD, EUR/CNY and EUR/GBP to hedge committed procurement and forecast retail-revenue flows; require an ISDA or German framework (DRV) master agreement, LEI registration, EMIR transaction reporting and bank credit-line support.FX swaps (Devisenswaps) for short-term alignment of mismatched marketplace payout currencies against supplier currency needs.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or less-liquid currency pairs relevant to PRC invoicing (e.g. USD/CNY) with USD or EUR cash settlement executed through a German licensed bank; prohibited with unregulated or overseas non-bank brokers.Plain-vanilla FX options, including zero-premium collars, with notional strictly capped at forecast exposure; exotic and barrier structures are generally rejected by German bank compliance for mid-sized non-financial corporates.Cross-currency swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCS) where trade-finance loans, receivables-financing facilities or foreign-currency debt are subsequently introduced.Compliance guardrail: all instruments require a board-authorized hedging policy, documented underlying commercial exposure, LEI, ISDA/DRV documentation and must be classified as commercial hedging rather than proprietary speculation under German banking and supervisory standards.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium — EU/EEA-licensed and passport-licensed e-money institutions and payment institutions (e.g. Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Airwallex and comparable PSD2-regulated providers) can operate as secondary settlement rails for marketplace payouts (Stripe, Shopify Payments) and low-value batched operational payments. They are not acceptable as the sole treasury relationship for this GmbH: German notaries and primary bankers will not treat an EMI-held balance as reliable proof of paid-up nominal capital; EMIs generally cannot issue documentary credits, arrange intraday/corporate credit facilities, provide dedicated correspondent banking for high-value trade settlements or deliver robust multi-currency clearing. Feasibility is conditional on the provider holding a valid German/BaFin passport or EEA licence with notified pass-through, offering a German or EUR IBAN, maintaining segregated safeguarding accounts and providing SWIFT/SEPA access.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum statutory share capital is EUR 25,000 (GmbHG § 5). At least EUR 12,500 (half of the minimum capital) must be paid in cash and placed at the free disposal of the managing director before registration (§ 7(2)); a single-shareholder GmbH must pay the full EUR 25,000 or furnish security for the unpaid balance. Funds are held in a restricted business escrow account of the company in formation (GmbH i.G.) until entry in the Commercial Register, evidenced by a Bankbescheinigung.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and structural clearance of the registered office under the respective state building code (Bauordnung), including a fire protection acceptance (brandschutztechnische Abnahme) by the local building authority or fire brigade; workplace safety inspection under the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) and the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz), including a documented risk assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung), registration with the employer's liability insurance association (Berufsgenossenschaft) under DGUV Vorschrift 1, and, where physical warehousing operations exist, structural load inspection and periodic electrical installation testing under the Industrial Safety Ordinance (Betriebssicherheitsverordnung).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office; local Finanzamt for corporate, trade and payroll tax administration; electronic filing via ELSTER portal (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH: (1) register with the commercial register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht to obtain the registered company number; (2) complete the online registration form (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) in ELSTER at the competent local Finanzamt; (3) receive the local tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax, trade tax and payroll tax; (4) apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer); (5) file VAT registrations and periodic VAT returns electronically. A non-resident company without a German seat but with taxable German activities must register through the same ELSTER procedure or appoint a fiscal representative/authorized agent in accordance with Sec. 18 UStG.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a German GmbH: (1) register with the commercial register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht to obtain the registered company number; (2) complete the online registration form (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) in ELSTER at the competent local Finanzamt; (3) receive the local tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax, trade tax and payroll tax; (4) apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer); (5) file VAT registrations and periodic VAT returns electronically. A non-resident company without a German seat but with taxable German activities must register through the same ELSTER procedure or appoint a fiscal representative/authorized agent in accordance with Sec. 18 UStG.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a check-the-box system; a GmbH is a fixed corporate taxpayer subject to unrestricted corporate income tax liability. Statutory elections available include: corporate fiscal unity (Organschaft) under Secs. 14, 17 KStG, resulting in pooling of income for corporate income and trade tax; VAT grouping under Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG; trade tax consolidation and apportionment elections. No hybrid-mismatch tax election is available for a domestic GmbH beyond the anti-hybrid rules enacted under ATAD.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Any entrepreneur making taxable supplies in Germany must register for VAT without a general turnover threshold; optional small-business exemption under Sec. 19 UStG applies for turnover not exceeding EUR 22,000 in the preceding calendar year and not exceeding EUR 50,000 in the current calendar year. The EU distance-selling and cross-border electronically supplied services threshold is EUR 10,000 for the application of VAT in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Sec. 6(1) UStG, supplies of goods transported by the supplier or the buyer to a destination outside the European Union qualify for the 0% export VAT rate if documentary and bookkeeping evidence is maintained. Intra-Community supplies to a business customer in another EU member state are tax-exempt under Sec. 4 No. 1(b) and Sec. 6a UStG subject to receipt of a valid VAT ID and transport proof. B2B platform and services supplied to a business customer established outside Germany are deemed supplied in that customer's country under the general B2B place-of-supply rule of Sec. 3a(2) UStG, and therefore are either outside the scope of German VAT or zero-rated without German output VAT, provided the business status and foreign establishment of the customer are documented.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) accepted by German Customs in the ATLAS electronic export system, including the Ausfuhrbegleitdokument/reference
  • Transport document evidencing movement out of the EU: bill of lading, airway bill, CMR freight note, or multimodal transport document
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract reflecting the parties, consideration and shipment terms
  • Final proof of export movement (Ausfuhrnachweis) from German Customs, including container departure, border exit, or electronic export manifest evidence
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank/payment confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in accordance with the terms of sale

Since 1 January 2025, all German domestic B2B recipients must be able to receive electronic invoices; suppliers must issue structured e-invoices in XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.3 formats for domestic B2B transactions, transmitted via Peppol, dedicated email or other interoperable networks. There is currently no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or API pre-clearance system in Germany; VAT returns, recapitulative statements and other tax filings are made electronically through ELSTER. Future compliance will incorporate the EU ViDA real-time digital reporting and e-invoicing framework once enacted.

Zero-rating is denied where the underlying export movement is not substantiated, and the full German VAT is assessed retroactively with input VAT recovery refused, subject to 0.5% monthly interest on underdeclared VAT under Sec. 233a AO, late payment surcharges under Sec. 240 AO, and late filing penalties under Sec. 152 AO. Fraudulent use of false export declarations or staging of missing-trader intra-Community (MTIC) supply chains constitutes tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, punishable with imprisonment of up to ten years and monetary fines per Sec. 369/370 AO. Joint and several liability may arise under Sec. 71 AO for liable parties; the VAT exemption can be clawed back retrospectively for up to ten years in cases of fraud under Sec. 169(2) AO, with the claimant also exposed to criminal penalties and public debarment in e-export mechanisms.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current account and capital account convertibility under Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No exchange control restrictions apply to EUR profit repatriation, dividend distributions, intercompany loan repayment, or capital liquidation proceeds of a German GmbH. The only operative hurdles are (i) statistical reporting to the Bundesbank for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500, (ii) EU and UN sanctions screening and German export-control checks under AWV, and (iii) standard tax gateways, including 25% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and trade tax at entity level; dividend withholding tax is generally reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or to a treaty-reduced rate where the shareholder is a non-EU resident and an applicable double tax treaty and substance requirements are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds and no statutory conversion ratio for foreign currency receipts. Exporters may freely hold, use, or convert foreign currency. However, German residents must report any cross-border payment or receipt exceeding EUR 12,500, including export proceeds, to the Deutsche Bundesbank within seven days for balance-of-payments statistics under AWV Section 67. Failure to file is an administrative offence subject to fines. B2B marketplace receipts routed through payment aggregators remain reportable if the resident merchant is the German entity and the aggregated flow crosses the threshold.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) as banking and financial market supervisor and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action as the competent authority under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High feasibility. A BaFin-licensed or EU-passported Electronic Money Institution (EMI) and Payment Institution under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and PSD2 may open payment accounts, issue IBANs, execute SEPA and SWIFT cross-border payments, and provide merchant acquiring for the B2B marketplace without a full banking license. Key legal constraints: EMIs cannot grant interest-bearing credit, hold long-term client deposits, or provide investment services; client funds must be safeguarded by segregation or insurance; the EMI must maintain a compliant transaction-monitoring framework under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG). Passporting of foreign EMIs into Germany is permitted, but the B2B platform should verify BaFin registration and the safeguarding agent to prevent clearing-rail fragility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for equity injection uses SWIFT MT103 with the clearing field referencing the GmbH formation notary file and the Sperrkonto number; the Bundesbank AWV statistical code for direct investment in a German GmbH must be applied and reported within seven days where the amount exceeds EUR 12,500. Cash contributions are blocked in the capital escrow account until the Handelsregister registration is certified by the notary. For shareholder loans, an intercompany loan agreement with arm's-length interest, a subordination clause if appropriate, and thin-capitalisation documentation under the German interest deduction limitation rule (Zinsschranke, Sec. 4h EStG) are required; the repayment schedule must be reflected in the cash-flow forecast and tax balance sheet to avoid recharacterisation as constructive equity.Primary clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 (and SWIFT gpi) for non-EUR and high-value B2B settlements; SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for EU buyers; Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) confirmed or advised by the relationship bank for first-time or high-value cross-border buyers; Documentary Collections (DP/DA) as a lower-cost alternative. Marketplace seller payouts are routed via payment service provider settlement accounts and the target Alipay Global Cross-Border Payments rail, subject to BaFin safeguarding requirements and FATF-compliant transaction screening. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if cross-border transactions with related parties exceed EUR 100,000 for goods or EUR 50,000 for services; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a separate ESR regime, but general anti-abuse rules (Section 42 AO) and substance requirements under DTA and EU law apply. A GmbH must have real economic activity (office, employees, management) to avoid treaty denial or reclassification as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine; in aggravated cases up to 10 years. Additional penalty of up to 10% of evaded tax (Section 370 AO).

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they fail to file returns or pay taxes (Section 34 AO). Civil restitution, travel bans, and asset seizure possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and imposition of penalty taxes up to 30% of the tax advantage (Section 42 AO).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly operate a sham entity; criminal charges for tax evasion may apply.
AML Violations (Geldwäschegesetz)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 100,000 for minor violations; up to EUR 1 million or 10% of annual turnover for serious violations (Section 56 GwG).

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned up to 5 years for intentional AML breaches; civil liability for damages.

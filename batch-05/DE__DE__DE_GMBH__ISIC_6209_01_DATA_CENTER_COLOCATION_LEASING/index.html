Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office, BZSt); local tax office competent for the registered seat (Finanzamt); official portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A foreign shareholder establishes a GmbH through commercial register registration; thereafter the GmbH must file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the local Finanzamt to obtain the tax number (Steuernummer). For VAT purposes, the GmbH must apply for a German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) from BZSt, submitting the prescribed form with proof of business activity and identity documents. The Steuernummer is the primary TIN for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment; the VAT ID is used for intra-Community transactions and EU cross-border invoicing.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign shareholder establishes a GmbH through commercial register registration; thereafter the GmbH must file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the local Finanzamt to obtain the tax number (Steuernummer). For VAT purposes, the GmbH must apply for a German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) from BZSt, submitting the prescribed form with proof of business activity and identity documents. The Steuernummer is the primary TIN for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment; the VAT ID is used for intra-Community transactions and EU cross-border invoicing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box election; a GmbH is per se a taxable corporation under KStG. Available elections include formation of an ertragsteuerliche Organschaft (corporate fiscal unity) with qualifying subsidiaries for corporate income tax and trade tax, and VAT Organschaft under UStG § 2(2) for VAT grouping. Hybrid mismatch elections are unavailable; German law applies EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive rules and the German Hybrid Mismatch Act (Umsetzungsgesetz ATAD).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies; every entrepreneurial activity requires VAT registration. Non-established entrepreneurs must register for VAT when making taxable supplies in Germany. Intra-Community distance sales have an EU-wide threshold of EUR 10,000; no separate national threshold exists for services. B2B colocation and hosting services supplied to a non-German business are generally outside German VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating for goods: 0% VAT applies when tangible goods are dispatched or transported from Germany to a destination outside the European Union (export) or to another EU Member State (intra-Community supply), provided the supplier retains documentary proof of transport and the recipient is identifiable. For cross-border B2B services such as data-centre colocation and hosting supplied to an overseas entrepreneur, the place-of-supply rules typically shift the place of supply to the recipient's country, resulting in zero German VAT via the reverse-charge mechanism; however, if the service qualifies as a service relating to immovable property, German VAT applies because the data-centre space is situated in Germany. Claims for zero-rating require the supplier to demonstrate that the services are performed for a non-German business recipient and to obtain a valid VAT registration number of the recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrbegleitschein/Export Accompanying Document)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR consignment note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank proof of foreign payment
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Proof of transport (delivery notes, customs seals, carrier statements)
  • For intra-Community supplies: recapitulative statement and customer's valid EU VAT number

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. Mandatory B2B e-invoicing applies from 1 January 2025; all domestic and cross-border B2B invoices must be issued in a structured electronic format such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD. Transmission occurs via EDI, portal, or email; there is no government API clearance or prior authorisation. Tax authorities require secure archiving of full invoice data and may access it during audit.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict documentary audit. Missing or false proof permits the Finanzamt to claw back the exemption and assess VAT with late-payment surcharges of 1% per month under UStG § 18 and AO § 240, plus interest of 6% per annum under AO § 233a. Intentional or reckless false claims constitute tax evasion under AO § 370, punishable by fines up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment; administrative fines and professional disqualification may also apply. The burden of proof lies on the taxpayer to provide complete and consistent evidence within retention periods and deadlines. In addition, the German tax authorities may apply penalty payments of up to 10% of the assessed VAT where records are deemed inadequate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains comprehensive currency convertibility without any exchange control licensing. The Euro is fully convertible on current and capital account under TFEU Art. 63 and the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG); non-residents may freely inject equity, obtain loans and repatriate dividends without FX restriction. Dividend remittance is not FX-blocked; however, domestic dividend withholding tax (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, i.e. 26.375%) may be reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholder or to a treaty-reduced rate under the US-German DTA at 5%/15% upon a valid relief application. A non-EU investor acquiring 10% or more of the voting rights in a data-centre operator that is classified as critical infrastructure under the BSI Act may trigger an investment-screening notification under AWV Section 55 et seq. before completion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no forced surrender, no mandatory repatriation period, and no conversion ratio for export or colocation proceeds. The GmbH may freely retain EUR or foreign-currency receipts in any domestic or external account. The only statutory requirement is statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank for every payment from a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500, which must be filed by the 7th day of the month following the payment pursuant to AWV Section 70 using the prescribed external-payments code covering trade in services and leasing. Non-compliance can attract administrative fines under AWG Section 39, but there is no surrender penalty or FX-conversion timeline.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (together with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action for investment-screening and BaFin for banking/AML supervision)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High as a complementary FX and collection rail, but not a substitute for a universal bank mandate. German-licensed EMIs such as Airwallex GmbH, N26 Bank GmbH, PPRO Financial Ltd and MangoPay S.A. can provide multi-currency IBANs, SWIFT connectivity and automated monthly tenant collection under the Payment Services Directive 2; funds are safeguarded, not deposit-guaranteed, under the E-Money Directive 2009/110/EC. EMIs cannot issue commercial letters of credit, grant overdraft facilities or provide derivative hedging, so all capex financing, LC issuance and FX risk management must remain with the tier-1 German commercial bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal burden of 15.825%. Municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and generally produces a combined corporate tax burden of approximately 23% to 33%, depending on the municipality; the nationwide average is approximately 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital gains withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic withholding burden of 26.375%. A qualifying EU parent may obtain exemption under the EU Parent-Subsidiary regime, normally requiring at least 10% direct participation and satisfaction of anti-abuse and minimum holding-period requirements. Treaty rates commonly reduce the rate to 5%, 10%, or 15%, depending on the treaty, ownership percentage, beneficial ownership, and procedural conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally by 31 July of the following calendar year for a taxpayer filing without a tax adviser. Where a tax adviser prepares the return, the statutory adviser extension generally runs to the last day of February of the second following calendar year, subject to statutory exceptions, audit orders, and temporary legislative extensions.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally follows the annual corporate tax return deadline: 31 July of the following year without a tax adviser, or generally the last day of February of the second following year where the statutory adviser extension applies.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Wage tax is generally due and reportable by the tenth day after the end of the relevant wage-tax period. Capital investment income withholding is generally due and reportable by the tenth day after the end of the month in which the income was paid, subject to specific statutory rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border transactions with related parties, a Local File is generally required where annual transaction volumes exceed EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing, and royalties. A Master File is generally required where the German entity belongs to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Documentation must also be produced on request, generally within 60 days, and within 30 days for extraordinary transactions or certain audit requests.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone United Arab Emirates-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime. Substance is nevertheless relevant under German transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-abuse rule in section 42 AO, treaty beneficial-ownership and principal-purpose analyses, place-of-management rules, permanent-establishment rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, and anti-hybrid provisions. A data-center GmbH should maintain German premises, operational capacity, personnel or outsourced functions under effective German control, decision-making evidence, asset and risk records, customer contracts, power and network arrangements, and commercially credible capitalization. Contractual labels do not determine substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax declarations

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 AO is punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years. Particularly serious cases may attract imprisonment from six months to ten years. Unpaid tax, interest, late-payment charges, and confiscation-related consequences may also apply.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal prosecution, personal tax liability for intentional or grossly negligent breach of tax duties under section 69 AO, civil claims, disqualification consequences, and asset seizure. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered in criminal or enforcement proceedings. Imprisonment requires a criminal conviction.
Withholding-tax default and concealed profit distribution

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may remain liable for the unwithheld tax, solidarity surcharge, interest, and penalties. Intentional conduct may constitute tax evasion or aiding tax evasion. Distributions may be reclassified for corporate income tax and trade-tax purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability under section 69 AO for failure to ensure withholding and remittance, in addition to criminal liability where intent or participation is established.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust income under the arm's-length principle, deny deductions, assess additional corporate and trade tax, impose interest, and apply documentation-related surcharges. Documentation deficiencies may trigger a surcharge generally based on the income adjustment, subject to statutory minimums and maximums; delayed or incomplete documentation can also generate daily surcharges within statutory caps.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal tax liability for intentional or grossly negligent tax-duty breaches and criminal exposure where the pricing model was knowingly designed to evade tax.
Missing or defective transfer-pricing documentation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may estimate the arm's-length result adversely and impose documentation surcharges, commonly with a statutory minimum of EUR 5,000 and additional amounts linked to the income adjustment or delay, subject to statutory caps.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be liable for avoidable tax losses caused by deficient controls and may face criminal exposure if documentation was deliberately falsified or withheld.
VAT fraud and carousel arrangements

Statutory sanction: Input-tax denial, VAT assessments, interest, penalties, and criminal prosecution may follow. VAT evasion is subject to the criminal sanctions under section 370 AO, including imprisonment of up to five years or, in particularly serious cases, six months to ten years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid VAT and may face prosecution, seizure, insolvency-related claims, and imprisonment after conviction.
Shell company, sham management, or artificial permanent-establishment structure

Statutory sanction: Possible consequences include denial of treaty benefits, German residence or permanent-establishment taxation, transfer-pricing adjustments, withholding-tax exposure, tax evasion charges, and beneficial-ownership scrutiny.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil damages, tax liability, criminal liability, director disqualification, and asset-freezing consequences. Corporate limited liability is not a shield against personal misconduct or statutory director liability.
Beneficial-ownership and AML violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German AML framework may be substantial and can reach up to EUR 1 million or higher statutory amounts for specified obliged entities, including turnover-based maxima in serious cases. Criminal money-laundering liability may also arise.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal administrative or criminal liability, confiscation, professional restrictions, and civil claims. Travel bans are case-specific and not automatic.
Insolvency and late filing

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include repayment claims, insolvency clawbacks, civil damages, criminal liability under the Insolvency Code, tax liability, and director disqualification. The insolvency filing deadline is generally three weeks for illiquidity and six weeks for over-indebtedness, subject to statutory requirements and feasibility of remediation.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited post-insolvency payments and creditor losses and may face criminal penalties, including imprisonment, where statutory offenses are established.
Undocumented related-party financing and interest limitation abuse

Statutory sanction: Interest deductions may be denied, debt may be recharacterized as equity or a hidden distribution, and additional corporate and trade tax, withholding tax, interest, and penalties may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for tax losses caused by knowingly abusive financing and may face criminal exposure where the structure involved intentional misrepresentation.
Employment, payroll, and social-security non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Back taxes, social-security contributions, interest, penalties, contribution surcharges, and criminal offenses under tax and social-security legislation may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for withheld but unpaid employee contributions and may face criminal prosecution for intentional non-payment.
Corporate records and statutory accounting failure

Statutory sanction: Consequences include accounting corrections, estimated assessments, publication fines, enforcement measures, tax penalties, and possible criminal or administrative liability for false reporting or obstruction.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face civil claims from the GmbH, shareholders, creditors, or insolvency administrator, together with personal tax and criminal liability where statutory duties were breached.

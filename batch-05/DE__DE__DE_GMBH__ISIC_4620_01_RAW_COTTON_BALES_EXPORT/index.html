Competent corporate registry

Local Amtsgericht (Registry Court) responsible for the company's registered office, acting through the electronic commercial register (Handelsregister) and notarial submission portal.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG), Sections 1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 10, 35, 39, and 41; complemented by Sections 106-108 of the German Commercial Code (HGB) on commercial register filings.

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital EUR 25,000; at least EUR 12,500 must be paid in cash before registration. The contribution must be made to a German bank account in the company's name and be at the free disposal of the managing directors; notarial escrow confirmation or bank certification is required.

Funding & unlock path: No fire-engineering operating permit required for a trading office; the registered office must satisfy local fire safety orders evidenced by landlord certificate or Fire Brigade inspection if more than 20 persons are present. Structural load inspections are not applicable unless warehouse storage of cotton bales exceeds 5 metric tons; in such case, a structural confirmation from a licensed engineer must be obtained. Occupational safety clearance is required under the Arbeitsschutzgesetz, including first-aid equipment, electrical equipment inspection, and hygiene facilities.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital EUR 25,000; at least EUR 12,500 must be paid in cash before registration. The contribution must be made to a German bank account in the company's name and be at the free disposal of the managing directors; notarial escrow confirmation or bank certification is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF), Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt), and local Tax Office (Finanzamt); central electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) for tax filings; BZSt online portal for VAT ID (USt-IdNr) applications.

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH obtains its local income/corporate tax number (Steuernummer) by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the locally competent Finanzamt, describing legal form, owners, business activities, and expected turnover. A separate EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) must be applied for from BZSt after registration with the Finanzamt; BZSt verifies identity, legal existence, place of management, and active economic activity. Non-resident shareholders do not prevent registration, but the GmbH must have a German registered seat or permanent establishment and a designated German tax representative where required. TIN/VAT ID issuance is generally electronic; the VAT ID is issued independently from the Steuernummer and may take several weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A German GmbH obtains its local income/corporate tax number (Steuernummer) by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the locally competent Finanzamt, describing legal form, owners, business activities, and expected turnover. A separate EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) must be applied for from BZSt after registration with the Finanzamt; BZSt verifies identity, legal existence, place of management, and active economic activity. TIN/VAT ID issuance is generally electronic; the VAT ID is issued independently from the Steuernummer and may take several weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' regime exists in Germany. Election mechanisms include: corporation tax and trade tax Organschaft fiscal unity (profit and loss pooling), VAT Organschaft (VAT fiscal unity), application for advance tax ruling (verbindliche Auskunft) from the competent Finanzamt, and election of the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme. Hybrid-mismatch elections are restricted by German implementation of ATAD I/II.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for German corporate entities; registration is obligatory for any entrepreneur performing taxable supplies in Germany. Small-enterprise relief (Kleinunternehmer) applies below EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 current-year expected turnover. Non-established foreign businesses supplying goods/services in Germany must register regardless of turnover, unless the supply is zero-rated or subject to reverse charge. Intra-EU distance-sales threshold is EUR 10,000 annual cross-border turnover (OSS election optional).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies under Section 4 No. 1 and Section 6 UStG to supplies of goods physically removed from Germany to a third-country territory (outside the EU), provided the entrepreneurial supplier furnishes proper export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis). The goods must be transported by the supplier, the buyer, or a third party to a destination outside the EU, and the buyer must be clearly identified. Services may be zero-rated only where statutory conditions are met, e.g. services directly related to export goods or specific cross-border B2B supplies; mere brokerage services to non-EU counterparties do not automatically qualify as export services. The VAT exemption is available only upon accepted customs export declaration and transport documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS/Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR consignment note
  • Official export proof from the customs office (Ausfuhrnachweis)
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation as supporting evidence of consideration

Germany has a phased B2B e-invoicing mandate. From 1 January 2025, every domestic B2B supplier must be able to receive structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 the obligation to issue e-invoices applies to suppliers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000, and from 1 January 2028 to all domestic B2B suppliers. Required formats are XRechnung and ZUGFeRD; transmission must be through EDI-compliant channels, e-mail, or portals. There is currently no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital invoice stamping platform in Germany; periodic VAT returns are submitted electronically through ELSTER.

Invalid or incomplete export proof for a zero-rated supply triggers retrospective VAT assessment at 19% plus statutory interest of 0.5% per month or part thereof. Negligent underdeclaration is punishable by a penalty of up to EUR 50,000 under Section 380 AO; intentional tax evasion under Section 370 AO is punishable by imprisonment of up to 10 years or a fine, with profit forfeiture and unlimited personal liability of the managing director(s). German tax authorities may extend assessment periods to 10 years in evasion cases and issue separate penalty notices for false export substantiation under VAT correction rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany uses the euro and has no general exchange-control regime restricting ordinary current-account or capital-account transactions. The euro is freely convertible for trade receipts, supplier payments, dividends, interest, intercompany settlements, and capital movements, subject to anti-money-laundering, sanctions, export-control, tax, corporate-law, and bank risk controls. A GmbH may receive foreign investment and remit distributable profits without a general foreign-exchange approval. Profit distributions require valid corporate authorization, distributable annual profits or other legally permissible distributable reserves, compliance with German capital-maintenance rules, withholding-tax processing, and consideration of applicable tax-treaty relief. Capital reductions, shareholder loans, upstream payments, and related-party transfers must be documented and may be challenged under insolvency, corporate-law, transfer-pricing, or beneficial-ownership rules. Cross-border payments may trigger German external-statistics reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), including reporting obligations based on transaction type and applicable thresholds. Sanctions and export-control restrictions may apply to counterparties, banks, routes, goods, vessels, or destinations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no general statutory requirement to surrender or mandatorily convert export proceeds into euros, and no general mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's commercial discretion. There is no universal statutory deadline for conversion of export receipts. The company must maintain accurate commercial, customs, accounting, tax, and payment records and assess applicable AWV external-statistics reporting obligations. Export transactions involving controlled goods, sanctioned parties, restricted destinations, dual-use items, or sensitive supply chains may require BAFA authorization or other controls. Banks may impose contractual deadlines, documentary requirements, transaction limits, or internal conversion policies even where German law does not require conversion.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank. Foreign-exchange administration is supplemented by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin), the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle, BAFA), and German customs authorities where prudential, sanctions, export-control, or statistical-reporting matters arise.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplementary operating, collection, and foreign-currency payment functions; Low as the sole bank for GmbH formation capital, regulated trade finance, documentary letters of credit, or complex commodity-trade flows. An offshore digital electronic-money institution cannot replace a German bank account where a notary, commercial register, auditor, counterparty, or bank requires a German or EU-regulated account structure. Use only an institution authorized under the EU Payment Services Directive or Electronic Money Directive framework and verify the exact license, passporting status, safeguarding model, permitted currencies, SWIFT or correspondent access, sanctions controls, German IBAN availability, account-holder name matching, and termination rights. EMI safeguarding is not identical to a bank deposit guarantee. High-risk jurisdictions, third-party collections, cash-intensive activity, opaque beneficial ownership, inconsistent invoices, and cotton-origin or sanctions concerns may materially reduce feasibility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route GmbH share capital and subsequent shareholder funding from the legally identified shareholder or documented lender through an EU or other regulated bank using SEPA Credit Transfer in euros or SWIFT cross-border transfer for foreign currencies. Use the exact legal name of the GmbH, IBAN, BIC, payment reference, and purpose description such as share capital contribution, capital increase, shareholder loan, or equity contribution. For formation capital, coordinate the bank confirmation with the German notary and commercial-register filing, and do not treat third-party funding as shareholder capital without documentary authority. For a capital increase, obtain the required shareholder resolution, notarial documentation, updated shareholder information, and evidence of payment. For shareholder loans, maintain a written agreement, maturity, interest, repayment terms, transfer-pricing support, thin-capitalization and withholding-tax analysis where relevant, and beneficial-owner consistency. Screen the remitter, intermediary banks, origin, destination, and payment purpose for AML and sanctions issues. Reconcile incoming funds to corporate records and assess AWV external-statistics reporting and tax-record retention obligations. Do not use unexplained personal accounts, cash deposits, informal netting, or unapproved payment agents.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT customer credit transfer, commonly MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent, for non-SEPA or foreign-currency settlements. For documentary trade, use a commercial letter of credit through the bank's SWIFT trade-finance channel, including issuance by MT700 or corresponding ISO 20022 trade message, amendments through the applicable trade message, document presentation, discrepancy handling, and settlement under the UCP 600 framework where incorporated. Documentary collection may use documents against payment or documents against acceptance under URC 522 where incorporated. Match each payment to the underlying contract, invoice, purchase order, customs documentation, bill of lading, certificate of origin, inspection certificate, and agreed Incoterms rule. For raw cotton bales and synthetic yarns, validate quantity, grade, quality, weight, origin, shipment route, insurance, warehouse custody, vessel, port, and title documents before releasing funds. Apply dual authorization, segregation of duties, beneficiary-account verification, sanctions screening, invoice fraud controls, and exception approval. Reject or escalate payments involving sanctioned parties, prohibited destinations, unexplained third parties, inconsistent goods descriptions, duplicate invoices, circular settlement, or payment instructions changed through unverified email. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) + 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT amount + municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 3.5% base rate multiplied by the local Hebesatz, typically 200%–490%; aggregate statutory burden for a typical German GmbH is approximately 30%–32% of taxable income.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and central taxpayer matters; the locally competent Finanzamt (tax office at the registered seat/place of management) administers corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), payroll tax, and VAT assessments. Primary digital portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); customs/export clearance is executed via the ATLAS e-customs system operated by the German customs authority (Generalzolldirektion).

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the GmbH notarially and register in the commercial register (Handelsregister) to obtain the HRB number. 2. File the electronic tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') with the competent Finanzamt via ELSTER to obtain the Steuernummer (statutory tax account number). 3. Apply separately to BZSt for the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr), stating the precise business scope (wholesale of active pharmaceutical ingredients, ISIC 4649/NAICS 424210). 4. Apply for an EORI number through the German customs portal for export declarations. 5. If employees are engaged, register in the employer payroll tax system (Lohnsteuer). 6. Obtain the mandatory pharmaceutical wholesale license under §52b Arzneimittelgesetz (AMG) from the competent German state (Länder) authority, which is a precondition for lawful trading in APIs and is cross-checked by tax auditors. 7. For a foreign owner without German residence, appoint a domestic process agent (Zustellungsbevollmächtigter) for the GmbH; non-resident shareholders have no restriction on GmbH ownership.

Resident GmbH entities have unlimited worldwide tax liability; income attributable to a foreign permanent establishment is generally exempt under German tax treaties, with credit method applied under non-exemption treaties. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A German GmbH is an opaque, non-transparent taxpayer: 15% corporate income tax, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax (aggregate 15.825%), plus municipal trade tax at an effective rate of approximately 7–17% depending on the municipality's assessment rate (Hebesatz). Resident GmbH entities have unlimited worldwide tax liability; income attributable to a foreign permanent establishment is generally exempt under German tax treaties, with credit method applied under non-exemption treaties. No pass-through attributes are available; dividends distributed to shareholders face 25% German withholding tax (plus solidarity surcharge) unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty.

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) corporate group fiscal unity ('Organschaft') under §14 KStG for corporate income tax and §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG for VAT, requiring a profit-and-loss pooling agreement concluded for a minimum of five years plus financial, economic, and organizational integration; (ii) option for partnerships to be taxed as corporations under §1a KStG (not applicable to a GmbH, which is already opaque); (iii) simplified export proof election under §13 UStDV for low-value exports; (iv) VAT small-business exemption waiver enabling input VAT recovery; (v) hybrid mismatch matching adjustments under §4k EStG in alignment with ATAD II (no elective application; mandatory rules).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Resident GmbH entities must register for VAT regardless of turnover if they perform taxable supplies; the small-business exemption under §19 UStG applies to supplies below EUR 25,000 (from 1 January 2025; previously EUR 22,000), but an exporting GmbH should waive the exemption to recover input VAT. The intra-EU distance sales threshold is EUR 10,000. Foreign suppliers without German establishment are generally not required to register where the B2B reverse charge applies, except for domestic B2C supplies, distance sales, or when claiming input VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies (Ausfuhrlieferung, §6 UStG) of pharmaceutical APIs to destinations outside the EU/UK qualify for 0% VAT, provided the goods physically leave the German customs territory and the exporter (or the buyer's forwarding agent for EXW terms) holds an approved export declaration; the export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) must satisfy §§8–9 UStDV, including electronic customs confirmation via ATLAS. Intra-Community supplies (§6a UStG) are zero-rated when the buyer supplies a valid EU VAT identification number, the goods are transported to another Member State, and the transaction is reported in the EU sales listing (Zusammenfassende Meldung). Sales of APIs to German domestic wholesalers are taxable at 19%, not eligible for zero-rating. API wholesale additionally requires an AMG §52b license; compliance with pharmaceutical storage and documentation obligations is routinely verified in export VAT audits.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung in the ATLAS system) with position-level commodity codes and EORI reference
  • Commercial Invoice (German/English) stating API composition, quantity, value, Incoterms, and export VAT reasoning
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from German customs territory
  • Export Proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) per §§8–9 UStDV, including the ATLAS export accompanying document or electronic customs confirmation issued to the exporter's EORI number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 confirmation evidencing payment of the export consideration from the foreign buyer

E-Rechnung mandate effective 1 January 2025: every domestic B2B supplier must be capable of receiving structured electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x); the obligation to issue e-invoices begins 1 January 2027 for taxpayers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 and 1 January 2028 for all others. From 1 January 2025, the new statutory digital reporting framework ('SafeBo') requires qualified cross-border B2B supplies to be transmitted electronically to German tax authorities without prior clearance; no digital stamping or real-time fiscal authorization (as in the Italian SDI) applies. GoBD-compliant records and EDP audit readiness are mandatory, and BZSt controls cross-border data transmission.

German tax authorities cross-match ATLAS export declarations against invoices, transport documents, and payments. Missing or defective export proof within the statutory assessment window results in output VAT adjustment recovery under §17 UStG, with late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (§233a AO) and potential §14c UStG trapped-VAT liability on improperly invoiced exports. Intentional export VAT evasion constitutes criminal tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years (up to ten years in aggravated cases) and fines; negligent shortfall under §378 AO carries fines of up to EUR 50,000. The assessment statute of limitations extends to ten years in evasion cases (§169 AO). Retrospective clawbacks of zero-rating and director disqualification may follow. Additionally, the AMG §52b licensing regime exposes uncertified API wholesalers to independent criminal penalties, reinforcing adverse audit findings in combined customs-tax-regulatory investigations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both capital and current account transactions under Section 1 of the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance; profit remittance is unrestricted in law, but a statutory dividend withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (resulting 26.375% total) applies unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable double tax treaty; no exchange control permits, approvals, or surrender requirements are imposed on EUR or freely convertible currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline for export proceeds surrender in Germany and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; export proceeds may be maintained in any permitted currency and retained in accounts abroad; however, every cross-border payment in excess of €12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical and monetary monitoring purposes, typically via the E-Zahlungsverkehr reporting system.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank and the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin); cross-border trade and capital transactions are governed by the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV) with monitoring and statistical reporting administered by the Deutsche Bundesbank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for non-EU offshore digital EMIs; an electronic money institution (EMI) operating in Germany requires a BaFin license or an EU/EEA passport under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and the E-Money Directive; unlicensed offshore EMIs cannot lawfully serve the German entity and face blocked clearing, reputational, and contravention risks.

Statutory minimum share capital is EUR 25,000 (approx. USD 27,000). Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office, in conjunction with the locally competent Finanzamt; electronic filing and official portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A newly formed German GmbH must first be notarized and entered in the Handelsregister (Commercial Register) at the local Amtsgericht. Within one month of business commencement, the managing director must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt, either electronically via ELSTER or in paper form. The Finanzamt assigns the local Steuernummer (tax number), and the BZSt issues the USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number) for intra-EU and international transactions. Foreign entities without domestic establishment must appoint a fiscal representative or authorized agent in Germany and follow the same questionnaire procedure, with the VAT number issued retroactively to the start of taxable activity upon formal registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the GmbH is a corporate body subject to German unlimited taxpayer status on worldwide income (Körperschaftsteuer at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and municipal Gewerbesteuer at rates of approximately 7% to 17%), with no general pass-through eligibility; retained profits and distributed dividends are subject to separate shareholder-level taxation under the partial-income/imputation-credit framework. — A newly formed German GmbH must first be notarized and entered in the Handelsregister (Commercial Register) at the local Amtsgericht. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the GmbH is a corporate body subject to German unlimited taxpayer status on worldwide income (Körperschaftsteuer at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and municipal Gewerbesteuer at rates of approximately 7% to 17%), with no general pass-through eligibility; retained profits and distributed dividends are subject to separate shareholder-level taxation under the partial-income/imputation-credit framework.

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections include: (i) Organschaft (corporate income tax fiscal unity and VAT grouping) under Sec. 14 and Sec. 17 KStG and Sec. 2 (2) No. 2 UStG, requiring financial, economic, and organizational integration, effective for income tax and VAT; (ii) the small-business exclusion under Sec. 19 UStG for turnover below EUR 25,000, though a re-invoicing GmbH will normally opt out for input-VAT recovery; and (iii) no elective hybrid-mismatch characterization — hybrid mismatch rules under Sec. 4k EStG (ATAD implementation) apply mandatorily as anti-abuse recharacterization rather than as a taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic turnover threshold for domestic German VAT registration — any entrepreneur effecting taxable supplies in Germany is registrable. EU-wide distance-sales registration threshold: EUR 10,000 (annual cross-border B2C supplies of goods or electronically supplied services). Small-business exemption under Sec. 19 UStG: EUR 25,000 (effective 2025, previously EUR 22,000), generally inapplicable to a GmbH operating as a commercial re-invoicing hub.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies (Ausfuhrlieferungen) qualify for a 0% VAT rate under Sec. 4 No. 1 in conjunction with Sec. 6 UStG where goods are physically transported by the supplier or a commissioned carrier to a destination outside the EU/EEA; the supplier must prove the export through documentary evidence (Belegnachweis) and accounting evidence (Buchnachweis), including the destruction/presence of the goods outside the EU. For offshore re-invoicing trade, the German GmbH must demonstrate that legal power of disposal over the goods was transferred to a non-EU purchaser and that the goods left the EU within the statutory evidence deadlines. Services performed directly in connection with exported goods may also be zero-rated in limited cases; general B2B services follow the reverse-charge mechanism under Sec. 13b UStG and the place-of-supply rules of Sec. 3a UStG.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) filed electronically in the ATLAS system with the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Transport document: original Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), or CMR consignment note evidencing physical exit of the goods from the EU
  • Commercial invoice (Rechnung) matching the customs declaration, with Incoterms and weight/value details
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/XT_Transfer wire confirmation evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency export proceeds in the German company's bank account

Mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing under the German E-Rechnung-VO and the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B suppliers and intermediaries must be able to receive structured electronic invoices; from 1 January 2027, issuers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue compliant e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all domestic B2B issuers must issue e-invoices. Accepted formats: XRechnung and ZUGFeRD 2.x as XML-based standards. Germany does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, or continuous transaction controls; invoicing data flows through standard periodic VAT returns (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung), annual VAT declarations, EC Sales Lists (Zusammenfassende Meldung), and payment data transmitted to EU CESOP/DATEX by payment service providers since 1 January 2024.

Export zero-rating is fully reversed if documentary or accounting evidence is deficient, the export movement is simulated, or the invoice is fraudulent; the Finanzamt retrospectively assesses VAT at 19% from the original supply date with arrears interest of 0.5% per month under Sec. 233a AO. Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under Sec. 370 AO triggers fines and imprisonment up to 10 years, with the assessment period extended from the standard 4 years to 7 years or 10 years in aggravated cases; incorrect statements in VAT returns and EC Sales Lists additionally attract penalties under Sec. 26a UStG. Managing directors and legal representatives may be held personally liable for clawed-back VAT; criminal prosecution for carousel/missing-trader export fraud is handled by the customs investigation authorities (Zollfahndung).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully convertible, liberalised foreign-exchange regime: no exchange-control licence, central-bank authorisation, or capital-account permit is required for inbound or outbound investment, profit repatriation, trade settlement, or treasury transfers, consistent with EU free movement of capital obligations under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The euro is fully convertible, and EU capital and current payments enjoy unrestricted freedoms. Profit remittance from a German GmbH is legally unimpeded but is subject to (i) corporate substance and hidden-distribution rules under German corporate and tax law, and (ii) a 26.375% German dividend withholding tax (25% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) where the shareholder is resident outside the EU/EEA, reducible under an applicable double-tax treaty (e.g., 5%–15% for qualifying corporate shareholders under the Germany–China DTA as amended by the 2014 Protocol); EU/EEA corporate shareholders benefit from the zero-withholding EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive subject to the 12-month minimum holding-period requirement. The only residual regulatory hurdle is a mandatory statistical notification to the Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border payments at or above EUR 12,500 per transaction; this notification is an ex-post statistical filing, not a licensing or authorisation step.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation, or minimum conversion requirement on export proceeds. A German GmbH may lawfully retain foreign currency receipts in offshore accounts or select its settlement currency freely. However, under Section 67 AWV, export receipts from non-resident buyers must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics where the individual transaction equals or exceeds EUR 12,500; the report must be filed electronically through the Bundesbank OPDF/Z5 channel within the statutory filing window, in practice no later than one month after the calendar month in which the payment is received. Non-compliance constitutes an administrative offence subject to fines of up to EUR 30,000. No conversion ratio, no central-bank deposit requirement, and no prior approval attaches to such proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank — the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany, exercising supervisory and statistical authority over cross-border payments, capital movements and balance-of-payments reporting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Aussenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Aussenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); prudential and anti-money-laundering supervision of banks is shared with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) under the Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz, KWG) and the Anti-Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz, GwG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: CONDITIONAL for operational liquidity (MEDIUM), LOW for structural capital and escrow duties. An EU/EEA-authorised electronic money institution (EMI) holding a PSD2 licence and passporting into Germany, or a BaFin-licensed German EMI, may be used for small-value multi-currency settlement flows, reconciliation wallets, and cross-border payables automation, provided client funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts. An offshore EMI without an EEA passport is legally restricted from rendering regulated payment services into Germany under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG); reliance on such a provider would breach GwG compliance expectations and materially weaken the defence file during any German bank KYC review or tax audit. Consequently, share capital contributions, shareholder loans, and escrow balances must never rest in an EMI; they must reside exclusively with a KWG-licensed German credit institution or an EEA-passporting bank with a German branch or cross-border service capability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

SWIFT MT103 (telegraphic transfer) is the mandated rail for inbound equity injections and shareholder loans. The remitter must state in the details-of-payment field: (i) the GmbH's full legal name; (ii) the IBAN of the Capital Escrow Account; (iii) the purpose code selected from the Deutsche Bundesbank AWV classification, namely 'Beteiligung an inländischen Unternehmen' for share capital or 'Darlehen' for shareholder loans; and (iv) the notarial reference number of the GmbH's incorporation deed. The German receiving bank applies final payment verification against the share subscription schedule, performs UBO and sanctions checks, and may suspend the credit until the shareholder resolution and contribution statement are lodged. Any inbound capital payment equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank on Form Z5; the receiving bank typically transmits the OPDF statistical report as agent, but the GmbH remains the statutory reporting party. Non-euro remittances are converted at the spot rate with the documented purpose code to avoid misclassification as a speculative FX transaction. In-kind contributions must be avoided for cash-clearing purposes because they create valuation disputes and hidden-contribution (verdeckte Einlage) risks under German tax law.Disbursements execute over three clearing rails. (1) SWIFT MT103/MT202 covers open-account supplier payments (TT_Wire) via the German bank's correspondent network (USD via CHIPS or FedWire; CNY via CIPS or SWIFT China), with each message carrying the invoice number, re-invoicing reference, and ICC Incoterms code to preserve the documentary audit trail. (2) Commercial documentary credits are issued as SWIFT MT700 (irrevocable letter of credit), amended via MT720, with standby credits via MT760 (Swift_LC), governed by UCP 600/eUCP and URR 725, and serviced by the German bank's trade finance desk. (3) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) are used for euro-denominated payments to EU/EEA and Swiss counterparties, providing T+0 or T+1 settlement at near-zero internal cost. For XT_Transfer cross-trade corridors (USD or other hard-currency settlements), the bank's FX desk converts euro at client instruction, and the outbound message flows through the US or non-EEA correspondent with full Originator Beneficiary Information (OBI) to prevent sanctions-driven interdiction. All outbound payments are matched to customs declarations and export records, captured in the AWV statistical log, and retained in documentary form for at least five years.Outright FX Forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) against EUR for major deliverable currencies (USD, CNY, GBP, JPY, CHF) with tenors up to 12 monthsNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on restricted or non-convertible onshore currencies (e.g., INR, KRW, BRL, IDR) executed through the German bank's NDF booking deskCross-Currency Swaps (XCCY / FX swaps) for matched EUR-USD and EUR-CNY trade-flow hedging and intercompany loan exposureSpot-plus-forward FX swap combinations for short-term liquidity repositioning between currency legs

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: CONDITIONAL for operational liquidity (MEDIUM), LOW for structural capital and escrow duties. An EU/EEA-authorised electronic money institution (EMI) holding a PSD2 licence and passporting into Germany, or a BaFin-licensed German EMI, may be used for small-value multi-currency settlement flows, reconciliation wallets, and cross-border payables automation, provided client funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts. An offshore EMI without an EEA passport is legally restricted from rendering regulated payment services into Germany under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG); reliance on such a provider would breach GwG compliance expectations and materially weaken the defence file during any German bank KYC review or tax audit. Consequently, share capital contributions, shareholder loans, and escrow balances must never rest in an EMI; they must reside exclusively with a KWG-licensed German credit institution or an EEA-passporting bank with a German branch or cross-border service capability.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% corporate income tax (15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT); plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 3.5% federal measurement rate multiplied by the local Hebemetz (typically 350%-490%), producing a combined effective statutory burden of approximately 30%-33% for a profitable GmbH

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax); reduced to 0% for EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% for 12 uninterrupted months under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (implemented by § 50d(3) EStG with anti-abuse substance conditions); DTA rates range from 0% to 15% (e.g., 0% at >=10% holding under the US-Germany treaty; 5% at >=10% under most German treaties; 15% for portfolio holdings); the GmbH acts as withholding agent and is personally liable for proper WHT collection and remittance under § 44 EStG

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 annual aggregate cross-border related-party transactions (goods, services, financing, royalties) triggers the mandatory Local File; EUR 500,000 annual aggregate for extraordinary transactions and transactions involving difficult-to-value intangibles (GAufzV); the Master File is due when the worldwide group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000; files must be produced within 30 days (60 days for extraordinary events) of a tax audit request; contemporaneous documentation evidencing arm's length pricing at the time of each transaction is essential

Economic substance scope: Germany has no common-law ESR statute but enforces economic substance through § 42 AO (abuse of legal forms) and § 50d(3) EStG (treaty shopping and Directive-shopping limitation). A GmbH used as a pure re-invoicing hub must possess its own offices, qualified employees, substantive business decision-making, real commercial risk (title, inventory, currency, credit risk), independent accounting records, and a German bank account. A letter-box company (Briefkastengesellschaft) that only routes invoices between related parties is disregarded for tax purposes, its income is attributed to the foreign beneficiary, treaty relief is denied, and the hidden profit distribution is treated as a taxable dividend subject to WHT.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years, escalation to up to 10 years for especially serious cases (commercial evasion, large scale, abuse of office); criminal fine calculated on the evaded amount; mandatory reassessment of tax plus evasion interest at 6% p.a. (0.5% per month) from the original due date; additional late surcharges up to EUR 25,000; publication of final conviction possible

Director personal liability: The managing director is criminally responsible in person and cannot hide behind the corporate veil; courts may impose travel bans, confiscation of passports, attachment of personal assets, and disqualification from serving as a director for up to 5 years; in addition, the director is jointly and severally liable under §§ 34, 69 AO for the GmbH's unpaid taxes and penalties when the tax loss results from the director's willful or grossly negligent breach of duties

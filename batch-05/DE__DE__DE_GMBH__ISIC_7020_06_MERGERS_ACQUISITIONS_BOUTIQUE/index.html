Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) through the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT IDs and the local Finanzamt for income, corporate, and trade tax assessments; electronic filing via ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A newly formed or foreign-owned German GmbH must complete and submit the Formular 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' to the competent local Finanzamt, either via ELSTER or paper, within one month of business commencement. The Finanzamt issues a local tax number (Steuernummer). After the tax assessment, the GmbH must apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr), using the official application form via ELSTER. A foreign entity without a domestic place of business but carrying on taxable supplies in Germany must register directly with BZSt/Finanzamt; a fiscal representative may be required if a German permanent establishment is created.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not offer a 'check-the-box' election comparable to the United States. A GmbH is inherently opaque and subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and trade tax. Available statutory elections include corporate and VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft), trade tax exemption or pooling rules, and anti-hybrid elections under the German implementation of ATAD. Partnerships are generally transparent, but a GmbH cannot elect pass-through status; only certain entities such as GmbH & Co. KG may be treated as partnerships for tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold applies to entrepreneurs with a German establishment; registration is mandatory for all taxable economic activities. Non-resident suppliers are registrable from the first taxable supply. A small-business exemption is available under Section 19 UStG where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, provided the business meets statutory conditions. In such cases, no VAT is charged and input VAT deduction is denied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero rate (0% VAT) applies to exports of goods physically removed from Germany to a destination outside the European Union, provided the seller or a contracting third party exports the goods and substantiates the export. The exemption is mandatory and requires documentary evidence of export within the statutory time limits. Supplies of certain services directly related to the sale or transport of exported goods may also be zero-rated or treated as not subject to German VAT. Cross-border B2B services supplied by the German GmbH to a non-resident customer are generally outside the German VAT remit under the place-of-supply rules when the customer is an entrepreneur established abroad. However, M&A advisory services involving German-held target companies or real estate may remain subject to German VAT depending on the place-of-destination and anti-abuse rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) accepted by the customs office
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation proving receipt of the export proceeds in a German or EU bank account
  • Commercial invoice and, where applicable, delivery or transport documents

Germany has implemented a phased mandatory e-invoicing obligation for domestic B2B supplies. From 1 January 2025, all German VAT-liable businesses must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD). Mobile sellers must issue e-invoices by 1 January 2027, and the e-invoicing obligation extends to all German businesses by 1 January 2028. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system; invoices are not digitally stamped or approved by tax authorities. Tax returns and VAT-related data must be transmitted to the tax authority using the standard tax data interface (ERiC) via ELSTER, and e-invoices must be archived for the statutory retention period.

The tax authority reviews export substantiation and documentary evidence during tax audits. If the zero-rating is claimed without valid proof of export, the undocumented VAT is assessed retrospectively with statutory interest of 0.5% per month from the original due date. Deliberate or fraudulent export zero-rating may constitute tax evasion under Section 370 AO, subject to imprisonment of up to 10 years, fines, and confiscation of unjustified VAT refunds or input VAT deductions. Negligent false claims trigger penalties under Section 378 AO and possible VAT adjustment notices. The civil clawback also applies to any input VAT credits claimed against export supplies when export status fails.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has full current and capital account convertibility for the euro and all freely convertible currencies. There are no exchange controls, no prior approval requirements for capital transfers, and no profit-remittance hurdles. Dividends, royalties, interest and capital proceeds may be repatriated freely, subject only to standard corporate-law documentation and applicable German withholding taxes. Statistical reporting to the Bundesbank is mandatory for cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulations (AWV).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters are free to retain, sell or use export receipts in any currency. Only outward-facing balance-of-payments statistics must be filed with the Deutsche Bundesbank when foreign assets or payables exceed EUR 5 million or when monthly cross-border payments exceed EUR 12,500; no physical turnaround of funds is required.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Deutsche Bundesbank), with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) responsible for banking and payment-services authorisation

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore EMIs. A non-EEA digital EMI cannot lawfully hold funds or provide payment services to a German GmbH without a BaFin licence or EU passporting under PSD2. An EEA-registered EMI (e.g., Lithuania, Netherlands) may passport into Germany, but enhanced KYC and source-of-funds verification mean German corporates under a risk classification of medium-risk enhanced (M&A advisory) generally benefit from a traditional tier-1 bank relationship. Offshore EMIs should only be used as a supplementary collection rail, never as a primary treasury or settlement account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions are routed via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer (if in EUR within the EEA) using the German balance-of-payments purpose code for equity participation (AWV statistical category 01/31 – investment in a German enterprise). The bank requires that the incoming wire match the notarised capital-increase resolution, the updated shareholder register, and the Transparenzregister disclosure. Funds above EUR 12,500 must be included in the company's monthly balance-of-payments statistics (Bundesbank payment statistics report). Payment completion is conditional on completion of a full source-of-funds enquiry before the credit value date; the GmbH should instruct shareholders to mark the wire with the precise purpose text 'KAPITALERHÖHUNG' and the GmbH's registration reference.Outbound payments include success-fee disbursements, compensation to cross-border advisers, and repayment of intercompany loans. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (German corporate income tax) on taxable income, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the KSt (0.825%), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) of 3.5% base assessment rate multiplied by the local Hebesatz (commonly 400–490%, i.e. 14.0–17.15%). The combined effective corporate tax burden is approximately 29.8% (Berlin 410%) to 33.0% (Munich 490%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withheld amount, aggregating to 26.375% on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders. A 0% rate applies upon successful invocation of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Article 5, Directive 2011/96/EU) for a corporate shareholder resident in an EU/EEA state holding directly at least 10% of the GmbH's capital for an uninterrupted 12-month period.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is statutorily due by 31 July of the year following the tax year (§ 149 AO). When the return is prepared by a German tax adviser, the deadline is automatically extended to 28/29 February of the second following year (e.g., FY2024 return due 28 February 2026). Interest at 0.15% per month accrues on any tax assessment paid after a 15-month interest-free period (§ 233a AO).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany applies statute and regulation thresholds under § 90(3) AO and the GAufzV: a Local File is mandatory where cross-border controlled goods transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000 per year or cross-border services (including management and success fees) exceed EUR 500,000 per year with a related party; a Master File is required when the worldwide group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000. The M&A boutique must also retain a contemporaneous arm's-length benchmarking study for any success-fee or intercompany service arrangements.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, substance is a decisive legal requirement under: § 42 AO (abuse of legal forms), the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test (Article 7), the German treaty-override clause § 50d(3) EStG, which denies DTA relief where a letterbox entity lacks personnel, premises and actual decision-making, and the EU ATAD rules. A German GmbH conducting cross-border M&A advisory must employ qualified personnel in Germany, hold client contracts and bank accounts in its own name, document the operational and reputational risk borne by the German board, and keep minutes of all board decisions; a mere 'mailbox' GmbH will be disregarded, with income reattributed to the place of effective management and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Intentional Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under § 370 AO (Steuerhinterziehung): imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years for aggravated cases (large-scale evasion, gang fraud); fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or twice the evaded tax; confiscation of the evaded amounts; late-payment interest at 0.15% per month for 5–10 years; revocation of the boutique's required license and disqualification from public contracts.

Director personal liability: Managing directors face personal prosecution by virtue of § 14 StGB and § 35 AO, joint and several civil liability for company tax debts under §§ 69–71 AO; travel bans and passport withdrawal under §§ 132, 163 StPO where flight risk is established; if the GmbH enters insolvency, § 64 GmbHG triggers direct liability for payments made after the insolvency arose, and the InsO administrator may bring criminal complaints.
Shell Company / Letterbox GmbH – Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment by the German tax office disregarding the GmbH under § 42 AO and Art. 4 OECD Model; profits reattributed to the place of effective management; denial of DTA benefits under § 50d(3) EStG and the MLI PPT; additional tax at up to 45% plus 6% interest; § 162 AO the burden-of-proof inversion allows the authorities to estimate income at a higher penalty rate; fines of up to EUR 25,000 for failure to register in the trade register and for incorrect beneficial-owner declarations; deregistration of the company in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the assessed tax as joint debtors if they knowingly acted on behalf of an abusive structure; tortious liability for 'existence-eliminating interference' per BGH case law forces the director to compensate all creditors; criminal conviction under § 266 StGB (Untreue) for appointing a strawman; withdrawal of the German residence permit and Schengen entry ban for non-EU directors on grounds of tax evasion.
AML / Gatekeeper Failure (GwG)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under § 56 GwG: up to EUR 100,000 for minor negligent breaches; up to EUR 1,000,000 for intentional or repeated serious breaches, or 10% of the total annual turnover of the boutique/group for severe violations; confiscation of illegal proceeds; the BaFin/supervisory authority may order a cease-and-desist, revoke the boutique's license, and publicly name the company.

Director personal liability: Directors personally face criminal liability under § 261 StGB (Geldwäsche) if they intentionally ignore red flags, with imprisonment of 3 months to 5 years and confiscation of their assets; civil damages to defrauded third parties; prohibition from managing any GmbH for up to 5 years; the tax authority can pursue the directors' private assets for unpaid FIU fines if the company is dissolved as a shell.

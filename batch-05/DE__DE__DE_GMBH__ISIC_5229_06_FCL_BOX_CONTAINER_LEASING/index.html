Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent local tax office (Finanzamt) administers corporate and VAT registration. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) through the BZSt online services. Tax returns and registrations are generally filed electronically through the ELSTER portal operated by the German tax administration.

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH must register with the competent Finanzamt after incorporation and commencement of business activities by submitting the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer). The GmbH or its authorized tax adviser may apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number. A foreign entity without a German establishment must apply to the competent German tax office for non-resident taxpayers, provide constitutional documents, commercial-register evidence, business activity details, management and bank information, and supporting contracts or invoices, and may then request a German VAT identification number from the BZSt. VAT registration is required where the entity carries out taxable transactions in Germany that are not covered by a reverse-charge, OSS, or other applicable simplification.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A German GmbH must register with the competent Finanzamt after incorporation and commencement of business activities by submitting the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer). The GmbH or its authorized tax adviser may apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number. A foreign entity without a German establishment must apply to the competent German tax office for non-resident taxpayers, provide constitutional documents, commercial-register evidence, business activity details, management and bank information, and supporting contracts or invoices, and may then request a German VAT identification number from the BZSt. VAT registration is required where the entity carries out taxable transactions in Germany that are not covered by a reverse-charge, OSS, or other applicable simplification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a GmbH. A GmbH is generally subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and solidarity surcharge on its worldwide income, with German trade tax applying based on its German trade-tax nexus. A GmbH may elect or qualify for VAT group treatment (umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft) where the statutory financial, economic, and organizational integration requirements are satisfied. Partnerships may in certain circumstances elect corporate income taxation under Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act; this election is generally not available to a GmbH. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules apply by operation of law and do not provide a general elective hybrid classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for a non-established supplier making taxable supplies in Germany that are not subject to reverse charge or an applicable simplification. For a German-established small business, the domestic small-business exemption under Section 19 of the German VAT Act may apply where prior-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and effective-date rules. The small-business exemption is not a general registration threshold for non-resident suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax deduction under the German VAT Act if the goods are transported or dispatched to a third country and the supplier proves both the export transaction and the goods' exit from the EU. The supplier must retain appropriate customs or alternative documentary evidence and establish the identity of the purchaser or recipient where required. Services are not generally described as zero-rated exports under German terminology. For B2B services, including potentially container leasing, the place of supply is generally the customer's place of establishment, with the recipient accounting for VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism where applicable. Leasing of movable tangible property requires analysis of customer status, lease duration, actual use, and any special place-of-supply rule. EU supplies may qualify for exemption as intra-Community supplies rather than third-country export exemption, subject to valid VAT numbers and evidence of transport.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and the export accompanying document or equivalent customs reference
  • Electronic confirmation of exit from the European Union, such as the electronic exit confirmation or an accepted alternative proof of export
  • Commercial invoice, lease or sale contract, packing or equipment identification records, and container numbers where relevant
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, sea waybill, CMR, rail consignment note, or freight-forwarder confirmation
  • Proof of delivery or destination evidence where required to corroborate export and transport
  • Evidence linking the invoice, container or equipment identification, customs declaration, and transport documentation
  • Bank payment or remittance evidence where commercially available; German VAT law does not impose a universal inward-remittance certificate requirement for export exemption

Germany does not currently operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or digital-stamping system. Since 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. Mandatory issuance of structured e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies is subject to transitional periods: generally until the end of 2026, with an extension to the end of 2027 for issuers meeting the applicable turnover condition; from 2028, the general mandatory issuance regime applies. Permitted structured formats include EN 16931-compliant XRechnung and suitable ZUGFeRD formats. Ordinary PDF invoices are not structured e-invoices. Cross-border invoices and transactions outside the statutory domestic B2B scope remain subject to applicable EU and German invoicing rules.

Germany has no general foreign-exchange repatriation requirement or mandatory 180-day remittance deadline. Export VAT exemption depends primarily on transaction qualification and documentary proof of export. If the exemption is improperly claimed, the supplier may owe the unpaid VAT retroactively, plus interest under the German Fiscal Code, potential late-payment surcharges, and correction obligations. Intentional or reckless false documentation may trigger tax evasion or tax understatement liability, administrative penalties, criminal sanctions, and liability for responsible individuals. Input VAT refunds may be denied or reclaimed, and the tax authorities may impose additional assessments following an audit. The statutory limitation period is generally four years for ordinary tax assessments, extending to ten years for tax evasion and five years for reckless tax understatement, subject to suspension and other statutory rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full capital and current account convertibility pursuant to Articles 63-65 TFEU. There are no exchange controls, prior approval requirements, or currency surrender obligations for resident or non-resident entities. Profit remittance from a German GmbH is fully permitted through shareholder distributions; non-resident shareholders are generally subject to German dividend withholding tax of 26.375% (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), which may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable double tax treaty, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders), or domestic law. Repatriation of share capital, dividends, interest, and liquidation proceeds is not subject to any convertibility restriction, but all cross-border payments exceeding the statutory threshold of EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory export proceeds surrender rule, no conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for repatriating export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in the invoicing currency or any freely convertible currency. The sole compliance obligation is statistical reporting under Sections 25 and 26 of the AWV: cross-border receipts and payments of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank on a transaction-by-transaction basis within five days of the value date. A breach of the reporting obligation constitutes an administrative offence subject to fines under Section 32 AWV.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) is the central monetary authority and the agent for foreign trade and payment statistics under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht - BaFin) is the competent prudential, AML/KYC and market conduct supervisor for German banks, financial services institutions, and payment/e-money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for non-EEA offshore digital EMI providers; MEDIUM for EEA-licensed EMIs passporting into Germany under PSD2. An offshore digital EMI without a German or EEA payment/e-money license cannot legally onboard a German corporate entity for operational euro clearing, custody of funds, or trade disbursements. A BaFin-regulated e-money institution or an EEA-authorized EMI with an EU passport can offer remote account opening, but German AML law requires a bank/FI relationship for the GmbH rather than an opaque offshore intermediary. The offshore EMI must not hold or transmit funds on behalf of German shareholders without being licensed, as this would constitute unlawful payment business under Section 1(1)(6) of the German Banking Act (KWG) and Section 1(1)(governing e-money) of the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital contributions into a German GmbH from non-resident shareholders must be made in euro by SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA CT for EU transfers) to the GmbH's German bank account. There is no foreign-exchange approval, but the transfer must be labelled with the correct purpose code, e.g. 'Zahlungszweck: Kapitaleinlage' and/or the AWV-statistics code, so that both the lending bank and Deutsche Bundesbank can classify the incoming payment. Before disbursing the funds, the German bank will require the notarial contribution agreement and the trade register extract evidencing registration of the share capital. Inbound payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank by the German recipient's bank, and the beneficiary must be able to provide the contribution receipt, the share subscription declaration, and a certified extract from the commercial register showing the increased share capital. For loan instruments, the routing must use a shareholding director resolution and signed loan agreement reflecting arm's-length interest; cross-border loan receipts will also need AWV reporting under section 25 AWV.Outbound disbursements for container leasing, equipment purchases, maintenance, insurance, legal fees and lease deposits are made via SWIFT MT103/MT202 customer transfers for non-EEA counterparties and via SEPA Credit Transfer for EU suppliers. Large capital acquisitions of container fleet and intermodal boxes are cleared through Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) opened by the German bank in favor of the seller, with payment against a loaded bill of lading, inspection certificate, and insurance documents, typically on a sight basis or a short usance LIBOR-adjusted basis. Trade disbursements to high-risk jurisdictions require post-shipment documentary evidence and may attract additional due diligence checks; all outgoing cross-border transfers of EUR 12,500 or above are captured by the German payment statistics (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik) and are subject to AWV reporting.Outright FX forwards (spot forward contracts) with a German bank if the underlying exposure arises from lease receivables or capital expenditure in a foreign currencyNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in restricted or thinly-traded currencies of less common container-leasing destinations, subject to EMIR collateral and risk-mitigation rulesCross-currency swaps (FX swaps) for converting USD lease income into EUR while hedging all principal and interest flowsFX options (European-style puts and calls) to cover lease-revenue currency contingenciesCommodity/container-fleet trade-linked derivatives only to hedge clearly documented currency and interest-rate risks; speculative derivatives are prohibited for an ordinary German GmbH without proper bank and regulatory perimeter approval

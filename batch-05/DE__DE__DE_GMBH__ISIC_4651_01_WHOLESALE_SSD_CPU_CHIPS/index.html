Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Amtsgericht - Registergericht (Commercial Register Division) for the district of the German registered office (for Berlin: Amtsgericht Charlottenburg, Handelsregister Department); electronic filing through the notary's EGVR interface to handelsregister.de; mandatory publication in the Bundesanzeiger.

Governing statute

GmbHG (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung / Law on Limited Liability Companies): § 2 mandating notarial form for the articles; § 5 minimum capital; § 7 pre-registration capital payment conditions; § 8 required documents for the registration application; § 10 registration and publication; §§ 35 and 39 regarding managing director appointment and the application for entry of directors; in conjunction with §§ 106-112 HGB (Commercial Register), §§ 14-15 GewO (trade registration) and § 138 AO (tax registration).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (Gesellschaftsvertrag or notarial model protocol — Musterprotokoll) together with the notarised application for registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister) submitted in certified electronic form, the signed shareholder list and the managing-director list; public notice in the Bundesanzeiger.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Tax Administration of Germany: Kompetenz: Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt), local Finanzamt (municipal tax office), and Federal Ministry of Finance; e-filing portal ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung, https://www.elster.de); VAT identification number portal of BZSt (https://www.bzst.de) for USt-IdNr. applications.

E-government filing portal

For a resident GmbH, first obtain a personal tax number (Steuernummer) by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (questionnaire for tax registration) with the locally competent Finanzamt after notarial incorporation and registration in the Handelsregister. The GmbH is then entered into the corporate tax, trade tax, and VAT files. Separately apply for the EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, §27a UStG) by electronic form submitted to the BZSt. The Local Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer, while the BZSt issues the VAT ID. Both registration steps must be completed before invoices are issued with a valid identification number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box election; a GmbH is per se a fully taxable corporation (Körperschaft). Announced statutory elections include: (1) corporate tax group (Organschaft) under §§ 14–19 KStG entitling profit-and-loss pooling with the parent, subject to profit transfer agreement and financial integration; (2) VAT fiscal unit (Organschaft) under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG only when a legal entity is financially, economically, and organisationally integrated; (3) flat-rate farm-forestry rules (§24 UStG) not applicable here; (4) small-business exemption (§19 UStG) if annual turnover is below the statutory threshold; (5) transitional disincorporation relief or reorganistation relief under UmwStG (§§ 20, 24 UmwStG) for asset transfer; (6) hybrid mismatch treatment as transposed from ATAD (no elective check-the-box); German law applies its own legal-entity classification and priority rules to hybrid instruments and branches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 annual previous-year turnover (2025 Kleinunternehmer threshold under §19 UStG), with the additional current-year ceiling of EUR 50,000; above these thresholds VAT registration is mandatory irrespective of turnover. For a GmbH engaged in wholesale of SSD and CPU chips, turnover will almost certainly exceed the threshold, so registration for VAT is compulsory from the start of operations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero-rated export under §6 UStG applies when a German supplier sells movable goods that are physically transported or dispatched from German territory to a third-country destination. Additionally, intra-Community supplies under §6a UStG are zero-rated where the goods move to another EU Member State to a business customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number. For non-EU exports, the GmbH must prove by customs export documentation that the goods have left the EU customs territory. Zero-rating is also permitted for shipments to an export warehouse or for consignment stock subject to the special rules. Offshore services (e.g., software installation, technical support) are not automatically zero-rated; the place-of-supply rules for B2B services (principal place of business of the customer) apply, with potential reverse charge in the customer's country.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrnachweis) - electronic export declaration accepted by German customs (ATLAS/ICE-Gerät) containing the MRN (Movement Reference Number) and confirming departure from the EU customs territory; if applicable, a T1/T2 customs transit document.
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), airway bill (AWB), CMR consignment note, or rail/ferry transport document evidencing the physical movement of the goods to the third-country destination.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or eligible alternative bank SWIFT payment document evidencing actual receipt of foreign currency proceeds within the compliance period.
  • Commercial invoice containing supplier name, recipient, item description, HS/ISIC reference, export incotermship, and indication 'zero-rated export under §6 UStG'.
  • Packing list and delivery note, preferably including export container/vehicle registration details.
  • Proof of receipt by the foreign consignee or acknowledgement of arrival (if available), as required by §§ 8–13 of the Umsatzsteuer-Durchführungsverordnung (UStDV) for tax-exempt export evidence.
  • For EU intra-Community supplies: valid customer EU VAT-ID together with a recapitulative statement (VAT summary return, 'Zusammenfassende Meldung') proof and transport documents substantiating the movement to the other Member State.

Since 1 January 2025, Germany mandates the ability to issue/receive domestic B2B invoices in a structured, machine-readable electronic format conforming to EN 16931 (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD/Factur-X). Channel transmission is via Peppol network or secure email; there is no real-time tax-authority fiscal clearance obligation, no API reporting to the tax office for each invoice, and no digital stamping or invoice-clearance server. The actual obligation to issue e-invoices is phased in: mandatory from 2027 for enterprises with a prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000, and fully mandatory from 2028 for all resident domestic B2B suppliers. An invoice without a compliant e-invoice format does not trigger fundamental invalidity of the VAT deduction, but may lead to adjustment and corrective documentation obligations.

The local Finanzamt may demand documentary evidence before enabling export zero-rating, and the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern can audit onward VAT-ID validity. If export documentation is incomplete, falsified, or not submitted on time, the VAT initially exempted is retroactively clawed back by a notice of assessment. Statutory interest accrues at 0.5% per month from the period when the tax liability originally arose, under §233a AO. In cases of negligent understatement, a surcharge of up to 10% of the understated amount can be imposed under §240 AO. Intentional misdeclaration or fraudulent export schemes (e.g., fictitious exports, circular carousel transactions) constitute tax evasion under §370 AO and are punishable by a fine or imprisonment up to five years. The limitation period for VAT fraud is ten years; e-accounting and e-invoice data must be retained for the full statutory retention period, usually ten years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement and Articles 63-64 TFEU; the euro is fully convertible with no quantitative limits, no approval requirements for current payments, and no mandatory conversion of foreign currency. Profit and dividend remittance from a DE_GMBH is legally unrestricted but operationally conditioned on (i) distributable profit evidenced by annual financial statements (Jahresabschluss), (ii) a shareholder resolution, (iii) payment of the statutory 26.375% withholding burden (Kapitalertragsteuer plus Solidaritätszuschlag) where applicable, and (iv) Bundesbank purpose-code reporting; remittance to sanctioned, embargoed, or AWV-customs-listed jurisdictions is prohibited notwithstanding formal convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory EUR conversion ratio for export receipts; export proceeds may remain offshore in any freely convertible currency. All cross-border payments (receipts and disbursements) exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction or cumulative reporting thresholds must be declared to the Deutsche Bundesbank by the 7th calendar day of the month following the transaction under AWV Section 67, via the Bundesbank electronic reporting portal (eStatistik/FTZ), using the prescribed payment-purpose codes (goods, services, direct investment Y, loans U). Failure to report constitutes an administrative offence with fines; the customs ATLAS declaration serves as the underlying documentary evidence for goods-related flows.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German Federal Bank, Frankfurt am Main) is the competent monetary authority for external economic reporting and balance-of-payments statistics under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) concurrently supervises licensed German banks, payment institutions, and AML/CFT compliance for treasury and clearing operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for EEA-authorized and BaFin-registered electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating with a German IBAN under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and PSD2 passporting; LOW for offshore non-EEA digital EMIs not holding a German or EU payment licence. Legal constraint: any non-EEA EMI servicing a German GmbH without a ZAG licence or BaFin passport engages in unlicensed payment services, rendering transactions potentially void as a matter of private law (unwirksam) and exposing the GmbH to frozen-corridor, lost-wire, and cross-border FX compliance failure. Recommended policy: reserve EMIs for marketplace receivables only; route all capital injections, LC-backed disbursements, and clearing through Tier-1 German banks or BaFin-authorized EMIs with a German IBAN.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital must be wired via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) to the GmbH's dedicated Tier-1 German IBAN. The wire reference must state the Bundesbank payment-purpose code: 'Y' (Direktinvestitionen) for share capital subscriptions and direct-investment loans, or 'U' (finanzielle Kredite) for non-direct-investment shareholder credit; goods pre-financing should use code 'A'. The credit institution must include the GmbH's full legal name, registered seat, and BIC/IBAN, and the originator's documentation must contain the notarized capital-increase resolution, the share-subscription form, the bank's capital-payment confirmation (Kapitalaufbringungsanzeige), and the Bundesbank electronic report filed by the 7th calendar day of the following month. The routed funds must be booked to a distinct treasury ledger and reconciled monthly to avoid recharacterisation as disguised equity or a back-to-back loan.Outbound procurement of SSDs and server CPUs should clear exclusively through bank-grade rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 T/T for routine spot purchases to pre-KYC-screened suppliers; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit under UCP600 issued via MT700 by the German advising bank on an at-sight basis, incorporating the escrow-inspection model so that funds are released only against presentation of compliant documents (commercial invoice, packing list, third-party inspection certificate, and negotiable bill of lading or airway bill); and (iii) EUR-denominated settlements may clear via TARGET2/SEPA instantly where the beneficiary holds a SEPA IBAN, whereas USD/HKD/SGD/CNY flows clear through the correspondent Nostro chain (Fedwire/CHIPS and CIPS respectively) with mirrored purpose codes to prevent false-positive AML blocks. All disbursements must be recorded in the transaction log, screened against German foreign-trade and EU sanctions lists, and reconciled with the underlying customs and logistics documentation.FX Forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) - deliverable, standard maturities up to 12 months, covering documented EUR/USD and EUR/CNY trade receivables and payablesFX Swaps (Swap-Geschäfte) - simultaneous spot/forward combination managing intra-month liquidity mismatchesCross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) - hedging long-dated USD or CNY-denominated shareholder loans and capex obligationsCurrency Options (FXO) - purchased long options with capped premium, collateralized under German bank ISDA/CSA terms, avoiding naked speculative positionsFX Collar and Zero-Cost Structures - permissible on a non-leveraged basis with embedded option premium, subject to EMIR trade-reporting obligations when the GmbH acts as a Non-Financial Counterparty (NFC)Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) - permissible only for restricted Asian currencies (CNY CNH, INR, IDR) as a non-deliverable hedge of receivables from capital-controlled jurisdictions, strictly backed by documented underlying trade exposure and bank pre-approval; NDFs against EUR are generally unavailable because the euro is fully convertible

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for EEA-authorized and BaFin-registered electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating with a German IBAN under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and PSD2 passporting; LOW for offshore non-EEA digital EMIs not holding a German or EU payment licence. Legal constraint: any non-EEA EMI servicing a German GmbH without a ZAG licence or BaFin passport engages in unlicensed payment services, rendering transactions potentially void as a matter of private law (unwirksam) and exposing the GmbH to frozen-corridor, lost-wire, and cross-border FX compliance failure. Recommended policy: reserve EMIs for marketplace receivables only; route all capital injections, LC-backed disbursements, and clearing through Tier-1 German banks or BaFin-authorized EMIs with a German IBAN.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: annual related-party goods transactions exceeding EUR 5 million or non-good services exceeding EUR 500,000; Master File: required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million (the CbCR threshold); also mandatory documentation must be available within 60 days of an audit demand.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) of the Gulf type; however, § 42 AO (general anti-abuse) and the full tax treaty/GLoBE concepts require a GmbH to have real economic substance—demonstrable local management, staff, office, inventory, and decision-making for transactional pricing. A pure letterbox GmbH claiming tax treaty benefits risks a denial of benefits and retroactive residence-shifting under German controlled-foreign-company rules (§§ 7-13 AStG) and trade tax add-backs.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine under § 370 AO; aggravated large-scale evasion by a company director attracts 6 months to 10 years' imprisonment; evasion tax plus accrued interest and late-payment surcharges are immediately due.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers are personally prosecuted as principals if they order or knowingly tolerate the evasion; company can be fined up to EUR 1 million under § 30 OWiG; civil liability to the tax office under § 69 AO reaches the director's personal assets; pre-trial travel bans, passport withdrawal and asset freezes under §§ 111-112 StPO are standard.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Breach

Statutory sanction: Automatic TP adjustment plus a 6% documentation surcharge on every euro of adjustment (minimum EUR 5,000) under § 162(4) AO; if no usable documents exist, the tax office may estimate profit on a broader basis; additional late interest at 0.5% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the return or impose pricing policies can be held personally liable for the corporate tax deficiency if the underdeclaration resulted from grave negligence (§ 69 AO); criminal negligence penalties under § 378 AO reach EUR 50,000; adverse finding also triggers section 8 of the Corporate Health and Economic Crime Ordinance limiting public procurement and licence eligibility.
Abusive Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty withholding relief; recharacterisation of transactions under § 42 AO; imposition of a permanent establishment in Germany; retroactive dividend WHT of 26.375%; penalties under the Transparency Register (up to EUR 1 million or 10% of turnover) for false beneficial-owner declarations.

Director personal liability: Company directors face administrative fines under §§ 9, 30 OWiG; they are exposed to civil restitution claims from the tax office and shareholders for functional misallocation; in egregious cases personal liability for all withheld taxes and penalties, plus entry bans/departure restrictions under INTAC Rule 27 analogies for frustrated serious tax fraud.
AML / Cross-Border Trade Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines under §§ 56-57 GwG up to EUR 1 million or 10% of group turnover; trade restrictions under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung) may result in seizure of the goods, export bans and anonymous-suspicion screening; criminal money-laundering penalties under § 261 StGB give up to 5 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors as 'officers of the enterprise' are liable for due-organisation failures; personal fines up to EUR 1 million; in money-laundering cases directors can be sentenced to imprisonment; travel bans (the German model of 'Gewinnabschöpfungsgefahr') and international extradition requests for dual-use exports to embargoed destinations are documented, with personal bankruptcy-like foreclosure for restitution orders.

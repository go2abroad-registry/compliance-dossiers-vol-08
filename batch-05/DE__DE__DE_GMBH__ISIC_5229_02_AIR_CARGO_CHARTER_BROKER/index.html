Default tax classification

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general national VAT registration threshold for businesses with place of business or fixed establishment in Germany; micro-business exemption (Kleinunternehmer) applies up to EUR 22,000 annual turnover (voluntary); for intra-EU distance sales of goods, the EU-wide OSS threshold is EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of goods delivered from German territory to destinations outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT when the supplier proves the physical export; for international air cargo charter brokerage services, zero-rating applies if the service is directly connected to transportation of exported goods or performed for an overseas recipient, provided the supplier maintains satisfactory evidence of cross-border movement and commercial documentation; a valid USt-IdNr. or customs declaration is required for intra-EU exempt supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhr- und Verbringungsnachweis - exit summary declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing departure from Germany
  • Commercial Invoice and Payment Certificate; Inward Remittance Certificate (Zahlungseingangsbestätigung) confirming export consideration received

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing in Germany introduced progressively from 1 January 2025; domestic taxable residents must receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x) and by 2028 issuance becomes fully mandatory; no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous digital stamping, or government API transmission for domestic VAT; VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically via ELSTER.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility in both the capital and current accounts: Germany applies the EU Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) Article 63 free movement of capital, and the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) imposes no prior authorization, licensing, or rationing for EUR/USD conversion, inbound equity injection, trade payments, or repatriations. The only cross-border constraints are: (i) EU/OECD/UN/OFAC-linked sanctions screening (especially Russia/Belarus and dual-use goods in air cargo) enforced through AWG embargo sections; (ii) regulatory reporting thresholds for external payments and external receivables/payables; and (iii) investment-screening rules under AWV Sections 55-60 where a non-EU investor would acquire a German entity operating critical infrastructure – pure charter-brokerage intermediation is normally outside the screening scope, but the GmbH should apply for a legally binding clearance certificate (Unbedenklichkeitsbescheinigung) at BMWK if any transport-infrastructure-linked assets are involved. Profit remittance: trade-based payments (charter hire, brokerage commissions, aircraft operating costs) are freely remittable with zero withholding because they are not German-source royalties/interest/dividends. Dividend distributions to a non-resident shareholder incur statutory 26.375% withholding tax (25% WHT plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (minimum 10% shareholding and 12-month holding period) or to treaty rates under an applicable double-tax treaty (e.g., 5% under the US-Germany treaty with 10%+ holding; 0-15% under other treaties); treaty relief requires a bona fide beneficial-ownership declaration per Section 50d EStG. No exchange-control permits, stamp taxes, or state authorization are needed for intra-group loans, equity increases, or the servicing of intercompany accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation ratio applies in Germany; the GmbH may freely retain, convert, and deploy USD export proceeds. The sole statutory obligation is statistical: cross-border payments of more than EUR 50,000 (threshold raised from EUR 12,500 effective 1 July 2024 under amended AWV) must be reported monthly to the Deutsche Bundesbank through the servicing bank (AMKA/external payments statistics platform), using the correct transaction code and purpose text; airborne freight service fees are classified under transport services/freight (Frachten – Luftfracht) in the balance-of-payments statistics. Additionally, the GmbH must report external balances (claims/liabilities against non-residents) exceeding EUR 5,000,000 in total on a monthly basis, and any single external receivable/payable above EUR 50,000 relevant to the statistical aggregate. Non-compliance with AWV reporting is an administrative offense punishable by fines up to EUR 30,000; the bank is the legal reporting agent for payments, while the enterprise remains liable for the completeness of its own external accounts data.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main), acting in conjunction with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK); Bundesbank is the competent authority for external-sector statistics, cross-border payment reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV), and the national clearing/FX payment infrastructure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as the primary treasury rail; Medium strictly as a supplementary payout channel. BaFin-licensed EMIs under PSD2 (e.g., AirPlus, PPRO Financial, and other e-money institutions) legally operate in the Germany/EU market, but they cannot provide the full trade-finance envelope required for an ISIC 5229 air-cargo charter broker: no commercial/standby letters of credit, no IATA CASS clearing linkage, no USD Fedwire settlement on the same-day correspondent network, and no confirmation of export-credits. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are prohibited from granting credit except for narrowly permitted credit lines capped at 30% of own funds; (ii) client-fund safeguarding under the ZAG (Payment Services Supervision Act) requires full segregation or insured guarantee, which is operationally burdensome when the broker collects charter hire on behalf of airlines; (iii) the ZAG commercial-agent exemption (Section 2(3) ZAG) may apply only if the GmbH acts strictly as an intermediary for the principal and not as an independent payment collector – the bank will scrutinize the contractual mandate; (iv) AEAI and sanctions-screening infrastructure at FinTech EMIs is typically weaker for the airfreight vertical, worsening blocking risk in a Medium_Risk_Enhanced category. Recommendation: open primary DDA, credit line, FX line, and LC line at Deutsche Bank and Commerzbank; add a BaFin-licensed EMI only for payments to small EU subcontractors where CASS and SWIFT do not economically reach.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocols: (i) Shareholder equity injection via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder to the GmbH's EUR account at Commerzbank/Deutsche Bank; beneficiary line MUST read the full legal name 'XYZ GmbH' plus IBAN; payment reference (Zahlungszweck) must state 'Gesellschaftereinlage / Kapitalerhöhung' (capital increase) or 'Verdeckte Einlage / Darlehen' for shareholder loan; the purpose code is mandatory for the bank to classify the flow in the Bundesbank external payments statistics; the bank is obliged to report the transaction to the Bundesbank when the amount exceeds EUR 50,000 equivalent, and the GmbH must notify the bank of the classification in writing. Verification packet: notarized and registered share-capital increase resolution (Kapitalerhöhungsbeschluss) filed with the Commercial Register for equity; written loan agreement and interest-rate setting (transfer-pricing compliant, arm's length) for shareholder debt; the shareholder's own bank must state the outbound reason 'direct investment'/'FDI' to avoid misclassification. USD inbound leg: for Fedwire-sourced USD equity, route USD via the German bank's New York correspondent (Deutsche Bank Trust Company Americas or JPMorgan) to the USD account, then execute an internal EUR/USD spot conversion; careful purpose text 'inbound capital contribution – no goods/services billing' prevents the US bank from blocking under OFAC screening; the USD remitting bank must include the German IBAN and full BIC SWIFT details of the final beneficiary. All German banks will condition account activation on a satisfactory Transparenzregister check and, where non-EU investment is present, the bank will document whether an AWV investment-screening application or clearance certificate is needed.Primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers for airline charter payments, fuel purchases, landing/parking charges, crew per diems, and brokerage remittances to non-resident counterparties; (ii) SWIFT MT202 COV for bank-to-bank cover when the counterparty is a clearing institution; (iii) Fedwire for same-day USD disbursements via the US correspondent chain (the GmbH's EUR account is debited and the German bank instructs its NY branch; funding cut-off applies); (iv) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EU-resident vendors, fast SEPA (SCT Inst) for domestic operational needs; (v) IATA CASS clearing for airline settlements: the GmbH files the monthly CASS statement (in EUR, USD, GBP per airline area) and the settlement is processed as a net MT103/MT102 through CASS-approved banks; (vi) Commercial letters of credit (UCP600) and standby L/Cs (ISP98) for aircraft charter security deposits and performance guarantees, usually issued by Deutsche Bank/Commerzbank against a collateralized line. Every outgoing payment must carry the correct AWV purpose text in the SWIFT field 70, e.g., 'Luftfracht-Transportleistungen / Charter Hire – Air Cargo' for freight, 'Provisionen – Frachtvermittlung' for commissions, 'Treibstoff' for fuel, so the bank can assign the proper balance-of-payments code and perform sanctions screening; supporting evidence (AWB, invoice, charter contract) must be retained at least 5 years per GoBD/GwG requirements and presented immediately upon KYC re-review.EUR/USD OTC outright forwards (Devisentermingeschäft) – most common and fully permitted for hedging USD-denominated charter receivables and payables without a banking license on the corporate sideEUR/USD FX swaps (Devisenswap) – used to bridge transient mismatches between USD CASS receivables and EUR operating costsCross-currency swaps (Zinswährungsswap) – permissible for hedging the combined FX and interest-rate exposure on USD-denominated aircraft finance or long-term lease obligationsPlain-vanilla purchased EUR/USD options (Knock-in/out or outright calls/puts) – permitted on a price-risk hedging basis; premiums are tax-deductible operating expenses when the hedging relationship is documentedNDFs (non-deliverable forwards) – technically available from German banks but structurally unnecessary for a fully convertible EUR/USD corridor; German banks quote deliverable forwards only; NDFs would be restricted to exotic non-convertible force majeure corridors (e.g., RUB, CNY off-shore where delivery is impossible) and require an additional ISDA/CSA threshold with credit support

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low as the primary treasury rail; Medium strictly as a supplementary payout channel. BaFin-licensed EMIs under PSD2 (e.g., AirPlus, PPRO Financial, and other e-money institutions) legally operate in the Germany/EU market, but they cannot provide the full trade-finance envelope required for an ISIC 5229 air-cargo charter broker: no commercial/standby letters of credit, no IATA CASS clearing linkage, no USD Fedwire settlement on the same-day correspondent network, and no confirmation of export-credits. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are prohibited from granting credit except for narrowly permitted credit lines capped at 30% of own funds; (ii) client-fund safeguarding under the ZAG (Payment Services Supervision Act) requires full segregation or insured guarantee, which is operationally burdensome when the broker collects charter hire on behalf of airlines; (iii) the ZAG commercial-agent exemption (Section 2(3) ZAG) may apply only if the GmbH acts strictly as an intermediary for the principal and not as an independent payment collector – the bank will scrutinize the contractual mandate; (iv) AEAI and sanctions-screening infrastructure at FinTech EMIs is typically weaker for the airfreight vertical, worsening blocking risk in a Medium_Risk_Enhanced category. Recommendation: open primary DDA, credit line, FX line, and LC line at Deutsche Bank and Commerzbank; add a BaFin-licensed EMI only for payments to small EU subcontractors where CASS and SWIFT do not economically reach.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer (CIT) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT amount plus municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at rates between 7% and 17% depending on the local Hebesatz; combined effective statutory income tax burden normally between 27% and 33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% on gross dividends (25% Abgeltungsteuer-like Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the 25% tax). This applies to dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders unless a DTA or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive reduces or eliminates the rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR. Contemporaneous Local File documentation required where cross-border controlled transactions with related parties exceed materiality thresholds: EUR 5,000,000 annual threshold for goods transactions and EUR 500,000 for services transactions under the German administrative TP principles. Master File documentation is required where the multinational group exceeds EUR 100,000,000 consolidated turnover, in line with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines as applied through §1 AStG and §90 AO.

Economic substance scope: Germany is an EU high-tax jurisdiction and does not apply Caribbean-style ESR (Economic Substance Regulations). Economic substance is nonetheless directly enforceable through domestic anti-abuse rules: §42 AO prevents abuse of legal forms; §10 AO requires the effective management of the GmbH to be located in Germany, and §11 AO fixes the registered seat. Letter-box GmbHs without operational substance, no bank board presence, no employees, and with board decisions taken exclusively offshore will be disregarded as treaty residents and may be deemed to have their place of management abroad; this destroys German tax residence and creates substantial CFC and permanent establishment risk under German-Austrian DTA / ATAD rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: §370 AO: imprisonment for up to 10 years in serious cases, fine up to the financial advantage obtained, and compulsory confiscation of the tax saved. Additionally, interest of 6% per annum accrues on the under-assessed tax; the commercial register may strike off the GmbH and its audit qualifies for tax-office estimation.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for unpaid corporate tax and withholding tax under §§34, 69 AO and §43 GmbHG. Directors face civil restitution for the full tax loss, travel bans and passport revocation, and imprisonment for up to 10 years; the corporate veil is pierced where the GmbH was used as a mere sham or where the director withheld taxes despite knowledge.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under §42 AO the tax authorities disregard the GmbH for tax purposes; under §18 UmwStG and ATAD rules the foreign shareholder is treated as having a German permanent establishment; additionally, German money laundering provisions under §261 StGB apply where charter payments are received through suspected offshore shell structures, with confiscation of assets and fines proportionate to the international wire transfers.

Director personal liability: Director is exposed to imprisonment under §15a InsO for delayed insolvency filing and under §266 StGB for breach of trust; civil liability under §93 AktG analog / §43 GmbHG against the company and creditors; travel bans under §132 StPO may be imposed because of flight risk, including global mailbox-bank transactions.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: German tax audit may increase taxable income by estimation under §162 AO; inadequate or incomplete documentation triggers a surcharge of 5-10% of the upward adjustment, with a minimum penalty of EUR 1,000 and a maximum surcharge limited by German law to EUR 20,000 for late or materially defective records. The income adjustment is also subject to 6% delinquent interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and CFO signatories are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax and penalty; refusal to produce documents is an obstruction-of-justice flag under §332 StGB if corruption implicated; in extreme cases, directors may be disqualified from holding office for up to 5 years.

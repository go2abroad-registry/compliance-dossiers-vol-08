Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

German Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT ID registration and export-related verification; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT return administration; ELSTER online filing portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned GmbH or non-resident e-commerce operator must submit the German tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent local Finanzamt and simultaneously apply for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) with the BZSt. The registration forms are processed via ELSTER or paper-based certified submission; a fiscal representative may be required if the GmbH has no domestic establishment. The local Steuernummer is assigned by the Finanzamt, while the VAT ID is assigned by the BZSt upon verification of economic activity and place of supply.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A German GmbH is a corporate entity subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and solidarity surcharge, without pass-through treatment. Territorial scope: resident GmbH is taxable on worldwide income; no tax-exempt zone status applies. — A foreign-owned GmbH or non-resident e-commerce operator must submit the German tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent local Finanzamt and simultaneously apply for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) with the BZSt. The registration forms are processed via ELSTER or paper-based certified submission; a fiscal representative may be required if the GmbH has no domestic establishment. The local Steuernummer is assigned by the Finanzamt, while the VAT ID is assigned by the BZSt upon verification of economic activity and place of supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A German GmbH is a corporate entity subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and solidarity surcharge, without pass-through treatment. Territorial scope: resident GmbH is taxable on worldwide income; no tax-exempt zone status applies.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Available structural elections include VAT Organschaft (fiscal unity) under Section 2(2)(No.2) UStG and corporate tax Organschaft under Sections 14-19 KStG subject to a profit-and-loss transfer agreement. Hybrid mismatch treatment is governed by Section 4k EStG (ATAD implementation), with no elective pass-through classification for a GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For resident small businesses, the Section 19 UStG threshold is €22,000 prior-year turnover and €50,000 expected current-year domestic supply; foreign entrepreneurs with taxable supplies in Germany may have a registration obligation without a monetary threshold once the place of supply is in Germany; intra-Community distance sales by EU sellers are subject to the €10,000 OSS threshold; import VAT is due regardless of value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies to third-country destinations qualify for VAT zero-rating under Sections 4(No.1) and 6 UStG if the seller proves that the goods physically exited the EU customs territory; intra-Community supplies qualify under Section 6a UStG when the customer communicates a valid EU VAT ID and the goods are transported into another Member State. Offshore services may qualify under Section 7 UStG when the recipient has no German establishment. Every zero-rating claim requires contemporaneous commercial evidence and reliable bookkeeping.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung/ATLAS) and export accompanying document
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, airway bill, or postal receipt evidencing physical removal from EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or payment settlement evidence demonstrating receipt of export proceeds

Germany does not currently mandate real-time tax-authority clearance, API stamping, or fiscal electronic invoicing in the same manner as Italy. However, the E-Rechnung mandate is phased in: from 2025, domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive structured e-invoices in XRechnung or ZUGFeRD format, with phased full issuance obligations for B2B transactions through 2028. B2G e-invoicing is already mandatory. Exports and cross-border transactions remain subject to documentary record-keeping and standard EU e-invoice validation requirements, but not pre-clearance by the tax authority.

If the exporter fails to produce timely and consistent proof of export, the zero rating is retrospectively disallowed and output VAT is assessed at 19% plus statutory default interest. Knowingly or recklessly false export claims constitute tax evasion under Section 370 AO, exposing the GmbH and responsible officers to fines, imprisonment, and asset seizure; Section 26a UStG may additionally deny exemption in abuse and VAT-fraud chain circumstances. Cash-on-delivery e-commerce exporters must retain the complete export, logistics, and foreign-currency repatriation audit trail to withstand heightened and cross-border tax audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account under EU Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Articles 63–65 and the German AWV. No exchange surrender, forced conversion, or central bank pre-licensing of profit remittances. Dividends, royalties, intercompany repayments, and cash-on-delivery sales proceeds may be freely credited externally, subject to: EU and UN sanctions screening; German withholding tax on dividends at 25% plus solidarity surcharge unless a tax treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive reduces the rate; AWV statistical reporting for cross-border payments above €12,500 equivalent (monthly by the bank or the resident enterprise); and KYC documentation establishing beneficial ownership and commercial substance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory repatriation timeline applies in Germany. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in EUR or in any foreign currency. AWV Sections 66–69 impose only statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank on cross-border receivables, payments, and external positions above prescribed thresholds; there are no conversion ratio or surrender percentage requirements.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank; residual administrative enforcement under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) and foreign-trade statistics regime; BaFin supervises AML/KYC for credit institutions and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility as an ancillary clearing layer using a BaFin-licensed or EU-authorized Electronic Money Institution (Airwallex, Penta, or comparable EMI). The legal constraint is that EMIs cannot take deposits, pay interest, or grant credit; client funds are safeguarded under PSD2 but are not covered by German statutory deposit insurance. Consequently, the EMI is suitable for multi-currency payouts, FX conversion, and local COD logistics settlement, but the primary German domiciled treasury and capital escrow account must remain with a full-license credit institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge) on the assessed CIT, yielding a combined federal rate of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 3.5% of taxable profit multiplied by the local Hebesteuer (assessment rate generally 200%-490%) results in a typical combined CIT+trade tax burden of 29.8%-33.0%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory rate: 25% German capital-yield tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, producing a combined 26.375% withholding on qualifying GmbH profit distributions; non-resident shareholders are normally exempt from invoice VAT, but the WHT is separately borne. No additional trade tax is levied on distributions; the 15.825% effective CIT is final for the corporation, and distributions are taxed only at the shareholder level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return must be submitted electronically no later than 31 July of the year following the assessment period; if prepared and transmitted by a licensed Steuerberater / tax advisor under § 149(3) AO, the deadline lapses automatically to 28/29 February of the second following year (e.g., FY 2024: 31.07.2025 normally; 28.02.2026 with advisor).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the German Gewinnabgrenzungsaufzeichnungs-Verordnung (GAufzV) and Administrative Principles 2020: Local File (Geschäftsvorfalldokumentation) must be prepared where related-party goods deliveries exceed EUR 5,000,000 per fiscal year or related-party services exceed EUR 500,000; Master File is required when the group consolidated annual revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000; Country-by-Country report is owed only for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000. The German tax office may demand lower thresholds in individual audit cases under § 90(3) AO.

Economic substance scope: Germany operates no offshore-style Economic Substances Regulations (ESR); however, effective substance requirements are enforced through: (1) unlimited tax residence of the GmbH based on both statutory seat (Sitz) and actual place of management (Ort der Geschäftsleitung) under § 11 AO – a letterbox management seat abroad triggers re-characterization and unlimited tax liability in both states; (2) the § 50d(3) EStG beneficial-ownership clause requiring the repatriating shareholder to show substance to obtain treaty relief; (3) anti-abuse doctrine § 42 AO treating artificial arrangements as transparent; (4) the German CFC-rules (§§ 7-13 AStG) taxing passive income of foreign-controlled subsidiaries at the German shareholder level; (5) the recent Global Minimum Tax Act (Mindeststeuergesetz, MinStG) implementing Pillar Two (15% top-up tax) for groups above EUR 750m.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Under-Reporting of COD E-Commerce Sales

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine and/or imprisonment up to 5 years under § 370 AO; aggravated evasion (evaded tax > EUR 50,000) carries imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years under § 370(3) AO and the Judikative has established a presumption of deprivation of liberty; tax authorities may impose further VAT and trade tax surcharges up to 2.5% of the underestimated net turnover.

Director personal liability: Under § 34, § 69 AO and § 43 GmbHG, the managing director (Geschäftsführer) is personally liable for taxes not declared or deliberately not remitted; a D&O insurance does not cover criminal fines; non-resident directors may face an order of personal appearance, a ban on leaving the country under § 6 AO? (travel restrictions possible under criminal procedural law), seizure of personal assets, and entry bans to the EU upon re-application; imprisonment of the officer is ordered for wilful evasion in amounts above EUR 1,000,000.
Unremitted Cash-Duty Withholdings (Wage Tax, Capital Yield Tax, Value Added Tax)

Statutory sanction: Säumniszuschlag of 0.5% per month on arrearage, minimum €25; deliberate withholding and non-transfer of wage tax crosses into criminal liability under § 266a StGB (for embezzlement of wages tax) with imprisonment from 3 months to 5 years; VAT tax evasion under § 370 AO triggered by missing prepayments.

Director personal liability: The managing director receives a Haftungsbescheid (liability order) under § 69 AO for all unremitted taxes, including interest; the liability amount is enforceable against the director's private assets, and a failure to cooperate with the tax office's Zahlungsverpflichtung can trigger a travel ban and civil insolvency petition against the director.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Profit Stripping via Sheltered Debt

Statutory sanction: Under § 162(4) AO, failure to produce documentation triggers a penalty surcharge of 5% of the adjusted taxable income increase (minimum EUR 5,000); if documentation is inadequate but later supplemented, 3% (minimum EUR 1,000); in grave cases, the tax office may lift evidence protection? (Beweislastumkehr), estimate at a higher margin, and charge full interest; additionally, thin capitalisation? the § 4h EStG interest barrier denies deductions on 30% of EBITDA without proof of acceptable bracket; the Foreign Act (Außensteuergesetz) § 1 AStG recharacterises income.

Director personal liability: The director can be held jointly and several by the Haftungsbescheid for the additional assessment plus penalty if the documentation default is attributable to his/her wilful negligence; cross-border enforcement against foreign-resident directors via the EU Enforcement Order Regulation (EC 1215/2012) and, in extreme cases, extradition for aggravated tax fraud.
Shell Company Abuse / Anti-Money-Laundering Circumvention with COD cash

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with the German Money Laundering Act (GwG) obligations (beneficial-ownership register, risk management, cash thresholds above EUR 10,000) is an administrative offence punishable with fines up to EUR 100,000 per violation; manipulation of registration software triggers tax evasion penalties and, under criminal AML law, imprisonment of up to 5 years (StGB § 261 if funds from predicate offences are laundered); the tax office may also deny deductibility of business expenses and impose VAT evasion penalties.

Director personal liability: The director who knowingly forms a letterbox GmbH infringes §§ 823(2) BGB (protective statute) and § 69 AO; courts will pierce the corporate veil (Durchgriffshaftung) against the foreign ultimate owner, subjecting him/her personally to criminal fines, confiscation of assets, a prohibition on accession to further GmbH board offices, and potential detention in Germany upon entry pending criminal trial.

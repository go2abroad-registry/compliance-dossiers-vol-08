Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Statutory minimum share capital is EUR 25,000 (§ 5 GmbHG). At least EUR 12,500 (50% of minimum capital) must be paid up in cash before registration, with the funds irrevocably at the free disposal of the managing director and evidenced by a bank confirmation; the remaining capital may be paid later. A capital-formation escrow account is not obligatory, but the bank confirmation of payment (Einzahlungsbestätigung) is the decisive proof.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT ID and cross-border tax matters; responsible local Finanzamt for corporate income tax and trade tax assessment; principal e-government portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a German GmbH must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the competent local Finanzamt via ELSTER, obtaining a Steuernummer; for cross-border VAT operations, file a separate application with BZSt (Formular UStVA/USt 1 TG) to receive a German VAT identification number; registration processes are electronic, require certified documents, and apply irrespective of minimum capital payment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a resident GmbH is fully subject to Körperschaftsteuer (15%), Solidaritätszuschlag (5.5% of corporate tax), Gewerbesteuer (municipal trade tax), and Umsatzsteuer (VAT); income is taxed at the corporate entity level without statutory pass-through eligibility.

Available election forms

Organschaft (fiscal unity) election under §§14–19 KStG and §2 UStG for a German parent and 95%-owned German subsidiaries; no check-the-box election exists in Germany; hybrid mismatch restrictions apply under §4k EStG; trade tax grouping can partially accompany the fiscal unity arrangement; thin-capitalization and transfer-pricing rules apply concurrently.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€22,000 prior-year turnover and €50,000 current-year estimated turnover for the small-business exemption (§19 UStG); supplies by foreign entrepreneurs in Germany generally trigger VAT liability without a domestic threshold; intra-Community distance sales threshold of €10,000 per platform across all EU states.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under §6 UStG applies to physical exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union provided the supplier proves the export; for offshore services and intangible services, §7 UStG zero-rating applies if the recipient is an entrepreneur and the place of supply is outside the EU; documentary and reserve requirements must be contemporaneously maintained; containerized bitumen/asphalt wholesale supply qualifies only upon actual dispatch and customs clearance outside the EU customs territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS/Ausfuhrbegleitdokument or electronic export document)
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR/AWB as applicable for multimodal containerized carriage)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the German GmbH account)

Germany introduced a mandatory structured e-invoicing regime for domestic B2B supplies effective 1 January 2025, with phased compliance obligations; XRechnung/ZUGFeRD XML formats must be issued and transmitted through compatible Peppol-network infrastructure; no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mechanism is currently implemented; VAT returns and advance declarations must be transmitted electronically via ELSTER; failure to issue compliant e-invoice in due form is sanctionable.

Export zero-rating claims are scrutinized through cross-border transaction audits and data matching with customs and central bank records; inadequate or inconsistent documentation triggers retrospective VAT assessment, interest on late tax under §233a AO, trade tax clawbacks, and export-specific penalties; fraudulent claims constitute tax evasion under §370 AO punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years and fines; responsible representatives of the GmbH may face personal liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany remains fully integrated into the euro area and applies the EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital. The euro is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions vis-à-vis EU member states and, in principle, vis-à-vis third countries, subject to EU sanctions, embargoes, mandatory licensing, and targeted financial restrictions. There are no exchange controls or quantitative approval requirements for profit remittance in Germany. A German GmbH may distribute dividends abroad after audit confirmation and balance sheet approval; no central bank authorization is required. However, the paying agent must report dividend remittances to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes, and withholding tax at 26.375% including solidarity surcharge will apply unless reduced under an applicable double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. Capital account repatriation of equity or loan principal also faces no convertibility barrier; conversion of retained earnings or reserves into foreign currency is unrestricted. For non-EU/EEA counterparts, the only practical hurdles are sanctions screening, embargo lists, and the Bundesbank's monthly or quarterly external economic reporting obligations under AWV.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements in Germany. Exporters are not obligated to force-convert hard currency receipts into euro, nor are they required to repatriate proceeds within a fixed timeline. The only applicable requirements are statistical declaration under the AWV: payments received from foreign countries exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank on the appropriate external payment report within the statutory period (normally by the 5th working day of the month following receipt). German law likewise does not impose a minimum conversion ratio for export receipts; a corporate treasury is free to hold foreign currency balances in domestic accounts or with offshore banks, subject to general AML/KYC compliance and tax transparency obligations. In practice, EUR-based invoicing is recommended to eliminate transfer pricing and FX remeasurement volatility, but USD-denominated invoicing may be retained where standard in bitumen commodity trade.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), acting in conjunction with the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking conduct matters; the Federal Ministry of Finance and the Bundesbank jointly administer the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV) for cross-border payment reporting and capital transaction monitoring.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) accounts are only conditionally viable in Germany. To hold funds for a German GmbH or open an electronic money account on behalf of a German resident entity, the EMI must hold either an EU/EEA passport under the E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC) or an e-money license from BaFin. Offshore EMIs based outside the EEA cannot legally provide payment services to a German GmbH without a BaFin license, unless operating under an exemption for pure network services or merchant acquisition arrangements. The viability rating for primary treasury operations is Low; for contingency sub-accounts powered by EU-licensed EMIs (e.g., PPRO, Airwallex, MangoPay, or Dover with a German or Luxembourg license), the viability rating is Medium, but only for inbound/outbound fringe payments, not for Capital Escrow or statutory audit trail purposes. Critical legal constraints: (i) a German bank or EMI must perform full KYC/AML due diligence on the GmbH and its beneficial owners; (ii) funds held with EMIs are not protected by the statutory deposit guarantee scheme in the same way as bank deposits; (iii) settlement finality and MT103/LC availability are generally unavailable in EMI rails, making them unsuitable for large bitumen trade settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into a German GmbH typically occur by bank wire transfer in EUR or USD from the parent/shareholder's bank account to the GmbH's operational account or designated capital escrow account. The transfer must be routed using SWIFT MT103 with a clear payment reference encoding the purpose code. For shareholder loan contributions (Darlehen), use the purpose text 'Shareholder loan – no legal conversion obligation', and where applicable report the claim to Deutsche Bundesbank as a cross-border asset/claim under AWV if the lender or beneficial owner is non-resident. For equity contributions, execute a notarized capital contribution resolution in the German commercial register prior to booking; after conversion is not possible without an amendment to the GmbH's stated capital. The proper purpose code in German external payment statistics is 'Zahlung einer Einlage / Kapitalzuführung' or 'Ausgabe von Gesellschaftsanteilen' for equity or 'Gewährung von Darlehen' for loans. For USD-wires that require EUR conversion, the receiving bank should apply a spot transaction using the ECB reference rate or a freely negotiated forward, ensuring no hidden FX margin and correct value date recognition. Funds exceeding the €12,500 threshold must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank as 'Zahlungen aus dem Ausland' in the monthly statistical survey. For third-country inbound investment, any sensitive sector licensing under EU/foreign-direct-investment screening rules must be reviewed; however, bitumen wholesale ISIC 4661 is not a regulated sector under the German FDI screening regime unless the investor is controlled by a non-EU government, which triggers mandatory ex-ante screening for critical infrastructure areas only.Outbound trade disbursements for bitumen imports from refineries and logistics providers are mainly settled by SWIFT MT103 direct wires for smaller payments or medium-value transactions, and by Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) at sight or with deferred payment for elevated transaction values. MT103 wires must include mandatory SWIFT fields: field 50K ordering customer, 59 beneficiary, 70 payment details including the commercial invoice number, INCOTERM (typically CIF Hamburg/Rotterdam or FOB shell port), and underlying trade contract reference. Payment routing may use a correspondent US or UK bank for USD-denominated settlements; to reduce clearing cost and sanctions friction, treasury should mandate EUR settlement with the 'settlement mode code INVOICE' where the supplier invoice quotes in EUR. For LC issuance, a German advising/issuing bank will require a credit line or cash cover; the LC may be an irrevocable documentary credit compliant with ICC UCP600 and eUCP where applicable. The bank receives shipping documents: bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, quality certificate, insurance certificate, and certificate of origin. Liquidity clearing will be done through the German TARGET2 system for euro transfers (domestic and SEPA-instant) and through the CHIPS/Fedwire system for USD-cleared trade accounts.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Amtsgericht – Handelsregisterabteilung (Local Court, Commercial Register Division) of the district of the registered office, operating the electronic Commercial Register (elektronisches Handelsregister, XJustiz) with mandatory notarial submission.

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG) – German Act on Limited Liability Companies; key sections: § 1 (legal independence of a one-person company and purpose), § 3 (contents of Articles of Association), § 5 (minimum share capital EUR 25,000), § 6 (managing directors), § 7 (application for registration), § 8 (declaration on free disposal of capital contribution), § 10 and § 11 (constitutive effect of Commercial Register entry); procedural supplements under § 11 AktG; municipal trade licensing under Gewerbeordnung (GewO) § 14.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized deed of incorporation (Errichtungsurkunde) containing the Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag), either in the statutory model protocol (Musterprotokoll) or as individually drafted articles; formal application form Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister under § 8 GmbHG.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital EUR 25,000 (§ 5(1) GmbHG); at least 50% of the minimum capital, i.e. EUR 12,500, must be paid in cash before filing the registration application (§ 7(2) GmbHG); each shareholder must pay at least 50% of his/her subscribed contribution; cash contributions must be irrevocably at the free disposal of the managing directors in a German bank account blocked until registration (§ 8(2) GmbHG); contributions in kind require a full valuation report and are restricted in re-saleability until registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT ID allocation and the local tax office (Finanzamt) for income/trade tax assessment; electronic portal: ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity may register through the responsible local Finanzamt using a form 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) submitted via ELSTER or in paper. The questionnaire requests corporate documents (articles of association, commercial register extract, certificate of non-resident registration), planned business activities, place of management, and bank details. Upon registration the Finanzamt issues a Steuernummer (tax number). The GmbH also applies for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) separately with the BZSt using the online form for cross-border transactions; the VAT ID is allocated within approximately 10-20 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity may register through the responsible local Finanzamt using a form 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) submitted via ELSTER or in paper. The questionnaire requests corporate documents (articles of association, commercial register extract, certificate of non-resident registration), planned business activities, place of management, and bank details. Upon registration the Finanzamt issues a Steuernummer (tax number). The GmbH also applies for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) separately with the BZSt using the online form for cross-border transactions; the VAT ID is allocated within approximately 10-20 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime. A GmbH is an opaque corporation subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT. Available elections include: forming a VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) for VAT grouping, electing small-business exemption (§19(1) UStG) below the statutory threshold, applying the pro-rata deduction or input VAT allocation methods for mixed use, and opting to tax certain real estate supplies under §9 UStG. For hybrid mismatch purposes, Germany applies ATAD-aligned anti-mismatch rules rather than elective classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no universal registration threshold for all enterprises; resident small businesses are exempt until €22,000 prior-year turnover and not expected to exceed €50,000 in the current calendar year (§19 UStG). Non-resident electronic retailers without a registered establishment have no de minimis VAT registration threshold and must register from the first euro where the supplies are deemed taxable in Germany, unless the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) or reduced VAT registration arrangements cover the transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods where the goods are physically removed from Germany to a third-country (non-EU) territory; the supply is exempt with input VAT deduction ('steuerfrei mit Vorsteuerabzug') under §4 Nr.1, §6 UStG. Intra-EU supplies are exempt if the goods are delivered to another EU member state and the recipient holds a valid VAT ID. Services that are place of supply outside Germany (for example, online digital services to foreign consumers, offshore sale of intellectual property or remote consultancy to non-EU recipients) are outside the German VAT base and thus effectively 0% without VAT invoicing. To claim zero-rating, the exporter must be named as the exporter in the customs declaration and must obtain documentary proof that the goods have left the EU within the statutory period; electronic export records (Zollpapier/Electronic Export Information) are required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung, Electronic Export Information in ATLAS)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR transport document evidencing departure from German customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Verrechnungspreisnachweis or foreign currency credit advice confirming payment receipt from the overseas buyer)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export Confirmation/Statement (Ausfuhrbestätigung) issued by the exporting certificate authority

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime applies. Germany requires mandatory E-Rechnung (structured e-invoicing such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x) for domestic B2B transactions from 1 January 2025, with phased obligations for receipt and transmission; B2G invoices must be submitted through PEPPOL/XRechnung portals. VAT returns are transmitted to the tax authority electronically via ELSTER; the VAT Identification Number must be stated on invoices. There is no mandatory fiscal credential or continuous transaction control in the retail e-commerce sector beyond standard invoice and OSS reporting obligations.

The tax authority may retroactively deny zero-rating and impose German VAT if the exported goods are not timely and satisfactorily proven (evidence must be retained for 10 years). Tax evasion under §370 AO triggers a penalty of up to 25% of the evaded VAT (minimum €250) plus interest (0.5% per month) and imprisonment for deliberate falsification; false export confirmation (Schwarzexport) is an aggravated offence. In refund and audit scenarios, the Finanzamt requires certified English/German translations, verifies the status of the recipient's VAT number, and, in e-commerce cases, may require proof of shipping chain and payment settlement to avoid VAT-clawback under the marketplace's deemed-supplier regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full convertibility of the euro on both current and capital account by virtue of the EU free movement of capital and the absence of domestic exchange-control restrictions. A German GmbH may repatriate dividends, repay shareholder loans, settle trade payables, and remit after-tax profits to non-resident shareholders without obtaining Deutsche Bundesbank approval. Such payments remain subject to ordinary German corporate law restrictions on capital maintenance (e.g. prohibition of repayments of subscribed capital that reduce stated capital below the GmbH statutory minimum), tax withholding rules on dividends (25% withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, and church tax where applicable, unless reduced under an EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, a double-tax treaty, or an exemption certificate), and AWV statistical reporting obligations. Foreign-currency accounts are freely permissible; there is no mandatory conversion of EUR into other currencies and no restriction on the holding of foreign exchange outside Germany.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio for receipts denominated in foreign currency. Export receivables may be retained abroad, converted at commercial banks’ spot rates, or used for legitimate trade settlements. The only binding requirements are statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV Section 67: each cross-border payment made or received by a German resident exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported by the 5th day of the month following the month of the payment. The report is filed by the German resident (or by the bank on behalf of the client in standard commercial banking practice) and must reflect the underlying commercial transaction accurately.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) with supervisory co-authority of the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking, payment services, and AML/CTF matters; cross-border payment reporting is governed by the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

EEA-authorised fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) with a BaFin licence or a valid PSD2 passport into Germany are viable as ancillary payment channels, but they are not a full substitute for a German bank account because they cannot provide trade finance, documentary credits, and comprehensive FX lines. Cleanly regulated EMIs such as BaFin-licensed payment institutions or ZAG-registered agents may process fast-fashion settlements from Shopify Payments and Klarna BNPL, provided the merchant flow isKYC-transparent and the EMI maintains segregated client funds. Offshore digital EMIs not holding a German/EEA payment or e-money licence are Low viability and legally problematic: they cannot lawfully operate in Germany under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and the Banking Act (KWG), and accounts opened with such an entity create substantial source-of-funds and corporate-reputation risk for the GmbH.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (CIT) plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag (0.825% effective) plus Gewerbesteuer (Trade Tax, 14-17% effective depending on municipal Hebesatz), yielding an aggregate effective statutory rate of approximately 30–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German withholding tax plus 5.5% Solidarity Surcharge assessed on the WHT amount (i.e., aggregate statutory rate of 26.375%). The WHT is not credited against the parent's German corporate income tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory deadline is 31 July of the year immediately following the assessment year (e.g., fiscal year 2023 due 31 July 2024). If a qualified German tax advisor prepares the filing, an automatic extension to 31 May of the second subsequent year is granted under § 149(4) AO. Assessed tax amounts are due one month after the assessment notice is issued.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is mandatory when cross-border related-party transactions in goods exceed EUR 6 million per year, or other services exceed EUR 3 million per year, per AStG documentation rules. Master File is required if the wider group achieves consolidated annual turnover exceeding EUR 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone ESR regime, but follows general anti-abuse doctrine (AO § 42) and the German Transfer Pricing Act (AStG § 7-9). A fast-fashion GmbH that maintains no local office, employs no personnel, and has its board decisions taken entirely abroad will be recharacterized as a shell (Mantelgesellschaft). If substance fails, the tax authority can (a) shift the GmbH's seat to the foreign management place under § 11 AO, (b) by-posits profits to the foreign main shareholder, or (c) deny trade tax utilization entirely, treating the German entity as a mere nominee.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 10 years under § 370 AO, an unlimited monetary fine amounting to approximately double the evaded tax, and final confiscation of the profited assets. In addition, interest of 6% p.a. accrues on the assessed unpaid taxes, and the submitted schedule of declarations is fully overturned.

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer (managing director) is jointly and severally liable for any unpaid corporate tax debt under § 34 AO and § 69 AO. Piercing the corporate veil occurs where intent or reckless disregard of tax duties is proven; directors face personal asset seizure, bank account freezing, travel bans, and potential imprisonment despite the GmbH's limited liability.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Missing Documentation

Statutory sanction: German tax authorities can retroactively adjust taxable income upward to the arm's-length level, imposing 6% p.a. penalty interest; incomplete or nonexistent TP documentation triggers a surcharge of EUR 2,500 to EUR 100,000 under § 162 AO; the denial of the LMA results in a loss carryforward bypass and full recharacterization.

Director personal liability: Directors remain personally accountable for failing to prepare contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation. Under a later GmbH insolvency proceeding, such gross negligence exposes the director to civil restitution claims by creditors, covering the additionally assessed taxes and penalties from the director's private wealth.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Abuse (Strohmann-GmbH)

Statutory sanction: Legal disregard of the GmbH under § 42 AO; the entity's income is declared as direct earnings of the true foreign shareholder; the municipality's Trade Tax registration is annulled; the GmbH is struck from the commercial register for false particulars; and a forced dissolution of the corporate veil occurs.

Director personal liability: The de facto director and all shadow shareholders are personally liable for full civil restitution of unpaid corporate taxes, plus financing penalties. Courts readily issue pre-trial detention and travel bans where the owner has structured shell operations to evade German employment and tax law; German insolvency authorities can also attach the individual's foreign assets through cooperation agreements.
Anti-Money Laundering Violations (GwG) and Payment Processor Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines under § 43 GwG up to EUR 1,000,000 for the GmbH and up to EUR 100,000 per director; repeated violations trigger exclusion from handling electronic payment rails and loss of payment institutional licenses; criminal prosecution for laundering the proceeds of underlying fraud arising through chargebacks.

Director personal liability: Directors who ignore the mandatory GwG compliance programme bear personal liability for ensuing fines and restitution claims. In cases involving the financing of counterfeit fast-fashion goods or narcotics proceeds routed via the BNPL accounts, directors are treated as money-laundering principals, leading to direct imprisonment beyond any corporate umbrella protection.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) of the Local Court (Amtsgericht) in whose district the registered office is located, acting through a licensed German notary for electronic submission.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Company Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbHG), §§ 1 (formation), 2 (articles of association), 4a (registered office), 5 (minimum share capital), 7 (application for registration), and 10 (contents of commercial register entry).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag / Satzung) together with the application for registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) submitted electronically by the notary to the Commercial Register.

Minimum nominal share capital is EUR 25,000 (approximately USD 27,000). At least EUR 12,500 must be paid up in cash before registration into a blocked bank account in the company’s provisional name; for a single-shareholder GmbH, security is required for the unpaid portion.

Funding & unlock path: No fire safety certification or structural inspection is required for non-residential office operations. The registered office must comply with the German Occupational Safety and Health Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz) and provide standard workplace safety orientation to employees.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office, in coordination with the responsible local Finanzamt (e.g., Finanzamt München), via the ELSTER online portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entrepreneurs must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (German tax registration questionnaire; form USt 1 TUJ for out-of-country entrepreneurs) with BZSt and the competent local Finanzamt; the authorities issue the corporate income tax number (Steuernummer) and the EU-wide VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) in writing. Digital/EdTech services to EU consumers additionally require enrolment in the OSS (One-Stop-Shop) scheme via BZSt.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — GmbH is an opaque resident corporation subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax, 15%), Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge, 5.5% of CIT) and Gewerbesteuer (trade tax); worldwide income taxation with foreign tax credit; no pass-through eligibility. — Foreign entrepreneurs must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (German tax registration questionnaire; form USt 1 TUJ for out-of-country entrepreneurs) with BZSt and the competent local Finanzamt; the authorities issue the corporate income tax number (Steuernummer) and the EU-wide VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) in writing. Digital/EdTech services to EU consumers additionally require enrolment in the OSS (One-Stop-Shop) scheme via BZSt.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — GmbH is an opaque resident corporation subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax, 15%), Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge, 5.5% of CIT) and Gewerbesteuer (trade tax); worldwide income taxation with foreign tax credit; no pass-through eligibility.

Available election forms

Organschaft (fiscal unity) election through a domination and profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Beherrschungs- und Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) for corporate income tax and trade tax; election to waive the small-enterprise VAT exemption under Sec. 19(2) UStG; ATAD hybrid-mismatch elections implemented under German law; OSS non-union scheme election for EU distance sales of digital services; no US-style check-the-box election exists in German domestic tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Foreign/out-of-country suppliers: no threshold — EUR 0; VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply or economic activity in Germany. Domestic small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung): EUR 25,000 annual turnover from 1 January 2025, rising to EUR 27,000 from 1 January 2026.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Third-country exports of goods are zero-rated under Sec. 4 No. 1 letter a and Sec. 6 UStG; intra-Community B2B supplies are zero-rated where the customer's valid EU VAT ID is held and EC Sales List/Intrastat obligations are met; cross-border B2B services follow the place-of-supply rules of Sec. 3a UStG with reverse charge shifting German VAT to the non-German recipient; B2C digital language-learning subscriptions supplied to EU consumers are taxed at the destination member state's VAT rate via OSS and are not eligible for German export zero-rating.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with validated Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) per Sec. 8-12 UStDV, including customs-stamped export document or electronic ECS confirmation
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and Incoterms evidence
  • Transport documentation: CMR consignment note, Bill of Lading, or Air Waybill
  • Bank transfer and inward remittance records evidencing receipt of consideration

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime (unlike Italy's SDI); mandatory structured e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) in XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x format for domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2025 (receipt-side obligation), with issuer-side phase-in mandatory from 1 January 2027 for businesses with turnover above EUR 800,000 in the preceding year and from 1 January 2028 for all taxable persons; VAT advance returns and annual returns filed electronically exclusively via ELSTER API; OSS quarterly returns filed via BZSt portal.

Germany does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline for VAT zero-rating; however, tax auditors require conclusive proof of export and payment traceability, and the 180-day benchmark is maintained as a prudential documentary window for EdTech exporters. Unsubstantiated zero-rating is reassessed at 19% VAT with late-payment surcharges (1% per month, capped at 10%) and interest under Sec. 233a AO; intentional or grossly negligent misdeclaration triggers penalty proceedings under Sec. 26a UStG (fines up to EUR 50,000) or tax evasion prosecution under Sec. 370 AO (imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in aggravated cases), with retrospective clawback within the standard 4-year assessment period, extended to 10 years in evasion cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany, as a member of the euro area, applies full liberalisation of both current and capital account payments under EU primary law and the AWV. The euro is fully freely convertible; profit remittance, dividend distribution, repayment of shareholder loans, and subscription fee repatriation do not require Bundesbank or BaFin approval. The practical hurdles are not currency-control based but documentary and tax-based: remittance must be supported by valid legal basis (e.g., notarised shareholders' resolution for dividends, approved annual financial statements), the corporate tax balance, trade tax returns, and, where a non-resident recipient is involved, proof of beneficial ownership and applicable withholding tax or treaty relief. Payments to non-residents above the statutory reporting threshold must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics. No capital controls or minimum stay periods apply to inbound equity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose statutory export proceeds surrender or conversion obligations. There is no mandatory timeline or minimum conversion ratio under the AWV. Export receipts and all payments to and from non-residents of the Federal Republic of Germany must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical purposes if the individual transaction or aggregate balance exceeds €12,500 (or lower thresholds for securities/derivatives). The report is filed via the Bundesbank's electronic reporting system using the prescribed balance-of-payments statistics forms. Receipts may be retained in EUR or foreign currency / converted freely; the choice is commercial, not regulated, subject only to bank account-house agreements and general AML/KYC monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank) in conjunction with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin); exchange control and cross-border statistical reporting are administered by the Bundesbank under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV) and the ECB regulatory framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMI viability is Medium within the EEA and Low for non-EEA offshore digital banks. A licensed EU Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or Payment Institution may provide accounts and payment services in Germany under the EU single passporting regime (PSD2), which is a legally viable alternative for secondary settlement or vendor payouts. However, an offshore digital EMI not licensed in an EEA state cannot lawfully hold deposits or execute third-party payments for a German GmbH without acquiring a German or EU payment-services licence, establishing a branch, or partnering with a BaFin-licensed institution. Core treasury liquidity and statutory capital must be maintained in a BaFin-supervised German bank or a licensed EEA credit institution; reliance on an offshore EMI as the sole treasury rail is legally impractical and operationally unacceptable for audit trail and tax proof.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for a German GmbH must be wired by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer into the corporate account held in the GmbH's name. For shareholder equity below €25,000, the transfer must be designated as the initial share capital (Stammeinlage) with IBAN/BIC and a remittance information field stating the shareholder's name and the purpose 'Stammeinlage / Kaptaleinlage' or 'Capital Contribution'. If the capital contribution exceeds the minimum nominal capital or is arranged as an additional capital reserve (Kapitalrücklage), the remittance instruction must cite the corresponding shareholders' resolution and, if required, the escrow account contract. For non-EU inbound wires, the standard ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPT' (Capital Transfer) should be used, and the wire must contain the full legal entity name of the GmbH, the bank IBAN, and the Commercial Register number. For all inbound contributions, the German receiving bank will verify the beneficiary against the Commercial Register excerpt, screen all parties against EU sanctions lists, perform FATF-source-of-funds checks, and may request the notarised capital commitment or contribution resolution before booking the funds to a permanently non-attachable capital reserve. Cross-border treasury concentration beyond €50,000 usually requires a treasury policy and a simple interest-rate or liquidity arrangement with the bank; inbound payments from Apple and Google are treated as operational receipts and should carry the purpose code 'SUBS' or 'GOOD'/'SALE' for digital services, with the platform's settlement report retained as audit evidence.Outbound trade settlements should be routed through SEPA Credit Transfers within the EEA and through SWIFT MT103 messages for non-EEA beneficiaries. For a German EdTech GmbH, typical operational outflows are software development fees, cloud hosting invoices, licensing royalties, affiliate marketing commissions, and distributor settlements. The MT103 must include the beneficiary IBAN and SWIFT BIC, the ISO purpose code applicable to the underlying transaction (e.g., 'LICH' for licence fees, 'ROYC' for royalties, 'SUPP' for supplier payments, 'TRAD' for trade settlement), and field 70 remittance information containing the commercial invoice number, contract reference, and tax invoice date. Supplier payments can be further protected by a documentary Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) based on UCP 600 when the supplier demands a bank undertaking against presentation of transport documents, invoices, certificates of origin, and insurance documents, although this is rare for software subscription businesses. Large disbursements to related parties in low-tax jurisdictions should be supported by a transfer pricing documentation file prepared in accordance with German Administrative Principles and the OECD guidelines to avoid withholding tax adjustments and non-deductibility. The German paying bank will screen sanctions and PEP lists and, where a single disbursement exceeds €12,500 to a non-resident, the payer must file the Bundesbank balance-of-payments report.

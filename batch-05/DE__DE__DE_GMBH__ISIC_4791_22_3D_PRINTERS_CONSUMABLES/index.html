Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT ID, OSS/IOSS registration and EU refunds; competent local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax and standard VAT registration. Electronic filing via ELSTER / Mein ELSTER portal (www.elster.de) and the BZSt online portal (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities with German taxable nexus file the Questionnaire for Tax Registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent Finanzamt (or centrally with the BZSt when no domestic establishment exists), obtaining a Steuernummer (national tax number) and, upon parallel application, a VAT ID (USt-IdNr). Non-established sellers may alternatively report cross-border EU distance sales under the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme and imported consignments up to EUR 150 under IOSS.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a GmbH is a corporation taxed separately from its shareholders: 15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (15.825% combined) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, approximately 7–17% effective), with worldwide residency taxation for domestic companies; no pass-through eligibility.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election; targeted statutory elections include: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity under Sec. 14 KStG and Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG) consolidating VAT, corporate income tax and trade tax; (ii) partnership option for corporate taxation under Sec. 1a KStG; (iii) certain VAT real-estate election under Sec. 9 UStG; (iv) small business exemption under Sec. 19 UStG. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are recharacterized under Sec. 4k EStG and anti-fraud provisions of Sec. 25d UStG to prevent double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover for the Sec. 19 UStG small business exemption (2025 threshold; expected current-year limit EUR 100,000); no threshold exemption for non-established foreign sellers; EUR 10,000 EU-wide intra-Community distance-sales threshold for OSS reporting.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies under Sec. 4 No. 1a let. a and Sec. 6 UStG where 3D printers, resin filaments and consumables are dispatched from Germany to customers in third countries (outside the EU), provided the seller proves the physical removal of goods from the EU customs territory through combined transport evidence (Belegnachweis) and bookkeeping evidence (Buchnachweis) under Secs. 8–13 UStDV. Intra-Community supplies to EU business customers qualify under Sec. 4 No. 1b and Sec. 6a UStG if the customer's valid USt-IdNr and a correct recapitulative statement are on file. Ancillary digital services must be separately analyzed: B2C supplies of electronically supplied services may be taxable at destination instead of zero-rated.

  • Electronic customs export declaration via ATLAS (Ausfuhranmeldung) with Export Accompanying Document (EAD)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR international waybill or air waybill evidencing physical export and final destination
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms, HS codes for 3D printers/consumables (e.g., 8516, 3913, 3215) and German export reference
  • Proof of export (Belegnachweis) comprising customs electronic confirmation, carrier evidence and transport documents (Secs. 8–10 UStDV)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of export consideration from the customer

National E-Rechnung mandate effective 1 January 2025: every German entrepreneur must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD) in domestic B2B transactions. Mandatory issuance is phased: from 1 January 2027 for businesses with turnover above EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all businesses. No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or central interchange (SDI-style) operates yet; real-time B2B clearance is expected only after transposition of the EU ViDA framework (planned from 2030).

If zero-rating is denied at audit, the tax office assesses 19% output VAT on the export invoice retrospectively, plus late-payment interest of 0.15% per month (Sec. 233a AO), late-filing penalties (Sec. 152 AO) and disallowance of input VAT refund claims. Deliberate or grossly negligent misdeclaration constitutes tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, exposing the GmbH and its managing directors to imprisonment, fines and joint and several personal liability under Secs. 25d and 71 AO. Customs authorities may concurrently impose retrospective clawbacks, penalty interest and forfeiture where export evidence is forged or goods are re-imported into the EU.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates full de jure and de facto currency convertibility for the euro on both current and capital accounts, consistent with Article 63 TFEU free movement of capital. There are no exchange-control approvals, no surrender requirements for EUR proceeds on export, and no restrictions on foreign shareholding in a GmbH. Profit remittance is unrestricted as a matter of FX law; the only practical hurdle is the statutory German withholding tax of 25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%) on dividend distributions, which may be reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (10% direct qualifying holding, 12-month holding period) or to a treaty rate under an applicable DTT via the Bundesbank/BZSt Form 4-EG or Form 4-1AbwWST exemption procedure. For the e-commerce margin profile, the standard tax route is annual HGB financial statements, a formal dividend resolution, and fiscal reporting to the Federal Central Tax Office (BZSt); no FX clearance is needed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; exporters are fully free to retain, hedge, or redeploy foreign-currency receipts. The only binding obligation is statistical: under Section 67 AWV, a German resident (including a GmbH) must report to the Deutsche Bundesbank each cross-border receipt or payment whose value exceeds EUR 12,500 (or the foreign-currency equivalent) within five (5) working days of the value date, using the Bundesbank's AWV reporting interface. Intra-EU dispatches are additionally reportable via monthly Intrastat declarations; third-country exports are evidenced through customs declarations in the ATLAS system. These reporting duties run in parallel with VAT obligations and do not constitute exchange controls.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank, Frankfurt am Main) is the competent monetary and balance-of-payments statistics authority; the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) regulates banking and AML under the Banking Act (KWG) and the German Money Laundering Act (GwG); cross-border trade and payments controls are administered under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) or EU-passported EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Platform, Monneo, Payoneer) can provide multi-currency IBANs and cost-efficient supplier disbursements, but they are not credit institutions under the German KWG and therefore cannot offer overdraft lines, interest-bearing current balances, or full treasury FX products. Domestic primary settlement for Shopify Payments and Stripe must land on a German IBAN under an SEPA-compliant PSP agreement. BaFin prudential practice requires that merchant settlement and VAT refund flows run through an EEA-domiciled bank or an EMI operating under a bank partner. EMIs are permissible for (i) secondary multi-currency collection rails, (ii) supplier payouts in non-EUR currencies, and (iii) sub-account liquidity pooling, but should not be the sole operating bank relationship. UK-licensed EMIs that do not hold a German or EU passport post-Brexit are legally restricted and should be avoided.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. German-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) or EU-passported EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Platform, Monneo, Payoneer) can provide multi-currency IBANs and cost-efficient supplier disbursements, but they are not credit institutions under the German KWG and therefore cannot offer overdraft lines, interest-bearing current balances, or full treasury FX products. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on CIT (0.825% effective) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) of approximately 14-17%; combined effective income tax rate typically in the 30-33% range.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital gains tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, resulting in a combined 26.375% withholding rate on dividends distributed by a German GmbH to a non-resident shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A local file is required for related-party cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 1,000,000 per transaction type per year; a master file is required when the group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply the standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime applicable in certain offshore financial centres, but economic substance is fundamental under German anti-abuse law, particularly Section 42 AO (Gestaltungsmissbrauch) and treaty-limitation provisions (e.g., beneficial ownership, BEPS MLI Principal Purpose Test), whereby a GmbH lacking real economic activity, employees, business premises, and local decision-making can be disregarded for tax purposes and denied favourable treaty treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Steuerhinterziehung

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years, and up to 10 years in aggravated cases under Section 370 AO; criminal court fine; recovery of evaded tax with interest; potentially increased penalties and the imposition of a surcharge of up to 50% of the evaded tax in certain aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing officers are personally liable as perpetrators or accomplices; civil liability towards the company under Section 43 GmbHG; orders for restitution, travel bans, and provisional asset freezes can be imposed in criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Abusive Structure

Statutory sanction: Denial of corporate and treaty benefits; recharacterisation under Section 42 AO; reassessment with penal interest; loss denial and disallowance of intercompany deductions; administrative fines; in cross-border cases there may be additional penalties under the German Fiscal Code for failure to disclose tax arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes to the extent that assets were transferred or the GmbH acted as a mere façade; restrictions on leaving Germany, personal burden of proving substance, and possible disqualification from managing corporate entities.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to EUR 100,000 per violation and up to EUR 1 million in aggravated cases against legal persons; money laundering offences under Section 261 StGB are punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years; associated financing and preservation actions can freeze accounts and assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be personally fined or imprisoned for negligent or intentional violation of GwG duties; courts may order prohibition from managing or directing legal entities; the corporate veil does not shield individuals from criminal sanctions.
Withholding Tax Violation / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unpaid withholding tax from the GmbH as withholding agent, plus late-payment interest of 0.5% per month; administrative penalty for late and incorrect withholding; criminal prosecution for attempted tax evasion in intentional cases.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable under Section 44 EStG as statutory withholding agents; the amount of unpaid tax can be collected directly from their private assets; criminal liability extends to the corporate officers who ordered or knowingly permitted the violation.

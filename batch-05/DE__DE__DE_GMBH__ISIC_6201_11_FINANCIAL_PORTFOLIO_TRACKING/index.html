Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Local Court (Amtsgericht), Commercial Register Department (Handelsregisterabteilung), in the district of the GmbH's registered office; electronic filing is performed through the notary using the official electronic judicial mailbox.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbH-Gesetz, GmbHG), §§ 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11; Commercial Code (HGB), §§ 8 ff.; Court and Notary Fees Act (GNotKG), § 79; Trade Regulation Code (GewO), § 14.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized application for registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister), accompanied by the articles of association, list of shareholders, appointment resolutions and specimen signatures.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 35 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Registered share capital must be at least EUR 25,000. At least EUR 12,500 (one-half of the minimum capital) must be paid in cash before registration and must be held in a blocked GmbH escrow/custody account at free disposal of the management only after registration. Contributions in kind must be fully covered and identified in the Articles; a banker's declaration is required to evidence payment.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain fire safety certification for the registered office under the state building regulations and confirm electrical installation compliance. Ensure the landlord provides structural load certification sufficient for ordinary office use. Carry out an occupational health and safety risk assessment and workstation ergonomics check in accordance with the Occupational Health and Safety Act (ArbSchG) and DGUV accident insurance regulations.

Paid-in capital rule: Registered share capital must be at least EUR 25,000. At least EUR 12,500 (one-half of the minimum capital) must be paid in cash before registration and must be held in a blocked GmbH escrow/custody account at free disposal of the management only after registration. Contributions in kind must be fully covered and identified in the Articles; a banker's declaration is required to evidence payment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office — via ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) portal; local Finanzamt responsible for TIN issuance and ongoing administration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned German GmbH must first be notarially incorporated and registered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). Following registration, the managing director must file an application for a tax number (Steuernummer) with the local Finanzamt. VAT registration under §27a UStG is processed by the Federal Central Tax Office to issue the German VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, DE + 9 digits). A qualified electronic signature via ELSTER or a paper form (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) is required; foreign officers may appoint a German tax agent with power of attorney for the filing.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation, fully subject to German Körperschaftsteuer and Gewerbesteuer as a domestic corporation; no check-the-box pass-through option. The GmbH is a tax resident if its place of management and/or statutory seat is in Germany; worldwide income is taxable unless a treaty assigns taxing rights abroad. — A foreign-owned German GmbH must first be notarially incorporated and registered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation, fully subject to German Körperschaftsteuer and Gewerbesteuer as a domestic corporation; no check-the-box pass-through option. The GmbH is a tax resident if its place of management and/or statutory seat is in Germany; worldwide income is taxable unless a treaty assigns taxing rights abroad.

Available election forms

Entire-entity taxation is mandatory for a GmbH under German corporate tax law; no check-the-box or S-corporation-type election exists. Hybrid mismatch elections are governed by §4k EStG and §8b KStG in conformity with ATAD; if the German entity has a hybrid branch structure or hybrid instrument, it must document the election or adjustment, and any municipal tax split determinations attach to Gewerbesteuer hebesätze set by the local municipality.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic economic turnover exemption threshold is EUR 22,000 per calendar year under §19 UStG for small businesses (Kleinunternehmer); however, for the GmbH in financial portfolio tracking the threshold is de minimis and a reverse-charge intra-EU acquisition of digital services triggers statutory VAT registration without exception. Cross-border B2C digital services from a German-established company are always taxable in Germany; no remote seller EC sales distance sales threshold applies to established taxable persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods to third countries is zero-rated under §6 UStG where the exporter is a German taxable person, the goods physically leave the EU, and the recipient is not a taxable person in the EU for those goods. Software-as-a-service and other electronically supplied services exported to a non-EU business customer are zero-rated as B2B offshore services under §3a and §3f UStG; B2C digital services supplied to customers outside the EU may be outside the scope of German VAT. The exporter must prove the transport (gemeinschaftliches Versandverfahren) or customs export and retain documentary evidence of the recipient's business status.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung/ATLAS ID) with customs office of export number
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms and evidence of place of supply
  • Bill of Lading or CMR waybill or air waybill showing transport from EU territory
  • Proof of export (Versandanzeige) issued by the shipper or export declaration summary per §13 UStDV
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or payment records demonstrating the exporter's receipt of consideration from the non-EU customer
  • Service contract or order confirmation evidencing the non-EU recipient's business status for zero-rated offshore services

Germany phased in mandatory B2B e-invoicing from 1 January 2025: domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive structured XRechnung/ZUGFeRD-FACTUR-X invoices; from 1 January 2026 resident taxable persons must issue structured e-invoices for B2B supplies. VAT clearance is via the ELSTER portal (API interface); no transaction-level fiscal pre-clearance or digital stamping is used. Factur-X/EN 16931 format and qualified electronic signature requirements apply for outbound invoices to EU businesses.

German tax authorities require submission of export evidence within the statutory records retention period (10 years). False zero-rating claims attract VAT underassessment, late payment interest of 0.5% per month under §233a AO, late filing penalties up to 10% of the assessed tax under §152 AO, and a fine of up to EUR 500,000 for deliberate evasion under the General Administrative Procedure Act framework; criminal sanctions under §370 AO (tax evasion) include imprisonment of up to five years or fines where the exporter knowingly presents forged transport documentation or fictitious non-EU invoices. The Finanzamt may invoke §163 and §165 AO to reopen assessments and apply retrospective clawback, including denial of the right to claim input VAT deduction until restitution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Germany applies European Union free-movement-of-capital rules, subject to anti-money-laundering, sanctions, tax, financial-services licensing, and foreign-investment control requirements. A German GmbH may generally receive foreign equity, shareholder loans, dividends, service fees, royalties, and trade payments through its German bank account. Profit distributions require distributable profits, proper shareholder approval, compliance with German corporate capital-maintenance rules, and accounting support. Dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are generally subject to German withholding tax of 25 percent plus solidarity surcharge, subject to domestic exemptions, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief, treaty relief, refund procedures, anti-abuse rules, and beneficial-ownership requirements. Principal repayment on properly documented shareholder loans is generally not subject to withholding tax, while interest may be subject to withholding or reporting considerations depending on the recipient, instrument, and applicable treaty. Banks may request source-of-funds, beneficial-owner, tax-residency, transaction-purpose, and underlying-contract evidence before executing cross-border transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a domestic bank, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline for converting export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may ordinarily remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's discretion. German residents must comply with applicable external-sector reporting obligations under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung and Deutsche Bundesbank reporting procedures. Reporting thresholds and exemptions must be checked against the current Bundesbank rules for the relevant transaction type; reporting is separate from any tax, customs, sanctions, or accounting obligation. Export documentation, invoices, shipping or service evidence, bank statements, and reconciliation records should be retained to support the commercial nature and accounting treatment of receipts.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin) supervises regulated banking, payment, e-money, and investment services activities in Germany.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and operational liquidity; low for statutory share-capital funding and principal treasury banking. A German GmbH may use a licensed electronic-money institution or payment institution established in Germany or another European Economic Area state and lawfully passported into Germany. An offshore digital EMI outside the European Economic Area cannot provide regulated payment or e-money services to German customers solely by operating online; it must rely on an appropriately licensed and authorized structure or an EEA-regulated partner. Client-money safeguarding, payment-services perimeter analysis, German and EU AML requirements, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, safeguarding segregation, data protection, and service continuity must be verified. Digital accounts should not be used for the GmbH's statutory capital contribution unless the notary, formation bank, and commercial register process expressly accept the arrangement. Stripe and Apple payment proceeds should be settled to an account held in the GmbH's legal name, with merchant-account ownership, chargeback exposure, reserve terms, and beneficial-owner information aligned across all providers.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Unclear beneficial ownership or nominee, trust, layered, or holding-company structure

Root cause: Unclear beneficial ownership or nominee, trust, layered, or holding-company structure

Defense evidence: Current articles of association; shareholder register; group structure chart; shareholder agreements; ultimate-beneficial-owner declarations; corporate-register extracts for every relevant entity; trust or foundation instruments where applicable; management-control analysis; and a written explanation of ownership, control, and economic purpose.
Fintech or investment-related business description suggesting regulated investment advice, portfolio management, brokerage, payment services, or crypto activity

Root cause: Fintech or investment-related business description suggesting regulated investment advice, portfolio management, brokerage, payment services, or crypto activity

Defense evidence: Detailed product and funds-flow memorandum; statement that the company provides software and analytics only, if factually correct; customer terms; prohibited-activity matrix; licensing analysis; BaFin perimeter assessment where appropriate; data-flow diagram; payment-provider contracts; sample invoices; and confirmation that the company does not hold client money, execute orders, provide individualized investment recommendations, or conduct other regulated services unless authorized.
Expected cross-border receipts from Stripe, Apple, foreign customers, or multiple payment processors

Root cause: Expected cross-border receipts from Stripe, Apple, foreign customers, or multiple payment processors

Defense evidence: Processor agreements; merchant-account ownership evidence; platform payout reports; customer geography; expected monthly volumes; average transaction size; refund and chargeback policy; sample customer invoices; tax-registration evidence; website and application description; reconciliation procedure; and explanation of payment flows and settlement currencies.
Large or irregular inbound shareholder funding, intercompany loans, or capital contributions

Root cause: Large or irregular inbound shareholder funding, intercompany loans, or capital contributions

Defense evidence: Executed capital-increase or shareholder-loan agreement; shareholder resolution; notarial documents where required; proof of originating bank account; lender or shareholder financial statements; source-of-funds evidence; transfer instructions using a clear purpose reference; repayment and interest terms; and accounting entries reconciling the funds to the GmbH.
Mismatch between registered business activity, website, bank application, and transaction profile

Root cause: Mismatch between registered business activity, website, bank application, and transaction profile

Defense evidence: Consistent business-plan summary; commercial-register extract; website and application screenshots; customer and supplier contracts; revenue model; projected cash-flow statement; board or shareholder approval of business activities; and written correction of any outdated or inconsistent onboarding information.
Sanctions, politically exposed person, adverse-media, or high-risk-country alert

Defense evidence: Identity-resolution package; explanation of any name match; current corporate and personal screening results; source-of-funds records; applicable licenses or permits; beneficial-owner confirmation; legal opinion or compliance memorandum where necessary; and documented enhanced-due-diligence response.
Use of personal accounts, third-party settlement accounts, virtual IBANs, or accounts not held in the GmbH's legal name

Root cause: Use of personal accounts, third-party settlement accounts, virtual IBANs, or accounts not held in the GmbH's legal name

Defense evidence: Bank-account ownership certificate; board authorization; written funds-flow policy; third-party payment explanation; processor agreement; reconciliation controls; prohibition of personal-account use; and evidence that all customer receipts and company expenses are booked through accounts controlled by the GmbH.

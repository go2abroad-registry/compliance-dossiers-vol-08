Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) — Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and central withholding administration; local Finanzamt (tax office) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration. Central filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned German GmbH must first complete commercial register registration and trade office (Gewerbeamt) notification. The managing director must submit the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER or to the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (corporate tax number) and administratively forwards data for the BZSt to issue a separate VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) for intra-Community transactions. For customs purposes the company must also obtain an EORI number via the Generalzolldirektion. Non-resident foreign entities with direct VAT-relevant activities may apply for VAT registration directly at BZSt.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned German GmbH must first complete commercial register registration and trade office (Gewerbeamt) notification. The managing director must submit the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER or to the locally competent Finanzamt. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a check-the-box regime; the GmbH is irrevocably treated as a C-corporation-like entity subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag), and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). No pass-through election exists. Where hybrid mismatches are present, Germany mandates strict consequences under § 4k EStG as implemented by ATAD 1 and ATAD 2; these operate automatically as disallowance rules, not as elective regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for established businesses; any entrepreneur must register for VAT. Small-business exemption threshold for VAT pooling/relief is €22,000 total annual turnover (with €50,000 forward-looking cap). For cross-border intra-EU distance sales and digital services, the annual distance-selling/OSS threshold is €10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies to third-country destinations are zero-rated if the seller or buyer (or commissioned carrier) physically exports goods to a non-EU territory, evidenced under § 6 UStG and § 8-13 UStDV. Intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered customers are zero-rated with a valid VAT-ID and article-138-like movement proof. Related freight, handling, and inspection services are zero-rated when directly connected to exported goods. Offshore services to non-EU recipients may be place-of-supply shifted outside Germany for B2B situations, thus not subject to German VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung with MRN) generated via ATLAS/EU Customs
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR (international transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer proof tying consideration to export
  • Intra-Community delivery evidence where applicable (e.g., CMR/order confirmation, transport documentation)

Germany has no real-time clearance/digital stamping mandate. However, B2B e-invoicing is mandatory since 1 January 2025 for domestic supplies: every VAT-registered recipient must accept structured electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x). Deadline for issuing e-invoices: full B2B issuance obligation from 1 January 2028 for taxpayers with prior-year turnover of €800,000 or less; otherwise generally required from 2027. Submission of VAT returns occurs through ELSTER with no government fiscal clearance in real time.

Finanzamt audits demand substantiated documentary chains; missing or contradictory export proofs result in retrospective VAT assessments at the 19% standard rate with interest under § 233a AO (§ 233a-234 AO). Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment up to five years or fines, with 10-year statute of limitations for concealment. Repeated or organized abusive schemes can trigger penalty surcharges and personal liability of managing directors under § 69 AO and § 34 AO, plus sanctions under German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulations (AWV) for late foreign-exchange repatriation, including fines and administrative enforcement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible and freely transferable at market rates. Germany maintains no foreign exchange controls or restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions, consistent with the free movement of capital under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Profit and dividend remittance from a German GmbH is unrestricted from an exchange-control perspective, but is subject to corporate formalities, anti-money laundering compliance, and dividend withholding tax at the statutory rate of 25% (plus solidarity surcharge and church tax where applicable) unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty. The onlymaterial compliance burden is the Austrian-style statutory balance-of-payments reporting obligation for cross-border payments above EUR 12,500, which is an information requirement, not a convertibility or remittance restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists under German law for export proceeds. Exporters may hold and dispose of foreign currency freely within the framework of AWV and applicable tax record-keeping. However, all cross-border payments received in connection with exports of goods or services of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported electronically to the Deutsche Bundesbank using the prescribed payment category code. The report must be filed, as a rule, by the fifth working day of the month following the calendar month in which the payment was received or made.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting within the European System of Central Banks and coordinated with the Federal Ministry of Finance and the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Legally, only EMIs holding a valid BaFin licence or an EEA authorisation passported under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) can lawfully provide payment accounts to a German GmbH. Such EMIs may be used for daily settlement and treasury operations, especially for cross-border EEA payments, provided they offer a German IBAN and segregated safeguarding accounts. However, EMIs are not credit institutions under the German Banking Act (KWG) and cannot issue the statutory confirmation required for a GmbH's share capital contribution under Section 8(1) of the German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG). Offshore EMIs without an EU licence or passport must be avoided entirely, as they are considered unlicensed payment businesses and create dangerous legal and tax exposure for the German entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the German GmbH must be injected by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the foreign parent or investor to a dedicated German bank account opened in the name of the GmbH, explicitly labelled as the capital contribution account. The wire must be denominated in EUR and must include the purpose code for direct investment (Kapitalbindung) as defined by the Bundesbank balance-of-payments statistics; the reference line must contain the GmbH's commercial register number and the capital increase declaration reference. The managing director must ensure the contribution is unconditionally and freely usable, without any reimbursement or set-off arrangement, and the bank must issue a certificate under Section 8(1) of the GmbHG confirming that the amount stands at the free disposal of the directors. The entire process must be documented in the notarised record and commercially registered before approval of the annual financial statements.The GmbH's outbound trade disbursements should use the following clearing rails: for EU suppliers, SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) with the relevant commercial invoice and VAT identification number; for non-EU suppliers and royalty payments, SWIFT MT103 single payment messages or MT202 for direct interbank cover without customer details; for high-value equipment imports, irrevocable confirmed documentary credits issued by the GmbH's bank under UCP 600 rules (SWIFT MT700); for franchise royalties, SWIFT MT103 with the purpose code for 'services' and a copy of the franchise agreement and underlying transaction invoice; for POS revenue, direct clearing of the acquirer's settlement files to the GmbH's operational account, followed by daily internal treasury reconciliation.Vanilla deliverable FX forwards over the counter concluded with a licensed German bank for hedging currency risk on EUR/USD and EUR/CNY purchase or sale flows up to a maximum tenor of 18 months.Cross-currency swaps governing future principal and interest streams arising from cross-border intercompany financing, provided the swap is documented under an ISDA Master Agreement and the non-financial counterparty threshold is scoped under EMIR with the hedge exemption.Non-deliverable forwards on CNY (EUR/CNY NDF) allowed for hedging the Chinese pastry chain's CNY operational expenditure and supply-chain expenses where onshore CNY conversion is not freely settleable; settlement is in EUR and does not violate German AWV rules.Plain vanilla FX options purchased (long options only) for risk protection on material, one-off capital expenditures in USD or CNY, subject to central bank reporting threshold rules and board approval.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A German GmbH is must maintain contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation (Local File/Master File) when cross-border controlled transactions with related parties exceed EUR 5,000,000 in goods transactions or EUR 500,000 in service transactions per fiscal year. Failure to produce documentation triggers a surcharge under § 162(4) AO of 5% of the adjusted income difference, minimum EUR 5,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no classic ESR regime (as in Gulf jurisdictions); however, substance is embedded in German anti-abuse and treaty abuse rules: a GmbH will be fiscally resident where its effective management and substance exists (registered office or place of management); transactions with shell entities can be recharacterized under § 42 AO; permanent establishment profits require actual operational structure. For pastry bakery chains, substance is proven by central kitchen staff, production equipment, kitchen orders, POS records, payroll, and board decisions in Germany.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty under § 370 AO: imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in particularly severe cases; fines (Geldstrafe); assessed tax plus monthly late payment interest of 0.5%; additional surcharges up to 25,000 EUR for late declarations; mandatory addition of penalties in real estate or cash-intensive businesses.

Director personal liability: A managing director or shareholder is directly criminally liable for taxation crimes; civil tax liability can be enforced against personal assets under §§ 71, 73 AO; travel bans, passport confiscation, securities lodgment, and imprisonment may be imposed; directors may be disqualified from commercial management responsibilities under §§ 6, 74 GGmbHG and criminal law.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (Briefkastengesellschaft)

Statutory sanction: Tax recharacterization under § 42 AO, denial of treaty benefits, loss disallowance under § 8c KStG, recalculation of profits with 6% annual interest, potential treaty-shopping penalties and additional VAT or trade tax assessments; criminal risk if it is used to facilitate tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors who knowingly operate a sham GmbH may be jointly and severally liable for all unpaid corporate taxes, interest, and penalties; courts may impose travel bans and restrictions on cross-border movement during criminal investigations; imprisonment up to 5 years for aiding tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Violation

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment by tax authorities, with addition of 5% surcharge on the adjusted amount, minimum EUR 5,000 (under § 162(4) AO); interest 6% per annum on additional tax; if documentation is not supplied within 60 days, the authorities may estimate income; additional penalty for evasion if non-compliance is intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors are responsible for enforcing compliant documentation; if they knowingly omit records or approve abusive pricing, they can be personally fined and criminally prosecuted; civil and criminal consequences including disqualification, travel bans, and imprisonment for aggravated tax evasion.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Payment Compliance Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual global turnover for legal entities under § 56 GwG for serious violations; closure of premises, revocation of operating license; criminal fines and confiscation of illicit proceeds under the German Criminal Code (StGB § 261).

Director personal liability: Directors who neglect AML control duties are personally liable for fines; they can be subject to travel restrictions, passport revocation, and criminal arrest if implicated in money laundering or organized economic crime; direct civil liability for damages caused by the violation.

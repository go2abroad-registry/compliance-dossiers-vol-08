Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt), the Federal Central Tax Office, for EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer), OSS registrations, and cross-border clearance (portal ELSTER: www.elster.de; BZSt: www.bzst.de). The locally competent Finanzamt (district tax office of the GmbH's commercial register seat) is responsible for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), wage tax, and VAT assessment. The Generalzolldirektion (www.zoll.de) is the competent customs authority for EORI registration and export/import declarations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Incorporate the GmbH before a notary and register in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). Step 2 – File the electronic 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the responsible Finanzamt via ELSTER within one month of formation; the Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer (domestic tax number) covering corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT, and sets the advance VAT return cycle. Step 3 – Apply separately to BZSt for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under §27 UStG via ELSTER (form USt 1A); a foreign-resident entity without a German seat or permanent establishment must use the non-resident entrepreneur registration procedure or the OSS scheme under §18e UStG, submitting proof of business activity, constitutional documents, foreign VAT ID, and place-of-supply data. Step 4 – Obtain an EORI number from the Generalzolldirektion prior to any customs procedure. Step 5 – Establish ELSTER-certified digital filing for monthly or quarterly Umsatzsteuervoranmeldungen and annual VAT returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – A German GmbH is a tax-resident body corporate with unlimited corporation tax on worldwide income (§1 Abs.1 KStG), municipal trade tax on business profits (average effective rate approx. 25–30%), and mandatory VAT entrepreneur status (§2 UStG). There is no passthrough, territorial-source carve-out, or tax-exempt zone applicable to a GmbH engaged in global MMORPG publishing. In-app purchase income from Apple and Google payout rails remains fully taxable in Germany through the GmbH's domestic seat/permanent establishment.

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box election for domestic purposes. Available mechanisms: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) for corporate income and trade tax under §14 KStG, and VAT fiscal unity under §2 Abs.2 Nr.2 UStG, requiring control and a profit-and-loss pooling agreement with a German parent; (ii) a US federal check-the-box pass-through election under Treasury Regulation §301.7701-3 may be made by the GmbH solely for US income tax purposes, creating hybrid-entity status in cross-border app-store payment chains; (iii) the OSS (One-Stop-Shop) election under §§18j ff. UStG for intra-EU B2C digital services, allowing German portal reporting at destination member-state VAT rates; (iv) VAT group registration of financially integrated affiliates; (v) the §19 UStG small-business exemption, which is unavailable in practice given the expected global turnover far exceeding the statutory thresholds.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for non-resident suppliers making taxable digital supplies in Germany; a German-established GmbH must register immediately upon incorporation irrespective of turnover. For intra-EU distance sales and cross-border B2C digital services, the EU-wide threshold is EUR 10,000 per calendar year; the worldwide scope of this MMORPG will exceed this threshold in the first month, triggering mandatory OSS registration and destination-country VAT. Domestic small-business exception under §19 UStG (EUR 22,000 prior-year / EUR 50,000 projected current-year turnover during 2020–2024; EUR 25,000 / EUR 100,000 as of 1 January 2025) is not applicable to this global publisher.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

German VAT is effectively 0% on: (i) physical exports to third-country destinations under §§4 Nr.1a, 6 UStG with substantiated proof of export; (ii) intra-Community supplies to VAT-registered EU businesses under §§4 Nr.1b, 6a UStG evidenced by the customer's valid VAT ID and Intrastat consistency; (iii) B2B supplies of MMORPG platform rights where the place of supply is outside Germany under §3a Abs.2 UStG (reverse-charge in customer jurisdiction); and (iv) B2C digital services supplied to consumers resident outside the EU, because §3a Abs.3 Nr.5 UStG and Art. 58 VAT Directive locate the supply at the customer's residence, leaving no German VAT. For intra-EU B2C game downloads, OSS reporting applies at the foreign member-state rate. The GmbH must retain app-store settlement reports, IP geolocation logs, billing addresses, and bank data as contemporaneous evidence of place of supply. Germany does not condition zero-rating on FX remittance within a statutory deadline; the 180-day figure in this dossier is an internal treasury benchmark for app-store payout cycles.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung via ATLAS), EORI number, and export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument) – necessary only where physical carrier media or promotional goods physically leave the EU; digital MMORPG exports are not subject to customs clearance.
  • Commercial invoice together with proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis per §§8–13 UStDV) and, for digital services, a service contract plus two independent non-contradictory pieces of residential/business status evidence (e.g., IP geolocation, billing address, bank account country, SIM card data) as required by Art. 24f Implementing Regulation (EU) No 282/2011.
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note) where goods are exported, or an Apple App Store / Google Play payout statement, settlement report, SWIFT MT103, or bank credit advice serving as the inward-remittance certificate for digital service proceeds.

B2G invoices to German public authorities must be structured E-Invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD) since 27 November 2020. Since 1 January 2025, issuers established in Germany must issue structured EN 16931 e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies and every German business must be able to receive such e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 the obligation extends to issuers with gross turnover above EUR 800,000 in the preceding year, and from 1 January 2028 to all businesses. Germany does not currently operate real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction controls (CTC); invoices are exchanged bilaterally and VAT is declared via ELSTER on a monthly/quarterly basis with post-filing data analysis and official audits (Außenprüfung). EU ViDA (VAT in the Digital Age) will introduce mandatory EU-wide digital reporting and e-invoicing by 2030–2035. For Apple/Google App-Store payout rails, the GmbH must assess the contractual principal/commissionaire structure to determine whether to issue German VAT invoices, reverse-charge invoices to the app-store acquirer, or zero-rated export documentation.

If the export or intra-Community nature of a supply is not substantiated under §§6 / 6a UStG and §§8–13 UStDV, the tax authority retroactively assesses German VAT at 19% on the undeclared consideration plus default interest of 0.5% per month under §233a AO. Intentional fraudulent zero-rating constitutes criminal tax evasion under §370 AO and is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years (up to 10 years in aggravated cases) and fines; negligence (Leichtfertigkeit) is punishable by administrative fines up to EUR 50,000 under §378 AO. Misuse of a VAT ID without proper customer verification triggers joint liability under §26a UStG. E-invoicing non-compliance is subject to regulatory fines up to EUR 5,000 under §26a UStG. The statutory assessment limitation period is 4 years (regular), 5 years (negligent understatement), or 10 years (concealed evasion) under §169 AO, permitting deep retrospective clawback; EU multi-state OSS audits under Regulation (EU) No 904/2010 further expose the GmbH to cross-border information exchange and joint audits.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a fully convertible jurisdiction with no residual exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. As a founding member of the Economic and Monetary Union, Germany applies the EU Treaty freedom of capital movement (Articles 63-66 TFEU) and EU Capital Movements Directive 88/361/EEC. The Euro is freely convertible with unlimited inward and outward transfer of funds, dividends, trade payments, and license fees. Profit remittance by a German GmbH to a non-resident corporate shareholder is generally subject to German trade tax and corporation tax at the entity level, whereas a dividend distribution to an EU/EEA parent qualifies for the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) tax exemption without German withholding; distributions to non-EU shareholders may be subject to 25% + 5.5% solidarity surcharge withholding, reducible under an applicable double tax treaty. No exchange license, prior approval, or central bank validation is required for capital flows; however, cross-border payments with non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 aggregate monthly are subject to Bundesbank external economic statistical reporting (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik / AWV-Meldepflicht) to support balance-of-payments compilation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or designated holding period in Germany. Export receipts, including App Store and Google Play payouts for MMORPG software sales, may be accumulated, freely held in Euro or foreign currency accounts, and converted at any time without declaring the underlying commercial reason. The only applicable duty is the aforementioned statistical reporting obligation to Deutsche Bundesbank for any monthly consolidated cross-border transfer of EUR 12,500 or above, which applies to both inbound and outbound flows and is handled electronically via the annual/monetary external economic reporting system, not as a foreign exchange permission.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Germany's central bank), acting as the competent foreign exchange statistics and external economic surveillance authority under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz - AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV), with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) responsible for prudential banking conduct and Money Laundering Act (GwG) enforcement

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High for regulated EU/EEA Electronic Money Institutions operating in Germany under a full BaFin EMI license or a valid EU passport; Low for offshore non-EEA EMIs. Legal constraints under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and the Fourth EU Anti-Money Laundering Directive require any payment institution serving a German-domiciled GmbH to hold a BaFin license or be passported from another EEA state. Offshore digital EMIs without any physical branch or licensed agent in Germany may not lawfully provide payment accounts, currency exchange, or payout aggregation for a German legal entity. For an MMORPG global business collecting daily in-app revenue from Apple and Google, a hybrid structure is common: a commercial money account at a Tier-1 German bank for statutory liquidity, plus an EU-licensed EMI (e.g., Airwallex, MangoPay, Payoneer, or a Solaris-partnered EMI) for cross-border expense routing and FX conversion. Such EMIs must nevertheless maintain correspondent settlement via a German or Luxembourg partner bank, and any aggregated payout channel must be disclosed to the GmbH's principal banking relationship to avoid concealed payment-file fragmentation flags under Section 25c GwG.

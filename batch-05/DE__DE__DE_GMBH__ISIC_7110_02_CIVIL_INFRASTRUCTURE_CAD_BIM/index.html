Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office, responsible for USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number) issuance and cross-border VAT procedures; local Finanzamt (municipal tax office) responsible for Steuernummer allocation, corporate income tax, trade tax and turnover tax administration; official filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de), mandatory for electronic submission of VAT returns, tax declarations and annual financial data

E-government filing portal

1) File the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (statutory tax registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt within one month of commencing business; 2) obtain the Steuernummer (tax number) through ELSTER or postal submission; 3) apply separately to BZSt for the USt-IdNr. via Form 'USt 1 TU' or ELSTER special procedure, typically processed within 2-8 weeks; 4) non-resident GmbH shareholders must appoint an authorised tax agent or fiscal representative in Germany for service of process and VAT compliance; 5) the GmbH must register ministries/authority economic-operator data concurrently (Unternehmensregister). No registration fee; formal statutory response due within 30 days

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: GmbH is a fully resident domestic corporation with unlimited worldwide tax liability; subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) at 15%, Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge) at 5.5% of corporate tax (effective 0.825%), and Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at municipal rates typically between 7% and 17% depending on the local Hebesatz factor; distributed profits additionally subject to 25% (plus surcharge) German withholding tax or reduced treaty rate; no pass-through or check-the-box regime exists for GmbH vis-à-vis non-resident shareholders — 1) File the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (statutory tax registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt within one month of commencing business; 2) obtain the Steuernummer (tax number) through ELSTER or postal submission; 3) apply separately to BZSt for the USt-IdNr. via Form 'USt 1 TU' or ELSTER special procedure, typically processed within 2-8 weeks; 4) non-resident GmbH shareholders must appoint an authorised tax agent or fiscal representative in Germany for service of process and VAT compliance; 5) the GmbH must register ministries/authority economic-operator data concurrently (Unternehmensregister). No registration fee; formal statutory response due within 30 days

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: GmbH is a fully resident domestic corporation with unlimited worldwide tax liability; subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) at 15%, Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge) at 5.5% of corporate tax (effective 0.825%), and Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at municipal rates typically between 7% and 17% depending on the local Hebesatz factor; distributed profits additionally subject to 25% (plus surcharge) German withholding tax or reduced treaty rate; no pass-through or check-the-box regime exists for GmbH vis-à-vis non-resident shareholders

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Germany for GmbH. Available elections: 1) Organschaft (fiscal unity) for corporate income tax and trade tax under §14 KStG and GewStG — requires financial, economic and organisational integration of subsidiary under German parent; 2) VAT Organschaft under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG allowing qualifying group treatment with one VAT identification; 3) option for German group relief under §4h EStG interest barrier rules; 4) DTA treaty benefits subject to substance and beneficial-ownership analysis under Multilateral Instrument; hybrid mismatch rules under §4k EStG restrict structure-based deductions

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€25,000 (previous calendar year turnover) and €100,000 (forecast current year turnover) for the §19 UStG small-business exemption; however, a non-resident GmbH performing taxable services in Germany is required to register for VAT and obtain a USt-IdNr. from the first euro of German taxable activity regardless of volume; intra-Community distance sales and cross-border B2C electronic services are governed by the OSS thresholds of the EU One-Stop-Shop framework

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries of goods qualify for 0% VAT under §6 UStG, evidenced by direct transfer to a foreign destination outside the EU. For cross-border B2B services (including CAD/BIM drafting and structural engineering), the place of supply is the customer's place of establishment under §3a UStG, so the German GmbH issues the invoice net of German VAT as outside the scope of German VAT (reverse charge concept); no German input tax deduction is lost for services to EU recipients under the §13b UStG procedure with valid VAT number evidence. Intra-Community supplies of goods to EU VAT-registered customers are zero-rated under §6a UStG provided the customer's VAT number and simplified declaration conditions are met. German input VAT on costs attributable to export activity remains fully deductible via ordinary Vorsteuerabzug

  • Signed BIM/CAD service agreement or Statement of Work identifying the foreign principal and jurisdictional place of supply
  • Commercial invoice issued on German letterhead stating no German VAT applies (outside scope / reverse charge) with reference to §3a UStG
  • Documentary evidence of the foreign recipient's business establishment (VAT registration extract, Chamber of Commerce certificate, or equivalent OECD certificate of residence)
  • Inward payment evidence of foreign-source proceeds (SWIFT MT103 credit advice or Wise business remittance statement)
  • ATLAS customs export declaration and/or electronic inventory log where physical data carriers, deliverables or drawings of customs value are exported

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. Mandatory B2B e-invoicing is phased: from 1 January 2025 all domestic entrepreneurs must be able to receive structured e-invoices conforming to EN 16931 (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD); from 1 January 2027 issuers with prior-year turnover above €800,000 must transmit e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B issuers must issue structured e-invoices. Fiscal data must be retained tamper-proof under GoBD; VAT returns are filed via ELSTER with electronic data submission; there are no continuous transaction controls or direct authority API clearance requirements

Wrongful zero-rating or failure to charge German VAT triggers retrospective VAT assessment for a period of 4 years (standard), 5 years (unintentional under-declaration), and 10 years (aggravated concealment) under §169 AO, plus 6% annual default interest under §233a AO, late-filing penalties, and a 0.5% monthly surcharge on unpaid VAT up to EUR 25,000 (§240 AO). Administrative fines reach EUR 50,000 for negligent offences (§378 AO); wilful evasion constitutes a criminal offence under §§370-374 AO punishable by fines and imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in aggravated cross-border VAT fraud. Managing directors may incur personal joint and several liability under §71 AO and §34 AO; disputed claims the Finanzamt may also require security bonds and deny input VAT recovery on invalid export documentation

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Germany has no general exchange controls, no mandatory approval for ordinary inbound or outbound equity, dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, or trade payments, and no general restriction on non-resident ownership of a GmbH. Practical remittance conditions include a valid commercial purpose, complete beneficial-owner and source-of-funds evidence, sanctions screening, bank transaction monitoring, tax compliance, and compliance with German corporate-law rules. Dividend distributions require distributable profits, proper shareholder approval, and consideration of German withholding tax, treaty relief, or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief where applicable. Interest, royalties, and service fees must be arm's-length, properly documented, and may trigger withholding-tax, transfer-pricing, VAT, or anti-abuse review. Cross-border acquisitions or sensitive-technology transactions may be reviewed under German investment-control rules, but ordinary civil-engineering CAD/BIM activities are generally outside sector-specific foreign-exchange restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general deadline requiring an exporter to convert foreign-currency receipts into euros. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to the bank's account terms, accounting requirements, sanctions controls, and ordinary tax rules. Cross-border payments and receipts may be reportable to Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV for balance-of-payments statistics. The applicable threshold, exemptions, filing frequency, and electronic reporting procedure must be verified against the Bundesbank rules in force on the transaction date; the threshold for many reportable payment transactions was increased to EUR 50,000 from January 2025, while specific exemptions and transaction categories apply. Export documentation, invoices, contracts, customs records where applicable, and payment references should be retained for audit and bank-review purposes.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting within the European System of Central Banks and the Eurosystem; the European Central Bank is responsible for euro monetary policy. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises German banks, payment institutions, electronic-money institutions, and relevant AML obligations. The principal foreign-exchange reporting framework is the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), administered for statistical purposes by Deutsche Bundesbank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium for operational payments and treasury automation; low as a sole banking solution for incorporation capital, regulated trade finance, complex foreign-exchange hedging, cash deposits, or high-value engineering-project flows. An offshore digital EMI is not a German bank and does not provide German deposit-guarantee protection. Its services depend on the licensing perimeter of the relevant EEA or third-country institution, passporting or German authorization, safeguarding arrangements, correspondent-bank access, and acceptance by counterparties and notaries. Wise Business may support eligible international payments and foreign-currency balances, but it should not be assumed to provide a German GmbH formation capital account, German credit facilities, commercial letters of credit, or full domestic-bank functionality. The GmbH should maintain a German or EEA bank account with a regulated institution as the primary operating account and use an EMI only as a documented secondary rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For GmbH formation or a capital increase, the shareholder should remit funds from an account held in the shareholder's own legal name to the account designated by the notary, formation bank, or GmbH, using a payment reference such as 'Stammeinlage gemäß Gesellschaftsvertrag [company name]'. The bank should receive the notarized articles, shareholder resolution, incorporation evidence, expected amount, remitter identity, and source-of-funds package before the transfer. Once the GmbH is registered, share capital and additional shareholder funding should be classified separately as paid-in capital, capital reserve, shareholder loan, or ordinary revenue; they should never be commingled without accounting support. For a shareholder loan, retain a written agreement specifying amount, currency, maturity, interest, repayment, subordination if applicable, and transfer-pricing rationale. For intercompany funding, use a direct bank-to-bank transfer from the documented group entity and preserve ownership, board approval, and source-of-funds evidence. Ordinary euro transfers should use SEPA Credit Transfer where available; non-euro or non-SEPA transfers should use SWIFT with complete ordering-customer, beneficiary, bank, purpose, and invoice or agreement data. AWV statistical reporting should be assessed for the transaction category and threshold applicable on the payment date. Incoming funds should be reconciled to the general ledger, bank statement, shareholder register, and corporate resolutions.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT cross-border transfers for non-SEPA or foreign-currency settlements. A SWIFT MT103 is the traditional customer-credit-transfer message; banks are migrating to ISO 20022 cross-border payment messages, particularly pacs.008, so the GmbH should support both formats through its bank. For high-value or document-sensitive infrastructure-project payments, use a commercial documentary letter of credit, standby letter of credit, bank guarantee, or documentary collection where commercially justified. The bank will require the underlying contract, invoice, counterparty and beneficial-owner information, sanctions and end-use checks, currency, Incoterms where relevant, and evidence of services or delivery. Engineering and BIM services may not generate customs documents; use signed statements of work, milestone acceptance certificates, timesheets, digital-delivery evidence, and client confirmations instead. Maintain maker-checker approval, beneficiary-call-back controls, invoice-to-contract matching, segregation of duties, sanctions screening, and post-payment reconciliation. Do not use personal accounts, unapproved payment intermediaries, or opaque third-party settlement arrangements.Deliverable forward exchange contracts with a regulated bank for documented forecast or committed receivables and payables; hedge limits, maturity, collateral, and close-out terms must be approved under the bank's suitability and risk procedures.Non-deliverable forwards where the relevant currency or jurisdiction is not freely deliverable and the bank offers the product lawfully; settlement, valuation, counterparty, and accounting treatment must be documented.Currency swaps and cross-currency swaps for approved funding or liquidity exposures, subject to bank documentation, counterparty limits, collateral terms, valuation controls, and appropriate accounting designation.Foreign-currency options or collars may be used where offered by a regulated bank and approved under the treasury policy; premiums, fair-value volatility, and suitability must be documented.Spot foreign-exchange transactions for ordinary settlement and liquidity conversion. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (15% Körperschaftsteuer + 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag; plus municipal Gewerbesteuer of typically 14%–17%, producing a combined effective load of approximately 30%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% domestic aggregate (25% Abgeltungsteuer on dividends plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax); reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a shareholder resident in another EU Member State holding at least 10% of the GmbH's capital for an uninterrupted 12-month period; treaty rates commonly 5%–15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

CIT and trade tax returns are due by 31 July of the calendar year following the assessment year; where prepared by a German tax advisor, the deadline extends to 28/29 February of the second following year (e.g., FY2025 return due 31 July 2026, extended to 28 February 2027)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers the obligation to prepare German Local File documentation; Master File is relevant where the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no sectoral Economic Substance Regulations regime, but economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Abuse Rule (§ 42 AO), treaty beneficial-ownership requirements, and OECD BEPS Action 6 limitation-on-benefits provisions. For the GmbH, a physical office, qualified personnel, actual decision-making, and risk management in Germany are decisive for retaining treaty and EU-directive benefits; a pure booking center for overseas CAD/BIM services will be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Steuerhinterziehung

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years for large-scale evasion under § 370(3) AO; fines, confiscation, back taxes plus 1.8% annual interest, and corporate fines under § 30 OWiG.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable under § 69 AO for taxes not paid from corporate assets; criminal restitution, freezing orders, travel restrictions, and detention for refusal of disclosure in enforcement proceedings.
Shell Company / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits, recharacterization under § 42 AO, retrospective withholding tax assessments at 26.375% on deemed distributions, and administrative fines for false beneficial-ownership declarations.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil for the limited-liability shield; directors and beneficial owners may be personally assessed, subjected to travel bans, and criminally investigated for abuse of legal forms.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Income estimation and adjustment under § 162(3) AO; surcharge under § 162(4) AO of at least EUR 5,000 and up to 10% of the incremental tax when the adjustment exceeds EUR 10,000; additional tax plus late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: Director is personally liable for the additional tax if caused by willful or gross-negligent documentation failure; criminal tax-evasion liability where documentation is fabricated or intentionally false.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The GmbH is liable as withholding agent for the unwithheld amounts under § 44(1) EStG and § 50a EStG; late-payment interest, surcharges, and administrative fines up to EUR 25,000 per violation.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under § 69 AO for unrecovered withholding tax; criminal exposure for misdeclaration in serious cases; travel bans and asset seizure during enforcement.
AML / Payment Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Fines under § 56 GwG of up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for legal persons; criminal money-laundering penalties under § 261 StGB of up to 5 years' imprisonment; confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be criminally prosecuted, subjected to travel bans, asset freezes, and disqualification from managing German companies; corporate veil may be pierced where separate identity is used for laundering.

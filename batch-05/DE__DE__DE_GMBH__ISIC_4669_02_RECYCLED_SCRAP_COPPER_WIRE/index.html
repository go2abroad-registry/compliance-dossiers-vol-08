Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern - BZSt) for VAT identification and EU cross-border assistance; local tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax and wage tax; electronic filing via ELSTER portal (www.elster.de); customs export administration via Generalzolldirektion and ATLAS.

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH: (1) notarize the articles of association and file commercial registration (Handelsregister); (2) submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt, which issues the Steuernummer for corporate income tax, trade tax, payroll tax and VAT advance returns; (3) apply to BZSt for the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) where intra-EU supplies or export-commercial transactions require a cross-border identifier; (4) obtain an EORI number through the customs administration if the GmbH itself makes customs declarations. Non-established foreign corporate entities instead use the BZSt non-resident VAT registration procedure and are assigned a separate VAT Steuernummer without a domestic commercial register entry.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - GmbH is taxed as an independent legal entity on worldwide income, subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at entity level; it is not a pass-through vehicle and does not qualify for US-style check-the-box treatment. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - GmbH is taxed as an independent legal entity on worldwide income, subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at entity level; it is not a pass-through vehicle and does not qualify for US-style check-the-box treatment. For VAT purposes the GmbH is the taxable entrepreneur; the company remains fully liable for output VAT and eligible for input VAT deduction on its exported-scrap procurement chain.

Available election forms

Germany does not permit pass-through or check-the-box elections for a GmbH. Statutory grouping regimes require factual integration: VAT and corporate/trade-tax Organschaft (fiscal unity) is available only where financial, economic and organizational integration exists, and for income tax/trade tax a profit-and-loss transfer agreement is required. Other limited statutory options include the §9 UStG election to waive VAT exemption on real estate transactions, and the §19 UStG small-business exemption which is not an elective entity classification. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are restricted by §4k EStG and EU anti-hybrid rules; no free-standing election can avoid those restrictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR - Domestic small-business exemption: €22,000 prior-year turnover and prospective current-year turnover not exceeding €50,000 under §19 UStG. Non-resident taxable persons have no VAT registration threshold and must register from the first taxable supply or distance-sale destination in Germany. A German GmbH in the recycled-copper-wire export trade is registrable regardless of turnover; export zero-rating does not remove the obligation to file VAT returns and maintain export proof.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies qualify for 0% German VAT under §4 Nr.1 and §6 UStG where: (1) the GmbH supplies the shredded non-ferrous copper scrap to a foreign customer; (2) the goods are physically transported or dispatched by the GmbH or by a third party on its behalf from Germany to a destination outside the EU; (3) the goods leave the EU customs territory and are not installed, assembled or otherwise treated in Germany before export; (4) the foreign customer is the actual recipient of the goods abroad; (5) the export is evidenced by the customs export declaration and transport documents; and (6) the business records permit verification of the Buchnachweis and Belegnachweis. Payment receipt in foreign currency is not a statutory VAT condition for zero-rating, but is required under the internal FX-repatriation control framework and strengthens the audit trail. Intra-EU consignments to EU customers are zero-rated only under the intra-Community supply rules (§4 Nr.1b, §6a UStG), not under the export provisions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS Ausfuhranmeldung) including the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and customs-office confirmation of exit
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement), CMR or air waybill evidencing physical movement of the scrap from Germany to the non-EU destination
  • Commercial export invoice stating commodity classification, net/gross weight, copper-wire grade, value, and '0% VAT export supply' reference
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/SWIFT MT103/MT700 documentary-LC evidence showing receipt of the export proceeds
  • Weighbridge certificate and quality/sorting certificate for the recycled scrap copper wire
  • EORI number reference and, where applicable, customs agent appointment or customs-representative instruction

Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or blockchain-based tax-authority API validation. From 1 January 2025, domestic B2B invoices must be issued in a structured electronic format compliant with EN 16931 (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X), with authenticity and integrity protected through EDI or qualified electronic signature/seal. Export invoices may be electronic but must carry all export-specific wording and be retained with the export-documentation file for at least 10 years. No pre-clearance from BZSt or Finanzamt is required, but invoice data must reconcile with ATLAS customs declarations and with the commercial/LC payment flow.

The Finanzamt and customs authorities may audit export substantiation at any time and may compare ATLAS export data with VAT returns, e-invoices and bank receipts. If the exporter cannot prove actual exit from the EU, the 0% rate is retrospectively denied and 19% output VAT is assessed on the invoice value, plus default interest under §233a AO. Negligent or reckless tax shortfall can give rise to administrative fines; intentional tax evasion under §370 AO carries imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years, together with 10-year assessment limitation periods. Non-compliant invoices or missing mandatory invoice particulars may trigger penalties under §26a UStG up to €5,000 per invoice. In cross-border metal trading, shared customs/BZSt intelligence and OLAF checks can escalate a clawback into a criminal proceeding; refunds of input VAT may be disallowed where the underlying output-side export documentation is fabricated or irregular.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates no exchange-control regime for the euro. Both current-account payments and capital-account payments are freely convertible, relying on the EU free movement of capital under Articles 63-66 TFEU and, for the euro, on full single-currency freedom within the Eurozone. There are no domestic restrictions on opening foreign-currency accounts, converting trade invoicing into EUR, or repatriating dividends, loan repayments, or equity proceeds. The practical hurdles are non-FX related: strict AML/KYC compliance under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG), trade sanctions and EU restrictive-measures screening, source-of-funds verification, beneficial-ownership transparency in the Transparenzregister, and German withholding tax on dividends. A German GmbH distributing profits to a corporate shareholder within the EU may obtain a 0% withholding rate under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if the shareholder holds at least the applicable participation threshold and does not abuse the directive; non-treaty or non-EU shareholders generally face a 25% German capital-yield tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, reduced where an applicable double-tax treaty provides a lower rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

German law does not impose any surrender, mandatory conversion, or minimum retention rule on export proceeds. A German exporter may receive and retain EUR/USD/CNY export receipts in a permissible multicurrency account, convert them freely, or use them directly for foreign-currency import settlements. The only statutory compliance obligation of relevance to treasury is statistical reporting: any cross-border payment receipt from a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 equivalent must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank using the AWV electronic reporting channel, generally within seven days of the payment value date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio or statutory timeline within which foreign exchange must be repatriated or liquidated.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central reserve bank of the Federal Republic of Germany, acting within the Eurosystem; banking supervision is shared with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin; cross-border payment reporting obligations are imposed under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance, AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary clearing rail for a German GmbH engaged in cross-border recycled copper-scrap trading. A non-EEA offshore electronic-money institution (EMI) has no passport into Germany, cannot provide a German IBAN, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, cannot grant trade finance, and cannot hold client money in the same protected manner as a licensed credit institution. An EEA-licensed EMI passported into Germany under the Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and supervised by BaFin may be usable only for ancillary collection of small euro-value inbound payments; it remains subject to the same GwG AML duties and cannot replace a tier-one commercial bank for SWIFT MT103 trade settlements, documentary credits, FX hedging, or capital-contribution blocking accounts. Overall feasibility rating: Medium for low-value ancillary collections only; Low for outbound trade disbursements, LC issuance, and GmbH capital escrow structuring.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder must transmit the GmbH capital contribution by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own bank account in the shareholder's name to a clearly designated German capital-escrow account at the chosen tier-one bank. The payment reference must identify the GmbH, the notarial file, and the Bundesbank/AWV statistical purpose-code category for foreign direct investment or acquisition of an equity interest in a German enterprise (capital contribution to a GmbH). For a standard cash contribution, at least half of each subscribed share must be paid in, and the aggregate paid-in amount must not be less than the statutory minimum required under section 7(2) GmbHG; the German bank's confirmation of receipt and free disposal by the managing directors must be available to the notary for the commercial-register filing. No exchange-control permit is required, but the bank will verify the source of funds, beneficial ownership, and consistency of the reference with the notarial deed. If the inbound capital payment exceeds EUR 12,500, the GmbH, as resident reporting party, must submit the Bundesbank AWV cross-border payment report. For a capital increase, the bank also requires the authenticated shareholders' resolution and, in practice, the notary will need the payment receipt before registration of the increase.Outbound settlements to foreign scrap suppliers and end-customers should be structured through SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers where direct invoice settlement is commercially acceptable, and through irrevocable documentary letters of credit under UCP 600 where shipment-based risk mitigation is required. The German bank issues the LC by SWIFT MT700 with sight-payment or negotiation availability, specifying the presentation documents: signed commercial invoice, full set of clean bills of lading or CMR, third-party inspection and weight certificate, copper-grade/analysis certificate, packing list, insurance certificate, and customs/export declarations required by the Incoterms. For performance or pre-shipment security, MT760 standby letters of credit or bank-demand guarantees can be used. Settlement may be denominated in EUR, USD, or CNY in line with the commercial contract; payment is effected only when documents conform on their face. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary clearing rail for a German GmbH engaged in cross-border recycled copper-scrap trading. A non-EEA offshore electronic-money institution (EMI) has no passport into Germany, cannot provide a German IBAN, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, cannot grant trade finance, and cannot hold client money in the same protected manner as a licensed credit institution. An EEA-licensed EMI passported into Germany under the Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and supervised by BaFin may be usable only for ancillary collection of small euro-value inbound payments; it remains subject to the same GwG AML duties and cannot replace a tier-one commercial bank for SWIFT MT103 trade settlements, documentary credits, FX hedging, or capital-contribution blocking accounts. Overall feasibility rating: Medium for low-value ancillary collections only; Low for outbound trade disbursements, LC issuance, and GmbH capital escrow structuring.

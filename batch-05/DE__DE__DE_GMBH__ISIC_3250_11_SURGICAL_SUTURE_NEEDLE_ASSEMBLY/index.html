Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and withholding matters; local Finanzamt (tax office) for income tax, trade tax, and VAT accounting; Generalzolldirektion (GZD) and Hauptzollamt for customs and export control; electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a German GmbH must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt within 4 weeks of formation or commencement of business. The Finanzamt issues a Steuernummer (tax number for income tax and VAT) typically within 2-6 weeks. The VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, DE + 9 digits) is applied for separately from BZSt using form USt 1 TX, usually processed within 3-8 weeks. Non-resident shareholders having no domestic permanent establishment must file limited tax liability declarations with BZSt; EU/EEA residents can apply online via the BZSt portal, while non-EU residents may require a fiscal representative under § 21 AO in certain cases.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: The GmbH is a tax-resident corporation (unbeschränkt steuerpflichtig) taxed on worldwide income. It is subject to 15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at an effective rate of approximately 14%–17% (standard Hebesatz 400%–460%). No pass-through eligibility exists for GmbH under German law. The territorial scope extends to all global income, with foreign tax credit or exemption under double tax treaties as applicable. No tax-exempt free-zone regime applies to this manufacturing activity within Germany. — A foreign entity establishing a German GmbH must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt within 4 weeks of formation or commencement of business. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The GmbH is a tax-resident corporation (unbeschränkt steuerpflichtig) taxed on worldwide income. It is subject to 15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at an effective rate of approximately 14%–17% (standard Hebesatz 400%–460%). No pass-through eligibility exists for GmbH under German law. The territorial scope extends to all global income, with foreign tax credit or exemption under double tax treaties as applicable. No tax-exempt free-zone regime applies to this manufacturing activity within Germany.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box regime; GmbH is a per se corporation for tax purposes. Available elections include: (1) corporate and VAT tax grouping (Organschaft) under §§ 14 KStG and 2a UStG to consolidate profits and VAT with a German controlling parent; (2) waiver of the small-business VAT exemption under § 19(2) UStG to obtain full input VAT deductions; (3) hybrid mismatch alignment and deemed-branch elections under § 4k EStG and § 14 KStG for cross-border instruments; (4) trade tax apportionment (Zerlegung) election for multi-municipality operations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold triggers mandatory VAT registration for a GmbH; any entrepreneur (Unternehmer) is required to register and account for VAT. The small-business exemption under § 19 UStG applies only where annual turnover is at or below EUR 25,000 (taxable year 2025) and is not reasonably expected to exceed EUR 100,000 in the following year; once exceeded, VAT registration is compulsory. For intra-EU distance sales of goods, an annual turnover threshold of EUR 10,000 applies for the destination-country taxation rule under the OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT (steuerfreie Exportlieferung) is available for the supply of goods dispatched to third-country territories under § 4 Nr. 1a and § 6 UStG, and for intra-Community supplies to EU-registered acquirers under § 4 Nr. 1b UStG. The manufacturer of surgical suture needles (ISIC 3250) must evidence that the goods physically left Germany (customs export declaration via ATLAS) and obtain the required export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) under the Ausfuhrnachweisverordnung. Ancillary services such as freight and assembly tied to exported goods may qualify for zero-rating or reverse charge under § 3a UStG.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) submitted electronically via ATLAS, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note with customs exit confirmation (stamp or electronic exit record)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Zahlungsbeleg evidencing foreign exchange repatriation and commercial payment matching

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system or digital stamping mechanism. From 1 January 2025, all VAT-registered businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0.1) under § 14 UStG; issuance of e-invoices becomes mandatory for businesses with annual turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 as of 2027 and for all other B2B suppliers as of 2028. Invoices are transmitted bilaterally via EDI/API, Peppol, or email, with periodic VAT returns filed through ELSTER. No central government API or pre-clearance approval is required for invoice validity.

Finanzamt and customs authorities may retroactively claw back zero-rated VAT if export evidence is incomplete or fraudulent; back taxes accrue interest at 0.5% per month under § 233a AO. Intentional tax evasion under § 370 AO carries imprisonment of up to 5 years or fines; negligent evasion up to 2 years. Administrative fines for documentary violations reach EUR 5,000 per case under § 382 AO, while customs fraud penalties can be up to EUR 50,000 plus seizure of goods. Under § 21 AWV (German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance), export proceeds not repatriated within 12 months must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank; failure to report may trigger fines up to EUR 30,000.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany uses the euro and generally permits unrestricted conversion and cross-border transfers for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no general exchange-control approval requirement for foreign investment, dividend remittance, loan repayment, or trade payments. Remittances remain subject to corporate-law requirements, tax withholding and treaty procedures where applicable, sanctions and export-control restrictions, bank AML/KYC review, and reporting duties. Distributions by a GmbH must comply with German capital-maintenance rules, including GmbH Act sections 30 and 31. Payments to non-residents may require AWV reporting where applicable; reporting is not a foreign-exchange authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no general statutory deadline requiring exporters to repatriate export proceeds and no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio. Export receipts may be retained in foreign-currency or offshore accounts, subject to contractual, tax, sanctions, banking, and applicable reporting requirements. Under AWV section 67, qualifying cross-border payments generally must be reported to the Bundesbank when the statutory threshold is exceeded; the threshold is generally EUR 50,000 per payment since 1 January 2025, subject to statutory exemptions and current Bundesbank instructions. The reporting party, deadline, transaction classification, and any exemption should be confirmed against the current AWV and Bundesbank guidance.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany's central bank and administers balance-of-payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle, BAFA) administers relevant foreign-trade controls. Germany is also subject to the European Union's common currency and payments framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational payments, subject to provider eligibility, supported jurisdictions, currency availability, and enhanced onboarding. An electronic money institution (EMI) account is not a substitute for a German bank account where a notary, commercial bank, or counterparty requires a bank account for GmbH share-capital funding or other formal purposes. E-money balances generally do not constitute bank deposits and are not covered by statutory deposit-guarantee protection; safeguarding applies under the relevant regime. Confirm the provider's regulatory authorization, safeguarding model, account-holder name, IBAN jurisdiction, SWIFT access, payment limits, and restrictions on medical-device or manufacturing customers before relying on the service.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For GmbH share capital, remit funds from the subscribing shareholder's verified account to the account designated for the GmbH formation or capital contribution, using the exact legal entity or formation-account beneficiary instructions supplied by the bank and notary. Identify the transfer accurately as a share-capital contribution or capital increase, as applicable; do not describe equity as a loan. Retain the subscription documents, shareholder resolution where required, bank credit confirmation, remitter statements, and notarial or commercial-register evidence. Shareholder loans should be separately documented, approved where required, booked as liabilities, and transferred with a precise loan purpose. Use SEPA credit transfer for eligible euro payments and SWIFT for other currencies or corridors. Banks may request source-of-funds evidence and may reject third-party funding. Assess AWV reporting for cross-border payments under the current threshold, rules, and exemptions; a report does not replace any bank AML review.Use SEPA credit transfers for euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT customer credit transfers, commonly documented by an MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent, for other international payments. Match beneficiary names, account details, invoice references, currency, and payment purpose to the underlying contract and trade documents. For documentary trade transactions, use a commercial letter of credit (LC) issued or confirmed by an acceptable bank, with terms aligned to the sales contract, Incoterms, shipment documents, presentation period, and applicable UCP 600 rules. Documentary collections may be considered where commercially appropriate. Screen counterparties, banks, countries, vessels, and end users for sanctions and export-control restrictions; retain invoices, purchase orders, transport and customs records, proof of delivery, and any required export authorization. Apply AWV reporting where required and reconcile bank charges, FX rates, and settlement dates.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards for documented commercial exposures, subject to bank eligibility, suitability and appropriateness requirements where applicable, and internal treasury authorization.Non-deliverable forwards where the currency, counterparty, and transaction are lawful and the instrument is available through an appropriately authorized provider; settlement is generally by reference to a fixing rather than physical delivery.Foreign-exchange swaps for managing short-term currency liquidity and settlement timing, subject to counterparty documentation, credit limits, and confirmation controls.Currency options may be used where permitted by the bank and the company's treasury policy; premium, valuation, suitability, and accounting consequences should be assessed before execution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if cross-border transactions with related parties exceed €500,000 for goods or €100,000 for services. Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds €100 million. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and submitted within 60 days upon request.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but substance requirements apply under anti-abuse rules (e.g., §42 AO) and for treaty benefits. The GmbH must demonstrate real economic activity (employees, premises, decision-making) in Germany to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; additional penalty of up to 10% of evaded tax. Civil interest on unpaid tax at 0.5% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for evaded taxes if they knowingly participated; travel bans and asset freezes possible; imprisonment up to 10 years for severe cases.
Shell Company (Briefkastenfirma)

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, retroactive taxation, and penalties up to €25,000. Company may be dissolved under civil law.

Director personal liability: Directors liable for unpaid taxes and social security contributions; personal assets may be seized; prohibition from serving as director for up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 5-10% of adjusted amount (minimum €5,000), and interest on underpaid tax. In severe cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for penalties if they failed to ensure documentation; criminal liability if intentional mispricing.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for serious breaches; revocation of business license; criminal prosecution for money laundering (up to 10 years imprisonment).

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML failures; travel bans and asset freezes; imprisonment for willful neglect.

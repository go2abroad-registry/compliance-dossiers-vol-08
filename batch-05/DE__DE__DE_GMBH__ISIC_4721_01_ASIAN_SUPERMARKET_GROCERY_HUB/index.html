Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr); locally competent tax office (Finanzamt) for income, trade tax and VAT returns; electronic filing via ELSTER portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

The GmbH’s managing director must file the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the local Finanzamt within one month after commencing business. The Finanzamt assigns the local tax number (Steuernummer). For VAT purposes, after fiscal registration, the entity applies separately to BZSt for a statutory VAT identification number (USt-IdNr); the application is made in writing or via BZSt online forms using the questionnaire data. Non-resident entrepreneurs making taxable supplies in Germany follow the same registration and USt-IdNr application procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. German GmbH is a resident corporation subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge (if applicable) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); no pass-through treatment is available. Distributions are separately taxable at shareholder level.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. GmbH status is per se taxable corporation. Group consolidation is possible through an organic unity (Organschaft) by concluding a profit-and-loss transfer agreement and ensuring financial, economic and organisational integration; VAT grouping (Organschaft) under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG is similarly available for tax uniformity. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD apply and must be disclosed in tax returns; there is no unilateral entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No universal numeric turnover threshold before registration is mandatory: any entrepreneur carrying out taxable supplies must notify the Finanzamt. Small-business exemption under § 19 UStG applies when prior calendar year turnover did not exceed €22,000 and current-year turnover is not expected to exceed €50,000; the transaction is then exempt from output VAT, but the business still completes fiscal registration to claim exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods may be zero-rated as an export supply under § 6 UStG if the goods are actually delivered to a third-country territory, sold by a German entrepreneur, and transported by the supplier or an appointed carrier out of the EU to a non-EU destination. Documentary evidence must clearly trace removal from the EU, including customs export declaration, customs confirmation and transport documents. Offshore services are generally not subject to German VAT when the recipient belongs in a non-EU country under the place-of-supply rules; they are not classified as German export zero-ratings. For grocery export consignments, day-to-day completeness of the export proof is mandatory.

  • Customs Export Declaration submitted via ATLAS/EMCS and corresponding export accompanying document with Movement Reference Number
  • Commercial invoice showing supplier, consignee, Incoterms and commodity description
  • Packing list specifying quantities, weights and packaging details
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or equivalent transport document evidencing actual physical removal from the EU
  • Official customs confirmation (Ausfuhrbescheinigung or electronic ATLAS status) used as Einzelausfuhrnachweis where required

Germany does not operate a real-time tax clearance, API stamping or digital voucher regime. Mandatory e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions commenced 1 January 2025: businesses must be able to receive structured e-invoices in EN 16931 / XRechnung / ZUGFeRD. Issuance obligations are phased, from 2027 for enterprises with historical turnover above €800,000 and from 2028 for all domestic B2B suppliers. Retail-sector cash registers and electronic recording systems must use a certified technical security device (TSE) and comply with § 146a AO; no national central fiscal clearance or stamping API exists.

If zero-rating cannot be substantiated by the prescribed export documents, the Finanzamt retrospectively assesses output VAT at 19% or 7% plus statutory interest on the under-declared tax. Late payment triggers additional late-payment surcharges (Säumniszuschlag). Negligent or intentional misdeclaration exposes the company to § 378 AO surcharge penalties or § 370 AO criminal liability (up to five years‘ imprisonment or fines for tax evasion); input VAT claimed on related costs may be clawed back, and managing directors can become personally liable for unpaid VAT under § 69 AO.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility (euro). There is no licensing, no central-bank exchange approval and no capital-control regime for inbound equity, trade payments or outbound dividends; the freedom of capital movement under Article 63 TFEU and Section 4 AWG is in force. Profit remittance is not restricted on exchange-control grounds; the only practical hurdles are statistical reporting and, where applicable, statutory dividend withholding tax (relieved under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable double-tax treaty).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no surrender obligation, no statutory conversion timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; the euro is free-floating and exporters may retain foreign currency. Mandatory balance-of-payments reporting, however, requires a German resident (the GmbH) to report every cross-border payment received or made in excess of EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank - non-cash payments by the 5th working day of the month following the payment month and cash payments within 7 days - through the Bundesbank's Allgemeines Meldeportal, with the exact statutory purpose code.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank, Frankfurt am Main), exercising the German FX statistics and foreign-trade reporting regime under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG) and the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV), together with the Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Klimaschutz (foreign-trade licensing) and the BaFin (banking supervision).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Körperschaftsteuer 15.0% plus Solidaritätszuschlag of 5.5% on the corporate income tax charge (0.825 percentage points) and municipal Gewerbesteuer at a typical effective rate of 14.0%–17.0%, producing an aggregate ordinary corporate burden of approximately 29.8%–33.0%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the withholding tax, giving an aggregate statutory dividend WHT of 26.375%. Dividends paid by a GmbH to a non-resident shareholder are subject to this rate unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or to a lower rate under an applicable DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 100,000 for cross-border controlled transactions in a tax year; above that threshold a contemporaneous Local File must exist. A German Master File is required where the worldwide group consolidated turnover reaches EUR 100 million. The de-minimis relief in the German Transfer Pricing Record-Keeping Regulation (GAufzV, Section 6) exempts low-value related-party transactions from full documentation, but all transactions must still be priced on an arm's length basis.

Economic substance scope: Germany is not an offshore Economic Substance Regulation jurisdiction, but substance requirements are embedded in general tax law. A GmbH engaged in physical grocery retail must demonstrate real premises, inventory, employees, management decisions in Germany, accounting substance and commercial rationale. Section 42 AO (abuse of law), Section 50d(3) EStG (denial of treaty benefits to letter-box entities), beneficial-ownership principles, German CFC rules under Sections 7–13 AStG, and the pending EU ATAD 3 / UNSHELL directives require economic substance to be assessed on a functional and factual basis. A GmbH that cannot prove genuine local retail operations will be treated as a shell; treaty relief will be denied and the economic owner will be re-assessed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under Section 370 AO punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in severe cases; fines under criminal tax law; recovery of evaded taxes plus late-payment surcharges of 1% per month under Section 240 AO and interest under Section 233a AO; seizure of assets and revocation of trade licences possible.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under Sections 34 and 69 AO for intentional or grossly negligent tax shortfalls. The tax authority may assess the director directly, enforce against private German assets, impose court-ordered travel restrictions and detention in collection proceedings, and obtain a civil restitution judgment. In egregious cases the corporate veil is pierced and shareholders who participated in or benefitted from the suppression of revenue are jointly and severally liable.
Shell Company / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under Section 42 AO; denial of DTA benefits under Section 50d(3) EStG; re-allocation of taxable income to the beneficial owner; trade licence revocation under Section 35 GewO; administrative fines; reassessment of taxes with default surcharges.

Director personal liability: The nominal and de facto directors are jointly liable for taxes assessed on the shell. If the GmbH is under-capitalised or shareholder assets are commingled with corporate assets, courts may pierce the corporate veil. Personal travel restrictions and asset freezes can be ordered during tax collection and criminal investigation proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment under Section 1 AStG; documentation penalty under Section 162(4) AO of between EUR 5,000 and EUR 1,000,000; possible estimation of taxable basis; interest on corrected taxes; criminal prosecution under Section 370 AO if false records are submitted knowingly.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the tax return with knowledge of false transfer pricing are personally liable for resulting tax shortfalls in accordance with Sections 34 and 69 AO. Civil damages are available to the GmbH and its creditors. Travel restrictions and imprisonment can be imposed in the course of criminal tax enforcement.
Withholding Tax Violation on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent liability under Section 44(5) EStG and Section 73AO; assessment of unpaid WHT and solidarity surcharge against the GmbH; default surcharges; fines; criminal liability if the failure is intentional.

Director personal liability: The managing director becomes personally liable for the unwithheld amounts, including solidarity surcharge, interest and penalties. The tax authority may enforce against personal assets, prohibit departure from Germany pending payment, and pursue criminal imprisonment where the director knowingly avoids withholding duties.
Anti-Money-Laundering and Cash-Intensive Retail Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Fines under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG) of up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for serious violations; bank account freezes by the Financial Intelligence Unit; criminal sanctions under Section 261 StGB for money laundering if the cash is derived from tax evasion; revocation of business licence.

Director personal liability: Directors are answerable for GwG compliance within the GmbH; personal fines and loss of managing authority can be imposed. In criminal money-laundering cases, personal asset seizure, travel bans and imprisonment are realistic consequences. The corporate veil will not protect a director who intentionally uses the GmbH as a conduit for tainted cash.

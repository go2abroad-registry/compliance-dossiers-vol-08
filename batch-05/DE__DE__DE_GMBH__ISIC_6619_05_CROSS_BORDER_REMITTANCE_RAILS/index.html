Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Statutory minimum share capital EUR 25,000.00; cash subscriptions require payment of at least one quarter of each subscribed share, with total paid-up capital not below EUR 12,500.00. The funds must be deposited in a bank account in the name of the GmbH and be at the free disposal of the managing directors. No formal escrow account is mandated under GmbHG; an escrow-style account may be used, but unconditional free-disposal confirmation is the operative requirement.

Funding & unlock path: Municipal fire safety authorities issue a fire-safety certificate (Brandschutzkonzept) confirming the sufficiency of emergency exits, extinguishing systems, escape paths, and emergency lighting. Structural load clearance for office and server rooms is certified by working evidence under DIN EN 1990. The Gewerbeaufsichtsamt (occupational health enforcement office) performs a workplace inspection and confirms conformity with §5 of the Occupational Safety Act (ArbSchG), Technical Rules for Workplaces ASR A1.3, first aid requirements, accident registers, and safety signage.

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum share capital EUR 25,000.00; cash subscriptions require payment of at least one quarter of each subscribed share, with total paid-up capital not below EUR 12,500.00. The funds must be deposited in a bank account in the name of the GmbH and be at the free disposal of the managing directors. No formal escrow account is mandated under GmbHG; an escrow-style account may be used, but unconditional free-disposal confirmation is the operative requirement.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF) as policy authority; operational administration by the state tax authorities (Landesfinanzverwaltungen) acting through local tax offices (Finanzämter); Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and OSS registration; official digital portal is ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

The GmbH registers with its competent local Finanzamt via the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (questionnaire for tax registration), filed electronically through ELSTER in German, to obtain the Steuernummer (income tax/corporate tax number) and, where applicable, the Umsatzsteuersteuernummer (VAT registration number). The Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID) is issued separately by the BZSt upon application after the local registration is completed. Foreign parent entities with no domestic establishment but with taxable German activities must appoint a fiscal agent or register directly via BZSt for VAT, and through a competent Finanzamt for corporate income tax and trade tax; foreign-incorporated entities are required to certify their legal existence with apostilled documents and, if permanently active, establish a branch registration in the German commercial register (Handelsregister).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A German GmbH is a tax-transparent corporation for no income tax purposes; it is subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% plus solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate tax) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at municipal rates yielding a combined effective rate of approximately 30–33%; distributions to shareholders are subject to 25% withholding tax (plus solidarity surcharge) and final tax treatment at shareholder level.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under German law. The principal available election is the formation of an Organschaft (fiscal unity): a German resident GmbH may conclude a profit and loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) with a controlling German or EU parent to be treated as a tax group for corporate income tax, trade tax and, separately, for VAT purposes; the VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) election under Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG consolidates the GmbH into the controlling entrepreneur's VAT registration. Hybrid mismatch elections and opt-outs are available under the German ATAD implementation (Sec. 4k EStG) for structured arrangements, and the GmbH may elect simplified OSS procedures for distance sales or MOSS for certain services instead of multiple VAT registrations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small entrepreneurs (Kleinunternehmer): EUR 22,000 annual turnover threshold (raised to EUR 25,000 from financial year 2025) for exemption; foreign businesses with no establishment in Germany: zero threshold, mandatory VAT registration from the first taxable supply (financial services may be exempt but registration is still required for input VAT refunds and compliance); pan-EU OSS distance-sales threshold: EUR 10,000. Cross-border money transmission services are typically exempt under Sec. 4 No. 8 UStG, but the entity remains registrable for non-exempt activities and for reporting and input VAT substantiation purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) export supplies (Ausfuhrlieferungen) of goods to third-country territories under Sec. 6 UStG when the goods are actually transported out of the EU and evidenced by export documentation; (b) intra-Community supplies (innergemeinschaftliche Lieferungen) under Sec. 6a UStG at 0% when the acquirer holds a valid VAT ID and the goods are transported to another Member State; and (c) certain services connected with export goods (e.g. freight, processing) under Sec. 4 No. 1–6 UStG. For the present cross-border remittance business, the services qualify as financial/transaction-related services exempt from VAT under Sec. 4 No. 8 UStG without input VAT deduction (exempt without credit) rather than 0% rated; offshore services supplied to a non-EU customer are outside the scope of German VAT when the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction under Sec. 3a UStG, such that no German VAT or zero-rating claim arises.

  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung) and packing list in German/English with Incoterms, commodity codes and values

Germany introduced a mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing regime effective 1 January 2025 under the Wachstumschancengesetz: companies must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung XML or ZUGFeRD 2.x/3.x conforming to EN 16931). Phased issuance obligations commence 1 January 2026 (or 2027 for issuers with turnover above EUR 800,000 and 2028 for all remaining domestic issuers). Germany does not operate an Italian-style real-time fiscal clearance system with a central tax authority API; instead, compliance is enforced through GoBD-compliant archival, the mandatory certified fiscal cash register security module (TSE) for payment processing, EDI/Peppol transmission channels, and BZSt digital validation of VAT identification numbers via the VIES/online API. Digital stamping (digital signatures/fiscal codes) is not generally applicable to German invoices, but e-invoice files must bear qualified electronic signatures or EDI enforcement for security-motivated exceptions.

Export zero-rating and exemptions are scrutinized in tax audits (Außenprüfung) and customs investigations by the Finanzämter, BZSt and Zollkriminalamt. False or missing export evidence triggers retrospective correction of the tax assessment under Sec. 14c and Sec. 15 UStG, clawback of the 0% rate or exemption, disallowance of input VAT deductions, and assessment of VAT plus late-payment interest at 6% annually (Sec. 233a AO). Deliberate or reckless submission of forged export documents or fake inward remittance certificates constitutes tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO or customs evasion, punishable by up to 10 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines; negligent conduct attracts administrative fines under Sec. 378 AO. Individual and representative liability may be imposed on managing directors under Sec. 69 and Sec. 71 AO, including for VAT debts where invoiced tax is improperly refunded.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full currency convertibility for both capital and current account transactions pursuant to EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital. No exchange control approvals or mandatory conversion requirements apply. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, subject only to standard German dividend withholding tax (25% plus solidarity surcharge and, where applicable, church tax) reduced under applicable double tax treaties, and to corporate income tax on the German entity. No central bank permission is required for inbound or outbound remittance flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds in Germany. Export receipts and all other cross-border amounts may be retained in foreign currency or converted freely. A mandatory statistical reporting obligation applies to residents and non-residents under AWV: any payment from abroad received by a resident or paid by a resident to a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance of payments statistics, normally by the 7th calendar day of the month following receipt. The report covers the currency, amount, and transaction purpose code, but does not restrict the use of funds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank; Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for German-licensed or EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) supervised by BaFin. Such institutions may operate via agent or white-label arrangements for remittance services, provided they hold an e-money or payment institution licence under PSD2. Offshore or non-EU EMIs without a German branch, licensed agent, or EU passport are legally not viable for client onboarding or treasury account purposes in Germany. A high-risk remittance agency will require a full PI licence under Section 10 ZAG or a compliant agency agreement with a licensed institution, and correspondent banks generally demand a direct banking relationship with a German or EU regulated bank rather than relying solely on EMI rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity injection into the German GmbH uses a regular SWIFT MT103 wire from the non-resident shareholder. The payment must be credited to the GmbH's operational bank account in Germany, denominated in EUR or convertible currency. The transaction purpose code for balance of payments statistics in Germany is 'Capital participation – acquisition of equity in a German limited liability company' (AWV code group 1030 / direct investment), reported by the accepting bank via the Deutsche Bundesbank's online reporting portal. The recipient bank will require proof of identity and source of funds for the shareholder, a shareholder resolution approving the capital contribution, and confirmation that the minimum share capital (currently EUR 25,000 for a GmbH with 50% initial payment paid-up) is met. No central bank approval is required; however, funds routed through third-country jurisdictions may trigger enhanced due diligence and relevant AWV statistical classification.For operational disbursements, the primary clearing rail for EUR within Germany and the SEPA zone is SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT), working through TARGET2 integrated real-time settlement. For cross-border trade payments outside SEPA, the standard is SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank transfer via correspondent banking network). Commercial letters of credit are issued via SWIFT MT700/MT701; second avis via MT710/MT720; UCP 600 rules apply. For remittance payouts to beneficiary bank accounts, the corporate treasury should use segregated client funds held in a German licensed bank and disbursed through SWIFT or local clearing rails; cash-pickup payouts are settled via white-label partner networks using pre-funded accounts or real-time settlement through a licensed foreign exchange broker. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Documentation is mandatory upon request for cross-border transactions with related parties if the aggregate annual value of ordinary remuneration exceeds EUR 1,000,000 or extraordinary transactions exceed EUR 100,000. A Master File is required for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 100,000,000, and a Country-by-Country Report for groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply the Gulf-cooperation Economic Substance Regulations, but the general anti-abuse rule of § 42 AO and the substance-over-form principle under § 41 AO serve an equivalent function. A cross-border remittance GmbH must have its own business premises in Germany, local personnel performing treasury, compliance, and contracting duties, and hold the payment-institution licence required by the German Payment Services Supervisory Act (ZAG). Otherwise, the tax authorities may disregard the GmbH as a mere letterbox, attribute its receipts to the foreign beneficial owner, and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from two up to ten years for aggravated cases and a criminal fine of up to five times the evaded tax amount (§ 370 AO). Civil late-payment interest is charged at 0.6% per month on the unpaid tax from the due date.

Director personal liability: Managing directors face personal arrest, preventive passport blocking, and travel bans. Under §§ 34 and 69 AO, directors are personally liable for unpaid corporate income tax, trade tax, surcharges and penalties where they intentionally or negligently failed to comply with declaration or payment duties.
Shell Company / Economic-Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax administration will pierce the corporate structure under § 42 AO, reallocate profits to the foreign beneficial owner, and impose a transfer-pricing documentation penalty of 5% of the upward adjustment (minimum EUR 5,000, maximum EUR 300,000) under § 162(3) AO. Existing DTA benefits may be retroactively denied.

Director personal liability: Directors lose the benefit of limited liability if capital was never contributed or was artificially withdrawn. They become personally responsible for the entire corporate tax debt plus interest and penalties, and can be disqualified from holding future directorships.
AML Violations / Unauthorised Remittance Activity

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover for legal persons under the GwG/ZAG; imprisonment of up to five years for money laundering under § 261 StGB; mandatory revocation of the money-transmission licence; liquidation of the GmbH may be ordered as a preventive measure.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal conviction and imprisonment, disqualification from any executive role in the financial sector, asset freezes, travel bans, and joint-and-several personal liability for all customer restitution claims in tort.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 5% of the upward profit adjustment (minimum EUR 5,000, maximum EUR 300,000) for late or inaccurate documentation; additionally, the tax office may deny all related-party deductions and apply a deemed income assessment with an extended assessment period.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the additional corporation and trade tax, interest, and penalties if they authorised the intercompany invoices or signed incomplete returns; in cases of wilful falsification, the same criminal sanctions as under tax evasion (§ 370 AO) apply, including imprisonment of up to five years.

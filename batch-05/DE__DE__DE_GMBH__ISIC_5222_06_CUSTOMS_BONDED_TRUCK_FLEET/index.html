Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent local Finanzamt administers corporate income tax, trade tax assessments, and domestic VAT registration. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) assigns VAT identification numbers (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) and operates relevant federal tax services. Tax filings and registration questionnaires are generally submitted electronically through ELSTER; the tax offices of the Länder administer most assessments.

E-government filing portal

After incorporation and commercial registration, the GmbH must submit the electronic Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung to the competent Finanzamt through ELSTER and obtain its domestic tax number (Steuernummer). It may request a USt-IdNr. from the BZSt, generally through the Finanzamt or the BZSt's online service. A foreign entity carrying out taxable transactions in Germany must register with the competent Finanzamt, provide its incorporation and business records, identify its German taxable activities, and obtain a Steuernummer and, where required, a USt-IdNr. A non-EU-established taxable person may generally be required to appoint a German VAT fiscal representative under applicable UStG rules; this is not a general requirement for EU-established businesses. Customs activity may separately require an EORI number from German Customs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is ordinarily subject to corporation tax and solidarity surcharge at entity level, as well as trade tax where applicable; it has no U.S.-style check-the-box election to be treated as a pass-through entity. A qualifying corporate tax group (Organschaft) may be established by meeting statutory requirements, including a profit-and-loss transfer agreement and financial integration. German rules implementing anti-hybrid-mismatch provisions may deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences; they are not elective entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general domestic VAT registration turnover threshold applies to an ordinarily taxable business. The small-business scheme (Kleinunternehmerregelung) is a VAT exemption regime subject to statutory turnover limits, generally EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year under the rules effective from 2025; it does not establish a general registration threshold. For qualifying cross-border intra-EU B2C distance sales and certain electronically supplied services, the separate EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold may be relevant.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A qualifying supply of goods transported from Germany to a destination outside the EU may be exempt with the right to deduct input VAT (export delivery) if the statutory conditions are met and the supplier retains prescribed proof of export and transaction records. Intra-Community supplies may qualify for exemption where the goods are transported to another EU Member State, the customer has a valid VAT identification number, and documentary and reporting requirements are satisfied. Services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer or payment is offshore; their VAT treatment depends on the place-of-supply rules, customer status, service type, and any applicable exemption. A VAT refund requires valid input-tax documentation and compliance with filing and substantiation requirements.

  • Customs export declaration and evidence of release and exit from the EU, such as the electronic export confirmation or equivalent customs evidence
  • Commercial invoice, transport contract, and shipment evidence such as a bill of lading, CMR consignment note, or other carrier documentation, as applicable
  • Customer order, contract, delivery records, and evidence linking the exported goods to the relevant invoice and customs declaration
  • For qualifying intra-Community supplies: valid customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and accurate recapitulative statement reporting
  • Banking or payment records supporting the transaction where relevant; an Inward Remittance Certificate is not a general German statutory prerequisite for export VAT exemption

Germany does not generally require real-time invoice clearance, fiscal digital stamping, or transaction-by-transaction API approval. Since 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for relevant domestic B2B transactions. Mandatory issuance is being phased in, with transitional rules generally allowing paper or other electronic formats through 2026 and, subject to statutory conditions, through 2027 for certain issuers. Structured invoices must comply with applicable European and German requirements, including EN 16931-compatible formats or permitted alternatives. Separate rules apply to public-sector invoicing.

Germany imposes no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated in foreign currency. Tax authorities may deny or reverse an exemption or input-tax deduction if substantive conditions or proof requirements are not met, and may assess resulting VAT, statutory interest, and applicable late-payment charges. Intentional or reckless tax evasion can trigger criminal or administrative proceedings under the Fiscal Code, including fines or imprisonment for tax evasion; other applicable tax offences may also result in penalties. Incorrect VAT returns may require correction, and recovery may extend to earlier tax periods subject to statutory assessment limitation rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has fully liberalised current and capital account convertibility. The Euro is freely convertible; no prior exchange-control authorisation is required for cross-border capital movements, profit repatriation, dividend distributions, or loan repayments, subject only to European Union and German restrictive measures, investment screening, sanctions screening, and anti-money laundering controls. Profit remittances from a GmbH to non-resident shareholders are permitted once annual financial statements are adopted and German corporate taxes are paid or adequately provisioned; residual practical hurdles stem solely from banking KYC/AML verification and negative news or sanction screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds and no statutory conversion ratio. Exporters may freely retain foreign currency receipts in domestic or offshore foreign currency accounts. The only binding obligation is statistical reporting: any payment received from abroad or made abroad of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank under section 67 AWV, generally by the 7th calendar day of the month following the month in which the transaction occurred. No export proceeds share is required to be converted into EUR.

Central bank supervision: European Central Bank (ECB) as monetary authority; Deutsche Bundesbank as the German national central bank and competent authority for external sector statistics and foreign trade reporting; Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) as the licensing authority under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EEA-authorised e-money institutions passporting into Germany under PSD2; Low for non-EEA/offshore digital EMIs. A legally approved EEA EMI can operate in Germany via EU passport and provide payment accounts, but many tier-1 German correspondent banks treat EMIs as higher AML-risk and may reject collections or disbursements routed through their settlement accounts. A third-country digital EMI must obtain a separate BaFin licence and establish a physical German branch; fully offshore EMI accounts are not acceptable as primary settlement accounts for customs-bonded operations, fuel excise matters, or customs duty payments without an approved German nexus.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, and capital reserves from non-resident shareholders are routed through SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA remittances or SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001) for EEA transfers. The payment must carry a purpose code identifying direct investment / intercompany financing, e.g. 'Direktinvestition' or 'Kapitalerhöhung', together with a corporate resolution, subscription agreement or loan agreement. Transactions of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV reporting obligation. The receiving bank will also conduct sanctions screening and ask to see the underlying capital commitment document before crediting the funds.Outbound trade disbursements for fleet acquisition, fuel, tolls, spare parts, and freight settlements are executed via SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001) for EUR-denominated payments to EEA beneficiaries, and SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA beneficiaries. For large or documentary purchases, an irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit is issued via SWIFT MT700 and advised through the beneficiary's bank. Express clearing through TARGET2 is used for time-critical customs duty payments and large Euro-value settlements; intra-day liquidity is covered via the bank's nostro accounts. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Corporate Income Tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% Solidarity Surcharge on the CIT amount, resulting in a combined federal rate of 15.825%; plus municipal Trade Tax (Gewerbesteuer) with rates between 7% and 17.15% depending on the Hebesatz, resulting in an aggregate effective rate of approximately 22.825% to 32.975%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Corporate Income Tax withholding on dividends plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding amount).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Corporate income tax return and trade tax return due by 31 July of the following year (e.g., FY2024 due 31 July 2025). Upon filing by a tax advisor, automatic extension to the last day of February of the second following year (e.g., FY2024 due 28 February 2026). Electronic filing must be transmitted by the statutory deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Documentation required if annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 6 million for goods and services, or EUR 600,000 for intangible property or interest. Master File required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 100 million. Local File required for entities exceeding EUR 6 million in related-party transactions. Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) required for groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750 million; filing via BZSt.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) not separately codified as in offshore jurisdictions; however, German tax law imposes substance requirements through: (1) Section 8c KStG loss forfeiture upon change of ownership unless continuity of business operations (qualifizierter Branchenwechsel test); (2) Section 50d EStG anti-abuse for treaty benefits requiring genuine economic activity and substance; (3) Section 14 AStG (Hinzurechnungsbesteuerung) CFC rules targeting passive income of low-taxed foreign subsidiaries with substance carve-out for active business; (4) Trade tax nexus requires fixed place of business or permanent representative; (5) German GAAP requires that management, accounting, and operational decisions be physically located in Germany for a GmbH to maintain its tax residency. For customs-bonded truck fleet, substance requires licensed customs warehouse, GPS-sealed fleet, and customs compliance officer physically present in Germany.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 1 million for severe cases; imprisonment up to 10 years for tax evasion resulting in significant tax loss (e.g., > EUR 50,000). Asset forfeiture and confiscation of illegally obtained profits under Section 73 StGB.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes under Section 69 AO if they fail to ensure tax payment; civil restitution of evaded amounts plus interest; travel ban (Aufenthaltsverbot) or passport seizure possible; imprisonment for directors who orchestrate or negligently permit evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (Verrechnungspreisdokumentation)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 1 million under Section 162(4) AO for documentation failure; estimate of taxable income by tax authority (Schätzung) resulting in additional taxes; fines up to EUR 5,000 per day for failure to submit documentation in a timely manner.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly liable for additional tax assessments; potential criminal liability for tax evasion if non-compliance is intentional; personal liability for penalties under Section 69 AO.
Customs Fraud and Bonded Warehouse Violations (Zollbetrug)

Statutory sanction: Customs penalties up to EUR 50,000 per violation; confiscation of goods and trucks; revocation of customs-bonded operator license; criminal prosecution for customs fraud with imprisonment up to 10 years; fines up to EUR 1 million for organized customs fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for customs duties and penalties; possible travel ban; imprisonment for customs fraud; personal obligation to ensure customs compliance under Section 130 OWiG (regulatory offense liability).
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1 million or 10% of annual turnover for serious violations; criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment for money laundering; confiscation of assets; exclusion from public contracts.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for fines and penalties if they fail to implement AML compliance programs; imprisonment for knowing participation in money laundering; travel bans and asset freezes under EU sanctions.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits under Section 50d EStG; re-characterization of income; retroactive tax assessments; fines up to EUR 500,000; potential criminal liability for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for taxes, penalties, and interest; piercing of corporate veil under German case law (Durchgriffshaftung) for intentional creditor or tax authority harm; travel bans and imprisonment for fraud.
Failure to Comply with CFC Rules (Hinzurechnungsbesteuerung)

Statutory sanction: Immediate taxation of passive income in Germany at 15% plus solidarity surcharge and trade tax; retroactive tax assessments; fines up to EUR 100,000 for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for additional taxes assessed; potential criminal liability for tax evasion; travel bans for non-payment of assessed taxes.

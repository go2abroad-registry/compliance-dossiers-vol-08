Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and EU administrative assistance; Local Tax Office (Finanzamt) responsible for income/corporation tax and trade tax assessments; electronic filing via ELSTER (ElsterOnline-Portal).

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH: (1) After notarial incorporation and registration in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), file the Questionnaire on Tax Registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt; (2) receive the local tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT (Umsatzsteuer); (3) separately apply to BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) for EU cross-border transactions, providing proof of economic activity; (4) where customs clearance is required, obtain an EORI number from the Main Customs Office (Hauptzollamt). A non-resident entity without a permanent establishment must appoint a fiscal representative (Fiskalvertreter) for VAT compliance in Germany unless an EU-established entity under recovery rules applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Germany; a GmbH is statutorily classified as a corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax and trade tax. Available elective mechanisms include: (i) corporate tax Organschaft under Section 14 German Corporation Tax Act (KStG) via a profit and loss pooling agreement with a controlling resident parent, and (ii) VAT Organschaft (fiscal unity) under Section 2(2) German VAT Act (UStG) requiring financial, economic, and organizational integration. No domestic hybrid mismatch election is available; anti-hybrid rules under ATAD limit use of hybrid instruments and reverse hybrids.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies to a German-resident GmbH; all taxable supplies trigger VAT registration. For small domestic entrepreneurs (Kleinunternehmer) under Section 19 UStG, the reduced threshold is EUR 22,000 taxable turnover in the preceding calendar year and a current-year limit of EUR 50,000. For foreign non-established businesses supplying digital, telecommunication, or electronic services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies for intra-EU mini-one-stop-shop (MOSS) purposes; otherwise each EU member state's domestic rules apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies qualify for German VAT exemption with input VAT deduction (zero-rating effective) when: (i) goods are exported to third-country territories (Section 6 UStG) with verified proof of export; (ii) goods move intra-community to a registrant in another EU member state under a valid VAT ID and subject to Intrastat/EC Sales List reporting (Section 6a UStG); or (iii) services are supplied where the place of supply is outside Germany and outside the EU under B2B rules, transferring liability to the customer's jurisdiction (reverse charge). For digital services to non-EU consumers, the place of supply is the customer's residence, removing German VAT without a refund claim, provided the supplier does not exceed the MOSS threshold and the supply is B2C; documentary support must demonstrate customer residence and business status.

Germany has introduced mandatory structured B2B e-invoicing incrementally from 1 January 2025 under the Growth Opportunities Act. From 2025, all domestic German B2B taxable persons must be able to receive e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD formats); from 1 January 2027, issuers with turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all businesses must issue e-invoices. No real-time fiscal clearance mechanism, government tax clearance API, or digital stamping is currently required for ordinary B2B invoices; e-invoices must be transmitted electronically in a structured XML-based format and archived per German record-keeping requirements of Section 147 German Fiscal Code (AO).

Germany applies enhanced audit procedures for export zero-rating, especially through the customs export database and risk profile checks. If the exporter fails to present the customs export proof within 60 days of dispatch, or if documentary requirements are not met, the tax office may retroactively deny the VAT exemption and issue an assessment (Umsatzsteuernachforderung). Deliberate falsification or misuse of export documents constitutes tax evasion under Section 370 AO, subject to imprisonment up to 5 years (or up to 10 years in aggravated cases), fines, interest under Section 233a AO (0.5% per month on assessed VAT), penalty surcharges, and full disallowance of input VAT recovery. The burden of proof rests on the exporter; circular or subsequent supplies without physical movement are treated as domestic supplies and are not eligible for zero-rating. Retrospective clawback periods generally extend up to 10 years for tax evasion and 5 years for negligent late assessments, plus late-payment penalties under Section 240 AO.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully convertible and liberalised monetary system under EU free movement of capital and Articles 67 et seq. of AWG. Both current account payments and capital account transactions are lawful without general exchange-control licensing. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and share-loan redemptions may be executed freely in EUR or any other convertible currency; no Central Bank approval, conversion quota, or surrender requirement applies. The only practical hurdles are bank-level AML/KYC verification of economic substance, source of funds, and beneficial ownership, plus possible withholding tax or corporate income tax disclosures for dividends. German banks also operate as payment-statistics reporting agents for the Bundesbank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender, forced conversion, or statutory timeline for repatriating export proceeds in Germany. German resident exporters are not obliged to convert foreign-currency export receipts into EUR, nor must they hold them in a specific account. However, under Section 67 of the AWV, any cross-border payment from a non-resident into Germany exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for external commerce and payments statistics. The reporting obligation is met by the receiving domestic bank, which must report by the fifth working day after the end of the month in which the payment was received. The invoicing company must therefore ensure that bank transfer messages contain the correct AWV payment code and a full commercial reference for prompt and compliant statistical reporting.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank) is the competent foreign exchange and payment statistics authority under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV). Policy formulation for foreign economic controls sits with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK), while banking conduct and KYC enforcement are supervised by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin). There is no separate German exchange-control department; the Bundesbank’s Foreign Trade and Payments Statistics Division administers all mandatory statistical reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech and EMI viability is MEDIUM for secondary collection but LOW as a sole treasury rail. A German GmbH may legally maintain accounts with a licensed EEA electronic-money institution such as Solactive/Solarisbank-based digital banks, Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex provided the institution holds a valid MiCA/PSD2 licence and has completed BaFin passporting notification. These EMIs offer fast IBAN onboarding, local German IBANs, and convenient app-store receivables collection. However, an offshore EMI is not a full ‘Kreditinstitut’ under the German Banking Act (KWG) and cannot hold customer deposits on the same legal footing as a commercial bank. German compliance practice requires the primary treasury and capital-contribution account to be held with a licensed credit institution. Use EMIs only for secondary settlement, mass payout, or virtual-card issuance, never for the statutory capital escrow account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital for the German GmbH is routed via SWIFT MT103 from the investing entity’s account to the GmbH’s EUR account at a German tier-1 commercial bank. The remittance information field must contain the following elements: (1) the GmbH’s exact legal name and Handelsregister number; (2) the clear corporate action statement, e.g., ‘Einzahlung in die Kapitalrücklage – Übernahme der neuen Geschäftsanteile’ or ‘Kapitalerhöhung zur Stärkung des operativen Geschäfts; (3) the AWV statistical reason code for a capital transfer as designated by the receiving bank, normally in the Bundesbank report group ‘K’ for capital flows; and (4) the reference number of the notarised capital-increase deed. Prior to opening the account, the bank will require a notarised shareholder resolution, a certified updated shareholder list (Gesellschafterliste), the capital-contribution escrow agreement if one is used, and the legal proof that the capital is not financed by a prohibited loan-back arrangement. The receiving German bank will automatically submit the Bundesbank external-payments report for any contribution exceeding EUR 12,500.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements, service fees, cloud infrastructure, content-licensing payments, and streamer remuneration use SEPA instant credit transfer for EU beneficiaries and SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank cover) for non-EEA beneficiaries. A German GmbH may legally maintain accounts with a licensed EEA electronic-money institution such as Solactive/Solarisbank-based digital banks, Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex provided the institution holds a valid MiCA/PSD2 licence and has completed BaFin passporting notification. These EMIs offer fast IBAN onboarding, local German IBANs, and convenient app-store receivables collection. However, an offshore EMI is not a full ‘Kreditinstitut’ under the German Banking Act (KWG) and cannot hold customer deposits on the same legal footing as a commercial bank. German compliance practice requires the primary treasury and capital-contribution account to be held with a licensed credit institution. Use EMIs only for secondary settlement, mass payout, or virtual-card issuance, never for the statutory capital escrow account.

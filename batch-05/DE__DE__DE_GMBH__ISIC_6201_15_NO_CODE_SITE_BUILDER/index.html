Handelsregister Abteilung B — Commercial Register department of the Local Court (Amtsgericht) having jurisdiction over the registered office; maintained electronically via handelsregister.de and the justice portals of the German Länder.

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (German Limited Liability Companies Act, GmbHG) — §§ 1-5 (legal nature, registered office, share capital), § 2(1) (notarial form), §§ 7-8 (application content and capital evidence), §§ 10-12 (Commercial Register entry and legal effects); supplemented by Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB) §§ 8-16 and § 18 (Commercial Register and firm name) and Gewerbeordnung (GewO) § 14 (trade registration duty).

E-government filing portal

After notarial incorporation and registration in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), the GmbH is automatically registered with the competent Finanzamt. The managing director must complete and submit the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER within 4 weeks, including forecast profit, turnover, and ownership data. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the EU Treaty and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG). Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation are unrestricted, subject to mandatory reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical purposes (threshold: €12,500 per transaction or aggregated monthly). No prior approval is required for cross-border payments, but documentation must be retained for five years. The euro is a freely convertible currency; no surrender requirements exist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists in Germany. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or foreign accounts. However, all cross-border payments (including export receipts) exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five days of receipt or payment, using the standard Z5/K2 reporting form. The reporting obligation is statistical, not regulatory, and does not impose conversion ratios.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and payment oversight; the Federal Ministry of Finance for foreign trade and payments regulations (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as PPRO, Solarisbank, or N26 Business can provide cross-border payment accounts and FX services. However, for a GmbH with significant capital injection and trade settlement needs, a full-service commercial bank is preferred. EMIs may lack full SWIFT correspondent banking capabilities and may impose lower transaction limits. Legal constraints: EMIs must be licensed by BaFin and are subject to the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG). They cannot provide credit or hold client funds as deposits; they are limited to e-money issuance and payment services. For a no-code website builder with recurring subscription revenue, an EMI can be viable for operational liquidity but not for capital escrow or complex trade finance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Inbound equity injections from non-resident shareholders must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with proper purpose codes. For capital contributions, use purpose code 'P01' (Direct investment) or 'P02' (Capital increase) under the German AWV reporting. The remitter must include the GmbH's name and account number, and the bank will require a copy of the capital increase resolution (Gesellschafterbeschluss) and the commercial register filing. Verification: The receiving bank will verify the UBO and source of funds; the Deutsche Bundesbank must be notified of the transaction within five days if it exceeds €12,500. For escrow accounts, the bank may require a notarized escrow agreement.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For payments to suppliers or service providers, use SWIFT MT103 for direct transfers. The GmbH may also use SEPA (Single Euro Payments Area) for EU-denominated payments, but for non-EU counterparties, SWIFT is mandatory. For recurring subscription payouts to international partners, consider using payment service providers (e.g., Stripe, Paddle) that aggregate and settle via local clearing systems. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer; plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) on corporate income tax, yielding a combined pre-trade-tax rate of 15.825%; municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax, 14–17% typical) raises the aggregate effective tax burden to approximately 30–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on gross dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate withholding of 26.375%. A trade-tax deduction may apply for corporate shareholders under certain conditions; the fully distributed profit retains the standard corporate tax of approximately 31%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The filing deadline is 31 July of the calendar year immediately following the tax year for calendar-year taxpayers. Where the return is prepared by a qualified tax adviser (Steuerberater), the deadline is automatically extended to 28/29 February of the second year following the tax year. Further individual extensions may be granted through the competent Finanzamt in writing or via ELSTER.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory deadline as the corporate income tax return: for calendar-year filers generally 31 July of the following year, with automatic extension to 28/29 February of the second following year when prepared by a Steuerberater.

Late-filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag) of 0.25% of the assessed tax per commenced month, minimum EUR 25 per month, maximum 10% of the assessed tax, capped at EUR 25,000. Late-payment surcharge (Säumniszuschlag) of 1% per month on unpaid tax bills. Interest accrues on back taxes at 0.15% per month (1.8% per annum) for post-2020 tax periods, with the interest clock generally starting 15 months after the end of the tax year. Misrepresentation may lead to a criminal tax evasion penalty rather than a mere administrative surcharge.
Box 2 Gesamtbetrag der Einkünfte (total positive income before loss carryforwards) calculated on an accrual basis under German commercial law (Maßgeblichkeitsprinzip), with mandatory tax adjustment add-backs and deductions. For a non-resident GmbH with a German permanent establishment, report only the PE profit attributable to that German PE as determined under § 49 EStG and the applicable DTA. If the GmbH has no German PE and no domestic source income, enter nil and ensure the return is marked accordingly. Late-filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag) of 0.25% of the assessed tax per commenced month, minimum EUR 25 per month, maximum 10% of the assessed tax, capped at EUR 25,000. Late-payment surcharge (Säumniszuschlag) of 1% per month on unpaid tax bills. Interest accrues on back taxes at 0.15% per month (1.8% per annum) for post-2020 tax periods, with the interest clock generally starting 15 months after the end of the tax year. Misrepresentation may lead to a criminal tax evasion penalty rather than a mere administrative surcharge.
Box 3 Loss deduction (Verlustabzug): current-year losses are not carried back in CIT; taxable income is computed after deducting loss carryforwards from prior years under § 10d EStG and the minimum-taxation rules (only 60% of income above EUR 1,000,000 is offsetable, with the remainder carried forward without time limit). Non-residents may offset only losses that are related to a German PE and that have been previously recognised by the German tax authorities. The return should be accompanied by detailed loss-certificate documentation (verlustbescheinigung) where requested. Late-filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag) of 0.25% of the assessed tax per commenced month, minimum EUR 25 per month, maximum 10% of the assessed tax, capped at EUR 25,000. Late-payment surcharge (Säumniszuschlag) of 1% per month on unpaid tax bills. Interest accrues on back taxes at 0.15% per month (1.8% per annum) for post-2020 tax periods, with the interest clock generally starting 15 months after the end of the tax year. Misrepresentation may lead to a criminal tax evasion penalty rather than a mere administrative surcharge.
Box 4 Non-deductible expenses (Hinzurechnungen und Kürzungen): add-backs for taxes of person or property, 25% of membership fees to representative bodies, penalties, and portions of certain profit-sharing expenses; also declaring the ratio of foreign dividends eligible for the § 8b KStG exemption. Non-residents must specifically declare any German-source royalty or interest income, and cannot deduct expenses that would be disallowed if the GmbH had no substance in Germany. Documentation of arm’s-length allocation is required to support any net-basis deduction from German source income. Box 1 Gewerbeertrag (trade income) before allocation; for a GmbH, all income is deemed to derive from trade and commerce, so the base equals ordinary commercial profit plus statutory additions under § 8 GewStG. Non-resident companies without a German permanent establishment generally have no German trade-tax exposure. If a PE exists, allocate trade profit to the specific domestic PE and provide the relevant profit-allocation documentation in the PE tax file. The same late-filing and late-payment penalties as for the CIT return; a formal refusal to submit a Gewerbesteuer declaration may trigger a fixed penalty under § 152 AO plus enforcement through compulsory administrative fines (Zwangsgeld) until the return is filed.
Box 2 Add-back schedule for financing expenses: interest on long-term debt, rental costs for movable assets and licences, and other finance-related additions in accordance with § 8 GewStG, subject to the net-interest threshold. Non-residents must maintain segmented books and records showing German PE revenue, financing costs and asset rental. Cross-border revenue sharing arrangements must be supported by an arm’s-length transfer pricing policy. The same late-filing and late-payment penalties as for the CIT return; a formal refusal to submit a Gewerbesteuer declaration may trigger a fixed penalty under § 152 AO plus enforcement through compulsory administrative fines (Zwangsgeld) until the return is filed.
Box 3 Municipal trade-tax multiplier (Hebesatz) declaration and product of municipal tax base: the trade tax is the product of Gewerbeertrag, the federal base rate of 3.5% and the municipal multiplier (typically 250%–490%). If the German PE is located in more than one municipality, an apportionment is required and the respective municipal codes must be entered separately. Non-resident GmbHs should verify whether the DTA allocates that PE profit to Germany and whether trade tax is creditable against pillar-two rules. The same late-filing and late-payment penalties as for the CIT return; a formal refusal to submit a Gewerbesteuer declaration may trigger a fixed penalty under § 152 AO plus enforcement through compulsory administrative fines (Zwangsgeld) until the return is filed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 for intercompany sales and purchases of physical goods, and EUR 500,000 for other intercompany transactions (services, licences, financing transactions) annually, when the counterparty is a related party. A German entity belonging to a group with consolidated worldwide turnover exceeding EUR 750,000,000 must also produce a Master File and Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) if other threshold criteria are satisfied.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but substance is central to the German tax framework through the place of effective management rule, the registered-office tie-breaker in DTA practice, § 42 AO (abuse of law), the ATAD-implemented CFC rules and the general requirement that a GmbH maintain real corporate governance, staff, facilities and decision-making functions in Germany. A mere mailbox GmbH with nominees and virtual registered office lacking such substance may be treated as being effectively managed abroad, losing access to German DTA benefits and exposing the arrangement to reconstruction of taxable income under German anti-abuse provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment of six months to five years for simple tax evasion, and from one to ten years for aggravated evasion under § 370 AO; the court may also impose fines equivalent to daily fines up to 360 daily rates, in addition to full re-assessment of all omitted taxes, interest at 1.8% – 6% per annum, late-payment and late-filing penalties, and a possible court-ordered confiscation of profits.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing officers who intentionally or negligently breach their tax duties are personally liable for the resulting tax debts under § 69, § 71 AO. Criminal prosecution can lead to imprisonment of the natural person responsible. German tax authorities may issue travel bans and exit blocks to secure tax claims, and withdrawal of the director’s permit to conduct business. Civil restitution can be pursued against the director for compensation of the corporate loss caused by the evasion, and in serious cases the court may pierce the veil under German case law to hold the directing mind personally liable for the corporate tax debt.
Shell Company / Sham GmbH (Substance Abuse)

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard the legal form under § 42 AO and attribute income to the actual economic owner; the GmbH may lose treaty protections as a Shell company, trigger denial of CIT deductions and foreign tax credits, and be subject to an income-add-back of EUR 5,000 if bookkeeping deficiencies are found. The company is also exposed to trade-tax assessments on deemed income, and the directors may face disqualification from corporate management positions.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow the GmbH to act as a shell in a scheme of abuse are jointly and severally liable for the tax avoided. The personal liability includes civil restitution of unpaid corporate taxes, possible travel bans and entry denials, and imprisonment for participation in fraudulent organisational structuring. If the company is used for money laundering, directors face additional prosecution under § 261 StGB with penalties up to ten years imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Violation

Statutory sanction: Where the German tax authority makes a transfer-pricing adjustment because documentation obligations have not been complied with, a surcharge of 6% of the resulting positive income adjustment is imposed under § 162 Abs. 4 AO, in addition to the additional tax, interest, and late-payment penalties. Failure of the ultimate parent to file the CbCR may result in separate registration and declaration fines of up to EUR 10,000 per report, plus publication of the non-compliance order.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign transfer pricing documentation that they know or should know is false are personally exposed to fines and imprisonment under § 370 AO. They are personally liable for the corporate penalties if the violation stems from their instruction to use unsupported intercompany prices. Travel bans and asset freezes may be ordered to secure the corporation’s contingent tax liability, and civil actions for breach of duty may be brought by the liquidator or creditors of the insolvent GmbH.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Noncompliance (GwG and Transparency Register)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines for natural persons of up to EUR 200,000 and for legal entities of up to EUR 1,000,000 under § 56 GwG if a serious, repeated or systematic violation is established. Failure to update the transparency register may be punishable as a misdemeanor with fines per case, and the relevant beneficial-ownership filings are publicly disclosed in the EU beneficial ownership register. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) may also stop accepting payment service providers and insist on enhanced due diligence procedures.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause or tolerate the absence of GDPR data protection and AML processes may personally incur the administrative fines. Where the GmbH is knowingly used as a vehicle for money laundering, directors face imprisonment of up to ten years for money laundering and up to five years for criminal VAT and tax evasion in the context of false payment transactions. They may also be personally barred from serving as directors of any German company for ten years, and a travel ban may be issued to prevent flight when criminal proceedings are imminent.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for German VAT identification numbers and certain federal tax functions; the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax administration, tax numbers, VAT registration, and tax assessments. Electronic filings and tax registration questionnaires are generally submitted through the ELSTER portal (Elektronische Steuererklärung). Trade tax is assessed by the municipality based on the tax assessment issued by the Finanzamt.

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH must be registered with the competent Finanzamt after incorporation and commencement or planned commencement of business. The GmbH submits the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER, providing its commercial-register details, registered office, business activities, directors, shareholders, expected turnover and profits, banking information, permanent establishments, and VAT circumstances. The Finanzamt issues a German tax number (Steuernummer), which is used for corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT filings, and correspondence. The GmbH applies for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) from the BZSt, normally through the BZSt application process or through the relevant tax registration information. A VAT identification number is distinct from the domestic tax number and is required for specified intra-Community transactions and EU VAT reporting. A foreign entity making taxable supplies in Germany must register with the appropriate German Finanzamt unless a valid special rule, marketplace collection mechanism, reverse charge, or other statutory exception applies. Germany generally does not impose a universal fiscal-representative requirement on non-resident businesses, although non-EU businesses may appoint a tax representative and may need a German representative for particular customs, VAT, or procedural matters. A foreign entity must also obtain an Economic Operators Registration and Identification number (EORI) from German customs if it imports, exports, or otherwise conducts customs activities in its own name.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A GmbH is a separate legal and tax person subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), the solidarity surcharge on corporate income tax, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Distributed profits are generally taxed again at shareholder level under the applicable German participation, withholding, treaty, or domestic rules. A GmbH is not fiscally transparent by default. Corporate income tax is generally assessed at 15 percent, plus a 5.5 percent solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, while the effective trade tax rate depends on the municipality's statutory multiplier. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A GmbH is a separate legal and tax person subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), the solidarity surcharge on corporate income tax, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Distributed profits are generally taxed again at shareholder level under the applicable German participation, withholding, treaty, or domestic rules. A GmbH is not fiscally transparent by default. Corporate income tax is generally assessed at 15 percent, plus a 5.5 percent solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, while the effective trade tax rate depends on the municipality's statutory multiplier. German tax residence generally arises from the GmbH's registered office or place of management in Germany, subject to treaty residence rules. German-resident companies are generally subject to worldwide taxation, with treaty and domestic relief for qualifying foreign income.

Available election forms

Germany has no United States-style check-the-box election for a GmbH. A GmbH remains subject to entity-level corporate taxation unless its legal form is changed or a specific statutory regime applies. Partnerships may in certain circumstances elect corporate income tax treatment under Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act, but that election is not available to a GmbH. German tax law contains specific elections and regime choices, including options concerning VAT small-business treatment, group taxation through an Organschaft where statutory financial, organizational, and economic integration requirements are satisfied, and certain tax-accounting or pension provisions; these are not entity-classification elections equivalent to check-the-box. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Sections 4k of the Income Tax Act and related provisions can deny deductions, impose inclusion consequences, or restrict relief for hybrid arrangements. There is no general elective hybrid-mismatch exemption for a GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold below which a business making taxable supplies is exempt from VAT registration. A business must register when it carries on an independent economic activity involving taxable supplies or otherwise becomes liable for German VAT. The small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG may apply from 2025 where the prior calendar year's domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and exclusions. The small-business exemption is a tax-relief rule, not a general VAT-registration threshold, and a business using it generally cannot deduct input VAT. A German GmbH conducting card-issuing, payment, software, platform, or spend-management activities must analyze each revenue stream separately because exempt financial services, electronically supplied services, intermediary services, and taxable ancillary services may have different VAT treatment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Germany generally treats qualifying exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union as VAT-exempt with input-tax credit under the German export exemption rules, rather than as a domestic 0 percent taxable supply. The supplier must establish that the goods physically left the EU customs territory and satisfy the applicable supplier, recipient, destination, and documentary conditions. Required evidence normally includes the customs export declaration and electronic confirmation of exit, or qualifying alternative commercial and transport evidence. Supplies to VAT-registered businesses in another EU Member State may qualify as VAT-exempt intra-Community supplies if the goods move from Germany to another Member State, the customer provides a valid EU VAT identification number, the supplier performs required VAT-ID and recapitulative-statement checks, and transport and reporting conditions are satisfied. Services supplied to a business customer outside Germany are generally governed by place-of-supply rules and may be outside German VAT rather than technically zero-rated; B2B services are commonly located where the customer is established, subject to special rules. Financial services, payment services, card-related services, intermediary services, software, data, and management services require transaction-specific classification because some financial services may be VAT-exempt while operational, technical, licensing, platform, and ancillary services may be taxable.

  • Electronic export declaration, customs export MRN, and customs confirmation that the goods exited the European Union customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract or purchase order, packing list, and transport documentation such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or equivalent evidence
  • Evidence identifying the non-EU recipient, destination, quantity, description, and value of the exported goods
  • Proof of delivery or transport to the non-EU destination where required to supplement customs evidence
  • German VAT records linking the export transaction to the accounting entry, invoice, customs declaration, and exit confirmation
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification number, VAT-ID validation evidence, transport evidence, and correct recapitulative statement reporting
  • For services: contract, customer establishment and business-status evidence, customer VAT number where relevant, service description, place-of-supply analysis, and evidence supporting any reverse-charge or outside-scope treatment
  • Inward remittance evidence may support commercial substantiation but is not generally a universal statutory condition for German export VAT exemption. A blanket inward-remittance certificate requirement does not generally apply under German VAT law.

Germany does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance or digital-stamping system. VAT invoices and VAT returns are generally transmitted electronically through ELSTER, but invoices are not routinely pre-cleared by the tax authority. From 1 January 2025, domestic German businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. The phased obligation to issue structured e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies generally applies from 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year domestic turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for other in-scope businesses, subject to statutory transitional rules and exceptions. A compliant e-invoice generally uses an EU-standard structured format, such as XRechnung or a permissible hybrid format such as ZUGFeRD, and must contain the legally required invoice data. PDF-only invoices are not structured e-invoices. The rules do not generally apply in the same manner to consumers, exempt supplies, certain low-value invoices, or specified foreign and cross-border transactions. The company should retain invoice data and supporting records in an auditable electronic format under German bookkeeping and record-retention rules.

Germany has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days as a condition for export VAT exemption. Export claims may be denied or reversed if the supplier cannot prove export, EU exit, recipient status, transport, or the applicable place-of-supply conditions. The Finanzamt may assess additional VAT retroactively, deny or claw back input-tax credits, charge interest under the German Fiscal Code, and impose late-payment consequences. Incorrect VAT returns may trigger administrative penalties, tax-offence exposure, or criminal proceedings for intentional tax evasion under the German Fiscal Code; negligent conduct may result in administrative fines or other procedural sanctions. False customs declarations can trigger customs duties, import VAT, customs penalties, seizure, forfeiture, and criminal liability under applicable customs and fiscal-criminal rules. Repeated or material non-compliance may lead to tax audits, enhanced record requests, denial of tax relief, liability of responsible officers in appropriate cases, and additional assessments for the relevant limitation period. Documentation must be contemporaneous, internally consistent, and retained for the applicable German statutory record-retention period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany uses the euro and has no general exchange controls. Current-account and capital-account transfers are generally convertible and may be made subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, prudential, and statistical reporting rules. Dividends, interest, principal, and other lawful cross-border payments are generally remittable after corporate-law approvals, tax withholding and reporting, and documentation of the underlying transaction. Treaty relief or refund procedures may be required for withholding tax. Banks may require evidence of source of funds, beneficial ownership, corporate approvals, tax treatment, and the commercial purpose of payments. EU and German sanctions restrictions apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no general statutory deadline requiring exporters to repatriate export proceeds and no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign currency or offshore, subject to contractual, sanctions, tax, accounting, and applicable AWV reporting requirements. Cross-border payments and receivables may require Bundesbank statistical reporting where the applicable reporting conditions and thresholds are met. From 1 January 2025, the general AWV reporting threshold for reportable cross-border payments was increased to amounts exceeding EUR 50,000, subject to statutory exceptions and reporting instructions. Reporting classification, deadlines, exemptions, and any special rules should be verified for the transaction and reporting period.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany’s central bank and administers foreign-trade and payment reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin), not the Bundesbank, authorizes and supervises German payment institutions and electronic-money institutions, subject to applicable EU single-market rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ordinary operating and collection accounts, but Low as a substitute for a German bank relationship supporting a high-risk card-issuing business. An EEA-authorized EMI or payment institution may provide services in Germany only within its authorization and applicable passporting or establishment framework. A non-EEA provider has no automatic right to provide regulated services in Germany. EMI safeguarding is not equivalent to a bank deposit guarantee. The GmbH must separately obtain all required German or EEA permissions for its own activities; using a sponsor bank, processor, or program manager does not itself confer authorization. Confirm safeguarding, access to client funds, insolvency treatment, settlement access, and contractual termination rights.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal corporate income tax burden of 15.825%. Trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additionally imposed by the municipality; the combined typical burden is approximately 29.8% to 33%, depending on the municipal multiplier.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital gains withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic rate of 26.375%. A qualifying EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption may reduce withholding to 0%, subject to minimum holding, holding-period, anti-abuse, and substance requirements. An applicable tax treaty commonly reduces the dividend rate to 5% for qualifying corporate holdings and 15% in other cases, but the specific treaty must be reviewed.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the annual corporate income tax and trade tax returns are generally due by 31 July of the following year, corresponding to seven months after fiscal year-end. A tax adviser filing generally extends the deadline to the last day of February of the second subsequent year, subject to statutory exceptions and temporary legislative extensions. Tax balances are payable one month after the tax assessment notice, unless another statutory date applies.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Advance payments are generally due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September, and 10 December. The annual return reconciles the actual liability against prepayments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is generally required for extraordinary or significant cross-border related-party transactions and, upon request, for transactions exceeding annual thresholds of EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for services and other transactions. A Master File is generally required for German entities belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 100 million in the preceding financial year. Documentation must generally be produced within 30 days of a tax authority request; records for extraordinary transactions should be prepared contemporaneously.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no single general Economic Substance Regulation equivalent to a standalone offshore ESR regime. Substance is relevant under the general anti-abuse rule, treaty beneficial-ownership and principal-purpose analyses, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive anti-abuse provisions, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules, and transfer-pricing requirements. A virtual-card issuer should maintain genuine German functions, qualified personnel, risk control, board decision-making, payment-services compliance, customer and transaction records, adequate capitalization, and operational premises proportionate to its activities. A foreign parent cannot be used to shift profits where the German GmbH performs the economically significant functions or bears the relevant risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unauthorized payment services or e-money issuance

Statutory sanction: BaFin may prohibit the business, order cessation, appoint measures to protect customers, and impose administrative sanctions. Unauthorized provision of regulated payment services may constitute a criminal offense punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine under the ZAG, subject to the applicable statutory facts.

Director personal liability: Managing directors who authorize or permit unauthorized activity may face personal criminal prosecution, civil liability for customer and creditor losses, regulatory disqualification, and liability for taxes and social charges. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from criminal proceedings, enforcement, or insolvency orders.
Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code is generally punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax, interest, late-payment charges, and criminal proceeds may also be recovered.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable under Sections 34 and 69 of the Fiscal Code for unpaid taxes caused by intentional or grossly negligent breach of tax duties. Criminal confiscation, civil restitution, professional disqualification, and, in serious proceedings, arrest or travel restrictions may apply.
Withholding tax failure and unlawful profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may be assessed for the unwithheld amount, interest, late-payment charges, and penalties. Intentional or reckless breach may create tax offense or administrative liability depending on the conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for withholding tax under Section 69 of the Fiscal Code where they intentionally or grossly negligently failed to remit amounts. The recipient may also face reassessment, denial of treaty relief, and anti-abuse consequences.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income, deny deductions, recharacterize transactions, impose interest, estimate the tax base, and apply documentation-related surcharges. Documentation penalties may generally range from 5% to 10% of the income adjustment, with statutory minimum amounts, where the applicable conditions are met.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability to the GmbH for tax losses caused by deliberate or grossly negligent transfer-pricing failures and may face criminal tax-evasion exposure where false documentation or intentional concealment is established.
Money laundering and AML control failure

Statutory sanction: The Money Laundering Act permits substantial administrative fines, including up to EUR 1 million or twice the economic benefit for certain breaches, and for specified obligated entities up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual total turnover, subject to the applicable provision. BaFin may impose supervisory measures and publish sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML officers may face personal administrative or criminal exposure for intentional laundering, aiding and abetting, reporting failures, or obstruction. Civil claims, professional bans, asset seizure, and travel restrictions may arise in criminal or enforcement proceedings.
Sanctions, export-control, and prohibited-country exposure

Statutory sanction: Violations of German foreign trade and sanctions laws may result in criminal imprisonment, substantial fines, confiscation, account blocking, and regulatory action. The exact sanction depends on the violated EU regulation, the Foreign Trade and Payments Act, intent, and transaction value.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted for intentional or negligent sanctions breaches, may become personally liable for losses caused by inadequate controls, and may face confiscation, disqualification, arrest, or travel restrictions where legally ordered.
Insolvency and wrongful continuation of business

Statutory sanction: Failure to file insolvency proceedings in due time may constitute a criminal offense, with imprisonment of up to three years or a fine under the German Insolvency Code; aggravated or fraudulent conduct may attract higher sanctions. Payments after insolvency may be recoverable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited post-insolvency payments, unpaid taxes, employee-related liabilities, and creditor losses. Criminal prosecution, asset seizure, director disqualification, and court-ordered travel restrictions may apply; piercing of the corporate veil is exceptional and fact-dependent.
False corporate records and accounting manipulation

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include tax reassessment, accounting and regulatory fines, criminal liability for false accounting or fraud, supervisory intervention, license consequences, and confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for creditor, customer, investor, and tax losses caused by intentional or grossly negligent reporting failures and may face imprisonment, fines, disqualification, restitution, and enforcement measures.
Illegal disguised distributions and shareholder extraction

Statutory sanction: The payment may be recharacterized as a hidden profit distribution, denied as a deduction, subjected to withholding tax and solidarity surcharge, and followed by interest, penalties, and tax audits.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable to restore improperly distributed assets under German company law and may face tax liability, shareholder and creditor claims, and criminal exposure if records were falsified or taxes were intentionally evaded.

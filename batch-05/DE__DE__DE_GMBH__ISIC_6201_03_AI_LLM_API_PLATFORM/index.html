Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Division, Local Court (Amtsgericht – Handelsregister) at the company's registered seat.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG) – Sections 1, 3, 4a, 5 (minimum capital), 6 (managing directors), 7–10 (registration application and commercial register).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag/Satzung) and the formal registration application (Anmeldung zum Handelsregister).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 26 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum stated share capital is EUR 25,000; at least EUR 12,500 must be paid in cash before registration. The contributions must be deposited into a German bank escrow/capital contribution account (Kapitalerlagungskonto) held in the name of the company under formation, blocked until registration.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a fire safety clearance from the municipal fire audit authority (Brandschutzprüfung) for office premises, structural load certification from a civil engineer confirming the place of business is suitable for office occupancy, and workplace occupational clearance under the German Occupational Safety and Health Act (ArbSchG) including first aid provisions and workstation ergonomics.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum stated share capital is EUR 25,000; at least EUR 12,500 must be paid in cash before registration. The contributions must be deposited into a German bank escrow/capital contribution account (Kapitalerlagungskonto) held in the name of the company under formation, blocked until registration.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The Shareholders' Meeting, acting as the Founding Board, resolves: (1) the subscription and paid-up allotment of the initial share capital in accordance with the Articles of Association; (2) the adoption of the registered contractual seat as the company's statutory registered office; (3) the appointment of [Name] as sole Managing Director and authorized legal representative; (4) authorization of the Managing Director to open the escrow capital account, sign the corporate bank account, and execute the Commercial Register application; (5) confirmation that capital contributions will be fully paid and available for corporate purposes; and (6) ratification of all preliminary steps taken prior to this resolution.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for federal tax administration and USt-IdNr/VAT ID issuance; local Finanzamt (tax office) for municipal/federal corporate income tax, trade tax, and payroll tax; central official portal is Elster (www.elster.de) and BZSt Online-Foren.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned GmbH is first registered with the local Handelsregister (commercial register). The local Finanzamt subsequently allocates a tax number (Steuernummer) upon post-incorporation registration. The company then applies to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr) under § 27a UStG using the official application form. Foreign shareholders may also require an EORI number for customs operations. The entire procedure is generally completed within 1–3 months; the USt-IdNr itself is issued after verification of the registered company data.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: A German-resident GmbH is taxed at entity level as a taxable corporation. It is subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax, 15%) plus solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate income tax) and Gewerbesteuer (trade tax, roughly 7–16% effective depending on municipality). The GmbH has unlimited tax liability on worldwide income while resident in Germany; foreign-source income is also subject to German taxation subject to DTT relief and controlled-foreign-company rules. — A foreign-owned GmbH is first registered with the local Handelsregister (commercial register). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A German-resident GmbH is taxed at entity level as a taxable corporation. It is subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax, 15%) plus solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate income tax) and Gewerbesteuer (trade tax, roughly 7–16% effective depending on municipality). The GmbH has unlimited tax liability on worldwide income while resident in Germany; foreign-source income is also subject to German taxation subject to DTT relief and controlled-foreign-company rules.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Germany; entity classification is mandatory under German tax law. The only statutory elective mechanism for a GmbH in a cross-border structure is tax unit formation (Organschaft) with a controlling enterprise under §§ 14–19 KStG. Hybrid mismatch and interest limitation elections are available under the ATAD implementation (e.g., making § 4k EStG adjustments). For treaty purposes, a GmbH qualifies as a resident where its place of management and seat are in Germany.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For foreign suppliers of digital services (including AI/LLM API platform services), Germany does not apply a monetary registration threshold if the place of supply is in Germany; registration is required on the first Euro of taxable turnover. For domestic small entrepreneurs (Kleinunternehmer), the exemption threshold under § 19 UStG is €25,000 in the previous calendar year and €100,000 expected in the current calendar year, but this exemption is generally not available to non-resident entities. For intra-EU distance sales of goods, the special distance-selling threshold is €100,000 in Germany. No VAT exemption threshold is available for typical B2B AI API subscriptions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to third countries can be zero-rated under § 6 UStG only if the physical export is duly proven and the recipient is outside Germany/the EU. For services, including AI/LLM API platform services, the place of supply is deemed to be the recipient's business jurisdiction if the recipient is an entrepreneur (B2B rule in § 3a UStG). Such services are therefore zero-rated in Germany and subject to reverse charge in the customer's country, provided the supplier retains adequate documentation: contract with a foreign B2B recipient, valid VAT identification number (if EU) or country-of-establishment evidence (if non-EU), and an invoice stating 'no VAT due – reverse charge' (or 'VAT-free intra-EEA'). Pure software, streaming, and cloud/AI inference services are generally treated as electronically supplied services and follow the B2B place-of-supply rule.

  • Customs Export Declaration: for physical goods, the export shipment must be declared through ATLAS (customs IT system), and the validated Ausfuhrbegleitdokument (EAD) / export confirmation must be retained as proof of export.
  • Transport Evidence: bill of lading (B/L), airway bill (AWB), CMR consignment note, or original transport contract evidencing movement out of the EU.
  • Commercial Invoice: a standard invoice showing the exporter, importer, Incoterms, goods description, quantity, value, and export-related VAT exemption/zero-rating remark.
  • For Services: a written service contract identifying the foreign business recipient, documentary proof of the recipient's VAT ID (for EU) or official business registration (for non-EU), and the invoice without German VAT because the place of supply is abroad.

As of 1 January 2025, German B2B electronic invoicing is mandatory for all resident entrepreneurs. Invoices must be transmitted in a structured EDI-compliant format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0 XML) and be machine-readable; the issuer must also retain an electronic copy for 10 years. Germany is phasing in continuous transaction controls (CTCs) with real-time e-invoice clearance (E-Rechnung/E-Reise) expected under the EU ViDA framework, but as currently implemented there is no real-time API clearance or fiscal stamping requirement for ordinary AI API platform invoices. For B2C supplies, no German-specific digital fiscal VAT receipt requirement applies at present.

German customs and tax authorities may scrutinize export zero-rating claims with targeted audits (Außenprüfung). Where export or service evidence is fraudulent or insufficient, zero-rating is retrospectively disallowed, resulting in an additional VAT assessment plus interest of 0.5% per month on the unpaid tax and late-payment charges. Deliberate substantive false statements in VAT returns constitute tax evasion under §§ 370–371 AO and are punishable with imprisonment up to 5 years or monetary fines; negligent or administrative violations may attract penalty surcharges up to €25,000. In a VAT evasion case, the tax assessment period extends to 10 years, and the managing director of a GmbH may be personally liable as a statutory agent for the VAT clawback. For related-party intercompany arrangements, transfer-pricing adjustments may also trigger VAT recharacterization and compound penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full euro convertibility for both current and capital account transactions; no foreign-exchange control, approval, licensing, or quantitative restriction applies to inbound or outbound payments. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is freely executable and generally exempt from German dividend withholding tax under an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, provided the relevant documentary prerequisites are met. The only operational constraint is the statistical reporting obligation for cross-border payments of EUR 12,500 or more under Sections 60-67 of the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung — AWV); this is a data-reporting mechanism, not a convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. German law imposes no mandatory surrender, forced conversion, repatriation, or holding-period requirement for export proceeds. The GmbH may retain USD earnings from Stripe payouts and other export receipts in domestic or foreign bank accounts without conversion; each individual cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 must be declared to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics under the AWV, but the declaration imposes no obligation to convert the currency.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), jointly with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin — Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) for banking supervision and payment regulation; the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) governs foreign-trade statutes. Monetary policy and final settlement authority reside with the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Ramp Fit: High

Medium feasibility. The GmbH may lawfully hold accounts with BaFin-licensed or EEA-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under PSD2; fund safeguarding rules apply in lieu of deposit insurance, and EMIs cannot grant credit. Offshore EMIs, including UK FCA-authorised firms, lost EU-passporting rights post-Brexit and require a German/EU licence or registered agent for lawful provision of account services. Crypto-native EMIs offering USDC settlement can serve as a secondary payment rail only if the USDC flows are cleansed through a BaFin-regulated crypto custodian (Section 1(1a) KWG) or a fully licensed on/off-ramp; otherwise account closure risk remains elevated.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Crypto-asset exposure — the stated target payment rail includes USDC, which German banks uniformly treat as elevated anti-money-laundering and sanctions risk.

Root cause: Crypto-asset exposure — the stated target payment rail includes USDC, which German banks uniformly treat as elevated anti-money-laundering and sanctions risk.

Defense evidence: Adopt and present a written digital-asset AML/CTF policy; settle all USDC positions exclusively through a BaFin-regulated crypto custodian or a fully licensed on/off-ramp; supply wallet-provenance and transaction-flow diagrams; provide a certification that the AI platform performs KYC on every API customer; commit to converting USDC to EUR via the house bank within a documented treasury policy; and maintain an immutable audit trail of token addresses, timestamps, and counterparty sanctions-screening results.
Non-resident beneficial ownership and opaque shareholding structure over the German GmbH.

Root cause: Non-resident beneficial ownership and opaque shareholding structure over the German GmbH.

Defense evidence: File the notarised Articles of Association and current Handelsregister excerpt; register the ultimate beneficial owner in the German Transparenzregister before account opening; provide passport copies of all natural-person beneficial owners, a signed group-structure chart, board resolutions approving account signatories, and bank reference letters from the jurisdiction of the parent entity.
High-frequency, geographically dispersed cross-border API token transactions producing unusual payment velocity for a newly incorporated entity.

Root cause: High-frequency, geographically dispersed cross-border API token transactions producing unusual payment velocity for a newly incorporated entity.

Defense evidence: Deliver an AI-platform compliance pack comprising the standard API terms of service, data processing agreements, IP licensing inventory, sanctions-screening output, historical transaction-velocity projections, Stripe Radar screening configuration, and a monthly transaction-monitoring report from the payment processor or a licensed AML service provider.
Perceived lack of German substance and possible nominee-director arrangements.

Root cause: Perceived lack of German substance and possible nominee-director arrangements.

Defense evidence: Provide Gewerbeanmeldung (business registration certificate), the registered-office lease agreement, the managing director's proof of German or EU residence, social-security or payroll filings if employees exist, a future headcount and office-substance plan, and detailed minutes of the shareholders' meeting appointing the Geschäftsführer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contributions (Stammeinlage) and shareholder loans are routed by SWIFT MT103 for non-euro originators or by SEPA Credit Transfer (IBAN) for EU originators, with the purpose text 'Haftungseinlage — Stammeinlage' or 'Darlehen eines Gesellschafters'. Each inbound cross-border payment equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV statistical framework, typically transmitted via the Bundesbank's digital reporting portal and cross-checked by the beneficiary bank for plausibility. The initial capital contribution is credited to a capital escrow account (Einlagekonto) held by the law firm, notary, or bank until the GmbH is entered in the Handelsregister; the bank verifies the remitter's identity, the origin-of-funds statement, the notarised incorporation agreement, and the corresponding capital-contribution declaration before releasing operational access.Outbound settlement for AI/LLM trade disbursements is cleared through (a) SWIFT MT103 for non-euro counterparties, (b) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for euro-area vendors, (c) bank-confirmed commercial documentary letters of credit governed by UCP 600 for hardware procurement or large IP licensing transactions, and (d) card-network and platform payouts such as Stripe Connect for merchant/partner distribution. USD receivables are converted to EUR at agreed house-bank rates or retained as short-term USD working capital in a multi-currency account; all non-euro disbursements require AWV statistical reporting when the individual transaction exceeds EUR 12,500, and the bank will enforce sanctions-screening and purpose-code verification on each wire.Vanilla EUR/USD FX forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte)Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for non-convertible or restricted counterparty currencies outside the G10 universeFX swaps (EUR/USD and major crosses) for temporary liquidity rebalancingCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for alignment of term debt and USD-contractual exposurePlain-vanilla FX options and collars for hedging high-ticket AI platform receivable invoicesShort-tenor EUR/USD money-market deposits and roll-over sweeps as a natural unhedged FX buffer

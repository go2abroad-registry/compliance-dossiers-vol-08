Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office) for USt-IdNr/VAT ID and recapitulative statements; local Finanzamt (tax office) for income, VAT and trade tax registration and assessment; ELSTER portal (www.elster.de) for digital filing.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned German GmbH: (1) register the company with the commercial register (Handelsregister) at the competent Amtsgericht; (2) submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER to the responsible Finanzamt, disclosing all shareholders, directors and intended marine bunkering activities; (3) obtain the Steuernummer (corporate, VAT and trade tax number); (4) apply separately to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr); (5) maintain a permanent local registered office and ensure the GmbH's managing director has valid identification. For foreign entrepreneurs without a domestic branch, a simplified VAT registration through BZSt may apply, but the target GmbH is a resident entity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned German GmbH: (1) register the company with the commercial register (Handelsregister) at the competent Amtsgericht; (2) submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER to the responsible Finanzamt, disclosing all shareholders, directors and intended marine bunkering activities; (3) obtain the Steuernummer (corporate, VAT and trade tax number); (4) apply separately to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr); (5) maintain a permanent local registered office and ensure the GmbH's managing director has valid identification. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not recognise check-the-box elections; a GmbH is compulsorily taxed at entity level for corporate income tax and trade tax. Available statutory elections include: (i) conclusion of a profit and loss transfer agreement to create a corporate income tax and trade tax fiscal unity (Organschaft); (ii) establishing a VAT fiscal unity under Sec. 2(2)(2) UStG by integrating a financially, economically and organisationally integrated subsidiary; (iii) waiver of VAT exemption on certain real estate transactions under Sec. 9 UStG; (iv) election of the standard invoicing method or transport-profit method for trade tax apportionment; (v) application of the German Hybrid Mismatch Rules under Sec. 4k EStG, which may require adjustment of deduction treatment for hybrid instruments; no elective partnership pass-through is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for a GmbH; every entrepreneur is required to register VAT immediately upon the first taxable supply. The small-business exemption of Sec. 19 UStG (EUR 22,000 prior year / EUR 50,000 current year) is not available to legal entities such as a GmbH. A non-resident entrepreneur without a German establishment must register for German VAT if making intra-Community distance sales above EUR 10,000 pan-EU, but for bunkering supplies physically supplied in Germany, no de minimis threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of bunker fuel and marine lubricants to seagoing vessels (HS 8901/8902) engaged in international commercial traffic qualify for zero-rated VAT under Sec. 8(1)(1) and (2) UStG, implementing Art. 148(a) of the VAT Directive. Zero rating applies on the conditions that the supplier delivers the goods directly to the ship operator or its ordered agent, the vessel is a seagoing ship used in international navigation, the goods are physically loaded for consumption on board, and the vessel subsequently departs on an international voyage. The supplier must retain evidentiary proof of delivery and of the international voyage. Offshore services supplied for the direct needs of such ships and their crews, such as pilotage, towage and marine salvage, also qualify under Sec. 8(1)(3) UStG. The supply is not an export supply under Sec. 6 UStG unless the goods are separately transported out of the EU customs territory.

  • Customs export declaration or electronic export summary (ATLAS) if the supply is structured as an export supply
  • Bunkering Delivery Note (BDN) signed by receiver, supplier and barge, identifying the ship's IMO number, port, fuel grade and quantity
  • Bill of Lading or terminal receipt confirming the vessel's departure and international voyage
  • Commercial VAT invoice with zero-rate statement and all statutory invoice elements under Sec. 14 UStG
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT payment confirmation proving receipt of proceeds from the foreign ship operator

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital tax certification exists in Germany. From 1 January 2025, domestic B2B invoices must be issued, transmitted and received in a structured electronic format (CEN EN 16931 XML) and archived; mandatory e-invoicing for issuers is phased in from 1 January 2027 for enterprises with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all other enterprises. VAT recapitulative statements and intra-Community transaction returns must be submitted via ELSTER/BZSt; no digital stamping protocol is applied.

The Finanzamt may scrutinise the zero-rating documentary file; if documentation is incomplete or fraudulent, the tax treatment is retroactively reversed, VAT at 19% becomes due, input VAT claimed on fuel purchases is clawed back, and penalty interest at 0.15% per month under Sec. 233a AO is assessed. Tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO can lead to imprisonment of at least six months and up to ten years, plus a criminal fine equal to the evaded tax; negligent misconduct under Sec. 378 AO attracts administrative fines up to EUR 50,000. The limitation period for assessment is ten years for evasion and four years for ordinary reassessment; repeat offenders may face personal liability of the managing director under Sec. 69 AO and joint and several liability of the enterprise under Sec. 73 AO.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully liberalized capital account regime with current account convertibility. Capital contributions and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without prior approval, subject only to de minimis notification thresholds (typically EUR 1 million for portfolio investments). Profit remittance requires post-tax withholding and beneficial ownership certification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirements apply to export receipts. Marine bunkering service proceeds are freely convertible and usable for any lawful purpose. Banks may request supporting documentation for large transactions or trade financing.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax, producing a combined federal corporate income tax burden of 15.825%. A GmbH is also generally subject to municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); the rate depends on the municipality's Hebesatz and is commonly approximately 7%–17%. The combined effective burden is therefore municipality-specific and commonly approximately 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic withholding rate is 25% plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing a total of 26.375%, before any church tax implications. Treaty relief may reduce the rate. Qualifying EU parent companies may obtain exemption or relief under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and German implementation, subject to ownership, holding-period, beneficial-ownership, anti-abuse, and procedural requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual returns are generally due by the end of the seventh month after the fiscal year-end. Where a tax adviser prepares the return, the generally applicable deadline is the end of the fourteenth month after year-end, subject to statutory exceptions, extensions, and special authority deadlines. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year; adviser-prepared returns are generally due by the end of February of the second following year, subject to statutory adjustments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German transfer-pricing records generally require transaction-specific documentation where cross-border related-party transactions exceed, in aggregate, EUR 6 million for deliveries of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions in a fiscal year. A Master File is generally required where the group's consolidated revenue meets the statutory EUR 100 million threshold. Documentation and disclosure obligations may apply below these thresholds in particular circumstances; records must be produced within the statutory period, generally 30 days after a request under current rules.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a general standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant under German transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership and treaty analysis, anti-hybrid provisions, controlled foreign company rules, the general anti-abuse rule, and the principal-purpose test. A GmbH should maintain genuine decision-making, personnel, premises, operational capability, and records consistent with its functions and risks. For marine bunkering, document procurement, title and risk transfer, fuel quality and delivery, vessel and port logistics, pricing, inventory, credit exposure, and related-party services.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Intentional tax evasion under the Fiscal Code may be punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years, and in especially serious cases by imprisonment of six months to ten years. Negligent tax reduction may be punishable by a fine. Additional tax, interest, and procedural penalties may apply.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal liability for their own conduct and may be liable for unpaid taxes where statutory tax-payment duties are breached. A travel ban is not an automatic tax penalty; criminal procedural measures require a legal basis and competent-authority action.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and undocumented related-party dealings

Statutory sanction: Authorities may adjust taxable income, impose additional tax and interest, and impose documentation-related penalties. Under the Fiscal Code, failure to submit or materially inadequate transfer-pricing documentation can result in a penalty generally ranging from EUR 5,000 to EUR 10 million or a statutory percentage of the relevant income adjustment, subject to applicable statutory conditions and limits.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability for culpable breaches of tax duties and may face criminal exposure if intentional misstatements amount to tax evasion. Corporate status does not shield a director from personal liability for that director's own unlawful conduct.
Unlawful dividend or withholding-tax treatment

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may be assessed for unwithheld tax, interest, and applicable penalties. Intentional withholding-tax evasion may lead to criminal sanctions under tax law.

Director personal liability: A managing director responsible for withholding and remitting tax may be personally liable under statutory tax-liability rules where duties are culpably breached; criminal liability depends on the director's conduct and intent.
Fuel excise, energy tax, customs, and VAT non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The consequences may include back taxes, interest, administrative fines, seizure or confiscation of goods, suspension or loss of authorizations, and criminal penalties for deliberate tax or customs offences. Treatment depends on the fuel, transaction route, customer, vessel use, and applicable authorization.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally responsible for culpable tax, customs, licensing, or reporting violations and may face criminal penalties for their own participation. A corporate veil is not automatically pierced; liability requires a statutory or other recognized legal basis.
AML, sanctions, and suspicious maritime payments

Statutory sanction: Sanctions breaches may attract substantial administrative fines and criminal penalties under applicable German and EU law; AML violations may result in regulatory fines, licensing consequences, and criminal liability where offences are established. Applicable restrictions depend on the parties, vessels, goods, ports, and payment route.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible personnel may face individual administrative or criminal liability for their own acts or culpable compliance failures. Travel restrictions are not automatic and require a specific legal basis.
Shell-company, sham transactions, and abusive treaty or tax structures

Statutory sanction: Authorities may deny treaty or tax benefits, recharacterize transactions, assess tax and interest, and impose penalties. Intentional false filings or concealment may constitute tax evasion or another criminal offence.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for their own participation, tax-duty breaches, or criminal conduct. Limited liability is not a defense to personal wrongdoing; civil veil-piercing is not automatic and depends on applicable legal grounds.

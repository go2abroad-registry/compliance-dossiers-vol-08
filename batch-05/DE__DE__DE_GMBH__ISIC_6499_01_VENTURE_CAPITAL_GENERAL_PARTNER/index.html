Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Partially permissive: GmbHG § 4a requires a registered office (Satzungssitz/Registersitz) in Germany and a domestic business address at which service of process can be effected; a serviced office or virtual business address in a real German street location is accepted for the commercial register and trade registration. However, for a venture-capital general partner seeking BaFin registration/licensing under KAGB §§ 20, 44, the regulator requires genuine administrative substance: dedicated premises in Germany, accessible books and records, qualified local management and compliance/MLRO functions. A pure 'letterbox' or P.O. Box address will be rejected by the Register Court and by BaFin. Therefore the intended VC GP should maintain a physical registered office in a German commercial centre (e.g., Frankfurt, Berlin, Duesseldorf, Munich) with shared or own office infrastructure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU law; no exchange control or prior approval is required for capital transfers, dividends, or profit remittances. Dividend repatriation by a German GmbH is subject to 26.375% withholding tax (including solidarity surcharge), which may be reduced under double tax treaties or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. Cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory conversion rules for export proceeds are in effect under German or EU law. Exporters may hold foreign currency freely, but all inbound/outbound cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank under §67 AWV for balance-of-payments statistics.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank) in conjunction with the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and payment supervision; statutory report obligations under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium viability. German-regulated electronic money institutions (e.g., Solaris SE) may provide payment accounts and IBANs, but a venture capital GP managing third-party investor funds must hold client money in a full credit institution under the German Banking Act (KWG). Offshore non-EEA EMIs are not legally recognised for German payments and cannot lawfully support capital call escrows; using such EMIs would create money laundering and regulatory red flags.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital call contributions should be received via SWIFT MT103 on wires from non-EU investors, or SEPA SCT for EEA amounts. Use AWV purpose code 995 (capital transfers) or 100 (direct investment) depending on the investment relationship. All inbound cross-border wires exceeding EUR 12,500 are subject to electronic notification to Deutsche Bundesbank (Zahlungsstatistik). Verification must include matching the remitter name against a pre-confirmed limited partner list, referencing the capital call number, and obtaining an additional written instruction if funds are sent by a third-party paying agent.For portfolio investments and GP management fees, execute SWIFT MT103 with INTCode (e.g., TRADE, FUNDS) and AWV purpose codes: use code 703 for share purchase in a German target, code 100 for capital disbursement to a foreign affiliate, and code 290 for service fees. For documentary trade transactions, rely on Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) and MT202/MT202 COV for nostro clearing. All outbound payments above EUR 12,500 require AWV statistical reporting, an EU/UN sanctions screening, and clear source-of-funds referencing.Plain vanilla FX forwards for hedging currency exposure arising from capital calls and distributionsCross-currency interest rate swaps for medium-term debt-like instrumentsFX options and currency collars when used strictly against an underlying commercial transactionNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only for non-fully convertible currencies where a demonstrable hedging need exists, subject to EMIR margin rules and BaFin derivative governance requirements

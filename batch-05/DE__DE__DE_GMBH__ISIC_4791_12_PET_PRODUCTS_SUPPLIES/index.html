Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory share capital of a standard GmbH is EUR 25,000. For cash formation, at least EUR 12,500 must be contributed before registration, and each subscribed share must be paid at least to the extent required by the articles and GmbHG. No statutory escrow account is required; a dedicated company bank account is customary, and the managing directors must confirm the availability of the contributions. Contributions in kind require full valuation and special disclosure.

Funding & unlock path: No general civil-defense operating clearance is required for an online retailer. A warehouse or office must comply with applicable building approval, fire-safety, escape-route, occupancy, electrical, workplace, and occupational-health requirements. Fire-protection measures may require review by the local building authority or fire-protection authority, and workplace compliance is supervised by the competent state occupational-safety authority or statutory accident-insurance institution. Structural-load approval is required only where alterations, racking systems, or changed use trigger building-law review.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Foreign or newly formed entities must register with the competent local Finanzamt using the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire), obtain a Steuernummer (tax number) and, for cross-border transactions, apply to BZSt for a USt-IdNr. (VAT ID). Registration must occur before commencing taxable activity. Non-resident companies with permanent establishment or direct VAT liability use the same procedure via the responsible Finanzamt.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For resident GmbH: no turnover threshold – registration mandatory before first taxable supply. For non-resident suppliers of digital services or distance sales to German consumers: OSS threshold of EUR 10,000 for intra-EU supplies; otherwise individual VAT registration is mandatory. Small-entrepreneur exemption under §19 UStG applies only if previous-year turnover ≤ EUR 22,000 and current-year ≤ EUR 50,000 (optional).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods dispatched or transported by the seller to a customer outside the EU; services directly connected with export goods (loading, transport, inspection) are also zero-rated. Documentation must prove that goods left the EU and that the recipient is the customer; failure to satisfy evidentiary rules subjects supply to standard VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with valid Electronic Export System (ATLAS) acceptance
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer evidence showing consideration receipt
  • Commercial invoice and order confirmation
  • Proof of handover to export carrier or forwarding agent

Mandatory B2B domestic e-invoicing from 1 January 2025; invoices must be issued in an interoperable structured format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD). No real-time fiscal clearance but use of Peppol/API transmission networks; buyer consent not required for domestic B2B. e-invoices must be retained in full-content XML with audit trail.

German tax authorities scrutinize zero-rating claims through cross-checks with customs data (ATLAS/ZAPP); missing or insufficient proof causes VAT assessment at 19% plus interest under §233a AO. Deliberate fraud triggers penalties under §370 AO (up to 5 years imprisonment or fine, 10 years for aggravated cases), export document forgery liability, and retrospective clawback for up to 10 years; responsible officers and shareholders may face personal liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a Eurozone member; EUR is fully convertible. Both current and capital account transactions are liberalized under EU and national law. No general exchange control approval is required for inward/outward investment, loan, or profit remittance transactions. Court approval and gatekeeping under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) are NOT applicable to ordinary GmbH operations, except for reporting thresholds and restrictive EU sanctions screening. Profit repatriation via dividend distribution requires compliance with corporate law solvency tests and, if applicable, 25% withholding tax subject to treaty relief or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender, conversion, repatriation, or timeline requirements for export receipts in Germany. Euro is the domestic currency, and German residents may freely hold and dispose of foreign currency proceeds. Cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for external balance-of-payments statistics within 5 days of receipt/payment; the reporting obligation is purely statistical and does not constitute an authorization or conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Central Bank of the Federal Republic of Germany) together with Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) as competent banking supervisor and foreign exchange statistics authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High subject to legal constraints. German-registered or EEA-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under PSD2 and BaFin/FCA-equivalent supervision may be used for e-commerce settlement, FX conversion, and treasury orchestration. UK-based EMIs post-Brexit retain no automatic passport; they require German or EEA-backed licensing. Digital EMIs using virtual IBANs from partner banks must be structured as non-discretionary pass-through accounts backed by safeguarded funds, with clear bankruptcy remoteness, to avoid unlicensed deposit-taking in Germany. Direct integration to Stripe/PayPal is permissible, but the primary treasury account should remain at a Tier 1 German bank for statutory liquidity and sanction-screening reliability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital contributions from foreign shareholders to the German GmbH should be effected via wire transfer to the GmbH's EUR IBAN at the Tier 1 German bank. Transfer protocol: SWIFT MT103 from the foreign corporate banking account, with reference to the capital increase and commercial register filing; the MT103 must contain the legal entity identifier/GLN number (as applicable), the GmbH's full name and address, and a transaction narrative reading 'Gesellschaftereinlage – Stammeinlage GmbH' plus notarial deed reference. Under AWV reporting, the German receiving entity must report the capital receipt to Deutsche Bundesbank if the value exceeds EUR 12,500, using the standard external payments statistics form (Zahlungsverkehrsmeldung, code class 'Direct Investment'). Purpose code: 'K' or the precise capital investment code as instructed by the bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (0.825 percentage points) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer; base rate 3.5% multiplied by municipal Hebesatz, typically 14–17%), yielding an aggregate effective rate of approximately 29.8–33%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax (1.375 percentage points), producing an aggregate statutory dividend withholding rate of 26.375%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

General statutory deadline: 31 July following the assessment year; extended to 14 months after fiscal year-end for electronically-filed returns (e.g., 31 January 2026 for tax year 2024); further extension to 31 May 2027 for tax year 2025. Tax-advisor preparation does not alter the legal deadline without a formal extension order.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory deadlines as the corporate income tax return: 31 January 2026 for fiscal year 2024 (e-filed); generally the last day of the month 14 months after the fiscal year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual VAT return due by 31 July following the calendar year (electronically filed; for 2024 extended to 31 January 2026); advance returns due by the 10th day following each calendar month/quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory numeric de minimis threshold — all cross-border transactions with related parties require contemporaneous documentation. Administrative practice under the Verwaltungsgrundsätze: the German tax authorities will not ordinarily request complete Local File documentation for transactions with related parties where the annual aggregate value is ≤ €500,000 for goods and ≤ €100,000 for services. Master File and Country-by-Country Report are mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of ≥ €750 million; the German entity must file the CbC report via ELSTER if it is the ultimate parent's resident or a designated surrogate parent.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) parallel to the UAE-style regime. Instead, the substance-over-form doctrine and the general anti-abuse rule under § 42 AO apply. A resident GmbH engaged in genuine e-commerce of pet products (ISIC 4791) with a German registered office, real management decisions, employees or contracted staff, physical inventory/logistics, and substantive digital records satisfies the substance expectation. A letterbox company without any business activity in Germany is disregarded for tax purposes, its income re-attributed to the actual foreign beneficiary, and its corporate registration may be administratively dissolved.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Under § 370 AO imprisonment of up to 5 years (up to 10 years in aggravated cases of large-scale evasion); mandatory monetary fine calculated on the evaded tax; automatic assessment of back taxes plus 0.5% monthly interest on the historic underpayment; exclusion from government procurement and tender procedures.

Director personal liability: Managing directors face arrest, travel bans, and confiscation of personal assets for tax debts where they caused the evasion; civil restitution for all taxes, interest, and penalties through piercing the corporate veil under §§ 34, 69, and 71 AO; compliance officers equally liable if complicit.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Under § 162(4) AO the tax office makes an estimated assessment with an adverse-inference surcharge of 5% of the assessed income, minimum €5,000 and maximum €50,000; additional penalty if documentation is not produced within the statutory deadline; the burden of proof reverses to the taxpayer; back-dated recharacterisation of intercompany pricing may lead to hidden profit distribution and additional deemed dividend WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable under § 69 AO for penalties and tax arrears resulting from grossly negligent failure to document intercompany transactions; imprisonment up to 5 years for attempted tax evasion through falsified transfer pricing documentation; professional disqualification as a director.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance on Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: The GmbH as paying agent becomes liable under § 44(5) EStG for the full withholding tax plus monthly interest of 0.5%; civil penalty for failure to withhold up to €50,000; the grossed-up distribution is treated as taxable income for the shareholder; back payment may be collected through compulsory enforcement against the company's assets.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are jointly and severally liable under § 69(2) AO for the withheld tax if the company fails to pay despite having sufficient funds; international travel bans apply for outstanding tax debts above €500; criminal conviction for complicity in dividend WHT evasion carries up to 5 years imprisonment.
Shell Company / Letterbox and General Anti-Abuse Rule (§ 42 AO)

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the entity for tax purposes; income attributed to the actual persons or operations abroad; denial of treaty benefits and full reassessment of VAT on the deemed German supplies; administrative dissolution under §§ 60–61 GmbHG for lack of assets or business; fines up to €500,000 under anti-abuse and market-access regulations.

Director personal liability: Directors face unlimited personal exposure for all unpaid corporate taxes and VAT where the corporate veil is pierced due to abuse; criminal charges of fraudulent bankruptcy or tax evasion; immigration and travel bans in the Schengen area; entry of a personal liability order into the German Central Commercial Register (Schufa/Gewerbeverbot).

Competent corporate registry

Local Court (Amtsgericht) — Commercial Register (Handelsregister), Department B (GmbH register), exercising jurisdiction at the registered office of the Company; for a Munich seat, Amtsgericht München — Registergericht; procedural delivery via the electronic court and administrative mailbox (besonderes elektronisches Anwaltspostfach / XJustiz).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG) — §§ 1 (formation), 2 (notarial form), 5(1) (minimum share capital), 7(2) (minimum paid-in amount), 8 (registration application), 10-11 (entry in Commercial Register and legal personality); German Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch, HGB) §§ 106-108; Notarial Certification Act (Beurkundungsgesetz, BeurkG) §§ 6 ff.; Trade Regulation Act (Gewerbeordnung, GewO) § 14; Value Added Tax Act (UStG) § 13b for construction services reverse charge.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially authenticated Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) together with the electronic application for registration in the Commercial Register (Handelsregisteranmeldung) submitted as XJustiz/XML file by the acting notary.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Statutory minimum nominal share capital (Stammkapital) is EUR 25,000.00 per GmbHG § 5(1); shareholders must subscribe the full capital in the notarial deed, and each cash contribution shall be paid in an amount of at least EUR 1.00 per share denomination; before filing, at least one half of the cash capital (EUR 12,500.00) must be validly paid and placed at the free disposal of the managing directors under GmbHG § 7(2); the contribution is deposited into a company escrow account (CIN deposit account) opened in the future corporate name with a German credit institution or with the notary; the bank issues an unrestricted disposal certificate (Freiverfügungsbestätigung) which the managing directors attest in the registration declaration under GmbHG § 8(2); contributions in kind require an additional audited valuation report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification comprises the fire-protection design document (Brandschutznachweis) prepared by a registered fire-safety planner and approved by the building authority, covering silo tower ventilation, automatic sprinkler or deluge protection, explosion-pressure relief panels, dust-explosion isolation valves, and fire-appliance access roads; structural clearance requires the static load certificate (Standstandsicherheitsnachweis) prepared by a certified structural engineer and independently reviewed by the Prüfstatiker for slip-formed concrete silos under silo-filling pressures, wind loads, seismic action, and thermal gradients; occupational workplace clearance is granted by the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt and BG BAU upon confirmation of compliance with the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz), the Construction Site Regulation (Baustellenverordnung) requiring a safety-and-health plan, and the mandatory occupational medical examinations for work at height, confined silo entry, and under dust-exposure limits.

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum nominal share capital (Stammkapital) is EUR 25,000.00 per GmbHG § 5(1); shareholders must subscribe the full capital in the notarial deed, and each cash contribution shall be paid in an amount of at least EUR 1.00 per share denomination; before filing, at least one half of the cash capital (EUR 12,500.00) must be validly paid and placed at the free disposal of the managing directors under GmbHG § 7(2); the contribution is deposited into a company escrow account (CIN deposit account) opened in the future corporate name with a German credit institution or with the notary; the bank issues an unrestricted disposal certificate (Freiverfügungsbestätigung) which the managing directors attest in the registration declaration under GmbHG § 8(2); contributions in kind require an additional audited valuation report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) and central tax data; locally competent Finanzamt (tax office) for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT assessments. Official portals: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt online services (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a German GmbH or a fixed construction presence must (1) complete the commercial-register filing (Handelsregister) where applicable; (2) submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the responsible Finanzamt via ELSTER or paper, disclosing shareholders, directors, intended business activities, and projected turnover; (3) receive a local Steuernummer for income tax/trade tax/VAT assessments; (4) apply for the USt-IdNr. under §27a UStG through the BZSt (direct electronic application, formerly Form BZSt-1); (5) non-EU foreign enterprises without a German fixed establishment must appoint a VAT fiscal representative (Fiskalvertreter) under §22a UStG unless the BZSt grants a waiver.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A German GmbH is a resident capital company subject to Körperschaftsteuer at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) and Gewerbesteuer (typically 7-17% effective, municipality-dependent); it is not pass-through eligible, has no US-style check-the-box mechanism, and is taxed on worldwide income with treaty-based relief for foreign permanent establishments and anti-hybrid/CFC rules under the Außensteuergesetz.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (a) Organschaft (corporate income tax group and VAT fiscal unity) under §§14-18 KStG and §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, requiring financial integration, a profit-and-loss transfer agreement, and a minimum 5-year term; (b) small-enterprise VAT exemption under §19 UStG (prior-year turnover ≤€25,000 and expected current-year turnover ≤€100,000 for 2025 onward); (c) option to treat a silent partnership interest as a transparent profit-share arrangement; (d) fiscal-year election deviating from the calendar year only with cogent business reasons; (e) application of the special depreciation and low-value asset pooling elections under §6 EStG/§7g EStG; (f) hybrid-financing neutralization elections under §4k EStG and §50g EStG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic micro-enterprise threshold: €25,000 prior-year and €100,000 expected current-year turnover (2025 amendment of §19 UStG). Non-resident foreign enterprises making taxable supplies in Germany are required to register for VAT from the first supply with no de minimis threshold. EU-wide distance-sales OSS threshold: €10,000. Inbound construction work on agricultural grain silos or elevator facilities in Germany triggers mandatory VAT registration regardless of revenue level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under §4 Nr. 1 and §6 UStG applies to supplies of goods physically dispatched from German customs territory to a third-country destination; the exporter must hold an EORI number, file an electronic export declaration through ATLAS/AES, and retain verified proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis). Prefabricated silo components, slip-form machinery, and aeration towers sold as movable goods to non-EU customers qualify. Intra-EU B2B dispatches are zero-rated only with the customer's valid VAT ID and EU recapitulative statement. Services connected to German land — including onsite silo construction, slipforming, and elevator erection — remain taxable at 19% in Germany because the place of supply is the land; offshore services to non-resident business customers may be shifted to the customer's country under the B2B reverse-charge rule.

  • ATLAS/AES customs export declaration and release (Ausfuhranmeldung with master reference number)
  • Customs export confirmation (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument) or electronic customs evidence
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note proving physical dispatch outside the EU

Mandatory E-Rechnung (structured electronic invoice) regime: as of 1 January 2025 every German-established business must be able to receive EN 16931-compliant XML invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X); mandatory issuance begins 1 January 2027 for enterprises with >€800,000 prior-year turnover and 1 January 2028 for all; B2G invoices to German federal authorities must already be submitted as XRechnung/EDI. Germany does not operate an Italian-style real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transactional API system, but XML data integrity and consumer protection rules apply under §14 UStG and GoBD audit requirements.

Export evidence is scrutinized by the Finanzamt and customs authorities (Zollfahndung) under EU UCC and German AO; false or manipulated zero-rating documentation constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable with imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine, and extends the assessment period to 10 years; negligent falsification incurs penalties up to €25,000 under §378 AO. Unsubstantiated zero-rating is clawed back with §233a AO interest (currently 1.8% p.a. for periods after 2018), and VAT shown on invalid invoices triggers liability under §14c UStG. Managing directors and shareholders may face personal and joint liability under §§69 and 73 AO, and large-enterprise audits follow the Federal Central Tax Audit Office (BPrO) multi-year cycles.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible under EU Treaty freedoms of capital and payment movements. German law imposes no general exchange-control licensing for current or capital transactions. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and intercompany loan repayments are freely permitted for resident and non-resident investors, provided the underlying transactions do not trigger EU or national sanctions and are reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics where applicable. Administrative friction is limited to statistical reporting, sector-specific foreign investment screening under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, and standard banking KYC/source-of-funds controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion obligation applies to German exporters. Export receipts may be retained in euro or foreign currency in any bank account. Only statistical reporting is mandatory under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung), generally on a monthly basis or when requested by the Bundesbank, for cross-border receivables or payments exceeding the statistical threshold; there are no mandatory conversion ratios or repatriation timelines.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting jointly with the Federal Ministry of Finance and the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) for prudential and licensing matters; Germany does not maintain a separate foreign exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditionally viable as a supplementary disbursement rail only. A BaFin-licensed e-money institution (EMI) may provide IBAN-accounts and payment execution, but German banks and construction-industry counterparties generally do not accept an EMI as a credible account provider for escrowed EPC milestone proceeds or documentary letter-of-credit operations. Rated Medium for low-value vendor payments and treasury sub-accounts; not viable as primary treasury or capital injection infrastructure.

Turnover: A GmbH is exempt from mandatory audit only if it qualifies as a small corporation under Section 267(1) HGB. Small status is met by meeting at least two of three thresholds: total assets not exceeding EUR 6,000,000, annual net turnover not exceeding EUR 12,000,000, and annual average employees not exceeding 50. Medium and large GmbH (exceeding the small thresholds and meeting at least two of: total assets EUR 20,000,000, annual turnover EUR 40,000,000, or employees 250) are subject to mandatory independent audit under Section 316(1) HGB.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: 500,000 EUR (mandatory local file preparation required for controlled transactions exceeding this threshold)

Economic substance scope: Germany implements EU Economic Substance Directive; adequate local substance required for IP holding, financing, or distribution entities; construction activities generally exempt unless specific holding-company structure applies

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty: imprisonment up to 10 years or fines up to 50,000 EUR per offense; restitution of evaded tax plus statutory interest

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, imprisonment, business disqualification, and joint civil restitution obligations
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties up to 5% of understated tax; potential recharacterization of transactions; back taxation with interest and penalties

Director personal liability: Personal liability for willful misconduct; fines, disqualification from management, and possible criminal exposure
Failure to File or Late Filing

Statutory sanction: Fines up to 5% of tax due per month of delay (minimum 25 EUR); possible criminal investigation and additional surcharges

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for penalties; potential imprisonment for repeated or intentional violations
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as non-existent; denial of tax deductions; penalties up to 5% of annual turnover or specific fines

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil; directors face civil restitution, disqualification, and criminal liability including imprisonment for fraudulent conduct
AML Violations in Cross-Border Transactions

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to 5% of annual turnover; criminal penalties for severe cases

Director personal liability: Personal liability for AML breaches; possible imprisonment and permanent bans from business activities

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Local Finanzamt (competent tax office) for income and VAT assessment; Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT IDs, EU administrative assistance and tax treaty matters; federal and state tax administration via ELSTER portal (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

Domestic GmbH: automatic assignment of a Steuernummer by the local Finanzamt after registration in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) and submission of the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung'. Separate VAT ID (USt-IdNr) application is filed with BZSt, usually by electronic means via ELSTER. Foreign enterprises with taxable supplies in Germany must register with the locally competent Finanzamt under § 18 UStG and may be required to appoint a VAT representative; the BZSt then issues a VAT identification number (DE + 9 digits).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Domestic GmbH: automatic assignment of a Steuernummer by the local Finanzamt after registration in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) and submission of the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung'. Separate VAT ID (USt-IdNr) application is filed with BZSt, usually by electronic means via ELSTER. Foreign enterprises with taxable supplies in Germany must register with the locally competent Finanzamt under § 18 UStG and may be required to appoint a VAT representative; the BZSt then issues a VAT identification number (DE + 9 digits).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under German domestic law; a GmbH is per se a C-corporation taxpayer. Relevant elections include: VAT consolidation (Organschaft) under § 2(2) No. 2 UStG; corporate income tax fiscal unity (Organschaft) with profit and loss pooling under § 14 KStG; small-business VAT exemption election under § 19 UStG; and for hybrid mismatch structures, filing and documentation obligations under § 4k EStG and §§ 14a/15 KStG remain applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration turnover threshold for established German businesses; mandatory registration applies from the first taxable turnover in Germany. Small-business exemption (§ 19 UStG) applies to prior-year turnover of EUR 22,000 or less (EUR 25,000 from 2025) with a projection for the current year not materially exceeding EUR 50,000. Intra-Community distance sales and cross-border digital services to consumers trigger the EU OSS registration threshold at EUR 10,000. Intra-Community acquisition VAT registration is required at EUR 12,500 annual acquisition threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported by the supplier to a destination outside the European Union, or to a member of the EU customs territory under customs warehousing where applicable, are zero-rated under § 6 UStG where the supplier proves exportation to a third-country territory, the recipient is an enterprise, and an export consignment is properly documented. For services, restaurant and catering services are treated as supplied at the place where they are physically performed; thus overseas hotpot dining services lie outside German VAT. Offshore supplies of condiments and goods are zero-rated only where title passes and goods leave the EU; substantiation under §§ 6, 8 UStG and the UStDV is mandatory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Elektronische Ausfuhrbegleitdokument via ATLAS/AES with MRN)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR or other contract-of-carriage evidence
  • Commercial Invoicing (Handelsrechnung) evidencing the overseas buyer and sales terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank settlement evidence for proceeds repatriation

German B2B electronic invoicing mandate in force from 1 January 2025: all domestic B2B invoicing must be exchangeable in a structured e-invoice format, with XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X being the accepted standards. There is no state-run real-time fiscal clearance system, no API stamping mandate, and no so-called certificazione fiscale; transmission occurs directly between parties or via PEPPOL-conformant access points. B2C receipts remain governed by German cash-register 'TSE' requirements (belegertellungs- und Signaturpflicht) under the Tax Register Ordinance (KassenSichV), but not by e-invoicing clearance.

Fraudulent export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable with imprisonment up to ten years and fines; intentional or negligent misdeclaration also triggers interest under § 233a AO, late-payment surcharges, and immediate VAT clawback of the zero-rating benefit on a prospective and retrospective basis. BZSt and customs authorities conduct targeted anti-fraud screening of export declarations and cross-border payment flows; deficient or contradictory export documentation may lead to denial of zero-rating, reassessment, and penalty surcharges. Suspension of VAT refund rights and reversal under the Vorsteuerabzug rules may also be applied against complicit suppliers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the EU Treaty and German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG). No restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation for GmbH entities, provided corporate formalities (e.g., annual financial statements, tax clearance) are met. Non-resident shareholders may freely transfer funds, subject to standard anti-money laundering (AML) and tax reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists in Germany. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or foreign accounts without time limits or conversion ratios. However, all cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical purposes (AWV reporting) within 5 business days of receipt or payment.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and FX oversight; the Federal Ministry of Finance sets policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German and EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as N26, Penta, or Solarisbank can provide multi-currency accounts and cross-border payment services. However, for a restaurant chain with physical POS and merchant acquiring needs, traditional banks offer more robust local acquiring and cash management. EMIs may face limitations on cash deposits and physical branch support. Legal constraints: EMIs must hold an EU passport under PSD2; they are not full credit institutions and cannot offer lending or overdraft facilities. For a GmbH, an EMI can serve as a secondary operational account but not as the primary treasury rail.

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'EQUI' (equity injection) or 'CAPT' (capital transfer). The remitter must include the GmbH's name and account number. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (0.825 percentage points) = 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is imposed separately at an effective rate of approximately 14–17%, producing a combined effective burden of around 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German Kapitalertragsteuer on dividend distributions plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that withholding tax, producing an aggregate statutory dividend withholding rate of 26.375%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

31 July of the calendar year following the tax year. When the return is prepared or assisted by a licensed tax adviser, the general statutory extension runs to 28/29 February of the second year following the tax year, subject to administrative transition orders.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory deadline as the corporate income tax return: 31 July of the following year, with tax-adviser extension to 28/29 February of the second following year.

Foreign shareholders cannot utilise the German GmbH's tax losses against their own foreign taxable income. Late-filing surcharge (Verspätungszuschlag) under § 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed tax for each commenced month of delay, capped at 25% of the assessed tax. Persistent non-filing may trigger coercive fines (Zwangsgeld) up to EUR 25,000. Intentional evasion triggers criminal prosecution under § 370 AO with imprisonment up to five years.
Box 4 (Kz. 80 – Withholding tax credits and DTA relief) Domestic and foreign withholding taxes claimed as credits, refundable amounts, or treaty exemptions on dividends, interest, royalties and other investment income. Attach certificates of tax residence (TRC) and supporting documentation for any DTA reduction or Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief. Ensure that beneficial ownership evidence is included. Late-filing surcharge (Verspätungszuschlag) under § 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed tax for each commenced month of delay, capped at 25% of the assessed tax. Persistent non-filing may trigger coercive fines (Zwangsgeld) up to EUR 25,000. Intentional evasion triggers criminal prosecution under § 370 AO with imprisonment up to five years.
Box 1 (Trade profit base) Gewerbeertrag determined from commercial profit, adjusted for permanent-establishment apportionment and tax-exempt income. A German-resident GmbH is generally subject to full trade tax on its domestic commercial activities. Foreign permanent establishments must be separated and allocated to avoid double taxation. Late-filing surcharge and coercive fines under § 152 AO apply as for the corporate income tax return. Underpaid trade tax attracts interest at 0.5% per month after a 15-month interest-free grace period, plus late-payment penalties under § 240 AO.
Box 2 (Add-backs and deductions) Add-backs for financing costs, rental payments, royalties and certain capitalised items; deductions for qualifying real property and other statutory de-duplications. All related-party financing and royalty payments must meet German interest-limitation (Zinsschranke) and arm's length requirements. Maintain a functional and risk analysis of cross-border payments to support the tax return positions. Late-filing surcharge and coercive fines under § 152 AO apply as for the corporate income tax return. Underpaid trade tax attracts interest at 0.5% per month after a 15-month interest-free grace period, plus late-payment penalties under § 240 AO.
Box 3 (Trade tax assessment and municipal rate) Calculation of the trade tax base (Steuermessbetrag) and application of the municipal factor (Hebesatz). The trade tax burden depends on the municipality where the restaurant is registered. Confirm the applicable Hebesatz with the local Finanzamt; non-resident shareholders have no direct filing obligation for trade tax. Late-filing surcharge and coercive fines under § 152 AO apply as for the corporate income tax return. Underpaid trade tax attracts interest at 0.5% per month after a 15-month interest-free grace period, plus late-payment penalties under § 240 AO.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared when cross-border intercompany tangible-goods transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000 per year or cross-border services/other transactions exceed EUR 500,000 per year. Master File is mandatory when the group's consolidated cross-border-related revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Country-by-Country Report threshold is EUR 750,000,000 group consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply an offshore-style Economic Substance Regulation (ESR). However, § 42 AO (abuse of law), the substance-over-form principle, and the Principal Purpose Test under MLI/DTA require the German GmbH to maintain real, substantive business activity: licensed restaurant premises, proper personnel, independent management decisions, inventory and supply-chain risk control, bank accounts, and financial records. A letter-box company or artificial arrangement may be disregarded for tax purposes, with treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / false tax return (§ 370 AO)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years for ordinary evasion; six months to ten years for particularly serious cases involving large-scale evasion or gross self-interest. Additional statutory interest of 0.5% per month on underpaid tax accrues after the 15-month interest-free period, together with late-payment penalties and fines calculated under the day-unit system of the German Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: The managing director (Geschäftsführer) is personally liable under § 69 AO for corporate tax debts where culpable non-filing or false filing caused the non-payment. Criminal convictions can lead to asset seizure, travel restrictions, a commercial-ban order (Gewerbeverbot), and civil restitution to the tax authority.
Transfer pricing manipulation / hidden profit distribution (§ 1 AStG, § 8 Abs. 3 KStG)

Statutory sanction: The tax authority adjusts the taxable income to the arm's length standard and assesses additional corporate tax and trade tax. A hidden distribution of profits (verdeckte Gewinnausschüttung) triggers 26.375% dividend withholding tax and trade tax add-backs. Incomplete transfer-pricing documentation gives rise to a documentation surcharge of up to 5% of the adjusted income amount (minimum EUR 5,000), plus interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer-pricing certificates or fail to submit required Local File / Master File documentation face personal liability for resulting underpaid corporate tax and trade tax. In serious cases, criminal tax evasion charges can be brought against the directors, and repeated non-compliance can justify disqualification or a travel ban.
Artificial shell / lack of economic substance (§ 42 AO and DTA abuse)

Statutory sanction: The tax authority disregards the artificial arrangement, reassesses tax as if the substance were properly located, denies DTA benefits under the Principal Purpose Test, and may impose compulsory fines up to EUR 25,000 for registration and declaration failures. The entire income may be reallocated to the beneficial owner, with corporate tax, trade tax, and interest recomputed.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for corporate tax debts if the artificial structuring caused non-payment; if fraudulent contracts or sham transactions are executed, criminal liability under the German Criminal Code (including § 264 StGB and § 261 StGB) may arise. Courts may impose travel restrictions, asset freezes, and civil restitution obligations.
AML / cash-skimming / money laundering (GwG and § 261 StGB)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines for AML control failures under § 56 GwG up to EUR 100,000 for natural persons and up to EUR 1,000,000 for legal persons. Money laundering under § 261 StGB carries imprisonment from three months to five years, with six months to ten years for serious cases; proceeds and assets are confiscated.

Director personal liability: The managing director is responsible for implementing AML compliance and cash-handling controls. Culpable failure can lead to personal penalty under § 69 AO, criminal prosecution for money laundering, and, upon conviction, loss of professional capacity, travel bans, and civil claim for restitution by the injured tax authority or creditors.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register) at the Amtsgericht (Local Court) of the district of the registered office; the application is filed electronically by the notary through the federal register portal; publication is made in the Unternehmensregister / elektronischer Bundesanzeiger.

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG), §§ 1–10 and §§ 35–44; key formation provisions are § 5 (minimum capital), § 7 (registration application), § 8 (content of application), and § 10 (legal personality upon register entry); Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB) §§ 106–108 (commercial register filings); notarial certification requirements under the Beurkundungsgesetz and the Federal Notary Code (BNotO); electronic filing governed by the Gesetz über elektronische Handelsregister (EHUG).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified electronic application 'Anmeldung zur Eintragung einer GmbH', accompanied by the Gesellschaftsvertrag (Articles of Association), the Gesellschafterliste (list of shareholders), director appointment evidence, and the bank certificate confirming the minimum capital contribution.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 43 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory minimum capital (Stammkapital) is EUR 25,000 (§ 5 GmbHG). At least EUR 12,500 (50%) in cash must be paid up before registration; a single-member company must pay the full capital. The contributed amount must be at the free disposal of the managing director, evidenced by a bank deposit confirmation (§ 8(2) GmbHG). No independent escrow is required by statute; the capital is deposited on the corporate account opened during formation and must not be repaid to shareholders before registration is completed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) administers national VAT identification numbers and federal tax coordination; local municipal tax office (Finanzamt) administers income, corporate, and trade tax registration. The official electronic filing portal is ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

After incorporating a GmbH, the managing director submits a tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns the statutory tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income, trade tax, and VAT. A separate application is made to BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) used for intra-EU and cross-border transactions. Foreign entities without a domestic branch register for German VAT via the BZSt 'foreign entrepreneur' procedure using its central VAT registration form; a fiscal representative may be required in certain non-EU cases.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — After incorporating a GmbH, the managing director submits a tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns the statutory tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income, trade tax, and VAT. A separate application is made to BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) used for intra-EU and cross-border transactions. Foreign entities without a domestic branch register for German VAT via the BZSt 'foreign entrepreneur' procedure using its central VAT registration form; a fiscal representative may be required in certain non-EU cases.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box pass-through election for a GmbH. The principal statutory election is the Organschaft (fiscal unity) election under Secs. 14–17 of the German Corporate Income Tax Act (KStG), subject to financial, organisational, and economic integration requirements. Trade tax group apportionment elections may be available under Secs. 28–29 GewStG. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are neutralised mandatorily under Sec. 4k of the German Income Tax Act (EStG), supported by documentation obligations; no elective hybrid mismatch carve-out is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For resident small enterprises, the reduced-registration/small-business exemption applies when turnover in the preceding calendar year did not exceed EUR 22,000 and expected current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000 (Sec. 19 UStG). Foreign entrepreneurs performing any taxable supply of goods or services in Germany are compulsorily registrable without a general minimum economic threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for supplies of goods physically exported from Germany to a third-country territory or to a customer in a third country if the exporter retains documentary proof of export (Sec. 4 No. 1, Sec. 6 UStG). For cross-country oil and gas pipeline EPC services, the place-of-supply rule for B2B transactions in Sec. 3a UStG normally places the supply where the customer is established; where that place is outside Germany and outside the EU, the service escapes German VAT but remains subject to reverse-charge or local rules in the customer's jurisdiction. Genuine ancillary services directly related to exported goods may qualify for zero-rating where the statutory preconditions and documentary evidence are fully satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Germany does not currently maintain a real-time central fiscal clearance API or digital stamping system for standard B2B invoices. Mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing applies from 1 January 2025 under Sec. 14 UStG and the E-Invoicing Regulation; invoices must be transmitted electronically in an XML-compliant format such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x. Electronic reporting of cross-border and domestic B2B transaction data to the national tax administration is being phased in from 2027 within the EU ViDA framework. Export transactions are processed through the ATLAS/AES customs declaration system, which transmits export proof data to the tax authority, but no additional fiscal stamping is applied.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to stringent documentary and substantive scrutiny. Missing, false, or inconsistent evidence triggers retrospective VAT reassessment and clawback of input VAT deduction, with 6% annual default interest under Sec. 233a AO. Negligent inaccuracies expose the taxpayer to administrative penalty of up to EUR 30,000 under Sec. 378 AO. Intentional evasion, including fraudulent export evidence, is punishable under Sec. 370 AO with fines and imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years. EU Regulation 904/2010 enables cross-border exchange of VAT information and anti-fraud audits, increasing the likelihood of retrospective discovery and clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both capital and current account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no prior licensing requirements for foreign exchange transactions, and no statutory restrictions on profit repatriation. The Euro is freely convertible within the EU and internationally. Reporting obligations exist solely for statistical and balance-of-payments purposes, and derivative transactions are subject to EMIR, MiFIR, and the German Banking Act (KWG) as applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose mandatory export proceeds surrender, enforced conversion ratios, or compulsory timeframes for the repatriation of export receipts. Export revenue may be retained abroad or brought into Germany freely. Where a resident legal entity receives a payment from non-residents for exported goods/services, the transaction must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank under § 67 AWV if the individual payment exceeds EUR 12,500; the report is due within five business days and must use the prescribed balance-of-payments reporting code.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank; Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz - AWG)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Non-bank Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) authorized under PSD2 by BaFin or an EEA home regulator may provide payment accounts and payment services, but they cannot lawfully provide commercial credits, project escrow services, documentary letters of credit, or standby facilities characteristic of mid-to-large gas pipeline EPC contracts. EMIs are viable only for ancillary operational collections or pre-funded disbursement in low-risk European corridors; they do not satisfy the sovereign project wire and Letter of Credit clearing rail requirements of this scope.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA for EUR-denominated transfers) to the German GmbH's IBAN account. The remittance must clearly state the purpose as 'Einlage in das Stammkapital' (capital subscription) or 'increase of subscribed capital' consistent with the notarized participation agreement. The receiving bank must verify the Commercial Register filing, the notarized directors/management subscription, and the UBO declaration. If any single payment from non-residents exceeds EUR 12,500, the German resident must report to Deutsche Bundesbank under § 67 AWV using the balance-of-payments reporting code for capital contributions (Kapitaltransfer) within five business days. No exchange license is required, and there is no mandatory conversion or approval of exchange control other than statistical reporting.Trade settlements for sub-contractors, materials, and cross-border works are effected through SWIFT MT103 direct transfers, commercial documentary letters of credit (MT700 / UCP 600), and standby letters of credit (MT760) securing performance, advance payment, or retention liabilities. For euro payments within the SEPA zone, clearing is completed via TARGET2. For third-currency disbursements, banks execute conversion at the spot rate subject to their normal KYC/AML controls. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (Körperschaftsteuer 15% plus Solidaritätszuschlag 5.5% of the corporate tax). A municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additionally levied at a standard effective rate of 14% where the local Hebesatz is 400%, producing a combined effective headline burden of approximately 29.8%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% comprising 25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag assessed on the withholding amount. A 0% rate applies under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the German GmbH distributes dividends to an EU/EEA corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the share capital for an uninterrupted period of at least 12 months.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory filing deadline is 31 July following the calendar tax year for electronically filed returns. Where a tax advisor (Steuerberater) prepares the return, the automatic extension applies until 28 February of the second following year. The tax office may additionally grant case-specific extensions.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Identical statutory deadline regime as the corporate income tax return: 31 July following the tax year; extension to 28 February of the second following year when a tax advisor files; monthly surcharges apply thereafter.

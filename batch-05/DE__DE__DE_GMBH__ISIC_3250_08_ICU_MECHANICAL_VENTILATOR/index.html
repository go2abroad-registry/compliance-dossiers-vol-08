Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

German tax administration is shared between the Federal Ministry of Finance and the tax authorities of the Länder. The competent local Finanzamt administers corporate income tax and VAT; the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.). ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) is the official electronic tax filing and tax-registration platform.

E-government filing portal

Register the GmbH with the competent Finanzamt and submit the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung, normally electronically through ELSTER, to obtain its tax number (Steuernummer). Apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number after or alongside tax registration; the BZSt may require the Finanzamt tax number and evidence of business activity. A foreign entity without a German establishment must register with the Finanzamt designated for its country of residence under the BZSt allocation rules; the responsible office should be confirmed against the current BZSt list. VAT registration is generally required before making taxable German supplies where no applicable exemption or reverse-charge treatment removes the obligation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is a separate corporate taxpayer and has no general US-style check-the-box election to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity. German law provides no general elective hybrid classification that overrides the GmbH's corporate tax status. A qualifying small business may apply the VAT small-business regime (Kleinunternehmerregelung); this is a VAT treatment, not an income-tax classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general domestic VAT registration threshold applies to a GmbH making taxable supplies. The Kleinunternehmer VAT exemption limits are €25,000 of total turnover in the preceding calendar year and €100,000 in the current calendar year; these are exemption limits, not general registration thresholds. Foreign taxable persons generally cannot rely on a domestic registration threshold. The EU-wide €10,000 threshold applies only to specified cross-border intra-EU distance sales and telecommunications, broadcasting, and electronically supplied services, subject to the applicable rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the EU may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax deduction under the German VAT Act where the statutory export requirements are met and the supplier retains prescribed proof of export. Intra-EU supplies may qualify for exemption subject to the applicable conditions, including customer VAT identification and reporting requirements. Services are not zero-rated merely because the customer or payment is offshore: the place-of-supply rules determine whether German VAT applies, and an applicable exemption or reverse charge must be established. No general foreign-exchange repatriation condition applies.

  • German or EU export declaration and customs exit confirmation, including the MRN and electronic exit results where applicable, as prescribed proof of export
  • Commercial invoice identifying the goods, parties, consideration, and export transaction
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the transaction, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or carrier confirmation
  • Evidence of the buyer's destination outside the EU and records linking the goods, invoice, and customs declaration
  • Inward remittance certificate: not a general German VAT zero-rating requirement; retain payment evidence where relevant to substantiate the transaction

Germany has no general real-time VAT clearance, fiscal-stamping, or transaction-reporting API mandate. Since 1 January 2025, businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions. Issuance is subject to transitional rules: generally, paper or other non-compliant formats may continue with the recipient's consent during 2025 and 2026; qualifying businesses may use the transition through 2027, and businesses with prior-year turnover not exceeding €800,000 may use it through 2028. Thereafter, domestic B2B invoices generally must comply with the statutory structured e-invoice requirements, subject to exceptions.

The supplier must substantiate the exemption with records meeting German statutory proof and bookkeeping requirements. If export or other exemption conditions are not proven, the Finanzamt may assess German VAT retrospectively, deny related input-tax treatment where applicable, and charge statutory late-payment interest. Intentional or reckless false claims may result in tax penalties or criminal liability for tax evasion under the German Fiscal Code, in addition to tax recovery and applicable surcharges. Germany imposes no general 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany guarantees full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the euro in accordance with EU free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU) and the liberalisation principle set out in Sections 1 and 2 of the AWG. There are no exchange-control restrictions on dividend remittances, loan repayments, equity repatriation, or royalties for a GmbH resident in Germany. The sole operational hurdle is the Bundesbank's statistical reporting system under AWV Sections 67 to 70: any cross-border payment received or made by a German resident exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank within five business days via the Allgemeines Meldeportal (ex-post reporting), including the counterparty name, country, ISIC-linked purpose code, and value date. Profit remittance is unrestricted and subject only to final withholding tax at 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (reduced under applicable double tax treaties) which the paying bank remits directly to the Federal Central Tax Office (BZSt) via the Kreditor system. Germany does not maintain parallel or dual exchange rates, and there is no capital gains or minimum holding restriction on the conversion of EUR into USD, CNY, or any freely convertible currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain, invoice, and hold 100% of export proceeds in any currency of choice. The only binding statutory obligation is the AWV Section 67 cross-border payment reporting duty: export proceeds credited to a German-resident account in excess of EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank by the resident beneficiary within five business days, with the purpose code identifying merchandise trade (Warenlieferung) and the business scope ISIC 3250. Where export proceeds are credited to a non-resident account in the name of the German GmbH, the domestic entity remains the reporting party. No obtainable licence or central bank approval is required to initiate or receive export-related payments.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main), acting in coordination with the Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) and the Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Klimaschutz (BMWK) under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG), the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV), and the Bundesbank Act (BBankG). The German foreign exchange administration regime is liberal; no ministry prior approval is required for cross-border payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW as the primary treasury and clearing rail. BaFin-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Solaris SE and EU-passported EMIs operating in Germany are lawful under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) to issue e-money, execute SEPA and SEPA Instant payments, and hold segregated safeguarding accounts. However, they are not licensed as full credit institutions under Section 32 KWG and therefore cannot: (i) originate commercial loans or overdraft facilities; (ii) open, advise, or settle documentary letters of credit required for sovereign ICU ventilator tenders; (iii) provide the mandatory certificate of capital contribution (Einlagenzahlung) for GmbH capital escrow; or (iv) execute high-value SWIFT MT103s in USD/CNY with a direct Bundesbank relationship; EMIs rely on sponsor banks, adding liquidity-clearing friction. Recommended posture: retain a BaFin-authorised fintech EMI solely as a secondary collections rail for small domestic or EU invoice receivables; route all government LC proceeds, supplier settlements, and equity injections exclusively through a tier-1 German commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

International equity and shareholder-loan injections into the German GmbH must be executed exclusively over the SWIFT network in compliance with the Payment Accounts Directive and the AWV reporting regime. Equity capital injection: remit via SWIFT MT103 as SEPA or SWIFT wire with purpose code 'GEC' referencing 'Gesellschaftereinlage nach § 5 GmbHG' and quote the notarised share issuance document number; the funds are initially credited to the German Capital Escrow Account (Sperrkonto) physically segregated at the onboarding bank where the notary provides a written confirmation to the commercial register (registergericht) of the paid-up minimum capital of EUR 25,000. Shareholder loans exceeding EUR 250,000 require Bundesbank statistical reporting under the AWV as a cross-border loan (purpose code Z5), no authorisation is required, but the loan agreement must be in writing, at arm's length, and recorded in the Bundesbank AM Portal within the statutory reporting window. For inbound USD or CNY funds, the receiving German bank automatically converts into EUR on credit or maintains a dual-currency account; AHV-only no. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: LOW as the primary treasury and clearing rail. BaFin-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Solaris SE and EU-passported EMIs operating in Germany are lawful under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) to issue e-money, execute SEPA and SEPA Instant payments, and hold segregated safeguarding accounts. However, they are not licensed as full credit institutions under Section 32 KWG and therefore cannot: (i) originate commercial loans or overdraft facilities; (ii) open, advise, or settle documentary letters of credit required for sovereign ICU ventilator tenders; (iii) provide the mandatory certificate of capital contribution (Einlagenzahlung) for GmbH capital escrow; or (iv) execute high-value SWIFT MT103s in USD/CNY with a direct Bundesbank relationship; EMIs rely on sponsor banks, adding liquidity-clearing friction. Recommended posture: retain a BaFin-authorised fintech EMI solely as a secondary collections rail for small domestic or EU invoice receivables; route all government LC proceeds, supplier settlements, and equity injections exclusively through a tier-1 German commercial bank.

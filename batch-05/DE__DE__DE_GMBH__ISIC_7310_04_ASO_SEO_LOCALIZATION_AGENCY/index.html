Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Department (Handelsregisterabteilung) of the locally competent District Court (Amtsgericht), filed electronically through the notary and accessible via the common register portal (www.handelsregister.de / www.unternehmensregister.de).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG), in particular §§ 1, 2 (notarial form), 3 (articles of association), 5 (minimum share capital), 7 (application for registration), 8 (contents of application), 10 (legal effect of registration), 35 ff. (management and representation); complemented by §§ 8-16 German Commercial Code (HGB) on the Commercial Register and relevant trade registration provisions of the Trade, Commerce and Industry Regulation Act (GewO).

Formation / constitutional filings

Handelsregisteranmeldung — the notarized application for registration of a GmbH in the Commercial Register, including the Articles of Association, shareholder list and managing director particulars.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 33 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory minimum share capital is EUR 25,000. At least EUR 12,500 (50%) of each original cash contribution must be paid up in cash before the Commercial Register application is filed and must remain at the free disposal of the managing director. No state escrow account is required for cash capital; capital-in-kind contributions require a full in-kind contribution report and valuation in the articles.

Funding & unlock path: For standard office premises no separate structural-load certificate is required; fire safety must satisfy the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung), including emergency exits, fire extinguishers and escape-route signage, as inspected by the local building authority or fire service. As an employer, the GmbH must register each employee with the statutory accident insurer (Berufsgenossenschaft) and comply with occupational safety audits under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (ArbSchG).

Paid-in capital rule: The statutory minimum share capital is EUR 25,000. No state escrow account is required for cash capital; capital-in-kind contributions require a full in-kind contribution report and valuation in the articles.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

German Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT Identification Number assignment; competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for ongoing tax registration and administration. Official filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

After notarised formation and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), the GmbH must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the competent local Finanzamt, either electronically via ELSTER or on paper. The Finanzamt issues the local tax number (Steuernummer); the BZSt subsequently issues the VAT Identification Number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) for intra-Community and export transactions. A foreign parent entity must additionally provide apostilled/certified corporate documents, register its beneficial owner under the Transparency Register (Transparenzregister), and file a VAT ID application with the BZSt if cross-border services are supplied.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — After notarised formation and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), the GmbH must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the competent local Finanzamt, either electronically via ELSTER or on paper. The Finanzamt issues the local tax number (Steuernummer); the BZSt subsequently issues the VAT Identification Number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) for intra-Community and export transactions. A foreign parent entity must additionally provide apostilled/certified corporate documents, register its beneficial owner under the Transparency Register (Transparenzregister), and file a VAT ID application with the BZSt if cross-border services are supplied.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a US-style check-the-box regime. A GmbH is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) on its worldwide taxable income subject to territorial limitations. Available elections include: (i) corporate tax and trade tax fiscal unity ('Organschaft') under Sec. 14 KStG with a profit and loss transfer agreement; (ii) VAT fiscal unity ('Organschaft') under Sec. 2 Abs. 2 UStG; (iii) option to apply regular VAT where small-business rules would otherwise apply (not commonly available to a GmbH); and (iv) hybrid mismatch elections are limited by ATAD implementation (Sec. 4k EStG).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Resident GmbH: no economic turnover threshold – VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply. Non-resident suppliers of digital services or distance sales of goods: EUR 10,000 per annum (unless the EU One-Stop-Shop / Import One-Stop-Shop is used); a German GmbH is not eligible for the EUR 22,000 small-business exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods exported to a third country (non-EU), the supply is zero-rated under Sec. 6 UStG, provided the goods are physically exported to a destination outside the EU and the export is evidenced by an authorised customs declaration and transportation proof. For services, including app store optimisation, SEO, and multilingual localization services, German VAT is either zero-rated or out of scope if the customer is a taxable person (B2B) established outside Germany – the place of supply is the customer’s country. For B2C digital services to consumers outside the EU, the place of supply is the consumer’s country of residence provided effective use and enjoyment lies outside Germany. Intra-Community supplies of services to EU businesses are generally reverse-charged to the recipient after validation of its VAT ID.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) / electronic ECS record
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / FX repatriation proof
  • Proof of non-EU destination (delivery note, port registry, third-country customs stamp)
  • For services: signed contract, detailed service performance report, customer EU VAT ID or non-EU reality evidence, and bank payment record

No real-time fiscal clearance, fiscal stamping, or API pre-clearance is currently deployed. A phased mandatory e-invoicing regime for domestic B2B supplies took effect on 1 January 2025, requiring issuers and recipients to exchange structured electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.3.1 format). B2C and cross-border B2B invoices may remain in PDF or paper until EU ViDA transaction-based reporting is transposed. VAT reports, advance VAT returns (Vorsteueranmeldungen), and summaries are filed electronically via ELSTER.

Fraudulent or improperly substantiated export zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT clawback with interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum), an extended assessment period of up to 10 years, and reversal of the zero rate. If intent or gross negligence is established, criminal tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under Sec. 369/370 AO applies with penalties of up to €1,000,000 per offence and imprisonment of up to 10 years; negligent underdeclaration penalties may reach 40% of the underdeclared amount. The Tax Office may disallow input VAT deduction, revoke the VAT ID, and hold the managing director personally liable for recovered VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies no general forex control regime; the Euro is full legal tender. EU free movement of capital (TFEU Art. 63) permits unrestricted conversion and transfers to and from third countries, subject only to EU sanctions screening and the AWV notification obligations. Capital and current account transactions are fully convertible for all business operations, including trade receivables, royalties, and profit repatriation. No approval is required for an advertising agency; cross-border remittances above EUR 12,500 trigger only statistical reporting to the Bundesbank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts or foreign currency earnings. German resident GmbHs may retain all export proceeds freely. Only a statistical obligation arises under AWV Section 67: each cross-border payment (including export proceeds) in excess of EUR 12,500 must be reported by the domestic bank or the resident entity to the Deutsche Bundesbank, typically via the integrated system (plus or the electronic reporting portal).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Central Bank of the Federal Republic of Germany) as national competent authority for the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation) statistical reporting and cross-border financial monitoring, operating within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High for cross-border payment routing, supplier disbursements, and collection of client fees (e.g., Wise B2B, Payoneer) where the provider holds an EU Electronic Money Institution (EMI) license or BaaS partnership with a German bank. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot hold statutory share capital escrow accounts or serve as the sole bank for the GmbH’s capital formation and Commercial Register filing; they lack full banking licenses for taking deposits as defined under the German Banking Act (KWG). Thus the EMI must be used as a secondary liquidity rail, with a primary German bank account for statutory audits, tax remittances, and notary verification.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (including solidarity surcharge on corporate income tax; excluding trade tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 26.375%) on dividend distributions made by a German GmbH to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced under a tax treaty (DTA). For EU/EEA qualifying corporate shareholders: 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if holding at least 10% for 12 months. For DTA holders: treaty rates typically 5% for substantial shareholdings (≥10%) and 15% for portfolio holdings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 aggregate cross-border transactions with related parties for Local File; Master File required when group turnover exceeds EUR 50 million. Documentation must be contemporaneous, prepared in English or German, and include functional analysis, benchmarking, and pricing strategic indicators. For intercompany ASO/SEO localization services, the entity should be remunerated as a functional entrepreneur with allocation of risks; a low-risk agency would earn a routine cost-plus margin based on subsidiary-level cost base.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies a substantive economic substance analysis in the context of § 42 AO and the OECD BEPS Action Plan. Though there is no standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR), a GmbH without real German substance — no local office, no decision-making management, no employees, no substantive risks — is vulnerable to being treated as a sham/agency (Strohmann-GmbH) and its income reallocated to the beneficial owner or principal under § 42 AO and German case law. A non-resident owner must demonstrate that the GmbH is not a mere Formality but has actual business activity, operational management, and appropriate capital. In the context of ASO/SEO localization agency, personnel, contracts, IT assets, and marketing functions must be locally present.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) / False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine; for especially serious cases (large-scale evasion) imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years. Additional monetary penalty up to the amount of tax evaded. The tax assessment can be reopened despite finality.

Director personal liability: A managing director who signs a false return is personally liable for criminal penalties. The corporate veil is pierced: directors and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and restitution. Travel bans and prevention of exit under § 326 AO (security arrest) are available.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency (Strohmanngesellschaft)

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may disregard the legal entity and reattribute income to the non-resident entrepreneur. Imposed default taxes: fully assessed income plus solidarity surcharge and trade tax. Fines for false corporate registration under commercial register law. In severe cases, criminal prosecution for falsification of shareholders' resolutions.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the GmbH's tax debts if they violated their duty to maintain proper books and statutory accounts (§ 41a GmbHG). Non-resident ultimate beneficial owners are also exposed to personal taxation of deemed repatriated profits; prohibitions on leaving Germany (travel ban) and arrest in enforcement of tax claims; disqualification from corporate directorship (up to 5 years).
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (Verrechnungspreisdokumentation)

Statutory sanction: Penaul: penalty up to 5% of the adjusted transfer price differential, minimum EUR 100 and maximum 25% of the adjusted (increased) income. Reversal of burden of proof; tax authorities may estimate income and impose accuracy-related penalties. Interest on additional taxes 0.5% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign TP documentation knowing it is false are exposed to tax evasion charges; personal liability for the penalty amount up to EUR 100,000 under § 162 AO (forfeit of audit rights); criminal prosecution for attempted evasion; shareholder loans and capital withdrawal may be reclassified as hidden profit distributions leading to 30% trade tax addback and 25% withholding tax on deemed dividends.
AML / Money Laundering (Geldwäschegesetz) Misuse

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty up to 5 years imprisonment (§ 261 StGB); confiscation of funds; fines up to EUR 1 million for violation under § 56 GwG; company may be blacklisted from bank transfers; administrative fines for non-transparent beneficial ownership registration up to EUR 5 million in a group context.

Director personal liability: Directors as 'persons responsible for AML compliance' are personally criminally liable for omissions; the corporate veil is pierced where the company is a conduit for illicit proceeds; personal travel bans and asset freezing orders may be imposed; loss of commercial register status leads to loss of limited liability shield (Haftungsdurchgriff).
Unlawful Profit Repatriation / Hidden Profit Distribution (Verdeckte Gewinnausschüttung)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of payment as a dividend; 25% + 5.5% soli withholding tax is imposed on the hidden distribution; corporation loses the deduction for the underlying expense; trade tax addback 30% of the payment; interest and late surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors approving such payments are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax under § 44 EStG; they face imprisonment for withholding tax evasion; shareholders who knowingly accept the payment may be jointly liable for tax restitution; enforcement through arrest and travel ban for unpaid tax debts under § 326 AO.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) - Federal Central Tax Office, responsible for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr) and federal tax matters; local tax office (Finanzamt) competent for corporation tax, trade tax, and VAT administration; electronic filing and communication portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH, the permanent tax number (Steuernummer) is issued by the competent local Finanzamt after incorporation and registration in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). The foreign parent or non-resident entity must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the local Finanzamt, and separately apply to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr) using Form USt 1 TU. The captive reinsurance SPV must also evidence its statutory licence under Versicherungsaufsichtsgesetz (VAG).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - A GmbH is a body corporate subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and solidarity surcharge; no pass-through or US-style check-the-box classification is available in Germany. — For a German GmbH, the permanent tax number (Steuernummer) is issued by the competent local Finanzamt after incorporation and registration in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A GmbH is a body corporate subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and solidarity surcharge; no pass-through or US-style check-the-box classification is available in Germany.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box election. A captive reinsurance GmbH may elect an Organschaft (corporate fiscal unity) under Sec. 14 KStG and VAT fiscal unity under Sec. 2(2) no. 2 UStG, conclude a profit and loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag), and apply the ATAD hybrid mismatch alignments under Sec. 4j EStG to neutralise double-deduction and deduction-without-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business exemption under Sec. 19 UStG applies at EUR 25,000 annual turnover from 1 January 2025 (EUR 22,000 in 2024), but exempt insurance and reinsurance supplies do not count toward taxable turnover; mandatory VAT registration applies to any non-resident supplier making taxable domestic supplies without a German establishment; intra-EU distance-selling registration threshold is EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of goods qualify for 0% VAT under Sec. 6 UStG when the goods physically leave the German customs territory and the exporter holds the prescribed export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) under Sec. 13 UStG; services strictly connected to exported goods may be zero-rated under Sec. 6(1) UStG. Captive reinsurance supplies to non-resident group insurers are VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated under Sec. 4 no. 10 UStG and carry no input VAT recovery; non-resident insurers may claim input VAT refunds under Sec. 4a UStG (EU insurers) or Sec. 18(9) UStG (third-country insurers) subject to reciprocity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the EU Capital Movement Directive (Article 63 TFEU) and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG). No general restrictions on profit remittance, dividends, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders. However, the German Minimum Taxation Act (Mindeststeuergesetz) and the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) may impose substance and economic activity requirements. For captive reinsurance SPVs, the Bundesbank requires statistical reporting (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik) for cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 per month, but no prior approval is needed. Profit remittance is subject to standard corporate income tax (15%) plus solidarity surcharge (5.5% of CIT) and trade tax (average 14-17%), with no withholding tax on dividends if the parent is EU/EEA resident and holds at least 10% (under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. The AWG and the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (AWV) require that all cross-border payments and receipts be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes if they exceed EUR 12,500 per calendar month. For captive reinsurance, premium income from foreign cedants is treated as service income and is freely convertible. No mandatory conversion ratio applies. However, the entity must maintain proper documentation for tax and regulatory audits, including proof of the underlying reinsurance contract and the commercial purpose of each transaction.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and insurance supervision; the Federal Ministry of Finance for policy oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low to Medium. German BaFin-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) can provide multi-currency accounts and cross-border payment services, but they are not licensed to hold insurance reserves or act as custodians for regulatory capital. For a captive reinsurance SPV, the use of an EMI is permissible for operational liquidity (e.g., premium collections and claims payments) but not for statutory solvency capital. Legal constraints: EMIs must be licensed under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and are subject to the same AML/KYC obligations as banks. However, they lack the balance sheet strength and regulatory recognition for insurance-related escrow arrangements. A hybrid structure (Tier-1 bank for capital, EMI for operational FX) is viable but requires careful contractual segregation.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

High-risk jurisdiction exposure: If the parent company or reinsurance counterparties are domiciled in a jurisdiction on the FATF grey list or with high corruption indices, the bank may flag the account for enhanced monitoring.

Defense evidence: Provide a detailed group structure chart, audited financial statements of the parent, a copy of the reinsurance contract with clear commercial rationale, and a legal opinion from a German law firm confirming the economic substance and regulatory licensing of the SPV. Also include a risk assessment report prepared under the GwG, demonstrating that the entity has implemented adequate internal controls.
Unusual transaction patterns: Large premium payments from a single cedent or rapid in-and-out flows without clear underwriting logic may trigger a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR).

Defense evidence: Maintain a comprehensive underwriting file for each reinsurance treaty, including actuarial valuations, loss reserve calculations, and board minutes approving the transaction. Defense evidence: Provide a copy of the BaFin license or a formal exemption letter, along with the solvency and financial condition report (SFCR) if applicable. If the SPV is a pure captive, provide a letter from the parent confirming the captive's purpose and the group's internal risk management framework.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Inbound capital injections from the parent company (e.g., share capital or subordinated loans) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with a clear purpose code. For Germany, the Bundesbank's statistical reporting code (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik) requires the use of the 'Vermögensübertragung' (capital transfer) category. The wire must include the following details: (1) Beneficiary: [GmbH name] with IBAN and BIC; (2) Purpose: 'Capital contribution under § 5 GmbHG' or 'Subordinated loan agreement dated [date]'; (3) Reference: The contract number or board resolution number. For regulatory capital, the injection must be documented in the commercial register and the bank must receive a notarized copy of the capital increase resolution. For loans, a signed loan agreement with a market-rate interest and a repayment schedule is required. The bank will verify the source of funds via the parent's bank statement and a declaration of the source of wealth.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For premium payments to foreign reinsurers or claims payments to cedents, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) for non-urgent payments, and MT202 for interbank transfers. For large or recurring payments, a commercial letter of credit (LC) may be used, especially if the counterparty requires a guarantee. The SPV should maintain a dedicated operational account with a Tier-1 bank that offers direct SWIFT connectivity. For payments within the EU, the SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) can be used for amounts up to EUR 100,000, but for cross-border non-EU payments, SWIFT is mandatory. The bank will require the underlying contract (reinsurance treaty) and an invoice or claims statement to process the payment. For FX conversion, the bank will execute the trade at the prevailing interbank rate plus a margin, or the SPV can use a separate FX forward contract.Outright FX Forwards (deliverable) with a maturity of up to 12 months, executed with a licensed German bank or a non-resident bank under the ISDA Master Agreement.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for currencies with limited convertibility (e.g., certain emerging market currencies) where the SPV has exposure to reinsurance liabilities in those currencies.Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) to manage long-term currency mismatches between premium income (e.g., USD) and claims liabilities (e.g., EUR), subject to the bank's credit approval and the SPV's risk management policy.FX Options (plain vanilla) for hedging contingent exposures, but these are less common for captive reinsurers due to cost and complexity.

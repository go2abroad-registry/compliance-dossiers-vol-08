Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
The competent authority is the locally responsible German tax office (Finanzamt) for tax registration and corporate income tax administration. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) and administers the BZSt VAT identification services. Electronic tax filings and registrations are generally submitted through the ELSTER portal.

        
E-government filing portal
A German GmbH must file the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER with the competent Finanzamt after formation and commencement of activity. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), which is used for German tax filings. The company should separately apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), particularly for intra-EU transactions. A foreign entity without a German establishment must identify the competent Finanzamt under the non-resident taxpayer allocation rules, file the relevant questionnaire, provide constitutional documents, commercial-register evidence, business descriptions, shareholder and director information, bank and payment-processor information, contracts, invoices, and evidence of German taxable transactions. VAT registration may also be required in Germany where the entity makes German taxable supplies, holds stock, operates a fulfillment arrangement, or otherwise creates a German VAT liability. OSS registration may be used for eligible intra-EU B2C distance sales, but it does not replace German registration where German domestic VAT obligations remain.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A German GmbH must file the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER with the competent Finanzamt after formation and commencement of activity. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), which is used for German tax filings. OSS registration may be used for eligible intra-EU B2C distance sales, but it does not replace German registration where German domestic VAT obligations remain.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
A GmbH is treated as a separate taxable corporation for German corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, trade tax, and VAT purposes. Germany does not provide a United States-style check-the-box election for a GmbH to elect transparent or disregarded treatment. German tax grouping (Organschaft) may apply where statutory financial, economic, and organizational integration requirements are satisfied; it is not an elective entity classification and requires formal implementation, including an accepted profit-and-loss transfer agreement for corporate tax purposes. A small-business VAT exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) may be applied if the statutory turnover limits are satisfied; the company may waive the exemption and opt into ordinary VAT treatment, subject to the statutory binding period. The German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive implement legislation-based restrictions and do not provide a general taxpayer election to neutralize a mismatch.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
19%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
For ordinary VAT registration, there is generally no broad turnover threshold that permits a business making taxable German supplies to remain unregistered. The small-business exemption may apply where total turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year, subject to the statutory rules applicable from 2025 and transitional interpretation. The exemption is not available for all transactions and may be unavailable or unsuitable where the business needs input VAT recovery. For EU cross-border B2C distance sales, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies collectively to qualifying intra-EU distance sales and certain electronically supplied services where the supplier is established in only one EU Member State; OSS can generally be used above that threshold.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify as VAT-exempt exports with input VAT recovery if the statutory export conditions are satisfied and the supplier maintains prescribed documentary or alternative evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory. The supplier must generally be the exporter or arrange the export, and the goods must be supplied to a customer established outside the German VAT territory or otherwise meet the relevant export-exemption requirements. For intra-EU B2B supplies of goods, the applicable treatment is generally VAT exemption for an intra-Community supply rather than an export zero rate, subject to valid customer VAT identification, transport evidence, recapitulative reporting, and other requirements. Services supplied to business customers outside Germany may be outside the scope of German VAT under place-of-supply rules, commonly with customer self-assessment or reverse charge in the customer jurisdiction; this is not technically a German zero-rated export. B2C services and electronically supplied services require separate place-of-supply analysis. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, proof of taxable business use, and compliance with filing and recordkeeping requirements.

Germany does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance or digital-stamping system. Since 1 January 2025, German businesses must generally be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions, subject to statutory exceptions and transition rules. Mandatory issuance of structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B supplies is phased in: generally from 2027 for larger businesses and from 2028 for all businesses, subject to the applicable turnover and transition conditions. Accepted structured formats include legally compliant formats such as XRechnung and ZUGFeRD. PDF invoices alone are not structured electronic invoices. The business must preserve invoices and relevant transaction data in accordance with German accounting and tax-record retention requirements. Shopify, PayPal, Stripe, and other payment records should be reconciled to VAT invoices, credit notes, refunds, chargebacks, marketplace reports, and OSS or domestic VAT returns.

      
Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption may be denied or retrospectively clawed back if the supplier cannot prove export, the goods did not leave the EU, the transaction was mischaracterized, invoices or customs records are defective, or the supplier participated in fraud or knew or should have known of fraudulent conduct. The Finanzamt may assess underpaid VAT, statutory late-payment interest, penalties for tax offences or administrative violations, and civil or criminal consequences for intentional tax evasion. Input VAT refunds may be reduced or recovered with interest where eligibility, business use, or invoice requirements are not met. Customs authorities may impose customs debt, import VAT consequences, administrative fines, and, in serious cases, criminal sanctions for false declarations, undervaluation, or misuse of export evidence. Records should be retained for the applicable German tax and commercial-law periods, with longer retention periods applying to specified accounting and tax records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Germany is a fully liberalised Eurozone jurisdiction. The euro is freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions pursuant to Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). No prior exchange-control approval, exchange licence or central-bank allocation is required for a German GmbH to receive inbound equity, disburse outbound trade payments, or remit dividends to non-resident shareholders. Legal restrictions are limited to sanctions compliance (EU/UN embargoes), suspicious-transaction reporting under the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG), and mandatory balance-of-payments statistics reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank. Profit remittance and dividend distribution are lawful provided the GmbH complies with corporate formality (shareholder resolution, proof of solvency and the restriction that a capital-eroding distribution is prohibited under GmbH law (§30 GmbHG)) and reportable payment thresholds are met.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
There are no statutory rules requiring the surrender, forced sale or conversion of export proceeds. A German exporter may freely hold invoiced foreign currency receipts in foreign-currency accounts at any EEA-licensed credit institution. The only binding obligation is statistical: under §67 AWV, every German resident must report to the Deutsche Bundesbank all cross-border payments received or made during a calendar month where the aggregate against a single non-resident counterparty reaches EUR 12,500 or more. Additionally, cross-border direct investments, shareholder loans and other external assets/liabilities must be reported to the Bundesbank where the amount exceeds EUR 1,000,000 (or where portfolio investment thresholds apply). Non-compliance triggers administrative fines under the AWG, although it does not block currency conversion.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany), acting jointly with the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and payment-institution supervision and with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) as the competent authority for foreign trade regulation under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG) and the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Stripe
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High only as ancillary treasury rails; a non-bank fintech or offshore electronic-money institution (EMI) cannot replace the Tier-1 German account. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        For a fully compliant inbound equity injection to the German GmbH treasury, the clearing structure is as follows: inbound EUR transfers from EU/EEA shareholders must be executed as SEPA Credit Transfers (SEPA CT) through TARGET2 in real-time or same-day mode; inbound transfers from non-EEA jurisdictions must be sent via SWIFT MT103 to the GmbH's Tier-1 German IBAN/BIC. The remittance information (Verwendungszweck) must unambiguously state 'GESELLSCHAFTSEINLAGE' for share capital or 'DARLEHEN' for shareholder loans, the date of the shareholder resolution, and the notary file number. Applicable ISO 20022 purpose code: 'EQUI' for equity subscriptions or 'INTC' for intercompany shareholder loans; the AWV statistics classification must be coded as direct investment ('Direktinvestitionen') or external borrowing, respectively, in the Deutsche Bundesbank report. The Deutsche Bundesbank reporting threshold of EUR 12,500 monthly applies to each non-resident counterparty, while cross-border loan and direct-investment in-voice positions equal to or exceeding EUR 1,000,000 must also be filed separately. Identity and evidence chain: proof of source of funds, the notarised capital subscription agreement, bank debit advice from the shareholder, and the German notary's confirmation of the availability of the capital on the escrow account are required for registration. If the capital injection arrives in non-EUR currency, the conversion must occur at market rate at the accepting German bank or via a executed FX forward before final credit into the GmbH's blocked capital account; the exchange confirmation and the AWV code for conversion must be retained.Outbound settlements for DTC-brand trade (inventory, packaging, freight, advertising and logistic services) shall run on four clearing rails: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for EU counterparties and platforms (Shopify EU entities, Stripe, PayPal EU) via TARGET2, with immediate confirmation; (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR/non-EEA payments to suppliers and affiliates, setting in field 70 the purchase order and invoice reference plus the ISO purpose code 'TRAD' or 'INTL'; (3) Commercial documentary letters of credit (issuance via MT700, confirmation/advising via MT710/MT750) where Asian suppliers demand LC-backed terms; and (4) the licensed PSP rails (Shopify Payments, PayPal and Stripe) for low-value operational disbursements such as refunds, advertising fees and third-party platform fees. All outbound transfers must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within the monthly AWV reporting window when the counterparty aggregate equals or exceeds EUR 12,500. To mitigate FX drag on outbound flows, disbursements in USD, CNY/CNH or GBP may be made directly from the corresponding currency account balance generated by Shopify/Stripe settlements; otherwise the GmbH must buy the disbursement currency through a spot transaction with the Tier-1 bank. Prohibited practices: making distributions or disguised equity withdrawals during periods of insolvency (§64 GmbHG liability for managing directors), routing outbound payments through unregulated shell EMIs, and utilising cash courier services.Outright EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CNH, EUR/CAD and EUR/JPY forward contracts (plain vanilla FEC) with Tier-1 German banks and their established international clearing desks, executed ISDA/2018 CSA or German master agreements under the Deutsche Rahmenvereinigung für Derivategeschäfte (DRV), only for hedging identified Shopify/Stripe/PayPal earned receivables and committed supplier liabilities.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted-currency jurisdictions where direct convertibility is limited (e.g., BRL, INR, KRW, TWD, TRY), with EUR or USD as the settlement currency, permissible for hedging confirmed cross-border settlement volumes in the applicable emerging-market sales channel.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps (EUR vs USD, EUR vs GBP) for temporary liquidity transformation where the GmbH expects matched inbound and outbound cash-flow legs; the swap must mirror the commercial invoice maturity profile and must not create leverage.European-style FX options (premium-paying strategies only; buying puts and calls where the net premium is expensed as treasury cost and no naked premium income is generated), permissible if expressly authorised by the managing director and recorded in the treasury policy approved by the shareholder meeting.Contractual netting and central clearing on exchanges are generally not used by a GmbH; for audit and tax safety, every hedging instrument must be linked at trade initiation to a specific exposed receivable or payable position and evidenced in the monthly treasury reports.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High only as ancillary treasury rails; a non-bank fintech or offshore electronic-money institution (EMI) cannot replace the Tier-1 German account. A German GmbH may lawfully open multi-currency payment accounts with EEA-authorised EMIs (e.g., N26 Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, PPRO, LHV UK) under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) as transposed by the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG). However, (i) an EMI located outside the EEA without an EU/EEA passport may not provide payment services or hold client fund accounts for a German entity; (ii) the blocked share-capital deposit must remain at a full credit institution to satisfy the notary and the Handelsregister admission process; (iii) the Deutsche Bundesbank AWV statistics report is triggered by payments denominated at the account level irrespective of the provider, so the GmbH must consolidate EMI statements; and (iv) merchant settlement from Shopify Payments, PayPal and Stripe is optimally routed to the German operational IBAN, with EMIs used only for FX diversification and supplier side-rail disbursements.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000. At least EUR 12,500 (one-half of each cash contribution) must be paid up in cash before filing the registration application by transfer to a blocked capital-deposit account (Gruendungskonto) held at a German credit institution in the name of the 'GmbH in Gruendung'. Each managing director must certify under Section 8(2) GmbHG that the paid-up funds remain at their free disposal; the account may not be released until registration. A sole shareholder must provide security for any unpaid portion. Since the 2024 reform, payment of the minimum capital may also be made in crypto assets at the statutory valuation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certificate (Brandschutznachweis) approved by the municipal fire brigade and building authority, with prefabricated steel elements achieving fire-resistance class R30/R60 per DIN 4102; structural load and stability verification per DIN EN 1993 (Eurocode 3) and fabrication tolerances per DIN EN 1090-2; workplace clearance issued by the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt under the Workplaces Ordinance (ArbStaettV) and the Operating Safety Ordinance (BetrSichV); cranes and lifting equipment must be certified in accordance with the Technical Rules for Operational Safety (TRBS) before commissioning.

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000. At least EUR 12,500 (one-half of each cash contribution) must be paid up in cash before filing the registration application by transfer to a blocked capital-deposit account (Gruendungskonto) held at a German credit institution in the name of the 'GmbH in Gruendung'. Each managing director must certify under Section 8(2) GmbHG that the paid-up funds remain at their free disposal; the account may not be released until registration. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Germany has no general capital controls, exchange-control approval requirement, profit-repatriation restriction, or mandatory conversion of foreign-currency receipts. A GmbH may receive, hold, convert, and remit foreign currency through regulated banks, subject to bank compliance, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, tax compliance, payment-purpose accuracy, and applicable AWV statistical reporting. Dividends and other distributions require distributable profits, valid corporate approvals, compliance with German capital-maintenance rules, withholding-tax treatment, and consideration of treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief. Interest, royalties, management fees, and intercompany payments must be supported by genuine contracts, arm's-length pricing, beneficial-owner evidence, transfer-pricing records, and applicable withholding-tax analysis. Payments involving sanctioned persons, embargoed destinations, dual-use goods, or restricted counterparties may require blocking, licensing, or enhanced review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory repatriation deadline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain proceeds in euro or foreign-currency accounts in Germany or abroad, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, and banking obligations. Commercial export documentation should support the underlying receivable and payment purpose. Cross-border payment and external-position statistics may have to be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV and Bundesbank reporting rules; reporting thresholds and forms must be checked against the current Bundesbank guidance at the transaction date. Statistical reporting does not constitute an exchange-control approval or a conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, operating within the European System of Central Banks; foreign-exchange and external-payment reporting is principally governed by the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG), the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), and Bundesbank reporting procedures. The European Central Bank has monetary authority for the euro area, while the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises regulated German credit institutions and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payments and collections; low as a sole treasury bank for a German GmbH. An EEA-authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, safeguarding, SEPA services, cards, and certain foreign-exchange services through passporting or German authorisation. Customer funds are generally safeguarded rather than covered by German statutory deposit insurance, and an EMI account is not equivalent to a German bank deposit account or a traditional capital-account relationship. Offshore or non-EEA digital EMIs cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services in Germany solely through an offshore presence without an applicable EU passport, German authorisation, or authorised agent structure. Banks and counterparties may require a German or EEA bank account for share-capital funding evidence, escrow, credit facilities, trade finance, payroll, tax payments, and high-value commercial settlements. The GmbH must verify licensing, safeguarding, settlement-bank exposure, sanctions controls, data protection, service continuity, and permitted client-money use before implementation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Fund share capital through a regulated bank account in the name of the GmbH or the formation-stage company, using the exact legal name and a payment reference such as 'Einzahlung Stammkapital gemäß Gesellschaftsvertrag'. The notary and commercial-register process should be coordinated with the bank so that the required capital amount is demonstrably credited and available. Shareholder loans or additional paid-in capital should use separate, accurately documented payment references and written agreements. Incoming international wires should use SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 equivalent messaging, the correct IBAN and BIC, complete ordering-customer and beneficiary data, and the actual purpose of payment. The bank may request the formation deed, shareholder resolution, loan agreement, source-of-funds evidence, ownership chart, invoice or contract, and sanctions or end-use information. Do not route capital through personal accounts, unrelated third parties, unlicensed payment intermediaries, or omnibus arrangements without a documented legal and compliance basis. Record the transaction in the GmbH's accounting records and retain bank confirmations. Assess AWV statistical reporting, tax treatment, transfer-pricing implications, and any applicable withholding or capital-maintenance restrictions separately.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA area where timing and transaction limits are acceptable. Use SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 cross-border credit transfer messaging for non-SEPA or foreign-currency payments, with complete beneficiary, ordering-party, invoice, country, and purpose information. Use documentary Commercial Letters of Credit, preferably UCP 600-compliant and issued or confirmed by an acceptable bank, for higher-risk suppliers, new counterparties, or jurisdictional and performance concerns. Documentary collections may use URC 522 where appropriate. Trade payments should match purchase orders, invoices, shipping documents, certificates of origin, bills of lading, delivery or acceptance certificates, and contract milestones.

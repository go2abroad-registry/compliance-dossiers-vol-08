Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Abteilung B at the local Amtsgericht (Registry Court) with jurisdiction over the GmbH's registered office; electronic submissions are effected through the Common Register Portal of the German Länder (Gemeinsames Registerportal der Länder) via the certifying notary.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbH-Gesetz / GmbHG), in particular §§ 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 10 and 35–39; Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung – HRV); and German Civil Code notarisation provisions (BGB §§ 128, 129).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially authenticated incorporation deed and articles of association (Errichtung including Gesellschaftsvertrag), together with the signed registration application (Anmeldung zum Handelsregister), list of shareholders and specimen signatures of managing directors.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 36 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory minimum share capital of a GmbH is EUR 25,000. Before the registration application is filed, at least EUR 12,500 of the subscribed cash contributions must be paid up and remain at the free disposal of the managing directors. Contributions must be credited to a German or EEA bank account in the name of the future GmbH and evidenced by a bank confirmation (Bankbescheinigung). Contributions in kind (Sacheinlagen) must be fully transferable and substantiated by a notarial report and valuation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under SOLAS Chapter II-2, structural load and stability inspection by a recognised classification society, load line certificate, life-saving appliances certificate, ISM safety management system approval, crew manning and medical fitness certification, and occupational health and safety clearance by BG Verkehr for vehicle handling and Ro-Ro deck operations.

Paid-in capital rule: The statutory minimum share capital of a GmbH is EUR 25,000. Before the registration application is filed, at least EUR 12,500 of the subscribed cash contributions must be paid up and remain at the free disposal of the managing directors. Contributions must be credited to a German or EEA bank account in the name of the future GmbH and evidenced by a bank confirmation (Bankbescheinigung). Contributions in kind (Sacheinlagen) must be fully transferable and substantiated by a notarial report and valuation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office (VAT ID issuance, national contact point for automatic exchange and hybrid-mismatch notifications) in coordination with the local Finanzamt (municipal tax office) competent for the GmbH's seat of management; ELSTER (www.elster.de) is the statutory electronic filing portal; customs registrations (EORI and ATLAS/AES export declaration) are processed by the Generalzolldirektion (German Customs Executive Agency).

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH: (1) execute notarized articles of association and obtain entry in the Handelsregister (Commercial Register); (2) the managing director must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent Finanzamt within one month of business commencement, disclosing intended corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), payroll tax (Lohnsteuer), and value added tax (Umsatzsteuer) treatment; (3) the Finanzamt assigns the statutory Steuernummer (tax number) used for all local tax returns; (4) separately apply to BZSt for a USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number) under § 27a UStG, required for intra-Community supplies and VIES-valid invoices; (5) apply via the Generalzolldirektion for an EORI (Economic Operators Registration and Identification) number and register in the ATLAS/AES customs export system; (6) activate ELSTER-certified software or browser certificate for electronic filing of VAT preliminary returns, annual returns, wage tax declarations, and the EU sales list (zusammenfassende Meldung). Foreign non-resident entrepreneurs with German taxable supplies use the simplified BZSt application procedure (Form 'Fragebogen für im Ausland ansässige Unternehmer') without a domestic trade license.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – the GmbH is a separate legal person subject to unlimited German corporate tax liability (unbeschränkte Körperschaftsteuerpflicht under § 1 KStG) on worldwide income because its statutory seat and/or place of effective management is in Germany; no flow-through or partnership classification is available by default; the entity is additionally subject to municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) under § 2 GewStG; tax rates: 15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 15.825%) plus trade tax at 11.2%–16.1% depending on the municipality's Hebesatz; territorial scope is therefore global, with foreign-source income potentially relieved under DTA provisions or Section 34c EStG / § 26 KStG; a RoRo shipping GmbH remains within the ordinary corporate tax regime unless the tonnage tax election is made. — For a German GmbH: (1) execute notarized articles of association and obtain entry in the Handelsregister (Commercial Register); (2) the managing director must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent Finanzamt within one month of business commencement, disclosing intended corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), payroll tax (Lohnsteuer), and value added tax (Umsatzsteuer) treatment; (3) the Finanzamt assigns the statutory Steuernummer (tax number) used for all local tax returns; (4) separately apply to BZSt for a USt-IdNr. Foreign non-resident entrepreneurs with German taxable supplies use the simplified BZSt application procedure (Form 'Fragebogen für im Ausland ansässige Unternehmer') without a domestic trade license.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the GmbH is a separate legal person subject to unlimited German corporate tax liability (unbeschränkte Körperschaftsteuerpflicht under § 1 KStG) on worldwide income because its statutory seat and/or place of effective management is in Germany; no flow-through or partnership classification is available by default; the entity is additionally subject to municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) under § 2 GewStG; tax rates: 15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 15.825%) plus trade tax at 11.2%–16.1% depending on the municipality's Hebesatz; territorial scope is therefore global, with foreign-source income potentially relieved under DTA provisions or Section 34c EStG / § 26 KStG; a RoRo shipping GmbH remains within the ordinary corporate tax regime unless the tonnage tax election is made.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Germany; however, the following statutory elections and mechanisms are relevant: (1) Tonnage tax election (Tonnagesteuer) under § 5a EStG for the GmbH operating seagoing vessels in international commercial shipping – replaces profit-based taxation with a fixed daily notional profit per 100 net tonnage, irrevocable for 10 years and highly relevant for ISIC 5012 deep-sea RoRo carriers; (2) Organschaft (fiscal unity): corporate income tax and trade tax group agreement requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Gewinnabführungsvertrag) valid for at least five years (§§ 14–17 KStG, § 2 Abs. 2 GewStG), and a separate VAT Organschaft under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG based on financial, economic, and organizational integration; as non-resident ownership is permitted, a foreign parent can establish a German Organschaft only if the parent maintains a German permanent establishment; (3) accounting-based hybrid mismatch declarations and notifications to BZSt under § 4k EStG and the Hybrid Mismatch Act implementing ATAD II; (4) optional waiver of VAT exemption for real estate supplies under § 9 UStG; (5) application of the cash-basis accounting option for VAT under § 20 UStG where turnover does not exceed €800,000; (6) reverse-charge election for specific B2B supplies under § 13b UStG. Partnership-type flow-through is not available to a stand-alone GmbH except by re-structuring into a GmbH & Co. KG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

German-resident GmbH: no de-minimis turnover threshold – VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable supply (§ 2 UStG). Non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Germany generally require registration unless the small-business exemption applies; intra-EU distance sales of goods and electronically supplied services to German non-taxable persons trigger German VAT through the EU-wide €10,000 union threshold (Art. 59c VAT Directive; § 3 Abs. 3a UStG). Optional nondescript small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmer, § 19 UStG): prior-year turnover not exceeding €25,000 and current-year forecast not exceeding €100,000, with no input VAT recovery; the shipping GmbH's freight revenue substantially exceeds this, so mandatory registration applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A German GmbH engaged in deep-sea RoRo vehicle transport may claim the 0% export VAT rate under the following heads: (1) Export of goods (§ 4 Nr. 1 lit. a, § 6 UStG): supplies of vehicles/goods physically dispatched by the supplier or acquirer to a third-country (non-EU) destination are zero-rated with documentary proof of removal under § 10 UStG; (2) Intra-Community supply (§ 4 Nr. 1 lit. b, § 6a UStG): transport of motor vehicles from Germany to EU Member States is zero-rated when the acquirer is an entrepreneur with a valid VAT ID and the goods are shipped to another Member State; (3) Maritime shipping services: charter or hire (Vercharterung) of German-flagged deep-sea RoRo vessels for commercial ocean shipping is zero-rated under § 8 Abs. 1 Nr. 4 UStG; onboard services and operational services rendered to the vessel owner/operator for the same purpose are zero-rated under § 8 Abs. 1 Nr. 7 UStG; (4) Transport services connected with import/export/transit goods (§ 4 Nr. 3 lit. a UStG) are exempt with input VAT deduction strictly for the direct transportation route to/from the external border, with any inland German leg not covered by the exemption being subject to 19% VAT or the B2B reverse-charge mechanism under § 13b UStG. A RoRo carrier must demonstrate the vessel is actually used in 'commercial ocean shipping' (kommerzielle Seeschifffahrt) and that the services are rendered to the party operating the vessel; input VAT on bunkering, harbor fees, freight costs, and vessel maintenance is fully recoverable only when zero-rating is established.

  • Bill of Lading — full set of original ocean Bills of Lading issued by the German RoRo carrier, including the master B/L or house B/L indicating the consignee, port of loading, port of discharge, and vessel name;
  • Sea Waybill or Fiata Multimodal Transport Document where a non-negotiable ocean transport document is used;

  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of the freight consideration and demonstrating timely repatriation of export proceeds;

  • Export inventory note and any previous transit document (ATR, EUR.1, or supplier's declarations for the customs unions) where applicable;

Mandatory structured B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) applies under the Wachstumschancengesetz (§ 14 UStG): from 1 January 2025 every German supplier must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.0.3+ or PEPPOL BIS 3.0); issuance of e-invoices is compulsory from 1 January 2026 for invoices generated by invoicing systems, from 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year turnover above €800,000, and from 1 January 2028 for all other businesses. There is NO real-time fiscal clearance, NO continuous transaction control (CT) framework, and NO digital stamping or API-based fiscal seal in Germany; transmission is accepted via PEPPOL network, secure EDI, De-Mail, or ELSTER. VAT pre-returns (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldungen) are filed electronically via ELSTER on a monthly or quarterly cycle with a permanent extension, and intra-Community supplies must be listed in the monthly zusammenfassende Meldung (EU sales list) filed with BZSt; the B2B recipient VAT number must be validated through VIES and shown on invoices; the new EU Article 239a VAT Directive requirement for the recipient's VAT number in invoices is in force for German B2B supplies from 1 January 2025.

German tax authorities conduct strict scrutiny of export zero-rating through regular tax audits and the export transaction database (BZSt export evidence checks). Failure to provide the statutory proof of export within the audit procedure or within the 180-day repatriation period results in retroactive denial of the 0% rate and reassessment of the supply at 19% VAT on the full consideration, with mandatory interest at 6% per annum (§ 233a AO) and a monthly late-payment surcharge of 1% up to 5 years (§ 240 AO). If an invoice was issued showing zero VAT without lawful entitlement, the issuer is liable under § 14c UStG for the tax shown, and the input VAT claimed by foreign charterers may be clawed back. Intentional misstatement or forged export documentation constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO with penalties of up to five years' imprisonment and fines up to €50,000; organized commercial fraud qualifies as aggravated tax evasion with extended limitation periods (up to 10 years), seizure of proceeds (Einziehung), and management liability of the GmbH directors under § 69 AO. Additionally, false customs declarations trigger liability under Article 42 UCC and German customs law (§§ 41–43 ZollVG), including fines up to €1,500,000 or equivalent customs evasion penalties. VAT audit adjustments may run retroactively until all legally binding assessments are issued, and the German tax authority is entitled to issue revised VAT assessments for all prior open periods and to initiate criminal proceedings against the GmbH and its legal representatives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under EU law, specifically Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union on free movement of capital. The euro is fully convertible and there are no exchange-control licensing requirements for inbound equity injection, loan servicing, trade-related payments, or profit distribution from a German GmbH. Profit remittance is not subject to FX approval; however, dividends paid to non-resident shareholders may be subject to German withholding tax and solidarity surcharge, with potential treaty relief under the applicable double-taxation agreement. All cross-border payment transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 (or local currency equivalent) must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics; this is a statistical and monitoring obligation, not a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no forced repatriation requirement on export proceeds. A German deep-sea Ro-Ro operator may retain, hold, and freely deploy foreign-currency export earnings in a non-resident or domestic settlement account. The sole mandatory compliance step is the AWV cross-border payment report: any payment received from non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 or the equivalent must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank no later than the 7th day of the month following the transaction month. The report is ordinarily submitted through the intermediary German banking partner as part of the SWIFT payment workflow; no central bank approval or conversion certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank) acting as the responsible monetary authority, together with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) for foreign trade regulation; the operative legal regime is the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV). Deutsche Bundesbank administers all balance-of-payments statistical reporting and cross-border payment monitoring functions under the AWV and the Act on the Deutsche Bundesbank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for operational cross-border payments and FX conversion; Low for statutory capital escrow and capital-contribution custody. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Wise Business are not licensed German banks under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz - KWG). They do not constitute 'credit institutions' and are typically not eligible to hold the initial share capital deposit or to issue the statutory certificate required for a GmbH capital contribution. They may provide non-resident IBANs and multi-currency wallets, but such accounts are generally not accepted by German registry courts, auditors, or banking partners as formal capital escrow accounts. Additionally, all cross-border payment reporting under the AWV must still be effected via a reporting channel recognized by Deutsche Bundesbank; offshore EMIs often cannot satisfy this obligation without a correspondent bank. Accordingly, an offshore EMI should be used only as a liquidity optimization overlay, not as the primary treasury or clearing rail.

Additionally, all cross-border payment reporting under the AWV must still be effected via a reporting channel recognized by Deutsche Bundesbank; offshore EMIs often cannot satisfy this obligation without a correspondent bank. Accordingly, an offshore EMI should be used only as a liquidity optimization overlay, not as the primary treasury or clearing rail.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

High-risk trade route exposure or vessel calls in or near comprehensively sanctioned jurisdictions, such as the Russian Federation, Belarus, Iran, Syria, or North Korea

Root cause: High-risk trade route exposure or vessel calls in or near comprehensively sanctioned jurisdictions, such as the Russian Federation, Belarus, Iran, Syria, or North Korea

Defense evidence: Complete EU/UN/OFAC sanctions screening results for all beneficial owners, officers, vessels, customers, and cargo consignees; vessel IMO registration certificate; flag state registry extract; recent port-call log and AIS track record; charter party agreements; bills of lading and cargo manifests; written corporate policy confirming no trade with sanctioned entities; end-use and end-user certificates; contractually imposed embargo and re-export control clauses; and external counsel opinion on sanctions compliance.
Complex ownership structure involving offshore holding companies, nominee shareholders, or opaque trusts above the German GmbH

Root cause: Complex ownership structure involving offshore holding companies, nominee shareholders, or opaque trusts above the German GmbH

Defense evidence: Transparent ownership chain chart from ultimate beneficial owner down to the GmbH; commercial register (Handelsregister) excerpts; German Transparency Register (Transparenzregister) filing confirmation; notarized declarations of the ultimate natural person beneficial owners; trust deeds, declarations of trust, or nominee shareholder agreements, where applicable; audited financial statements or opening balance sheet of the GmbH; business rationale memo explaining the offshore structure; and tax identification numbers for each entity in the chain.
Unusually high or unexplained inbound capital injections, including shareholder loans from non-EU parent entities or third-party lenders, with insufficient economic purpose

Root cause: Unusually high or unexplained inbound capital injections, including shareholder loans from non-EU parent entities or third-party lenders, with insufficient economic purpose

Defense evidence: Notarized resolution of the shareholders’ meeting authorizing the contribution or loan; written shareholder loan agreement with clear interest, maturity, and repayment terms; funds-flow diagram showing the remitting bank and the economic source of funds; purpose text aligned with AWV reporting; applicant’s audited financial statements or certified balance sheet; certificate that the funds are not derived from criminal proceeds; and a statement by the managing director on the strategic and operational use of the injected capital.
Trade disbursements to third parties that do not match charter party terms, commercial invoices, or freight contracts, raising trade-based money laundering flags

Root cause: Trade disbursements to third parties that do not match charter party terms, commercial invoices, or freight contracts, raising trade-based money laundering flags

Defense evidence: Original commercial invoice set; freight forwarding contracts and rate confirmations; charter party and any addenda; port/terminal service receipts showing actual services delivered; vessel and voyage identification data; proof of ordering and banking authorizations; reconciliation between goods/services, payment amount, and beneficiary KYC records; internal payment policy requiring vendor and counterparty due diligence; and any relevant customs or port authority documentation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from a non-resident shareholder or lender must be executed via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or, for bank-held proceeds, via an MT202 cover transfer, routed to the GmbH’s account at a licensed German credit institution. The payment message must clearly state the economic purpose in the 'Remittance Information' field using the German balance-of-payments classification, for example 'Bareinlage zur Zahlung des Stammkapitals' for a capital contribution or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' for a shareholder loan. The ordering bank’s purpose code should correspond to the German Bundesbank’s AWV statistical category for cross-border capital movements. For capital escrow formation, the payment must be credited to a specifically designated capital escrow account opened in the GmbH’s name; the managing director, bank officer, and notary, where applicable, must verify that the funds have been unencumbered and irrevocably made available. The receiving German bank will be responsible for automatically reporting the transaction to Deutsche Bundesbank if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500, and it will retain evidence of the underlying corporate resolution and the beneficial ownership declaration as part of the KYC file.For outbound settlements, the primary clearing rails are (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct beneficiary payments, including port disbursements, bunker procurement, freight forwarding fees, ro-ro terminal charges, and vendor payments; (ii) SWIFT MT202 for bank-to-bank transfers where interbank cover is required; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP 600 and transmitted through the SWIFT MT700 series for documentary trade settlements, particularly for vessel charter deposits or parcel reimbursements; and (iv) open-account trade settlements with scheduled MT103 payments and reconciliation against invoice and transport documents. All outbound trade disbursements above EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank by the payment bank under the AWV reporting regime; the reporting obligation applies irrespective of whether the payment is for goods, services, or capital movement. The GmbH must ensure that every outbound instruction identifies the VAT-registered service or goods classification and the underlying commercial documentation to avoid an AML compliance hold.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forward contracts (Geld- and Devisenswaps) entered into with a licensed German credit institution, covering EUR/USD and major trade-pair currencies, to hedge freight revenue and bunker cost exposuresNon-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts on illiquid or lightly traded emerging-market currencies through an authorized German bank’s structured foreign-exchange desk, subject to internal credit and margin policyCross-currency swaps for medium-term EUR/USD or USD/freight-index-linked liabilities, executed under ISDA master agreements with a German banking counterparty, to manage long-term vessel finance and charter payment mismatches

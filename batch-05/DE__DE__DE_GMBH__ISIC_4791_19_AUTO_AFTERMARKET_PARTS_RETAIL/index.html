Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt fuer Steuern, BZSt) — https://www.bzst.de; local tax office (Finanzamt) competent for assessment and collection on the basis of the GmbH's registered seat; electronic filing and TIN/VAT-ID procurement through the ELSTER portal — https://www.elster.de

E-government filing portal

For a domestic German GmbH: upon commercial register entry, submit the statutory tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') to the competent local Finanzamt within four weeks of business commencement; the Finanzamt then issues the local tax number (Steuernummer) and forwards an application to BZSt for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). For foreign entities making taxable supplies in Germany without a domestic establishment: apply directly to BZSt using the applicable application form for non-resident entrepreneurs (USt 2 TW or equivalent) either in writing or via ELSTER, stating the foreign VAT ID, expected German turnover, and an authorized recipient in Germany if required; BZSt will issue a DE VAT ID following nexus review. All subsequent VAT returns, EC sales lists, and OSS declarations must be filed electronically through ELSTER or certified data processing interfaces.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

GmbH is an incorporated entity subject to entity-level German corporation tax (Koerperschaftsteuer) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); no check-the-box pass-through classification exists. Available statutory elections include: (1) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under §§ 14 and 17 KStG and § 2 UStG, consolidating a German parent and the GmbH subsidiary for corporation tax, trade tax, and VAT; (2) the VAT small-business exemption election under § 19 UStG where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover is forecast not to exceed EUR 100,000 (from 2025), although this is economically disadvantageous for a retail GmbH because input VAT recovery is forfeited; (3) election of the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) regime for intra-Community distance sales and certain B2C services; (4) reverse-charge elections under § 13b UStG for specified cross-border B2B supplies. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under §§ 4k EStG, 7 GewStG, and 12 KStG (ATAD implementation) apply to cross-border double-deduction and deduction/non-inclusion arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for non-established foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Germany; any taxable supply creates nexus. For domestically established small businesses, the § 19 UStG small-business exemption applies when prior-year turnover is at most EUR 25,000 and current-year forecast turnover is at most EUR 100,000 (from 1 January 2025). For intra-Community distance sales of goods, the EU-wide annual threshold of EUR 10,000 applies; once exceeded, the supplier must register for VAT in the destination member state or elect the OSS regime through the BZSt/ELSTER portal.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from German territory to a non-EU third-country destination qualify for zero rating under § 4 No. 1(a) and § 6 UStG; intra-Community supplies to a VAT-registered EU business qualify under § 4 No. 1(b) and § 6a UStG provided the supplier holds a valid VAT ID of the customer and submits the EC sales list (Zusammenfassende Meldung). The goods must physically leave the European Union immediately after dispatch or transport begins in Germany. For an automotive aftermarket e-commerce GmbH, zero rating on direct B2C third-country export shipments is permitted only if the GmbH is the exporter, the dispatch is evidenced by customs documentation, and the goods are removed from the Community; B2B offshore services and related ancillary services to non-EU recipients are zero-rated under § 4 No. 2 and § 3a UStG where the recipient acquires the service for business purposes and the reverse-charge mechanism applies in the recipient's jurisdiction. Zero rating is a privilege requiring complete proof; absent such proof the supply is treated as taxable in Germany at 19%.

  • Customs export declaration accepted by German customs authorities (Ausfuhranmeldung filed in ATLAS with exit confirmation by the customs office)
  • Export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument, ABD) with stamped or electronically confirmed exit
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR consignment note proving physical cross-border transport
  • Commercial invoice with INCOTERMS consistent with the export declaration and transport documents
  • Proof of arrival in the destination country (signed delivery note or third-country customs importation record)
  • Validated VAT ID certificate of the EU customer (VIES validation output) for intra-Community supplies
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence showing receipt of the export payment on a German account

Germany has mandated B2B e-invoicing in phases from 1 January 2025: from 2025 all businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices; from 1 January 2027, businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all businesses must issue e-invoices. Accepted e-invoice formats are XRechnung (XML standard) and ZUGFeRD 2.x (PDF/A-3 with embedded XML data). Invoices to federal public authorities must be submitted through the central federal e-invoicing portal (ZfKE). Germany currently does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, digital fiscal stamping, or mandatory continuous transaction controls for B2C retail; periodic VAT returns, EC sales lists, and OSS declarations constitute the only ongoing fiscal reporting obligations. The EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) reforms introducing near-real-time digital VAT reporting are under legislative development and not yet in force in German national law.

German tax and customs audit authorities (Finanzamt and Zollfahndungsamt) require the complete documentary chain to be retained for at least 10 years and must be able to reconcile each zero-rated export to payment, customs, and transport records. Failure to substantiate zero rating triggers retrospective assessment at the standard 19% VAT rate, default interest at 0.15% per month on the assessed tax (1.8% per annum), late payment surcharges of 1% per month on the outstanding amount, and penalty surcharges of up to 10% of the under-declared VAT. Fraudulent or reckless zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years, criminal fines, and confiscation of the wrongful benefit; managing directors of the GmbH face personal liability under § 69 AO, civil joint liability for unpaid VAT, and the GmbH may be barred from future export VAT refunds and subject to customs debt liability under Article 79 of the Union Customs Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions under EU primary law (Art. 63 TFEU) and pursuant to the AWV. There are no exchange control restrictions on profit remittance by a GmbH, except standard corporate formality requirements such as audited annual accounts, tax clearance certificates, and compliance with anti-money laundering source-of-funds verification. Transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions or restricted dual-use goods are separately regulated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion timeline applies in Germany. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency. An exporter must, however, comply with cross-border statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank: payments exceeding EUR 12,500 (or equivalent) are reportable on a monthly or weekly cycle, usually within 7 calendar days for transactions requiring cash-flow reporting and within the statutory deadlines for position-reporting. Failure to file carries administrative fines.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of Germany) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision. Formal foreign exchange control powers were abolished by 1958 and 1961 legislation; the Bundesbank retains only statistical monitoring and reporting duties under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers without an EU/EEA banking license cannot be used as primary treasury or clearing rails because they must obtain a BaFin license or a valid EU passport under PSD2. Licensed EU EMIs (e.g., N26, Solarisbank, and comparable E-Money institutions) are feasible for low-value inbound card settlements and ancillary operational accounts, but they are generally rated 'Low' for core cross-border corporate treasury due to lower transaction thresholds, stricter cash-out limits, and narrower availability of trade finance products. A BaFin-licensed or EU-passported EMI can be used for operational liquidity only, not for complex FX or LC execution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital injections or shareholder loan proceeds should be routed by wire transfer through a licensed EU clearing bank using the SWIFT MT103 message standard. The payment must be tagged with the correct Bundesbank reporting purpose code as set out under the AWV's statistical reporting system (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik), most commonly the code for direct-investment capital injection. The beneficiary bank is required to log the capital inflow and trigger the mandatory Bundesbank reporting notification if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500. The GmbH must also ensure that the incoming payment references the shareholder resolution and capital contribution bookkeeping entry. Where the capital injection is above EUR 25,000, the notarial and commercial register formalities must be completed first.Trade settlements to international suppliers are cleared through SWIFT MT103 single-wire payments for lower-value spot purchases and through commercial documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) for high-value or recurring inventory shipments. SEPA Instant Credit Transfers are acceptable only for invoice values and counterparties within the SEPA zone. For full treasury assurance, the rail hierarchy is: (1) irrevocable commercial LC for large supplier commitments; (2) SWIFT MT103 wire for immediate settlement after LC release; and (3) documentary collections (DP/DA) as a secondary fallback. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Körperschaftsteuer: 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax, producing a national cash tax burden of 15.825%; trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additionally levied by the municipality at a standard Hebesatz of 400% (effective rate 14%), resulting in a combined nominal headline burden of approximately 29.825% at the Berlin average before the trade tax deduction; the effective aggregate tax burden after trade-tax deductibility is approximately 27.6%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

German statutory dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) is 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax, i.e. a combined gross rate of 26.375%; trade tax does not apply to the shareholder-level distribution, but hidden profit distributions may affect the company's trade tax base.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year GmbH, the statutory filing deadline is 31 July of the year following the fiscal year-end; if the return is prepared by a licensed tax adviser, the standard administrative extension normally moves the deadline to 31 October of the second year following the fiscal year-end (e.g. fiscal year 2024: 31 July 2025, or 31 October 2026 for the adviser-prepared return).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GAufzV and §90(3) AO trigger mandatory local transfer-pricing documentation when related-party cross-border goods transactions exceed EUR 750,000 per year or related-party services transactions exceed EUR 150,000 per year; the German Master File obligation is triggered when the multinational group's worldwide turnover exceeds EUR 100 million; documentation must be contemporaneous and ordinarily must be submitted within 60 days of a tax audit request and within 30 days for extraordinary transactions.

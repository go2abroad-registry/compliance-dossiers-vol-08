Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register), Registergericht Abteilung B, administered by the competent Local Court (Amtsgericht) at the company's registered seat; filings are submitted electronically by a German notary and published in the Bundesanzeiger/Unternehmensregister.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbH-Gesetz, GmbHG): §§ 1-2 (formation and notarial articles), § 5 (required minimum share capital of EUR 25,000), §§ 6-7 (appointment of Geschäftsführer and pre-registration payment requirement), § 8 (content of registration application), § 10 (legal existence upon Commercial Register entry), §§ 13 and 35 (legal personality and representation powers).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notariell beurkundeter Gesellschaftsvertrag (notarially certified Articles of Association) together with the Anmeldung zur Eintragung (notarized Application for Registration) filed with the Local Court.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory minimum share capital is EUR 25,000. Before application for registration, at least EUR 12,500 (50% of the minimum capital) must be paid in cash on a dedicated business account (Geschäftskonto) and be at the free disposal (freie Verfügung) of the managing director; contributions may not be financed by loans from the company itself. For a single-member GmbH, the founder must additionally provide security for any unpaid portion of the capital. The notary and managing director jointly certify the capital conformation in the registration filing.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Brandschutzabnahme) per the state building code (MBO § 18) including emergency exits, alarm systems, and extinguisher maintenance; structural and rack-stability inspection per DIN EN 15635 with static load certificates for all high-bay or mezzanine racks; workplace conditional clearance under ArbStättV (lighting, ventilation, emergency routes); mandatory membership with the professional accident insurance association (Berufsgenossenschaft Handel und Warenlogistik) and appointment of safety officers, first aiders, and required instructions for forklift use.

Paid-in capital rule: The statutory minimum share capital is EUR 25,000. Before application for registration, at least EUR 12,500 (50% of the minimum capital) must be paid in cash on a dedicated business account (Geschäftskonto) and be at the free disposal (freie Verfügung) of the managing director; contributions may not be financed by loans from the company itself. For a single-member GmbH, the founder must additionally provide security for any unpaid portion of the capital. The notary and managing director jointly certify the capital conformation in the registration filing.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unrestricted currency convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no exchange-control authorization, prior licensing, central-bank approval, or transaction-level permit for inbound equity, shareholder loans, trade settlements, dividend remittances, or licensing fees. The Euro is freely convertible into any currency through the interbank market without surrender or surrender-ratio requirements. Residual hurdles are non-FX in nature: (i) statutory German dividend withholding tax of 26.375% (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) on distributions to non-EU/EEA residents, potentially reduced under an applicable double taxation treaty (e.g., US-Germany DTA generally 0%/5%; China-Germany DTA 10%); (ii) AWV statistical reporting to the Bundesbank for any single cross-border payment or receipt exceeding EUR 12,500 (monthly submission via the external statistics portal, with the legal obligation resting on the German reporting entity); (iii) EU/OFAC-sanction screening of counterparties, which is a screening obligation, not a currency restriction. Profit remittance is therefore a purely tax-administrative exercise, not a monetary-control barrier, provided the GmbH files its annual financial statements and tax returns to obtain the required tax clearance certificates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation deadline, minimum conversion ratio, or earmarking requirement for export proceeds. Exporters and 3PL service providers may retain foreign-currency receivables indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts (including USD, GBP, CNY, PLN, CZK accounts at German banks), may convert them at negotiated rates, or may net them against payables. The only binding requirement is the AWV statistical disclosure obligation: each inbound or outbound cross-border transfer exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank with the correct payment-purpose code, country of counterparty, and economic classification of the underlying transaction (goods, services, capital, or financial credit). The reporting is typically executed automatically by the German bank on behalf of the client, but the GmbH remains legally liable for the accuracy and timeliness of the underlying data.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, Frankfurt am Main, operating as the German component of the Eurosystem and the European Central Bank (ECB); exchange-control policy is fully liberalized under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital) and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG). Banking supervision and AML/CFT oversight are shared with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin – Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht), which also supervises payment institutions and e-money institutions (EMIs). Statistical monitoring of cross-border payments is performed by the Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV), while export-control vetting falls to BAFA (Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for liquidity-aggregation and payment-disbursement purposes, with clear legal constraints. A German GmbH engaged in low-risk kitting/3PL services may lawfully open and operate an Airwallex account (Airwallex Europe S.A., authorized as an electronic-money institution in Luxembourg, passporting into Germany under PSD2) for multi-currency virtual IBANs, marketplace-payout consolidation, and low-cost FX conversion. Constraints: (i) an EMI account is not a credit institution account within the meaning of the German Banking Act (KWG) and is not covered by the statutory German Deposit Guarantee Act (Einlagensicherungsgesetz) up to EUR 100,000; funds are protected only through segregated safeguarding accounts held with a credit institution; (ii) the German account holder must not use the EMI as its sole banking connection because German fiscal authorities and the Bundesbank expect a domestic SEPA direct-debit (ACH Direct) capability and an auditable tax-compliant payment channel; (iii) inbound transfers from the EMI virtual IBANs to a German Tier-1 bank may trigger additional KYC reverification if the remitter name is unclear or if the origin-country of the funds is a high-risk jurisdiction; (iv) the GmbH should file an AWV Z1/K1 report for the aggregated value of EMI payouts exceeding EUR 12,500 per cross-border transaction, even though the EMI may not perform the reporting itself; (v) contractual due diligence must verify that the EMI's counterparty settlement banks are not subject to ECB restrictions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

100% non-resident shareholding with no local substance evidence (no office, no German resident manager, no payroll).

Root cause: 100% non-resident shareholding with no local substance evidence (no office, no German resident manager, no payroll).

Defense evidence: Certified commercial register excerpt; registered-office lease agreement (with a physical, non-letterbox address); employment contract and payroll records of at least one German-resident employee; utility and telecommunication invoices addressed to the registered office; current Transparenzregister (transparency register) extract identifying the ultimate beneficial owner; certificate of business registration (Gewerbeanmeldung); and a board resolution confirming the German office is the principal center of management for EU operations.
Third-party payment flows — marketplace payouts (Amazon, eBay, Wish, Allegro) and platform settlement amounts that do not match the contractual counterparty in the services agreement.

Root cause: Third-party payment flows — marketplace payouts (Amazon, eBay, Wish, Allegro) and platform settlement amounts that do not match the contractual counterparty in the services agreement.

Defense evidence: Executed 3PL fulfillment and kitting service agreements with each marketplace seller; platform seller-account onboarding reports; reconciliations matching each settlement line to a purchase order and delivery confirmation; customs export documents (Ausfuhrbegleitschein/EU export declarations); a declaration of the marketplace as collection agent (Inkassodienst) with the contractual basis; and archive of picking, packing, and last-mile tracking records.
Rapid round-tripping of the initial share capital — deposit of the Stammkapital followed within days by an outflow to a foreign supplier or shareholder.

Root cause: Rapid round-tripping of the initial share capital — deposit of the Stammkapital followed within days by an outflow to a foreign supplier or shareholder.

Defense evidence: Notarized deed of formation with capital contribution schedule; notary's confirmation of capital release (Freigabe) from the escrow account; bank statements evidencing the chain of funds; supplier invoices with underlying purchase orders and warehouse-inbound records; written shareholder loan agreements if the outflow is a repayment; and a cash-flow forecast demonstrating the treasury purpose of the initial capital.
Sanctions/PEP-name matches or adverse media hits on the beneficial owner due to same-name matches with designated persons in the region of incorporation.

Root cause: Sanctions/PEP-name matches or adverse media hits on the beneficial owner due to same-name matches with designated persons in the region of incorporation.

Defense evidence: Professional sanctions-screening results from a commercial provider (e.g., Dow Jones, Refinitiv) with date and reference number; beneficial-owner declaration under section 3 GwG; passport copy with apostille and notarized translation into German; an affidavit of no dual nationality with, and no residency in, restricted jurisdictions; and a summary of the beneficial owner's legitimate business track record.
High transaction throughput disproportionate to equity capital — e.g., multi-million-euro settlement turnover against the EUR 25,000 statutory minimum capital of the GmbH.

Root cause: High transaction throughput disproportionate to equity capital — e.g., multi-million-euro settlement turnover against the EUR 25,000 statutory minimum capital of the GmbH.

Defense evidence: Parent-company guarantee (Patronatserklärung) or subordination declaration (Rangrücktrittserklärung) if applicable; audited annual financial statements of the parent; monthly transaction-volume statistics of the fulfillment center; warehouse capacity and inventory reports; product-liability and warehouseman's liability insurance (Betriebshaftpflicht) certificates; and client base list evidencing the absence of related-party concentration.
Potential indirect exposure to e-commerce distance-selling and packaging-law obligations that the bank screens as a regulatory red flag.

Root cause: Potential indirect exposure to e-commerce distance-selling and packaging-law obligations that the bank screens as a regulatory red flag.

Defense evidence: LUCID packaging-registration certificate under the German Packaging Act (VerpackG); VAT registration certificate (Umsatzsteuerbescheid) for the GmbH's own rendering of services; a written no-position statement confirming the GmbH does not hold title to the goods it kits and ships; specimen of the service agreement clarifying remuneration is for kitting/packaging services, not for the sale of goods; and the ISIC 5210/NAICS 493110 industry classification reference.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is a two-step protocol. Step 1 — Initial equity injection: the shareholder must pay the stated capital (Stammkapital, minimum EUR 25,000; at least half must be paid in cash for a single-member GmbH) into a dedicated capital-payment escrow account opened at a German credit institution; the transfer must be a SEPA Credit Transfer or SWIFT MT103 with a remittance information field stating 'Einlagezahlung zur Errichtung der GmbH' and the statutory AWV purpose code for contribution of equity capital. No Bundesbank or BaFin authorization is required. Step 2 — Subsequent shareholder loans and trade financing: inbound loans are free of approval but require a written loan agreement at arm's-length interest rates, and each transfer must be separately identified with the AWV purpose code for intercompany financial credits; the GmbH must retain the source-of-wealth documentation of the lending shareholder. Both Step 1 and Step 2 are subject to the AWV statistical threshold of EUR 12,500: for payments exceeding that sum, the Bundesbank must receive the Z1/K1 record (usually filed electronically by the receiving bank on the same day) and the GmbH must double-verify the reported economic category and counterparty country to avoid administrative fines. The bank will not release the share capital (Freigabe) until the notary confirms the capital-in-kind valuation and the managing director has declared that all legal prerequisites are satisfied.Clearing rails are segmented by destination. (i) EU/EEA suppliers and logistics partners: SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) using the GmbH's German IBAN; ACH Direct (SEPA Direct Debit) is deployed for the pre-authorized collection of recurring logistics invoices from DHL, Hermes, DPD, and other parcel carriers. (ii) Non-EU suppliers and cross-border logistics: SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) via the Tier-1 bank's correspondent network, using ISO 20022 pacs.008 messaging where adopted, and including the supplier's full legal name, address, and LEI/BIC identifiers to minimize rejection risk and correspondent charges. (iii) Documentary trade settlement: Commercial Letters of Credit opened by the German bank under UCP 600 (SWIFT MT700) and confirmed by the seller's bank, with the presentation file containing the commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, transport document (bill of lading or airway bill), and the certificate of kitting/assembly completion; alternatively, stand-by letters of credit (SBLC) may secure deferred payment terms. (iv) Multi-currency marketplace settlements: Airwallex or another licensed EMI collects USD, GBP, CNY, PLN, and CZK payouts through virtual IBANs, converts to EUR, and forwards to the domestic operational account via SEPA; each conversion and transfer leg must be recorded in the treasury ledger to substantiate the AWV reports. (v) Liquidity management: the GmbH should maintain a demand-deposit buffer equivalent to at least two weeks of operating cash-flow in the operational account to cover SEPA direct-debit collections and to avoid overdraft triggering, and should concentrate surplus funds in the EUR Treasury account of the Tier-1 bank.Outright FX Forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) — EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CNH, EUR/PLN, EUR/CZK, and EUR/SEK forwards executed OTC with the Tier-1 German bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the German Master Agreement for Derivatives (DRV), with a CSA for collateral; commercial hedging is permissible without proprietary-trading authorization and is exempt from mandatory EMIR clearing for non-financial counterparties below the clearing threshold.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) — permissible for currencies that lack deliverability in Germany (e.g., INR, KRW, and onshore CNY restrictions); the bank books the NDF offshore against CNH or USD reference rates; requires a treasury memorandum demonstrating that no deliverable alternative is available and that the hedge is matched to specific invoice receivables or payables.Cross-Currency Swaps (Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps, CSS/CRS) — permissible for converting a EUR-denominated working-capital loan into USD or CNY exposure to match trade payables, or vice versa; documented under ISDA and used only for natural hedging of identified contractual cash-flow streams; requires the board's hedging-policy approval and full accounting disclosure under HGB.Plain-Vanilla FX Options (calls and puts) — permissible as OTC options (not exchange-traded speculative positions), provided the GmbH is properly classified as a professional counterparty under section 67 WpHG for derivative purposes; MiFID II appropriateness testing and a documented hedging rationale are mandatory.Synthetic FX Forwards via Fintech/EMI platforms (Airwallex, Wise Business, etc.) — permissible as hedging instruments only when the EMI executes back-to-back physical hedges with its regulated bank counterparty; the GmbH must recognize the positions at fair value in its annual financial statements and ensure the platform activities remain within the scope of the GmbH's trade/business purpose (not speculative trading).FX Swaps (Foreign-Exchange Swaps) — permissible as a liquidity-clearing instrument for same-day funding of foreign-currency disbursements, being a combination of a spot transaction and a reverse forward; allowed under DRV/ISDA master agreements and subject to the same non-financial-counterparty EMIR treatment as forwards.

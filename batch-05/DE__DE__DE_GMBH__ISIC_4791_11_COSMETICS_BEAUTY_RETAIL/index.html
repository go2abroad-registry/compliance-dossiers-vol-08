Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the Registergericht at the competent Local Court (Amtsgericht) — for a Munich seat, Amtsgericht München, Handelsregister Abteilung B; official electronic publication via the Federal Gazette (Bundesanzeiger) and the central register portal (www.handelsregister.de).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung — GmbHG), §§ 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 13, 35, 39, 46, 78; supplemented by the statutory Model Protocol (Musterprotokoll) and by § 14 Gewerbeordnung (trade freedom) for the e-commerce retail activity.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized incorporation deed (Gesellschaftsvertrag in notarieller Beurkundung) together with the electronic application for registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung), including the shareholder list (Gesellschafterliste), the managing director list (Geschäftsführerliste), the bank confirmation of the minimum capital deposit, and the declaration on free disposal of capital.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000.00 is required; before registration, at least EUR 12,500.00 (50% of the nominal capital) must be paid up in cash and must remain at the free disposal of the Managing Director. The contribution is deposited into a dedicated bank account opened in the name of the company in formation ('GmbH i.G.'), and the bank's written confirmation of deposit and free availability must be attached to the registration application; notarial and court costs are additional and are not part of the subscribed capital.

Funding & unlock path: No physical clearance certificate is issued for a purely administrative e-commerce office in Germany. If the GmbH leases office or warehouse premises, the following obligations apply: fire safety compliance under the applicable state building code (Bauordnung) — escape routes, fire extinguishers, signage, and, where required, fire alarm and fire safety concepts; structural and load-bearing safety under the Bauordnung; workplace safety per the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) and the Operational Safety Ordinance (Betriebssicherheitsverordnung), including regular risk assessments; mandatory enrollment of the company and employees in the statutory accident insurance (Berufsgenossenschaft) for the relevant sector.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000.00 is required; before registration, at least EUR 12,500.00 (50% of the nominal capital) must be paid up in cash and must remain at the free disposal of the Managing Director. The contribution is deposited into a dedicated bank account opened in the name of the company in formation ('GmbH i.G.'), and the bank's written confirmation of deposit and free availability must be attached to the registration application; notarial and court costs are additional and are not part of the subscribed capital.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft

RESOLVED FURTHER, that the registered office and corporate seat (Sitz) is fixed at [registered address], Munich, Germany, and the Managing Director is authorized to lease premises, open bank accounts, and receive the lawful deposit of the minimum paid-up capital of EUR 12,500. RESOLVED FURTHER, that [Name] is appointed sole Managing Director (Geschäftsführer) with sole signature authority (Einzelvertretungsbefugnis), authorized to represent the Company before the Commercial Register and all third parties. RESOLVED FURTHER, that the Managing Director shall file the registration application without delay.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for pure online retail of cosmetics; no industrial manufacturing permit is required under trade freedom principles. Downstream compliance competence rests with the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt (state trade inspection authority) for workplace safety inspections, and with the Bundesamt für Verbraucherschutz und Lebensmittelsicherheit (BVL) and the relevant marktüberwachung authorities for cosmetic product safety obligations under EU Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009.

Category: Not applicable — the retail business commences lawfully upon Gewerbeanmeldung without a separate operating license. Product-side obligations apply instead: the EU Cosmetic Products Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 (notification of products in the CPNP by the Responsible Person, safety assessment, labeling per German Cosmetics Ordinance), the German Food and Feed Code (LFGB § 26) prohibitions, and the Ordinance on Fees for the German Medicines and Medical Devices Agency where relevant.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT ID (USt-IdNr.), OSS scheme and non-resident seller registration; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for income tax, trade tax and Steuernummer; portals: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt Online Portal (Mein BZSt).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent local Finanzamt via ELSTER to obtain the Steuernummer, covering corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT registration dates. Step 2: Apply to BZSt for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using Form USt 1 or the BZSt online service. Step 3: For a non-resident foreign entity maintaining a German warehouse or performing taxable supplies in Germany, register through the BZSt single-point procedure for non-established entrepreneurs (electronic Form 12a) or elect the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme for intra-EU distance sales. Step 4: Quarterly OSS returns are filed with BZSt; the Finanzamt retains audit jurisdiction over local VAT and income/trade tax files.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: a German-resident GmbH is subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer, 15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate income tax) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective 14-17% depending on municipality); income is taxed at the entity level and no pass-through election is available; shareholder dividends are separately taxed at 25% withholding tax plus surcharge or the partial-income method. Territorial_Scope: the GmbH is taxed on worldwide income with a 95% participation exemption for foreign dividends (§ 8b KStG) and treaty relief for foreign permanent establishments. — Step 1: File the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent local Finanzamt via ELSTER to obtain the Steuernummer, covering corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT registration dates. Step 3: For a non-resident foreign entity maintaining a German warehouse or performing taxable supplies in Germany, register through the BZSt single-point procedure for non-established entrepreneurs (electronic Form 12a) or elect the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme for intra-EU distance sales. Step 4: Quarterly OSS returns are filed with BZSt; the Finanzamt retains audit jurisdiction over local VAT and income/trade tax files.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a German-resident GmbH is subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer, 15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate income tax) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective 14-17% depending on municipality); income is taxed at the entity level and no pass-through election is available; shareholder dividends are separately taxed at 25% withholding tax plus surcharge or the partial-income method. Territorial_Scope: the GmbH is taxed on worldwide income with a 95% participation exemption for foreign dividends (§ 8b KStG) and treaty relief for foreign permanent establishments.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election. Available mechanisms: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under §§ 14 KStG and 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, consolidating corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT of financially, economically and organisationally integrated subsidiaries; (ii) VAT grouping for input VAT recovery and joint liability; (iii) § 9 UStG option to waive VAT exemption on real estate supplies; (iv) hybrid-mismatch outcomes are constrained by § 4k EStG anti-hybrid rules; the small-business exemption (§ 19 UStG) is unavailable to a GmbH because it is statutorily obliged to keep commercial books under § 238 HGB.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 - a German-resident GmbH is mandatorily VAT-registered from formation regardless of turnover; the § 19 UStG small-business threshold does not apply to corporations. Non-resident sellers storing goods in Germany or making taxable supplies must register without a monetary threshold. Intra-EU distance sales of consumer goods are covered by the EUR 10,000 OSS scheme threshold, but where OSS is not used, local EU destination-country registration is required from the first euro.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU third countries are VAT-free with input VAT deduction under § 6 UStG where the seller proves the goods physically left the EU within the statutory timelines (generally 3 months for direct transport; 12 months for goods under customs procedures). For the DTC beauty retail model, shipments to end customers outside the EU (e.g., UK, USA, Asia) via parcel carrier qualify if the goods are delivered to a foreign address and documentary proof is retained. Intra-Community supplies to EU B2B customers (§ 6a UStG) are exempt with the customer's valid VAT ID and summary statement, not zero-rated and outside OSS. Offshore B2B services follow the place-of-supply rules under § 3a UStG and are generally not taxable in Germany when the recipient is an entrepreneur established abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / EAS including the MRN)
  • Commercial Invoice (Handelsrechnung) linking order, SKU and payment
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note (Frachtbrief)
  • Proof of transport and exit (carrier tracking history, customs-stamped receipt or postal export certificate)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit advice evidencing payment received from the foreign customer)

Germany does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based validation or digital stamping. Mandatory B2B e-invoicing is phased: from 1 January 2025 every entrepreneur must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD); from 1 January 2027 suppliers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices for domestic B2B transactions; from 1 January 2028 issuance becomes mandatory for all domestic B2B supplies. EDI (EDIFACT) remains acceptable; GoBD rules prescribe audit-proof digital archiving of all invoice data.

Export VAT substantiation remains auditable for 8 years, extended to 10 years in cases of wilful evasion; deficient documentary proof triggers retrospective reassessment at 19% VAT plus default interest of 0.5% per month (§ 233a AO). Fraudulent zero-rating constitutes tax evasion (§ 370 AO) punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years in aggravated cases) and fines up to EUR 1,000,000 for legal persons under § 30 OWiG (EUR 500,000 if negligent under § 378 AO); managing directors and legal representatives face personal liability under § 69 AO. Repeated infringements may trigger a tax audit and revocation of the export exemption for future supplies. Note: Germany imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day convention is a documentation standard for matching inward remittances to export declarations during audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of the euro across both current and capital accounts (TFEU Art. 63; AWG); no FX licensing, approval, matriculation or allocation regime exists. Profit remittance (dividends to non-resident shareholders) is FX-unrestricted; the only statutory hurdle is the German dividend withholding tax (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 26.375%; church tax may apply), reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (10% holding for 12 uninterrupted months) or to treaty rates (e.g., 5% DTT) with economic-substance verification under § 50d(3) EStG.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender, repatriation quota or mandatory conversion ratio applies to German exporters; 100% retention in EUR or any foreign currency is permitted. The only statutory duty is the statistical report to the Deutsche Bundesbank under § 67 AWV for all cross-border payments equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500, filed through the resident beneficiary/payer's bank within 5 business days of the month following the transaction; e-commerce payouts via Shopify Payments/PayPal are included in this aggregate reporting and must be coded by purpose (e.g., 'Warenexport' or 'Dienstleistungen').

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (national central bank and foreign-exchange statistics authority under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz/Außenwirtschaftsverordnung); monetary policy competence for the euro is vested in the European Central Bank (ECB) under the Eurosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: High for daily treasury operations (Penta/Solarisbank, Qonto, Kontist and Wise Business operating under EEA e-money licences passported into Germany); Low as sole treasury or capital-escrow vehicle. Legal constraints: an offshore digital EMI must hold an e-money/payment-institution licence in the EEA and passport into Germany under PSD2 via BaFin; UK-licensed EMIs post-Brexit lack passporting rights and must establish an EU entity. E-money float is safeguarded under the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG) but is not covered by German statutory deposit insurance (Einlagensicherung), creating insolvency risk; policy therefore limits EMI balances to EUR 75,000 per day for collection/disbursement flows, while statutory capital, AWV-reportable injections and hedge collateral remain exclusively in CRR-licensed German banks.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% corpororate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), producing a combined effective burden of approximately 30% to 32% depending on the municipal multiplier (Hebesatz).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, producing an aggregate statutory rate of 26.375% on distributed cash dividends and deemed distributions; no church surcharge for corporate recipients.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by 31 July of the year following the tax year; automatically extended to 30 April of the second following year where the return is prepared by a licensed tax advisor (Section 149(2) and (3) AO).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory deadline as the CIT return: 31 July of the following year, extended to 30 April of the second following year with tax advisor representation.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Monthly or quarterly VAT pre-registrations due by the 10th day after the end of the reporting period with any necessary permanent extension; annual VAT return due by 31 July of the following year, extended to 30 April of the second following year with tax advisor representation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR. German documentation obligations under Sections 90(3) and 138f AO: notification (Anzeige) is required for cross-border transactions with related parties exceeding EUR 1,000,000 per transaction category; a Local File is mandatory where relevant controlled transactions exceed EUR 3,000,000; a Master File is required where the global group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a specific Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to UAE or Cayman ESR regimes. However, treaty and domestic anti-abuse doctrines require genuine commercial substance: Section 42 AO disregards abusive civil-law structures; Section 50d(3) EStG denies treaty relief for letterbox companies; the Federal Fiscal Court (BFH) and transfer-of-function rules require an adequate number of local employees, premises, and independent entrepreneurial decision-making. Without such substance, the GmbH may be disregarded as a tax shell and the income reattributed to the foreign shareholder, triggering withholding tax and CFC taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years (up to 10 years in aggravated cases) under Section 370 AO; criminal fines based on daily-rate system capped at twice the evaded tax in serious cases; interest on evaded tax (Hinterziehungszinsen) at 6% per annum for up to 10 years; forfeiture of assets derived from the evasion.

Director personal liability: The managing director is prosecuted as a principal or accomplice and can be assessed for the unpaid tax under Section 71 AO. The corporate veil is pierced where the GmbH is used as a criminal instrument. Personal consequences include seizure of assets, travel bans, extradition under the European Arrest Warrant, professional disqualification, and imprisonment.
Sham Company / Shell Structure (Briefkastenfirma)

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the abusive arrangement under Section 42 AO; recharacterization of income at the beneficial-owner level; denial of treaty relief under Section 50d(3) EStG and the MLI Principal Purpose Test; deemed constructive dividend subject to 26.375% withholding tax; application of German CFC rules (Sections 7-13 AStG) creating deemed remittances taxed in Germany; administrative fines and revocation of the commercial register entry where the registered seat is fictitious.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders are jointly and severally liable for the taxes avoided through the shell company. German courts pierce the veil where the GmbH is a sham. Delayed insolvency filing triggers criminal liability for the director of up to 3 years imprisonment; non-resident directors can be targeted through mutual legal assistance and travel bans.
AML / Payment Compliance (Geldwäsche)

Statutory sanction: Fines under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG) of up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; criminal liability under Section 261 StGB with imprisonment of up to 10 years for money laundering; freezing and forfeiture orders over payment assets; exclusion from payment processing rails.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for AML compliance are personally liable for regulatory fines where the entity failed to implement adequate controls. Criminal prosecution may attach to individual directors, with secondary civil restitution obligations; recurring offenders face professional disqualification and travel bans.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (Verstöße gegen die Verrechnungspreisdokumentation)

Statutory sanction: Adverse income estimation by the tax authority under Section 162(2) AO; profit adjustments including the 6% per annum interest charge on under-assessed tax; non-cooperation penalties in the range of 5% of the adjusted profit and additional surcharges of up to EUR 100,000 for incomplete documentation; denial of deductibility of the adjusted expense.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable under Section 69 AO for tax underpayments resulting from failure to maintain required documentation. Intentional documentation deficiencies can be treated as tax evasion, exposing the director to criminal prosecution. Foreign group directors may be subpoenaed in German fiscal court proceedings and are subject to personal assessment where the GmbH is disregarded as a conduit.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For general GmbH formation, Germany permits a service address ('c/o' arrangement) and a registered office at the managing director's address or a virtual office provider, provided the address is not a pure letterbox that lacks any business substance. However, for an equity crowdfunding portal carrying out regulated underwriting or trading-like activities under BaFin supervision, a virtual office is materially non-compliant: the entity must maintain a physical place of business in Germany where the management board is accessible, where the IT infrastructure and server access are located, and where the statutory records, accounting books, and compliance documentation are kept for inspection. A virtual office may be accepted only as a secondary address for correspondence; the primary registered office must satisfy the substance requirement of the BaFin's supervisory practice and the German Statutory Seat Doctrine (effektiver Verwaltungssitz).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authority for corporate tax registration is the locally responsible Finanzamt (tax office) in the GmbH's registered or management location. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues the German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer or USt-IdNr.) and administers the BZSt online VAT identification application portal. Tax returns and registrations are generally transmitted through the ELSTER electronic tax portal operated by the German tax authorities. Customs registrations and export declarations are handled through German Customs (Generalzolldirektion) and the ATLAS system.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate and register the GmbH with the commercial register and obtain the trade registration, where applicable. 2. Submit the tax registration questionnaire for corporations (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung für Kapitalgesellschaften) electronically through Mein ELSTER to the responsible Finanzamt. 3. Provide the commercial-register extract, articles of association, registered address, management details, beneficial-owner information, business description, expected turnover and profit, bank information, accounting period, and details of related parties or foreign shareholders. 4. The Finanzamt assigns the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), which is used for corporate income tax, trade tax, and domestic VAT compliance. 5. Apply separately to the BZSt for the USt-IdNr.; the application may be made electronically using the domestic tax number and entity data. 6. A foreign entity without a German establishment applies to the Finanzamt responsible for non-resident taxpayers, generally the Finanzamt Hannover-Nord for many foreign businesses, subject to allocation rules and changes in administrative competence. 7. A foreign entity must register for German VAT where it carries out taxable German transactions for which it is liable for VAT and cannot use a valid reverse-charge or special-regime treatment. 8. An EORI number must be obtained from German Customs before conducting customs activities. VAT registration does not itself create an EORI number. 9. The entity must maintain German accounting and tax records sufficient to support VAT returns, invoices, export evidence, financial-account reporting, and regulated financial-intermediation activities. A tax number and VAT identification number do not constitute a financial-services licence; the crowdfunding business must separately satisfy applicable German and EU licensing requirements, including potentially the European Crowdfunding Service Providers Regulation and German supervisory requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is ordinarily subject to German corporate income tax at entity level, solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax. Germany has no general US-style check-the-box election permitting a GmbH to elect partnership or disregarded-entity treatment. The corporate tax election under Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act is available to qualifying partnerships and is not a classification election available to a GmbH. A GmbH may participate in a tax consolidation group (Organschaft) if statutory financial, ownership, profit-transfer, duration, and other requirements are satisfied; this is not a change of legal entity classification. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Section 4k of the German Income Tax Act can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences for imported mismatches, deduction/non-inclusion outcomes, or double deductions. These rules are mandatory and are not elective. Treaty, EU directive, participation-exemption, and controlled-foreign-company analyses apply separately from entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany has no general turnover threshold below which a business established in Germany may ignore VAT registration for taxable supplies. The domestic small-business exemption under Section 19 of the German VAT Act may apply instead of charging VAT if total turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year, subject to statutory conditions and the effect of the exemption election or waiver. The exemption is not available for transactions for which the business is liable under special rules and generally does not eliminate registration or reporting obligations where taxable cross-border transactions, imports, intra-Community transactions, or other registration triggers exist. For an EU-established supplier using the Union One Stop Shop, the common EU threshold for certain intra-Community distance sales and telecommunications, broadcasting, and electronically supplied services is EUR 10,000, subject to the applicable OSS conditions. Non-EU suppliers may be required to register from the first relevant taxable transaction unless a special regime or reverse-charge rule applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the European Union may qualify as VAT-exempt exports with input VAT deduction under Sections 4 number 1(a) and 6 of the German VAT Act if the goods are transported or dispatched to a third country and the statutory export, purchaser, and documentary-evidence conditions are satisfied. The supplier must retain reliable proof that the goods left the EU customs territory and must link the proof to the transaction and invoice. A service is not technically zero-rated merely because it is described as an export. For B2B services, the place of supply is generally the customer's business establishment under the German VAT Act and EU VAT rules; the service may therefore be outside German VAT, commonly with reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction. For B2C and special-category services, the place-of-supply rules must be applied separately. A crowdfunding portal's offshore-service treatment depends on the customer, contractual recipient, actual service, customer location, and any financial-intermediation exemption. Export or cross-border treatment does not apply automatically to services supplied to a German issuer or investor.

  • Commercial invoice and transaction contract or order documentation identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, and destination
  • Electronic export declaration submitted through the German Customs ATLAS system, where required, with the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Export Accompanying Document or equivalent customs export record and electronic exit confirmation from the EU customs territory
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the mode of transport, such as a bill of lading, sea waybill, air waybill, CMR consignment note, courier record, or carrier confirmation
  • Proof of delivery or destination outside the EU where required to complete the evidence chain
  • Evidence linking the exported goods to the invoice, order, customs declaration, and transport record
  • Purchaser and destination data, including third-country address and, where relevant, evidence that the purchaser is not established in Germany
  • Payment and accounting records supporting the transaction; an inward-remittance certificate is not generally a universal statutory condition for German export VAT exemption
  • For services, customer-location evidence, contractual documentation, VAT identification data where relevant, reverse-charge wording, and records demonstrating the applicable place-of-supply rule rather than a goods-export customs file

Germany does not operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or mandatory digital-stamping system. VAT returns, recapitulative statements, and most tax filings are submitted electronically through ELSTER. Since 1 January 2025, a domestic business must generally be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. Issuance is subject to transitional rules: paper invoices and non-structured electronic invoices, including PDF invoices, may generally continue during 2025; issuers with prior-year total turnover not exceeding EUR 800,000 may generally continue through 2027; other issuers may generally continue through 2026. From 2028, the statutory structured e-invoice requirements apply subject to exclusions and special rules. Accepted structured formats include compliant European-standard EN 16931 formats, including XRechnung and compliant ZUGFeRD versions. The 2025 receiving obligation does not generally cover B2C transactions, foreign-recipient transactions, or exempt categories outside the statutory domestic B2B scope. Invoices must contain the statutory German VAT particulars, including supplier and customer details, invoice date, sequential invoice number, description, consideration, applicable rate, VAT amount, exemption or reverse-charge wording, and relevant VAT identification numbers. Electronic records must be retained in accordance with German tax-record retention rules, generally eight years for invoices and ten years for specified accounting and tax records, subject to the applicable statutory category and retention period.

Germany has no general foreign-exchange control requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days. Export proceeds may generally be held abroad, subject to accounting, transfer-pricing, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, banking-reporting, and statistical-reporting obligations applicable to the facts. If export VAT exemption or input VAT recovery is claimed without sufficient evidence, the Finanzamt may deny or retrospectively revoke the exemption, assess output VAT, disallow or recover input VAT, impose interest under the German Fiscal Code, and issue amended assessments for prior periods. Incorrect invoices may require correction and repayment of unduly shown VAT. Intentional tax evasion may constitute a criminal offense under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code; reckless tax reduction may constitute an administrative offense under Section 378. Additional consequences may include late-filing penalties, late-payment surcharges, tax audit adjustments, joint or secondary liability in legally specified cases, customs penalties, repayment of improperly obtained refunds, and loss of interest or other tax benefits. Deliberate fabrication, alteration, or misuse of customs or transport evidence may create separate customs, fraud, document, AML, and regulatory exposure. The company should retain the complete evidence package and transaction-level audit trail for the applicable statutory retention periods.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return must be filed by 31 July of the following year; where prepared by a German tax advisor, automatic extension to 28/29 February of the second following year. Late filing triggers statutory penalties and assessment interest.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Company Identification Enter the GmbH's Steuernummer, fiscal year, legal form, registered office, and responsible Finanzamt. The GmbH files under its German tax identification; non-resident shareholders are not reporting parties on this form. Late filing surcharge under §152 AO is 0.25% of the assessed tax for each commenced month, capped at EUR 25,000. Late payment surcharge under §240 AO is 1% per month. Interest under §233a AO is 0.15% per month from the fifteenth month after the fiscal year-end. Non-payment may lead to seizure, enforcement costs, and director liability under §69 AO.

The GmbH must declare its worldwide income; no allocation of revenue to individual non-resident investors is required for corporate tax. Late filing surcharge under §152 AO is 0.25% of the assessed tax for each commenced month, capped at EUR 25,000. Late payment surcharge under §240 AO is 1% per month. Entity-level calculation unaffected by non-resident ownership. No separate form is required from foreign shareholders.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register) maintained by the Amtsgericht (Local Court) via the gemeinsames Registerportal (www.handelsregister.de); entry of the company is handled by the Registergericht. Foreign founders may also use the Online-Gründung (Online Incorporation) procedure under § 2 Abs. 3 GmbHG via a qualified notary (Notar) with video identification.

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG): §§ 2 (notarial formation), 3 (minimum capital), 5 (capital contributions), 7 (registration application), 8 (content of application), 9 (liability for false statements), 10 (registration effect), 11 (legal personality), 12 (company name), 13 (registered office), 35 (representation), 37 (shareholder resolutions), 45 (supervisory board), 46 (shareholder powers), 47 (voting), 48 (resolutions), 49 (calling meetings); Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB) §§ 106-113 (branch/registration duties), 238-263 (accounting).

Formation / constitutional filings

Handelsregisteranmeldung (Commercial Register Application) in notarized form, accompanied by Gesellschaftsvertrag (Articles of Association), Geschäftsführer-Bestellung (Managing Director Appointment), and Gesellschafterliste (Shareholder List).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

GmbH minimum share capital is EUR 25,000 (§ 5 GmbHG). At incorporation registration, at least EUR 12,500 (half) must be paid in cash as paid-up capital; contributions in kind must be fully contributed. The bank must confirm receipt in a blocked GmbH account (Geschäftskonto in Gründung) before the notarized register application is filed. The managing director must ensure that the paid-in amount is at the free disposal of the company and must confirm this to the notary under § 8 Abs. 2 GmbHG. Any capital not yet paid in remains a shareholder obligation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Brandschutzbescheinigung) from the local fire department; structural load inspection and building permit (Baugenehmigung) under Landesbauordnung; occupational safety clearance from Gewerbeaufsichtsamt; workplace risk assessment under ArbSchG; certification for DG storage and handling under GGVSEB/GGVSee; emergency response plan approved by the competent authority; insurance for environmental and DG liability.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification numbers, withholding tax certificates and central tax administration; locally competent Finanzamt for corporate income tax, trade tax, and payroll tax; Generalzolldirektion / Hauptzollamt for customs and export procedures. Digital portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); BZSt online portal (www.bzst.de); ATLAS/AES for customs declarations.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a German branch or GmbH complete mandatory electronic registration via ELSTER using Form 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) at the competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer (local tax number) for corporate income tax and trade tax. The VAT identification number (USt-IdNr) is applied for separately from BZSt using Form 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer'; foreign entrepreneurs without a domestic place of business may file directly with BZSt, supported by constitutional documents, proof of entity existence, director identification, and, where relevant, a German VAT representative. Processing typically takes 4–6 weeks; retroactive registration effective from the date of business commencement or VAT-liability event.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities establishing a German branch or GmbH complete mandatory electronic registration via ELSTER using Form 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) at the competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer (local tax number) for corporate income tax and trade tax. The VAT identification number (USt-IdNr) is applied for separately from BZSt using Form 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer'; foreign entrepreneurs without a domestic place of business may file directly with BZSt, supported by constitutional documents, proof of entity existence, director identification, and, where relevant, a German VAT representative. Processing typically takes 4–6 weeks; retroactive registration effective from the date of business commencement or VAT-liability event.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German law does not provide a check-the-box election. A GmbH is automatically treated as an entity-level taxpayer for corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Available statutory elections include: fiscal unity/Organschaft under §14 KStG and §4a GewStG; VAT grouping under §2 UStG; small-business VAT exemption under §19 UStG; interest limitation carryforward elections; and non-application of certain anti-hybrid rules only where EU law permits. Hybrid mismatch deductions are restricted under German ATAD implementation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany imposes no general VAT registration turnover threshold on established entrepreneurs; every taxable person must register. Domestic small-business exemption threshold is EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 current-year turnover. Non-resident suppliers making intra-EU distance sales or cross-border digital supplies to German consumers are subject to an EU-wide EUR 10,000 registration threshold under the One-Stop-Shop scheme. German B2B supplies by non-resident entities generally trigger reverse-charge instead of supplier registration where the German recipient is VAT-registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under §4 No. 1 letter a and §6 UStG applies to taxable supplies of goods physically delivered to a third-country destination where the exporter is the entrepreneur. For the dangerous-goods freight forwarding business model, zero-rating is also available for cross-border transport services and directly related ancillary services where the place of supply is outside Germany or where the recipient is a non-EU entrepreneur; the supplier must hold documentary evidence of export and commercial proof of the transport chain, including dangerous-goods declarations (IMDG/IATA/ADR) and customs exit confirmation.

  • ATLAS/AES electronic Export Customs Declaration and Export Accompanying Document (ABD)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list, including UN dangerous-goods classification and DG declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing dispatch to non-EU destination
  • Proof of export / Abgangsbestätigung (customs exit confirmation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT credit advice evidencing payment repatriation

Since 1 January 2025, all German domestic B2B invoices must be issued in a structured electronic format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD), and recipients must be able to receive such invoices. Obligation phases: 2025 e-invoice receipt capability; 2026–2027 transmission obligation; 2028 removal of transitional rules and mandatory PEPPOL-network exchange once real-time reporting is introduced. Germany does not currently operate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transaction-level tax-authority clearance; VAT returns and advance VAT returns are filed electronically through ELSTER APIs, and customs export declarations are processed electronically via ATLAS/AES.

Export zero-rating is denied if the required export evidence is incomplete or not retained at the time of invoicing. Retrospective VAT assessments, interest under §233a AO at 0.15% per month, and reversal of zero-rating can result. Fraudulent export documentation or deliberate misdeclaration constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO and negligent tax shortfall under §378 AO, with fines and imprisonment up to 5–10 years; customs investigation authorities (Zollfahndungsamt) may seize goods and records. Export records must be retained for 10 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under an open-market regime; the Euro is freely convertible and profit remittance does not require central bank approval. Remittance of distributable profits by a GmbH is restricted only by ordinary corporate law (balance sheet test and creditor protection under GmbHG Sections 29–30) and by EU/UN sanctions and dual-use export control screening. No exchange control permit, surrender obligation, or foreign investment approval is required for standard capital flows or dividends.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, no statutory conversion ratio, and no timeline for repatriation of export proceeds. However, under AWV Section 67, every resident enterprise must report cross-border payments and transfers exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank, generally by the 5th banking day of the following month, using the official AWV reporting channels (Z3/Z1 forms); export invoices issued to non-residents above EUR 12,500 may also trigger a monthly receivables report (Z4).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank) acting with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium legal viability. German-licensed EMIs (e.g. Pliant, Qonto, Airwallex via authorized partner banks) can open remote accounts, but DG/hazmat freight forwarding is classified as a high-risk sector under GwG (German Anti-Money Laundering Act), and most EMI program managers impose a blanket prohibition on dangerous goods logistics, dual-use transport, or sanctions-adjacent flows. E-money balances on EMI platforms are not bank deposits and enjoy no statutory deposit protection. Use an EMI only as an auxiliary collection corridor for low-value border-intra-EEA payments; the primary treasury and cross-border settlement backbone must be a fully regulated German commercial bank with correspondent banking access.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to a German GmbH are routed via SWIFT MT103/TARGET2 in EUR using the corporate's IBAN, with full SWIFT UETR traceability. The remitter must quote a compliant purpose code corresponding to the AWV classification of a direct investment (Direktinvestition) into the GmbH's Stammkapital; the receiving bank reports the transaction to Deutsche Bundesbank using Form Z1 (capital investment) and, where the shareholder is a related undertaking, further detailed reporting on Form Z3 applies. Payments above EUR 12,500 must be reported under AWV Section 67; for capital increases, the filing must accompany the commercial register (Handelsregister) resolution and the shareholder's contribution receipt. Verification at the receiving bank will require the anti-money-laundering source-of-funds declaration for the injecting shareholder, including bank statements, asset trail, and beneficial-owner identification.Intra-EEA/EUR disbursements for trade settlements flow through SEPA Credit Transfer (no SWIFT MT103 needed) with the corporate's IBAN and the creditor's IBAN, provided the creditor is an EEA bank. For non-EEA and non-EUR disbursements, payment is executed as a SWIFT MT103 via TARGET2 or correspondent banks, using UETR and sanctions screening by both instructing and beneficiary banks. For high-value dangerous goods cargo, documentary security is provided by commercial letters of credit issued under UCP 600/eUCP, advised and usually confirmed through a German commercial bank (e.g. Deutsche Bank, Commerzbank) with a confirming bank at the destination; presentation must include DG certificates, packing lists, IMDG/DGR declarations, and transport documents compliant with the letter of credit's terms.

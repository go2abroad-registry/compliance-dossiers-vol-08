Applicable AML statute: The principal statute is the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG), particularly Sections 1, 2, 3, 10 through 17, 19, and 20 through 26a. Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A GmbH must have a domestic registered office and a domestic business address capable of receiving service under GmbHG Sections 4a and 8 and HGB Section 29. A serviced or virtual office may be acceptable if it provides a genuine German business address, reliable receipt and forwarding of official mail, and access appropriate to the company's activities. A mailbox-only address, accommodation address, or address lacking real management access is not sufficient. For urban light-rail EPC, a virtual office is high-risk as the sole operating location because the business requires project management, engineering, procurement, health and safety, document control, subcontractor oversight, equipment and records management, and credible capability for public-sector procurement. A separate operational office or project facility is strongly recommended. Railway infrastructure construction may also require trade registration, building and site permits, railway-sector approvals, environmental approvals, public-procurement qualification, and technical authorizations depending on the project and contracting authority; incorporation alone does not satisfy those requirements.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office, responsible for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.); main portal: https://www.bzst.de. Local competent Finanzamt (municipal tax office) within whose district the GmbH's registered seat (Sitz) is located, responsible for Steuernummer (tax number), corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) and VAT assessments; national filing portal: ELSTER (https://www.elster.de). For foreign non-resident suppliers, the competent authority is the BZSt for VAT registration via the Form 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer USt-IdNr.' (Form USt 1 TU) and the Finanzamt Neubrandenburg for VAT assessment matters.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarize articles of association, deposit the mandatory minimum capital of €25,000 (at least 50% paid-in, i.e., €12,500) with an EU/EEA credit institution; register the GmbH with the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the Local Court (Amtsgericht). Step 2: Within 4 weeks of commercial commencement, submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (statutory questionnaire on tax registration) electronically via ELSTER to the competent local Finanzamt, including the shareholders, directors (Geschäftsführer), projected turnover and profit expectations; the Finanzamt then issues the local Steuernummer (Form USt-Aufnahme) and determines trade tax assessment rates (Hebesätze). Step 3: Apply for the VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) with the BZSt via ELSTER or Form 'USt 1 TU' (for foreign entities) relying on §27a UStG; issuance within 1–6 weeks after identity checks (ID, extract from Commercial Register, proof of business activity). Step 4 (foreign entities without German establishment): simplified online registration under §18 Abs. 4c UStG via BZSt, requiring proof of VAT identity in the country of origin, description of the intended German supplies, and nomination of a German tax representative (fiscal representative) if required by the local Finanzamt. Note: an operating GmbH is VAT-registered from the date of legal effectiveness without applying any small-business threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a check-the-box system; legal form determines classification. A GmbH is always a body corporate (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to entity-level corporate income tax at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax (typically 14–17% effective). Available statutory elections: (1) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under §§14–17 KStG and §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, by which the GmbH (as controlled body) is fiscally consolidated into the controlling parent for corporate income tax (profit/loss transfer agreement plus financial, economic, and organisational integration) and for VAT groups (unified taxable person), requiring a written application to the local Finanzamt; (2) election under §4k EStG to neutralize hybrid mismatches by declaring a foreign permanent establishment or hybrid entity German taxable for asymmetric purposes; (3) real-estate election under §9 UStG to waive the VAT exemption of real property leases for input-VAT deduction; (4) option for input-VAT deduction on intra-community acquisitions and small-entrepreneur status under §19 UStG (available only to natural persons/partnerships, not to GmbH).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business (Kleinunternehmer) threshold under §19 UStG: €22,000 prior-year gross turnover cap and €50,000 current-year forecast cap. Foreign/non-resident taxpayers: no exemption threshold — any taxable supply in Germany triggers mandatory VAT registration. A German-resident GmbH is VAT-liable from the first invoice without applying the small-business exemption; Art. 42 of the German Fiscal Code (AO) also attributes registration to a fixed establishment in Germany if service-performance nexus is established.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies under §4 Nr. 1(a) and §6 UStG to 'Ausfuhrlieferungen' (export supplies) where the goods are physically transported or dispatched by the supplier or the foreign customer to a destination outside the EU customs territory; the export must be fully substantiated within 3 months of removal from the supplier's possession, and the customs-approved exporter status or direct export evidence must be retained. Intra-Community supplies (§6a UStG) are likewise zero-rated when the customer discloses a valid USt-IdNr. assigned by another Member State and transport to that Member State is documented. EPC services to non-EU infrastructure sites (e.g., installation of track laying and overhead catenary systems for urban light rail transit) fall outside the German place-of-supply rules (§3a Abs. 3 Nr. 1 UStG) for services connected with immovable property located abroad — thus outside the scope of German VAT rather than formally zero-rated; ancillary services B2B to non-EU recipients follow the recipient-location rule. Where the construction site is German soil, the supply is subject to German VAT at 19% and, if the recipient is an entrepreneur (including a German public authority under §13b UStG), the VAT is reverse-charged to the recipient on the invoice. There is no statutory German 180-day FX-repatriation obligation for export proceeds; the 180-day value below denotes the standard documentary retention and risk-scrutiny window for export substantiation in typical German export transactions, but the lawful rule is the 3-month export-evidence deadline under §6 UStG and the general 10-year retention under §147 AO.

  • Electronic customs export declaration (ATLAS-Ausfuhranmeldung) with Master Reference Number (MRN) and the customs house printout evidencing release for export
  • Export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument, ABD) / Export Accompanying Document (EAD) with the customs office's voice-automated confirmation or export certificate (Ausfuhrbescheinigung)
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement) or non-negotiable sea waybill; for land transport, CMR consignment note with international customs stamps and driver customs declaration; for air, Air Waybill (AWB) showing physical dispatch from the EU
  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung) stating the export-specific wording, Incoterms 2020 rule, discount rates, and statement '0% VAT pursuant to §4 Nr.1(a) UStG'
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 payment confirmation / credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency or EUR consideration from the non-EU customer
  • Delivery note (Lieferschein), project bill of quantities (BOQ), and the underlying EPC contract signed with the non-resident client confirming transfer of title and risk to the foreign purchaser

Mandatory electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) for domestic B2B supplies under §14 UStG as amended by the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025, every taxable person must be able to receive e-invoices in the structured formats XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0.1; from 1 January 2027, taxable persons with prior-year turnover above €800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all taxable persons must issue e-invoices. No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is in force for VAT; monthly/quarterly VAT returns (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung) are transmitted electronically via ELSTER by the 10th day of the following month; Intra-Community statistical reports (Intrastat) use a separate digital channel. From 1 January 2026, payment service providers (PSPs) must transmit cross-border B2C payment data (CESOP/DA-DAC7) to the Federal Central Tax Office for stored and aggregated identification of suppliers. The 2024/25 ViDA proposal, once adopted, will introduce mandatory e-invoicing and domestic digital reporting within 10 working days, with a phased post-2030 application to Germany; currently only the B2B receipt mandate applies.

Export zero-rating is a priority German audit area. Defective or missing export proof triggers retroactive VAT assessment at 19% on the gross export consideration, plus default interest of 0.5% per month (§233a AO; total up to 5 years), late-payment surcharges of 1% per month capped at 10% (§240 AO), and denial of input VAT deduction. Fraudulent or grossly negligent overstatement of zero-rating claims constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO for the GmbH's Geschäftsführer personally, punishable with imprisonment of up to 5 years (aggravated cases 6 months–10 years) and an unlimited fine; the tax authority may also impose enforcement interest and issue a personal liability notice (§71 AO) against the director for the evaded VAT. In addition, the Finanzamt applies a rebuttable presumption of sham (Scheingeschäft) where funds are not repatriated within the 180-day window set in the export documentation, leading to clawback of the zero-rating, disallowance of bad-debt relief, and initiation of criminal proceedings, including under the anti-money-laundering remit of the FIU. All export evidence must be retained for 10 years (§147 AO) and produced on demand; failure triggers the same revocation and penalty consequences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany, as an EU member state, maintains full current and capital account convertibility under Articles 63-66 TFEU. There are no exchange-control approvals for profit remittance or capital transfers. Practical hurdles are limited to: (i) mandatory statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank for all payments from non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500; (ii) German withholding tax on dividend distributions (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 26.375%, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying corporate parents, subject to conditions); (iii) corporate law capital maintenance rules under GmbHG Sections 30-31, which prohibit distributions that would reduce the guaranteed capital. Profit remittance is otherwise freely permitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender or conversion requirement. Exporters may retain, hold, and freely dispose of foreign currency receipts. The only obligation is statistical in nature: any payment received from a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments purposes by the 5th day of the calendar month following the month of receipt (AWV reporting obligation). No conversion ratio is applicable.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank, Frankfurt am Main) together with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the Federal Ministry of Finance. The foreign exchange administration is codified in the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as the primary treasury channel. An offshore digital EMI lacking a German banking license or EU/EEA passport cannot operate within the German Banking Act (KWG), cannot accept deposits repayable on demand, and has no access to TARGET2 or statutory deposit protection. Such entities are unsuitable for escrow accounts, performance bond issuance, or government-bond-secured infrastructure contracts. An EEA-passported EMI may legally provide payment accounts in Germany after notifying BaFin under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), but it remains a payment institution rather than a bank and cannot issue credit or bank guarantees. Viable role is limited to low-value operational disbursements, not treasury settlement or liquidity management.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an inbound equity injection from a foreign shareholder, the compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 for cross-border settlement or SEPA for euro transfers within the EEA. Field 70 of the MT103 must state the purpose in a verifiable manner, e.g., 'CAPITAL INCREASE IN GERMAN GMBH - EINLAGE GEMAESS NOTARIELLER URKUNDE'. In ISO 20022 payment formats, the purpose code 'EQUI' (equity securities) or 'CLEQ' (cleared equity settlement) should be used; for shareholder loans, 'LEND' is appropriate. The receiving German bank will trigger the Deutsche Bundesbank reporting obligation when the payment exceeds EUR 12,500, and the company must submit the corresponding monthly AWV report. Due-diligence verification requires submission of the notarial capital increase deed, current Commercial Register extract, UBO declarations and source-of-funds documentation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility as the primary treasury channel. An offshore digital EMI lacking a German banking license or EU/EEA passport cannot operate within the German Banking Act (KWG), cannot accept deposits repayable on demand, and has no access to TARGET2 or statutory deposit protection. Such entities are unsuitable for escrow accounts, performance bond issuance, or government-bond-secured infrastructure contracts. An EEA-passported EMI may legally provide payment accounts in Germany after notifying BaFin under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), but it remains a payment institution rather than a bank and cannot issue credit or bank guarantees. Viable role is limited to low-value operational disbursements, not treasury settlement or liquidity management.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (German Körperschaftsteuer at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT liability; municipalities additionally levy trade tax, producing a combined effective headline rate of approximately 28% to 33%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital gains withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) on dividend distributions plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, producing statutory effective withholding of 26.375%. A subsequent trade tax charge is not remitted by the distributing GmbH, but foreign corporate shareholders can invoke DTA exemptions/reductions (typically 0-15% depending on DTA and shareholding percentage; EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive may eliminate the tax where the parent holds at least 10% for 24 consecutive months and has an economic substance claim).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full German Local File documentation is expected for all cross-border controlled transactions exceeding €5,000,000 in annual volume for procurement and construction services and for substantial intercompany financing. The Master File is required where the consolidated German group revenue exceeds €100,000,000. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more. For an urban light-rail EPC GmbH, the local documentation must cover: procurement of catenary materials and rolling stock components, subcontractor margins, equipment rental, software licensing, engineering services, guarantee fees, and intra-group cash pooling.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain separate Economic Substance Regulations of the UAE/OECD format; instead, the German tax administration applies: 1) §42 AO anti-abuse rule; 2) §50d(3) EStG denial of treaty benefits to entities without economic substance; 3) BEPS Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test; and 4) German CFC rules under §§7-14 AStG for passive income routed through low-tax jurisdictions. A GmbH engaged in EPC activities must maintain qualified employees, real office or site management, executive decision-making, books and records, and a distinct bank account. Merely holding contracts and invoicing from a foreign principal while German site staff perform the work will cause the Finanzamt to attribute a German permanent establishment and impose unlimited German corporate income tax, trade tax, and wage withholding obligations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abusive Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under §370 AO attracts a term of imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine; especially serious cases carry ten years imprisonment. Assessed taxes are enforced with interest at 0.5% per month (Hinterziehungszinsen), and the company may lose public procurement eligibility and be excluded from German infrastructure tenders. Fines and forfeiture of the unpaid tax amounts can equal or exceed the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Managing directors personally liable for taxes due from the company when they deliberately execute or tolerate the evasion under §69 AO; the court may issue travel bans and European arrest warrants, the company veil does not protect the director from criminal conviction, and the director may be subject to civil claw-back claims by the company and tax authority for restitution of diverted funds.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: The tax authority can estimate income under §162 AO and impose a transfer pricing surcharge of up to 10% of the adjustment (minimum €1,000, maximum €50,000) for non-documentation; penalties rise when the taxpayer fails to cooperate. Interest at 0.5% per month accrues on tax deficiencies, and the entity is denied deduction for the related payment if the foreign recipient lacks beneficial ownership and substance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be haled personally for the additional taxes if they signed false certifications of documentation, co-mingled company funds, or used the entity as a vehicle for fraud; personal liability reaches civil restitution, can trigger a Schengen-area entry ban, and can lead to imprisonment for perjury or fraudulent certification.
Dividend, Royalty and Interest Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: The German payer becomes jointly and severally liable for the withheld and unpaid WHT plus solidarity surcharge; late payment surcharges and 0.5% monthly interest apply. If the foreign recipient is a shell, the exemption certificate is denied retroactively, and the GmbH is also assessed for wage-related taxes if expatriate engineers were placed on a foreign payroll without German register filings.

Director personal liability: The director is personally liable under §§44a/50d EStG and §69 AO for unremitted withholding tax, and can be pursued for civil restitution in the collective insolvency process; a managing director who instructs the company to omit WHT on a €10 million dividend faces criminal proceedings for withholding tax embezzlement and a travel ban pending trial.
Shell Company, Permanent Establishment Concealment, and AML Transparency Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities pierce the arrangement: €42 AO disregards the shell structure, a permanent establishment is treated as created at the EPC site, and income is reattributed to that PE. Failure to file beneficial ownership disclosures attracts pecuniary penalties up to €100,000, and the entity may be barred from bank financing and public tenders. Civil courts additionally allow creditors to trace assets to the foreign parent when the GmbH is merely a sufficient, under-capitalized facade.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell GmbH face personal liability for tortious abuse of legal form; they cannot rely on limited liability where the company is manifestly unfit to perform its obligations. German enforcement authorities may issue Schengen-wide travel bans to ensure that the responsible director does not flee while tax and AML investigations are pending; conviction can result in imprisonment for tending to be alongside AML charges under the German Geldwäschegesetz and Criminal Code.

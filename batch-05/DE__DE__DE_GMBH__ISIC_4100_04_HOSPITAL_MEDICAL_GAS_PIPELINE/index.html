Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Official Commercial Register portal: Handelsregisterbekanntmachungen and register information through www.handelsregister.de; electronic filings are submitted through the competent local court's register system. There is no single nationwide statutory name-reservation portal for GmbHs.

Name reservation / lock period

If an IHK or notary advises that the name is problematic, obtain the written objection, identify the exact issue under Sections 18 and 30 HGB or applicable sector law, and submit an amended name or legal analysis through the notary. If the register court formally rejects or refuses registration, request the written order and file the available legal remedy, normally an immediate complaint (sofortige Beschwerde) under the German Code of Civil Procedure provisions applicable to register matters and the FamFG, generally within the period stated in the order. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital of EUR 25,000; at least EUR 12,500 (50%) must be paid up in cash and deposited in a German escrow bank account before applying for registration, or full contribution in kind if provided for in the Articles. Notary verifies deposit via bank confirmation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office, Berlin/Bonn (VAT identification number USt-IdNr. allocation); local Finanzamt (Steuernummer tax number, tax assessment, VAT returns); electronic filing portal: ELSTER / Mein ELSTER (https://www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign GmbH must file the official registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) — paper form or digitally via ELSTER — with the locally competent Finanzamt before commencing business, stating legal form, place of management, German permanent establishment, forecast turnover, and VAT status; the Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer within approximately 2-8 weeks; a separate online application to BZSt is required for the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr. under § 27a UStG), typically issued within 2 weeks; non-resident entities with no German PE but with taxable German-source activities must also register and may appoint a fiscal representative where required for VAT enforcement.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign GmbH must file the official registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) — paper form or digitally via ELSTER — with the locally competent Finanzamt before commencing business, stating legal form, place of management, German permanent establishment, forecast turnover, and VAT status; the Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer within approximately 2-8 weeks; a separate online application to BZSt is required for the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr. under § 27a UStG), typically issued within 2 weeks; non-resident entities with no German PE but with taxable German-source activities must also register and may appoint a fiscal representative where required for VAT enforcement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists; a GmbH is per se a capital company subject to Körperschaftsteuer (15%), Gewerbesteuer (approx. 14-17% depending on municipality), and Solidaritätszuschlag (5.5% surcharge on corporate tax). Available statutory elections include: corporate tax grouping (Organschaft) under § 14 KStG with a profit-and-loss pooling agreement for corporate/trade tax/VAT consolidation; fiscal-transparency elections for certain hybrid entities under ATAD implementation (§ 4k EStG tie-breaker notices for hybrid mismatches); and reverse-charge allocation choices under § 13b UStG for construction works. No direct pass-through election is available for a GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general domestic registration threshold — any entrepreneur (§ 2 UStG, including foreign GmbH with domestic taxable activity) must register from the first taxable transaction. The intra-EU distance-sales threshold for OSS is €10,000; the small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) is €22,000 for 2024 and €25,000 from 2025 but is generally unavailable to a commercial GmbH. B2B supplies and hospital EPC services to taxable customers are subject to mandatory registration with possible reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0% VAT) applies to export supplies under § 4 Nr. 1a and § 6 UStG where goods are physically removed from the EU to a third-country territory, and to intra-Community supplies under § 4 Nr. 1b and § 6a UStG where the customer's valid VAT ID is held and the goods are dispatched to another Member State. For a hospital medical-gas pipeline EPC project, exported equipment and manifold parts qualify only if the GmbH (exporter) or a directly related consignor proves physical export; installation, engineering, and on-site commissioning services connected to foreign immovable property are deemed supplied at the place of the property (§ 3a UStG) and therefore fall outside German VAT; B2B services rendered to foreign entrepreneurs are generally reverse-charged to the recipient, with supporting contracts and service descriptions retained.

  • ATLAS electronic customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / Export Accompanying Document EAD / Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) per §§ 8-10 UStDV — stamped customs certification, electronic customs manifest, or equivalent confirmed export declaration
  • Delivery and installation acceptance certificate for staged medical gas pipeline works
  • Intrastat declaration for intra-EU dispatches in excess of the dispatch threshold (when applicable)
  • EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung) for zero-rated intra-Community supplies
  • AWV export-payment evidence and Deutsche Bundesbank reporting records (Zahlungsmeldung / Forderungsmeldung) verifying repatriation

No government-run real-time fiscal clearance system (unlike the Italian SdI model). However, the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz) mandates structured electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung in XRechnung / ZUGFeRD formats): from 1 January 2025 all domestic B2B invoices must be receivable in a structured format; from 1 January 2027 structured issuance becomes mandatory for businesses with annual turnover above €800,000, and from 1 January 2028 for all domestic B2B transactions. VAT pre-returns (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung) must be submitted electronically via ELSTER within 10 days after each month or calendar quarter; EU VAT-in-the-Digital-Age (ViDA) near-real-time digital reporting (RTIR) is anticipated by approximately 2030-2035.

German tax authorities may conduct Außenprüfung (external audit) and retrospectively correct zero-rated invoices where export evidence is missing, incomplete, or contradictory; VAT is clawed back with interest of 6% per annum under § 233a AO and an additional surcharge of up to 25% of the assessed shortfall under § 152 AO. Fraudulent export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under §§ 369, 370 AO, punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to 10 years; the limitation period extends to 10 years for intentional evasion (§ 169 AO); BZSt, Customs, and the Gemeinsame Finanzermittlungsgruppe coordinate criminal prosecution and may invalidate the VAT identification number with immediate effect, including joint-and-several liability for invoiced VAT where the exporter knew the supply was not genuinely zero-rated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unqualified convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. For a German GmbH there are no exchange-control approvals, no mandatory central-bank localization of funds, no capital-account conversion restrictions, and no limits on profit or dividend repatriation. The only binding handrails are: (i) a statistical reporting obligation on each cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 or equivalent, filed by the German resident via the Bundesbank's AWV Section 67 reporting portal; (ii) automatic bank-side reporting of inbound/outbound flows by the German account-holding bank; (iii) active KYC duty on the bank to identify the economic purpose behind every inbound capital or third-party payment under the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG); and (iv) dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders attract German Kapitalertragsteuer at 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 26.375%), reduced or eliminated under Germany's double-taxation treaties or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) when the recipient holds the requisite qualifying shareholding and substance. In the hospital medical-gas pipeline business, domestic healthcare-authority receivables are EUR-denominated and settle inside SEPA/TARGET2 without conversion risk; third-country hospital contracts denominated in USD, CHF, or GBP require only standard market-conform conversion at the anchor bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, no designated-account restriction, no repatriation timeline, and no minimum retention period. All export receipts from medical-gas pipeline installation, hospital engineering, or component supply remain fully disposable in the GmbH's ordinary operating accounts, in EUR or foreign currency at the exporter's election. Residual obligations are purely informational and precautionary: (a) AWV Section 67 reporting to the Bundesbank for each inbound payment over EUR 12,500 (the report reference must be retained for five years); (b) monthly/quarterly Intrastat declarations where intra-EU dispatches exceed EUR 500,000 in the reporting year (arrivals threshold EUR 800,000), and Extrastat export declarations through customs; (c) the receiving bank will screen the counterparty for EU/UN sanctions and may request the underlying EPC contract, delivery notes, or invoices to evidence the commercial purpose of the funds; and (d) where controlled components (e.g., cryogenic valves, high-pressure oxygen regulators) are incorporated, BAFA export-clearance or a no-licence confirmation is advisable. Export proceeds themselves are never subject to conversion, custody, or surrender requirements.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank, Frankfurt am Main) is the statutory exchange-control statistics and cross-border payment surveillance authority under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz - AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV); banking supervision and anti-money-laundering conduct supervision for credit and payment institutions rests with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin, Bonn/Frankfurt). The Bundesbank operates the German TARGET2 settlement node, hosts the AWV cross-border payment reporting gateway, and administers the national EMIR trade-repository interface for derivative instruments. Export-control licensing and sanctions enforcement sit with the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) and the European Commission, layered over the Bundesbank operational framework.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: €1 million annual turnover threshold; Master File: €50 million consolidated group revenue

Economic substance scope: Germany enforces economic substance via General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules, and BEPS Action 5; no dedicated ESR but substance required for tax benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Up to 5 years imprisonment and fines up to €1 million or 50% of tax evaded

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability, restitution of evaded taxes, possible imprisonment and professional bans
Aggressive Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions, back taxes, penalties up to 5% of turnover, interest

Director personal liability: Personal liability for additional taxes, potential disqualification from management roles
Failure to Maintain Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax deductions, recharacterization, penalties, and potential loss of treaty benefits

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil, civil claims, criminal liability in extreme cases

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt – Federal Central Tax Office, portal www.bzst.de) as central office for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) and for centralized VAT statistics; the locally competent Finanzamt (local tax office), accessed through the ELSTER online platform (www.elster.de), as main tax authority for the Steuernummer, corporate income tax and trade tax; Generalzolldirektion (German customs, portal www.zoll.de) for EORI registration, export declarations and customs audit matters.

E-government filing portal

For a German-resident GmbH, including one with foreign shareholders: (1) complete commercial register registration (Handelsregister) and business registration (Gewerbeanmeldung); (2) file the statutory registration questionnaire 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the locally competent Finanzamt, by paper or via ELSTER, to obtain the taxable-persons Steuernummer (corporate income tax and trade tax, VAT advance return account); (3) apply online to BZSt (through ELSTER or the BZSt online form) for the EU VAT identification number under § 27a UStG; BZSt typically issues the USt-IdNr. within 4-6 weeks; (4) obtain an EORI number (DE-prefix) from Generalzolldirektion before any customs declarations; (5) if the GmbH operates through a branch or permanent establishment in Germany, the non-resident shareholder registers that PE separately in the Finanzamt district. Since the GmbH is itself a German taxpayer, no separate fiscal representative appointment is required for ordinary VAT registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – a GmbH is per-se a corporation taxable as a separate person (Trennungsprinzip), never a pass-through entity; it is resident in Germany when its seat or place of management (Ort der Geschäftsleitung) is in German territory; the residence carries unlimited worldwide tax liability (Welteinkommensprinzip) with foreign tax credit relief; no pass-through election exists; no tax-exempt-zone status is applicable; headline profit tax burden of approximately 30%, calculated as 15% corporate income tax plus 0.825% solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax on the order of 14-17% depending on the municipal Hebesatz. — For a German-resident GmbH, including one with foreign shareholders: (1) complete commercial register registration (Handelsregister) and business registration (Gewerbeanmeldung); (2) file the statutory registration questionnaire 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the locally competent Finanzamt, by paper or via ELSTER, to obtain the taxable-persons Steuernummer (corporate income tax and trade tax, VAT advance return account); (3) apply online to BZSt (through ELSTER or the BZSt online form) for the EU VAT identification number under § 27a UStG; BZSt typically issues the USt-IdNr. within 4-6 weeks; (4) obtain an EORI number (DE-prefix) from Generalzolldirektion before any customs declarations; (5) if the GmbH operates through a branch or permanent establishment in Germany, the non-resident shareholder registers that PE separately in the Finanzamt district. Since the GmbH is itself a German taxpayer, no separate fiscal representative appointment is required for ordinary VAT registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a GmbH is per-se a corporation taxable as a separate person (Trennungsprinzip), never a pass-through entity; it is resident in Germany when its seat or place of management (Ort der Geschäftsleitung) is in German territory; the residence carries unlimited worldwide tax liability (Welteinkommensprinzip) with foreign tax credit relief; no pass-through election exists; no tax-exempt-zone status is applicable; headline profit tax burden of approximately 30%, calculated as 15% corporate income tax plus 0.825% solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax on the order of 14-17% depending on the municipal Hebesatz.

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. The mechanically available elections, all elective and none making the GmbH transparent, are: (a) corporate income tax and trade tax Organschaft under §§ 14, 17 KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 GewStG, requiring financial integration, management subordination, and a valid notarized profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) with 75% shareholder assent; (b) VAT Organschaft under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, merging the GmbH with its German parent into one VAT taxpayer; (c) election to apply German tonnage taxation under § 5a EStG, which is unavailable to tug and salvage vessels because they do not qualify as commercial cargo-carrier Handelschiffahrt engaged in international goods or passenger transport; (d) no elective option to zero-rate maritime services – VAT exemption for towage and salvage services follows statute and mandatory evidence rules, not an election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for foreign entities or non-resident suppliers: any taxable German supply, including B2B maritime towage and salvage services physically performed within German territorial waters, creates an immediate German VAT registration obligation regardless of turnover volume. For resident small businesses the substantive VAT exemption threshold is €22,000 of taxable turnover for 2024 and €25,000 from 1 January 2025, but this Kleinunternehmer exemption is practically inapplicable to a GmbH making exclusively B2B marine services. The €10,000 OSS distance-selling threshold is irrelevant to the offshore tug and salvage service model.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Two regimes apply. (A) For goods: zero-rating under § 4 Nr. 1 UStG in conjunction with § 6 UStG requires that the goods are physically shipped to a third-country (non-EU) destination, that the exporter has filed an ATLAS/AES export declaration, that transport and commercial documents evidence actual departure to the third country, and that the goods are not re-imported; all probative evidence must be assembled in the Beleg- und Buchnachweis. (B) For offshore towage and salvage services: tax-exempt treatment is available under §§ 4 Nr. 2, 8 UStG for services directly serving the direct needs of seagoing vessels or their cargo, provided the vessel is used in international navigation and the service is supplied to the vessel operator; in many cross-border B2B contracts, however, the place of supply is in the recipient's country under § 3a Abs. 2 UStG, so German VAT does not arise at all and the foreign customer applies reverse charge. Claiming the exemption or proving non-German place of supply is a documentary question in each contract, not a matter of turnover size.

  • Customs export declaration filed in ATLAS/AES (Ausfuhranmeldung) containing the MRN and evidencing third-country exit
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill showing the non-EU destination port and shipper/consignee particulars
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms, export declaration clause and vessel/operation references
  • Packing list and freight or insurance transport contract covering the specific consignment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Zahlungsempfangsbestätigung or bank payment confirmation under the Außenwirtschaftsverkehr reporting rules) proving FX repatriation to Germany
  • Carrier certificate, dispatch log, GPS record or salvage operation report evidencing performance of the service outside the customs territory where relevant

Germany does not impose a real-time continuous-transaction-control (CTC) clearance model and no digital stamping or central fiscal API validation exists. Instead, structured electronic invoicing is being phased in: from 1 January 2025 every taxable person that issues domestic B2B invoices must be able to receive e-invoices in XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x formats; issuance becomes mandatory from 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year turnover exceeding €800,000 and, for all remaining taxable persons, from 1 January 2028. E-invoices must be exchanged through EDI, portal or machine-readable XML without prior tax-authority validation; the invoices must be transmitted in a format guaranteeing authenticity of origin and integrity of content. Export invoices to third-country recipients need not comply with XRechnung but must still satisfy the documentary evidence and bookkeeping requirements (Beleg- und Buchnachweis).

Export zero-rating claims are a priority audit target in German Betriebsprüfung (tax audit). If the export documentation bundle is incomplete, untimely or internally inconsistent, the Finanzamt will issue a retrospective VAT assessment: rescission of the exemption, clawback of the VAT previously not charged, reversal of input VAT credits and monthly default interest of 0.5% under § 233a AO from the original due date. Deliberate falsification of export evidence constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment up to five years, and in aggravated commercial-scale cases from six months to ten years, with unlimited confiscation of the VAT amount. under § 370 Abs. 3 AO and related provisions, third-party organisers and invoice-chain participants may be held jointly and severally liable; under § 25f UStG transporters and warehouse keepers can be liable in cross-border VAT fraud. Retroactive reassessments may reach relevance under § 173 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 AO whenever new facts regarding the export movement come to light, and the burden of proving third-country arrival remains permanently on the exporter.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account. Germany applies no exchange-control licensing, no currency allocation ceilings, and no prior approval for inbound or outbound payments in EUR, USD, or other freely convertible currencies. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and repatriation of loan principal and interest may proceed without FX authorization, subject to German tax compliance: corporate income tax (15%) plus solidarity surcharge, municipal trade tax (typically 14–17% combined) and, for dividends to non-resident shareholders, 25% capital-yield withholding tax (plus surcharge where applicable) reduced under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, German tax treaties, or the Interest and Royalty Directive for qualifying intercompany flows. Residual hurdles are not FX-related: (i) statistical registration of cross-border balances; (ii) EU financial sanctions and maritime embargo obligations; and (iii) substance, interest-barrier (section 8a KStG), anti-hybrid, and CFC rules applicable to the GmbH.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists in Germany: export proceeds in any currency may be retained, held in foreign-currency accounts, or converted at commercial bank rates without any mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline. The sole binding requirement under AWV Section 67 is regulatory reporting: each receipt from a non-resident for exported towage and salvage services exceeding EUR 12,500 (or the foreign-currency equivalent) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics by the second business day after receipt, using the electronic statistics portal and the applicable transaction code for maritime transport services (C51) or other commercial services (C52). Banks will independently confirm the purpose code on the MT103/SEPA message and retain the underlying commercial contract.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), exercising prudential and statistical supervision in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) as the euro-area monetary authority. Foreign exchange monitoring and cross-border payment surveillance derive from the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) administered by the Bundesbank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate as a primary clearing channel; moderate as a supplementary collection rail. German payment institutions under PSD2 (BaFin-licensed or EU passporting) may open and maintain pass-through float accounts for the GmbH but may not accept deposits on their own balance sheet, issue credit, or support ship-mortgage-linked escrow structures. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., pan-European entities licensed in Cyprus, Lithuania, or non-EU centers) without a German-custody agent arrangement and a German IBAN are not viable as a sole treasury bank because: (i) German payroll and VAT-relevant vendor payments require a German IBAN with BIC for e-invoicing (XRechnung) and SEPA credit-transfer processing; (ii) BaFin-supervised banks classify such EMI relationships as potential substitute-payment-account red flags under GwG Section 25e; and (iii) commercial letters of credit and Lloyd's Standard Form escrow mechanics are outside the EMI license perimeter. Recommendation: engage a BaFin-regulated German EMI only for receivables collection of small salvage deposits and card-based port fees, while all operational and escrow balances reside at a Tier-1 bank.

Root cause: USD-denominated contract exposure and FX hedging operations (perceived as speculative treasury activity)

Defense evidence: Board-approved FX risk management policy (hedging only of contracted net exposures, no open speculation); ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or GMRA plus credit support annex if hedging is executed with the Tier-1 bank; underlying USD receivables ledger and aging schedule matched to the hedging notional; monthly mark-to-market and hedge-effectiveness assessment under IFRS 9 cash-flow hedge rules; and a confirmation letter limiting hedging tenors to a maximum of 24 months and requiring dual-signatory execution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholders inject funds by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own account (not a third-party payer) to the GmbH's Tier-1 German IBAN in EUR (converting USD at the bank's commercial rate if the facility is USD-denominated). Bank-level verification requires: (i) a signed capital contribution resolution or shareholder loan agreement; (ii) UBO and source-of-funds confirmation; and (iii) matching of the MT103 remittance fields to the corporate authorization. Regulatory verification is with Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV Section 66(1): the GmbH (as resident) must report the transaction electronically no later than the second business day after receipt, using the Bundesbank's balance-of-payments statistics portal. Purpose codes: the 'S' capital-transaction series applies, with 'S1' for equity contributions and direct-investment participations (including share premium and capital reserves) and the defined Bundesbank loan code (S-series) for shareholder advances and intercompany credits; a 'G' series is not used for capital injections. The bank will also confirm the statutory purpose-code allocation in the underlying ISO 20022 message (pain.001) to avoid misclassification. Funds flow: the inbound payment is credited to the Capital Escrow Account; release to the Operational Account follows an approved board disbursement schedule and twin-authorization rule; no early withdrawal is permissible for equity-contributed escrow amounts before the relevant vessel or project security is in place.Trade settlements use: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for direct payables to non-EU vendors (fuel suppliers, subcontract tug operators, spare-part houses, classification societies, salvage security providers); (ii) SEPA Credit Transfers (ISO 20022 pain.001.001.03) via TARGET2 for EU/EBA counterparties; (iii) irrevocable commercial letters of credit under UCP 600 (ICC Publication No. 600) opened by Commerzbank or Deutsche Bank for vessel acquisitions, dry-dock works, and major equipment; and (iv) documentary collections (URC 522) for low-value towage subcontracting settlements. Every outbound reference must carry the correct Bundesbank purpose code: 'C51' (maritime transport service), 'C52' (port, ship, and auxiliary services), 'C60' (repair and maintenance of ships), 'D1' (imports of goods), or the applicable 'S'-series capital code for outbound loans, and must be reported to the Bundesbank once the individual amount exceeds EUR 12,500. Document retention obligations: invoice, purchase order, bill of lading or port call report, Lloyd's salvage security receipt, and the UETR reference. Disbursement approval requires a two-person rule, dual signatures for amounts over EUR 50,000 or the USD equivalent, and a standing instruction that escrow balances may never be used for working-capital payments except under the approved project budget.EUR/USD outright foreign-exchange forward contracts (Devisen-Termingeschäfte) with tenors of up to 24 months, concluded on a delivery basis for hedging contracted USD-denominated salvage awards, towage hire, and bail payments.FX swaps (spot/forward and tom/next structures) for short-dated USD-EUR liquidity rebalancing and value-date management, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with the Tier-1 bank.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) to align USD-denominated project debt or shareholder loans with the EUR reporting currency and hedge refinancing risk over the life of long-term salvage contracts.EUR/USD currency options (straight or zero-cost collar structures) limited to contingent exposures, in particular Lloyd's Open Form security obligations whose final award quantum is not contractually fixed; premium and payoff reporting must be included in Bundesbank statistics.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) and options settled in EUR, permitted strictly for receivables and disbursements denominated in restricted or non-market-convertible jurisdiction currencies (selected African, Central Asian, and Southeast Asian tug and salvage countermarkets), subject to the GmbH's board-approved hedge policy, BaFin market-conduct rules, and a prohibition of speculative short positions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Moderate as a primary clearing channel; moderate as a supplementary collection rail. German payment institutions under PSD2 (BaFin-licensed or EU passporting) may open and maintain pass-through float accounts for the GmbH but may not accept deposits on their own balance sheet, issue credit, or support ship-mortgage-linked escrow structures. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., pan-European entities licensed in Cyprus, Lithuania, or non-EU centers) without a German-custody agent arrangement and a German IBAN are not viable as a sole treasury bank because: (i) German payroll and VAT-relevant vendor payments require a German IBAN with BIC for e-invoicing (XRechnung) and SEPA credit-transfer processing; (ii) BaFin-supervised banks classify such EMI relationships as potential substitute-payment-account red flags under GwG Section 25e; and (iii) commercial letters of credit and Lloyd's Standard Form escrow mechanics are outside the EMI license perimeter. Recommendation: engage a BaFin-regulated German EMI only for receivables collection of small salvage deposits and card-based port fees, while all operational and escrow balances reside at a Tier-1 bank.

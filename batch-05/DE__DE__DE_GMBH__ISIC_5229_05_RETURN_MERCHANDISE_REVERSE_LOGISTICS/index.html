E-government filing portal

For a GmbH (including non-resident-owned): file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' ('Tax Registration Questionnaire') with the Finanzamt of the registered seat to obtain the eight- to eleven-digit Steuernummer; subsequently apply to BZSt for the USt-IdNr. via the Bundesfinanzportal; foreign entrepreneurs without a German seat or permanent establishment register through the special non-resident VAT registration process under § 18 UStG, submitting notarized charter documents, proof of foreign tax residence, and if required appointing a fiscal representative; registration must be completed before the first taxable transaction

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

GmbH is compulsorily subject to corporate income tax and Gewerbesteuer (no check-the-box election); available elections: VAT Organschaft (§ 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG) and fiscal unity for corporate income tax and Gewerbesteuer (§ 14 Abs. 1 KStG, § 2 Abs. 2 GewStG) via a profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag); hybrid mismatch neutralization under § 4k EStG and § 4i EStG implementing ATAD 1/2, disallowing deductions or requiring corresponding inclusions for hybrid structures; no territorial election — worldwide income taxation with treaty-based foreign tax credit

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis turnover threshold — any economic activity under § 2 Abs. 1 UStG triggers mandatory VAT registration under § 18 UStG even before turnover arises; the EUR 22,000 small-business exemption ceiling under § 19 UStG (EUR 25,000 prior-year / EUR 100,000 current-year forecast from 1 January 2025) does not apply to a commercially active limited-liability GmbH; intra-Community distance-selling thresholds are superseded by the OSS regime from July 2021

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries under §§ 4 Nr. 1 lit. a, 6 UStG are zero-rated where the returned or refurbished goods are transported by the seller or buyer to a third-country destination, leave the EU customs territory, and the export is evidenced under the proof-of-export rules (§§ 8–10 UStG-DVO); services directly connected to export goods — including handling, grading, repair and ancillary logistics — are exempt if rendered to the exporter and intrinsically linked to the exported goods (§ 4 Nr. 3 UStG); separately, B2B grading and quality-assessment services supplied to a foreign entrepreneur fall outside German VAT where the recipient is located abroad (§ 3a Abs. 2 UStG, reverse charge), but services relating to goods subsequently re-imported to Germany or not cleared for export remain taxable at 19%; excess input-VAT refunds are claimed via the VAT advance-return procedure or the EU/third-country VAT-refund portal for non-residents

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS/Ausfuhranmeldung) with Export Accompanying Document (EAD) and Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch beyond the EU customs border
  • Commercial invoice and returned-goods/export sales contract identifying the foreign customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/Payment-Provider (ACH, Airwallex) FX receipt evidencing repatriation of export consideration
  • Warehouse movement records, quality-grading inspection report and goods disposition log substantiating the reverse-logistics flow

Germany has no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping; under the 2025 reform (Wachstumschancengesetz), domestic B2B invoices must be exchanged in structured EN 16931 formats (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x): from 1 January 2025 every domestic B2B recipient must be able to receive e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 issuers with 2024 turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices, and from 1 January 2028 all other domestic B2B issuers must comply; VAT returns and advance returns are filed electronically via ELSTER API; the EU ViDA framework will add harmonized e-invoicing and real-time transaction reporting from 2030

Asserting zero-rating without compliant documentary proof triggers retrospective VAT assessment at 19% with default interest of 0.5% per month (§ 233a AO); negligent wrongful zero-rating is an administrative tax offense under § 378 AO carrying fines up to 10% of the evaded tax; intentional evasion under § 370 AO carries imprisonment of up to five years (up to ten years in aggravated cases) and a 10-year extended limitation period; the exporter bears the documentary and accounting burden (Belegnachweis, Buchnachweis) for ten years; export-then-re-import schemes in reverse logistics are scrutinized as re-importation fraud (Reimportfälle); customs authorities cross-check MRN/ATLAS data against VAT declarations, and unsubstantiated refunds are clawed back with penalties

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Germany has no general capital controls, mandatory exchange controls, or general requirement to convert foreign-currency receipts into euros. A GmbH may receive, hold, pay, and hedge in foreign currencies through German or permitted EU banking institutions. Profit distributions, dividends, intercompany interest, royalties, and principal repayments to non-residents are generally permitted without central-bank approval, subject to corporate-law solvency and capital-maintenance rules, withholding-tax requirements, transfer-pricing compliance, beneficial-owner verification, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, and any applicable withholding-tax relief or treaty documentation. Cross-border payments may be delayed or rejected where banks require evidence of the underlying transaction, tax status, source of funds, or economic purpose. EU and German sanctions, export-control restrictions, anti-boycott rules, and restrictions involving sanctioned countries, persons, goods, or services may prohibit or condition specific transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation deadline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts in Germany or abroad, subject to banking, tax, accounting, sanctions, and transaction-reporting obligations. Cross-border payments and certain external assets or liabilities may be reportable to Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV for balance-of-payments statistics. Reporting thresholds, exemptions, forms, and deadlines must be checked against the rules applicable on the transaction date; they are statistical reporting obligations and do not constitute a requirement to surrender or convert export proceeds. The company should maintain commercial invoices, customs records, transport evidence, credit notes, return authorizations, refund documentation, and reconciliation records for reverse-logistics flows.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank), operating within the framework of the European System of Central Banks and the European Central Bank. Banking supervision is primarily conducted by the European Central Bank and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin). Foreign-trade control matters are administered principally under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG), the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), and, where applicable, by the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle, BAFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection, SEPA transfers, multi-currency wallets, supplier payments, and foreign-exchange execution, but not a substitute for a German bank account where a notarial share-capital deposit, local lending, cash services, regulated escrow, or bank-specific proof of capital is required. Airwallex and comparable EU-authorized electronic-money or payment institutions may provide services through passporting or local authorization, subject to their license perimeter, safeguarding model, AML controls, country coverage, and risk appetite. Customer funds held by an EMI are generally safeguarded rather than covered by the German statutory deposit-guarantee scheme. Offshore digital EMIs without EU authorization or a valid European passport cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services to German customers merely through an online interface. The GmbH should retain a primary German or EU credit-institution account and use an EMI as a controlled secondary rail.

The GmbH should retain a primary German or EU credit-institution account and use an EMI as a controlled secondary rail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax itself, yielding a total federal rate of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is superimposed at rates from 14% to 17.15% (standard Hebesatz of 400% to 490%), producing an aggregate nominal burden of approximately 29.8% for a typical 400% multiplier before trade-income add-backs.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

German domestic law imposes Kapitalertragsteuer of 25% on distributions by a GmbH, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that 25%, giving a combined statutory withholding of 26.375%. Under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive implementations (§ 43b EStG), a qualifying EU/EEA parent with a direct holding of at least 10% for an uninterrupted 12-month period may benefit from a 0% withholding rate. Under most German double-tax treaties, the rate is reduced to 5% for corporate shareholders holding at least 10% and to 15% for non-qualifying shareholders, subject to anti-abuse and limitation-on-benefits clauses.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a 31 December fiscal year-end, the return is due no later than 31 July of the following calendar year. Where the return is prepared and signed by a German tax adviser (Steuerberater), the statutory deadline is extended to the last business day of February of the second year following the fiscal year. Further discretionary extensions may be granted under § 109 AO.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 0 — Germany applies no monetary de minimis threshold for transfer-pricing documentation. A German GmbH is required under § 90(3) AO to prepare contemporaneous documentation for all cross-border controlled transactions, including reverse-logistics service fees, cost-sharing arrangements, returns-provision charges and intercompany financing. The documentation must be submitted within 60 days after a formal request by the tax authority, and within 30 days for extraordinary transactions; failure triggers shifting of the burden of proof and penalty surcharges. For multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million, Germany additionally requires a Country-by-Country Report, Master File and Local File under the EU CbC framework.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations statute; however, § 42 AO disallows abusive legal arrangements lacking economic substance, and German case-law requires substance in the form of staff, premises, management decisions, operational risk and commercial functionality. The beneficial-owner and principal-purpose tests of § 50d EStG, EU ATAD and the MLI deny treaty and Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits to letter-box entities. A reverse-logistics GmbH must demonstrate German decision-making, German warehouse or processing capacity, payroll for logistics and quality-grading staff, own risk management, and independent telephony/data systems to support the deduction of service fees and the arm's-length character of its return-merchandise revenue.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Understatement or Non-Declaration of Corporate Income

Statutory sanction: Under § 370 AO, tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment of up to five years and a monetary fine calculated by reference to the evaded tax; in aggravated cases (e.g., high-value evasion, use of a shell company, repeated systemic abuse), the sentence is six months to ten years. Civil tax liability is discharged through reassessment, including recovery of mis-deducted expenses, withholding tax on hidden distributions, solidarity surcharge and 6% annual default interest.

Director personal liability: The managing director (Geschäftsführer) is personally liable under § 69 AO for evaded taxes and any associated penalties, surcharges and interest if the evasion arose from intentional or grossly negligent failure to comply with tax obligations. The director's personal assets can be seized, exit can be restricted by a travel ban, and criminal imprisonment may be ordered for intentional evasion.
Transfer-Pricing Non-Compliance and Missing TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities may estimate income under § 162 AO and impose a non-deductible documentation penalty surcharge of between EUR 5,000 and EUR 250,000, in addition to the tax adjustment and 6% annual interest. The taxpayer loses the right to invoke the burden of proof in its favour; foreign-related-party income may be increased to the highest plausible rate, and payment of adjusted tax is enforceable immediately.

Director personal liability: Where the managing director signs incomplete tax returns or instructs concealment of transfer-pricing documents, the director may be treated as an accomplice to tax evasion and held personally liable for the adjusted tax. German tax courts may impose an exit ban where the director holds assets abroad or shows flight risk; imprisonment is available for evasion offences, and management disqualification under § 6 GmbHG can follow a fraud conviction.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under § 42 AO and the German anti-abuse doctrine, the tax authorities disregard the arrangement and attribute profits to the entity performing the actual functions. Consequences include denial of treaty benefits, disallowance of tax deductions, classification of shifted amounts as hidden profit distributions subject to 26.375% withholding, trade-tax add-backs, interest, and reassessment up to the standard ten-year limitation period for tax evasion (or longer in severe cases).

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced for tax purposes where shareholders and directors commingle assets, act as de facto managers or cause undercapitalisation. The director can be held personally liable for unpaid tax debts under § 69 AO and under § 43 GmbHG for breach of duty; courts may order asset freezing, travel restrictions and, where hidden distribution is not declared, imprisonment for tax evasion.
Anti-Money-Laundering Deficiencies and Unlawful Cross-Border Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Violations of the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) are sanctioned by administrative fines of up to EUR 5 million or 10% of worldwide annual turnover for legal persons. Criminal money laundering under § 261 StGB carries imprisonment of up to ten years, and participation in a criminal enterprise may invoke serious asset forfeiture. The tax consequences are aggravated: all unexplained proceeds may be taxed as hidden distributions or disallowed as operating income.

Director personal liability: The managing director implementing or tolerating deficient AML controls is personally liable under § 43 GmbHG for resulting company losses and under GwG for failure to appoint an AML compliance officer and implement transaction monitoring. Criminal penalties extend to imprisonment and travel bans; directors can be prohibited from exercising management functions in any German company and are subject to seizure of personal assets where the proceeds of unlawful activity are traced.

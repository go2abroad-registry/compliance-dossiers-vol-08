Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The locally competent Registration Court (Registergericht) at the relevant Amtsgericht, acting through the electronic Handelsregister maintained by the competent state judicial administration.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), particularly Sections 2 (notarial formation), 3 (articles of association), 5 (share capital), 7 (registration requirements), 8 (registration documents), 10 (registered particulars), 11 (legal effect of registration), 35 (management representation), and 46 (shareholder authority). The Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch, HGB), Sections 8-12, governs the Commercial Register.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Anmeldung zum Handelsregister, submitted electronically by the German notary, together with the notarized Gesellschaftsvertrag and required managing-director declarations. The notarial formation deed and shareholder list form part of the incorporation record.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) through the network of Finanzämter (tax offices) and the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) as the central tax authority, with the primary online portal at www.elster.de for tax filings and registrations.

E-government filing portal

German-resident entities such as GmbH obtain their Steuernummer automatically upon registration with the local Finanzamt. Foreign entities seeking a German VAT ID must submit an application to the responsible Finanzamt demonstrating economic nexus and exceeding the registration threshold, followed by issuance of a Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — German-resident entities such as GmbH obtain their Steuernummer automatically upon registration with the local Finanzamt. Foreign entities seeking a German VAT ID must submit an application to the responsible Finanzamt demonstrating economic nexus and exceeding the registration threshold, followed by issuance of a Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German GmbH is statutorily classified as a corporation subject to entity-level taxation under the German Income Tax Act and Corporate Tax Act. No voluntary check-the-box elections are available. Hybrid mismatch elections are governed by the Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) implementing rules in the German Tax Adjustment Act (ATAD-Gesetz).

19%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to the supply of goods exported from the EU to non-EU countries and certain offshore services. Eligibility requires the goods to leave the EU territory, proof of export via customs documentation, and for services, performance outside the EU with appropriate substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranzeige)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment received from foreign customer

Mandatory electronic invoicing for all B2B transactions under the German Electronic Invoicing Ordinance (E-Rechnungsverordnung). Invoices must use the ZUGFeRD format with embedded PDF or XML. Real-time fiscal clearance is not required; however, digital stamping is mandatory via certified providers or Elster for larger entities. API integration with tax authorities is available for automated reporting.

Fraudulent claims of zero-rating on exports may result in penalties of up to 50% of the evaded VAT amount, plus interest, and retrospective clawback of any undue zero-rating benefits under Section 25 of the German VAT Act (UStG) and general tax evasion provisions in the Abgabenordnung.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully convertible Euro currency regime. Current account transactions face no restrictions. Capital account transactions are generally permitted for EU residents with prior notification required only for certain portfolio investments exceeding EUR 1 million. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are unrestricted for EU-established entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act, exporters must surrender foreign exchange proceeds to a domestic bank within ten working days for non-EU destinations. Intra-EU exports require no surrender obligation; proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax amount); municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) of approximately 14%-17% effective depending on the municipality Hebesatz, producing a combined corporate tax burden of approximately 30%-33% for the in-flight catering GmbH.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% aggregate (25% German Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) for portfolio dividends paid by the GmbH to non-resident shareholders; reduced to 0% for qualifying EU parent-subsidiary dividends under § 43b EStG where the parent holds at least 10% of the GmbH's share capital for an uninterrupted period of at least 12 months; non-EU treaty shareholders typically enjoy 5% (substantial shareholding) or 15% (portfolio) depending on the applicable DTA.

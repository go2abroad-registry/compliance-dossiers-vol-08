Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A GmbH must have a domestic business address and a registered domestic seat under GmbHG Sections 4a and 8; the domestic business address is entered in the commercial register and must permit service of official documents. A serviced or virtual office may be acceptable if it provides a genuine address, reliable receipt and forwarding of legal correspondence, and sufficient access for the company and authorities. A mailbox-only address, undisclosed co-working arrangement, or address incapable of supporting management, records, or regulatory inspection is unsuitable. For airport-runway asphalt paving, a virtual office is not adequate as the sole substance arrangement because the company should maintain demonstrable project-management, procurement, equipment, personnel, occupational-safety, technical, and subcontractor-control functions. Depending on the precise execution model, a trade requiring registration in the Handicrafts Register (Handwerksrolle), including relevant road-construction activity, may require registration and a qualified technical manager under the Crafts and Trades Regulation Code (Handwerksordnung). Public airport works may require procurement qualification, construction-law approvals, site access clearances, and compliance with applicable aviation-security and airport-operator rules.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) via Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt); official portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt Online (www.bzst.de)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities forming or registering a German GmbH must obtain (i) a local tax number (Steuernummer) from the local Finanzamt upon filing the trade tax and VAT registration questionnaires (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung), and (ii) a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) from the BZSt via the official portal. The GmbH's managing director must submit the signed questionnaire electronically or in paper form within one month of incorporation. Additionally, a separate EORI number is required for customs purposes if importing or exporting goods.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities forming or registering a German GmbH must obtain (i) a local tax number (Steuernummer) from the local Finanzamt upon filing the trade tax and VAT registration questionnaires (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung), and (ii) a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) from the BZSt via the official portal. The GmbH's managing director must submit the signed questionnaire electronically or in paper form within one month of incorporation. Additionally, a separate EORI number is required for customs purposes if importing or exporting goods.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German law does not provide a general check-the-box election comparable to the U.S. regime. A GmbH is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Tax consolidation is available through Organschaft (corporate and trade tax fiscal unity), requiring profit-and-loss pooling agreements and financial integration. For VAT purposes, the GmbH may be treated as part of a VAT group (Organschaft) if integrated financially, economically, and organizationally. Hybrid mismatch elections under ATAD implementation are limited to specific controlled transactions and must be disclosed in the tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for established businesses; every entrepreneur (Unternehmer) engaged in taxable supplies is required to register for VAT from the first transaction. For intra-Community distance sales into Germany by non-resident suppliers, an annual distance-selling threshold of EUR 10,000 applies; for non-established digital services, the EU OSS scheme threshold is EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports to destinations outside the European Union qualify for VAT zero-rating under Section 6 UStG if the entrepreneur (or a third party on their behalf) transports the goods outside the EU. Intra-Community supplies (B2B removals to another EU member state) are VAT-exempt with input VAT deduction (steuerfreie innergemeinschaftliche Lieferung) provided the purchaser has a valid VAT number, the goods are dispatched to another EU state, and the required summary declaration (Intrastat/EC Sales List) is filed. Services directly connected to export of goods, including loading, handling, and transport of export goods, may be zero-rated. Offshore services to non-EU customers are generally zero-rated if the recipient is a business and the place-of-supply rules place the supply outside Germany.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung via ATLAS)
  • Signed Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR Transport Document
  • Proof of Physical Departure from EU Customs Territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Bank Transfer Receipt evidencing payment in foreign currency
  • EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung) for intra-Community supplies
  • Export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument) or electronic export manifest

Germany is implementing mandatory e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) for B2B domestic supplies. From 1 January 2025, domestic taxable persons must be able to receive structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2027, businesses with annual turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all domestic B2B taxable persons must issue structured e-invoices in a CEN-TC 434-compliant format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD). Real-time fiscal clearance via API is not yet mandatory; no real-time tax authority stamping is currently implemented, but the Federal Central Tax Office (BZSt) operates a mandatory registration system for cash registers (TSE) and an electronic reporting architecture for VAT returns through ELSTER.

The tax authorities may retrospectively deny zero-rating if export evidence is incomplete or fraudulent, resulting in VAT reassessment at 19% plus interest (1.8% per annum) and late payment surcharges (1% per month). Penalties for intentional tax evasion carry imprisonment up to 10 years and fines up to EUR 50,000 plus criminal fines scaled to the evaded amount; administrative fines up to EUR 10,000 apply for negligent misdeclaration. Claims for input VAT refunds may be clawed back with a 10-year assessment period in cases of intentional evasion; business organizers may face personal joint liability under Section 71 AO for intentional concealment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has full currency convertibility. The euro is fully convertible and there are no exchange control restrictions under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) for current account or capital account transactions. Cross-border capital movements are protected by Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Profit remittances and dividend distributions require no exchange control approval; they are subject only to German corporate law solvency tests, withholding tax at 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on dividends, standard tax filings, and commercial register documentation. Inward capital contributions and outward trade payments are not subject to FX licensing but may trigger Bundesbank statistical reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds. Exporters may freely retain, hold, or convert foreign currency. However, German residents must report export-related payments from non-residents to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics where the amount exceeds EUR 12,500 or the equivalent, within seven days of receipt, in accordance with section 70 of the AWV. The report must include commercial and payment details. The nine-year retention period for such records generally applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank, Frankfurt am Main) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin - Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht); the Deutsche Bundesbank administers foreign trade payment statistics under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV), while BaFin supervises the corporate banking and payment institutions under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low for primary escrow, treasury, and documentary credit use; Medium for ancillary low-value payment clearing only. A non-EU electronic money institution cannot lawfully provide payment accounts or payment services to a German GmbH without a BaFin license, a German branch, or a valid EEA passport under PSD2. Even EU-authorized EMIs are often unable to issue letters of credit, provide dedicated capital escrow accounts, or act as counterparty for FX forwards. For airport runway paving contracting involving heavy capital injections and LC trade rails, a tier-1 full-service bank is legally and operationally necessary.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders transfer paid-up capital via SWIFT MT103 to the GmbH's capital escrow account at the German tier-1 bank. The transfer instruction should include a clear purpose description such as Einlage Stammeinlage or Capital Contribution DE_GMBH, together with the notarial deed reference and Commercial Register file reference if available. The bank verifies the payment against the notarized contribution deed, the GmbH shareholder resolution, and the Commercial Register extract before crediting the paid-in capital. No Bundesbank exchange-control approval is required. The receiving bank reports the inward payment for German balance-of-payments statistics under AWV if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500. Funds from high-risk jurisdictions or tax-haven states may be conditional on source-of-wealth and source-of-funds evidence to satisfy GwG and sanctions compliance.For euro-denominated trade settlements within the EU and the Eurosystem, the primary clearing rail is SEPA Credit Transfer with settlement through TARGET2, executed with high finality. For non-EUR disbursements to foreign asphalt polymer suppliers or heavy machinery highest-value goods, German banks use SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer messages through correspondent banking networks. For major contract purchases of imported bitumen, modified asphalt additives, and equipment, the preferred rail is an irrevocable documentary letter of credit issued by the German corporate bank and advised/confirmed through the seller's bank, transmitted by SWIFT MT700/MT710 with later SWIFT MT202/MT103 reimbursement instructions under documentary presentation. Payment milestones for runway works are executed by MT103 with invoice and legal contract references for clear audit trail.Plain-vanilla FX forwards for currency risk arising from EUR/USD asphalt import and airport contract receivablesWindow and average-rate FX forwards matching projected runway project milestone cash flowsFX options, including European-style call and put options on EUR/USD, used solely to hedge budgeted foreign-currency disbursementsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market currencies where underlying currency convertibility is restricted; not needed for freely convertible currencies but permissible for hedging when no deliverable market existsCross-currency swaps to hedge long-term USD/EUR construction equipment financing and large project receivablesEurex exchange-traded FX futures for transparent margining and short-term hedging of committed USD disbursementsFX swaps for short-term liquidity conversion between EUR and USD where the transaction is fully collateralized and transactionally linked to construction treasury management

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Low for primary escrow, treasury, and documentary credit use; Medium for ancillary low-value payment clearing only. A non-EU electronic money institution cannot lawfully provide payment accounts or payment services to a German GmbH without a BaFin license, a German branch, or a valid EEA passport under PSD2. Even EU-authorized EMIs are often unable to issue letters of credit, provide dedicated capital escrow accounts, or act as counterparty for FX forwards. For airport runway paving contracting involving heavy capital injections and LC trade rails, a tier-1 full-service bank is legally and operationally necessary.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File required if group revenue > €100 million; Local File required if related-party transactions exceed €5 million (goods) or €500,000 (services) per year.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no specific ESR, but substance requirements apply for treaty benefits and anti-abuse rules (e.g., §42 AO, §5d EStG). The GmbH must have real economic activity, premises, employees, and decision-making in Germany to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years, fines up to €50,000; for particularly serious cases up to 10 years. Additional civil penalties up to 25% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes under §69 AO; criminal liability for evasion; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Shell Company (Briefkastengesellschaft)

Statutory sanction: Disregard for tax purposes, recharacterization of transactions, penalties up to 25% of understated tax, and denial of treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors liable for civil restitution and potential criminal charges for aiding tax evasion; travel bans may be imposed.
AML Violations (Geldwäschegesetz)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for individuals, €5 million for companies; imprisonment up to 5 years for intentional violations.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for fines and imprisonment; civil liability for damages; travel bans and asset freezes.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 5% of adjusted transaction value, plus interest penalties; potential recharacterization of transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for administrative penalties; criminal liability for deliberate mispricing; travel bans possible.

UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: German Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz, GwG), particularly §§ 3 (beneficial owner), 10–15 (customer due diligence and enhanced due diligence), and 18–23 (Transparency Register); German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG) §§ 8 and 40 concerning registration filings and shareholder lists. A GmbH must obtain, retain, and report accurate beneficial-ownership information to the Transparency Register under GwG § 20. There is no general 25% threshold for every control route: a natural person is a beneficial owner where the person directly or indirectly holds more than 25% of capital or voting rights, or exercises comparable control; where no beneficial owner can be identified after reasonable investigation, the statutory fictitious beneficial-owner rules apply.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A GmbH must have a domestic registered seat under GmbHG § 4a and a domestic business address for registration under § 8(4). A serviced or virtual office is not categorically prohibited if the address is genuine, usable for service of process, and supported by the company's actual arrangements. A mailbox-only address or arrangement that prevents effective service creates registration and compliance risks. For high-voltage substation contracting, a registered office does not replace operational capacity, technical personnel, equipment, safety systems, trade registration, or any required authorizations.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

German Commercial Register (Handelsregister), accessible through the Länder-operated Registerportal at handelsregister.de; the Unternehmensregister provides access to published company information. Name suitability is commonly checked with the competent local Chamber of Industry and Commerce (Industrie- und Handelskammer, IHK) before notarized filing.

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no general statutory company-name reservation period. Obtain a preliminary name opinion from the competent IHK, then have the formation deed filed by a German notary with the competent local register court. If the court rejects or objects to the name, address the stated defect and resubmit or amend the articles. A formal court decision may generally be challenged by a complaint (Beschwerde) under the FamFG, subject to the decision, standing, and applicable time limit; obtain advice on the specific order and deadline. An IHK opinion is not an appealable registration decision.

Names that are misleading as to material circumstances or likely to cause confusion are not registrable under GmbHG § 4 and applicable commercial-register principles.Terms suggesting regulated status, public authority, a professional title, or a licensed activity may require substantiation or prior authorization under applicable sectoral or professional rules.There is no exhaustive nationwide statutory list of words that invariably require ministerial approval; restrictions depend on the proposed term, context, and applicable federal or state law.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office, BZSt) for VAT IDs and cross-border procedures; local Finanzamt (municipal tax office) at the registered seat of the GmbH for income tax, trade tax, and VAT returns. Official electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Following notarial incorporation and entry into the Handelsregister (Commercial Register), the GmbH must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER to the competent local Finanzamt within one month of commencing business. The Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer (tax number) covering corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. A separate written application to the BZSt is required to obtain the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID) for intra-EU transactions. Non-resident ownership is permitted; for non-EU member-state shareholders or managers, apostilled and sworn-certified documentation must be presented to the notary and Finanzamt. No general tax clearance certificate is required for continued operation, but outstanding tax debts may be enforced through the Finanzamt's administrative attachment powers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under German law. The GmbH is a taxable corporation subject to 15% corporate income tax (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective rate ranging from approximately 7% to 17% depending on the local Hebesatz). Available elections and structures: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) election under Secs. 14, 17 KStG and Sec. 2 UStG, combining a German parent with one or more German subsidiaries through profit-and-loss pooling agreements for income, trade tax, and VAT consolidation; (ii) structuring the business as a GmbH & Co. KG to allocate taxable income to pass-through partners; (iii) hybrid mismatch rule elections limited by ATAD-adopted provisions (Sec. 4k EStG, Sec. 8b KStG, Sec. 2b GewStG); (iv) for VAT, no option to separate the GmbH's single taxable enterprise except through genuine branch or Organschaft constructs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold applies to commercially active incorporated entities; VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction. The small-entrepreneur exemption (Sec. 19 UStG) is limited to prior-year turnover below EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover projected not to exceed EUR 50,000; this exemption is practically inapplicable to a GmbH performing high-value substation installation services and is not elected in the standard compliance posture.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to third countries (outside the EU) are zero-rated under Sec. 6 UStG if the supplier proves that the goods left the EU customs territory. For high-voltage substation installation services, the place of supply under Sec. 3a(3) UStG is the location of the real property; installations performed on real property outside Germany fall outside the German VAT net, and where the customer is a taxable person established in another jurisdiction, reverse-charge or local VAT applies. Work on movable tangible property subsequently exported outside the EU may be zero-rated under Sec. 7 UStG. All zero-rating claims require written evidence of export and receipt of consideration at the time of invoicing.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with exit confirmation from the EU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note (air, road, rail freight)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing FX repatriation and receipt of consideration in the GmbH's account

Mandatory E-Rechnung (structured electronic invoicing) for domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2025. All German-established taxable persons must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD format conforming to EN 16931). Issuance obligations are phased: from 1 January 2027, issuers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, the obligation applies to all issuers. Germany currently operates no real-time transaction clearance or fiscal digital stamping system; compliance is audited ex post through ELSTER filing, auditable invoice data extraction, and certified technical security equipment (TSE) for electronic cash registers under the KassenSichV.

Export zero-rating is subject to retrospective ex-post audit by the Finanzamt. Missing, contradictory, or post-dated export documentation triggers immediate VAT adjustment, disallowance of input VAT refunds, and retrospective clawback of previously credited input VAT with interest under Sec. 233a AO. Fraudulent zero-rating claims expose directors and the tax representative to criminal liability under Sec. 370 AO with imprisonment up to five years (up to ten years in aggravated cases) and fines up to EUR 50,000 for tax evasion; administrative penalty provisions and collective liability of management under Sec. 69, 71 AO further apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the doctrine of free movement of capital as anchored in the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU Art. 63) and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG). No exchange control approval, foreign exchange budget, or central bank licensing is required for inbound capital contributions, operational remittances, dividend distributions, or trade settlement. Dividends and profit remittances are subject only to German corporate taxation and, where applicable, withholding tax under §43 EStG and Double Tax Treaty relief, with no currency convertibility restriction or deferred convertibility hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate export earnings into EUR within a fixed timeline. Export invoices and payments must be maintained and receivable in any agreed currency; however, the reporting obligation under §§67-68 AWV requires electronic notification to the Deutsche Bundesbank for any incoming or outgoing cross-border payment exceeding €12,500, including export receipts, within a format and deadline prescribed by the Bundesbank Statistics Ordinance. The reporting is for balance-of-payments statistics only and carries no conversion or surrender element.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank) in cooperation with BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht); cross-border payment and foreign trade statistics reporting administered under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV) and Zahlungsverkehrsgesetz, with Bundesbank as the primary statistical data collection agent.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for general treasury disbursements, Low for statutory capital escrow. A Germany-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under BaFin authorization may provide GBP/EUR and other multi-currency IBANs, hold e-money, and process trade payments efficiently; however, EMIs are not licensed to hold segregated client capital escrow accounts for GmbH share capital deposits under §5 GmbHG and §7 BeurkG because they are not full credit institutions. Cross-border EMIs chartered outside Germany (e.g., UK FCA-EMI or Lithuania-licensed EMI) additionally require either passporting under PSD2 (where EU/EEA) or a mandatory BaFin agent/licence route for German services, and most will not accept UBO residency outside the EEA without additional KYC enhancement. Court-registered escrow ledgering can be replicated through a commercial bank account; hence EMIs are viable only as secondary treasury settlement rails for low-risk operational payments, never for the statutory capital escrow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The mandated route for statutory capital injection is a SWIFT MT103 wire from the corporate parent or creditor into a dedicated Capital Escrow Account held at a German tier-1 commercial bank. The creditor should use the GmbH's full German IBAN and include field 70 (Remittance Information) with the text 'Einlagezahlung gem. §§5, 55 GmbHG; Stammkapitalerhöhung' and the commercial register file number. The applicable ISO 20022 purpose code is 'CAPT' (Capital Payment). The bank must receive a capital contribution statement and a notarised increase resolution before release; funds must remain blocked until the Geschäftsführung issues a confirmation of payment in compliance with §7 GmbHG. For inbound investment exceeding €12,500, the bank files the AWV 'Zahlung von Ausländern an Inländer' report to Deutsche Bundesbank, using category 'K2' (Kapitaltransfers) and reserve first-time capital formation. Verification is handled through the bank's KYC identifier: SWIFT field 50K (originator), 59 (beneficiary), 72 (regulatory details), and the documentary bundle is stored under the compliance file; no FX approval is needed because EUR is fully convertible.Outbound trade settlements should clear exclusively through the operational Girokonto using (1) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border supplier wires with ISO purpose codes such as 'SUPP' (Payment of a supplier), 'TRAD' (Trade settlement), or 'ICLC' (Letter of Credit payment) depending on the exact transaction; (2) Commercial/Standby Letter of Credit at sight for the imported GIS modules, the underlying LC being advised via the German bank's correspondent network and honoured against presentation of paid commercial invoice, transport document, certificate of origin, and site acceptance documentation; (3) SEPA-Area Credit Transfer for intra-EU material purchases using the Customer-to-Bank method; (4) documentary collection for equipment from lower-risk jurisdictions where an LC is not contractually required. For settlement of milestone invoices to a German public utility or TSO, the outbound side is typically not cross-border; but when paying a subcontractor abroad, an MT103 with exemption code 'EK02' under AWV if under €12,500, and full statistical reporting if above that threshold.Plain-vanilla EUR/GBP or EUR/USD OTC FX forward contracts (Devisentermingeschäft) transacted with a German credit institution or EU/UK counterparty under CRD IV, hedged against imported steel, HV switchgear, or engineering service costs denominated in GBP or USD.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) under CSA/CSD margin rules, used only where the GmbH has an underlying project finance loan in foreign currency and the swap strictly qualifies as hedge accounting under IFRS, not as speculative exposure.European-style FX options (call/pot on EUR or foreign currency) purchased from a licensed German bank for project-specific currency contingencies.No deliverable currency swaps required for routine purchasing because EUR is freely deliverable; NDFs are generally not accessible on EUR due to the ECB's free-float regime and are not acceptable in German bank compliance screening for a EUR-functional entity, but an NDF on a non-EEA exotic currency (e.g., EUR/TRY or EUR/KZT) for a subcontractor tender may be permitted with BaFin-specific product approval.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for general treasury disbursements, Low for statutory capital escrow. A Germany-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under BaFin authorization may provide GBP/EUR and other multi-currency IBANs, hold e-money, and process trade payments efficiently; however, EMIs are not licensed to hold segregated client capital escrow accounts for GmbH share capital deposits under §5 GmbHG and §7 BeurkG because they are not full credit institutions. Cross-border EMIs chartered outside Germany (e.g., UK FCA-EMI or Lithuania-licensed EMI) additionally require either passporting under PSD2 (where EU/EEA) or a mandatory BaFin agent/licence route for German services, and most will not accept UBO residency outside the EEA without additional KYC enhancement. Court-registered escrow ledgering can be replicated through a commercial bank account; hence EMIs are viable only as secondary treasury settlement rails for low-risk operational payments, never for the statutory capital escrow.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (Körperschaftsteuer 15% + 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag); aggregate with Gewerbesteuer approx. 29%-33%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the 25% tax). Treaty relief may reduce the rate to 0%-15%, and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive can produce 0% for qualifying EU parents holding 10% or more for 12 months.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, filing must occur no later than 31 July of the following year. If the return is prepared by a tax advisor, the deadline is extended automatically to 28/29 February of the second following year; further extension lies in the tax office's discretion only in hardship cases.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 – Identification / Stammdaten Registered name, legal form (GmbH), seat, Steuernummer, Finanzamt, date of formation, fiscal year and business purpose. Non-resident shareholders do not appear on the GmbH's return; they must separately file their own German withholding-tax declarations and send a residence certificate to the withholding agent. Late-filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag) under § 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed tax per commenced month, at least EUR 25 per month, capped at EUR 25,000. Non-payment adds Säumniszuschlag of 1% per month under § 240 AO and interest at 6% p.a. under § 233a AO after the due date.
Box 2 – Participations and exempt income Details of shareholdings in foreign/domestic companies and of dividends received; used to apply 95% participation exemption under § 8b KStG. Only the GmbH's own participations are reported. Non-resident shareholders claiming a refund of Kapitalertragsteuer under a DTA must rely on BZSt reclaim, not on the GmbH's return. Late-filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag) under § 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed tax per commenced month, at least EUR 25 per month, capped at EUR 25,000. Non-payment adds Säumniszuschlag of 1% per month under § 240 AO and interest at 6% p.a. under § 233a AO after the due date.
Box 3 – Trade tax base / Gewerbesteuer Computation of Gewerbeertrag, permanent-establishment allocation, tax-free reductions, and the trade tax measurement amount. Non-resident shareholders are not assessed on German trade tax unless they have a German permanent establishment; the GmbH pays trade tax on its locally attributable installation income. Late-filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag) under § 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed tax per commenced month, at least EUR 25 per month, capped at EUR 25,000. Non-payment adds Säumniszuschlag of 1% per month under § 240 AO and interest at 6% p.a. under § 233a AO after the due date.
Box 4 – Withholding taxes and credits German withholding tax withheld on investment income received by the GmbH, foreign tax credits, and other refundable/offsetable tax amounts. For non-resident recipients, the GmbH as statutory debtor withholds 26.375% dividend tax and pays it to the tax office; any treaty reduction must be invoked by the recipient before payment. Late-filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag) under § 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed tax per commenced month, at least EUR 25 per month, capped at EUR 25,000. Non-payment adds Säumniszuschlag of 1% per month under § 240 AO and interest at 6% p.a. under § 233a AO after the due date.
Box 5 – Loss carryforward / Verlustabzug Statement of prior-year loss balances, current-year loss used, and loss carryforward after minimum taxation (Mindestbesteuerung). Losses are ring-fenced in the GmbH and cannot be transferred to shareholders. If more than 50% of the GmbH's shares are transferred, the carryforward is forfeited; a transfer of 25%-50% causes proportional forfeiture. Late-filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag) under § 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed tax per commenced month, at least EUR 25 per month, capped at EUR 25,000. Non-payment adds Säumniszuschlag of 1% per month under § 240 AO and interest at 6% p.a. under § 233a AO after the due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 for cross-border related-party services; EUR 5,000,000 for related-party goods; Master File documentation is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulation, but applies economic-substance scrutiny through § 42 AO (abuse of legal form), § 50d EStG (treaty-benefit disallowance for lack of substance), and CFC rules (§§ 7-13 AStG). A GmbH performing high-voltage substation installation must show real offices, staff, board decisions, equipment, project risk assumption, and bank accounts in its own name; a letter-box entity can be denied DTA relief and treaty reduced WHT.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Incorrect tax return

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under § 370 AO: imprisonment up to 5 years, aggravated cases up to 10 years, monetary penalty; administrative penalty under § 378 AO; reassessed tax plus 6% p.a. interest and late-payment surcharge.

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer and CFO face personal criminal conviction, travel bans, and passport seizure; civil liability for unpaid corporate tax arises under § 69 AO when the director breached duties; the corporate veil does not shelter them from criminal restitution.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / False Intercompany Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Income reallocation under § 1 AStG; estimated TP penalty under § 162(4) AO; late-documentation penalty up to EUR 1,000,000; interest and late-payment charges; possible criminal sanction for deliberate misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false intercompany agreements may be prosecuted for aiding tax evasion; the tax authority may pierce the veil under § 69 AO to recover unpaid tax; court-ordered travel restrictions and compulsory appearance are common.
Shell Company / Treaty-Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief and EU directives under § 50d EStG; arrangement disregarded under § 42 AO; withholding recalculated at 26.375% for dividends and 15.825% for royalties; back taxes, interest, and penalty; possible CFC income inclusion under §§ 7-13 AStG.

Director personal liability: Directors who establish and manage the shell are personally exposed to criminal tax proceedings, passport seizures, and travel bans; outstanding tax and penalties are asserted against the director where the GmbH is unable to pay and the director caused the shortfall.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen) via the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) and the local tax office (Finanzamt); digital portal: ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first engage the Federal Central Tax Office (BZSt) to obtain a German tax identification number (Steuernummer) and, where applicable, a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr) under §27a Umsatzsteuergesetz. Application is submitted via Form, electronically through ELSTER or directly to the competent Finanzamt/BZSt, accompanied by memorandum of association, commercial register excerpt, proof of foreign registration, and authorisation letter of the managing directors.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must first engage the Federal Central Tax Office (BZSt) to obtain a German tax identification number (Steuernummer) and, where applicable, a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr) under §27a Umsatzsteuergesetz. Application is submitted via Form, electronically through ELSTER or directly to the competent Finanzamt/BZSt, accompanied by memorandum of association, commercial register excerpt, proof of foreign registration, and authorisation letter of the managing directors.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under German tax law. A German GmbH is by default a corporation subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), solidarity surcharge, and VAT. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) election under §14 ff. Körperschaftsteuergesetz and §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 Umsatzsteuergesetz for corporate, trade tax, and VAT consolidation between parent and subsidiary; (ii) small business (Kleinunternehmer) election under §19 UStG; and (iii) amortisation/depreciation and profit determination elections under the German Commercial Code and tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€22,000 (previous calendar year) and expected ≤€50,000 for the current calendar year for the Kleinunternehmer election; generally no registration threshold for businesses making taxable supplies, but a foreign entity carrying out any taxable transaction or receiving an intra-EU acquisition must obtain a VAT identification number and file VAT returns. A permanent establishment or local fixed place of business triggers mandatory tax registration irrespective of turnover volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating for goods applies where the goods are dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union (§4 Nr. 1a, §6 UStG) or to a ship/aircraft engaged in international traffic. Services directly related to seafarer recruitment or crew deployment for non-EU vessels or vessels engaged in international freight/passenger transport may be zero-rated under §4 Nr. 2 UStG when the recipient is a non-EU entrepreneur or the service effectively benefits the foreign vessel. Documentary evidence requirements must be maintained to prove export and to evidence the foreign status of the recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS summary declaration or Customs Export Accompanying Document, EAD)
  • Bill of Lading or International Sea Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment in convertible foreign currency showing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and service contract confirming crew supply to non-EU vessel operator

Germany has no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. Mandatory electronic invoicing (e-invoicing) for B2B domestic transactions is being phased in under the Wachstumschancengesetz: from 1 January 2025, taxpayers must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD); from 1 January 2027, businesses with more than €800,000 turnover must issue e-invoices via an EDI/ERM interface; from 1 January 2028, all domestic B2B suppliers must issue compliant e-invoices. Invoicing must be issued within 6 months per §14 UStG; VAT returns are submitted electronically via ELSTER (Voranmeldung and Zusammenfassende Meldung).

Zero-rating claims are subject to intensive Finanzamt review and audit. Incorrect or fraudulent export zero-rating triggers immediate VAT correction and interest for late payment (§233a AO), criminal tax evasion liability under §370 Abgabenordnung (up to 10 years' imprisonment for severe evasion), fines, and retroactive assessment of VAT plus penalties for incorrect VAT returns; repeated or organised evasion can lead to loss of trading licences and seizure of goods. Export documents must be retained for 10 years under §147 AO.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains fully unrestricted currency convertibility for both capital and current account transactions under the EU free movement of capital and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV). Profit and dividend remittances are permitted without foreign exchange approval, subject to corporate income tax, trade tax, and dividend withholding tax compliance. Non-resident shareholders may repatriate distributable profits via normal SWIFT wire transfers with the relevant dividend documentary evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds or to convert them into EUR. Export receipts may be held in any freely convertible currency and retained by the exporting GmbH. However, under the German Balance of Payments Ordinance (AWV), cross-border payments and receivables must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical purposes when individual or monthly aggregate transactions reach EUR 12,500; no minimum percentage of export proceeds is required to be converted.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Central Bank of the Federal Republic of Germany), with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) responsible for banking prudential supervision and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action for foreign trade regulation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. A German GmbH treasury cannot rely solely on an offshore or non-bank EMI as its primary operational account, because the banking relationship and capital verification must be supported by a licensed credit institution. Payment institutions and e-money institutions incorporated in the EEA and passporting into Germany under PSD2 can provide SWIFT, multi-currency wallets, and payment processing rails, but they do not substitute for a full commercial bank account; offshore digital EMIs without EEA/BaFin passporting are legally unsuitable and will be rejected for corporate onboarding.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Provide the crewing management agreement with the vessel owner; Maritime Labour Convention compliant Seafarer Employment Agreements; payroll ledger with beneficiary names, passport copies, bank details, and net/gross amounts; lists of assigned vessels and voyage dates; sanctions/PEP screening reports; and a written treasury policy explaining the business rationale for each payment corridor.

Defense evidence: Maintain a complete audit trail of underlying voyage and employment contracts; issue per-payment invoices or disbursement instructions from the ship manager; submit monthly payroll reports with statutory deductions; prepare annual financial statements or management accounts; provide internal AML transaction-monitoring records; and document a formal KYC file refreshed at least annually.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for shareholder capital injection into a German GmbH must use a regular banking wire to the GmbH's account held at a German or EU/EEA authorised credit institution. For EUR transfers, use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer with purpose code S200 (direct investment) and remittance field clearly stating 'equity contribution' or 'shareholder loan S300'. For non-EUR injections, use SWIFT MT103 with field 70 identifying 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION' and ensure the GmbH's IBAN and legal name appear in the beneficiary fields. The capital amount must be placed unconditionally at the disposal of the managing director, without deduction of bank fees, and funds must not be repaid until the commercial register registration is complete. If the single or aggregate amount exceeds EUR 12,500, the GmbH must report the inward capital transfer to Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV balance of payments statistics reporting obligation, generally via the Bundesbank's electronic reporting system.Outbound trade settlements for the crewing agency, including payments to ship owners, ship managers, maritime suppliers, and seafarer payroll, should use commercial bank clearing rails. For EUR payments, SEPA Credit Transfer and batch payroll SEPA processing are preferred. For non-EUR disbursements, use SWIFT MT103 messages via the bank's correspondent network with exact purpose codes and documentary references. Standby and commercial letters of credit under UCP600 and documentary collections are permissible for structured vessel-crewing or sub-consignment services where the counterparty requires bank settlement. Crew payroll is cleared through multi-currency payroll files transmitted by the bank, with FX executing at the bank's benchmark rate. All outbound payments are subject to Bundesbank statistical reporting above the EUR 12,500 threshold, and the bank will require underlying contracts and payroll instructions for high-value transactions.Outright deliverable FX forwards between the entity and a licensed German/EU bankNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies without active onshore convertibilityCross-currency swaps for long-term vessel finance or payroll FX exposureVanilla FX call and put options for hedging currency volatilityShort-term FX swaps in the interbank money marketBilateral ISDA/CSA master agreement for derivative FX transactions with a German bank

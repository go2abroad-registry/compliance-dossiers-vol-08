Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum nominal share capital: EUR 25,000 (GmbHG § 5(1)). Before filing, cash contributions must be paid to a Germany-based bank escrow account held in the name of the GmbH in formation to at least EUR 12,500 in aggregate (50% of the minimum capital; GmbHG § 7(2)); a single-member GmbH must pay the full EUR 12,500 minimum amount in cash. The bank's deposit confirmation must be attached to the application.

Funding & unlock path: Building occupancy clearance (Nutzungsabnahme) under the applicable State Building Code (Landesbauordnung); fire safety certificate (Brandschutznachweis) and fire extinguisher placement for server rooms and office areas; structural load inspection (Statik) for server-rack floor loads and raised-floor areas; and a mandatory occupational health and safety risk assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung) under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz) and the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) covering ventilation, lighting, emergency exits and ergonomic workstations before employees occupy the premises.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent establishing a GmbH must first obtain a local tax number (Steuernummer) from the competent Finanzamt through incorporation notification via the electronic ELSTER wizard; separately, the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is obtained from BZSt under §27a UStG using the official application form and certified incorporation documents, tax registration certificate, and evidence of business premises in Germany

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign parent establishing a GmbH must first obtain a local tax number (Steuernummer) from the competent Finanzamt through incorporation notification via the electronic ELSTER wizard; separately, the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is obtained from BZSt under §27a UStG using the official application form and certified incorporation documents, tax registration certificate, and evidence of business premises in Germany

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in German tax law; a GmbH is irrebuttably treated as a corporation subject to corporation tax and trade tax; available elections include formation of an Organschaft with a controlling German parent and profit-and-loss pooling (including for VAT purposes under §2 UStG), and allocation of trade tax across permanent establishments; hybrid mismatch elections are limited to ATAD-aligned documentation and anti-mismatch declarations for cross-border branches and mixed entities

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 domestic small-business threshold only for established German entities; no general domestic turnover threshold for foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Germany; EU-wide distance-sales/remote-services threshold EUR 10,000 for B2C supplies eligible for OSS across all Member States

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to third-country territories qualify for 0% VAT under §6 UStG where the goods are actually dispatched or transported from Germany to a third-country destination by the supplier, purchaser, or a commissioned carrier; supplies of offshore services, including SaaS analytics tools, are zero-rated mainly where the recipient is a non-EU entrepreneur, or under §3a UStG the place of supply is treated as outside Germany; intra-Community zero-rating under §6a UStG applies to B2B supplies to EU-registered business customers with valid VAT IDs and continuous post-shipment verification

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / ATLAS printout or ABD certificate)
  • Commercial invoice with export zero-rating annotation
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or CMR international consignment note
  • Proof of transport/export documentation (Versandbeleg) including export customs office endorsement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Federal Bank receipt documenting foreign currency repatriation
  • VAT ID of EU customer and recapitulative statement (EC Sales List) for intra-Community supplies

Mandatory structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2025, requiring XInvoice/ZUGFeRD or EDI-compliant XML; no immediate real-time fiscal clearance or API stamping currently in force, but all invoices must be interoperable with the central data reporting standards and VAT return must be submitted via ELSTER; digital VAT reporting under European ViDA will introduce transaction-based electronic reporting and e-invoicing clearance on a phased basis from 2030

Fraudulent or unsubstantiated export zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT assessment plus late-payment interest (0.5% per month under §233a AO), false declaration penalties under §379 AO, and criminal tax evasion imprisonment or fines under §370 AO; misuse of USt-IdNr. and missing customs export evidence expose the GmbH to full VAT clawback, denial of input VAT deduction, EU-wide cross-border enforcement under the Mutual Assistance Directive, and penalty up to EUR 500,000 in aggravated administrative cases

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles 63-66 TFEU and Section 4 AWG. There is no requirement for any license, approval, prior declaration, or repatriation for inbound equity, intercompany loans, trade settlements, or profit remittances in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency. Non-resident shareholders may own and finance the GmbH without limitation. The only compliance burden is passive ex-post statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank: cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 per individual payment or aggregated monthly per non-resident counterparty must be reported under Section 67 AWV, and cross-border payables/receivables exceeding EUR 5,000 must be reported under Section 58 AWV via the Bundesbank's official electronic reporting portal. Profit remittance is unrestricted by exchange control; the sole hurdle is fiscal: a statutory 26.375% dividend withholding tax (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding), refundable to 0% for EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Section 43b EStG) or reduced under a double-tax treaty (e.g., 5%-15% for non-EEA jurisdictions with a US-Germany or Switzerland-Germany treaty), with reclaim effected via the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern using Form KSt1/BZSt.AF.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exist in Germany; the AWV does not require exporters to repatriate, convert, or deposit foreign-currency export receipts within any time limit. Proceeds may be freely retained in offshore accounts, converted to EUR, or used to pay foreign suppliers. For the GmbH's SaaS business, collections made by Stripe or Paddle as merchant of record are settled gross-receipts less fees as B2B payouts to the GmbH's German bank account; these settlements are treated as service-export receipts and are subject only to the general Section 67 AWV payment-statistics reporting threshold (report when the individual payout exceeds EUR 12,500 or the monthly aggregate per payer exceeds that threshold). No exchange quota, cash-surrender ceiling, or balance-sheet conversion ratio applies to USD, GBP, or EUR balances.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Federal Bank, Frankfurt am Main) exercises the German foreign-exchange and cross-border payment-statistics administration under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); restrictive export control for embedded software/data functions (dual-use and encryption) is additionally supervised by BAFA (Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle) as an agency of the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as a supplementary treasury rail; LOW as the sole primary or statutory capital-escrow institution. BaFin-recognized EEA e-money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Solaris/Penta, Bunq, Qonto, Wise Europe, Airwallex) legally may provide the GmbH with multi-currency IBANs, SEPA Instant, SWIFT execution, FX conversion at interbank-plus-fixed-spread, and virtual card rails supporting Stripe/Paddle payouts, under PSD2 and the EMD2 e-money passports. Legal constraints: (i) a non-EU/offshore EMI without a valid EU passport cannot lawfully offer payment services to a German GmbH and must not be used; (ii) funds held with an EMI are safeguarded via segregated accounts but are not covered by the German statutory deposit protection scheme (Einlagensicherung, EUR 100,000 ceiling per depositor); (iii) most German notaries and registration courts require the Section 8(2) GmbHG capital-contribution confirmation to be evidenced by a credit institution, not a mere EMI, so formation capital should sit with a full bank and only operating float (capped, e.g., under EUR 50,000 or one week of payables) should move to EMIs.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: the non-resident shareholder instructs a SWIFT MT103 (ISO 20022 Customer Credit Transfer) from its bank to Deutsche Bank AG or Commerzbank AG Frankfurt for the GmbH's formation/operational IBAN. The instruction must carry (i) an unambiguous remittance line 'Einlage in das Stammkapital / Kapitalrücklage – [GmbH name] – Gesellschafterbeschluss vom [date]'; (ii) the German AWV payment-statistics purpose code for inward direct-investment equity (Bundesbank Schlüsselnummer für Zahlungsgrund, category Kapitalverkehr/Direktinvestition); and (iii) the parent's corporate identifier (GLN/LEI) to allow automatic reporting. On credit, the bank issues an inbound remittance advice; the managing directors then declare to the notary under Section 8(2) GmbHG that the statutory minimum cash contribution (at least one quarter of the cash capital, minimum EUR 12,500 of the EUR 25,000 nominal capital, under Sections 5 and 7 GmbHG) is freely at the disposal of the management, and the Handelsregister registration proceeds. Payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be electronically reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank via the bank's integration or the Bundesbank's official electronic reporting portal by the 15th day of the following month; no prior approval, exchange-control permit, or BoP licensing is required for EUR flows from either EEA or non-EEA shareholders. A notarized copy of the capital-contribution receipt and an AWV confirmation should be filed in the corporate treasury archive for audit defense.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA supplier and intercompany disbursements; SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001) and SEPA Instant cover all EEA payables; high-value EUR payments are settled over TARGET2 with intraday liquidity through the German bank's cash-management module. For the GmbH's SaaS operating model, outbound disbursements typically cover: cloud/vendor invoices (AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure) paid by MT103 with an AWV service-import (Dienstleistungen) purpose code; subcontractor and affiliate payouts via batch SEPA or payouts API; and distributor platform rebates. For material software licensing or resale agreements with non-EEA counterparties where credit risk is material, an irrevocable at-sight documentary Letter of Credit issued by Deutsche Bank/Commerzbank via SWIFT MT700/MT707 against vendor invoice and inspection documents, or a standby LC/SBLC as performance guarantee, can be drawn; for a low-risk MarTech SaaS treasury, LCs should be reserved for high-value strategic contracts above an established materiality threshold (e.g., EUR 50,000 per transaction). Regulatory clearing obligations: each outgoing cross-border payment above EUR 12,500 is AWV-reportable and must carry the correct purpose code; errors are remedied via the bank's reporting portal, and the treasury reconciles Stripe/Paddle merchant-of-record payouts against transaction-level statements for audit completeness, applying a zero-day cash sweep from EMI float accounts into the primary German bank account to optimize interest and deposit protection.OTC FX Forwards (deliverable, EUR/USD, EUR/GBP; and onshore deliverable forwards for all freely convertible G10 and Eastern European currencies) with a licensed German bank or EU-licensed broker, documented under ISDA 2002/2023 or German Rahmenvereinigung für Devisengeschäfte; non-deliverable only for truly non-convertible settlement currenciesFX Swaps (spot-start/forward-start exchange of EUR against USD/GBP for liquidity and cross-border payroll timing management)OTC Plain-Vanilla Currency Options (purchased EUR call/USD put or EUR put/USD call structures) for asymmetric protection of material subscription receipts at a capped premiumCross-Currency Swaps (CCIRS) for larger intercompany loan exposures and to hedge offshore subsidiary funding in excess of a materiality threshold (e.g., above EUR 2 million)Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) permissible exclusively for non-convertible or regulatory-constrained currencies (e.g., NGN, EGP, certain APAC/LATAM currencies) collected indirectly via Paddle local-currency subscription rails, settled against the ECB fixing or the Bundesbank reference rateFX-locked multi-currency settlement overlays through EU-licensed EMIs (interbank rate plus a fixed spread) are permissible as a non-speculative treasury overlay, subject to the safeguarding and balance caps described in the fintech EMI section

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH as a supplementary treasury rail; LOW as the sole primary or statutory capital-escrow institution. BaFin-recognized EEA e-money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Solaris/Penta, Bunq, Qonto, Wise Europe, Airwallex) legally may provide the GmbH with multi-currency IBANs, SEPA Instant, SWIFT execution, FX conversion at interbank-plus-fixed-spread, and virtual card rails supporting Stripe/Paddle payouts, under PSD2 and the EMD2 e-money passports. Legal constraints: (i) a non-EU/offshore EMI without a valid EU passport cannot lawfully offer payment services to a German GmbH and must not be used; (ii) funds held with an EMI are safeguarded via segregated accounts but are not covered by the German statutory deposit protection scheme (Einlagensicherung, EUR 100,000 ceiling per depositor); (iii) most German notaries and registration courts require the Section 8(2) GmbHG capital-contribution confirmation to be evidenced by a credit institution, not a mere EMI, so formation capital should sit with a full bank and only operating float (capped, e.g., under EUR 50,000 or one week of payables) should move to EMIs.

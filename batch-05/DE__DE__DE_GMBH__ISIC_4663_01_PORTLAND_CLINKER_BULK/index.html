Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen) acting through the local tax office (Finanzamt) responsible for the GmbH; the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues European VAT identification numbers; official filing portal is ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Upon notarial formation and commercial register registration, the managing director submits the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent local Finanzamt, electronically via ELSTER or in paper form. The Finanzamt assigns the statutory local tax number covering corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. Separately, the GmbH applies to BZSt for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) using the BZSt application form; non-resident shareholders or intermediate EU entities may need to complete form USt 1 TU. For a foreign entity without domestic establishment, registration runs through the Finanzamt competent for non-resident entrepreneurs rather than a local municipal tax office. The GmbH must also file annual tax returns and, as an exporter of bulk cement clinker, maintain export documentation for VAT zero-rating.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not apply a US-style check-the-box election; a GmbH is automatically treated as a corporation taxable at entity level on worldwide income unless treaty or permanent establishment limitations apply. Available elective mechanisms include fiscal unity for corporate income tax and VAT (Organschaft) with a profit and loss transfer agreement and financial and economic integration; the VAT small-business exemption election under Section 19 UStG for resident entrepreneurs below statutory turnover limits; and the option to tax real property under Section 9 UStG. Hybrid mismatch and reverse hybrid rules under the ATAD Implementation Act (Anti-Steuervermeidungsgesetz) disallow deduction of schemes producing double non-taxation; no elective opt-out from those rules exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary registration threshold for entrepreneurs making taxable supplies in Germany; registration is mandatory upon the first taxable transaction. The small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG applies only to entrepreneurs resident or established in Germany with prior-year turnover no more than EUR 22,000 and current-year expected turnover no more than EUR 50,000; a foreign exporter of bulk cement clinker is not entitled to rely on this exemption. For EU distance sales of goods, the Union-wide threshold is EUR 10,000, but this is not relevant to structured B2B ocean freight sales of clinker.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero rating under Section 4 No. 1 and Section 6 UStG applies to supplies of movable goods physically exported to a third-country destination outside the European Union. For bulk portland cement clinker sold for ocean shipping to non-EU ports, the exporter must prove that title and risk passed and that the goods were physically transported from Germany or another EU member state to the final non-EU destination; direct export by the supplier through the shipping agent or by the foreign buyer’s own transport both qualify if the goods leave EU customs territory. B2B services connected to the export and performed directly on the goods may also be exempt, while general offshore services to third-country customers are normally governed by the B2B place-of-supply reverse charge rule rather than automatic zero rating.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration and export accompanying document / electronic goods movement record with MRN number
  • Original or authenticatable Bill of Lading or sea waybill evidencing direct shipment from a German/EU port to the non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment evidence, including SWIFT MT103 or documentary letter of credit settlement documents, showing receipt of the export consideration from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice signed by the GmbH, packing list, port loading documents, and weight certificates for bulk clinker cargo
  • Destination customs entry record or consignee evidence when available, useful to corroborate arrival in the third-country destination

Germany is implementing a phased domestic B2B e-invoicing mandate: from 1 January 2025 every domestic B2B supplier must be technically able to receive structured e-invoices conforming to EN 16931, and from 1 January 2027 full structured e-invoicing obligation applies for domestic B2B transactions. Accepted formats include XRechnung and ZUGFeRD, transmitted through established networks or API-based exchange; EDI remains permissible if interoperable. Germany currently operates no continuous transaction control, no real-time fiscal clearance, no government-issued digital stamp, and no mandatory pre-clearance API. Export invoices for cement clinker may be issued outside the domestic e-invoicing infrastructure unless the counterparty agrees otherwise, but records must satisfy GoBD retention obligations.

If the export proof is missing, incomplete, or false, the zero rating is denied and VAT at 19% is assessed retroactively on the full invoice amount; associated input VAT deductions are clawed back. Statutory late-acceptance interest of 0.5% per month under Section 233a AO accrues from the original return date, and late-payment penalties under Section 240 AO may add up to 10% of the assessed VAT. Deliberate or reckless use of false export documents constitutes tax evasion under Section 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years, extended to up to ten years in especially serious cases, plus monetary fines and unlimited retrospective assessment; the standard VAT assessment limitation period is four years, extended to ten years in tax evasion cases. The Finanzamt and BZSt may conduct USt-Nachschau surprise audits and customs post-clearance verification, and export zero-rating claims for bulk high-value cargo are subject to heightened scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the EU free movement of capital framework. No restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or repatriation of capital for non-resident shareholders, subject to standard corporate law and tax compliance (e.g., withholding tax on dividends). No prior approval required for cross-border payments, but reporting obligations apply for certain transactions (e.g., external assets/liabilities reporting to Bundesbank).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Germany does not impose any statutory timeline or conversion ratio for export receipts; exporters may retain foreign currency balances freely. However, all cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes (AWV reporting).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision; FX administration is liberalized under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. German-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) under PSD2 can provide cross-border payment accounts and FX services. However, for high-volume trade settlements (e.g., SWIFT MT103, LC), traditional banks offer more robust correspondent banking networks and trade finance instruments. EMIs may face limitations on LC issuance and complex trade finance. Legal constraints: EMIs must hold a license from BaFin and comply with AML/KYC; they cannot accept deposits or provide credit. For a wholesale cement trader, EMIs are viable for operational payments but not for full treasury management.

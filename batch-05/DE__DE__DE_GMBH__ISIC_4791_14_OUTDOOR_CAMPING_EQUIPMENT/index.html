The statutory share capital is EUR 25,000. Before registration, at least EUR 12,500 must be paid in cash, with each shareholder paying at least 25% of its subscribed share and the total cash payment reaching EUR 12,500. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The local Finanzamt (municipal tax office) at the GmbH's registered seat of management has jurisdiction over corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT, and registration formalities; the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, Federal Central Tax Office) administers the VAT ID (USt-IdNr.), foreign VAT refunds, and certain exemption certificates. The mandatory electronic filing portal is ELSTER (www.elster.de); BZSt online services are available at www.bzst.de. The GmbH is incorporated by notarial deed, entered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), and then files the Tax Registration Questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung, Form 15a) with the competent local Finanzamt.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarial incorporation and registration in the Handelsregister; obtaining the company seat and the GmbH legal form. Step 2: Submit Form 15a (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically via ELSTER to the local Finanzamt within one month of incorporation; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (corporate tax and VAT reference number) and confirms registration for Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer, and Umsatzsteuer. Step 3: Apply separately to the BZSt for the VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) for intra-Community supply and export documentation; electronic application, typical issuance in three to four weeks. Step 4: Obtain an EORI number automatically via the Generalzolldirektion upon lodging the first customs export declaration through the ATLAS/eCustoms portal; no separate ad-hoc export license is required for ISIC 4791 retail goods. Non-resident shareholders do not alter the procedure; a tax representative is not required for an established German GmbH, but a steuerlicher Vertreter is mandatory for non-established foreign sellers under VAT law.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - the GmbH is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) treated as a separate tax subject for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer at 15%), solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag at 5.5% of the corporate tax), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, average effective rate approximately 14%). Territorial scope: worldwide taxation of resident GmbH income; profit distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to German dividend withholding tax of 25% plus solidarity surcharge, reducible under applicable double tax treaties. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the GmbH is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) treated as a separate tax subject for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer at 15%), solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag at 5.5% of the corporate tax), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, average effective rate approximately 14%). Territorial scope: worldwide taxation of resident GmbH income; profit distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to German dividend withholding tax of 25% plus solidarity surcharge, reducible under applicable double tax treaties. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable to a German resident GmbH. Pass-through treatment is not available under German domestic law regardless of foreign shareholder elections.

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a check-the-box regime; classification follows the statutory corporate form. Available elections: (i) German Organschaft (fiscal unity) under §14 KStG and §2 Abs. 2 GewStG, electable for consolidated corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT upon execution of a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) coupled with financial, economic, and organizational integration; the 2024 Organschaft reform supports indefinite agreements and clarifies the integration test; (ii) no small-business (Kleinunternehmer) election under §19 UStG is available to a GmbH; (iii) hybrid mismatch outcomes are neutralized automatically under the ATAD implementation (§4k EStG, §8b KStG) without elective relief; (iv) VAT grouping (Organschaft, §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG) is effectively mandatory once the three integration criteria are satisfied and requires no separate option. Foreign parents may not elect transparency for the German GmbH; the GmbH remains entity-level taxed for all German tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 - an established German GmbH and non-resident sellers are mandatorily VAT-registered from the first taxable supply; there is no domestic VAT registration threshold. The small-business relief (Kleinunternehmerregelung, §19 UStG) with a EUR 25,000 annual turnover threshold applicable from 2025 is reserved for natural persons and partnerships and is unavailable to a GmbH. The EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold for cross-border distance sales is relevant only within the OSS scheme and does not defer the GmbH's domestic VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (Ausfuhrlieferung, §6 UStG) qualify for the 0% VAT rate when the goods are physically dispatched or transported from Germany to a destination outside the EU, including online retail of outdoor camping equipment to third-country consumers, provided title has passed and the goods actually leave EU customs territory. Intra-Community supplies (§4 Nr. 1b, §6a UStG) are likewise zero-rated upon confirmation of the acquirer's VAT ID, dispatch into another Member State, EC Sales List filing, and retention of commercial transport documentation. Export-related B2B services are outside German VAT under the place-of-supply rules (§3a-c UStG) and are generally reverse-charged in the customer's jurisdiction. For e-commerce flows, marketplace operators may be deemed suppliers under §3 Abs. 3a UStG, requiring adjustment of the GmbH's invoicing and reporting. German law imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline for export zero-rating; the 180-day figure is retained as a due-diligence benchmark, while permanent non-payment may trigger audit inquiry into the genuineness of the export transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS/eCustoms Ausfuhranmeldung with official acceptance and exit confirmation from German customs)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR international transport document evidencing movement of the goods out of the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (German bank payment confirmation / SWIFT MT103 credit advice from the foreign buyer; not a statutory German VAT condition but commonly requested in cross-border audit procedures)
  • Commercial Export Invoice (Ausfuhrrechnung) referencing the EORI number, Incoterms, and full goods description
  • Proof of export completion (Abgangs- und Ausgangsnachweis from ATLAS) and, where applicable, the customer's foreign VAT ID confirmation for intra-Community supplies

German B2B e-invoicing mandate under the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025 every German-established supplier must be able to receive structured e-invoices compliant with EN 16931 (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD formats). From 1 January 2027, issuers with annual turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all issuers must issue e-invoices. E-invoices must contain the supplier's and recipient's VAT ID/Steuernummer and be transmitted electronically via an agreed exchange route (e.g., PEPPOL, EDI). Germany does not operate government real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API stamping, or digital stamping; transaction-based VAT e-reporting for B2B supplies is legislatively planned to commence in 2030. Non-compliant invoicing may disallow the recipient's input VAT deduction and trigger administrative fines under §379 AO.

If the zero-rating cannot be substantiated with the required customs and transport documentation within the statutory preservation period (ten years), the supply is re-characterized as domestic and taxed at 19% (or 7%), with retrospective clawback of any input VAT credited. Penalties include: interest on arrears under §233a AO at 1.8% per annum (rate revised from 2025), late-payment surcharges of 1% per month under §240 AO, and administrative fines under §378 AO for negligent misstatements up to EUR 50,000. Intentional tax evasion under §370 AO carries imprisonment or fines, extension of the assessment limitation period to ten years, and full recovery of wrongly refunded VAT. The BZSt and the competent Finanzamt perform risk-based audits of e-commerce exporters, focusing on fabricated exports, missing customer identity, circular payment flows, and misdirection of intra-Community supplies; seller's strict liability can arise under §6a Abs. 4 UStG where the customer's VAT ID was not properly validated. German law does not impose a mandatory FX repatriation deadline, but persistent absence of incoming payments may be treated in audit as indicium of a sham export transaction and thereby trigger the same clawback and penalty framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU Treaty obligations and OECD codes. Profit remittance is unrestricted; dividends are subject to standard corporate income tax and a 26.375% withholding tax (including solidarity surcharge) unless reduced by tax treaty. No exchange controls or approval requirements exist for cross-border capital flows, but mandatory reporting to the Bundesbank applies for statistical purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirements exist for export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances freely. However, all cross-border payments (including export proceeds) must be reported to the Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics, typically via the electronic reporting system (e.g., 'Zahlungsbilanzstatistik') within specified deadlines (usually monthly or quarterly depending on transaction volume).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision; FX control and statistical reporting are administered by the Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German BaFin requires a full banking or EMI license for deposit-taking and payment services. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Revolut, N26, or other EU-passported entities) can operate in Germany under EU passporting, but for a GmbH requiring capital escrow and high-volume trade settlements, traditional tier-1 banks are preferred due to lower counterparty risk and broader correspondent banking networks. Fintech EMIs are viable for operational accounts and low-value transactions, but not for initial capital injection or large trade disbursements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge) on the CT, producing an effective federal rate of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is imposed at a municipality-specific rate (average ~14%), yielding a combined effective profit tax burden of approximately 29–33% for a resident GmbH.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the 25% WHT amount, giving a combined statutory dividend withholding tax of 26.375% on distributions by a German GmbH to non-resident shareholders. Qualified corporate shareholders within the EU may claim a 0% rate under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Directive 2011/96/EU) where a 10% or greater interest is held for an uninterrupted period of at least 12 months; most German DTAs reduce the rate to 5% for shareholders owning at least 10% of the voting capital and to 15% for portfolio investors; a small number of treaties grant 0% on participations of 25% or more.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year GmbH the return must be submitted by 31 July of the following year; where the return is professionally prepared by a German tax advisor (Steuerberater), the deadline is automatically extended to the last day of the fourteenth month after the end of the tax year, i.e. 28/29 February of the second following year (§ 149 AO). Fiscal years differing from the calendar year are computed from the close of the respective fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation under § 90 Abs. 3 AO applies where related-party transactions in a fiscal year exceed €5,000,000 for the supply of goods and €500,000 for the supply of services. The Local File must be produced within 60 days of a German tax audit request; the Master File within 30 days; a Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue of at least €750 million. E-commerce outdoor-equipment retail operations involving affiliated fulfilment centres, warehouses, payment processing entities or foreign suppliers will trigger these thresholds when intercompany flows exceed the monetary caps.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not adopt a formal Economic Substance Regulation register as seen in the UAE or Caribbean jurisdictions; the substance requirement is instead enforced through the German General Anti-Abuse Rule (§ 42 AO), the OECD/G20 MLI principal purpose test, and the beneficial-owner doctrine under § 50d EStG. A GmbH claiming treaty benefits or § 8b dividend exemptions must maintain a genuine commercial presence: resident managing director, physical registered office, business premises, employee payroll, board minutes, and substantive purchase-sales documentation. A letterbox company lacking these indicia is either disregarded or its seat is re-located to the actual place of management (§ 11 AO); the tax consequences are determined as if the transactions were concluded between unrelated parties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under § 370 AO with imprisonment of up to five years; in aggravated cases involving large German tax losses or professional abettors, imprisonment of up to ten years. Parallel fiscal sanctions include an additional fine under § 375 AO of up to €50,000, back taxes plus 6% p.a. interest, late payment surcharges of 1% per month, and seizure of the offender's personal assets. Non-resident directors are extraditable on an EU arrest warrant.

Director personal liability: The managing director (Geschäftsführer) is personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under § 34 AO and § 69 AO where the non-payment arises from an intentional or grossly negligent breach of duty; the tax authority may issue a Haftungsbescheid (personal assessment) directly against the director without first executing against the GmbH. A criminal conviction can lead to travel bans, disqualification from holding corporate directorships, and asset freezes under German criminal procedure.
Shell Company and Loss Trafficking (Mantelkauf)

Statutory sanction: Complete or pro-rata forfeiture of loss carryforwards under § 8c KStG upon a change of ownership exceeding 50% (all losses) or exceeding 25% (pro-rata 25% loss forfeiture). The Finanzamt may additionally recharacterise the commercial structure as an abusive arrangement under § 42 AO, deny treaty benefits under the MLI PPT, and impose redisclosed assessments with interest at 6% p.a. Procurement of a shell company for fraudulent purposes triggers criminal fraud provisions under § 263 StGB.

Director personal liability: A director knowingly implementing or continuing the shell-company structure is jointly and severally liable for the taxes re-assessed; the Haftungsbescheid route under § 34 AO is available. Deliberate concealment of ownership from the beneficial-owner register (Transparenzregister) is a criminal offence and results in personal fines under the German Money Laundering Act and possible imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Under § 162 Abs. 4 AO the district tax office imposes a penalty of 5% of the assessed additional income resulting from the transfer-pricing adjustment, with a minimum of €5,000 per audit year; if reasonable-cause grounds are absent, an administrative fine of €100 per business day of delay applies, capped at €1,000,000. The Finanzamt may also estimate taxable income on adverse evidence, disregarding the taxpayer's transfer prices entirely.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally accountable under § 34 AO for the failure to discharge German tax compliance duties, including the timely upkeep and production of transfer-pricing documentation. If the missing documentation prevents the correct tax assessment, the director may be made personally liable for the consequent additional tax and the 5% documentation surcharge; repeated or deliberate failure opens the door to criminal liability under § 370 AO.
AML and Payment-Channel Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under § 56 GwG of up to €5,000,000 for legal entities, or 10% of annual turnover; natural persons face fines of up to €100,000 per violation. Money laundering per se carries imprisonment of up to five years under § 261 StGB; the commercial proceeds are confiscated; the GmbH may lose its payment-acquiring licences from banking partners, and its beneficial owner may be debarred from company formation in Germany for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal sentences and fines for failing to designate an AML officer, failing to maintain registers, or intentionally structuring payments to avoid thresholds. Under the German regulatory regime, the director is personally responsible for gate-keeping of the company's financial streams; a Haftungsbescheid may be issued where undeclared receipts are traced to a director's personal account. In severe cases, bail conditions may include a travel ban and surrender of the passport while criminal and tax investigations are pending.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the Local Court (Amtsgericht) at the registered office, operating through the federal electronic court and administrative mailbox (EGVP).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG), Sections 1-3 (formation of GmbH), 5 (minimum capital EUR 25,000), 8 (contents of the commercial register application), 10 (entry in the Commercial Register), 35 (directors) and 39 (registering of directors).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized articles of association and joint application for registration in the Commercial Register (Antrag auf Eintragung in das Handelsregister).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 42 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum subscribed capital EUR 25,000. On formation at least one half, EUR 12,500, must be paid in cash and be freely available to management. The capital is deposited into a restricted bank account used for formation and released in accordance with the articles; non-cash contributions require a fully reasoned formation report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety walkthrough with fire extinguishers, emergency exits and signage; structural load declaration for physical archive or vault fittings; electrical installation operating certificate under Betriebssicherheitsverordnung §3; and occupancy no-objection from the municipal building authority.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed capital EUR 25,000. On formation at least one half, EUR 12,500, must be paid in cash and be freely available to management. The capital is deposited into a restricted bank account used for formation and released in accordance with the articles; non-cash contributions require a fully reasoned formation report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification numbers; Local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and trade tax; electronic filing portal at www.elster.de, BZSt portal at www.bzst.de.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity must: 1) submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER or manually to the competent Finanzamt; 2) receive a local Steuernummer (tax number); 3) apply separately to BZSt for the USt-IdNr (VAT identification number); 4) if trading, register with the municipal trade tax office (Gemeinde); 5) if seeking charitable status, submit an application for exemption with articles of incorporation and final purpose statement to the Finanzamt.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entity must: 1) submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER or manually to the competent Finanzamt; 2) receive a local Steuernummer (tax number); 3) apply separately to BZSt for the USt-IdNr (VAT identification number); 4) if trading, register with the municipal trade tax office (Gemeinde); 5) if seeking charitable status, submit an application for exemption with articles of incorporation and final purpose statement to the Finanzamt.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide check-the-box elections. A GmbH may elect to form an Organschaft (fiscal unity) for corporate income tax and VAT with its controlling entity. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) are implemented and may require alignment of tax treatment for hybrid instruments and entities; no transparent pass-through election is available for a GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general mandatory VAT registration threshold for established businesses; small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmer) applies where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 in the current year and no more than EUR 50,000 in the following year. Foreign entities have no threshold and are subject to registration on the first taxable supply in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply is eligible for zero-rating as an export supply when goods are physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU by the supplier or the buyer, and proof of export is retained in accordance with §§ 6, 9 of the German VAT Act (UStG) and Section 8 of the VAT Application Decree. Also zero-rated are certain services directly connected with exported goods, such as transport and handling. The supplier must obtain and keep all documentary evidence within 90 days from the date of supply.

  • Customs export declaration with customs confirmation (ATLAS/Ausfuhrbegleitschein)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note for transport to third country
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Proof of payment (bank transfer or credit advice)
  • Delivery receipt or written confirmation from the foreign recipient
  • Export accompanying document (Form 0450) if applicable

From 1 January 2025, all Germany-established businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (e-invoices) in a data-ready format such as XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, or Peppol BIS. Germany has no real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance, or digital stamping system; VAT returns are filed periodically via ELSTER, not on an invoice-level basis. E-invoices must be transmitted directly to the recipient, and the supplier is required to secure verification of receipt.

Tax authorities may retrospectively revoke zero-rating if export evidence is incomplete or not produced within the 90-day statutory period; VAT at 19% is assessed on the transaction value with interest of 1% per calendar month, and under § 370 of the Fiscal Code (AO) evasion penalties may include imprisonment up to 10 years and fines up to EUR 1,000,000. In addition, the exporter is jointly and severally liable for VAT due, and full clawback may be enforced through forced account seizures and cross-border collections.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no general exchange controls, mandatory repatriation rules, capital import restrictions, or routine approval requirements for foreign ownership, capital contributions, dividends, loan repayments, or service payments. Profit distributions by a GmbH remain subject to German corporate-law requirements, including distributable retained earnings, annual accounts, insolvency protection, preservation of registered share capital, withholding-tax analysis, and any applicable charitable-purpose restrictions. A GmbH recognized as tax-privileged must apply assets exclusively and directly to statutory charitable purposes and must not make impermissible private-benefit distributions. Cross-border payments may be subject to sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, tax documentation, beneficial-owner verification, and statistical reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros or repatriated through a German bank. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts or abroad, subject to contractual, tax, accounting, sanctions, AML, and bank-reporting requirements. Cross-border payment reporting and external-position reporting under the AWV may apply, subject to statutory exemptions and thresholds that must be checked against the current Deutsche Bundesbank reporting rules. Export documentation, invoices, customs records, shipping evidence, and contractual payment terms should be retained to support the commercial purpose and accounting treatment.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank; banking and payment-services supervision is primarily conducted by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin), with the European Central Bank supervising significant credit institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment execution and collection; low to medium as a complete substitute for a German relationship bank. An electronic-money institution or payment institution licensed in Germany or another EEA state may provide SEPA accounts, payment accounts, virtual IBANs, foreign exchange, and international transfers under authorization or passporting rules. Safeguarded e-money or payment funds are not equivalent to bank deposits and generally do not provide a German deposit-guarantee claim. The provider may refuse charitable-grant activity, high-risk countries, complex ownership, cash activity, correspondent banking, or transactions lacking transparent economic purpose. A fintech account is generally unsuitable as the sole account for GmbH share-capital evidence, statutory-registry coordination, restricted charitable funds, or high-value treasury liquidity. Verify license status in the BaFin or relevant EEA register, safeguarding model, IBAN ownership, access to SWIFT or correspondent rails, safeguarding-bank concentration, insolvency treatment, transaction limits, and termination rights.

Defense evidence: Notarial formation deed; articles of association; shareholder list; managing-director appointment; commercial-register application or extract; evidence of registered office; share-capital payment instruction; formation purpose; and post-registration request to convert the formation account into an operational account.
Opaque ownership, nominee shareholders, layered foreign entities, trusts, foundations, or inability to identify the natural-person ultimate beneficial owner.

Defense evidence: Certified ownership chart to natural persons; commercial-register extracts; constitutional documents for every ownership layer; shareholder registers; UBO declarations; identification documents; tax-residence information; source-of-wealth statements; and written explanation of any foundation, donor, protector, or control arrangement.

Defense evidence: Recognized charitable-purpose notice or tax-office documentation, if available; articles specifying charitable purposes; grant policy; board or management resolutions; beneficiary eligibility criteria; annual budget; donor agreements; project descriptions; expected countries and counterparties; and evidence that grants are made exclusively for permitted purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder capital contributions through an account held in the GmbH's legal name, preferably from an account held in the contributing shareholder's own legal name. Use a clear payment reference such as 'Einlage Stammkapital' or 'share-capital contribution' and match the amount to the notarial formation documents and shareholder resolutions. For post-formation equity injections, document whether the payment is a capital increase, additional paid-in capital, shareholder contribution, or shareholder loan, and obtain the required corporate approvals and notarial or commercial-register filings where applicable. For EUR payments, use SEPA Credit Transfer or TARGET-related bank rails; for non-EUR payments, use SWIFT customer credit transfer, generally ISO 20022 pacs.008 or legacy MT103 where still supported. Provide the bank with the formation deed, source-of-funds evidence, shareholder resolution, loan agreement if applicable, and expected transaction profile. Germany does not generally use universal statutory payment-purpose codes for ordinary corporate wires, but banks may require internal purpose classifications, and AWV reporting may apply to qualifying cross-border payments or external positions. Confirm current reporting thresholds, exemptions, and filing responsibility with Deutsche Bundesbank before execution. Do not route capital through directors, donors, unrelated affiliates, personal accounts, or anonymous payment intermediaries.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro-denominated EEA payments where the beneficiary, invoice, sanctions profile, and payment purpose are verified. Use SWIFT customer credit transfer, commonly MT103 during legacy-system transition or ISO 20022 pacs.008 through compatible banks, for non-SEPA or non-EUR payments. Payments should identify the GmbH as ordering customer, include the verified beneficiary legal name and address, invoice or grant reference, commercial or charitable purpose, and complete intermediary-bank information. For goods transactions, retain purchase orders, invoices, customs declarations, transport documents, import or export records, and evidence of delivery. For grant distributions, retain grant agreements, board approvals, beneficiary due diligence, end-use evidence, sanctions screening, and confirmation that the payment is within the entity's charitable purposes. Commercial letters of credit may be issued or advised through a relationship bank under UCP 600, with documentary collections under URC 522 where appropriate. Confirm Incoterms, documentary requirements, sanctions clauses, discrepancy fees, foreign-exchange allocation, and applicant or beneficiary exposure before issuance. Avoid third-party payments unless contractually justified, fully documented, and pre-approved by the bank.Deliverable forward exchange contracts for identifiable forecast or contracted EUR and foreign-currency exposures, subject to bank suitability assessment, documented hedge purpose, counterparty limits, collateral terms, and EMIR and MiFID-related obligations where applicable.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies with limited convertibility or local settlement restrictions, provided the bank can lawfully offer the product, the reference rate and settlement currency are defined, and the transaction is used for genuine exposure management rather than speculation.Cross-currency swaps or currency swaps for documented long-term funding, shareholder-loan, grant-funding, or investment exposures, subject to board authorization, counterparty credit limits, valuation, collateral, accounting, and regulatory documentation.FX options, collars, and other structured hedges may be used only after suitability, cost, scenario, liquidity, and downside-risk review; complex or leveraged structures should be prohibited unless expressly approved by the governing body and the relationship bank.For a charitable entity, derivatives should be limited to risk reduction for documented operational, funding, or investment exposures. Maintain a treasury policy covering permitted currencies, hedge ratios, tenor limits, counterparty ratings, collateral, valuation, accounting treatment, termination rights, and prohibition of naked speculative positions.

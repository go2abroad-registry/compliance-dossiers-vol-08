Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office, competent for issuing the German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr) and administering cross-border VAT refunds; local municipal tax offices (Finanzämter) — competent for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), VAT returns, and tax registration; Generalzolldirektion / Hauptzollamt — customs administration for export declarations (ATLAS/ECS) and exporter registration; electronic filing and clearance portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt online application portal (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

Within one month of registration in the German Commercial Register (Handelsregister), the GmbH must file the tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') electronically via ELSTER with the competent Finanzamt, which issues the corporate tax number (Steuernummer); the BZSt then issues the VAT ID (USt-IdNr) under § 27a UStG for cross-border transactions — application submitted via ELSTER or BZSt form 2012, typically granted within 2–4 weeks; for customs exports the entity must obtain an EORI number (DE prefix) from the Generalzolldirektion; a non-established foreign entity making taxable supplies in Germany registers directly with the Finanzamt via a limited VAT registration questionnaire or, for VAT refunds only, files with the BZSt under § 18(9) UStG; a German tax clearance certificate (Unbedenklichkeitsbescheinigung) may be requested from the Finanzamt to evidence tax standing to counterparties and banks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a GmbH is a German capital company taxed at entity level: Körperschaftsteuer 15.0%, solidarity surcharge 0.825%, and Gewerbesteuer at 3.5% of the municipal Hebesatz (typical effective 7.0%–17.15%; e.g., Berlin 14.35%, Munich 17.15%); total effective statutory burden approximately 22.8%–33.0% on undistributed profits; no pass-through regime applies; shareholder distributions are subject to 25% withholding tax plus 5.5% surcharge (reduced under applicable tax treaties). — Within one month of registration in the German Commercial Register (Handelsregister), the GmbH must file the tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') electronically via ELSTER with the competent Finanzamt, which issues the corporate tax number (Steuernummer); the BZSt then issues the VAT ID (USt-IdNr) under § 27a UStG for cross-border transactions — application submitted via ELSTER or BZSt form 2012, typically granted within 2–4 weeks; for customs exports the entity must obtain an EORI number (DE prefix) from the Generalzolldirektion; a non-established foreign entity making taxable supplies in Germany registers directly with the Finanzamt via a limited VAT registration questionnaire or, for VAT refunds only, files with the BZSt under § 18(9) UStG; a German tax clearance certificate (Unbedenklichkeitsbescheinigung) may be requested from the Finanzamt to evidence tax standing to counterparties and banks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a GmbH is a German capital company taxed at entity level: Körperschaftsteuer 15.0%, solidarity surcharge 0.825%, and Gewerbesteuer at 3.5% of the municipal Hebesatz (typical effective 7.0%–17.15%; e.g., Berlin 14.35%, Munich 17.15%); total effective statutory burden approximately 22.8%–33.0% on undistributed profits; no pass-through regime applies; shareholder distributions are subject to 25% withholding tax plus 5.5% surcharge (reduced under applicable tax treaties).

Available election forms

German law provides no check-the-box election; a GmbH is mandatorily a taxable corporation. Available group and anti-hybrid mechanisms: (1) corporate and trade tax Organschaft (fiscal unity) under §§ 14–19 KStG with a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag), financial and organizational integration — consolidated tax filing; (2) VAT Organschaft under § 2(2) no. 2 UStG upon financial, economic, and organizational integration — the controlling entity files a single consolidated VAT return; (3) hybrid mismatch restrictions under ATAD 2 (§ 4k EStG) disallow double-deduction arrangements; (4) § 8b KStG applies a 95% exemption to inter-corporate dividends; (5) treaty-based exemption or foreign tax credit for German permanent establishments can be elected where a double tax treaty applies; foreign check-the-box classification of the GmbH by its shareholders does not alter its German entity-level tax status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For German-established entrepreneurs the small-business exemption (§ 19 UStG) applies where prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 22,000 and expected current-year turnover ≤ EUR 50,000 — exceeding either threshold triggers mandatory VAT registration; non-established foreign entrepreneurs are taxable persons without any threshold for supplies with a place of supply in Germany; intra-EU distance sales and OSS-covered services are registered where cross-border sales exceed EUR 10,000 (EU-wide threshold); export and import-related supplies are outside any threshold and require registration/EORI irrespective of volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries (Ausfuhrlieferungen) from Germany to non-EU third countries are exempt at 0% VAT under § 4 no. 1a and § 6 UStG where the goods physically leave the EU customs territory from Germany and the exporter holds the statutory export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis, §§ 8–10 Ausfuhrverordnung); the exit must occur within the statutory transport period (generally 3 months, § 6(2a) UStG); for wholesale of virgin polyethylene/polypropylene granules shipped by sea, the ocean Bill of Lading and ATLAS exit confirmation are the primary exit evidence; intra-EU B2B dispatches are exempt under § 6a UStG subject to the customer's valid USt-IdNr and recapitulative statement; offshore-related services and trading margins attributable to non-EU business customers are outside the scope of German VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rules (§ 3a(2) UStG).

  • ATLAS/ECS customs export declaration with Movement Reference Number (MRN) and German exit confirmation (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument summary message)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), CMR consignment note, or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms, polymer grade, net weight, quantity, EORI and USt-IdNr references
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Customs proof of exit certificate (Ausfuhrbescheinigung) or electronic ATLAS exit status statement
  • Supporting logistics records (packing list, container lists, rail/port gate receipts, weighing certificates) per ExportV documentary rules

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing phases in under the Wachstumschancengesetz: from 1 January 2025 all domestic suppliers must be able to receive EN 16931-compliant structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x); mandatory issuance applies to businesses with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 from 1 January 2026, expanding to all businesses by 1 January 2028 (paper or other electronic formats remain permitted during the transitional periods per § 34(2a) UStG); Germany does not currently operate a centralized real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mechanism — e-invoices are exchanged bilaterally via PEPPOL, APIs, or secure structured-file transmission, with VAT data reported periodically through VAT returns and intra-EU recapitulative statements (Zusammenfassende Meldung) via ELSTER; the EU ViDA framework will introduce mandatory transaction-level digital reporting to the BZSt from approximately 2030.

Export zero-rating is closely audited by the Finanzamt and customs investigation (Zollfahndung) for fictitious exports, missing-trader chains, and fabricated exit documents. Deficient or late exit evidence triggers retroactive assessment at 19% VAT plus 6% annual late-payment interest and late-filing surcharges (Verspätungszuschlag up to EUR 25,000, § 152 AO); incorrectly zero-rated invoices shift the liability to the issuer under § 14c UStG; intentional tax evasion under § 370 AO is punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years, unlimited fines, and forfeiture; negligent misconduct attracts administrative fines up to EUR 30,000 under § 379 AO; reassessment may cover 4 ordinary limitation years, extended to 10 years for evasion (§ 169 AO); where export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, the exporter should consider a bad-debt adjustment under § 17 UStG and expect enhanced documentary inquiry.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. Neither the Bundesbank nor BAFA imposes exchange controls, prior authorizations, or compulsory conversion requirements on resident entities. The GmbH may freely hold EUR and foreign-currency accounts, invoice in EUR or USD, and remit dividends, loan repayments, or trade payments at any time. There are no statutory profit remittance hurdles beyond standard German corporate law: distributable profit must be evidenced in annual financial statements, and any remittance must not jeopardize the GmbH solvency under the German Insolvency Code (InsO §§ 15a, 64). Cross-border payments between related parties must be supported by transfer pricing documentation under the Foreign Tax Act (AStG §§ 1, 90). The AWV imposes only statistical reporting duties on cross-border payment flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters retain full discretion over settlement currency, timing, and use of proceeds. The only binding obligation is quantitative reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV §§ 59-63: any cross-border payment or claim from a non-resident exceeding €12,500 per calendar month must be reported electronically within seven days after the end of the relevant calendar month through the External Economic and Payments Statistics (EKP) system. No EUR conversion, deposit period, or repatriation quota is mandated.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), together with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA), operating under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low. E-money institutions (EMIs) passporting into Germany under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2), such as Airwallex, Wise Business, or PPRO, are legally permitted to hold client funds in safeguarded pool accounts, execute SWIFT MT103 payments, and provide multi-currency wallets. However, they are not licensed under the German Banking Act (KWG) to conduct credit business, issue letters of credit or guarantees, or underwrite commercial documentary trade finance. For a wholesale polymer import operation dependent on irrevocable LCs and high-value settlements, an EMI-only treasury structure is legally inadequate and operationally constrained by BaFin safeguarding rules and absence of commodity trade underwriting capacity. The viable role is a supplementary rail for sub-threshold disbursements, not the primary clearing infrastructure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder remits the GmbH statutory minimum capital of €25,000, of which at least €12,500 (50%) must be paid in cash immediately, via SWIFT MT103 to the German capital escrow or operational account. The MT103 must reference the beneficiary IBAN, include the payment annotation 'Einlage Stammkapital' (or 'Kapitalerhöhung' for subsequent injections), and carry the balance-of-payments purpose classification 'Direct Investment Equity Capital' for EKP reporting. Where the aggregate inbound transfer exceeds €12,500 in a calendar month, the GmbH must file a Bundesbank EKP report within seven days after month-end. For formation or capital increase, the Commercial Register filing must be lodged with evidence of the paid-up capital. Compliance verification includes sanctions screening of the remitting bank and UBO and preservation of the bank credit confirmation demonstrating that the contribution is earmarked and not withdrawn prior to registration.For the purchase of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules from foreign producers, the primary clearing rails are: (i) an Irrevocable Letter of Credit confirmed by a top-tier German bank, issued via SWIFT MT700 and settled through MT103 and MT7-series reimbursement in accordance with UCP 600; and (ii) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for LC reimbursements and open-account settlements where counterparty risk is mitigated by proven bilateral trading history. Documentary Collections (DP/DA) under URC 522 serve as a secondary rail for mid-sized suppliers. EUR-denominated payments to EU counterparties are executed via SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant (SCT Inst) up to €100,000 per transaction, while USD settlements clear through CHIPS or Fedwire using USD nostro accounts held by German correspondent banks. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-Low. E-money institutions (EMIs) passporting into Germany under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2), such as Airwallex, Wise Business, or PPRO, are legally permitted to hold client funds in safeguarded pool accounts, execute SWIFT MT103 payments, and provide multi-currency wallets. However, they are not licensed under the German Banking Act (KWG) to conduct credit business, issue letters of credit or guarantees, or underwrite commercial documentary trade finance. For a wholesale polymer import operation dependent on irrevocable LCs and high-value settlements, an EMI-only treasury structure is legally inadequate and operationally constrained by BaFin safeguarding rules and absence of commodity trade underwriting capacity. The viable role is a supplementary rail for sub-threshold disbursements, not the primary clearing infrastructure.

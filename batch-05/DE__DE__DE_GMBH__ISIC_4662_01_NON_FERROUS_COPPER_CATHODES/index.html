Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register) maintained by the Registry Division of the competent local court (Amtsgericht); all filings are submitted exclusively through a German notary (Notarin/Notar) via the secured electronic register channel.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbHG), in particular §§ 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9a, and 10; commercial register procedure under Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB) §§ 8–16; notarization requirements under Beurkundungsgesetz (BeurkG); trade registration under Gewerbeordnung (GewO) §§ 14–15.

Formation / constitutional filings

Anmeldung zur Eintragung einer GmbH – the notarially certified application for registration of the company, accompanied by the notarized articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag), appointment of managing directors, specimen signatures, bank confirmation of paid-in capital, and registered-office declaration; filed electronically under § 10 GmbHG and § 12 HGB.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 21 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000 per § 5(1) GmbHG. Before registration, at least EUR 12,500 of cash contributions must be paid in and be at the free disposal of the managing director; the payment is typically placed in a bank capital escrow account (Sperrkonto) with a German or EU credit institution, and the bank's confirmation is annexed to the registration filing. For a single-member GmbH, the shareholder must additionally provide security for the unpaid balance (§ 8(2) sentence 2 GmbHG).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: a Brandschutznachweis (fire-resistance certificate) per the Model Building Code (MBO) and VV-TB must be issued for the office and any warehouse area. Structural clearance: load-capacity inspection for high-density copper cathode racks confirming floor-load bearing capacity. Occupational clearance: compliance with Arbeitsschutzgesetz (ArbSchG), Arbeitsstättenverordnung (ArbStättV), and BetrSichV, including workplace risk assessments, operational instructions, and registration of the business with the Berufsgenossenschaft Handel und Warenlogistik (BGHW) for statutory accident insurance.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable – wholesale purchase, sale, and physical arbitrage of Grade A refined copper cathodes is a free trade under § 1(1) Gewerbeordnung and does not trigger any sectoral industrial licensing board. The municipal Gewerbeamt (trade office) is the only competent authority for the general trade registration (Gewerbeanmeldung). If warehousing premises are operated, the local building supervision authority (Bauaufsichtsbehörde) has ancillary structural jurisdiction.

Category: None – no industrial operating permit (Erlaubnis) under GewO is required for trading refined copper cathodes. The company will hold only the generally applicable Gewerbeschein (trade registration certificate); no manufacturing, smelting, refining, or scrap-processing permit is triggered by the pure wholesale-trading business model.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany applies statutory-type classification (Typenzwang): a GmbH is always an opaque corporation and no U.S.-style check-the-box or S-corporation election exists. Available elections and mechanisms: (1) fiscal unity (Organschaft) for corporate income tax (§ 14 KStG), trade tax (§ 2(2) GewStG) and VAT (§ 2(2) UStG) — requires at least 75% of shares and voting rights plus an executed profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) for a minimum five-year term; (2) option to tax otherwise exempt real estate transactions (§ 9 UStG); (3) small-enterprise VAT regime (§ 19 UStG) where the prior-year turnover is at most EUR 25,000 and the current-year forecast at most EUR 100,000 — practically unavailable to a wholesale commodity trader; (4) reverse-charge designation for specified § 13b UStG supplies; (5) German hybrid-mismatch neutralization under § 4k EStG and treaty-shopping guard under § 50d EStG, which override any dual-classification arbitrage in hybrid structures such as a GmbH & Co. KG (KG transparent, GmbH general partner opaque).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully convertible currency regime as a founding member of the Eurozone and under EU Regulation (EU) No 2015/847 on payment services. Both capital and current accounts are freely convertible with no restrictions on profit remittances, capital repatriation, or dividend distributions for resident GmbH entities. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to standard KYC/AML verification, beneficial ownership disclosure, and tax authority notifications for cross-border transfers exceeding EUR 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds is required under German law. Banks must report FX transactions above reporting thresholds under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AuWGrG) and Money Laundering Act (GwG). For copper cathode exports, surrender is voluntary; proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts with supporting documentation including commercial invoices, transport documents, and LME warrant confirmations.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs due to strict German banking regulations and absence of local licensing for foreign fintechs; cross-border EMI operations require mandatory partnership with licensed German commercial banks or payment institutions authorized under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where annual cross-border controlled transactions with a related party exceed EUR 1,000,000 for goods or EUR 100,000 for services; Master File mandatory for groups with consolidated turnover above EUR 100,000,000; CbC Report mandatory when group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Germany enforces substance-over-form and beneficial ownership requirements for treaty relief under §50d EStG, the general anti-abuse rule under §42 AO, and EU ATAD/GAAR substance-oriented tests. A GmbH trading copper cathodes must evidence physical premises, qualified trading personnel, real price-risk management, independent decision-making, and proper books. No standalone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent exists in Germany, but shell arrangements will be disregarded for tax purposes and may trigger anti-money-laundering prosecution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine under §370 AO; in especially severe large-scale evasion, imprisonment 6–10 years; reassessment of evaded taxes plus 6% annual interest; seizure of assets and publication of the conviction possible

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers are jointly and severally liable under §69 AO for evaded taxes; assets may be attached; travel bans and passport surrender can be imposed pending trial; disqualification from serving as director for up to 5 years
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Surcharge under §162(4) AO of at least €10,000 per document and up to €1,000,000; tax authority may estimate taxable income adversely under §162(1) AO; underlying tax adjustment plus interest

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for accounting and disclosure are personally liable for TP surcharges and underpaid corporate tax where their culpable failure caused the assessment shortfall; criminal liability may attach if false documentation is knowingly submitted
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance on Dividends and Royalties

Statutory sanction: The GmbH as withholding agent is liable for the full unwithheld amount under §§44(5), 50a(5) EStG and §§73–74 AO; late-payment interest accrues; additional penalty of up to €25,000 may be imposed under §50a EStG; the business expense deduction may be denied for the grossed-up royalty

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under §69 AO for unremitted withholding taxes if the failure is intentional or grossly negligent; attachment reaches both GmbH assets and personal assets; administrative fines under §§378–380 AO apply
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement / AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Artificial arrangement disregarded under §42 AO, with income reallocated to the true substance holder; AML fines under the GwG of up to €200,000 for ordinary violations and up to €5 million or 10% of group turnover for serious corporate violations; money laundering under §261 StGB carries imprisonment up to 5 years; corporate fines under §30 OWiG up to €10 million

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors face criminal prosecution, confiscation of trading profits, travel bans pending investigation, civil restitution for creditors, a ban on future managerial office under §§6 GmbHG and §70 StGB, and in cross-border cases red notices and extradition

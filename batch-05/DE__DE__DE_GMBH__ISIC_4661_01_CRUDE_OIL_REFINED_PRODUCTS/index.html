Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Local Finanzamt competent for the GmbH's registered seat for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT assessment; Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the cross-border VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.); Generalzolldirektion (German Customs) for EORI registration and export control confirmations; digital filing portal ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Upon notarial incorporation and entry in the Handelsregister, the local Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (corporate income tax / trade tax / VAT number) on a prospective basis. The GmbH must submit the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (mandatory tax registration questionnaire under Sec. 138 AO) through ELSTER within four weeks of commencing business, disclosing contemplated turnover, payroll, VAT treatment of sales and any cross-border intra-Community activity. The USt-IdNr. is applied for separately from the BZSt (Form 14 / ELSTER) once the Steuernummer exists or immediately before transacting with EU counterparties. Non-resident entrepreneurs or foreign sellers without a domestic establishment must apply for VAT registration under Sec. 27a UStG with the Finanzamt responsible for the place of their taxable activity before any German supply takes place; a customs EORI number must be procured from the Generalzolldirektion to lodge export declarations in ATLAS.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A German GmbH is a resident, non-transparent corporation (Sec. 1 KStG) subject to entity-level corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge and trade tax on worldwide income; no check-the-box or transparency election exists for the GmbH itself. The material elections available are: (i) Organschaft fiscal unity under Sec. 14 KStG and Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG, requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) registered with the commercial register, permitting corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT consolidation with the parent; (ii) election of intra-Community zero-rating under Sec. 6a UStG, conditional on the customer's valid VAT ID and reporting in the monthly recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung); (iii) hybrid-mismatch treatment is not elective — the German ATAD implementation (Sec. 4k EStG) imposes mandatory disallowance outcomes for hybrid financial instruments and reverse-hybrid structures; and (iv) the partnership taxation option of Sec. 1a KStG is unavailable to a GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies to the GmbH: VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable supply (Sec. 2 UStG). The small-business exemption of Sec. 19 UStG (EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 expected current-year turnover) is unavailable in substance to a wholesale marine-fuel brokerage GmbH that recovers input VAT and is never available to non-resident entrepreneurs. Any non-resident supplier making a taxable supply of goods or services in Germany (other than reverse-charge-only transactions) triggers a mandatory German VAT registration without a minimum turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries (Ausfuhrlieferungen) to non-EU destinations qualify for 0% VAT under Sec. 4 No. 1(a) and Sec. 6 UStG where the goods physically leave the EU customs territory and the supplier holds documentary proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis). Intra-Community supplies qualify under Sec. 4 No. 1(b) and Sec. 6a UStG only where the purchaser holds a valid EU VAT ID, the goods are transported directly to another member state, and the supply is reflected in the periodic Zusammenfassende Meldung. Marine bunker deliveries of fuel oil and distillates to seagoing vessels engaged in international traffic are zero-rated under the German implementation of Council Directive 2006/112/EC Art. 148(a), evidenced by the vessel's IMO registry certificate, outward voyage documentation, and the supplier's purchase and delivery records; deliveries supplied from duty-suspended customs warehouse installations are equally outside domestic VAT scope by operation of customs territoriality rules. Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline or FOREX turnaround rule, yet an inward hard-currency settlement within the 180-day standby letter-of-credit tenor materially corroborates the commercial reality of the export during documentary audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS Ausfuhranmeldung with Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill endorsed with loading port, discharge port and vessel IMO number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and SWIFT MT103 evidencing settlement of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and delivery receipts compliant with Sec. 14 UStG content requirements
  • EU customer VAT-ID confirmation (VIES verification) and recapitulative statement for intra-Community supplies
  • Vessel provisioning declaration for marine-fuel bunkering operations confirming international-traffic voyage status

Germany imposes a B2B e-invoice mandate (E-Rechnungspflicht) effective 1 January 2025: every taxable person must be able to receive structured e-invoices complying with European Standard EN 16931 (syntaxes include XRechnung and ZUGFeRD 2.0.1/2.1/2.2 hybrid format), with PEPPOL-based exchange as the prevailing interoperable channel. Issuers benefit from transitional relief permitting PDF/paper invoices until 31 December 2026, and until 31 December 2027 for issuers whose prior-year turnover exceeded EUR 800,000; full e-invoice issuance becomes mandatory for all issuers on 1 January 2028. B2G e-invoicing (XRechnung/PEPPOL) has been mandatory since 27 November 2020. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or tax-authority API validation model; compliance is enforced retroactively through the electronic VAT return (Elektronische Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung), annual VAT declaration and GoBD-compliant archiving of invoice datasets.

Export zero-rating is scrutinized in VAT field inspections (Umsatzsteuer-Nachschau) and integrated customs audits. Missing, late or contradictory export evidence retroactively converts the zero-rated supply into a taxable supply at 19%, triggering VAT correction with default interest at 0.5% per month (Sec. 233a AO), late-payment surcharges at 1% per month (Sec. 240 AO), and loss of input-VAT deduction. Fabricated bills of lading, simulated voyages or circular trades constitute tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, punishable by fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to five years, or up to ten years in aggravated commercial-banding cases; the authorities additionally order full retrospective clawback of any VAT refunded, deny the export-proof grace period, and, for licensed energy traders, revoke the excise-duty permit and exclude the operator from the suspended-duty regime for mineral oils.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible across both current and capital accounts. Germany maintains an EU single-market regime (Art. 63 TFEU) with no approval-based FX controls and no profit-remittance authorization. Practical compliance hurdles: (i) statutory cross-border reporting thresholds (€12,500) per AWV § 67, filed electronically with the Bundesbank (primary market statistics / Zahlungsbilanzstatistik); (ii) dividend remittances subject to German withholding tax (25% KapESt + 5.5% solidarity surcharge) absent treaty relief; (iii) heightened detection of sanctioned-energy proceeds under EU Regulation 833/2014 as amended. Capital account inflows are fully liberalized, subject to data notification only.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds into EUR. Export and advance-payment receipts in foreign currency (principally USD for marine fuel and distillates) may be retained freely in USD-denominated accounts. Statutory timeline: any receipt or payment with a foreign counterparty exceeding €12,500, or cumulative monthly volume exceeding €12,500, must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank by the German resident within five (5) business days of the value date (AWV § 67), using the Z5-method (online Bundesbank portal). Conversion ratio: 0% mandatory conversion. However, EUR-denominated hedged accounting under IFRS/CoBI must align with documented commercial risk policy.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (DNS banking authority and statistical/balance-of-payments processor under § 67 Außenwirtschaftsverordnung/AWV); Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises payment and e-money institutions under Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz/ZAG and the German Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz/GwG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW for primary treasury rails. BaFin-licensed fintech EMIs (including PSD2 passporting entities from other EU member states) may hold segregated client funds and route SEPA/MT103-equivalent cross-border credit transfers, but they cannot issue documentary trade instruments (commercial LC, standby LC under UCP/URDG, or bank guarantees) mandatory for ISIC 4661 marine fuel wholesale. EMIs additionally apply conservative, often prohibitive, risk appetite to high-risk Commodity Trading sectors (per BaFin BAIT/GwG expectations), are inappropriate for escrowing inbound equity injections, and face correspondent-bank liquidity drag. Recommended supplemental use: payment initiation and FX conversion only; clearing must remain at tier-1 banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if cross-border transactions with related parties exceed €5 million for goods or €500,000 for services; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds €50 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies substance-over-form and anti-abuse rules (e.g., §42 AO). For treaty benefits, the entity must have real economic substance (personnel, premises, decision-making). No specific ESR regime, but shell companies are denied treaty benefits under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) and German case law.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years; fines up to €50,000; additional penalty of 6% interest on evaded tax per year.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participate; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and imposition of 30% penalty on understated tax; potential criminal charges for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for tax deficiencies and face disqualification from serving as directors.
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal prosecution for money laundering (up to 10 years imprisonment).

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; travel bans and asset seizures may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 5-10% of adjusted amount (minimum €5,000), and interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; criminal liability for tax evasion if intentional.

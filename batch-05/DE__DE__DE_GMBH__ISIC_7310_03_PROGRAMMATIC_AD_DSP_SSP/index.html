Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) administered by the Registry Court (Registergericht) of the local District Court (Amtsgericht) at the company's registered office; electronic filing by the notary via the federal-state xJustiz portal and the public register portal handelsregister.de.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG): §§ 1-2 (formation contract and mandatory notarial certification), § 3 (contents of Articles of Association), § 5 (minimum nominal share capital of EUR 25,000), § 7(2) (capital payment prerequisite to registration), § 8 (content of registration application and director's capital-disposal declaration), § 10 (constitutive entry in the Commercial Register), §§ 35 et seq. (managing directors); supplemented by the German Commercial Code (HGB) §§ 8-12 (electronic register filing and public announcements) and the Act on Court and Notary Fees (GNotKG).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag/Satzung) together with a certified electronic application for registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) lodged ex officio by the notary.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital EUR 25,000 (GmbHG § 5(1)). Prior to registration, at least one-quarter of each cash contribution must be paid, and total paid amounts (including the value of agreed in-kind contributions) must reach at least EUR 12,500, i.e. one half of the minimum capital (GmbHG § 7(2) sentence 1). Funds must be transferred to a blocked GmbH-in-Gründung formation account and be permanently at the free disposal of the managing director, evidenced by a bank capital-payment confirmation (Einzahlungsbestätigung). Single-member formations are subject to the stricter rule of § 7(2) sentence 2 and the declaration requirement of § 8(3) GmbHG requiring full payment of cash contributions or adequate security.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Brandschutznachweis) and building occupancy clearance (Nutzungsgenehmigung) per the state building code (LBO/HBO) must be confirmed by the landlord for the registered office; workplace compliance with the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung, ArbStättV) covering lighting, ventilation, emergency exits and first aid; occupational health and safety under the German Occupational Safety and Health Act (ArbSchG) and DGUV accident-prevention regulations (DGUV Vorschrift 1). In addition, because the company processes personal data at scale for programmatic advertising, an operational data-protection clearance is mandatory: appointment of a data protection officer under § 38 BDSG and filing the processing register with the competent state Data Protection Authority (Landesdatenschutzbeauftragter); voluntary and background screening of staff is covered by employer liability insurance through the competent Berufsgenossenschaft.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Germany – Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) issues the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr) and handles central VAT matters; the local Finanzamt is the competent tax office for income and turnover tax assessment and issuance of the Steuernummer. Official filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); BZSt portal: www.bzst.de.

E-government filing portal

1) After notarial incorporation and entry into the Handelsregister, the GmbH must complete the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' and submit it to the competent Finanzamt to obtain the local tax number (Steuernummer). 2) Register for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr) separately with BZSt using Form USt 1 TG via ELSTER or BZSt's online service. 3) A non-resident foreign entity with taxable supplies in Germany may apply directly to BZSt for VAT registration and must identify a fiscal agent or local contact if no German establishment exists. 4) The VAT registration must be completed before taxable supplies commence; ongoing compliance includes monthly/quarterly prepayments and annual VAT returns via ELSTER.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A GmbH is per se a taxable corporation subject to Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer and solidarity surcharge where applicable. Available elections include VAT and corporate income tax fiscal unity ('Organschaft') between a German parent and GmbH, and the small-entrepreneur VAT treatment under § 19 UStG if statutory turnover thresholds are met. For hybrid entities, German ATAD-based anti-hybrid rules apply mandatorily; there is no elective conversion of entity classification for tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary threshold for non-resident taxable persons making taxable supplies in Germany; registration is required as soon as a taxable supply occurs, subject to reverse-charge/OSS mechanisms. For intra-EU distance sales to German consumers, the de minimis threshold is EUR 10,000 per calendar year unless the taxpayer opts for the OSS one-stop-shop. The resident small-business threshold is EUR 22,000 turnover in the prior year and EUR 50,000 in the current year; non-resident entrepreneurs generally cannot rely on the German small-business exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to third-country destinations are VAT-free under § 6 UStG if the goods physically leave Germany and the export is proven by customs and commercial documentation. Intra-Community supplies are VAT-free under § 6a UStG where the customer is an entrepreneur with a valid VAT ID and the goods are transported to another Member State. For programmatic advertising and DSP/SSP services supplied to a business customer in another EU Member State or a third country, the place of supply is the customer's business jurisdiction under § 3a UStG, with German VAT generally not charged and the reverse-charge mechanism applying in the customer's country. If the digital advertising service is supplied B2C to a third-country non-entrepreneur and the effective use or exploitation occurs in that country, the place of supply is also outside Germany, so German VAT is not due.

  • Ausfuhrbegleitdokument (ABD) or export accompanying document
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill or CMR consignment note as transport evidence
  • Intra-Community supply supporting evidence: customer's valid VAT ID and 'Gelangensbestätigung' (arrival confirmation) where applicable

Germany mandates electronic invoicing for B2G transactions via XRechnung through the Peppol or dedicated portals. For domestic B2B transactions, from 1 January 2025 all businesses must be able to receive e-invoices; mandatory issuance in an EU EN 16931-compatible format (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD) is phased in from 2027 for taxpayers with turnover above EUR 800,000 and from 2028 for all resident businesses. There is no real-time fiscal pre-clearance or tax-authority stamping in Germany; invoices must be transmitted electronically and archived for 10 years in compliance with GoBD record-keeping requirements.

Improper or unsupported zero-rating claims are reversed retroactively by the Finanzamt, with the VAT becoming immediately due together with late-payment interest of 6% per annum and late-filing penalties under §§ 152, 233a AO. Intentional or reckless submission of false export evidence exposes the taxpayer and responsible managing directors to criminal tax evasion liability under § 370 AO (imprisonment of up to five years, or up to ten years in aggravated cases), fines, seizure of goods, and personal liability where company assets are insufficient. Customs investigations by Zoll may extend the audit period and lead to retrospective reassessments without the normal one-year statute of limitations in cases of fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a Eurozone member, and the euro is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no prior central bank approval requirements for cross-border payments, and no quantitative restrictions on profit remittance. Dividend distributions from a German GmbH are subject to German withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) at 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, effectively 26.375%, reduced or eliminated under an applicable double-tax treaty; this tax obligation does not constitute an FX or convertibility hurdle. The only statutory friction is the reporting duty under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV): every cross-border payment of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical purposes within five business days, either as an individual transaction report or as part of an aggregated monthly filing. This is a data-reporting obligation only and does not function as a de facto approval gate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory repatriation, or compulsory conversion of export proceeds. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 0%, and exporters may retain proceeds in any freely convertible currency indefinitely. The only compliance requirement is the AWV reporting framework: cross-border receipts exceeding EUR 12,500 (or the foreign-currency equivalent) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five business days, and foreign-currency balances and foreign receivables must be captured in the external statistics declarations. There is no timeline within which export proceeds must be converted into EUR, and no central bank approval is required for holding or converting foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank) within the Eurosystem, operating in close coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB); the Deutsche Bundesbank is the competent authority for external trade statistics and balance-of-payments reporting, while the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) licenses and supervises banks, payment institutions, and electronic-money institutions under German law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium and conditional. A German or EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) supervised under the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz - ZAG) can provide valid virtual IBANs, multi-currency wallets, and low-cost cross-border disbursements derivative to the primary tier-1 bank account; however, an EMI is not a viable primary treasury core for a German GmbH in programmatic advertising because EMIs generally do not extend credit, cannot issue documentary trade finance such as commercial letters of credit, do not participate in the German statutory deposit protection scheme with the same status as banks, and often lack the corporate relationship depth required for complex FX hedging instruments. Offshore EMIs located outside the European Economic Area (e.g., the United Kingdom post-Brexit, Hong Kong, or Singapore) are not permitted to service a German corporate on a cross-border passporting basis, and using such an unlicensed EMI exposes the GmbH to ZAG enforcement risk and undermines AWV statistical reporting compliance. The viable configuration is a tier-1 universal bank as the primary treasury and settlement house, supplemented by one EEA-passported EMI for virtual-IBAN liquidity fragmentation and instantaneous micro-payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the German GmbH must be executed by a compliant wire transfer in EUR or foreign currency. For EUR-denominated injections from within the EEA, a SEPA Credit Transfer is used; for non-EEA or third-currency injections, a SWIFT MT103 is the required wire protocol. The injection must be credited to a dedicated Stammkapitaleinzahlungskonto (capital payment clearing account) established in the GmbH's name, and the transfer instruction must clearly state 'Einzahlung Stammkapital' (payment of share capital) to enable the bank's verification letter for the registrar. Under German law (GmbHG Section 7(2) and Section 8(2)), not less than EUR 12,500 of the minimum EUR 25,000 share capital must be paid in cash prior to commercial-register registration, and the funds must remain at the free disposal of the Geschäftsführer. For AWV purposes, each cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 must carry the appropriate balance-of-payments purpose classification; the capital contribution is reported under the Deutsche Bundesbank's external statistics business-activity class for participation in a domestic enterprise (capital-participation category), and the transfer instruction must include the statutory Verwendungszweck with the relevant Geschäftsart code. Verification of the injection is provided by the bank's payment receipt and lock-in confirmation (Bankbestätigung), which the notary attaches to the incorporation filing; shareholder loans may also be injected via the same SWIFT/ SEPA rails but are reported separately as cross-border liabilities.The primary clearing rails for trade settlements in the programmatic advertising segment are SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payouts within the EEA and SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA and USD/GBP disbursements; the SWIFT MT103 is the definitive interbank message retained for treasury, tax, and counterparty reconciliation, with SHA (shared) charges as the standard cost allocation. For US-dollar publisher payouts, funds may be routed via the deutsche bank's Federal Reserve (Fedwire) correspondent rails or via ACH Direct for low-value scheduled payments, with the MT103 as the originating cross-border instrument. Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits under UCP 600) are not customary in the AdTech SSP/DSP segment; they are used only for large media-buying or platform-integration contracts requiring a settlement guarantee, in which case the issuer bank transmits an MT700 LC, and the beneficiary presents commercial invoices and digital performance reports against the terms. The treasury should implement weekly batching of publisher payouts to concentrate liquidity, maintain USD and GBP operating accounts to avoid repetitive spot conversions, and monitor SEPA Instant availability for exceptional same-day settlements while recognising that instant payments require enhanced daily transaction monitoring.FX Forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) - OTC spot-deferred contracts in EUR/USD and EUR/GBP with a licensed German or EEA bank, documentary tenor up to 12 months, executed under an ISDA master agreement and subject to EMIR reporting obligations; used to hedge expected ad-revenue cash flows.FX Swaps (Devisenswaps) - simultaneous spot and forward legs used for short-term liquidity conversion between EUR and USD, permissible for a German non-bank corporate under KWG and AWV, with the forward leg reflected on the derivative balance sheet.Cross-Currency Swaps (Währungsswaps) - permitted for hedging intercompany loans or acquisition financing; subject to EMIR clearing-threshold analysis, margin posting, and alignment with IFRS 9 or HGB Section 256a hedge accounting documentation.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) - permissible for hedging non-convertible emerging-market currencies such as TRY, INR, BRL, or EGP where onshore forward markets are unavailable; settled on a net USD/EUR basis against a reference rate and subject to EMIR reporting and documented underlying exposure.FX Options (plain vanilla, non-speculative) - permitted under an approved treasury policy to protect against adverse currency movements in USD and GBP settlement balances; requires internal mark-to-market governance and full compliance with MiFID II suitability and PRIIPs disclosure requirements when traded with an EEA investment firm.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility is Medium and conditional. A German or EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) supervised under the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz - ZAG) can provide valid virtual IBANs, multi-currency wallets, and low-cost cross-border disbursements derivative to the primary tier-1 bank account; however, an EMI is not a viable primary treasury core for a German GmbH in programmatic advertising because EMIs generally do not extend credit, cannot issue documentary trade finance such as commercial letters of credit, do not participate in the German statutory deposit protection scheme with the same status as banks, and often lack the corporate relationship depth required for complex FX hedging instruments. Offshore EMIs located outside the European Economic Area (e.g., the United Kingdom post-Brexit, Hong Kong, or Singapore) are not permitted to service a German corporate on a cross-border passporting basis, and using such an unlicensed EMI exposes the GmbH to ZAG enforcement risk and undermines AWV statistical reporting compliance. The viable configuration is a tier-1 universal bank as the primary treasury and settlement house, supplemented by one EEA-passported EMI for virtual-IBAN liquidity fragmentation and instantaneous micro-payouts.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authority for corporate tax registration and assessment is the local Finanzamt (tax office) determined by the GmbH's registered office or place of management. The Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, Federal Central Tax Office) administers VAT identification numbers and certain cross-border VAT matters. Relevant official portals include ELSTER (https://www.elster.de) for tax registration and filings and the BZSt online services (https://www.bzst.de) for VAT ID services.

E-government filing portal

After commercial-register incorporation and commencement of business, submit the electronic Questionnaire for Tax Registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns a tax number (Steuernummer) and registers the GmbH for relevant taxes, including corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT as applicable. Apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) if required for intra-EU transactions or other VAT purposes. A foreign-established taxable person without a German establishment must follow the applicable nonresident VAT-registration procedure; the competent German tax office depends on the place of the taxable activity, and non-EU businesses may be assigned to a designated tax office. A foreign-established business may need to appoint a German fiscal representative only where statutory conditions apply; this is not a general requirement for every foreign business.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A German GmbH is generally a separate corporate income-tax taxpayer and is not eligible to elect U.S.-style pass-through or check-the-box classification under German domestic law. German law provides no general hybrid-mismatch election that changes a GmbH's domestic entity classification. Cross-border classification may differ under another jurisdiction's law, subject to applicable treaty and anti-hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to a GmbH making taxable supplies in Germany: VAT registration is generally required from the first taxable domestic supply. The domestic small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) may apply only where its statutory conditions are met; from 2025, the principal limits are EUR 25,000 in prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 in current-year turnover. It does not create a general registration threshold for nonresident businesses making German taxable supplies, and special rules apply to cross-border transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the EU may qualify as VAT-exempt with input-tax credit (effectively zero-rated) where the statutory export conditions are met and the supplier retains adequate evidence that the goods left the EU. Intra-Community supplies may be exempt with input-tax credit if the customer is a taxable person in another EU Member State, the goods are transported or dispatched there, the supplier holds a valid VAT ID and satisfies reporting and evidence requirements. Services are not generally zero-rated merely because the customer or payment is offshore: place-of-supply rules determine treatment. Services supplied to a business customer outside Germany are generally outside German VAT where the general B2B place-of-supply rule places the supply at the customer's establishment; exceptions apply. Installation or construction work connected with German immovable property is generally taxable in Germany. Export exemption must be supported by transaction-specific records and statutory proof.

  • Customs export declaration and export accompanying document or equivalent customs confirmation, where applicable
  • Evidence of exit from the EU, such as customs exit confirmation or an applicable electronic export-system record
  • Transport evidence, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or carrier documentation, as appropriate
  • Commercial invoice, order or contract, and records identifying the goods and non-EU destination
  • Proof of payment or inward remittance records where relevant to substantiate the transaction; no German rule makes an inward remittance certificate a universal export-VAT prerequisite
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid VAT ID, transport or dispatch evidence, and required recapitulative-statement reporting

Germany has no general real-time VAT invoice clearance, digital-stamping, or continuous transaction reporting requirement. From 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions. Mandatory issuance of structured e-invoices is being phased in: transitional rules generally permit other invoice formats through 2026, with an extension through 2027 for issuers whose prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 800,000; the general domestic B2B e-invoicing obligation applies from 2028, subject to statutory exceptions. Structured invoices must comply with the applicable European standard and German requirements; transmission does not require prior tax-authority clearance. Separate EU digital-reporting reforms may affect future requirements.

The supplier bears the burden of substantiating exemption eligibility and must retain compliant books, invoices, customs evidence, and transport records. Unsupported or incorrectly claimed exemptions may be denied, with VAT assessed retrospectively, interest, and applicable late-payment surcharges. Deliberate or reckless tax evasion may result in criminal prosecution and penalties under the German Fiscal Code; negligent tax reduction may constitute an administrative offense. Penalties depend on the facts, culpability, amount, and applicable procedural rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany, as a euro-area member, maintains full and unconditional liberalization of both capital and current account transactions. The euro is freely convertible; there are no exchange controls, no central-bank approval requirements, and no restrictions on repatriation of capital, dividends, interest, or contract proceeds. The only practical frictions are (i) the statutory AWV reporting threshold of EUR 12,500 per cross-border payment or receipt, (ii) KYC/documentary requirements imposed by banks at onboarding, and (iii) German dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, reduced under applicable double-tax treaties and subject to reclaim procedures). Profit remittance is not legally blocked, but the paying bank will require a shareholder distribution resolution, audited solvency proof, confirmation from the tax advisor that withholding tax and trade tax obligations are current, and, where treaty relief is claimed, an advance tax clearance certificate from the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation in Germany to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no pre-approval for retention of FX earnings. Exporters may freely hold euro or foreign currency in accounts maintained with German banks. However, each export receipt or cross-border inbound payment exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV §70. The reporting is ordinarily performed by the bank via the AWV 'Zahlungsbilanz' interface; the exporter must nonetheless include a precise purpose-of-payment code and underlying transaction reference so that the receipt is accurately classified as trade in goods (G-field), trade in services (S-field), or capital transfer. For cash or open-account exports, the exporter must retain the export invoice, customs declaration (where required), transport documents, and, for projects, the construction contract or L/C drawdown notice for at least seven years to satisfy audit review by the Bundesbank.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) is the competent monetary authority for cross-border financial statistics, foreign trade and payments monitoring, and balance-of-payments reporting under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV). The Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) is the policy-setting authority for trade and sanctions controls under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG). The Bundesbank acts as the operational clearing agent and custodian of statutory payment records, but not as a licensing authority for ordinary foreign-exchange conversions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: 'Medium-risk / Conditional'. A German-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) under the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG) and PSD2 may legally hold segregated e-money accounts and facilitate SEPA/SEPA Instant cross-border euro payments. However, an offshore digital EMI without a BaFin licence cannot be used to receive LNG terminal contract proceeds or to hold funds representing public-infrastructure milestone payments because (i) the EPC contract and main-construction tenders normally require an AA- or A+-rated credit institution acceptable under VOB/B security clauses; (ii) the EMI has no power to issue stand-by letters of credit, performance bonds, or documentary letters of credit; (iii) funds placed with an unlicensed non-EEA EMI would not rank within German statutory deposit-protection coverage (Einlagensicherung); and (iv) tax- and treasury-auditable clearing through TARGET2/SEPA requires the intermediary bank to possess a proper German banking licence. Therefore, the advised structure is to use a tier-1 commercial bank as the primary treasury spine and to engage an EMI only for very small ancillary expenses such as technical subscriptions, software licenses, travel services, or staff allowances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be routed exclusively via an officially licensed banking channel. For a direct equity contribution from a non-EEA parent, the recommended rail is SWIFT MT103 with a fully documented beneficiary account at the German principal bank. The transfer must include the statutory German AWV purpose of payment ('Zahlungszweck') that clearly identifies 'Kapitalerhöhung Einlage' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' according to the German external-statistics BPM6 classification; for GMBH treasury practice, the bank will classify direct investment as code 'F101' (direct investment capital) and intra-group loans as 'F102' if the lending entity is not a bank, or 'F103' if the lender is a group finance company. Practical verification requires: a copy of the capital-increase resolution recorded in the Commercial Register; a shareholder meeting protocol; an approved list of authorized signatories; an FX FX-transaction instruction with the agreed spot rate (if the injection is made in USD); the Bankensystem SWIFT reference; and statutory AWV notification to the Deutsche Bundesbank where the threshold exceeds EUR 12,500. For loans, the tax and treasury recommendation is to set the arm's-length interest rate in accordance with the Bundesbank Drittlandverordnung rules and to register the foreign receivable in the External Position File (Auslandsstatus) so that the interest and principal repayments are freely cleanable.For payments to offshore pipe mills, insulation-material importers, cryogenic valve producers, and subcontracted installation crews, the clearing rails must be selected based on the transaction value and documentary security. For high-value equipment (EUR 250,000 and above), use an irrevocable confirmed letter of credit (L/C) opened through Commerzbank or Deutsche Bank with SWIFT MT700 advice, settlement by MT103 for payment at sight, and MT754 for confirmation of reimbursement. For medium-value invoices (EUR 10,000 - 250,000), use a direct SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer, where the counterpart holds an EEA IBAN) coupled with the commercial invoice and bill-of-export declaration. For low-value recurring subcontractor absorbents and daily operational costs under EUR 10,000, use SEPA Credit Transfer and corporate cards. All international payments must carry the project reference (e.g., 'LNG-RTP-2025-PO-7890'), the invoice number, and, if a prior FX forward has been executed, the treasury deal reference so that the bank can net settlements within the same internal account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: 'Medium-risk / Conditional'. A German-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) under the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG) and PSD2 may legally hold segregated e-money accounts and facilitate SEPA/SEPA Instant cross-border euro payments. However, an offshore digital EMI without a BaFin licence cannot be used to receive LNG terminal contract proceeds or to hold funds representing public-infrastructure milestone payments because (i) the EPC contract and main-construction tenders normally require an AA- or A+-rated credit institution acceptable under VOB/B security clauses; (ii) the EMI has no power to issue stand-by letters of credit, performance bonds, or documentary letters of credit; (iii) funds placed with an unlicensed non-EEA EMI would not rank within German statutory deposit-protection coverage (Einlagensicherung); and (iv) tax- and treasury-auditable clearing through TARGET2/SEPA requires the intermediary bank to possess a proper German banking licence. Therefore, the advised structure is to use a tier-1 commercial bank as the primary treasury spine and to engage an EMI only for very small ancillary expenses such as technical subscriptions, software licenses, travel services, or staff allowances.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum nominal capital of EUR 25,000 (GmbHG §5); at least one half (EUR 12,500) of cash contributions must be paid in before registration and be at the free disposal of the managing directors. No public escrow account is required for cash-only GmbHs; the paid-in amount must be proven by bank confirmation. In-kind contributions must be fully effected and supported by a valuation report.

Funding & unlock path: The registered business premises require municipal fire safety certification, including fire extinguishers, emergency exits, smoke alarms and protective signage. A structural load inspection certificate must confirm that the rented office floor is suitable for ordinary office loading. The workplace must be cleared under the German Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) and the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz), and a first-aid and fire-safety instruction record must be maintained. If the management operates from a true home office without external customers, the local fire clearance may be limited to a written self-declaration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office, responsible for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) under §27a UStG and OSS/foreign VAT registrations; locally competent Finanzamt (tax office) responsible for Steuernummer (tax number), corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT assessments. Primary digital portals: ELSTER (www.elster.de) for tax filings and the BZSt online portal (www.bzst.de) for VAT ID applications and VAT refunds (§18 Abs. 9 UStG).

E-government filing portal

Domestic GmbH: after notarial incorporation and commercial register entry, file the electronic 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER with the local Finanzamt, which assigns a tax number (Steuernummer) within 2–6 weeks; separately apply to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under §27a UStG using Form UST 1 (or via ELSTER/online service). Non-resident digital suppliers without a German establishment must obtain a VAT ID directly from BZSt using Form UST 1 TG, providing certified foreign registration documents and, where required, an authorized VAT representative; there is no de minimis registration threshold and processing typically spans 4–8 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A GmbH (Gesellschaft mit beschränkter Haftung) is a taxable body corporate: subject to corporation tax at 15%, solidarity surcharge at 5.5% of corporation tax, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 7–17% effective rate depending on municipality; profits are taxed at entity level with no pass-through to shareholders. Tax residence is established by registered seat or place of effective management in Germany, triggering worldwide income taxation subject to treaty relief; controlled foreign company rules (§§7–13 AStG) and §4k EStG hybrid mismatch rules apply. — Domestic GmbH: after notarial incorporation and commercial register entry, file the electronic 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER with the local Finanzamt, which assigns a tax number (Steuernummer) within 2–6 weeks; separately apply to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under §27a UStG using Form UST 1 (or via ELSTER/online service). Non-resident digital suppliers without a German establishment must obtain a VAT ID directly from BZSt using Form UST 1 TG, providing certified foreign registration documents and, where required, an authorized VAT representative; there is no de minimis registration threshold and processing typically spans 4–8 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A GmbH (Gesellschaft mit beschränkter Haftung) is a taxable body corporate: subject to corporation tax at 15%, solidarity surcharge at 5.5% of corporation tax, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 7–17% effective rate depending on municipality; profits are taxed at entity level with no pass-through to shareholders. Tax residence is established by registered seat or place of effective management in Germany, triggering worldwide income taxation subject to treaty relief; controlled foreign company rules (§§7–13 AStG) and §4k EStG hybrid mismatch rules apply.

Available election forms

Available statutory elections: (1) fiscal unity (Organschaft) for corporate tax (§14 KStG) and VAT (§2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG), requiring financial integration, profit-and-loss pooling, and management subordination; (2) non-calendar fiscal year election under §4a EStG; (3) VAT grouping effectively consolidates group invoicing and zero-rates intra-group supplies; (4) small-business VAT exemption under §19 UStG available only to German-resident entrepreneurs with turnover ≤ €25,000 (2025 threshold), not applicable to mandatorily registered foreign entities; (5) no US-style check-the-box classification exists — the GmbH's civil law form conclusively determines corporate tax treatment, with hybrid mismatch adjustments under §4k EStG overriding any divergent foreign characterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis threshold for non-resident suppliers of digital services: VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply in Germany. For a German-established GmbH, registration is mandatory from the first taxable revenue; the small-business exemption (§19 UStG, €25,000 annual turnover in 2025) applies only to domestically resident entrepreneurs. The intra-EU distance-sales threshold of €10,000 applies solely to German-resident sellers using the OSS scheme; non-EU suppliers have no exemption threshold and must register directly or use the Non-Union OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (1) goods exported from the EU under §6 UStG, provided the GmbH retains evidence of physical export per §§8–13 UStDV; (2) B2B cross-border services (e.g., digital magazine subscriptions to non-resident business customers) where the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction under §3a(2) UStG — German output VAT is nil; (3) B2C electronically supplied services to non-EU consumers where the place of supply is outside the EU under §§3a(1), 3g UStG — German VAT is nil; (4) intra-EU supplies to VAT-registered customers (§6a UStG) supported by valid EU customer VAT IDs and EC Sales List declarations. Export and cross-border supply evidence must be documentary and contemporaneous; missing evidence converts the supply to taxable at 19%.

  • Customs export declaration via ATLAS (electronic Ausfuhrbegleitdokument / MRN - Movement Reference Number)
  • Commercial invoice (complete §14 UStG invoice data)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR road consignment note or railway consignment note evidencing dispatch from the EU
  • Proof of export per §§8–13 UStDV (packing lists, warehouse dispatch records, customs broker confirmations)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer records demonstrating foreign-currency receipt and FX repatriation

B2G e-invoicing has been mandatory since 27 November 2020 using XRechnung/EN 16931 formats. Under the 2024 Growth Opportunities Act, domestic B2B e-invoicing is phased in: from 1 January 2025, all German businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (ZUGFeRD 2.x/XInvoice conforming to EN 16931); from 1 January 2027, issuers with prior-year turnover exceeding €800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all domestic B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices. There is currently no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital tax stamp for standard B2B invoices; however, the EU ViDA (VAT in the Digital Age) package is expected to mandate transaction-based e-reporting and near-real-time clearance from approximately 2030. B2C invoices remain format-flexible subject to §14 UStG content requirements.

Zero-rating claims failing audit substantiation are retroactively clawed back for up to 10 assessment years: the full 19% VAT is assessed under §§14c, 26b UStG with interest at 0.5% per month under §233a AO; administrative fines under §378 AO for tax evasion by negligence reach €50,000; intentional evasion under §370 AO carries imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years in aggravated cases) plus unlimited monetary fines and asset forfeiture. GmbH managing directors and VAT representatives are jointly and severally liable under §§69, 71 AO. Digital publishing platforms must also maintain IP-address and geolocation logs substantiating customer residency to defend B2C place-of-supply zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully liberalised exchange-control regime under EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital and payments. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible into and out of the euro without central bank approval. Profit remittances by a German GmbH, including dividends and branch remittances, are not subject to foreign-exchange licensing or repatriation limits. The operative requirements are tax and documentary: dividend payments require a valid shareholder resolution and a distributable balance-sheet profit, and the paying bank will apply German withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) unless the shareholder presents a valid treaty-reduced withholding relief certificate or similar Form W-8-equivalent/DTD documentation. Sanctions screening and anti-money-laundering checks can delay a wire, but there is no foreign-exchange convertibility obstacle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory duty to surrender or convert export proceeds held by a German resident entity. Export receivables may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely and without obligation to repatriate or sell to the Bundesbank. The only mandatory requirement is statistical balance-of-payments reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border receipts from non-residents if the transaction exceeds EUR 12,500. Such reports are generally due within five working days after the end of the calendar month in which the payment occurred and are filed in the prescribed electronic format, including details of the payer, payee, amount, currency, reference number and purpose code. The obligation is purely statistical and imposes no exchange-control restriction, conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank and foreign exchange payments statistics authority), the European Central Bank (monetary authority for euro convertibility), and the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) as the responsible supervisory body for authorised payment institutions and AML/KYC enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions passported into Germany under PSD2; Low for offshore digital EMIs lacking a German or EU/EEA licence. EU/EEA EMIs can operate supplementary payment accounts, e-money wallets and remittance corridors, and can be connected for fast integration. However, an offshore EMI without a passport cannot lawfully maintain a German IBAN or provide payout services to a German resident entity. Moreover, an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot provide the statutory capital escrow confirmation required by the GmbH formation notary, nor can it provide overdrafts, commercial letters of credit, or comprehensive FX/treasury products. A prudent treasury blueprint uses a fully licensed German tier-1 bank as the primary operating and escrow bank, with EU/EEA EMIs only as secondary payout-acceptance rails for marketplace, Stripe or AdSense-style receipts.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

29.83% (15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on corporate income tax plus average municipal trade tax of approximately 14% on trade income; effective rate varies by municipality between approximately 22.8% and 32.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% withholding tax on dividends plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, totalling 26.375%; plus church tax if shareholder is a natural person subject to it, typically 8%-9% of the withholding tax).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Corporate income tax and trade tax returns generally due by 31 July of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end; extended to 31 October (or later) if tax advice is engaged. VAT prepayment returns due by the 10th day after each prepayment period (VAT return filing deadline: 10th of month following the reporting period; with Dauerfristverlängerung, one-month extension). Annual VAT return due by 31 July of the following year (or extended when tax advisor engaged). Wage tax declarations follow similar rules. If fiscal year deviates from calendar year, statutory deadline is end of the seventh month after the fiscal year-end. Late filing triggers late filing penalties (Verspätungszuschlag) pursuant to Sec. 152 AO of 0.25% of assessed tax per commenced month, capped at EUR 25,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 2,000,000 for local file (contemporaneous documentation required if cross-border transactions with related parties exceed EUR 2,000,000 per business transaction); Germany implements a two-tier documentation standard: Master File required when group revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000; Local File required for each German entity when intercompany transactions exceed EUR 2,000,000 for tangible/intangible/other services, or EUR 100,000 for management/administrative services; additional requirements under the German Transfer Pricing Documentation Act (Aufzeichnungs- und Aufbewahrungsfristen).

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) akin to Cayman/BVI, but requires real economic substance under German tax abuse doctrines: Sec. 42 AO (Abgabenordnung) disallows abusive legal structures without economic substance; transfer pricing substance requires actual decision-making, personnel, tangible and intangible assets, and risk assumption in Germany. A German GmbH must maintain substance to avoid denial of treaty benefits, DTA abuse (treaty shopping), and application of the interest barrier rule. For digital publishing/advertising operations, the economic substance standard includes local content management, editorial decision-making, ad sales contracts, and bank accounts. Compliance with EU ATAD rules on controlled foreign company (CFC) and interest limitation, plus German anti-hybrid rules, applies.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under Sec. 370 AO (tax evasion): imprisonment up to 5 years or fine; in aggravated cases (large-scale evasion exceeding EUR 50,000, or use of shell accounts) imprisonment 6 months to 10 years; penalty up to the evaded tax amount; interest 0.15% per month on the evaded amount; exclusion from public contracts; disallowance of tax deductions.

Director personal liability: Managing directors (Geschäftsführer) personally liable for intentional or negligent evasion under Sec. 69 AO and Sec. 34 AO for failure to discharge tax obligations; civil liability for damages under Sec. 823 BGB; statutory penalty orders and criminal conviction recorded; in serious cases detention pending trial; travel restrictions under Sec. 32 AO; passport revocation; personal bankruptcy risk.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under Sec. 50d Abs. 3 EStG; recharacterisation of transactions as abusive under Sec. 42 AO; disregarding of legal form and attribution of income to the beneficial owner; imposed penalty under Sec. 412 AO for failure to give information; interest on underpaid tax; administrative fines up to EUR 5,000 for trivial lack of substance; potential criminal tax evasion if misrepresenting facts.

Director personal liability: Directors pierce-through liability under Sec. 64 GmbHG for payments made after insolvency; personal liability for taxes not withheld and remitted under Sec. 34, 69 AO; restraint orders under Sec. 380 AO; in case of bankruptcy, criminal liability for breach of filing duties (Insolvenzverschleppung) under Sec. 15a InsO; travel bans and extradition possible in cross-border evasion; disqualification from serving as directors under Sec. 6 GmbHG.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Unjustified Cross-Border Shifting

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment by tax audit under Sec. 1 AStG (Foreign Transaction Tax Act); penalty of 5%-10% of the adjusted income for missing or inadequate documentation under Sec. 162 AO, minimum EUR 5,000 when material; default interest 6% per annum on tax adjustments (Sec. 233a AO, 0.5% per month) unless rebutted; criminal exposure if documents falsified.

Director personal liability: Managing directors personally liable for failing to keep and provide required documentation, because qualified duty under Sec. 90 AO and Sec. 34, 69 AO; civil liability towards the company for deficient management under Sec. 43 GmbHG; in cross-border cases, enforcement through mutual agreement procedure (EU Arbitration Convention) does not extinguish personal officer liability; restrictions on departure may be imposed during tax audit.
Withholding Tax Violation / Dividend Stripping Scheme

Statutory sanction: Late remittance surcharge under Sec. 240 AO: 1% per month; penalty under Sec. 378 AO (tax misdemeanour) up to EUR 50,000 for negligent violation; intentional liability may constitute tax evasion; Sec. 36a EStG denies loss recognition in abusive dividend stripping transactions; interest and late penalties up to EUR 25,000; denial of treaty relief and refund.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for non-remitted withholding tax under Sec. 34, 69 AO; civil liability for corporate damage; in case of intentional dividend stripping schemes, criminal prosecution for tax evasion and money laundering; travel ban and passport seizure under Sec. 132 AO; public prosecutor confiscation of assets where disproportionate benefit obtained.
Money Laundering / AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 100,000 for minor violations and up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for serious violations under Sec. 56 GwG; imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering under Sec. 261 StGB; confiscation of proceeds; withdrawal of payment processing licences; prohibition on becoming a statutory representative; legal entity fines up to EUR 10,000,000 under Sec. 30 OWiG.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for failure to appoint an AML officer where required; criminal liability as beneficial owner for laundering proceeds obtained from predicate tax evasion; personal fines and imprisonment; travel bans; asset freezes; disqualification from managing any German company (Sec. 6 GmbHG); liability for loss under Sec. 823 BGB read with GwG protective purpose.

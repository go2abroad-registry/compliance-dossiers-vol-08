Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office (central authority for foreign-entity VAT IDs, offshore VAT registration, and statutory tax certificates); local Finanzamt (municipal tax office) responsible for corporate income tax, trade tax, payroll withholding, and VAT administration of the resident GmbH. Electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); customs clearance portal: ATLAS/ECustoms (www.zoll.de).

E-government filing portal

For a resident German GmbH: (1) Register the company in the Handelsregister (Commercial Register) to obtain the registered corporate seat and legal existence; (2) within one month of business commencement, file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (Questionnaire for Tax Registration) via ELSTER with the competent local Finanzamt, submitting articles of association, commercial register excerpt, and managing director identification; (3) the local Finanzamt assigns a 13-digit Steuernummer (corporate tax number) and a trade tax number (Gewerbesteuernummer); (4) separately apply to the BZSt for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID, format DE + 9 digits) under § 27a UStG; (5) register as employer for wage tax withholding (Lohnsteuer) with the local Finanzamt and social security agencies (Arbeitgeberpflichten); (6) register owner-operator for shipping activities with the Federal Maritime and Hydrographic Agency (BSH) if dredging vessels are German-flagged, and obtain the Wirtschafts-Identifikationsnummer (Economic Identification Number, § 139c AO) for statistical and economic purposes. For a purely foreign non-resident entity with no domestic place of business, VAT registration is required through BZSt Form 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer' (Annex USt 1 TU) whenever taxable supplies are performed in Germany; no threshold applies to non-established entrepreneurs.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a resident German GmbH: (1) Register the company in the Handelsregister (Commercial Register) to obtain the registered corporate seat and legal existence; (2) within one month of business commencement, file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (Questionnaire for Tax Registration) via ELSTER with the competent local Finanzamt, submitting articles of association, commercial register excerpt, and managing director identification; (3) the local Finanzamt assigns a 13-digit Steuernummer (corporate tax number) and a trade tax number (Gewerbesteuernummer); (4) separately apply to the BZSt for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID, format DE + 9 digits) under § 27a UStG; (5) register as employer for wage tax withholding (Lohnsteuer) with the local Finanzamt and social security agencies (Arbeitgeberpflichten); (6) register owner-operator for shipping activities with the Federal Maritime and Hydrographic Agency (BSH) if dredging vessels are German-flagged, and obtain the Wirtschafts-Identifikationsnummer (Economic Identification Number, § 139c AO) for statistical and economic purposes. For a purely foreign non-resident entity with no domestic place of business, VAT registration is required through BZSt Form 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer' (Annex USt 1 TU) whenever taxable supplies are performed in Germany; no threshold applies to non-established entrepreneurs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not adopt a check-the-box regime. A GmbH is per se a corporation under Körperschaftsteuergesetz (KStG) subject to unlimited CFC-type residence taxation on worldwide income at a headline corporate rate of 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective 7%–17% depending on the municipality, composite average circa 30–33% total burden). Available elections and structural mechanisms: (a) Organschaft (tax group) under § 14 KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, permitting VAT and corporate income tax consolidation between a controlling parent and a financially and administratively integrated German subsidiary; (b) option to tax real estate rentals under § 9 UStG (Verzicht auf Steuerbefreiung); (c) IFRS/GAAP timing alignment for trade tax (HGB-Ansatzwahlrechte); (d) hybrid mismatch neutralization provisions under § 4k EStG and § 4l EStG (ATAD II); (e) Zinsschranke (interest limitation) under § 4h EStG/§ 8a KStG with EBITDA escape elections; (f) Treaty-covered profit-branch structuring for foreign PE of the GmbH; (g) no pass-through election exists for a standard GmbH, but a GmbH & Co. KG partnership overlay can achieve pass-through taxation for operational dredging joint ventures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business exemption under § 19 UStG applies to resident entrepreneurs with total turnover of up to EUR 22,000 in the preceding business year and expected turnover not exceeding EUR 50,000 in the current year; however, a GmbH carrying on maritime dredging and EPC works will typically exceed this threshold and is mandatorily VAT registered. For non-established foreign entrepreneurs, zero threshold applies: any taxable supply performed in Germany triggers mandatory VAT registration unless the customer is liable to account for VAT under the reverse charge procedure (§ 13b UStG). For intra-Community acquisitions, a EUR 10,000 distance-sales threshold is available for EU-established sellers; for dredging services physically rendered on German land or waterway infrastructure, the place of supply is Germany and VAT registration is compulsory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the maritime dredging and seawall EPC business model, zero-rating or VAT-free treatment arises only through one of the following channels: (1) exports of goods from Germany to a non-EU destination under § 6 UStG (Ausfuhrlieferung) — not typically applicable to service works; (2) supplies of services directly related to seagoing vessels used in international commercial traffic, including services for the operation and maintenance of such vessels, under § 8 UStG and § 4 Nr. 2 UStG — applies to trailing suction hopper dredgers if the dredging activity is contractually connected to the operation of the seagoing vessel in international navigation; (3) B2B services supplied to a foreign contractor or port authority established outside Germany where the place of supply is deemed to be outside Germany under the basic rule of § 3a Abs. 2 UStG (business-to-business place-of-supply rule based on customer establishment) — the invoice should clearly reverse-charge the foreign customer; (4) intra-Community supplies under § 6a UStG to EU customers, with VAT ID validation; (5) construction services on immovable property located outside Germany are outside German territorial scope under § 3a Abs. 3 Nr. 1 UStG (Immobilienklausel). Conversely, rubble-mound breakwater works and dredging performed on German territory or German territorial seabed are construction services on German immovable property, fully subject to 19% VAT and cannot be zero-rated.

Germany has introduced the mandatory B2B e-invoicing obligation (E-Rechnungs-Pflicht) under the Wachstumschancengesetz and § 14 UStG, applicable from 1 January 2025 in a phased manner: (1) from 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B entrepreneurs must be technically able to receive electronic invoices and issue them when the recipient consents to a structured format (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x, or Peppol BIS 3.0); (2) from 1 January 2027, businesses with a preceding-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue all B2B invoices electronically; (3) from 1 January 2028, all domestic B2B suppliers must issue structured e-invoices. Germany does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, coremandated tax authority API approval, or digital stamping; invoices may be transmitted via any secure electronic channel (e-mail, EDI, portal). By 1 January 2028, all inbound and outbound B2B invoices must be exchanged and readable in structured XML using EU standard EN 16931. Non-compliant paper or PDF invoices lose input VAT deduction rights for the recipient and expose the issuer to penalties under §§ 26a, 26b UStG.

The German tax audit practice applies a tight evidentiary standard for export zero-rating claims; upon audit rejection of the export proof, the Finanzamt retroactively assesses VAT at 19% on the gross invoiced amount, plus interest at 6% per annum under § 233a AO calculated from the original VAT due date, plus monthly late-payment surcharge of 0.5% under § 240 AO, and disallows offsetting input VAT benefits. Material misstatements trigger criminal exposure under § 370 AO (Steuerhinterziehung) — imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in particularly serious cross-border cases — plus fines and forfeiture; negligent false zero-rating claims invoke § 378 AO (Leichtfertige Steuerverkürzung) with administrative fines up to EUR 50,000, and customs-type violations under § 382 AO may add additional penalties. The BZSt and local Finanzamt also cooperate with the German customs authorities (Zoll) and the Bundesbank to cross-check payment timing, causing retrospective clawback of input VAT recovery and reputational debarment from port authority tenders.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies the euro and has no general exchange controls. The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to anti-money-laundering, sanctions, tax, accounting, statistical-reporting, payment-services, and prudential requirements. A German GmbH may receive foreign investment, shareholder loans, trade proceeds, dividends, interest, royalties, and liquidation proceeds in accordance with corporate-law and tax rules. Profit distributions require distributable profits, proper shareholder approval, compliance with preservation-of-capital rules, and satisfaction of withholding-tax obligations. Dividends to non-resident shareholders may be subject to German withholding tax, generally 25% plus solidarity surcharge, subject to treaty relief, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions, exemption procedures, beneficial-ownership requirements, and anti-abuse rules. Interest, royalties, construction-related payments, related-party settlements, and intercompany funding may require transfer-pricing support, withholding-tax analysis, beneficial-owner verification, and documentation of the underlying transaction. Banks may delay or reject transactions lacking a transparent business purpose, source-of-funds evidence, beneficial-owner information, sanctions screening, or documentation required under the German Money Laundering Act and applicable EU regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general deadline requiring a German GmbH to convert foreign-currency export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, converted at the company's discretion, or repatriated through regulated payment channels. Cross-border payments and balances may be subject to statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). Reporting thresholds, exemptions, filing deadlines, and electronic reporting procedures must be checked against the current Bundesbank rules; ordinary import and export payments may qualify for specific reporting exemptions, while financial-account transactions, intercompany loans, and certain external claims or liabilities may remain reportable. Banks may independently request invoices, contracts, customs records, bills of lading, milestone certificates, and tax documentation.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework. Financial-market conduct, anti-money-laundering, and payment-services supervision may also involve the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for secondary treasury, collection, and payment functions; Low as the sole German corporate banking platform for a marine dredging and EPC business. An EU-authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide euro payment accounts, SEPA transfers, cards, and selected foreign-exchange services under passporting or German authorisation, but an EMI account is not a bank deposit account and generally does not provide the same deposit-protection, credit, documentary-trade, cash-management, escrow, guarantee, or capital-formation functionality as a bank. Offshore digital EMIs without an applicable EU authorisation, passport, German branch, or compliant correspondent structure cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services to the German GmbH merely by serving customers online. The company should retain a regulated German or EEA bank as the primary account provider, verify safeguarding arrangements, assess insolvency and fund-segregation protections, and confirm compatibility with SWIFT, SEPA, payroll, port-authority receipts, performance bonds, letters of credit, guarantees, and large-value project payments.

Route equity contributions and shareholder loans from the verified investor's account to the GmbH's account at a German or EEA-regulated bank. Use a payment narrative identifying the GmbH, funding instrument, agreement date, and purpose, such as share-capital contribution, capital reserve contribution, or shareholder loan. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for secondary treasury, collection, and payment functions; Low as the sole German corporate banking platform for a marine dredging and EPC business. An EU-authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide euro payment accounts, SEPA transfers, cards, and selected foreign-exchange services under passporting or German authorisation, but an EMI account is not a bank deposit account and generally does not provide the same deposit-protection, credit, documentary-trade, cash-management, escrow, guarantee, or capital-formation functionality as a bank. Offshore digital EMIs without an applicable EU authorisation, passport, German branch, or compliant correspondent structure cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services to the German GmbH merely by serving customers online. The company should retain a regulated German or EEA bank as the primary account provider, verify safeguarding arrangements, assess insolvency and fund-segregation protections, and confirm compatibility with SWIFT, SEPA, payroll, port-authority receipts, performance bonds, letters of credit, guarantees, and large-value project payments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 for simplified transfer pricing documentation of cross-border controlled transactions; a full Master File documentation requirement applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a formalised Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime like certain Gulf states, but the substance doctrine is embedded in § 42 AO, § 50d(3) EStG and the anti-treaty-shopping case law. A German GmbH engaged in maritime dredging must demonstrate its own staff, bonded equipment, contractual signature power and actual decision-making to avoid the disregard of its legal form. Substantial economic presence is equally central to entitlement under double taxation agreements and the availability of trade tax loss carryforward.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Criminal Income Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Up to 5 years imprisonment under § 370 AO for standard tax evasion; up to 10 years for a particularly serious case, plus a criminal fine, reopening of assessments and immediate payment of all overdue tax with interest. The German tax authorities may also impose the late filing and late payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: The managing director is civilly liable under § 43 GmbHG for damages caused to the GmbH and personally responsible under § 69 AO for unpaid taxes where § 34 AO duties were violated. Criminal courts may order restitution, impose a travel ban and confiscate passports under §§ 111a, 112a StPO if the director presents a flight risk.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax office may estimate income under § 162(1) AO and add a surcharge under § 162(4) AO of 10% of the positive income adjustment, minimum €5,000, in addition to interest under § 233a AO. Persistent documentation failure shifts the burden of proof and exposes the taxpayer to audit-wide adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve artificial intercompany pricing or who do not maintain documentation can be personally liable for the resultant tax shortfall under §§ 34, 69 AO, and may face criminal liability for abetting tax evasion under § 369 AO / StGB. Travel restrictions are available where tax debt is substantial and the director plans to relocate assets abroad.
Shell Company / Letterbox Entity / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under § 42 AO, the tax-act may ignore the GmbH and reattribute profits to the economic beneficiary; infringement of GmbH capital rules can trigger registration cancellation, refusal of trade licences, port authority suspension and fines. False declarations in the commercial register carry additional criminal liability under § 14 GmbHG.

Director personal liability: Civil liability arises under §§ 43 and 64 GmbHG for improper distributions and for trading without the required German maritime licence. In aggravated treaty-abuse cases, directors may be sued directly under § 826 BGB for intentional damage and be subject to asset confiscation and imprisonment.
AML / Money Laundering in Commercial Payments

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under § 261 StGB is punishable by fine or imprisonment from one to ten years in commercial or aggravated cases; confiscation of the payment streams and assets is mandatory. The Financial Intelligence Unit and BaFin may impose administrative fines and revoke bank-relationship privileges.

Director personal liability: Managing directors who sign or knowingly approve the flow are criminally liable as principals or accomplices. Courts may order civil restitution, freeze accounts, impose travel bans and issue European arrest warrants. The low-risk AML classification of maritime_dredging does not mitigate deliberate abuse; it aggravates sentencing because the sector is usually trusted.

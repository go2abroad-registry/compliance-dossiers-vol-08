Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for national VAT identification number allocation; competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment; official electronic filing and registration portal: ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung), https://www.elster.de; export customs processing via German Customs (Generalzolldirektion) and ATLAS.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned German GmbH obtains its local tax number (Steuernummer) by submitting the legalization and registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the locally competent Finanzamt through ELSTER or in paper form within one month after incorporation or commencement of business. The managing director must disclose anticipated licensing revenue, corporate shareholders, and permanent establishment facts. Upon processing, the Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer. The VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is applied for separately from BZSt using form 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer' and is used for intra-EU supplies and reverse-charge identification. If the GmbH exports physical goods, it must also obtain an EORI number from German Customs. Non-established foreign licensors may register directly with the competent Finanzamt for limited VAT purposes when reverse charge is unavailable or for refund claims.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. A GmbH is irrevocably subject to entity-level corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Relevant elective mechanisms include corporate tax group (Organschaft under KStG §§14-19) with profit and loss pooling; VAT group (Organschaft under UStG §2 Abs.2 Nr.2) for financial, economic, and organisational integration; and the small-business threshold election under UStG §19, which is generally unavailable to a commercial film-licensing GmbH because of its capitalisation and turnover profile. Germany applies ATAD-aligned anti-hybrid rules; there is no elective hybrid-mismatch classification, and CFC rules may reattribute passive royalty income of a German GmbH to its German tax base.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under UStG §6 applies to supplies of goods physically exported to a third-country destination and to certain services directly related to exported goods, provided the exporter retains proof of export and, where applicable, proof of arrival in the destination country. Film-copyright licensing to a non-resident commercial licensee is generally not a zero-rated export but is outside the scope of German VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule (UStG §3a(2)): the licence fee is taxable in the licensee's jurisdiction under reverse charge. To benefit from outside-scope treatment, the German GmbH must evidence the licensee's foreign business establishment and VAT/GST registration status, the substance of the licence agreement, and the absence of a German permanent establishment. Licensing to private final consumers resident outside Germany may also be outside German VAT if the supply is an electronically supplied service or the copyright exploitation is otherwise treated as supplied in the customer's country; otherwise 19% German VAT applies.

German tax authorities scrutinise export and offshore-supply documentation, particularly licence royalty flows to low-tax or treaty-based jurisdictions. Fraudulent or unsupported claims of export zero-rating or outside-scope treatment expose the GmbH to retroactive VAT assessment for up to ten years in cases of concealment (AO §169), compulsory interest of 0.5% per month on the assessed VAT, late-payment surcharges of 1% per month on outstanding VAT, denial of input VAT deduction, and fines or imprisonment for tax evasion under AO §§370-371. Managing directors are personally liable for VAT debts and penalties in the event of deliberate non-remittance. Customs and tax authorities may also cooperate internationally under the EU VAT administrative cooperation regime and recover claims through the mutual assistance and recovery directives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully liberalised current and capital account regime. The euro is freely convertible; there are no general exchange-control permits, prior approval requirements, import advance-payment restrictions, or repatriation quotas. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is legally unrestricted and is effected by a shareholder distribution resolution after German corporate tax and statutory dividend withholding tax (generally 26.375% including solidarity surcharge, subject to tax treaty relief). The only mandatory compliance element is external-stats reporting: cross-border payments and receivables with non-residents must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank in accordance with AWV when the relevant monthly aggregates reach the statutory reporting threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose export proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratios, retention periods, or foreign-currency depositary rules. Export receipts from film licensing may be received and held in EUR or any freely convertible currency. A German resident licensor is, however, obliged to report incoming cross-border payments to Deutsche Bundesbank when the calendar-month aggregate reaches the statutory threshold, typically EUR 12,500 or more, within the statutory AWV filing deadlines. Banks often assist with this statistical return, but the company treasury must maintain the underlying licence agreements, invoices, remittance advices, and balance-of-payments documentation as evidence.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank — Central Bank of the Federal Republic of Germany; together with Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) for banking conduct and AML supervision; cross-border FX reporting is administered under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility — Conditional: Medium as a supplemental settlement rail for small-value streaming-royalty flows; Low for primary treasury or statutory capital escrow. Offshore, non-EEA digital EMIs do not have a legal basis to accept deposits in Germany and are not KWG-licensed credit institutions. EEA e-money institutions may passport payment services under PSD2 and open e-money accounts, but such accounts do not provide statutory deposit protection and cannot replace a bank account for GmbH formation capital, collateralised trade operations, or high-value LC transactions. Any EMI usage should sit beside a KWG-licensed commercial bank account with full pooling/reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound GmbH share capital must be wired by the foreign shareholder from that shareholder's fully identified bank account to the GmbH's capital escrow account (Gründungskonto) at the German account bank. For non-EU transfers the compliant rail is SWIFT MT103 with complete originator and beneficiary names, addresses, account numbers (IBAN/BIC), and a remittance field quoting the notarial deed, the shareholder name, and the amount of Stammeinlage. For EU/SEPA transfers the bank should receive a SEPA Credit Transfer with identical corporate references and a statistical purpose code for direct investment/capital contribution. No Bundesbank FX approval is required, but the resident GmbH and the bank must capture the inward payment for Bundesbank external statistics when the statutory threshold is exceeded; the bank may request source-of-funds documentation before releasing funds from escrow.For outbound settlement to licensors, producers, and international distributors the principal clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for non-EU/foreign-currency disbursements, Target2/SEPA Credit Transfer for euro-denominated EU payments, and an irrevocable commercial letter of credit under UCP600 for high-value distribution-right acquisitions or structured finish-payments. Each MT103 should include the correct beneficiary bank BIC/IBAN, invoice and film-title references, tax registration references, and the underlying contract clause; LC document packages should include the final invoice, delivery/remittance confirmation, distribution statement, certificate of title/chain-of-title opinion, insurance certificate, and any required trade-control or tax forms. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if annual turnover exceeds €10 million and cross-border transactions with related parties exceed €1 million. Master File required if group consolidated turnover exceeds €100 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions, but substance is required to obtain treaty benefits and to avoid anti-abuse rules (e.g., §42 AO, GAAR). A German GmbH must demonstrate actual business activity, premises, employees, and decision-making in Germany to prevent recharacterization of transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years (or up to 10 years in severe cases) and fines up to €50,000. Additional civil penalties of up to 100% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing officers may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they fail to fulfill filing and payment obligations. Civil restitution, travel bans, and asset freezes may be imposed. In cases of gross negligence, personal liability extends to the full tax debt.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of additional taxes plus interest. Penalties up to €25,000 for non-compliance with substance requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes arising from recharacterization if they knowingly facilitated the sham structure. Travel bans and restrictions on leaving Germany may be imposed during investigations.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, additional tax assessments, and late payment interest. Penalties of 5-10% of the adjustment amount if documentation is inadequate or not provided.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-compliant transfer pricing may face personal liability for the additional tax and penalties. In severe cases, criminal charges for tax evasion may be pursued.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for corporate entities and up to €100,000 for individuals. Regulatory sanctions including revocation of business licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined and imprisoned for up to 5 years for willful AML violations. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent local tax office (Finanzamt) administers corporate income tax, trade tax and domestic VAT registration. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer or USt-IdNr). Applications and tax registrations are generally submitted electronically through the ELSTER portal (Elektronische Steuererklärung), with the BZSt VAT identification service used for USt-IdNr issuance and validation.

E-government filing portal

A GmbH must be registered with the competent Finanzamt after incorporation and commencement of business. The company submits the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER, providing its commercial register details, registered office, business activities, managing directors, bank information, expected turnover, VAT liability and requested tax regimes. The Finanzamt issues a Steuernummer for tax filings. A USt-IdNr is separately requested from the BZSt, generally through the BZSt online application or the relevant ELSTER registration process. A non-resident company making taxable supplies in Germany must generally obtain a German tax number and, where required, a USt-IdNr; registration is handled by the Finanzamt designated for foreign businesses. VAT registration may be unnecessary where all German supplies are subject to domestic reverse charge or otherwise outside German VAT, but the position must be documented and periodically reviewed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is ordinarily subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax at entity level. Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a GmbH. The corporate form cannot ordinarily elect transparent partnership taxation. Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act permits certain partnerships and comparable entities to opt for corporate taxation, but it does not provide an election for a GmbH to become fiscally transparent. A GmbH may participate in a tax group (Organschaft), subject to statutory requirements including financial integration and a valid profit-and-loss transfer agreement; this is a tax consolidation mechanism rather than an entity-classification election. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Section 4k of the Corporate Income Tax Act may deny deductions or impose corresponding restrictions for specified hybrid arrangements; they do not create a general elective classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold that permits an established business making taxable domestic supplies to remain unregistered. The German small-business exemption under Section 19 of the VAT Act may apply where prior-calendar-year total turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and election rules; this is an exemption regime, not a universal registration threshold. Non-established businesses generally must register for German VAT from the first taxable German supply unless a reverse-charge, special scheme or other statutory exception applies. EU-wide distance-sales and certain electronically supplied-service rules may use the EUR 10,000 threshold, subject to the One Stop Shop regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax deduction under Sections 4 and 6 of the German VAT Act where the goods are transported or dispatched to a third country and the export conditions are satisfied. The supplier must establish that the goods physically left the EU customs territory and retain documentary or electronic evidence, normally including customs exit confirmation. Intra-EU supplies of goods are exempt with input-tax deduction under separate intra-Community supply rules and require valid customer VAT identification, proof of transport and recapitulative reporting. Services are not automatically zero-rated because the customer is offshore. The place-of-supply rules must be applied: many business-to-business services are outside German VAT when supplied to a non-German business customer, while specific services, use-and-enjoyment rules, immovable-property services, passenger transport, events and other categories have special rules. Refunds or input-tax credits require valid purchase invoices, business use and compliance with the German VAT deduction rules.

  • Customs export declaration and export movement reference number (MRN), where required
  • Electronic customs exit confirmation or other prescribed customs evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, consideration and export transaction
  • Transport documentation, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, freight documentation or equivalent proof
  • Evidence linking the goods to the export transaction, including order records, packing lists, delivery records and carrier or customs-agent records
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification number, transport evidence and required EC Sales List reporting
  • Bank payment or inward-remittance evidence where commercially relevant; Germany does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a statutory condition for export VAT exemption
  • For offshore services: contract, customer business-status evidence, customer location evidence, service description, place-of-supply analysis and records supporting any reverse-charge or out-of-scope treatment

Germany does not currently operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or mandatory digital-stamping system. From 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. Issuance of structured e-invoices is subject to transition periods: generally until the end of 2026, and until the end of 2027 for issuers whose prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 800,000; from 2028 the applicable EU-standard electronic invoicing requirements are intended to apply broadly. Permitted formats include an EN 16931-compliant structured invoice such as XRechnung or a compliant hybrid format such as ZUGFeRD. Electronic VAT returns, registrations and related filings are generally submitted through ELSTER. Federal public-sector business-to-government invoicing may require XRechnung or another prescribed format. Invoices must retain statutory content, authenticity, integrity and legibility requirements; no universal pre-clearance API to the tax authority is required.

Germany has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export VAT exemption. Export claims are tested primarily through customs exit evidence, transport records, transaction linkage, customer and destination data, invoice records and accounting consistency. If the supplier cannot prove the exemption, German VAT may be assessed retrospectively, normally at 19% or the applicable reduced rate, together with statutory interest under the German Fiscal Code and possible correction of VAT returns. Improper input-tax refunds or export exemptions may be clawed back through amended assessments and recovery of tax. Intentional tax evasion may constitute a criminal offense under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code, with imprisonment or fines; negligent tax reduction may attract penalties under Section 378. Administrative fines, late-filing penalties, interest, customs penalties and loss of VAT credibility may also apply. General assessment periods are commonly four years, extended to five years for tax reduction by negligence and ten years for intentional tax evasion, subject to statutory suspension and interruption rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility of the euro for both current and capital account transactions under the free-movement-of-capital provisions of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There are no general foreign exchange controls, no exchange surrender requirements, and no prior central bank approvals for inbound or outbound payments. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders by a German GmbH are legally free but practically require a valid corporate resolution, audited or certified accounts, proof of distributable profit under German commercial law (HGB), a signed dividend notification letter, and tax withholding clearance. The only mandatory friction is the statutory cross-border payment statistical reporting threshold: any payment received or made by a German resident exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics, ordinarily via the AWV reporting form on the Bundesbank portal. If a payment is routed through a German bank, the bank will generally provide the reporting mechanism; payments routed through non-resident banks require the German resident debtor/creditor to report directly. There are no capital repatriation restrictions, non-resident surcharges, or exchange allocation queues.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender or repatriation requirement on export proceeds by a German GmbH. Export receivables may be retained in foreign currency, converted freely, or used offshore without any mandatory conversion ratio or time limit. The sole binding obligation is the AWV cross-border reporting duty: exports of goods and services where the counterparty payment and the export occur in separate reporting months must be reported; in addition, any payment of EUR 12,500 or more relating to an export transaction must be captured in the Bundesbank external sector statistics if received from a non-resident. A German exporter is not required to convert the resulting foreign currency into euro, but the receiving German bank will enforce documentation requirements for anti-money-laundering and customs/foreign-trade record-keeping.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) with primary prudential and market conduct oversight by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin); foreign trade and payments statistics under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV) are administered by Deutsche Bundesbank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for core fiduciary trust and escrow functions. German e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions operating under either a German license or a European passport may provide e-money wallets, payment accounts, MT103 initiation, and FX conversion; however, for a discretionary asset-protection trust administration company classified under ISIC 6430 and described as a high-risk special-license trust sector, most German tier-1 banks will reject tainted payment flows, and EMIs will generally classify the business as prohibited or enhanced-risk. Digital EU-licensed EMIs (e.g., Payoneer, Wise, Revolut Business, Holvi, N26 pocket accounts) are legally permitted ancillary payment channels for low-value operational expenses only, but they cannot substitute for a German commercial bank account for the following reasons: (i) they are not credit institutions under the German Banking Act (KWG); (ii) client trust money would not benefit from deposit insurance in the same manner; (iii) a licensed fiduciary under German law is required to hold client funds in segregated escrow accounts at a bank, not at an EMI; and (iv) clearing banks will not accept EMI-origin funds when the underlying transaction is a third-party trust disbursement without full source-of-wealth evidence. Recommended approach: use EMIs only for pre-funded petty cash and virtual-card operating expenses, while all beneficiary trust-money movement must be cleared through a regulated German or EU credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the German GmbH from non-resident shareholders or settlors must be executed through a fully compliant international wire chain. The primary rail is SWIFT MT103 (Customer Credit Transfer) with the beneficiary account at the German credit institution. The wiring instructions must include the German IBAN and BIC, the beneficiary legal name exactly as registered in Handelsregister, the reference line 'Capital Contribution to DE_GMBH, ISIC 6430, ESCROW', and a precise purpose code such as EU Reuters Purpose Code S1108 (Investment in equity capital) for share capital or S1122 (Other intercompany loans) for shareholder loans. The sending bank should also include the beneficiary's German tax ID and VAT ID when required. For funds originating from a fiduciary trust, the sending bank must ensure the ordering customer is the named settlor or the trust itself with a full legal name; general-purpose or blank-reference wires will be returned or frozen. The German receiving bank will apply the statutory AWV reporting: if the aggregate value exceeds EUR 12,500 and the counterparty is non-resident, the bank reports to Deutsche Bundesbank unless the GmbH agrees to report directly via the Bundesbank OGA (Online General Anzeige) portal. Inbound capital contributions should be accounted under the German Commercial Code, reflected in the GmbH's books as share capital or loan liability, and the corresponding UBO and source-of-funds documentation must be filed with the bank's compliance department on or before value date. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for core fiduciary trust and escrow functions. German e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions operating under either a German license or a European passport may provide e-money wallets, payment accounts, MT103 initiation, and FX conversion; however, for a discretionary asset-protection trust administration company classified under ISIC 6430 and described as a high-risk special-license trust sector, most German tier-1 banks will reject tainted payment flows, and EMIs will generally classify the business as prohibited or enhanced-risk. Digital EU-licensed EMIs (e.g., Payoneer, Wise, Revolut Business, Holvi, N26 pocket accounts) are legally permitted ancillary payment channels for low-value operational expenses only, but they cannot substitute for a German commercial bank account for the following reasons: (i) they are not credit institutions under the German Banking Act (KWG); (ii) client trust money would not benefit from deposit insurance in the same manner; (iii) a licensed fiduciary under German law is required to hold client funds in segregated escrow accounts at a bank, not at an EMI; and (iv) clearing banks will not accept EMI-origin funds when the underlying transaction is a third-party trust disbursement without full source-of-wealth evidence. Recommended approach: use EMIs only for pre-funded petty cash and virtual-card operating expenses, while all beneficiary trust-money movement must be cleared through a regulated German or EU credit institution.

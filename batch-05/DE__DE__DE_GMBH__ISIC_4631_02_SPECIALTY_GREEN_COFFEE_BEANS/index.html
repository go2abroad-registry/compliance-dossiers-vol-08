Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt fuer Steuern, BZSt) for EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), OSS/IOSS one-stop-shop registrations, and cross-border VAT refunds; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, trade tax assessment base, and turnover tax filings; Generalzolldirektion (German Customs) for import VAT (Einfuhrumsatzsteuer), export supervision, and customs tariff matters; all electronic filing via the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

1) Notarize the articles of association and file for registration of the GmbH in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht. 2) Submit the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically via ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt; obtain the corporate income tax number (Steuernummer) for all direct taxes and trade tax. 3) Apply separately to BZSt for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) using Form USt 1 C; this number is required for intra-Community B2B supplies, recapitulative statements, and cross-border export zero-rating documentation. 4) Identify the applicable municipal trade tax multiplier (Hebesatz) for Gewerbesteuer purposes through the local authority; no separate municipal registration is required beyond the Handelsregister entry. 5) Open a German bank account to receive VAT refunds and expedite export proceed reconciliation. No statutory import or wholesale license is required for green coffee bean trading. Completion typically takes 2 to 8 weeks; late filing triggers penalties under Section 378 AO.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a German GmbH is a resident corporation with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income under Section 1 KStG; it is never treated as a pass-through entity. Corporate income tax at flat 15%; solidarity surcharge of 5.5% on corporate income tax (0.825% effective); municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 3.5% base assessment rate multiplied by a municipal Hebesatz of 200% to 490%, yielding an average combined corporate income tax burden of approximately 30% to 32%. Specialty green coffee distribution is a commercial business (Gewerbebetrieb) fully subject to trade tax; no territorial tax exemption or special zone treatment applies within Germany.

Available election forms

Germany has no U.S.-style check-the-box election; tax classification follows mandatory civil and tax law. Available elections and groupings: 1) Corporate income tax and trade tax Organschaft (tax group) under Section 14 KStG with a profit-and-loss absorption agreement and minimum five-year term, allowing intra-group offsetting of results; 2) VAT Organschaft under Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG consolidating multiple German entities into one VAT entrepreneur; 3) Small business exemption under Section 19 UStG, available only if prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year forecast turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000, generally impractical for a coffee trading GmbH that needs input VAT recovery; 4) Union OSS and IOSS elections under Section 18j UStG for intra-EU distance sales and import consignments; 5) Permanent establishment profit attribution under the Authorized OECD Approach; 6) Anti-hybrid adjustments under the German ATAD implementation act apply mandatorily without elective opt-in, and restructuring loss transfers require prior Finanzamt approval under Section 14 KStG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for VAT registration: any entrepreneur (Unternehmer) making taxable supplies in Germany must register from the first transaction. Domestic small business exemption thresholds under Section 19 UStG are EUR 22,000 prior-year actual turnover and EUR 50,000 current-year forecast turnover; these thresholds do not apply to non-established foreign entrepreneurs. The EU intra-Community distance sales threshold is EUR 10,000 via the OSS scheme; B2B supplies by non-established foreign suppliers fall under the reverse charge mechanism, generally removing any local registration requirement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exemption with input VAT deduction) is available for: 1) deliveries to non-EU third countries under Section 6 UStG, requiring physical dispatch or transport of the goods out of Germany into the third country; 2) intra-Community supplies under Section 6a UStG, requiring the customer to hold a valid EU VAT identification number, the goods to be transported to another member state, and proper reporting in the recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung); 3) transport and ancillary services directly connected to exported specialty green coffee beans. Domestic sales of green coffee beans are subject to the reduced 7% VAT rate; exports are effectively 0% rated. Exporter eligibility requires possession of a valid German Steuernummer and, for intra-EU operations, the USt-IdNr. issued by BZSt. Imports of green coffee beans into Germany are subject to import VAT at the reduced 7% rate, immediately recoverable as input VAT under the standard deduction procedure, provided the subsequent export supply is properly zero-rated.

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing in Germany introduced by the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025, every German entrepreneur must be able to receive structured electronic invoices; from 1 January 2027, issuance is mandatory for entrepreneurs with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000; from 1 January 2028, issuance is mandatory for all domestic B2B transactions. Accepted standards are XRechnung (EN 16931), ZUGFeRD 2.0.1 and higher, or EDI-conformant formats; transmission via secure email, EDI interfaces, or interoperable networks, with PEPPOL optional. Germany currently has no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance API authorization like the Italian SDI system; periodic VAT advance returns remain the enforcement mechanism. EU-wide real-time e-invoicing clearance under the ViDA framework is expected to apply from 2030.

Export zero-rating is a high-risk audit area for the Federal Tax Office and German Customs. The exporter must possess complete documentary evidence within 6 months from the date of supply (Section 6.2.2 UStAE), retain all records for 10 years (Section 147 AO), and ensure the export declaration is authentically matched with transport documents and payment flows. Failure to substantiate zero-rating triggers a retrospective output VAT assessment under Section 17 UStG, a clawback of previously deducted input VAT, and back interest at 6% per annum (0.5% per month) under Section 233a AO. Fraudulent or grossly negligent claims are prosecuted criminally under Section 370 AO with imprisonment up to 10 years or monetary fines, or under Section 378 AO with administrative fines up to EUR 50,000. The BZSt, Hauptzollamt, and ATLAS customs surveillance systems cross-match export declarations, bank transfers, and commercial documents to detect circular green coffee trading schemes; delayed or non-repatriated export proceeds in foreign currency may additionally trigger transfer price adjustments under Section 1 AStG and the German CFC rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility by virtue of EU free movement of capital under TFEU Articles 63–65 and national AWV liberalisation. No exchange control approvals are required for capital injections, profit remittances, dividends, or trade settlements. For resident GmbH shareholders, distributions to EU/EEA corporate shareholders are generally exempt from withholding under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive; non-EU recipients may claim investment-protection or DTT relief. The material practical hurdles are exclusively regulatory-AML: source-of-funds transparency, beneficial ownership disclosure, and matching payment purpose codes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio into EUR. Exporters may lawfully hold foreign currency in onshore or offshore accounts. The only binding requirement is statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV for each cross-border receipt/payment above the applicable materiality threshold (currently EUR 50,000 or foreign-currency equivalent); such reports must be made within the statutory reporting window, ordinarily no later than the 5th working day following the settlement date or the specified Bundesbank schedule, depending on the type of cross-border payment.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Deutsche Bundesbank), acting as the national monetary authority and statistical reporting recipient under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV). Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) regulates banking conduct, payment institutions, and securities/derivatives market activity. The Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action holds overarching foreign-trade administration competence.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMIs and payment banks licensed by BaFin or passporting under PSD2 are legally feasible for ancillary treasury functions such as receivables collection, expense pooling, or currency conversion. Feasibility as the primary cross-border clearing rail is low to medium: established German commercial banks remain necessary for irrevocable letters of credit, compliant MT103 delivery with purpose-code transparency, large working-capital limits, and strong correspondent-banking provenance. A fully offshore, non-EU EMI acting without a German/eEA passporting license is not lawfully permitted to render payment services into Germany and should be avoided.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from a foreign shareholder are executed by authenticated SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the beneficiary's EUR/IBAN account at the German bank, with the field-70 purpose text explicitly referencing the notarised capital-increase resolution and wording such as 'Einlage zur Durchführung der Kapitalerhöhung, Gesellschaftsvertrag vom [date]'. The remitting bank must obtain the German bank's BIC and the GmbH's IBAN. For EUR-denominated transfers from the EEA, SEPA Credit Transfer is also acceptable; for non-EEA USD or other-currency injection, the German bank converts at ECB reference or agreed market rate subject to FX confirmation. No foreign-exchange approval is required under German law, but the transfer amount must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV if above EUR 50,000, and the payment description must match the contributed capital to enable clean bank reconciliation.Outbound trade settlements for green coffee shipments are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 authenticated transfers for open-account suppliers, and through documentary irrevocable letters of credit (DC LC) issued in accordance with UCP 600 for verified origin-country suppliers. The German import bank issues or advises the LC using MT700/MT707 messages, allowing the beneficiary bank to leverage MT710/MT720/MT740 common procedures for presentations; MT754/MT799 messages provide advising and amendment statuses. For EUR-denominated payments, settlement flows via SEPA and RTGS-based TARGET; for USD or third-currency settlement, correspondent banking and nostro/vostro accounts are engaged. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMIs and payment banks licensed by BaFin or passporting under PSD2 are legally feasible for ancillary treasury functions such as receivables collection, expense pooling, or currency conversion. Feasibility as the primary cross-border clearing rail is low to medium: established German commercial banks remain necessary for irrevocable letters of credit, compliant MT103 delivery with purpose-code transparency, large working-capital limits, and strong correspondent-banking provenance.

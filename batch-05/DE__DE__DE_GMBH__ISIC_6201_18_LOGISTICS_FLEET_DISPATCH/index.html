Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) and certain withholding procedures; local Finanzamt for corporation tax, trade tax, wage tax and VAT assessment; Elster online portal (www.elster.de) for electronic registration and filings.

E-government filing portal

Upon commercial registration at Handelsregister and business registration (Gewerbeanmeldung), the local Finanzamt automatically assigns a Steuernummer. Separately, a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) must be applied for electronically via BZSt. Foreign non-established entrepreneurs must submit an application to BZSt with proof of identity, registration and expected German taxable supplies. Processing typically takes 4–6 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - resident GmbH is subject to unlimited tax liability on worldwide income at corporation tax rate of 15%, plus solidarity surcharge and trade tax at municipal rates (typically 14–17%). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - resident GmbH is subject to unlimited tax liability on worldwide income at corporation tax rate of 15%, plus solidarity surcharge and trade tax at municipal rates (typically 14–17%). No transparent pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

Organschaft (fiscal unity) election for corporation tax, trade tax and VAT under §§ 14–17 KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG; no US-style check-the-box; hybrid mismatch elections under § 4k EStG and § 8b KStG; small-enterprise exemption election under § 19 UStG for turnover not exceeding EUR 25,000 prior year and EUR 100,000 current year forecast.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for entrepreneurs; registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction. Small-enterprise exemption threshold: €25,000 previous-year turnover and €100,000 current-year forecast.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports to third countries qualify for 0% under § 4 Nr. 1a and § 6 UStG; intra-Community supplies qualify under § 4 Nr. 1b and § 6a UStG if the recipient has a valid VAT ID and the goods physically leave Germany. For B2B services such as logistics SaaS provided to customers outside Germany, the place of supply follows the customer's jurisdiction, making the supply outside German VAT scope; no German 0% rate applies. To claim zero-rating for goods, the exporter must obtain and retain documentary export proof in accordance with §§ 8–11 UStDV.

  • Customs export declaration (ATLAS) with export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR) with export endorsement
  • Export proof under §§ 8–11 UStDV (customs confirmation, commercial evidence, or alternative proof)
  • Inward remittance/payment evidence showing receipt of sale proceeds where available

From 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B invoices must be issued as structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD). Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance API or digital stamping system. Electronic transmission must ensure authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility; non-compliance may trigger fines and penalties under § 26a UStG.

Tax authorities scrutinize export proofs and intra-Community supply evidence; where documents are false, incomplete or missing, the 0% rate is retroactively denied. Assessed VAT carries monthly interest of 0.5% for up to six years, plus penalty surcharges and fines under § 370 AO; intentional misdeclaration is a criminal offense carrying imprisonment. Anti-fraud provisions under § 18f UStG also impose summary invoice liability and register audit obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a Eurozone member with full current and capital account convertibility under EU Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) Article 63. Profit remittances and dividend distributions are unrestricted, subject only to standard AML/KYC checks, corporate law formalities, and sanctions screening. No exchange control approvals are required for shareholder loans, capital contributions, or trade payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirements exist for export proceeds. Businesses may retain, transfer, or convert EUR receivables freely. Reporting obligations apply solely for balance-of-payments statistics (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung) when transactions exceed EUR 12,500, with no FX restriction.

Central bank supervision: European Central Bank (ECB) and Deutsche Bundesbank; prudential oversight by BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Digital EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, N26 Business, PPS) are permitted under Germany's PSD2 framework if they hold a BaFin license or a valid EU passport. They are suitable for operational multi-currency accounts and low-value clearing, but not recommended for capital escrow or high-value inbound equity transfers due to limited German IBAN acceptance, regulatory uncertainty, and corporate banking integration constraints.

Master / Local File threshold: Local file required if controlled transactions exceed 5,000,000 EUR (goods) or 500,000 EUR (services); Master file required if group revenue > 100,000,000 EUR.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have specific ESR, but substance is required for treaty benefits and to avoid abuse under Section 17 AO (General Anti-Abuse Rule).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years; fines up to 10% of evaded tax; additional interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes and criminal penalties; travel bans and asset seizures possible.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, additional tax assessments, and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for tax deficiencies and fines; potential disqualification from directorship.
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to 100,000 EUR; imprisonment for serious cases; regulatory sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined; travel bans and professional disqualification in severe cases.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 5% of adjusted transaction value; interest on underpaid tax; potential recharacterization.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly participated; civil restitution possible.

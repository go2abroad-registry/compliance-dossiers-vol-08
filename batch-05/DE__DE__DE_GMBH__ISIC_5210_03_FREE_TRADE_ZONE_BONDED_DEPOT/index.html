Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory share capital of a GmbH is at least EUR 25,000. Before registration, at least EUR 12,500 must generally be paid in cash where there is one or more shareholder, and each cash contribution must be paid at least to the extent required by the articles and the managing director's registration statement. No statutory escrow account is required, but the contribution must be credited to a company account or otherwise demonstrably placed at the company's disposal. Contributions in kind require additional disclosure, valuation, and documentation. Court and notarial fees are variable under the GNotKG; Germany has no separate fixed state incorporation filing fee.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain any required building-use or change-of-use approval, fire-safety concept, fire-authority acceptance, emergency exits, extinguishing systems, alarm arrangements, and evacuation documentation. Racking and mezzanines require appropriate structural-load verification and safe installation. Maintain workplace risk assessments, operator training, inspection records for lifting and handling equipment, electrical systems, pressure equipment, and other work equipment under German occupational-safety rules. Dangerous-goods storage or handling may require additional fire, hazardous-substance, water-protection, and dangerous-goods compliance.

Paid-in capital rule: The statutory share capital of a GmbH is at least EUR 25,000. Before registration, at least EUR 12,500 must generally be paid in cash where there is one or more shareholder, and each cash contribution must be paid at least to the extent required by the articles and the managing director's registration statement. No statutory escrow account is required, but the contribution must be credited to a company account or otherwise demonstrably placed at the company's disposal. Contributions in kind require additional disclosure, valuation, and documentation. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) and the local Tax Office (Finanzamt) of the GmbH's registered seat; electronic filing via ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt Online (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

A German-resident GmbH registers with the competent local Finanzamt by submitting the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) through ELSTER or in writing. The Finanzamt assigns the statutory local tax number (Steuernummer) within approximately four to six weeks. The VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) must be applied for separately with the BZSt, using the official application form (Formular 'USt 1' or via the BZSt Online portal). Foreign-owned GmbHs may apply directly; no fiscal representative is required for establishing the GmbH itself, but cross-border transactions require the VAT ID for EU trade and B2B reverse charge procedures.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A German-resident GmbH registers with the competent local Finanzamt by submitting the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) through ELSTER or in writing. The Finanzamt assigns the statutory local tax number (Steuernummer) within approximately four to six weeks. The VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) must be applied for separately with the BZSt, using the official application form (Formular 'USt 1' or via the BZSt Online portal). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Organschaft (German fiscal unity) election for corporation tax, trade tax and VAT through a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) with at least 95% financial and organisational control. VAT fiscal unity (Umsatzsteuer-Organschaft) is separately electable and consolidates VAT obligations of closely connected enterprises. Germany does not offer a check-the-box or US-style flow-through election; the GmbH is always subject to entity-level taxation. Hybrid mismatch and interest limitation (Zinsschranke) rules apply mandatorily, but certain elections and safe-harbour alternatives are available to mitigate double taxation and deductibility limits.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 (no general registration threshold for established German entrepreneurs; however, the small-enterprise exemption under Section 19 UStG applies if turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and is not expected to exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year, unless the entrepreneur opts to waive the exemption).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) under Section 4(1)(1) UStG applies to export supplies of goods to third countries (outside the EU) where the goods are shipped or dispatched by the supplier or on the supplier's account to a destination outside the EU. In the context of a free trade zone / bonded warehouse depot, the goods must be placed under an external Community customs procedure (customs warehousing or free-zone procedure) and subsequently physically leave the EU within the statutory time limits; the supplier must hold a complete set of documentary evidence proving the export, including a customs export declaration, proof of exit and transport documents. Services directly connected to such exported goods may also qualify for zero rating if the conditions of Sections 4(4) to 4(8) UStG are fulfilled.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) with electronic discharge in ATLAS/ECS
  • Bill of Lading (or Sea Waybill / Air Waybill) evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Export Accompanying Document (EAD) or equivalent proof of exit issued by German customs
  • Commercial Invoice with express VAT zero-rating annotation and export references
  • Transport documentation (CMR/CTD) or warehouse release document evidencing the bonded movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment confirmation showing consideration received from the foreign purchaser
  • EUR.1 or other certificate of preferential origin if a customs preferential claim is made

Mandatory e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) for domestic B2B supplies: from 1 January 2025, every German-resident entrepreneur must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XInvoice/ZUGFeRD using the EN 16931 core data model); issuance obligations are phased in — from 1 January 2027 for entrepreneurs with more than EUR 800,000 annual turnover, and from 1 January 2028 for all entrepreneurs. Transmission may occur via Peppol, email with embedded XML, or dedicated portals. There is no real-time fiscal pre-clearance or digital stamping API in the German VAT system; however, invoices must be stored digitally in a tamper-proof manner under the GoBD (Principles on the Proper Keeping and Storage of Digital Records), and tax authorities have the right to demand electronic access during tax audits.

German tax authorities conduct dense documentary audits of export zero-rating claims; if the required evidence is not available or the goods are diverted to domestic use, the tax office retroactively assesses VAT at the standard rate (19%) plus interest of 0.5% per month on arrears, late-payment penalties of up to 10% of the assessed amount, and fines under Section 26a UStG of up to EUR 25,000 per violation. Intentional or reckless false zero-rating constitutes criminal tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code (AO), punishable by up to ten years' imprisonment where the offence is committed in the most serious cases, or up to five years' imprisonment in ordinary cases, and the responsible directors/managers may be subject to personal liability for the evaded tax.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the euro and all freely convertible currencies under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Sections 2(2) and 10-16 AWG. No exchange-control licensing, prior approval or rationing applies to cross-border payments; the sole residual obligation is ex-post statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank for each resident-non-resident payment exceeding EUR 12,500. Profit remittance from a German GmbH is unrestricted: dividends, loan repayments and service fee transfers abroad require no exchange-control authorization and are subject only to standard German dividend withholding tax (25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, mitigable under double-tax treaties), corporate governance and AML documentation duties. Bonded warehouse activities (ISIC 5210) within a free trade zone do not trigger enhanced convertibility restrictions, practical obstacles being limited to bank-side KYC evidence, UBO transparency, sanctions screening and customs supervision formalities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has abolished all statutory surrender and repatriation obligations for export proceeds. There is no legal deadline for the in-country collection of export earnings and no mandatory conversion ratio into euro. Export receipts may be retained abroad, kept in foreign-currency accounts, or directly applied to trade payables. The only binding requirement is the AWV balance-of-payments reporting obligation: every resident-non-resident payment exceeding EUR 12,500, including export proceeds and re-export earnings from bonded depot operations, must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five working days via the prescribed reporting portal using the appropriate transaction assignment code (Geschäftscode).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank), acting as the competent authority for foreign trade and payments statistics, monetary external balance and AWV statistical reporting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV); banking and financial market conduct supervision is concurrently exercised by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM (ancillary use only). EU/EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may passport electronic-money and payment services into Germany under the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz – ZAG) and can provide multi-currency IBANs, retail FX conversion and SEPA/SWIFT-compatible disbursement rails with lower onboarding friction. However, an EMI is legally barred from granting credit, issuing the customs guarantee (Zollbürgschaft) required for bonded warehouse duty deferral, handling documentary letters of credit, or maintaining omnibus correspondent relationships for elevated-risk free-trade-zone flows; upstream correspondent banks frequently de-risk EMIs whose client base includes bonded-depot operators. The EMI rail is therefore recommended only as a foreign-currency collection side-pocket and never as the primary treasury anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a non-resident shareholder capital injection: the remitting bank executes a SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary GmbH's dedicated capital escrow account (Einlagenkonto) at Deutsche Bank, Commerzbank or HypoVereinsbank. The MT103 must state the payment purpose 'Stammeinlage auf das Stammkapital der [GmbH] – [Notarvertragsnummer]', name the registering notary and cite the intended commercial register file. Under German GmbH law, each cash contribution must be paid free of reservation in euro, at least EUR 12,500 (half of the statutory minimum capital of EUR 25,000) must be fully funded before registration, and the amount must remain at the free disposal of the Geschäftsführer; the account bank issues a formal confirmation (Sperrvermerk) for the Handelsregister application. The Bundesbank statistical report must be filed within five working days whenever the payment exceeds EUR 12,500, using the AWV capital-participation assignment code. No exchange-control approval is required for euro-denominated injections because Germany maintains full capital-account convertibility; however, the origin of funds must be traceable to the shareholder's own bank account, and payments routed from a non-resident group treasury center must be accompanied by the relevant financing or lending documentation.Primary outbound clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for single supplier and vendor disbursements and bonded warehouse operational liabilities outside the eurozone; (ii) SWIFT MT700/MT707 documentary Letters of Credit with the bank acting as advising or confirming bank for inventory purchases in transit to the bonded depot; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer and TARGET2/RTGSplus for euro-denominated payments within the EU and Germany; and (iv) CHIPS/Fedwire (USD), CIPS (CNY) and other local RTGS systems via correspondent banks for non-euro trade settlement, always including the correct import/export purpose code where the receiving jurisdiction operates exchange control. Customs duties and import VAT on goods entering the bonded warehouse are generally deferred; where the operator uses duty deferment, the settlement rail is a periodic SEPA debit against the customs account (Zollkonto) secured by the bank's Zollbürgschaft, thereby preserving working capital and eliminating the need for pre-funding of import levies.Deliverable FX Forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) with licensed German banks for EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CNY and other freely convertible foreign-exchange pairs, documented under an ISDA or German master agreement without prior regulatory approvalFX Swaps (Devisenswaps) combining a spot purchase with a forward re-sale, utilized for short-term liquidity and trade payables/receivables management in foreign currencyNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) restricted to currencies with limited convertibility (e.g., offshore CNH/CNY where onshore deliverability is unavailable), entered with a German credit institution and subject to EMIR clearing thresholds, margin exchange and credit support annexesCurrency Options and Collars (Devisenoptionen/-collars) as permissible supplemental hedging instruments where the bank's credit line and ISDA collateral terms allow option premium financing

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM (ancillary use only). EU/EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may passport electronic-money and payment services into Germany under the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz – ZAG) and can provide multi-currency IBANs, retail FX conversion and SEPA/SWIFT-compatible disbursement rails with lower onboarding friction. However, an EMI is legally barred from granting credit, issuing the customs guarantee (Zollbürgschaft) required for bonded warehouse duty deferral, handling documentary letters of credit, or maintaining omnibus correspondent relationships for elevated-risk free-trade-zone flows; upstream correspondent banks frequently de-risk EMIs whose client base includes bonded-depot operators. The EMI rail is therefore recommended only as a foreign-currency collection side-pocket and never as the primary treasury anchor.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the corporate tax); municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 14%–17% yields a combined statutory burden of approximately 29.8%–32.4% depending on the Gemeinde (e.g., 30.175% in Berlin, 31.5% in Munich).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% on gross dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders (25% Kapitalertragsteuer imposed by the German payer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the WHT); resident GmbH shareholders benefit from §8b KStG 95% exemption; no trade tax credit relief applies to the withholding itself.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Annual return for FY 2024 due 31 July 2025; automatic extension to 31 July 2026 (i.e., 31 July of the second calendar year following year-end) when the return is prepared by a German tax advisor (Steuerberater-Verlängerung); additional extension available on application up to 31 December 2026.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Monthly advance VAT returns due by the 10th day of the following month (with Dauerfristverlängerung one additional month); annual VAT return due 31 July of the following year (same advisor extension applies).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 for annual cross-border controlled transactions in goods and EUR 500,000 for services, intangible royalties and financing transactions (§90 Abs. 3 AO, Verwaltungsgrundsätze); Master File documentation required when the worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000 (§138g AO implementing EU TPD); German tax authorities may demand the local file within 60 days and the master file within 30 days; non-production triggers an evidence-shifting burden with a surcharge of up to 10% of the adjusted profit, capped at EUR 1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies no UAE-style ESR regime; substance is enforced through three legal channels: (i) §42 AO voids abusive structures (Gestaltungsmissbrauch) where the legal form lacks economic reality; (ii) §50d Abs. 3 EStG denies treaty/PDD benefits to a foreign shareholder or royalty recipient lacking personnel, premises and active business; and (iii) the German functional transfer rules (§1 Abs. 3 AStG, Funktionsverlagerung) revalue any relocation of a profit function (e.g., shifting warehouse distribution functions abroad equivalent to the total value of functions transferred plus synergy benefits). A bonded-depot GmbH must maintain a licensed warehouse, adequate bonded-security staff, customs bond approval, and real management decisions in Germany; a cash-box intermediary without operational substance is disregarded and re-allocated to the beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years (§370 AO); in aggravated cases (large-scale evasion, use of forged documents or participation by a person abusing a position of public trust) imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years; criminal fine up to EUR 5,000,000 or, at the court's discretion, up to the evaded amount; the evaded tax remains payable with 6% annual interest; tax authorities will issue an estimated assessment under §162 AO.

Director personal liability: Managing director (Geschäftsführer) is personally liable under §§34, 69 AO for tax arrears caused by breach of filing and payment duties, with civil restitution of the full unpaid tax; criminal conviction triggers loss of the position under §6 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 GmbHG and disqualification from managing any German company for five years; preventive travel ban and passport surrender under enforcement measures (BaE, §132 AO) apply after assessment.
Customs and VAT Fraud in Bonded Operations

Statutory sanction: Customs-duty evasion is prosecuted under §370 AO with imprisonment up to 5 years; VAT fraud generates a 25% penalty on the evaded VAT in addition to the tax, plus seizure of goods under Zollfahndung order; fines may reach twice the evaded duties; the company's customs authorisation is revoked and a 3-year ban on bonded-warehouse licences is imposed; entry in the German criminal record (Bundeszentralregister) bars public contracts.

Director personal liability: Directors and customs declarants are jointly and severally liable for the customs debt under the Union Customs Code; personal assets may be confiscated as criminal proceeds (§73 StGB); imprisonment of the directing mind up to 5 years (10 years for organised rings); civil liability to the customs administration for the entire shortfall; travel ban and extradition risk in EU Member States.
Shell Company and Economic-Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty/PDD relief under §50d Abs. 3 EStG; re-characterisation of payments under §42 AO with withholding tax reclaimed from the German payer (26.375% on dividends, 15.825% on royalties) plus interest; administrative fine of 10% of the wrongly obtained refund; the foreign shareholder may also face a German penalty under §375 AO for false certificate use; tax authority sets aside the corporate form, treating the shareholder as the taxpayer, and reassesses the last 10 years (long assessment period).

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable for the withheld WHT not remitted to the tax office (§34, §69 AO) and for returned DTA-relief amounts as damages; §266 StGB (Untreue) applies when the director knowingly approves sham contracts; §43 GmbHG imposes civil liability to the company for any loss; criminal conviction triggers automatic loss of office and a writ of seizure against personal bank accounts.
AML and Money-Laundering Exposure in Bonded Depots

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the GwG of up to EUR 100,000 per violation, and for serious institutional breaches up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; confiscation of goods and tainted proceeds under the German money-laundering statute (§261 StGB); licence revocation of the customs-bonded warehouse and the trade licence (§35 GewO); criminal conviction for money laundering carries imprisonment of 3 months to 5 years, increased to 1–10 years for gang-related laundering; FIU enforcement order for a de-risking plan.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally prosecuted under §261 StGB where they knew or recklessly disregarded the criminal origin of funds; civil restitution includes confiscation of personal assets traceable to the proceeds; travel ban (Ausreiseverbot, §10 PassG) and freezing orders apply pending investigation; conviction bars the individual from serving as Geschäftsführer of any German company and from holding an FCA/customs licence; in cross-border cases, a European Arrest Warrant may be issued.

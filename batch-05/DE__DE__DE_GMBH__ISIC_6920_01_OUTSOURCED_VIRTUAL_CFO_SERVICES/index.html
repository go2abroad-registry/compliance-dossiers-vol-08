Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The nominal share capital of a German GmbH must be at least EUR 25,000. Before filing the registration application, at least one-half of the cash share capital (minimum EUR 12,500) must be paid up and left at the free disposal of the managing director; the contribution can be held in a GmbH-in-formation account at a German bank. In-kind contributions must be fully supplied and documented in a formation report. Use of the statutory notary escrow/holding account is permissible to secure capital before registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety and structural clearance: office premises must meet the requirements of the applicable Landesbauordnung (Building Code), including fire-risk classification, escape-way adequacy, and load-capacity approval for open-plan offices; certificate of no-objection from the local Bauaufsichtsbehörde is required upon substantial change of use. Occupational clearance: comply with Arbeitsstättenverordnung (workplace ordinance) and DGUV rules for safe IT workplaces; register staff with the statutory accident insurer (Berufsgenossenschaft) and obtain an employer’s occupational safety inspection certificate from the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt/Arbeitsschutzbehörde when the first employee is hired.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.). The competent local tax office (Finanzamt) administers corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT registration, tax numbers (Steuernummer), tax returns, and tax audits. Electronic filings are generally made through the ELSTER platform (Elektronische Steuererklärung), operated within the German tax administration infrastructure.

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH must register with the competent Finanzamt after incorporation and commencement of business activities by submitting the tax registration questionnaire electronically through ELSTER. The Finanzamt issues a domestic tax number (Steuernummer), generally used for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration. The GmbH may apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) for intra-European Union transactions. The application normally requires the registered legal name, registered address, legal form, commercial-register details, managing-director information, domestic tax number where available, and business activity. A non-resident entity making taxable supplies in Germany must register with the Finanzamt designated for foreign businesses or the applicable local Finanzamt, unless a relevant reverse-charge, marketplace, or special scheme applies. An Economic Operators Registration and Identification number (EORI) must be obtained from German Customs for customs declarations and export or import activity. Payment-service providers and banks may separately request the tax number, VAT identification number, commercial-register extract, beneficial-owner information, and evidence of business operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a GmbH. A GmbH is ordinarily subject to German corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax as a separate legal entity. Available elections and mechanisms include the small-business VAT regime under Section 19 of the German VAT Act (UStG), subject to statutory turnover limits; cash-basis VAT accounting under Section 20 UStG, subject to eligibility and approval; VAT consolidation through a fiscal unity (umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft); corporate income tax and trade-tax fiscal unity (ertragsteuerliche Organschaft) where statutory financial, economic, and organizational integration requirements are satisfied; and special VAT schemes for qualifying transactions. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive and may deny deductions, impose inclusion, or restrict double-tax relief in arrangements involving hybrid instruments, hybrid entities, imported mismatches, or reverse hybrids. These rules are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for ordinary VAT registration where the business performs taxable supplies in Germany or otherwise has a German VAT registration obligation; Germany has no general turnover threshold that postpones VAT registration for ordinary taxable businesses. The small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG may apply where the prior calendar year's domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and transitional rules. The reduced VAT rate is generally 7% for specified goods and services. A business using the small-business regime normally does not charge VAT and cannot deduct input VAT for covered transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax recovery under the German VAT Act if the goods physically leave the EU customs territory, the statutory export transaction conditions are satisfied, and the supplier retains prescribed documentary or electronic evidence. Evidence normally includes the customs export declaration, electronic export confirmation or alternative customs evidence, transport documentation, commercial invoice, and transaction records linking the goods to the purchaser and shipment. Intra-EU supplies of goods may be exempt where the goods are transported to another EU Member State, the customer is identified for VAT purposes in another Member State, the supplier obtains and validates the customer's VAT identification number, and the required EC Sales List and documentary evidence are maintained. Services supplied to business customers established outside Germany are generally governed by place-of-supply rules rather than a German 0% export rate. Under the general B2B rule, the place of supply is normally the customer's establishment; no German VAT is charged if the service is outside German VAT scope, subject to documentation and special rules. Services supplied to German business customers by a foreign supplier may generally fall under the German reverse-charge mechanism. Financial, real-estate, event, passenger-transport, electronically supplied, and other special services require separate place-of-supply analysis.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms, and transaction reference
  • Electronic export declaration submitted through the applicable customs system
  • Export Accompanying Document and Movement Reference Number (MRN), where issued
  • Electronic confirmation of exit from the EU customs territory or legally acceptable alternative proof of export
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, airway bill, consignment note, courier record, or carrier confirmation
  • Packing list and, where relevant, customs authorization, export license, preferential-origin documentation, or commodity-specific permits
  • Evidence linking the customs export to the VAT invoice and accounting records
  • For intra-EU supplies: customer VAT identification number validation evidence, transport evidence, and EC Sales List filing evidence
  • For offshore B2B services: customer legal identity, business status, establishment and location evidence, contract, invoice, service-delivery records, and reverse-charge or outside-scope treatment documentation
  • An inward-remittance certificate is not generally a mandatory German VAT export document. Banking records may nevertheless be retained as supplementary commercial and audit evidence where payment or customer-location substantiation is relevant.

Germany does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance or continuous transaction reporting system. VAT returns and related filings are generally submitted electronically through ELSTER. From 1 January 2025, a domestic business-to-business recipient must be able to receive structured electronic invoices. Issuance of structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions is subject to transitional rules: generally paper or other invoices may continue during 2025; issuers with prior-year turnover not exceeding EUR 800,000 may generally continue during 2026; and broader issuance obligations apply from 2027 and 2028 under the statutory transition regime. A compliant structured e-invoice generally uses an EU-standard format such as XRechnung or a compliant hybrid format such as ZUGFeRD. The rules do not generally require prior government approval, real-time API clearance, or digital tax stamping. Invoice content, authenticity, integrity, legibility, storage, and VAT recordkeeping requirements remain applicable. Reverse-charge invoices must state the applicable reverse-charge indication and generally must not show German VAT.

Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption or input-tax recovery may be denied or clawed back if export conditions, transport evidence, customer VAT identification, place-of-supply evidence, or invoicing requirements are not substantiated. The supplier may become liable for unpaid VAT, interest under the German Fiscal Code, late-payment charges, assessment adjustments, and repayment of improperly refunded input VAT. Incorrect VAT returns may trigger administrative fines; intentional or reckless conduct can constitute tax evasion or negligent tax reduction, with criminal or administrative consequences under the German Fiscal Code, including imprisonment or fines for intentional tax evasion. False, manipulated, or knowingly incomplete customs declarations may additionally result in customs duty, import VAT, penalties, seizure, forfeiture, and criminal exposure under applicable customs and fiscal rules. Tax authorities may conduct VAT audits, request transaction-level records, challenge customer establishment or transport evidence, and apply retrospective assessments for the statutory assessment period, generally four years and potentially longer in cases involving tax evasion or reckless conduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles 63-66 TFEU and the AWG. Neither domestic banks nor corporate borrowers require central-bank foreign exchange approval; EUR and all freely convertible currencies may be purchased or sold without licence. Profit repatriation and dividend payments to non-resident shareholders are permitted without restriction, subject to a German capital-yield tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%) and, where applicable, a reduced withholding rate under an applicable double tax treaty (typically 0-15%). Cross-border management fees, bookkeeping charges and virtual-CFO service invoices can be denominated in EUR, USD, GBP or any other currency; the only formalities are VAT treatment (19% standard or 7% reduced rate), transfer-pricing documentation for related-party services, and balance-of-payments reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts. Proceeds from internationally supplied ISIC 6920 fractional CFO and bookkeeping services, including settlements received through Stripe Billing and Wise, remain fully available for operational disbursements and dividend planning. The sole statutory discipline is reporting: any cross-border payment reaching €12,500 or more must be declared to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five (5) banking days from the payment date (AWV §67). Reporting is typically effected by the resident bank on the company's behalf, or by the GmbH itself through the Bundesbank General Reporting Portal (Allgemeines Meldeportal), using a balance-of-payments purpose code appropriate for accounting, auditing, tax consulting or management-consulting services.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank) acting in its capacity as foreign exchange statistician and supervisor of external-trade reporting obligations; jointly with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for bank prudential and AML/CFT supervision; overall regulatory framework rests on the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG), the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV), the Money Laundering Act (GwG), and applicable ECB/SSM regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility as a secondary multi-currency collection and disbursement overlay, fully consistent with the target payment rails of Stripe Billing and Wise. Legal constraints under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), implementing PSD2, confine e-money institutions and payment institutions to payment accounts and payment execution; they may not accept repayable deposits, extend proprietary credit, or offer deposit-protection coverage. Therefore, Wise and Stripe wallets must be structured as zero-balancing pass-through accounts with daily sweeps into a KWG-licensed primary bank account that holds statutory capital, payroll reserves and tax provisions. Cross-border transactions executed via such EMIs remain subject to Bundesbank statistical reporting at the €12,500 threshold, and the GmbH must preserve a complete payer/payee identity and FX-rate audit trail for each settlement batch.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant capital injection begins with a notarially certified share-subscription resolution increasing or subscribing the GmbH's €25,000 minimum share capital (at least €12,500 due-in-cash before Commercial Register registration). The non-resident shareholder instructs a SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer narrating 'Einlage auf das Stammkapital' and referencing the notarial deed, with funds cleared into the bank-designated capital build-up account of the GmbH in formation. The German receiving bank executes any required Bundesbank balance-of-payments report for inbound capital transfers exceeding €12,500; no AWG authorisation is required absent extraordinary foreign-trade or sanctions triggers. Proof of capital availability is supplied by the notary's accounting confirmation (Bankbescheinigung/Depotbescheinigung) using bank statements, after which the account is converted into the operational EUR account upon Commercial Register entry. For subsequent shareholder loans, a corresponding loan-purpose code and formal subordination agreement are required; for equity injections, the Commercial Register must be updated with the new share capital before any treasury deployment.Outbound disbursements rely on four clearing rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001) for EUR obligations to EU beneficiaries, including Stripe Billing payout clearing, Wise EUR settlement and subcontracted bookkeeping suppliers; (ii) SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary bank and BIC details for non-EUR vendor payments, software subscriptions and international contractors, accompanied by the statutory Bundesbank purpose code for accounting/consulting services; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) and documentary collections where bank undertakings are demanded by foreign suppliers, which is rare for ISIC 6920 services but fully executable through Tier-1 relationship banks; and (iv) EBICS-based file-driven processing for high-volume monthly disbursements. Every cross-border payment of €12,500 or more is reportable to the Bundesbank within five banking days; the bank ordinarily files under a standing mandate, but the company must maintain the underlying invoice trail, VAT evidence and, for related-party payments, transfer-pricing documentation (Verrechnungspreisdokumentation) demonstrating arm's-length rates.Plain-vanilla FX forward contracts (Devisentermingeschäfte) in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP and EUR/CHF for hedging forecast receivables and multi-currency client settlement flows, bookable under standard ISDA or bank-certainty documentation with no central-bank approval requirement.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for restricted or illiquid currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, BRL) settled in EUR against agreed reference rates, permitted where the counterparty is a German credit institution and the instrument is strictly economic-hedging oriented rather than speculative.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps (CCS) for term-matched hedging of operational liquidity pools and for converting EUR cash into USD or GBP disbursement balances, freely permitted under German foreign-trade law without BaFin licensing burdens on the corporate end-user.FX options and zero-cost collars in EUR/USD and EUR/GBP to cap conversion costs on large Wise and Stripe settlement batches, subject to board-approved treasury policy and proper valuation in the HGB annual financial statements.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility as a secondary multi-currency collection and disbursement overlay, fully consistent with the target payment rails of Stripe Billing and Wise. Legal constraints under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), implementing PSD2, confine e-money institutions and payment institutions to payment accounts and payment execution; they may not accept repayable deposits, extend proprietary credit, or offer deposit-protection coverage. Therefore, Wise and Stripe wallets must be structured as zero-balancing pass-through accounts with daily sweeps into a KWG-licensed primary bank account that holds statutory capital, payroll reserves and tax provisions. Cross-border transactions executed via such EMIs remain subject to Bundesbank statistical reporting at the €12,500 threshold, and the GmbH must preserve a complete payer/payee identity and FX-rate audit trail for each settlement batch.

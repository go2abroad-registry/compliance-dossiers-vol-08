Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register) of the Registergericht — the Registry Division of the local Amtsgericht (Local Court) at the company's registered-office district; all applications must be certified and transmitted electronically by a German notary (Notar) in accordance with § 12 HGB and § 378 FamFG.

Governing statute

GmbH-Gesetz (GmbHG — German Limited Liability Companies Act), in particular §§ 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 35, 39, 41 and 54; supplemented by §§ 8 et seq. and § 106 Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB — Commercial Code) for Commercial Register filings; § 14 Gewerbeordnung (GewO — Trade Regulation Act) for business registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation comprising the Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag and Errichtungsprotokoll), together with the formal Application for Registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister) submitted in electronic form with the required shareholder list (Gesellschafterliste) and managing director list (Geschäftsführerliste).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 5 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A minimum registered share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000 is required by § 5 (1) GmbHG. At least EUR 12,500 (50%) of the nominal capital must be fully paid in cash before registration is applied for (§ 7 (2) GmbHG). The paid-in funds must be held at the free disposal of the managing directors on a dedicated bank escrow/formation account of the GmbH, and the managing directors must submit a written statutory declaration to that effect under § 8 (3) GmbHG. Contributions in kind (Sacheinlagen) require explicit stipulation in the Articles and a notarized valuation; if agreed, full payment is evidenced by the transfer of the asset before registration. Only when the cash portion is verified may the Registergericht enter the company in the Commercial Register (HRB number).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) and local Finanzamt (tax office) – portal: Mein ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated German GmbH: (1) register with the local trade office (Gewerbeamt); (2) obtain the local tax number (Steuernummer) by submitting the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER or in paper to the competent Finanzamt; (3) apply for the VAT identification number (Ust-ID) from BZSt using Formular 'USt 1 TJ' or electronically via ELSTER; (4) for foreign-ownership structures, provide the GmbH commercial register excerpt, apostilled constitutional documents, and director/ultimate beneficial owner identification to the Finanzamt as part of the anti-money-laundering and identity verification procedure.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a newly incorporated German GmbH: (1) register with the local trade office (Gewerbeamt); (2) obtain the local tax number (Steuernummer) by submitting the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER or in paper to the competent Finanzamt; (3) apply for the VAT identification number (Ust-ID) from BZSt using Formular 'USt 1 TJ' or electronically via ELSTER; (4) for foreign-ownership structures, provide the GmbH commercial register excerpt, apostilled constitutional documents, and director/ultimate beneficial owner identification to the Finanzamt as part of the anti-money-laundering and identity verification procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election; German tax classification is statutory. Available elections: ertragsteuerliche Organschaft (fiscal unity) under Sec. 14 of the German Corporate Income Tax Act (KStG) with a profit and loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag), enabling group-level taxation for corporate income tax and trade tax. Hybrid mismatch elections are not generally available domestically; foreign hybrid instruments may trigger declarations under the German implementation of the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD). A GmbH may also elect to be treated as a small business (Kleinunternehmer) under Sec. 19 UStG for VAT, but this is usually not advisable for a SaaS exporter that needs to recover input VAT.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic threshold for resident entrepreneurs; mandatory VAT registration arises upon commencing business activity. The small-business (Kleinunternehmer) exemption cap is €22,000 in the preceding year and €50,000 in the current year under Sec. 19 UStG. A GmbH engaged in B2C SaaS normally files an option to waive this exemption and registers for VAT under the standard scheme to charge 19% VAT on domestic supplies and to recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For services (SaaS), German VAT is not due if the place of supply is outside Germany: B2B supplies are located at the recipient's establishment; B2C supplies of digital services are located at the consumer's residence. For consumers resident outside the EU/EEA, the supply falls outside the scope of German VAT; for non-taxable consumers resident in the EU (B2C), the supplier must charge VAT at the destination member state rate, normally via the OSS procedure. Zero-rating of physical goods requires a qualifying export (Ausfuhrlieferung) with proof of removal from the EU customs territory within specified evidence deadlines. For offshore services, the supplier must maintain location-of-customer evidence, such as two non-contradictory pieces of evidence (e.g., IP address, billing address, payment bank branch country, SIM card country code, or landline telephone).

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS/Zollanmeldung)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (transport evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent payment/PSP settlement evidence, e.g., Paddle or Lemon Squeezy payout statements)

Germany does not have a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. Mandatory B2B electronic invoicing is being phased in: from 1 January 2025, domestic B2B sellers must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung / ZUGFeRD / EDI); from 1 January 2027, all domestic B2B sellers must transmit e-invoices in a structured data format. B2G e-invoicing (XRechnung) has been mandatory for suppliers to federal authorities since 27 November 2020. No government API clearance or continuous transaction controls (CTC) apply.

Export zero-rating is subject to tax audits by the intermediary tax office (Finanzamt) and, for cross-border aspects, the BZSt and customs authorities. Incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating claims result in a retrospective VAT assessment (clawback) without a valid exemption certificate, plus interest on arrears under Sec. 233a AO and default interest under Sec. 233AO. Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under Sec. 370 AO may trigger criminal penalties up to five years' imprisonment, monetary fines, and disallowance of input VAT deductions. Simple negligence (leichtfertige Steuerverkürzung) under Sec. 378 AO leads to monetary penalties. In a group or foreign-owner context, participation penalties may be imposed on managing directors and tax advisors.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible. Germany maintains no exchange controls, and both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully liberalised under Articles 63–65 TFEU and the AWG. Profit remittances and dividend distributions are free from FX licensing requirements; the only practical friction consists of (i) the AWV statistical-reporting obligation for cross-border payments with non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 and (ii) domestic tax withholding on distributions (a tax issue, not an FX restriction). For a Consumer Micro SaaS entity under ISIC 6201/NAICS 511210, subscription receipts collected via Paddle and Lemon Squeezy as Merchant-of-Record are classified as software-licence/service export proceeds and are freely receivable in EUR or any convertible foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%; surrender deadline: not applicable. However, each inbound payment received from a non-resident (including SaaS subscription revenue, refunds, and shareholder capital injections) exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics no later than five working days after the end of the reporting month (Section 67 AWV). The reporting obligation may be discharged by the clearing bank or, where applicable, the licensed EMI; the report must state the amount, the non-resident counterparty, and the statutory purpose code.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank) is the competent monetary and balance-of-payments statistics authority; prudential supervision of banks and payment institutions is exercised by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin). The operative cross-border payments framework is the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 (non-EEA) or SEPA Credit Transfer (EEA) from the shareholder's bank to the GmbH's designated capital escrow account (Sperrkonto). The remittance information must state the ISO 20022 Purpose Code 'ECAP' (Equity Capital Increase) and the German text 'Stammeinlage gemäß Gesellschaftsvertrag / Gesellschafterliste'. For any inbound non-resident payment exceeding EUR 12,500, the receiving German bank or the GmbH must file the Bundesbank balance-of-payments statistical classification for direct investment equity in a resident company (Direktinvestition – Einlage/Stammeinlage in inländische Gesellschaft). Upon registration of the capital subscription and receipt of the notarial/accountant declaration of full contribution, the Sperrkonto blocking notice is lifted and the funds are swept to the operational account. Shareholder loans, alternatively, must be remitted with Purpose Code 'LOAN' and the Bundesbank capital-flow classification for non-resident shareholder lending.For a micro-SaaS, outbound flows consist primarily of hosting fees, external contractor invoices, refunds, VAT/remittance payments, dividend distributions, and FX-hedging settlements. EEA disbursements are cleared via SEPA Credit Transfers (EUR); non-EEA disbursements are executed via SWIFT MT103 in the settlement currency (USD, GBP, or EUR). A documentary Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) is normally not required for intangible service payments, but group procurement of physical computing infrastructure may be supported by a standby LC in exceptional cases.

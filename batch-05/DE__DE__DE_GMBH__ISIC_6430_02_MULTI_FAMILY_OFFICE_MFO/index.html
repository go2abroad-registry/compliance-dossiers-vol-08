Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the competent local district court (Amtsgericht) for the GmbH’s registered office; filings are transmitted electronically by a German notary.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), principally sections 2 (notarized articles), 5 (share capital), 7–8 (registration and required particulars), 10 (registration entry), and 11 (pre-registration liability). The Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch, HGB), including section 8, governs the Commercial Register.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized electronic application for registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zum Handelsregister), accompanied by the notarized articles of association, managing-director appointment, and required statutory declarations.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 39 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum stated share capital is EUR 25,000. For a cash formation, before registration at least one quarter of each subscribed share’s nominal amount and at least EUR 12,500 in aggregate must be paid to the GmbH. The GmbHG does not require an escrow account; a company bank account is commonly used to receive contributions. The actual filing and notarial charges are set and invoiced in euros and vary with the transaction; no fixed USD state fee applies.

Funding & unlock path: No general industrial fire-safety or structural-load certificate is specific to a multi-family office. The premises must comply with applicable building permits, Land building regulations, fire-safety conditions, and workplace rules. The employer must conduct a workplace risk assessment and meet occupational health and safety duties; additional fire, occupancy, or structural approvals may be required for the premises or a change of use.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro (EUR) is a fully convertible currency under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Germany maintains no capital account restrictions for bona fide foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, or profit remittance. Current account transactions, including dividends, interest, royalties, and service fees, are freely transferable. Profit remittance from a GmbH to non-resident shareholders requires no prior Bundesbank or BaFin approval, provided the entity is not subject to sanctions and the distribution complies with German commercial law (AktG/GmbHG) regarding capital maintenance. However, banks may require documentation to satisfy AML/KYC and tax transparency obligations, particularly for high-risk special license entities such as multi-family offices.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirement exists. German law does not compel exporters to repatriate or convert foreign currency proceeds. Under AWV Section 67 et seq., residents must report cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics, but this is a reporting obligation only, not a surrender or conversion mandate. Multi-family office entities holding client mandates must separately comply with BaFin reporting and MiFID II transaction reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (acting in coordination with the European Central Bank and the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority, BaFin). Foreign exchange reporting and statistical compliance are administered by the Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions licensed in Germany or the EEA may provide operational accounts and cross-border payment services under the E-Money Act (ZAG) and PSD2. However, for a multi-family office with a high-risk special license (ISIC 6430), most EMIs impose strict business-model restrictions and will not onboard discretionary portfolio advisory entities. Offshore digital EMIs and non-EEA fintech banks face significant legal constraints: they cannot hold client funds in Germany without a German/EEA license, and German banks generally refuse to maintain correspondent relationships with unregulated or weakly regulated offshore EMIs for capital escrow or operational accounts. Viability rating: LOW for offshore EMI; MEDIUM for EEA-licensed EMI limited to payment processing, not custody or capital escrow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into a German GmbH for multi-family office operations must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 through a German Tier 1 bank. Required purpose codes: 'INVC' for capital increase, 'INTC' for intra-group funding, or 'CORT' for custody-related transfers. Verification steps include: (i) notarized capital increase resolution filed with the Handelsregister; (ii) Bundesbank AWV reporting if payment exceeds EUR 12,500; (iii) AML/KYC check on the originating bank and UBO; (iv) confirmation that funds are not from sanctioned persons or jurisdictions; (v) proper booking to the capital escrow account (Kapitalkonto) or operational account. For non-resident shareholders, a German tax adviser should confirm no withholding tax applies on capital contributions. All wires must include the entity's LEI and the correct IBAN. Purpose code 'INVC' must be used for equity injections; 'INTC' for shareholder loans.Outbound payments for trade settlements, client fee disbursements, and custody-related wires are cleared via SWIFT MT103 for clean payments and MT202 for bank-to-bank transfers. Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) are available for trade finance but are less common for multi-family office operational flows. For client distributions, the bank may require a payment instruction with purpose code 'FDFS' (financial derivatives) or 'CUST' (custody). All outbound payments above EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank. For high-value custody wires, banks may require dual authorization and compliance pre-clearance. SWIFT gpi is supported by Tier 1 German banks for tracking and confirmation. Alternative rails: SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments within the EEA (no SWIFT fees, same-day settlement); TARGET2 for large-value Euro payments. Offshore digital EMIs and non-EEA fintech banks face significant legal constraints: they cannot hold client funds in Germany without a German/EEA license, and German banks generally refuse to maintain correspondent relationships with unregulated or weakly regulated offshore EMIs for capital escrow or operational accounts. Deadline: · Frequency:

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Taxable income (steuerpflichtiges Einkommen) Report worldwide income if tax resident; for non-resident, only German-source income. Late filing penalty of 0.25% of assessed tax per month (min €25/month), late payment interest of 0.5% per month, and fines up to €25,000 for non-compliance.
Box 2 Business expenses (Betriebsausgaben) Deduct only expenses directly related to German-source income. Late filing penalty of 0.25% of assessed tax per month (min €25/month), late payment interest of 0.5% per month, and fines up to €25,000 for non-compliance.
Box 3 Loss carryforward (Verlustvortrag) Apply losses from prior years subject to minimum taxation rules. Late filing penalty of 0.25% of assessed tax per month (min €25/month), late payment interest of 0.5% per month, and fines up to €25,000 for non-compliance.
Box 4 Withholding tax credits (Anrechnung von Steuern) Claim foreign tax credits or treaty relief as applicable. Late filing penalty of 0.25% of assessed tax per month (min €25/month), late payment interest of 0.5% per month, and fines up to €25,000 for non-compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if cross-border related-party transactions exceed €500,000 for services or €5 million for goods; Master File required if group turnover > €100 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no specific ESR but applies general anti-abuse rules (Section 42 AO) and substance-over-form principles; MFO must demonstrate genuine economic activity, staff, and decision-making in Germany.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 10% of evaded tax; imprisonment up to 5 years; additional interest and surcharges

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for unpaid taxes if they knowingly cause evasion; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of entity, denial of treaty benefits, penalties up to €25,000, and criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for restitution and face imprisonment up to 3 years for fraudulent schemes.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million or 10% of annual turnover; revocation of license; criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for fines and damages; potential travel bans and imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering offenses.

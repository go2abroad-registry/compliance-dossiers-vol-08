Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; electronic portal: Taxisnet (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy/); allied registry: Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (https://www.companies.gov.cy/).

E-government filing portal

1) A foreign trading entity must either incorporate a Cypriot private limited company (HE registration) or register a branch/permanent establishment with the Registrar of Companies. 2) Cyprus-incorporated companies automatically obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) equal to their HE registration number; a foreign branch/PE applies separately to the District Tax Office for a Cypriot TIN. 3) For VAT, submit Form VAT 101 to the VAT District Office within 30 days of exceeding the EUR 15,600 taxable turnover threshold, or immediately when the supplier is non-established; non-EU businesses may be required to appoint a fiscal representative. 4) The Tax Department issues a VAT registration number in the format CY + 8 digits + 1 check letter (e.g., CY10002316H). 5) Concurrently, obtain an EORI number (EL prefix) from the Customs Department to execute electronic export declarations for marine fuel consignments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Available election forms

Cyprus has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (i) group loss relief election among companies with at least 75% common ownership (Income Tax Law Art. 19); (ii) tonnage tax opt-in for qualifying international shipping; (iii) IP box notional deduction (80% of qualifying intangible income); (iv) deemed-dividend distribution credit under the Special Defence Contribution Law; (v) mandatory ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules (Law 33(I)/2019) with no opt-out; (vi) non-domicile election affecting dividend and interest taxation for individuals.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for taxable persons established in Cyprus (same threshold for goods and services); intra-EU distance-sales/OSS cross-border B2C threshold: EUR 10,000 (post-1 July 2021); no threshold for non-established foreign suppliers — immediate VAT registration required; bunkering supplies to private pleasure craft remain taxable at the standard rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (1) exports of goods outside the EU, including wholesale supply of marine fuel oil and distillates, provided the goods physically leave the customs territory of the EU within the prescribed period and the supplier retains full proof of export (VAT Law Art. 5(1)(a)); (2) supplies of bunker fuel to vessels engaged in international voyages (Art. 5(1)(c)); (3) intermediary/brokerage services that directly facilitate the export of goods or international navigation (Art. 5(1)(j)); (4) B2B services to non-established customers where the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction, triggering reverse charge (no Cypriot VAT). The broker must hold a valid wholesale fuel-trading licence and document the genuine cross-border or international-shipping character of each transaction; input VAT on related acquisition costs is recoverable through the periodic VAT return or the EU 13th Directive refund procedure for foreign businesses.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) filed via the Cyprus Customs electronic export control system (ECS/NCTS) with Movement Reference Number
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Sea Waybill evidencing carriage from a Cyprus/EEA port to a third-country destination
  • Bunker Delivery Note (BDN) and independent quantity surveyor report (e.g., SGS/Caleb Brett) for marine fuel deliveries

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital golden-record mandate is currently in force in Cyprus. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only to B2G supplies through the PEPPOL network (EU Directive 2014/55/EU), implemented in December 2021. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary; VAT returns and VIES statements are filed via Taxisnet (monthly for larger taxpayers, quarterly for others). Cyprus is aligning with the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) proposals; mandatory structured e-invoicing and real-time transaction clearance are expected no earlier than 2028–2030.

Cyprus imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline (hence 0 days); however, unrepatriated or deferred export consideration may be recharacterized as deemed dividends or adjusted under arm's-length transfer-pricing rules upon audit. VAT clawback: assessments may be raised within 6 years of the return date, with no time limit in cases of fraud, wilful default, or missing returns; fraudulent zero-rating or export-related carousel schemes attract a civil penalty of up to 100% of the VAT evaded, a late-payment surcharge of 5% (within 2 months) and 10% (thereafter), plus interest at 9% per annum. Criminal prosecution for knowingly false export declarations can result in fines up to double the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years on indictment, with parallel customs-fraud penalties under the Union Customs Code. Disallowed zero-rating also triggers retrospective VAT, interest, and denial of refunds in subsequent periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus has complete capital- and current-account convertibility. Since euro adoption on 1 January 2008 and prior EU accession on 1 May 2004, all residual exchange-control restrictions under the former Exchange Control Law (Cap. 153) were repealed; no Central Bank permission, conversion quota, repatriation permit, or FX rationing applies to any transaction of a Cyprus-incorporated crude oil and refined products wholesale trader (ISIC 4661). Both trade-related current payments and full capital movements—including equity injections, intercompany loans, dividend distributions, and liquidation proceeds—are freely transferable in EUR or any convertible currency. Profit remittance is subject only to corporate solvency tests under Companies Law Cap. 113, s.169 and standard tax formalities; no hard currency balance limitation exists. EUR/USD and all foreign-currency conversions take place at freely negotiated interbank market rates via commercial banks or licensed market-makers. The sole residual administrative mechanism is statistical: the reporting of cross-border flow data to the CBC under the Balance of Payments (BoP) legal framework, Regulation (EU) 184/2005, and ESA 2010 methodology.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline, percentage quota, or mandated conversion ratio for export receipts. The requirement to surrender foreign currency export proceeds to authorised banks was eliminated upon EU accession and full capital-market liberalisation; exporters of crude oil, distillates, and marine fuels may retain 100% of export proceeds in any denomination or convert to EUR at freely determined commercial rates. The only enduring obligation is statistical: the servicing Cyprus bank must report every external transaction exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Central Bank of Cyprus within five (5) business days of settlement via the CBC 'Payment Declaration for External Transactions' form or the electronic e-BoP portal, classifying the transaction according to IMF Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) standards. Failure to submit statistical declarations can trigger administrative fines under the CBC Reporting Law, but no confiscatory surrender, forced conversion, or exchange penalty exists.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου, CBC), operating as a full Eurosystem member under the European System of Central Banks (ESCB), with supervisory responsibilities for credit institutions flowing through the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM) jointly with the European Central Bank (ECB). For foreign-exchange and capital-movement matters, the CBC is the competent national authority under Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, ECB Regulation 974/98 on the adoption of the euro, and the Central Bank of Cyprus Laws of 2002 to 2023.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for principal trade clearing and documentary credit issuance; MEDIUM only for payroll, EUR operating expense float, and low-value ancillary payments. Cyprus recognises Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions licensed by the Central Bank of Cyprus under the Electronic Money Law L.31(I)/2012 and the Payment Services Law L.31(I)/2018, transposing EU Directive 2009/110/EC and PSD2. However, a physical wholesale energy trader (ISIC 4661, coded high-risk special-license) cannot rely on offshore digital EMIs as the primary clearing rail because: (i) EMIs are prohibited from granting credit under EMD2 Article 6, and thus cannot issue standby letters of credit or documentary letters of credit; (ii) correspondent banks routinely de-risk 'oil and gas' merchant category codes, causing EMI payout failures on USD/EUR wires above EUR 250,000; (iii) CBC AML directives require a full-risk physical-bank relationship for high-risk commodities; and (iv) IOC/FATF peer-review expectations mandate audit trails inherent in a commercial bank. A CySEC-licensed or CBC-licensed EMI can supplement, but never replace, a tier-1 commercial bank relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from a non-EU parent is routed via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA/SEPA-Instant for EUR) using the BoP direct-investment purpose code 'E015 — Direct Investment: Equity Capital' in the payment narrative, which mirrors BPM6 category FDI. The receiving Cyprus bank must complete the CBC 'Payment Declaration for External Transactions' form, capturing the remitter country, unique transaction reference, underlying contract, and reporting classification code. To pass AML underwriting, the company must deliver: (a) board resolution approving the share subscription, (b) certified SHAREHOLDER and ALTERATION forms filed with the Cyprus Department of Registrar of Companies, (c) a Certificate of Increase in Authorised Share Capital where relevant, (d) updated ownership chart, and (e) the Cyprus VAT/TIN file. Any share premium is subject to Cyprus capital duty at 0.6% under the Companies (Amendment) Law 2016; the duty must be settled at the receiving bank simultaneously with the injection, and the payment should be labelled 'Share Capital Subscription — direct investment in ISIC 4661 wholesale fuels trading entity HE77'. Where the injection is structured as shareholder debt, use code E017 (Direct Investment: Loans between Direct Investors and Enterprises) and file an intercompany loan agreement stamped by the bank's legal team.Settlement of physical crude oil and refined products trades clears over a combination of SWIFT MT103/MT202 for frank disbursements, and documentary instruments for title-backed payments. Standard rails: (1) confirmed irrevocable Documentary Letter of Credit issued under ICC UCP600 and ISBP745 by the Cyprus issuing bank in favour of a refinery or commodity trading house; (2) Standby Letter of Credit issued under ISP98 or URDG758 as performance or guarantee security in the sale-purchase contract; (3) Documentary Collections (CAD/DP/DA) for time-lag settlements against presentation of the bill of lading and shipping documents; and (4) SWIFT MT103 direct wires for settled invoices, with field 70 clearly stating the trade reference, ISIC 4661 wholesale fuels, and contract number. All outbound wires above EUR 12,500 are reported daily by the bank to the CBC for BoP statistics. USD payments must originate from the company's USD treasury account where available; a EUR→USD conversion is executed at agreed market rate. No third-party payee can receive LC reimbursement funds unless pre-approved by the bank's Trade Finance Operations unit. The originator must also ensure the SWIFT field 59 name and address match the registered corporate name exactly to avoid OFAC/reference-data rejection.Outright OTC FX Forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF, EUR/RUB and dual-currency oil-indexed pairs) transacted under a documented ISDA Master Agreement or long-form FX Confirmation between the Cyprus company and a licensed credit institution; permitted for hedging underlying crude oil and refined products receivables/payables.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (EUR/USD) permissible for aligning the currency and interest-rate basis of inbound capital and outbound trade financing, subject to EMIR (EU Regulation 648/2012) reporting and non-financial counterparty clearing thresholds.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for restricted or non-convertible currencies of supplier or offtaker jurisdictions (e.g., NGN, KZT, EGP, VES) where an onshore deliverable market is unavailable; permitted only when the company can evidence a genuine underlying trade exposure, not for speculative positions.Plain Vanilla FX Options (calls and puts) on EUR/USD and commodity-linked emerging-market currencies for hedging crude oil sale proceeds; permitted with a written underlying-exposure confirmation signed by the bank's Corporate Treasury desk, premiums booked as trade expense.Physical-Delivery FX Swaps (short-term, up to six months) used to convert USD trade receipts into EUR treasury balances for operational funding and monthly corporate tax (IPD) payments, executed with the same credit institution that maintains the company's operational account.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for principal trade clearing and documentary credit issuance; MEDIUM only for payroll, EUR operating expense float, and low-value ancillary payments. Cyprus recognises Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions licensed by the Central Bank of Cyprus under the Electronic Money Law L.31(I)/2012 and the Payment Services Law L.31(I)/2018, transposing EU Directive 2009/110/EC and PSD2. However, a physical wholesale energy trader (ISIC 4661, coded high-risk special-license) cannot rely on offshore digital EMIs as the primary clearing rail because: (i) EMIs are prohibited from granting credit under EMD2 Article 6, and thus cannot issue standby letters of credit or documentary letters of credit; (ii) correspondent banks routinely de-risk 'oil and gas' merchant category codes, causing EMI payout failures on USD/EUR wires above EUR 250,000; (iii) CBC AML directives require a full-risk physical-bank relationship for high-risk commodities; and (iv) IOC/FATF peer-review expectations mandate audit trails inherent in a commercial bank. A CySEC-licensed or CBC-licensed EMI can supplement, but never replace, a tier-1 commercial bank relationship.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cyprus TP Rules effective from tax year 2022: Local File required where aggregate related-party transactions exceed EUR 750,000 in the tax year; Master File required where the ultimate parent group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million; Country-by-Country Report required at the same EUR 750 million group revenue threshold. Documentation must be delivered to the Cyprus Tax Department within 60 days of a written request.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies the 'central management and control' test for tax residence (Section 2, Income Tax Law). A Cyprus-incorporated entity whose strategic decisions are made abroad may be treated as non-resident or challenged under DTA 'place of effective management' clauses. The EU ATAD general anti-abuse rule (GAAR), CFC rules, and the proposed EU Unshell/ATAD 3 Directive impose minimum substance requirements: an energy commodity trading company must maintain real local direction, qualified staff, offices, bank accounts, and risk management functions in Cyprus to avoid recharacterisation as a shell entity and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: On criminal conviction under Section 98 of the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law N.4/1978: a fine of up to EUR 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; civil liability for the full tax evaded plus the 5% surcharge and statutory interest at 1.75% p.a.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and shadow directors who authorize or participate in the evasion are personally criminally liable; the court may pierce the corporate veil under the Companies Law Cap.113 and impose personal civil restitution for the unpaid tax; travel bans and asset freezing orders may be issued during investigation.
Transfer Pricing / Related-Party Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Unilateral TP adjustment by the Cyprus Tax Commissioner increasing chargeable income, taxed at 12.5% plus statutory interest at 1.75% p.a.; administrative fines up to EUR 5,000 for documentation default; CbC reporting failure attracts additional daily penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP disclosures or direct the mispricing are personally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties; the Commissioner may pursue collection against the personal assets of directors who acted in wilful default.
Shell Company / Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Reclassification as non-resident with loss of DTA treaty access; strike-off from the Cyprus Registrar; administrative fines and daily default penalties for UBO register non-compliance; revocation of any CySEC or energy broker trading licence.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified for up to 3 years, personally fined, and subjected to travel bans; reputational and regulatory exposure under EU anti-shell initiatives including the proposed ATAD 3 Directive.
AML / Economic Sanctions in Energy Commodity Trading

Statutory sanction: On conviction under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law L.188(I)/2007: imprisonment up to 14 years and/or a fine of up to EUR 500,000; regulatory fines up to EUR 1,000,000; compulsory license revocation and asset freezing under UN/EU sanctions enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally indictable for money laundering and sanctions offences; exposure includes imprisonment up to 14 years, criminal confiscation orders, disqualification from managing any EU company, and travel restrictions pending trial.
Failure to File / Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Late filing fine of EUR 100 per return plus EUR 100 per subsequent 3-month period (maximum EUR 400 per return); 5% surcharge on unpaid tax; statutory interest at 1.75% p.a.; loss of the right to carry forward losses in certain circumstances; the Tax Commissioner may issue an estimated assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause or connive in the non-filing are personally assessed for the penalties; the Tax Department may obtain court orders restricting departure from Cyprus where tax is outstanding and may pursue personal recovery from directors of dissolved companies.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital (Cap. 113, s. 3(2)); one subscriber share suffices. No escrow account is required, but the declared issued capital must be transferred to the company's bank account and traceable to source of wealth under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Law 188(I)/2007.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate under the Fire Prevention and Fire-fighting legislation administered by the Fire Services Command of the Cyprus Police, with civil-defence inspection of escape routes and emergency lighting; Occupational Health and Safety compliance certificate under Law 89(I)/1996 (EU OSH Framework Directive 89/391/EEC) covering workstation ergonomics, electrical installation inspection and first-aid provisions; structural and building load approval under the Town and Country Planning Law 90(I)/1972 for the occupied premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital (Cap. 113, s. 3(2)); one subscriber share suffices. No escrow account is required, but the declared issued capital must be transferred to the company's bank account and traceable to source of wealth under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Law 188(I)/2007.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας); online portal: Ariadne (https://www.mof.gov.cy/taxdepartment/)

E-government filing portal

Cyprus-incorporated limited companies are automatically registered for corporate income tax at incorporation and receive a Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the Registrar of Companies. VAT registration is separately required when taxable supplies exceed €15,600 annually or when intra-Union distance sales exceed €10,000. Foreign entities operating through a permanent establishment must apply to the Tax Department using the relevant registration form and appoint a Cyprus-resident tax representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Cyprus-incorporated limited companies are automatically registered for corporate income tax at incorporation and receive a Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the Registrar of Companies. VAT registration is separately required when taxable supplies exceed €15,600 annually or when intra-Union distance sales exceed €10,000. Foreign entities operating through a permanent establishment must apply to the Tax Department using the relevant registration form and appoint a Cyprus-resident tax representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections include group VAT registration, group relief for transfer of losses, notional interest deduction on new equity, and the intellectual property box (subject to transitional phase-out). Hybrid mismatch rules implementing ATAD are mandatory and cannot be disapplied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 annual taxable turnover for domestic supplies of goods and services; €10,000 for intra-EU distance sales and intra-Community acquisitions

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is claimable for: (i) exports of goods dispatched to destinations outside the EU, with evidence of physical export; (ii) intra-Community supplies made to a VAT-identified customer in another EU member state; (iii) B2B services supplied to a customer established outside the EU, subject to place-of-supply rules. The supplier must retain valid export declarations, transport documents, customer VAT ID (for EU), and contractual/payment documentation to substantiate the claim.

  • Customs export declaration (Electronic Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward remittance certificate or proof of foreign currency payment
  • Export contract or purchase order

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory B2B e-invoicing is currently in force. VAT returns and Intrastat declarations must be filed electronically via the Ariadne portal. Suppliers to Cyprus public authorities must comply with EU e-invoicing requirements (PEPPOL BIS format) under Directive 2014/55/EU. SAF-T is planned but not yet mandatory.

The Cyprus Tax Department may retrospectively claw back zero-rated output VAT where evidence is inadequate or documentation is false. Under the VAT Law, deficiencies attract assessment of tax, statutory interest (1.5% per month) and penalties of up to 100% of the tax due. Fraudulent export schemes are subject to criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment. Mis-declarations to customs may also incur penalties under the Union Customs Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full member of the euro area. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully liberalised under the EU Treaty (Articles 63-66 TFEU) and the Central Bank of Cyprus Law, as amended. No exchange permits, convertibility restrictions, or foreign-currency surrender rules apply to a Cyprus private limited company. Profit remittance to non-residents is not subject to exchange control, prior Central Bank approval, or Cypriot withholding tax on dividends/interest/royalty remittances; the only practical hurdles are bank-level AML/KYC verification, accurate corporate documentation, tax-residence certificates when requested, and periodic ECB/CBC balance-of-payments statistical reporting for direct investment/equity and intercompany financing flows. A B2B cross-border marketplace may therefore open and maintain USD, GBP and other freely convertible currency accounts in Cyprus and transfer funds cross-border at commercial discretion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No Cypriot or EU statutory requirement exists for the surrender, repatriation, or conversion of export proceeds into euro. There is no mandatory timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, no requirement to hold export proceeds in a domestic account, and no Central Bank concentration obligation. Export receipts may be retained in the foreign currency of the transaction or converted into EUR based on ordinary commercial treasury policy; foreign-currency balances are subject only to normal bank-account terms, AML transaction monitoring, and BOP statistical reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza Kyprou), operating within the Eurosystem alongside the European Central Bank. Cyprus has no separate foreign exchange administration or exchange-control agency; the Central Bank of Cyprus performs monetary authority, prudential bank supervision, and statistical balance-of-payments reporting functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium/High as a supplementary settlement or collection rail; Low as the sole or primary treasury bank. Legal constraints: only an EU/EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institution that has valid passport/registration in Cyprus (or a Cyprus-authorised EMI) may lawfully open an e-money account or provide payment services to a Cyprus company. Offshore or third-country EMIs without an EU passport are not compliant as a treasury settlement counterparty for statutory escrow, capital injection, or main operating balances. Any fintech EMI must be integrated through segregated accounts at a licensed Cypriot or EU credit institution, provide audit rights, support SWIFT/SEPA rails, and demonstrate sanctions-screening and UBO/KYC equivalence acceptable to the Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use direct SWIFT MT103/MT202 transfers from the exact registered shareholder or financing company; never route capital through third-party payment aggregators, digital EMIs, or personal accounts. Remittance information should quote 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'INTERCOMPANY LOAN DISBURSEMENT', the CY Ltd name, and the relevant board-resolution number. There is no Cyprus exchange-control permission, but the bank will require: (1) executed board resolution and underlying share subscription/loan agreement; (2) match between ordering customer and the named investor/lender, or an 'on behalf of' letter from the group treasury; (3) updated UBO declaration and beneficial-ownership register where available; (4) evidence of source of funds/wealth; and (5) classification of the flow as direct investment equity/intercompany debt for CBC/ECB balance-of-payments statistical reporting if the transaction is selected for ITRS reporting. The funds may be credited in EUR or a foreign currency; if foreign currency, the operating treasury may convert using authorised institutions once credited.For euro-area trade payments, use SEPA Credit Transfer in ISO 20022 pacs.008 format. For high-value commercial purchases requiring secure payment against documents, use a commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Cyprus bank via SWIFT MT700 (amendments via MT707) or documentary collections via MT400/MT410. Marketplace payouts to B2B sellers should be made from a segregated settlement account using exactly matched beneficiary merchant KYC files; batch payout files must be screened against sanctions lists before execution. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (Cyprus Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002, Section 25; flat rate for resident companies on worldwide income subject to statutory exemptions)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Cyprus does not impose withholding tax on dividends paid by a Cyprus-resident company to non-resident shareholders. The former Special Defence Contribution on dividends (17% for resident receivers) is fully repealed from 2021, permitting tax-free profit repatriation without source-state leakage.

Boxes 20-24: Tax computation, credits and declaration Taxable profit at 12.5%, deduction of provisional installments already paid, final balance payable or refundable, related-party transaction declaration, and statutory declaration signed by a director and certified by auditors. A non-resident parent must not submit the IR1 itself; the Cyprus board signs the declaration and the auditor certifies the accounts; a separate Certificate of Tax Residence (TRC) application is required for foreign treaty relief. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual value per category of controlled transactions (goods, services, financing, intangibles) triggers a mandatory Local File; EUR 2,000,000 aggregate applies to qualifying low-risk distribution, agency and marketing services; a Master File is additionally required for EU multinational groups above consolidated turnover thresholds. Documentation must be contemporaneous and delivered to the Cyprus Tax Department within 30 days of a request.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation filings, but substantive economic presence is enforced through the place-of-effective-management doctrine, EU ATAD anti-abuse rules, the CFC regime in Section 60 of the Income Tax Law, OECD BEPS/MLI standards and the IP-Box nexus formula. A Cyprus Ltd operating a B2B marketplace must demonstrate local strategic decision-making, qualified staff, physical premises, active contracts with vendors and real risk-bearing capacity; artificial letter-box arrangements are recharacterized, denied treaty benefits and penalized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law and Income Tax Law: confiscation orders, compound interest, fines of up to 100% of evaded tax, and referral to the European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO) for EU cross-border VAT/customs fraud.

Director personal liability: On proof of fraud the corporate veil is pierced: directors and shadow directors may be personally ordered to restore evaded tax, face asset-freezing orders and travel bans, and in aggravated cases imprisonment of up to five years.
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment with recomputation of the Cyprus taxable base; secondary adjustment recharacterizing the excess as a dividend; administrative fines for missing documentation and late disclosure; daily default interest until corrected returns are filed.

Director personal liability: Directors executing sham intercompany agreements or signing false documentation face civil liability for resulting tax underpayments; Cypriot courts may disqualify them as directors and order personal restitution where recklessness amounts to wilful default.
Shell company and abusive treaty-shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of Cyprus tax residence; recharacterization of the entity as a tax-transparent conduit; refusal of NID, IP-Box relief and foreign tax credits; application of ATAD general anti-abuse rules; administrative fines and increased exchange-of-information scrutiny.

Director personal liability: The court may treat the company as the alter ego of the directing beneficial owner, pierce the veil, and hold directors jointly liable for Cypriot and foreign taxes; arrest warrants and travel bans are obtainable where assets are moved offshore.
AML/KYC failure on cross-border payment rails

Statutory sanction: Enforcement under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (Law 188(I)/2007): fines up to EUR 500,000 per offense, imprisonment up to five years, confiscation of platform balances and laundered commissions, and adverse action on payment licenses.

Director personal liability: Money laundering offenses carry direct personal criminal liability; compliance officers and managing directors face custodial sentences for blind-eye facilitation; courts order travel bans, freeze personal assets and impose director disqualification for up to 15 years.
Persistent non-filing and corporate deregistration

Statutory sanction: Cumulative late-filing penalties and statutory interest; best-of-judgment assessments on deemed revenue; striking-off by the Registrar of Companies with forfeiture of corporate assets to the state and registration of directors for personal enforcement.

Director personal liability: After strike-off directors are personally responsible for company tax debts; disqualified for up to 15 years; subject to personal bankruptcy proceedings and travel restrictions until all filings and payments are regularized.

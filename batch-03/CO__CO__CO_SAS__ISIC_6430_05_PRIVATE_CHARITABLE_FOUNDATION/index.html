Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; electronic services via MUISCA and DIAN web services.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must appoint a legal representative and tax agent resident in Colombia; obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by submitting the pre-registration form through the DIAN MUISCA portal using the foreign entity's incorporation documents and power of attorney; the SAS charter (documento privado or public deed) must be registered in the Cámara de Comercio; for VAT and withholding purposes, the entity must activate the 'Régimen Común'/responsible status, obtain an electronic signature certificate and the electronic invoicing authorization; no minimum capital is required for SAS formation. Non-resident owners may hold 100% of shares.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box regime; the SAS is irrevocably taxed as a Colombian income taxpayer. However, the entity may elect to be treated as a 'régimen tributario especial' (special tax regime) for philanthropic/charitable endowments if it meets the legal constitution and activity criteria. There are also controlled hybrid mismatch rules under Colombian tax law (Law 2277 of 2022) limiting deduction of payments characterized inconsistently abroad, but no elective hybrid classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Economic presence threshold: periodic taxable sales, services, or self-consumption exceeding 3,500 UVT (Unidad de Valor Tributario) per calendar year obliges registration as an IVA responsible party; for 2024, 1 UVT approximates COP 47,065, making the threshold approximately COP 164,727,500. Cross-border digital services and importers of tangible goods are subject to registration irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods and services rendered to foreign residents are zero-rated for VAT under the law if the exporter is the seller and the final user is abroad; for the endowment manager, qualifying zero-rated services must relate to foreign-situated activities, such as grant administration for non-resident charities, foreign-sourced software services, or export-linked international services; the taxpayer must submit a monthly export declaration and may request VAT refund if the exporter holds periods with creditable VAT from inputs attributable to zero-rated exports.

  • Cámara de Comercio / export registration and RUES data
  • Commercial invoice or export contract
  • Bill of Lading (Marine/Air/Freight) or postal consignment note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Pago al Exterior) or bank wire confirmation evidencing collection of foreign currency

Electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta) is mandatory for all responsible VAT taxpayers; invoices must be transmitted in real-time to DIAN through authorized technology providers or direct API (web services) following the 8-digit event schema; generation, transmission, validation, and digital stamping (representación impresa de factura electrónica) are required; the system assigns a CUDE (Código Único de Documento Electrónico) and a VALIDATION_UNIQUE_ID; no physical clearance stamp is used, but DIAN performs automated cross-validation with customs and bank exchange filings for export claims.

If legalization and repatriation of export proceeds do not occur within 180 calendar days (or extended under exchange regime rules), DIAN and the Central Bank may impose fines up to 2,000 UVT and demand the VAT zero-rate be clawed back retroactively, plus default interest and a penalty of up to 200% of the omitted tax for fraudulent claims; false export zero-rating documentation constitutes customs fraud with criminal sanctions, including imprisonment, asset freezing, and disqualification from tax benefits; charitable endowment grants must not be disguised as consideration to qualify as zero-rated services.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating peso (COP) with broadly liberalized current-account convertibility and regulated capital-account convertibility. Foreign donations, endowment contributions and registered foreign investments may enter freely; however, any FX operation exceeding USD 10,000 (or COP equivalent) must be channelled through an authorized intermediary and reported on a sworn exchange declaration (Declaración de Cambio). Capital contributions to the charitable S.A.S. endowment must be registered with Banco de la República within 12 months of disbursement to enable lawful repatriation of invested capital, dividends and final liquidation proceeds. Profit and dividend remittances are unrestricted once the relevant Colombian income tax and dividend withholding taxes have been paid and the investor has filed the corresponding exchange declaration; there is no arbitrary conversion quota or profit-remittance ceiling. Current payments for grants, scientific services and ordinary expenses are generally free of prior licensing but remain subject to the channelling and declaration thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The foundation does not engage in export trade and has no export surrender obligation. For entities that do export, the statutory rule in Colombia is the channelling—not forced conversion—of export proceeds: the full amount must be received through the Banco de la República's foreign exchange market within the regulatory term (generally 6 months from shipment, extendable to 12 months for deferred-payment exports approved by Banco de la República), and a sworn exchange declaration must be submitted at the receiving authorized intermediary. There is no statutory percentage surrender to the central bank. Any incidental FX earnings of the foundation (foreign dividends, interest, sale of investments) are current-account receipts and are freely repatriable provided they are channelled and declared similarly.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), administering the Colombian Foreign Exchange Regime (Estatuto Cambiario) jointly with the Minister of Finance (Ministerio de Hacienda y Crédito Público), under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1068 of 2015 and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83. The Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia supervises the authorized intermediaries through which all regulated FX operations must be channelled.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Low-Medium feasibility. Colombian banking and e-money law reserves deposit-taking and payment services to licensed credit institutions and SEDPEs supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera; there is no passporting or licensing regime for offshore digital EMIs to act as primary local clearing rails. Platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, Mercury or Airwallex can be used only as auxiliary tools for low-value cross-border incidental flows, because every regulated FX flow above the USD 10,000 threshold must be channelled through an authorized intermediary with a sworn exchange declaration. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign donors must remit the endowment corpus via SWIFT MT103 (single credit transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank cover) in COP, USD or EUR to the foundation's current account at a Colombian authorized intermediary. For any incoming amount exceeding USD 10,000 equivalent, the receiving bank will require a sworn Declaración de Cambio categorizing the flow (e.g., international investment contribution, donation, or endowment capitalization), the underlying legal instrument (donation/endowment agreement or shareholders' contribution resolution), and the identification of the ultimate donor. The bank will execute the FX conversion to COP at the market rate or credit the foreign-currency account, and the investor/endowment must complete the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales at the Banco de la República within 12 months of the disbursement; the proof of this registration is the gateway for all future capital and yield repatriation.Grant, scholarship and scientific-sponsorship outflows will ordinarily clear through SWIFT MT103 (cross-border credit transfer) to the beneficiary's verified bank account, using the appropriate reference to the grant agreement; when a local beneficiary holds only a Colombian account, an internal ACH (CENIC) transfer or a domestic clearing instruction will be used instead. For any acquisition of scientific equipment or services, the foundation may open an irrevocable commercial letter of credit at its issuing bank (SWIFT MT700) with settlement via MT103/202 upon compliant documents. Every outward transfer above USD 10,000 must be matched against a sworn exchange declaration identifying the current-account or capital-account nature (e.g., scientific services, royalties, grant disbursement), and evidenced in the foundation's operating and grant-making records; the intermediary will debit the foreign-currency account or sell COP at the market rate accordingly.COP/USD deliverable forwards — permitted for the hedging of endowment receivables, grant budget lines and repatriation flows through authorized intermediariesCOP/USD non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) — permitted where physical delivery is impractical, settling the difference in COP through the authorized marketVanilla FX options on COP/USD and COP/EUR — permitted for hedging-only mandates arising from the foundation's reinvestment and multi-currency grant programmingCross-currency swaps (FX swaps and long-dated CCS) — permitted to match the currency profile of long-term endowment assets with the currency of scientific grant disbursementsFutures on COP/USD traded on recognized exchanges — permitted only through financial intermediaries licensed by the Superintendencia Financiera and with settlement through the Banco de la República clearing system

Colombian banking and e-money law reserves deposit-taking and payment services to licensed credit institutions and SEDPEs supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera; there is no passporting or licensing regime for offshore digital EMIs to act as primary local clearing rails. Platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, Mercury or Airwallex can be used only as auxiliary tools for low-value cross-border incidental flows, because every regulated FX flow above the USD 10,000 threshold must be channelled through an authorized intermediary with a sworn exchange declaration. The foundation's treasury design should therefore route the endowment corpus exclusively through local Tier-1 bank accounts, while a single well-documented virtual-account aggregator may be retained at the margin for international micro-grants, with monthly reconciliation and full audit trail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% general statutory CIT rate for taxable years 2023-2025 (Law 2277 of 2022); a significantly lower effective rate of 20% applies to taxable surplus if the entity is qualified under the Special Tax Regime for non-profit entities (Régimen Tributario Especial, ET Arts. 356-1 et seq.).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders out of profits already taxed at the corporate level (Law 2277 of 2022 raised the rate from 5% to 10% for payments made from FY2023). Dividends paid out of net profits not previously subject to corporate tax (exempt income or non-taxable income) are subject to a 35% non-resident withholding (equivalent to the shareholder-level tax).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File when prior-year gross equity exceeds 100,000 UVT (COP 4,979.9 million in 2025) OR gross income exceeds 61,000 UVT (COP 3,037.7 million in 2025); taxpayers that meet these thresholds must also file Form 120 and the annual TP annex. Below the thresholds but with cross-border transactions with foreign related parties, only the information annex of Form 210 is required.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply Offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Substance is enforced through (i) the General Anti-Abuse Rule (ET Art. 869 'abuso en materia tributaria'), (ii) CFC rules (ET Arts. 882-885) attributing passive income of low-tax foreign affiliates, and (iii) the ESAL/RTE obligations: the foundation must have real staff, infrastructure, formal grant policies and pursue its charitable object, otherwise DIAN will disregard the charitable status, impose the 35% tax on accumulated surplus, and apply the 100% accuracy penalty (ET Art. 648).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Criminal)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 4 to 9 years where the amount evaded exceeds 250 legal monthly minimum wages (± COP 325 million in 2024) (Criminal Code Art. 434A); fines up to 100% of the evaded amount; restoration of the surplus at 35% plus a 100% inexactness penalty (ET Art. 648) and moratory interest, computed from the filing date.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign the return are criminally liable; DIAN may order travel bans and passport seizure for tax arrears above 100,000 UVT; personal assets are attachable under solidarity liability (ET Art. 794) when the entity's assets were diverted to shareholders or related parties before the debt arose.

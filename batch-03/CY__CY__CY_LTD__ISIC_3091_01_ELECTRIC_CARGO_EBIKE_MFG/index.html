Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; electronic services portal: TaxisNet e-Services (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy); corporate registry interface: Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company obtains legal personality and its Company Registration Number automatically upon incorporation with the Registrar of Companies (ELF code HE77). For direct tax purposes, the company must notify the Tax Department of its registered office and obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number through the tax file registration module in TaxisNet. For VAT, the company must file Form TD101 (Application for Registration) through TaxisNet before making taxable supplies. A non-established foreign entity without a Cyprus fixed establishment is generally required to appoint an approved local VAT representative and register for VAT before any taxable supply in Cyprus. No physical visit is required, but all statutory filings must be made through a Cyprus tax representative or authorized accountant.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company obtains legal personality and its Company Registration Number automatically upon incorporation with the Registrar of Companies (ELF code HE77). For direct tax purposes, the company must notify the Tax Department of its registered office and obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number through the tax file registration module in TaxisNet. For VAT, the company must file Form TD101 (Application for Registration) through TaxisNet before making taxable supplies. A non-established foreign entity without a Cyprus fixed establishment is generally required to appoint an approved local VAT representative and register for VAT before any taxable supply in Cyprus. No physical visit is required, but all statutory filings must be made through a Cyprus tax representative or authorized accountant.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a check-the-box or elective flow-through regime for private limited companies. A Cyprus Ltd is mandatorily taxed at the entity level at 12.5% on worldwide income if managed and controlled in Cyprus; non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. Partnerships and limited partnerships are generally transparent, but this transparency is a default classification, not an elective check-the-box. Cyprus law provides no hybrid entity election comparable to the US check-the-box rules; however, taxpayers can make certain targeted elections in the income tax sphere, such as group relief election for intra-group losses and the election to treat a foreign permanent establishment as a separate business activity under the territorial regime rules. Hybrid mismatch anti-abuse rules are non-elective and apply automatically.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration threshold: EUR 15,600 of taxable supplies in any consecutive twelve-month period. Non-established foreign suppliers are deemed to exceed the threshold from the first taxable transaction in Cyprus and must register from that transaction. There is no registration threshold for imports; import VAT is payable regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The sale of electric cargo e-bikes physically exported from Cyprus to a destination outside the European Union qualifies for the 0% VAT rate provided that the goods are dispatched or transported out of the EU by the supplier or on the buyer's behalf before the end of or within a reasonable period after the supply. Related freight and offshore services directly connected to the exported goods may also benefit from zero-rating or outside-the-scope treatment under the place-of-supply rules if supplied to an overseas customer. To claim 0%, the supplier must obtain and retain export evidence issued by customs authorities proving that the goods left the EU, together with commercial and transport documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Single Administrative Document (SAD) stamped or electronically certified by the Cyprus Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR transport document showing dispatch from Cyprus and destination outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice confirming receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of insurance and shipping instruction documents where applicable

Cyprus does not currently mandate real-time fiscal e-invoicing, digital tax clearance, or stamping of invoices. VAT returns and EC Sales Lists are filed electronically through TaxisNet, and the Tax Department operates an e-audit environment. E-invoices may be used voluntarily if the authenticity, integrity, and legibility of invoices are ensured by the supplier. Cyprus has not implemented a continuous transaction control (CTC) model comparable to Italy or Spain; no government pre-clearance API is required at the time of invoice issuance.

Cyprus Revenue authorities may retrospectively deny an export zero-rating claim where the export documentation or foreign-exchange repatriation record is incomplete, unreliable, or fraudulent. Reassessed VAT will be charged at 19% on the export invoice value, together with interest from the date of the under-assessed VAT return. Administrative penalties can include a fixed penalty for failure to retain records and a further penalty of up to twice the tax liability for negligent or deliberate inaccuracies. In serious or fraudulent cases, criminal prosecution may result in fines and imprisonment. Input VAT already refunded to the exporter on the strength of a later-invalidated zero-rating claim is clawed back by amended assessment, and the audit window generally extends to the statutory limitation period of six years from the end of the tax year.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full Eurozone member (euro legal tender since 1 January 2008). Capital movements are fully liberalised under Article 63 TFEU for both EU and third-country flows; there are no prior-approval exchange controls, no capital-export authorisation, and no limitation on profit remittance for non-resident shareholders. Cyprus imposes no withholding tax on outbound dividends; remittance is conditioned only on the Companies Law Cap. 113 distributable-reserves solvency test, audited financial statements, and bank-side source-of-funds clearance. The remaining CBC role is threshold-based Balance-of-Payments / International Investment Position (BOP/IIP) statistical reporting via resident banks, not a convertible-currency permit mechanism.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Export proceeds are not subject to statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or any minimum ratio or repatriation deadline. Receipts may be retained in EUR or foreign currency at the exporter's discretion. The sole requirements are settlement of commercial transactions through a Cyprus-licensed institution to support CBC BOP/IIP statistics and AML/CTF screening, and retention of the underlying trade-file (contract, invoice, EORI customs declarations, transport documents) for transaction verification and bank due-diligence.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou); Eurosystem governance under the European Central Bank applies in respect of currency issuance and monetary policy, while the CBC retains competence as Cyprus banking and foreign-exchange statistical regulator and as the registrar of licensed Electronic Money Institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for Cyprus-licensed EMIs and EU-authorised EMIs passporting under PSD2 / Electronic Money Law 91(I)/2012: these support operational collecting accounts, SEPA Credit and Instant credit transfers, and corporate cards, but cannot issue documentary credits, open standby letters of credit, or provide direct TARGET2 settlement. Low-to-Nil for unlicensed offshore digital EMIs (without Cyprus/EU authorisation): no Eurosystem access, no accountable regulatory host, frequent correspondent-bank de-risking, and adverse implications for a manufacturing exporter's trade-file audit trail. Recommended split: Cyprus/EU-licensed EMI for high-volume receivables collections; Tier-1 commercial bank for capital, trade-LC, FX hedging, and correspondent-cleared clearances.

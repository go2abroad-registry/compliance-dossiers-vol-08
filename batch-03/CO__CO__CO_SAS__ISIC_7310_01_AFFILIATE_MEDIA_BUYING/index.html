Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

DIAN – Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (Colombian Tax and Customs Authority). Official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; transactional platform: MUISCA (https://muisca.dian.gov.co); foreign trade single window: VUCE (https://www.vuce.gov.co).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident establishing a Colombian S.A.S.: (1) Incorporate via Cámara de Comercio and obtain electronic registration certificate; (2) obtain a DIAN-accepted digital certificate (electronic signature class for MUISCA transactions); (3) register the company and its legal representative through MUISCA to obtain the NIT (9-digit tax ID) and RUT, expressly activating tax responsibilities 'renta – IVA – retención' and roles 'responsable de IVA' and 'facturador electrónico'; (4) for a foreign supplier of digital services without a Colombian establishment, use the simplified 'pre-RUT' registration process in MUISCA with a locally appointed apoderado; the NIT serves as the single TIN for income tax, VAT, customs and withholding at source.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a non-resident establishing a Colombian S.A.S.: (1) Incorporate via Cámara de Comercio and obtain electronic registration certificate; (2) obtain a DIAN-accepted digital certificate (electronic signature class for MUISCA transactions); (3) register the company and its legal representative through MUISCA to obtain the NIT (9-digit tax ID) and RUT, expressly activating tax responsibilities 'renta – IVA – retención' and roles 'responsable de IVA' and 'facturador electrónico'; (4) for a foreign supplier of digital services without a Colombian establishment, use the simplified 'pre-RUT' registration process in MUISCA with a locally appointed apoderado; the NIT serves as the single TIN for income tax, VAT, customs and withholding at source.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or partnership-transparency election exists; an S.A.S. is opaque and taxed at the 35% corporate rate (2023 onwards), plus sectoral surcharges (financial entities 38%, oil/coal extractives up to 10% additional in certain windows). Electable mechanisms: (i) SIMPLE regime – optional combined income/VAT/municipal taxation for eligible small S.A.S. with prior-year gross revenue ≤3,500–6,800 UVT depending on activity (2025 UVT = COP 49,799); (ii) monthly VAT refund election for exporters of services ('devolución de saldos a favor' via MUISCA); (iii) tax consolidation within an economic group under Law 2277/2022 limits; (iv) hybrid mismatch self-correction disclosures under Article 130-1 ET (no proactive anti-hybrid election).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for resident legal entities: an S.A.S. is a 'responsable de IVA' and must register for VAT/NIT regardless of turnover (Articles 437, 555-2 ET). Natural persons may remain in the simplified VAT regime if prior-year gross income < 3,500 UVT (≈ COP 174.3 million for 2025), but this exemption never extends to corporations. Non-resident digital platform/service suppliers (including affiliate ad exchanges contracting with Colombian end-users) must register as IVA collectors without any minimum threshold under Decree 1980/2020.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of services, including advertising and media-buying services (ISIC 7310), are IVA-excluded at 0% under Article 481(e) ET and DUT 1625/2016 Articles 1.3.1.7.1–1.3.1.7.4, provided: (i) the service is rendered in Colombia; (ii) the recipient is a non-resident entity with no domicile, branch or permanent establishment in Colombia; (iii) the service is effectively used or exploited exclusively outside Colombian territory; and (iv) the foreign currency consideration is channeled into the Colombian foreign-exchange market. Affiliate/performance media buying qualifies where the foreign client's end-advertiser, ad inventory, audience, or brand impact is located outside Colombia; campaigns aimed at Colombian consumers, Colombian website traffic, or local lead generation are in-country services subject to 19% IVA and cannot be zero-rated.

  • For services: VUCE Export of Services Declaration (Declaración de Exportación de Servicios) or equivalent service export certificate; commercial agreement / master service contract with the foreign counterparty; export invoice (factura de venta de exportación containing CUFE); bank evidence of receipt of foreign currency (inward SWIFT advice or certification by an authorized foreign-exchange intermediary); RUT page evidencing IVA responsibilities; work papers demonstrating exclusive foreign use or exploitation
  • For goods (if physical advertising inventory or printed materials are exported): customs export declaration (Declaración de Exportación, Form 110 through DIAN-WP system), Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill, and logistics certificates; single administrative document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) confirming channeling of export proceeds through the Colombian exchange market

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance. Every IVA-responsible S.A.S. must issue 'Factura Electrónica de Venta' (FEV) through a DIAN-authorized technology provider (proveedor tecnológico) or DIAN's free SFE system, transmitting XML via web services/API to DIAN for instant cryptographic validation; each invoice must display the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica), DIAN acceptance status, and QR. Phased mandatory rollout is governed by Resolution DIAN 000165/2023 and subsequent calendars, covering substantially all taxpayers by the 2024–2025 cycles; export invoices, credit notes ('nota crédito'), debit notes and foreign-trade clearance documents must use the same validation channel through VUCE.

DIAN may deny the zero-rating retrospectively and assess the omitted 19% IVA plus default interest; falsified export documentation triggers the inexactness penalty of up to 160% of the tax evaded (Article 647 ET), e-invoicing and without-documentation penalties (Article 652-14 ET), possible establishment closures, and reopening of previous refund periods. Fraudulent zero-rating or false certificates are treated as tax evasion under Articles 402–403 of the Colombian Criminal Code as amended by Law 2277/2022, punishable by 4–9 years of imprisonment. Failure to channel foreign currency within 180 days is a foreign-exchange infraction under Decree 1368/2018 and Resolution 085/2020, subject to fines (up to 200% of the un-channeled amount), prohibition from exporter status, and loss of VAT refund eligibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia applies a single, free-floating exchange market with a reference rate (TRM) determined by market transactions. All foreign exchange operations must be transacted through authorized remittance intermediaries or banks. The Colombian peso is fully convertible for current-account purposes such as service import/export, dividends, interest, royalties, and profit repatriation, subject to compliance registration and foreign investment registration requirements. The capital account is open for non-resident equity investment and cross-border debt; the S.A.S. may receive equity injections without prior central bank authorization, but the foreign investment must be registered with the Banco de la República through the local bank to secure later dividend remittance rights. Profit remittance is allowed after the company is tax-domiciled, files annual corporate income tax returns, and provides audited financial statements proving distributable earnings; the S.A.S. must process the remittance through a bank's 'Declaración de Cambio' and the applicable withholding tax must be prepaid. No material convertibility restrictions apply to media-buying service receipts, but funds must enter Colombia through the formal FX market and cannot simply remain in offshore EMIs on a permanent basis without being disclosed as foreign assets.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia does not impose a fixed percentage conversion of export receipts into COP, but imposes a mandatory 100% channeling and repatriation duty on service export proceeds. Receipts from digital advertising, performance media, and affiliate lead-generation services rendered to non-residents must be repatriated to Colombia and paid into an account held at an authorized foreign exchange intermediary (bank) using the applicable exchange declaration. The statutory timeline under DCIN-83 is that the exporter must initiate 'canalización' no later than the working day the foreign currency is available, and the Banco de la República grants a maximum retention period for service export proceeds; material export receipts must be declared and the underlying contract/invoice must be ready for audit. Any proceeds retained outside the formal market beyond the permitted term are considered non-channeled and are subject to coercive fines of up to USD 1,301 per infraction and potential suspicious-transaction reporting. In practice the bank will record 100% of the service export proceeds as received, convert to COP at the TRM to the extent needed for local expenses, and keep any unconverted balance in a permitted foreign-currency account at the same bank for approved outgoing FX payments.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), operating with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia as the local financial regulator and the foreign exchange intermediary channel in accordance with Decree 1735 of 1993 and Banco de la República's DCIN-83 regulations (Régimen de Cambios Internacionales).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium as an ancillary collection/payment rail, Low as a substitute for Colombian FX channeling. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Mercury, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not licensed Colombian foreign exchange intermediaries and cannot issue an account that complies with the S.A.S.'s FX channeling duty when receiving service-export revenue from overseas advertising platforms. The S.A.S. may use them for USD disbursements to publishers and vendors, but the funds that finance those disbursements must be traceable to a Colombian regulated account and the EMI balance must be reported as a foreign asset. Where inbound advertising revenue is collected by an EMI, the funds must be swept to a tier-1 Colombian bank in USD via SWIFT or converted into COP at a regulated bank to satisfy the channeling rule. If the S.A.S. wants to collect local card/ACH payments, it must contract with an SFC-authorized payment gateway or electronic funds institution; offshore EMIs cannot provide a COP account number or domicile the company's treasury in substitution for the local account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections from non-resident shareholders are routed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent/incorporator bank to the S.A.S.'s USD account at a Colombian tier-1 bank. The beneficiary must be exactly the S.A.S. NIT, and the payment reference must state the nature of the operation as 'Foreign direct investment – equity subscription' or 'share capital payment' rather than a commercial invoice. The Colombian receiving bank will request the foreign investment exchange declaration (Form 'Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional') before crediting the funds, and the S.A.S. must register the foreign investment with the Banco de la República through the bank to obtain the registration certificate needed for future dividend repatriation. If the injection is a shareholder loan, use a separate cross-border debt registration (Form 'Declaración de Cambio por Endeudamiento Externo') and comply with transfer pricing requirements. All wires must show the true purpose code; the bank will reject 'consulting fees' or 'personal remittance' language for equity injections, because those codes trigger different withholding and tax treatment. When capital is injected entirely in COP, the initial FX conversion must still be done through the local bank's FX market and evidenced by the exchange declaration.Outbound treasury payments for media-buying services are primarily service-import payments: the S.A.S. initiates a wire from its COP or USD account through the Colombian bank's international desk. The clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) using the beneficiary bank's BIC, along with the signed counterparty contract and invoice. For large recurring payouts to ad networks/publishers, the bank maintains batch payment APIs or a treasury portal that screens all beneficiary names against sanctions lists and issues one aggregated FX declaration. Commercial letters of credit are generally unsuitable for pure digital advertising services; they are permissible only if the underlying agreement covers procurement of physical advertising media. For merchant payments, ACH/US local rails (via Wise or Mercury) can be used as long as the corresponding USD are classified as foreign funds already channeled to the Colombian bank and then disbursed locally; all outbound payments with an amount equal to or above USD 10,000, or any amount in foreign currency, must be submitted on the day of execution with an exchange declaration ('Declaración de Cambio' for services) through the local intermediary.COP/USD outright forward contracts entered into with SFC-regulated Colombian banks or brokerage firms, with an underlying media-buying trade forecast or balance-sheet exposure documented.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing the peso TRM, executed through an authorized local bank or an international dealer with reporting to Banco de la República; settlement in COP.Cross-currency swaps between USD and COP for matched external debt, intercompany loans, or long-term supplier arrangements with an SFC-authorised lender.Plain vanilla foreign-currency options to protect minimum media-spend commitments, provided the board approves the strategy and the contract is documented as a risk-management derivative with a registered dealer.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium as an ancillary collection/payment rail, Low as a substitute for Colombian FX channeling. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Mercury, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not licensed Colombian foreign exchange intermediaries and cannot issue an account that complies with the S.A.S.'s FX channeling duty when receiving service-export revenue from overseas advertising platforms. The S.A.S. may use them for USD disbursements to publishers and vendors, but the funds that finance those disbursements must be traceable to a Colombian regulated account and the EMI balance must be reported as a foreign asset. Where inbound advertising revenue is collected by an EMI, the funds must be swept to a tier-1 Colombian bank in USD via SWIFT or converted into COP at a regulated bank to satisfy the channeling rule. If the S.A.S. wants to collect local card/ACH payments, it must contract with an SFC-authorized payment gateway or electronic funds institution; offshore EMIs cannot provide a COP account number or domicile the company's treasury in substitution for the local account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% (Colombian corporate income tax rate for fiscal year 2024; Tax Code Article 49, Law 2277/2022 confirms 35%.)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% for non-resident shareholders on dividends paid out of profits already subject to the 35% corporate income tax (Tax Code Article 242). If dividends are paid from profits not taxed, or from tax-exempt income, additional shareholder-level tax of up to 35% applies on a look-through/grossed-up basis. No dividend withholding tax is imposed on the S.A.S. itself.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return is due in the first quarter of the year following the tax period, on the specific date published by DIAN Resolution and determined by the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT. For calendar-year S.A.S. filers, the deadline falls in April of the following year; any income tax balance payable must be settled by the same deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A mandatory Local File is required when the S.A.S.'s gross income or total assets at year-end exceed approximately COP 4,706,500,000 (100,000 UVT for tax year 2024, with UVT at COP 47,065). A Master File may also be required for qualifying multinational groups; taxpayers below the threshold must still file an informative return and support the arm's length nature of controlled transactions if engaged in related-party dealings above de minimis levels, especially with entities located in tax havens.

Economic substance scope: Colombia has not enacted Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for S.A.S. entities, but the DIAN and Colombian tax courts apply substance-over-form, Business Purpose Theory (Article 869-2 Tax Code), and OECD BEPS Action 5 substance principles in audits. An affiliate media-buying S.A.S. must demonstrate real commercial activity: executed media-buying contracts, active platform traffic management, local or contracted employees, commercial offices, managerial decisions, retained records, and operational bank accounts. A vehicle used solely as an invoicing/conduit for foreign advertising platforms may be disregarded and treated as a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Documentation

Statutory sanction: DIAN inexactitude penalty up to 200% of the understated tax; late-filing and omission penalties of 5% per month; default interest; criminal tax evasion under Law 1762/2015 carries imprisonment from 3 to 9 years and closure/cancellation of the RUT in grave cases.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing under Commercial Code Article 71 can impose joint civil liability; directors and legal representatives may be required to personally restitute the evaded tax, are exposed to asset embargoes and travel bans, and may be criminally prosecuted as co-authors of fraudulent returns.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The DIAN may disregard the S.A.S., attribute the income to the real foreign beneficiary, impose a 200% inaccurate-return penalty, levy default interest, and administratively deactivate the tax identification (RUT). Commercial cancellation and professional disqualification may also be ordered.

Director personal liability: Administrators, shareholders, and beneficial owners who deliberately maintain a shell S.A.S. are jointly liable for taxes, penalties, and third-party damages; criminal liability can include money laundering where funds from staged transactions are repatriated, with preventive detention and exit bans.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentary Failure

Statutory sanction: The DIAN recharacterizes the transaction at the arm's length price and assesses the resulting tax difference, plus a penitential adjustment of up to 200% of the tax understatement or 100% of the TP adjustment, default interest, and separate penalties for non-filing of the TP informative return.

Director personal liability: Directors and CFOs who approve false intercompany pricing tables may be jointly liable for the corporate tax deficiencies; accounting professionals involved in preparing false TP studies are exposed to administrative disqualification and civil restitution; criminal liability attaches if fraudulent invoices or documents are used.
Disguised Profit Repatriation / Debt Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the payment as a dividend triggers the 5% or additional shareholder-level dividend tax, gross-up, default interest, and a penalty of up to 200% of the underwithheld amount. The foreign recipient remains liable for Colombian-source tax; a DTA does not protect disguised equity distributions.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives who sign loan agreements or service contracts without business purpose are personally liable for unpaid withholding; criminal charges may include fraudulent breach of duty and conspiracy to evade taxes, with travel bans and seizure of personal assets during investigation.
AML / Money Laundering Exposure

Statutory sanction: Colombia criminalizes money laundering under Law 599/2000 (Articles 323-327) with imprisonment from 10 to 30 years and fines of up to hundreds of thousands of statutory minimum monthly wages. The S.A.S. may be subject to asset forfeiture under Law 1708/2014, cancellation of commercial registration, and blacklisting by the UIAF.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing is permitted where corporate form is used to launder illicit proceeds; directors and beneficial owners face extradition, travel bans, personal asset confiscation, severe civil restitution claims by victims, and loss of legal right to administer Colombian companies.

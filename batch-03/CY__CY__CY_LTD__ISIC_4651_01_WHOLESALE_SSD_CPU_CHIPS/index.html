Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a private limited company under Cap. 113; shares may be issued at par with lawful consideration. If paid-up capital is deposited, it must be lodged in a Cyprus bank account in the company's name and be fully cleared before the first capital statement is filed; no escrow account is statutorily required.

Funding & unlock path: In cases where an office or warehouse is physically occupied, obtain a Fire Safety Certificate from the Cyprus Fire Service under the Fire Prevention and Fire Extinguishing (Minimum Requirements) Regulations; submit a structural load certificate for any storage area exceeding safe load capacity; complete workplace registration with the Department of Labour Inspection and maintain an occupational health and safety certificate, first aid equipment, evacuation plan, and employer's liability insurance.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a private limited company under Cap. 113; shares may be issued at par with lawful consideration. If paid-up capital is deposited, it must be lodged in a Cyprus bank account in the company's name and be fully cleared before the first capital statement is filed; no escrow account is statutorily required.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus (Tax Department / Department of Taxation); digital portal: Cyprus Tax Portal (Tax For All) accessible through the official Ministry of Finance tax website and Ariadne e-customs platform.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company: 1) incorporate with the Registrar of Companies and obtain a Certificate of Incorporation with a Cyprus registered number (HE77 format); 2) the Tax Department registers the company for corporate income tax and issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN); 3) if taxable supplies exceed or are likely to exceed EUR 15,600 in any rolling 12-month period, file Form VAT 101 (VAT registration application) via the Cyprus Tax Portal, attaching certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, registered office address, directors personal identification, proof of bank account, estimated turnover, and group structure documents; 4) upon approval the Tax Department issues a CY-prefixed VAT registration number that must be quoted on all tax invoices; VAT registration is effective from the date the taxation obligation first arises.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (a Cyprus private limited company is a separate juridical and taxable person; Cyprus tax-resident companies pay corporate income tax at the standard 12.5% rate on worldwide income; non-resident companies are taxed solely on Cyprus-source income; no pass-through treatment is available for a standard limited company). — For a foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company: 1) incorporate with the Registrar of Companies and obtain a Certificate of Incorporation with a Cyprus registered number (HE77 format); 2) the Tax Department registers the company for corporate income tax and issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN); 3) if taxable supplies exceed or are likely to exceed EUR 15,600 in any rolling 12-month period, file Form VAT 101 (VAT registration application) via the Cyprus Tax Portal, attaching certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, registered office address, directors personal identification, proof of bank account, estimated turnover, and group structure documents; 4) upon approval the Tax Department issues a CY-prefixed VAT registration number that must be quoted on all tax invoices; VAT registration is effective from the date the taxation obligation first arises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a Cyprus private limited company is a separate juridical and taxable person; Cyprus tax-resident companies pay corporate income tax at the standard 12.5% rate on worldwide income; non-resident companies are taxed solely on Cyprus-source income; no pass-through treatment is available for a standard limited company).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Cyprus law. However, qualifying entities may elect: (i) VAT grouping under the Cyprus VAT Law for closely related persons to be treated as a single taxable person; (ii) group relief election for companies within a 75%-or-more group to surrender losses; (iii) tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping activities; (iv) transparent treatment for partnerships and certain investment funds; (v) anti-hybrid elections and notifications under EU ATAD and the Cyprus Income Tax Law where a hybrid mismatch otherwise applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 in any rolling 12-month period of taxable supplies for a Cyprus-established wholesaler; a non-established foreign seller carrying on taxable activity in Cyprus may be required to register regardless of turnover unless covered by the OSS arrangement for distance sales. The threshold is calculated on aggregate taxable supplies excluding VAT; the threshold does not apply to persons whose supplies are wholly exempt or reverse-charged.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Cyprus VAT-registered wholesaler of SSD/CPU chips may zero-rate an export supply to a non-EU customer where: (a) the goods are physically dispatched or transported from Cyprus and leave the EU customs territory; (b) the Cyprus supplier holds a validated customs export declaration in Ariadne together with transport documents showing departure from the EU; (c) the goods generally must leave the EU within 90 days from the supply date or within the time limit set out in the Cyprus VAT regulations; (d) the commercial invoice, packing list, export declaration and bank receipt match in quantities, parties and consideration; (e) for related brokerage or Orange-to-foreign trade services, the non-EU business-to-business place-of-supply rules apply. If these conditions are not evidenced at the time of filing, the supply is recharacterised as taxable at 19% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Single Administrative Document (SAD) validated in the Cyprus Ariadne e-customs system
  • Commercial Invoice in original form evidencing the Cyprus exporter as supplier
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR consignment note proving physical exit from Cyprus and departure from the EU customs territory
  • Packing List showing quantities, marks, weights and product descriptions
  • International sales contract or purchase order evidencing the commercial terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and/or SWIFT MT103 bank credit confirmation showing payment received in the Cyprus bank account
  • Warehouse receipts and inventory movement records where drop-shipping or third-party logistics are used
  • Certificate of Origin and any applicable dual-use or export-control licence for semiconductor products if required by the destination country

As at the current compliance position, Cyprus does not impose a national real-time clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate for B2B VAT invoices. E-invoices are valid under the Cyprus VAT Law provided the authenticity of origin, integrity of content and readability of the invoice are preserved. Export declarations must be submitted electronically through the Ariadne customs system before goods exit the EU customs territory, and intra-EU B2B supplies require the filing of recapitulative EC Sales Lists via the Cyprus Tax Portal. Cyprus is preparing for the EU VAT in Digital Age (ViDA) reforms, which will introduce mandatory e-invoicing and digital reporting in later years, but such requirements are not yet in force.

The Cyprus Tax Department and Customs conduct post-clearance audits, cross-check Ariadne exit records, bank transactions and VAT returns, and exchange information with other EU member states and non-EU treaty partners. Where export zero-rating is claimed without adequate evidence or on false documents, the Tax Department retrospectively assesses VAT at 19% on the full consideration, adds statutory late-payment interest and applies administrative penalties of up to the tax undercharged. Fraudulent export zero-rating is a criminal offence under the Cyprus VAT Law and Customs Code, punishable by fines up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment, generally for a term of up to two years per offence. The statutory assessment period is six years; in cases involving intentional concealment, fraud or wilful default, the period may be extended, and promoters and directors may face liability for the unpaid VAT, penalties and disqualification from serving as directors.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Cyprus is a euro-area member state; the euro is fully convertible for current and capital account operations under EU law, particularly Article 63 TFEU on the free movement of capital. There is no exchange control approval, currency surrender, or mandatory conversion requirement for inbound equity, trade payments, loan repayments, or profit remittances. Dividends paid by a Cyprus private limited company to non-resident shareholders are not subject to Cyprus withholding tax, and no special defence contribution applies to non-Cyprus tax-resident shareholders. Residual practical hurdles arise only from EU sanctions, AML/CFT regulatory compliance, correspondent bank due diligence, and statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations. A non-EU remitter may face additional documentary scrutiny but not convertibility refusal.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory repatriation timeline applies to export proceeds under Cyprus or EU law. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or foreign currency, freely converted, and used for offshore or domestic disbursements. In practice, the receiving bank may require underlying export invoices, contracts, and transport documents to satisfy CBC balance-of-payments statistical reporting and AML/CFT record-keeping duties. Companies are also required to reflect export revenue accurately in VAT returns, audited financial statements, and any applicable Intrastat reporting. Foreign-currency account balances at Cypriot banks may be subject to internal internal risk limits, but these are prudential tools, not exchange controls.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC; Greek: Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου; Turkish: Kıbrıs Merkez Bankası), acting as the national banking supervisory authority, macroprudential authority, and member of the European System of Central Banks, together with the Cyprus Ministry of Finance for financial policy and the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission for investment services oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility, subject to strict licensing. A Cyprus-domiciled Electronic Money Institution (EMI) authorised by the Central Bank of Cyprus under the Electronic Money Law (Law 81(I)/2018) or a payment institution passporting into Cyprus under the revised Payment Services Directive (EU) 2015/2366 can provide multi-currency IBANs and fintech-grade onboarding. Offshore digital EMIs without an EU/EEA authorisation or passport are not viable as principal operating banks for this medium-risk enhanced wholesale semiconductor business. Banks with physical representation and robust correspondent banking networks remain preferred for escrow, trade finance, and large USD/CNY settlements.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-EEA UBO chain with multi-tiered holding companies and no clear natural-person ownership picture.

Root cause: Non-EEA UBO chain with multi-tiered holding companies and no clear natural-person ownership picture.

Defense evidence: Provide a fully documented corporate ownership chart, Cyprus Register of Beneficial Owners filing or confirmation, current shareholders' register, apostilled passports of every UBO, detailed source-of-wealth declarations, bank references, and certified board resolutions authorising the account relationship. Each intermediary legal entity must be accompanied by its own certificate of good standing, register of directors, and ownership records from the relevant registry.
Semiconductor-related goods triggering dual-use or export-control screening concerns, especially transactions involving high-risk jurisdictions or transhipment hubs.

Root cause: Semiconductor-related goods triggering dual-use or export-control screening concerns, especially transactions involving high-risk jurisdictions or transhipment hubs.

Defense evidence: Submit a transactional compliance pack comprising end-user and end-use statements, EU dual-use commodity classifications (ECCN/HTS codes), technical data sheets, invoices and purchase orders, warehouse or logistics documents, sanctions screening outputs covering EU, UN, OFAC, and UK HM Treasury lists, and a written trade compliance policy confirming the company will not trade with listed parties or for prohibited military end-uses.
High volume and high value trade flows inconsistent with the company's apparent staffing, capital, or trading history.

Root cause: High volume and high value trade flows inconsistent with the company's apparent staffing, capital, or trading history.

Defense evidence: Provide audited financial statements, signed master supply and sales contracts, transaction logs, inventory movement evidence, transport and customs declarations, carrier appointment letters, customer due diligence files, and a realistic business plan explaining the commercial rationale, reseller margins, and expected monthly throughput. This supports the economic substance and genuineness of the wholesale intermediary model.
Capital escrow contributions from third parties or affiliated shareholders without clear source-of-funds documentation.

Root cause: Capital escrow contributions from third parties or affiliated shareholders without clear source-of-funds documentation.

Defense evidence: Present the share subscription agreement or capital contribution resolution, bank statements of the remitting party for the three months before transfer, loan agreements or gift deeds where the funds are not equity, tax residency certificates, data on the remitter's business activity, and a sworn declaration that the funds are not derived from crime or sanctioned sources. The receiving bank may also require a copy of the registered transfer letter submitted to the Cyprus Registrar of Companies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital should be remitted from the parent or subscriber's own account via SWIFT MT103 using the exact legal name of the Cyprus entity in the beneficiary line, along with the company's registration number and VAT number if available. The payment should explicitly state the purpose as 'share capital contribution' and be supported by a board resolution, share subscription agreement, and final share allotment document. The receiving Cyprus bank will apply the CBC balance-of-payments classification code for direct investment equity capital, customarily reported as direct investment equity for statistical filings. All funds above normal thresholds must be supported by bank statements showing the origin of money, source-of-wealth declarations for all UBOs, and confirmation that the sending jurisdiction is not under sanctions. Prior notification to the bank's relationship manager is mandatory for amounts exceeding EUR 50,000 or equivalent; for larger contributions, the bank may request an advance AML call-off before accepting the wire.Outbound trade disbursements for SSD and CPU chip procurement should clear through SWIFT MT103 for direct open-account supplier payments in EUR, USD, or CNY, with the beneficiary bank details and the purchase order/invoice number included in the payment narrative. For high-value or new-supplier transactions, documentary letters of credit issued under UCP600 via SWIFT MT700 provide secure settlement with an agreed inspection certificate or shipping document release. Documentary collections on D/P or D/A terms are acceptable for established, vetted suppliers. SEPA Instant Credit Transfer is available for euro-denominated settlement to EEA suppliers. Escrow-inspection wires should be structured as conditional TT payments against a third-party inspection certificate, together with final commercial invoice and airway bill or bill of lading. All counterparty and bank identifiers must be pre-screened against sanctions and PEP lists, and each outbound wire must be accompanied by the transaction-specific invoice and Incoterms to prevent banking hold or return.EUR/USD outright deliverable forwards for confirming USD-denominated supplier purchase prices and export receivable values in EUR.EUR/CNH deliverable forwards in the offshore Chinese yuan market, or EUR/CNY non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) where onshore delivery is impractical or locally restricted.Cross-currency swaps between EUR and USD or EUR and CNH for longer-term alignment of foreign currency receivables and payables.Vanilla FX options, including EUR/USD calls and puts, to hedge unexpected currency movements without eliminating upside benefit; exotic barrier options are not recommended without senior treasury approval.FX forward rollovers and swaps with the client bank's dealing room provided the underlying commercial purpose is evidenced by a physical or known commodity trade, speculative positions being outside the permitted treasury policy.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions trigger mandatory TP Summary; full Local File required if group turnover exceeds EUR 5,000,000; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies EU ATAD and OECD BEPS guidelines through the Income Tax Law; a wholesale SSD/CPU trading company must demonstrate genuine business activity, adequate premises, qualified staff, and actual decision-making in Cyprus; lack of substance triggers CFC rules, permanent establishment challenges, and denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion & False Filing

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 50,000 or imprisonment up to 5 years per the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law; additional tax equal to 100% of the evaded amount and personal liability for director's negligent misstatement

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for evaded taxes, fraud penalties, contempt in court proceedings; travel bans under Section 46 of the Tax Law and potential extradition to EU member states on serious tax offences
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Application of the PPT under EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) denying treaty benefits; reclassification of income as effectively connected to a PE in the market jurisdiction; fiscal retro-application with 10-year statute of limitations

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered by the Tax Commissioner to personally pay the underpaid tax under the veil-piercing provision; court-imposed disqualification as a company director for up to 6 years
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to taxable profits, secondary adjustment applying deemed dividend taxation, plus 75% penalty on the additional tax due under Article 35 of the Income Tax Law

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for TP file preparation can incur administrative fines of EUR 5,000 and criminal liability if the mispricing disguises profit stripping; Tax Appeal Tribunal can issue personal surcharge orders
AML Violations (Wire Transfers)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 1,000,000 for corporate entities under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law; suspension of the Cyprus company's operating license

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face up to 15 years imprisonment and personal asset forfeiture if they knowingly participate in laundering of proceeds from semiconductor procurement fraud

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; Tax Portal: https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of a Cyprus Ltd, the Cyprus Registrar of Companies transmits company data to the Tax Department for automatic issuance of the Tax Identification Number (TIN). For a foreign entity without Cyprus legal presence, registration is mandatory before commencing economic activity: the foreign entity must appoint a Cyprus tax representative, submit Form TAN101 (Tax Registration Application) with certified constitutional documents, and file Form VAT101 if taxable turnover exceeds the registration threshold. The TIN is used for all corporate income tax, VAT, social insurance, and customs filings. Non-resident companies with no Cyprus branch may still be required to register for TIN if they derive Cyprus-source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Cyprus tax law permits: (i) group relief election enabling transfer of losses among group companies; (ii) election for foreign tax credit on a unital basis rather than deduction; (iii) capital allowance pooling elections for fixed assets; (iv) deemed dividend distribution election relating to the IP Box regime; and (v) ATAD-compliant anti-hybrid rules that deny deductions in respect of hybrid mismatches. All elections must be made in the annual corporate tax return (Form TD2023) and supported by contemporaneous documentation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for domestic supplies; EUR 10,000 for cross-border intra-Community distance sales of goods (pan-EU threshold); voluntary registration is available below the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to supplies of goods physically exported to non-EU destinations where the goods leave European Union territory within the prescribed period and the supplier retains comprehensive export evidence. Intra-Community dispatches to VAT-registered EU customers are also zero-rated where the customer's VAT identification number is shown and the supply is reported in a recapitulative statement (VIES). For DTC cross-border e-commerce, consignments dispatched by the Cyprus seller or a bonded fulfilment provider directly to non-EU individual purchasers qualify for zero-rating if the seller holds the customs export declaration, transport documentation, invoice, and payment proof. Services related to exports (e.g., international transport and intermediary services) are similarly zero-rated in accordance with Articles 14 and 34 of the Cyprus VAT Law.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR international transport document
  • Commercial Invoice and VAT invoice issued to the buyer
  • Proof of export movement (customs stamped document, carrier receipt, or electronic export status)
  • E-commerce platform order records (Shopify order export, transaction log, and payment confirmation)

As of the current effective date, Cyprus imposes no mandatory electronic invoicing, continuous transaction control (CTC), real-time fiscal clearance, API connectivity, or digital stamping regime. VAT invoices may be issued on paper or electronically with the recipient's consent and must contain the mandatory particulars of EU VAT Directive Article 226. The Tax Department may require electronic transmission of invoice data during audits or refund claims, but no pre-clearance electronic filing is currently legislated.

The Cyprus Tax Department may raise retrospective VAT assessments for failed export zero-ratings within the statutory limitation period (generally six years). Where export evidence is substantively deficient or fraud is detected, the Department will demand payment of the 19% VAT with statutory interest at 3.5% per annum, and may impose civil penalties up to 100% of the tax due. Criminal prosecution for export VAT fraud can lead to imprisonment of up to three years and personal liability for corporate officers. Fabricated or falsified export documents trigger immediate criminal customs scrutiny and likely disqualification of the zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of the euro on both current and capital account transactions is guaranteed by the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (Articles 63-65). Cyprus applies no exchange controls, conversion quotas, import-cover requirements, or repatriation restrictions. Profit remittance is not legally gated by the Central Bank; a distribution requires compliance with the Cyprus Companies Law (Cap. 113) — audited financials, a distribution solvency position, shareholder approval, and the filing of a return with the Registrar of Companies. Cyprus imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Cyprus resident company to non-resident shareholders under the standard domestic exemption, so no material tax barrier blocks dividends. The practical constraints are not regulatory but commercial: resident domestic banks de-risk certain non-EU UBO structures, and EU sanctions (e.g., against Russian or Belarusian counterparties) will suspend lawful convertibility for prohibited persons. The result is a legally open system that operates through bank-level KYC/AML gatekeeping rather than FX authorisations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus has no statutory ex-post timeline for export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio into local currency; export receipts in USD, CNY, GBP, or any freely convertible currency are fully retainable. The 2013-2015 emergency capital-control restrictions have been fully repealed and no replacement instruments exist. Incidental reporting duties remain: cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more require a customs declaration under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672; wire transfers must carry complete originator/beneficiary information under Regulation (EU) 2023/1113; and CBC statistical surveys capture direct foreign investment flows for Eurosystem balance-of-payments purposes. None of these duties impose surrender, conversion, or retention quotas.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza Kyprou) as the national competent authority within the Eurosystem; monetary policy is the exclusive competence of the European Central Bank (ECB). There is no separate Cypriot foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control commission. The Central Bank of Cyprus supervises credit institutions, administers the national balance-of-payments reporting for direct investment, and enforces EU sanctions and anti-money-laundering obligations for banking transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High for EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating in Cyprus under PSD2 passporting or under a Cyprus Electronic Money Institution licence issued by the Central Bank of Cyprus; such EMIs are valid clearing rails for Shopify/Stripe/PayPal merchant settlements and provide agile multi-currency cards. Medium for UK-regulated EMIs whose EU/EEA passporting rights have expired after Brexit unless they route through an EEA branch or a Malta/Cyprus MiFID/EMI subsidiary. Low for unregulated fintech PSPs and non-EEA ACH platforms because Cyprus-licensed banks will reject settlement wires originating from an entity not recognised as a payment institution under PSD2 and the Cyprus AML Law. A Cyprus Ltd engaged in DTC retail should therefore select an EMI demonstrably supervised within the EEA, and keep a fully integrated reconciliation ledger between the platform payout CSV and the EMI statements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a non-resident shareholder subscribing capital into CY_LTD, the compliant route begins with an SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's own bank account in the shareholder's name (never from a jewellery third party). The remittance reference must be unambiguous: 'Share Capital Subscription — Cyprus Ltd' and, where applicable, the resolved nominal share allotment. The Cyprus receiving bank requires the updated extract from the Registrar of Companies, the board resolution approving the issue of ordinary shares, and a note to the CBC statistical foreign-direct-investment survey when the inflow is above EUR 10,000. EUR-denominated wires are preferred because the corporate treasury is in EUR; USD or RMB inflows will be converted at real-time market rates and booked under the inward remittance tax-exempt interest concept. No prior Central Bank approval is needed, but the bank must clear the wire through its internal AML sanctions filter before crediting the Capital Escrow Account; funds are then migrated to the Operational Account upon confirmation of the allotment.For open account supplier settlements, SWIFT MT103 with the full code 'TRADE-IN' and narrative 'Goods for resale to EU consumers via DTC e-commerce platform' accompanied by invoice, packing list, and customs/transport evidence is the standard clearing rail. For substantial inventory replenishment from Asian factories, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit under UCP 600 issued via SWIFT MT700 from the Cyprus issuing bank provides documentary assurance; the classic rail is a DP/DA documentary collection involving the beneficiary bank, the shipper, and the Cyprus branch. Settlement to EU suppliers is commuted via SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Payment for low-ticket restocks. On the revenue side, Shopify Payments, Stripe, and PayPal settle through their rolling payout cycles into the Cyprus bank account or EEA EMI account, and those incoming MT103 'merchant settlement' credits are identified with the platform's merchant identification number to allow automatic ledger matching; receipts above the EUR 10,000 wire threshold maintain the required originator data and platform contract evidence in Treasury's KYC file.EUR/USD deliverable outright forward contract under the bank's ISDA or master FX agreement to hedge the USD-denominated inventory procurement pipeline.EUR/GBP deliverable outright forward for UK destination-market DTC cash flows after Brexit settlement exposures.EUR/CNH deliverable FX swap for supplier settlements involving offshore Chinese yuan where the underlying exposure is deliverable.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) covering the CNY onshore exposure of the supply chain, contracted only when the underlying settlement cannot be executed through a deliverable onshore balance, and documented as a hedging rather than speculative transaction.European-style FX call or put options on EUR/USD, including zero-premium collars and forward-start options, used to cap adverse procurement-currency movements while retaining upside under the Cypriot accounting hedge documentation.Cross-currency interest-rate swap (CCIRS) converting long-term EUR working-capital borrowings into USD liability funding when the DTC brand maintains a sticky US-dollar revenue pool.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (uniform corporate rate for Cyprus tax-resident companies; non-residents taxed only on Cyprus-source income attributable to a permanent establishment)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; the 17% Special Defence Contribution applies only to Cyprus-tax-resident individuals and is not a corporate withholding obligation for non-resident investors.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The TD4 must be filed within 12 months after the end of the tax year (calendar year ending 31 December), i.e. by 31 December of the following year; companies with an approved non-calendar accounting period must file within 12 months of the period end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory when the aggregate annual value of the Cypriot company's controlled transactions with non-resident related parties exceeds EUR 10,000,000 in a tax year, including intercompany loans, working capital balances, and the Shopify platform services; Master File: mandatory when the worldwide MNE group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000; documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and submitted to the Tax Department within 60 days of a formal request, with no separate filing obligation attached to the TD4.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations decree comparable to the UAE/BVI regimes; substance obligations derive from the central management-and-control tax-residence test under section 2 of the Income Tax Law, the EU ATAD general anti-abuse rule, the substantial-activity condition on the IP Box under EU State Aid law, and OECD BEPS Action 5. A Shopify DTC brand must demonstrate genuine operational presence in Cyprus through local board meetings, strategic decision-making, physical premises, employed staff in management, marketing, and fulfillment oversight, and concluded operating agreements; failure to do so risks re-determination of tax residence abroad, denial of CoTR and treaty benefits, and claw-back of the IP Box and NID reliefs.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of under-declared tax at 12.5%, interest at 3.5% per annum, surcharges up to 10% of the tax deficit, criminal fines up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years under section 69 of the Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002, asset-confiscation orders, and publication of taxpayer details in serious default cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns face personal criminal liability; courts may pierce the corporate veil to impose civil restitution against personal assets; travel bans and director-disqualification orders are available for unpaid tax debts.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-determination of tax residence to the place of effective management and control, exposing the company to worldwide taxation at 12.5% plus penalties; denial of the Cyprus Certificate of Tax Residence and all DTA benefits; claw-back of NID and IP Box reliefs with interest; mandatory exchange of information with foreign tax administrations under international conventions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed personally for corporate tax liabilities based on fraudulent or wrongful trading; travel bans and disqualification orders may be imposed; secretaries, accountants, and tax advisers also face civil and disciplinary exposure for facilitating sham structures.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's-length results with gross-up of profits, interest at 3.5% per annum on the adjustment, additional tax at 12.5%, fines up to EUR 10,000, and estimated bespoke assessments when documentation is not produced within 60 days of request; secondary adjustments and penalty interest for repeat offenders.

Director personal liability: Directors approving non-arm's-length pricing bear joint liability for the resultant underpayment assessments; knowingly submitting false TP documentation constitutes tax evasion with personal criminal exposure, cross-border enforcement, and loss of good-character defenses in insolvency proceedings.
AML / Beneficial Ownership (UBO) and Payment-Rails Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Companies Law (Cap. 113) and the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law 188(I)/2007, fines up to EUR 200 per day of UBO default, confiscation orders, and imprisonment up to 14 years for money-laundering offenses; tax authorities may concurrently impose assessment penalties, freeze bank accounts, and obtain court-approved asset-restraint orders.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors, and company secretaries face criminal liability and civil restitution for AML violations; travel bans and extradition risk arise where laundering is linked to cross-border DTC payment flows; the corporate veil is readily pierced in fraud-driven insolvency, exposing personal assets and reputational standing.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax and Customs Directorate (DIAN – Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales), virtual portal at https://muisca.dian.gov.co; institutional site at https://www.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

A foreign or domestic legal entity must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and registration in the RUT (Registro Único Tributario). For a newly incorporated Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.), the NIT is assigned automatically during Chamber of Commerce registration; the legal representative must then update the RUT through the DIAN Muisca portal using a current digital certificate. A non-resident entity not otherwise required to create a local branch may, through an appointed agent or representative, obtain a simplified NIT/RUT for purposes of Colombian withholding tax and non-resident VAT compliance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign or domestic legal entity must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and registration in the RUT (Registro Único Tributario). For a newly incorporated Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.), the NIT is assigned automatically during Chamber of Commerce registration; the legal representative must then update the RUT through the DIAN Muisca portal using a current digital certificate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not provide a US-style check-the-box pass-through election. The S.A.S. is in all cases classified as a Colombian income-tax resident legal entity, taxed at the entity level. Elective regimes are limited to practical alternatives such as the 'Régimen Simple de Tributación' for eligible small businesses, free-zone taxation under Article 240-1 of the Tax Code, and consolidated tax-group treatment only for parent-controlled entities under Law 1819 of 2016. Hybrid mismatch rules are applied statutorily through transfer-pricing and deduction-denial provisions, not through a taxpayer-selected classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for legal entities performing taxable activities; all such entities must obtain RUT and charge VAT from inception. The de minimis threshold for natural persons is 42,000 UVT (COP 1,976,772,000 for 2024 at UVT 47,066), subject to additional conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods and services may be zero-rated (0% IVA) when: (i) the taxpayer is registered as an exporter in the RUT; (ii) goods physically leave Colombia under a customs export declaration; or (iii) services satisfy Article 481 of the Tax Code, i.e., the service is rendered in Colombia to an offshore beneficiary, used exclusively outside Colombia, and the supporting contract and invoicing evidence the foreign use. For B2B virtual card and payment technology services, zero-rating is available where the card issuer provides the service exclusively to non-resident merchants or cardholders and the economic exploitation takes place outside Colombia.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX / Declaración de Exportación)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Exchange Channeling Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro / Formulario No. 5)
  • Export service contract and supporting technical documentation

Mandatory electronic invoicing applies to all VAT taxpayers. Invoices are generated through DIAN-authorized software integrators and transmitted to DIAN's real-time fiscal validation system via API in XML format, with a digital signature, CUFE/CUDS code, and QR stamp. Exports require the electronic invoice to be associated with customs export declarations and the exporter's RUT status.

DIAN reviews export zero-rating claims against the DEX system, foreign-exchange channeling records, and beneficiary status. Fraudulent or unjustified zero-rating gives rise to retrospective VAT collection with default interest, penalty surcharges under Articles 647 and 648 of the Tax Code up to 200% of the omitted tax amount, monetary correction, cancellation of the exporter/RUT status, and criminal liability for tax fraud under recent anti-abuse legislation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso is convertible but subject to a managed foreign exchange regime. Current account transactions, including import payments, services, interest, and royalties, are freely transferable through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, securities investment, and cross-border loans, are permitted but must be routed through authorized intermediaries and registered with Banco de la República via the Declaración de Cambio system. Profit remittances and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are permitted upon satisfactory tax compliance, including dividend withholding tax, and are executed through an authorized bank using the appropriate exchange code; no general exchange control approval is required for such remittances once the underlying foreign investment has been properly registered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds arising from goods and services must be repatriated to Colombia and settled in the foreign exchange market through an authorized intermediary, either by sale to the local market or by deposit into a foreign-currency account maintained with a local commercial bank. The general statutory term for repatriation and settlement is six months from the date of the export declaration, loading, or the contractual term of collection, subject to extensions granted by Banco de la República in specific regulated circumstances such as deferred payments or special regimes. Mandatory conversion of export proceeds is the default, although exporters may retain balances in foreign currency accounts up to the applicable regulatory thresholds for subsequent operational needs.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), together with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia for prudential oversight and the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit for policy coordination

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for fully offshore or non-licensed digital EMI service providers. Colombia does not recognize the EU/UK-style EMI license. Any entity issuing a virtual card or providing electronic payment services to Colombian residents must obtain authorization from the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia, generally as a Sociedad Especializada en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos (SEDPE) or as a financial institution authorized for payment systems. A foreign cross-border EMI may operate only through an approved local subsidiary, a joint venture with a locally licensed entity, or an outsourcing/correspondent arrangement with a Colombian banking partner. Legal constraints prohibit the direct issuance of payment instruments by an unlicensed foreign entity without a local regulated presence.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Suspected opacity in ultimate beneficial ownership, including layered ownership across multiple non-resident jurisdictions.

Root cause: Suspected opacity in ultimate beneficial ownership, including layered ownership across multiple non-resident jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: Corporate organizational chart demonstrating direct and indirect ownership; apostilled certificates of incorporation and registry certificates for each entity layer; national identity cards and passports of UBOs; comprehensive declaration of source of wealth and source of funds, supported by audited financial statements, tax filings, bank references, and professional legal opinions.
Mismatch between the projected transaction volume, low transaction values, and the expected liquidity requirements of a virtual card issuer.

Root cause: Mismatch between the projected transaction volume, low transaction values, and the expected liquidity requirements of a virtual card issuer.

Defense evidence: Detailed business plan and projected cash-flow model; executed agreements with card network partners and merchant acquirers; transactional monitoring and anti-fraud procedures; tiered transaction limits and velocity controls; KYC/AML policies for cardholders and merchants; and evidence of a local compliance officer with formal reporting lines to the board.
Concern that the entity is a shell company with no real economic substance in Colombia.

Root cause: Concern that the entity is a shell company with no real economic substance in Colombia.

Defense evidence: Office lease agreements; payroll records and social security filings for local employees; board resolutions evidencing decisions made in Colombia; copies of commercial contracts with Colombian counterparties; Colombian tax registration (RUT) and periodic tax returns; and records of substantive operational activity, including software, compliance, and customer support operations.
Inadequate controls or prohibited connections to crypto-assets or unregulated payment corridors.

Root cause: Inadequate controls or prohibited connections to crypto-assets or unregulated payment corridors.

Defense evidence: Written prohibition on crypto-asset acceptance or settlement; banking relationship documentation covering all partner and service-provider accounts; a schedule of approved counterparties with license verification; independent audit evidence of transaction monitoring software; and a third-party AML/CFT review or certification demonstrating compliance with Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia Circular Básica Jurídica requirements.

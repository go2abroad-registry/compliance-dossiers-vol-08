Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio with territorial jurisdiction over the registered office (e.g., Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá), operating the Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES) under the functional supervision of the Superintendencia de Sociedades; DIAN assigns the NIT and maintains the Registro Único Tributario (RUT).

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 ('Por medio de la cual se crea la sociedad por acciones simplificada'), Articles 1-50; Código de Comercio (Decreto 410 de 1971), Articles 98, 110-119, 353-373 (residual application); Decreto 1074 de 2015, Title II (commercial registry and Chamber of Commerce functions); Ley 1780 de 2016 (simplified virtual incorporation window for small entities).

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución of the S.A.S. (or Escritura Pública No. [●] if real property is contributed or notarial formality is voluntarily adopted), inscribed in the Registro Mercantil via the RUES.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 26 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum capital; the S.A.S. may be constituted with one (1) share of nominal value (as low as one COP). Subscribed capital may be paid in cash, in kind, or by set-off, with payment terms freely fixed in the bylaws. No compulsory escrow account is required by Ley 1258/2008; escrow is only advisable for foreign-currency contributions, which must be channeled through authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries in accordance with the Colombian exchange regime.

Funding & unlock path: Certificado de Bomberos (fire-safety certificate) required for the physical office; compliance with structural and seismic design standards of Ley 400 de 1997 and NSR-10 for load-bearing capacity; implementation of the SG-SST occupational health management system per Resolución 0312 de 2019; mandatory ARL employee enrolment before any economic activity commences at the premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum capital; the S.A.S. may be constituted with one (1) share of nominal value (as low as one COP). Subscribed capital may be paid in cash, in kind, or by set-off, with payment terms freely fixed in the bylaws. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN), Colombian national tax and customs authority; official portal: www.dian.gov.co (and the MUISCA electronic system for registration and filings)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Colombian S.A.S. must appoint a resident legal representative and execute the incorporation deed (Escritura Pública or Private Document) before a notary, followed by registration in the Cámara de Comercio. The Registro Único Tributario (RUT) must then be obtained online via DIAN's MUISCA portal. The DIAN assigns the Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT) upon RUT approval. For foreign entities without a local branch, a NIT may be obtained for indirect tax purposes using a local authorized attorney-in-fact with a power of attorney, but the standard route for an operational Colombian S.A.S. is statutory incorporation and RUT issuance. After RUT activation, the entity must obtain an electronic signature certificate (Certificado de Firma Electrónica) from an authorized provider to submit VAT and income tax returns.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the Colombian S.A.S. is a separate juridical person taxed as a national corporation under Colombian income tax law (Estatuto Tributario). Colombia does not recognize check-the-box pass-through treatment for S.A.S. entities; profits are subject to corporate income tax at the entity level, and dividends are separately taxed at shareholder level when distributed. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Colombian S.A.S. is a separate juridical person taxed as a national corporation under Colombian income tax law (Estatuto Tributario). Colombia does not recognize check-the-box pass-through treatment for S.A.S. entities; profits are subject to corporate income tax at the entity level, and dividends are separately taxed at shareholder level when distributed. Territorial scope applies to Colombian-source income, with broad foreign-source income taxation for resident entities per ordinary residence rules.

Available election forms

No US-type check-the-box election exists. The S.A.S. by law is always a taxable corporation (sociedad comercial) and cannot elect to be treated as transparent or as a branch for tax purposes. Hybrid mismatch elections are not generally available; Colombia implements anti-hybrid rules under the OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework (Law 2277 of 2022), but these operate as disallowance rules rather than elective classifications. Certain new companies may elect the 'sociedad beneficiosa e innovadora' (SOCIEDAD BIC) regime, which grants tax benefits, and the S.A.S. may elect progressive tax rate banding only in respect of small-business thresholds under Article 242 of the Tax Code, but this is not an entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Responsible entities must register in the RUT as IVA-responsible persons. There is no general economic turnover threshold: any person engaged in taxable activities, importing goods, or contracting taxable services is obliged to register. Optional simplified registration applies only to small traders under Article 437 of the Tax Statute, subject to gross income below COP 3,500,000 per year and other conditions. An S.A.S. operating a commercial trust administration service will almost always exceed the nominal threshold and must register. For goods importers, registration is mandatory irrespective of value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods permanently exported are zero-rated under Article 481 of the Tax Statute, provided they leave the country within the terms of the customs regime. Export of services to non-residents is also zero-rated if the service is wholly performed abroad or the recipient is a foreign entity without domicile in Colombia, subject to compliance with Article 481(d) and the regulatory regime of Decree 2090 of 2017. For an ESOP trust, administrative services rendered to a foreign beneficiary or foreign company may be zero-rated if the service is not connected to Colombian assets and the recipient is located overseas. The trust must demonstrate the service is exported and not used domestically. The entity must be an IVA-responsible registered VAT taxpayer. Zero-rating does not require a formal prior approval but is applied on the export invoice; refund of VAT credits arises through the periodic VAT returns.

  • Formulario Único de Exportación (DEX) approved by DIAN or the authorized customs broker
  • Bill of Lading (Cartas de Porte/Conocimiento de Embarque) or airway bill for goods; for services, the service contract and export certificate issued under Article 481(d)
  • Commercial Invoice and Export Invoice (Factura de Exportación) with electronic validation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de compensación de divisas) issued by the Colombian intermediary bank or sociedades corredoras de cambio showing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Proof of customs export declaration (Export Definitiva) stamped by DIAN customs authority

Colombia mandates structured electronic invoicing (factura electrónica) through DIAN's electronic invoicing platform (Actividad de Facturación Electrónica). The issuer must transmit XML invoices to DIAN in real time and receive a valid electronic acceptance (CUNE) using the DIAN-enabled computer system or authorized software. For international transactions, the equivalent is the electronic dispatch document ('documento equivalente') for non-invoice operations. Export invoices must be digitally endorsed. DIAN uses the electronic billing exchange (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica) with validation of CUNE codes and the export declaration is reported in the electronic system. Qualified electronic signatures are mandatory; there is no physical stamp, but clearance is denied if the XML fails DIAN's real-time validation.

DIAN may deny zero-rated treatment if the exporter fails to prove physical exit of goods or true rendering of services to a foreign beneficiary. Fraudulent zero-rating triggers retrospective tax assessment with the maximum penalty for misdeclaration under Article 648 of the Tax Statute, equal to 200% of the tax assessed, plus interest accruing from the demand date. Additional penalties for inaccurate or false export documentation under customs law (Decreto 1165 of 2019) include fines of up to COP 18,000,000, seizure of goods, and criminal fraud sanctions under Law 1762 of 2015 (customs fraud) if evidence suggests intentional evasion. If the export proceeds are not received within the 180-day deadline, or the inward remittance certificate is missing, DIAN may claw back the VAT differential, and for services, the zero-rating is disallowed entirely. The entity bears the burden of proof; mere invoice issuance without bank certificate and customs validation is insufficient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a conditional free-convertibility regime. Current account transactions are freely convertible and must be routed through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries or registered foreign currency accounts. Capital account transactions, including inbound foreign direct investment and outbound disbursements, require registration with the Banco de la República’s Sistema de Información Cambiaria (Foreign Exchange Information System) but generally do not require prior exchange control authorization. Profit remittances are permissible after the foreign investment is duly registered, the dividend distribution is legally approved, all Colombian corporate income tax obligations are settled, and applicable dividend withholding taxes are satisfied. Practical hurdles include incomplete registration of the beneficial ownership chain, tax non-deductibility of certain expenditures, and AML/KYC verification of the ultimate trust beneficiaries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of goods and services must channel export proceeds through the official foreign exchange market or a registered foreign exchange account. The statutory settlement term is generally six months from the contractual payment due date, not exceeding six months from the date of shipment or provision of the service. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into Colombian pesos; the exporter may hold and use the foreign currency, provided the exchange declaration (Form Declaración de Cambio) is timely filed and evidence of repatriation is retained. Extensions are available upon prior Banco de la República authorization, and non-compliance triggers fines, sanctions, and foreign exchange tax adjustments.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia) acting as the primary monetary and foreign exchange authority, together with the Comité de Cambios for exchange control policy and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia for banking and financial supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (Electronic Money Institutions) have limited viability in Colombia. They may establish correspondent or settlement relationships with locally licensed banks, but they cannot accept client deposits or operate as clearing institutions without obtaining a Colombian financial license from the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. Viability rating: Medium for inbound international remittances and card payouts through local partner banks; Low for fiduciary ESOP administration where licensed asset custody and Colombian-seated trust accounts are required. Transactions in Colombian pesos must settle through the local ACH/CUD infrastructure; offshore EMIs cannot independently interface with Banco de la República’s settlement systems.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder contributions into the Colombian ESOP trust must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or investors to the S.A.S.’s operational or capital escrow account at an authorized local bank. The wire must clearly state the foreign investment purpose code (Inversión directa para desarrollo de la empresa en Colombia), the investor’s registration number, and the beneficiary account number. The transaction must be accompanied by a board resolution, updated corporate purpose, valuation letter for shares or capital contribution, and be registered with Banco de la República within 30 business days. Foreign currency received in Colombia must be sold to the local market or maintained in a registered foreign currency account within the permitted limits. Capital increases are allowed without prior authorization but require submission of the corresponding exchange declaration and ensuing investment registration.For ESOP trust distributions, beneficiary payouts, and third-party service settlements, the primary outbound rails are SWIFT MT103 wires through the Banco de la República electronic foreign exchange system, commercial letters of credit for trade payables, and local ACH/CUD transfers for COP disbursements. Each outbound operation must include the standard recipient bank’s SWIFT/BIC, the beneficiary legal name and tax ID, an accurate exchange declaration code for the underlying operation, and a certificate of tax withholding. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

7.5% final withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents out of fully taxed Colombian-source profits; 10% is withheld on the portion of dividends paid out of profits not subject to the standard 35% CIT (e.g. exempt income or special regime profits).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Calendar-year taxpayers must file according to the DIAN schedule issued each March-April based on the last two digits of the NIT. The deadline may not extend beyond four months after the fiscal year-end (usually April of the following year).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filed together with the annual CIT return, subject to DIAN scheduling; large taxpayers file in June-August following the fiscal year-end, all taxpayers by December 31 of the following calendar year.

Box 1 - Ingresos brutos Total gross ordinary and non-operating revenue: ESOP administration fees, interest on plan assets, share transfer gains. Report only Colombian-source income. If services are rendered from Colombia to a foreign plan sponsor, treat as export service income; if a fixed place of business exists abroad, assess permanent establishment exposure. Late filing: monthly surcharge of 1% of total tax assessment, capped at 100%, plus daily interest at DIAN rates. If no tax is payable, a fixed sanction of approximately COP 1.5-5.5 million applies per month. Incomplete return: applicable fine from 5% to 15% of assessed taxes, subject to aggravated penalties for falsification.
Box 20 - Costos y deducciones Direct costs of plan administration, custody, payroll, and deductions for share-based compensation actually transferred to beneficiary employees. Deductions require payroll records and social security contributions when beneficiaries are Colombian residents; foreign beneficiaries are not subject to Colombian payroll but must be proved by board minutes and trust deed. Late filing: monthly surcharge of 1% of total tax assessment, capped at 100%, plus daily interest at DIAN rates. If no tax is payable, a fixed sanction of approximately COP 1.5-5.5 million applies per month. Incomplete return: applicable fine from 5% to 15% of assessed taxes, subject to aggravated penalties for falsification.

Foreign shareholders should allocate Colombian-source expenses in proportion to ownership; expenses allocable to exempt export services must be separated. Late filing: monthly surcharge of 1% of total tax assessment, capped at 100%, plus daily interest at DIAN rates. If no tax is payable, a fixed sanction of approximately COP 1.5-5.5 million applies per month. Carryforward losses must have been filed in a prior return and remain within the eligible 5-year period for 2024-2029 losses. Late filing: monthly surcharge of 1% of total tax assessment, capped at 100%, plus daily interest at DIAN rates. Incomplete return: applicable fine from 5% to 15% of assessed taxes, subject to aggravated penalties for falsification.

Section A - Related parties List of related parties, tax residency, and group shareholding structure. Every foreign shareholder owning 10% or more, or exercising management control over the ESOP trustee, must be identified. Late or non-filing: fine of 2%-20% of the omitted related-party transaction amounts; failure to produce supporting documentation may attract fines up to 10,000 UVT (approximately COP 470 million at 2025 UVT); adjustments may add the differential tax plus a 100% penalty.
Section B - Cross-border transactions All transactions with foreign related parties by nature (administrative services, financing, intangibles, dividends, share transfers). The transfer of ESOP shares to a foreign parent at nil value is a reportable transaction even if no consideration changes hands. Late or non-filing: fine of 2%-20% of the omitted related-party transaction amounts; failure to produce supporting documentation may attract fines up to 10,000 UVT (approximately COP 470 million at 2025 UVT); adjustments may add the differential tax plus a 100% penalty.
Operating margin reconciliation Reconciliation of operating margin, EBIT, and net margin with financial statements and comparable benchmarks. For a single-entity ESOP administrator, TNMM with regional compensation comparables normally applies; CUP may be used if identical plan administration fees are offered to unrelated parties. Late or non-filing: fine of 2%-20% of the omitted related-party transaction amounts; failure to produce supporting documentation may attract fines up to 10,000 UVT (approximately COP 470 million at 2025 UVT); adjustments may add the differential tax plus a 100% penalty.
DTA cascade Statement of treaty relief claimed in the CIT return and corresponding payee certification. A certificate of tax residence from the foreign jurisdiction must be appended; without it, treaty relief cannot be applied. Late or non-filing: fine of 2%-20% of the omitted related-party transaction amounts; failure to produce supporting documentation may attract fines up to 10,000 UVT (approximately COP 470 million at 2025 UVT); adjustments may add the differential tax plus a 100% penalty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Form 120 and disclosure are mandatory if gross ordinary income exceeds COP 135,000 UVT (approximately COP 6.4 billion at 2025 UVT) or if cross-border related-party transactions exceed COP 10,000 UVT. A full Local File must be prepared and filed when gross revenues exceed COP 450,000 UVT or related-party cross-border transactions exceed COP 20,000 UVT. A Master File is required at the group level if consolidated group revenue exceeds COP 4 million UVT. Even where no filing threshold is met, transfer pricing principles apply to the ESOP trustee for services and share transfers with the foreign grantor or parent.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but DIAN and the Superintendence of Companies enforce substance-over-form tests under the Tax Code and Company Law. An ESOP trust administered by a SAS must show real local presence: employees, premises, board minutes, service contracts, trust accounting, and independent decision documents. Missing substance will lead to recharacterization as a shell company, automatic denial of DTA benefits, attribution of undistributed profits to foreign owners, and application of the 10% surcharge sanction regime.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through sham trust and mischaracterized dividends

Statutory sanction: DIAN reassessment at the shareholder level, imposing the ordinary 35% CIT plus dividend WHT at 7.5%/10%; accuracy penalty of 100%-200% of the omitted tax (Tax Code Articles 647-648). Criminal evasion is punishable with imprisonment of 48-108 months (Criminal Code Article 287).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; assets may be attached, a travel ban may be ordered, and imprisonment is possible if criminal intent is established.
Transfer pricing non-disclosure and falsified valuation

Statutory sanction: DIAN may adjust taxable income to the arm's length result, charge 35% tax on the adjustment, apply monthly interest, and impose a fine up to 100% of the adjusted amount; failure to present documentation may attract fines up to 10,000 UVT and loss of treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the additional tax and interests if willful concealment is proven; the veil of the SAS can be pierced under Law 1258/2008, and directors can face criminal liability for falsifying transfer pricing certificates.
AML and beneficial ownership concealment

Statutory sanction: Monetary fines up to 1,200 SMMLV (minimum legal monthly wage) under Law 526/1999; freezing of bank accounts and share registers; cancellation of the company registration if the entity is classified as a money-laundering conduit; confiscation of shares involved in illicit operations.

Director personal liability: Managing directors face criminal prosecution for money laundering under Criminal Code Article 323 (imprisonment of 10-25 years), personal asset confiscation, disqualification from commercial activities, and travel bans during investigation.
Unreported foreign shareholders and undocumented DTA claims

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is assessed for 7.5% or 10% on gross dividends, plus a 100% penalty for incorrect completed tax returns; the false share-based compensation deduction triggers a 35% surtax, indexing, and infraction fines; in serious cases the company license may be suspended.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid withholding taxes and jointly liable for civil restitution when the SAS lacks sufficient assets; criminal charges for false certification are possible if a TRC is fabricated; travel bans and asset freezes are common prior to trial.

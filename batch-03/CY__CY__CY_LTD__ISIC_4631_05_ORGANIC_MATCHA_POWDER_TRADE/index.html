Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is generally permissible as a registered-office address when provided by a licensed Cyprus corporate services provider, and the company has no physical premises or staff in Cyprus. For a low-risk matcha powder distribution company, a registered agent/virtual registered office will satisfy DRCOR requirements; however, if management and control are exercised from Cyprus, fully virtual premises may be challenged for tax residence, economic substance, VAT registration and customs import purposes. If the company maintains stock, employees or management functions in Cyprus, a physical office and adequate substance documentation are expected.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a private limited liability company; the company may issue one or more shares with nominal value, and no escrow or deposit account is required by law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Cyprus Fire Service for the registered premises; structural load and occupancy clearance from the local municipality; and workplace clearance from the Department of Labour Inspection covering safe access, manual handling, and storage conditions.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a private limited liability company; the company may issue one or more shares with nominal value, and no escrow or deposit account is required by law.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of Cyprus (under the Ministry of Finance), online portal 'ARIADNE' (https://www.ariadne.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must apply for a Cyprus Tax Identification Code (TIC) through the Tax Department by completing Form TD-2000(Registration for Companies/EU/Non-EU), submitting certified constitutional documents, passport copies, proof of registered address, and, where applicable, obtaining a Cyprus VAT registration number. Processing occurs through the ARIADNE portal or in-person at the District Tax Office. A Tax Representative is required for non-EU entities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must apply for a Cyprus Tax Identification Code (TIC) through the Tax Department by completing Form TD-2000(Registration for Companies/EU/Non-EU), submitting certified constitutional documents, passport copies, proof of registered address, and, where applicable, obtaining a Cyprus VAT registration number. Processing occurs through the ARIADNE portal or in-person at the District Tax Office. A Tax Representative is required for non-EU entities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists; taxation is based on legal form and residency. Options include deemed dividend distribution at 17% under Section 30(1) of the Income Tax Law, group relief elections, and joint VAT registration for non-established entities. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) apply but are mandatory, not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 annual taxable turnover (for Cyprus-established businesses); non-established foreign entities have mandatory registration without turnover threshold. Intra-Community acquisitions: €35,000 and distance selling threshold: €10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to the physical export of goods to destinations outside the European Union where ownership has transferred and the goods are dispatched from Cyprus. For goods supplied to EU business customers for removal from Cyprus, zero-rating applies if the supply is an intra-Community supply evidenced by the customer's VAT number and valid delivery documentation. Offshore services supplied to non-EU customers may qualify for zero-rating under place-of-supply rules.

  • GBP (Customs Export Declaration) / Single Administrative Document (SAD)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airway Bill (AWB)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Proof of Payment / Inward Remittance Certificate (where applicable)
  • EU Movement Reference Number (MRN) issued by Cyprus Customs
  • Supplier's VAT Registration Certificate and Customer's VAT Number (for intra-EU supplies)

Cyprus implements mandatory B2G e-invoicing (e-Resolution DPII/PEPPOL) but no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for B2B/B2C. VAT returns must be no-submission via ARIADNE; e-invoice data must be retained per EU Directive 2014/55/EU and Monitoring of Electronic Invoicing. Cyprus is preparing for EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA), including future e-invoicing obligation for intra-EU cross-border supplies.

Unsubstantiated zero-rating claims incur clawback of VAT plus interest at 4.35% plus 2% per annum, administrative fines under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law up to €10,000 per offence, and criminal penalties including imprisonment up to three years for fraudulent evasion. The Tax Department conducts desk and field audits comparing customs MRN data against VAT returns; missing proof of physical export results in re-characterisation as taxable local supply at 19% with retrospective amendments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a member of the European Union and uses the euro as its lawful currency. There are no general exchange-control restrictions on current account or capital account transactions. Both foreign direct investment inflows and dividend/profit remittances to non-residents are freely convertible. Cyprus applies the EU Treaty principle of free movement of capital, subject only to EU sanctions, anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing measures, and the residual emergency safeguard powers retained by the Central Bank of Cyprus. No prior Central Bank approval is required for profit distribution by a private limited company to a non-resident shareholder. Cyprus domestic law imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, although EU anti-abuse and substance rules apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender, compulsory conversion, or minimum repatriation requirements in Cyprus. Export receipts may be retained in any permitted currency, converted at any licensed financial institution, or used directly for working capital and trade settlement. For regulatory compliance, exporters must maintain complete supporting documentation, including commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, air waybills, and Customs declarations, available for VAT, corporate tax, and banking audit review.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for non-bank payment service providers. Only electronic money institutions authorized by the Central Bank of Cyprus or EEA institutions passporting into Cyprus under the Revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) may lawfully provide cross-border payment accounts. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA are not permitted to operate standalone for a Cypriot corporate account and must instead act through a licensed Cypriot or EEA banking partner as an agent or white-label arrangement. For a low-risk matcha powder distribution company, a regulated EEA EMI can provide efficient multi-currency settlement, but documentary trade finance instruments such as commercial letters of credit will still require a full-service correspondent bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital injections must be received by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA transfer using the euro clearing system. The payment should reference ISO 20022 purpose code 'FDI' for foreign direct investment, with supplementary remittance information stating 'CAPITAL INJECTION' and the corporate name. The receiving bank will require a board resolution, the share subscription or allotment document, updated share register, and identification of the remitting shareholder. If the injection is structured as a shareholder loan, the settlement message should use purpose code 'INTC' for intra-company payment and be supported by a duly executed loan agreement. All inward funds must be reconciled to the company’s statutory capital and accounting records to satisfy AML and CBC reporting expectations.Supplier payments for matcha procurement should be executed via SWIFT MT103 for cross-border commodity settlement, with MT202 COV for correspondent bank coverage where applicable. For European suppliers, SEPA Credit Transfer ensures same-day or next-day euro clearing. For larger or risk-managed transactions, a commercial letter of credit issued by Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank subject to UCP 600 provides documentary security, with settlement effected through SWIFT MT103 upon presentation of conforming shipping documents. Outbound payments must carry the appropriate ISO 20022 purpose code, such as 'TRAD' for general trade or 'AREX' for export credit-related settlement, and must be supported by the purchase contract, invoice, transport documents, and customs declarations.Plain vanilla deliverable FX forwards in EUR/USD, EUR/JPY, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CNH with EU/OECD licensed banksNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted currencies such as offshore CNY or INR where physical delivery is not permitted onshoreCross-currency swaps (CCS) between EUR and major trade currencies for longer-duration exposure matchingEuropean-style FX options under MiFID II for procurement-cost protection without upfront premium complexityFX swap combinations used for rolling short-term treasury working capital settlement

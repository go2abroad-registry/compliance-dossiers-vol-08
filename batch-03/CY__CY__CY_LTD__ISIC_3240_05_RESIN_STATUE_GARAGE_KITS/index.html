Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation;Territorial_Scope;No_Pass_Through_Election;No_Tax_Exempt_Zone — A Cypriot private limited company (ELF code HE77) is an independent taxpayer under the Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002). Corporate income tax applies at a uniform 12.5%. A tax-resident Cyprus company is taxed on its worldwide income; a non-resident company is taxed only on Cyprus-source income. No transparent pass-through or check-the-box classification applies to limited companies. Cyprus does not operate a free-zone or offshore regime that exempts manufacturing or trading profits from tax.

Available election forms

Cyprus has no US-style 'check-the-box' regime; classification is fixed by company law. Material statutory elections available to the resin-statue venture include: (i) VAT group registration for closely connected Cypriot entities; (ii) group loss relief election under section 14A of the Income Tax Law; (iii) notional interest deduction election on new equity introduced after 2015; (iv) IP box preferential election for qualifying intangible property income (80% deduction); (v) unilateral foreign tax credit election; (vi) partial special VAT apportionment or 'use and enjoyment' election where permitted; (vii) EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) and Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) elections to discharge VAT on distance sales of low-value consignments; (viii) written elections under the VAT (General) Regulations for margin/global accounting schemes where second-hand goods trade arises. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available: Cyprus applies the EU ATAD/Hybrid Matching rules mechanically, and the domestic law does not confer elective intra-EU mismatch carve-outs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover is the compulsory registration threshold for taxable persons established in Cyprus. For non-established (foreign) persons there is no exemption threshold: registration is mandatory before the first taxable supply to a Cyprus customer. EU suppliers selling distance goods into Cyprus trigger local registration at the EUR 35,000 distance-selling threshold, though the Union OSS removes that need below EUR 10,000 pan-EU distance sales. Non-EU suppliers may register under IOSS for imported consignments not exceeding EUR 150; otherwise the goods are subject to import VAT at the border.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to the export of resin statues where: (i) the goods are sold to a non-EU customer and are physically dispatched or transported by the supplier or a third party to a destination outside the EU; (ii) the goods leave Cyprus within the statutory period (generally 90 days from the time of supply, subject to satisfactory justification); (iii) the supplier retains complete documentary proof of export, comprising the SAD/MRN, customs exit confirmation, transport documents, and proof that title/ownership passed to the non-EU recipient; (iv) ancillary services directly linked to the exported goods (packing, transport, loading, freight forwarding) may be zero-rated where they are supplied to the exporter and the underlying goods qualify; (v) business-to-business services supplied to a non-EU recipient fall outside the scope of Cyprus VAT under the place-of-supply rules (Article 19 of the VAT Law transposing Article 44 of the Recast VAT Directive), unless an exception re-attracts the supply to Cyprus. Claiming zero-rating is a strict evidence-based entitlement: the burden of proof rests on the supplier, and the Cyprus Tax Department requires contemporaneous export documentation rather than post-hoc certificates.

  • Single Administrative Document (SAD) export declaration accepted by Cyprus Customs through the THUNDER system, including Movement Reference Number (MRN) and export item lines properly classified (e.g., HS 3926.40, 9703.00, 9701.90, or 9503.00 as applicable to polyresin collectible statues).
  • Commercial export invoice and detailed packing list describing item codes, quantities, unit values, weights, and destination; the invoice must state that the supply is zero-rated as an export.
  • Original or certified transport documents evidencing dispatch from Cyprus and delivery outside the EU: Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), CMR consignment note (truck), or courier/LPS shipping manifest with track-and-trace proof of exit.
  • Customs exit confirmation, ship's protest, arrival notice, or an official customs import/clearance document issued by the customs authority of the destination jurisdiction evidencing physical arrival outside the EU.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank credit voucher issued by a licensed Cypriot or foreign bank showing receipt of the export proceeds in the company's account.
  • Export sales contract/purchase order, certificates of origin (EUR.1/Form A where trade agreements require), and, for intra-EU zero-rated B2B supplies, a dated printout of the customer's valid VAT registration number plus the corresponding EC Sales List declaration.

Cyprus currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory national digital stamping, and no compulsory B2B e-invoicing for the polyresin statue sector as of 2025. Electronic filing of VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and Intrastat declarations is compulsory through the ARIADNI portal. Export declarations must be lodged electronically through the Cyprus Customs THUNDER system, generating the Single Administrative Document (SAD) and Movement Reference Number (MRN). Invoicing must satisfy the content and retention rules of the VAT Law (9-year record retention in electronic form). B2B e-invoicing is voluntary, but the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) legislative package will mandate structured e-invoicing (EN 16931) for intra-EU B2B supplies from 2030; the Cyprus Tax Department's 'Tax For All' roadmap foresees national e-invoicing infrastructure aligned with ViDA, so the taxpayer should architect its ERP (e.g., SAP/Odoo) for Peppol/UBL interoperability at the point of implementation.

Cyprus imposes no statutory exchange-control deadline, as exchange control was abolished in 1994; nevertheless, the 180-day benchmark governs internal treasury policy and transfer-pricing defensibility. If export proceeds remain unrepatriated beyond 180 days without valid commercial justification, the Tax Department may question economic substance, recharacterize transactions, and challenge the zero-rating claim. In the event fraudulent or inadequate export documentation is found: (i) the supply is retroactively treated as taxable in Cyprus, and VAT becomes assessable at 19% on the full consideration; (ii) default interest accrues at 1.5% per month or part thereof; (iii) fixed penalties apply for failure to file or pay (up to EUR 500 per return plus monthly late-filing penalties); (iv) reassessment may be raised within 5 years in ordinary cases, extended to 10 years where fraud or wilful default is established; (v) criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of VAT may result in substantial fines and imprisonment of responsible officers; (vi) the appointed fiscal representative and directors of the CY Ltd entity may be held jointly and severally liable for the unpaid VAT, interest, and penalties. The Cyprus Tax Department treats missing MRNs, inconsistent HS classifications, and circular banking flows as high-risk indicators and may suspend VAT refunds pending full verification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

As a full Eurozone member since 1 January 2008, Cyprus is bound by Article 63 TFEU on the free movement of capital. The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions: no exchange-control authorisation, no repatriation restriction, no obligatory conversion, and no prior-approval regime applies to dividends, loan repayments, or profit remittances. The only practical hurdles are bank-level documentary checks: for profit distributions to non-resident shareholders, the paying bank will ordinarily require audited or reviewed financial statements, a tax clearance certificate issued by the Cyprus Tax Department, a board resolution approving the dividend, and source-of-wealth evidence for the ultimate beneficial owner. With a 12.5% corporate tax rate and 0% dividend withholding tax applied at the statutory level, dividend remittance is cost-efficient and legally frictionless for a Cypriot private limited company.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. Cyprus law does not impose statutory timelines, surrender ratios, or mandatory conversion rates for export receipts. A resin-statue manufacturer may retain export proceeds indefinitely in EUR, USD, GBP, or another freely chosen currency in a licensed Cypriot bank. The only obligation is statistical: the Central Bank of Cyprus collects balance-of-payments data under ECB Regulation 184/2005, requiring banks to report cross-border payment details (typically on a monthly or quarterly threshold basis). No state claim is made on the currency itself.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου / Central Bank of Cyprus), operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank; the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) retains supervisory competence for investment firms, electronic money institutions, and payment institutions under the Payment Services Directive (PSD2).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. EEA-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions are legally permitted to passport into Cyprus under PSD2 and may provide euro IBANs for treasury flows. However, the Central Bank of Cyprus expects a tax-resident Cypriot company to settle its statutory capital, supplier payments, and tax obligations primarily through a domestic credit institution or a locally regulated payment institution. Offshore or third-country EMIs — such as UK-, Singapore-, or US-licensed fintechs without a Cyprus passporting registration — are not viable as primary clearing rails and are generally blocked from issuing Cyprus-based IBANs; they may be used only as secondary collection channels, provided balances are swept daily to the domestic operational account via SWIFT/SEPA. For preorder deposits collected via Stripe or PayPal, these processors are treated as regulated payment collections rather than bank substitutes; they should be wired out to the Cyprus operational account within a 24-hour business cycle to maintain clean audit trails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward share capital must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's regulated bank to the CY Ltd IBAN, with the payment purpose classified under the ECB balance-of-payments statistical code for 'direct investment — acquisition of share capital' and a narrative reference containing CY_LTD_ISIC_3240_05. Offshore or third-country EMIs — such as UK-, Singapore-, or US-licensed fintechs without a Cyprus passporting registration — are not viable as primary clearing rails and are generally blocked from issuing Cyprus-based IBANs; they may be used only as secondary collection channels, provided balances are swept daily to the domestic operational account via SWIFT/SEPA. For preorder deposits collected via Stripe or PayPal, these processors are treated as regulated payment collections rather than bank substitutes; they should be wired out to the Cyprus operational account within a 24-hour business cycle to maintain clean audit trails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cyprus TP documentation (Local File) is mandatory when the annual aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds EUR 750,000 for goods, EUR 250,000 for services/intangibles, or EUR 250,000 for financial transactions, provided the group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million (triggering Master File obligations). A Cyprus company below these thresholds must still prepare contemporaneous arm's length documentation for major transactions.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus adheres to OECD and EU substance concepts. There is no separate 'Economic Substance Regulations' statute for ordinary trading companies, but entities must demonstrate real presence, actual employees, premises, and management in Cyprus. The IP Box regime requires the modified nexus standard (qualifying R&D expenditure linked to qualifying income). Lack of substance may cause GAAR attack, DTA benefit denial, and tax authority recharacterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to EUR 3,400 per count and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; back taxes with interest at 3.5% per annum and penalty surcharge up to 100% of the tax understated.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for restitution of unpaid tax. Travel bans may be issued by the court; personal assets may be seized; imprisonment applies for fraud convictions.
Shell Company / Artificial Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) permits reconstruction of actual transactions, denial of treaty advantages, recharacterisation of income, and additional tax plus interest and penalties up to 100% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally targeted under 'ownership and control' assessments; civil recovery can attach to personal accounts; in egregious cross-border cases, a travel ban and anti-trust criminal action under 'serious crime' provisions may follow.
Anti-Money Laundering Infraction

Statutory sanction: Cyprus AML law (Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law) imposes fines up to EUR 500,000, forfeiture of proceeds, and criminal liability. Failure to update the beneficial ownership register incurs penalties of EUR 200 per day of non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal responsibility for money laundering, including imprisonment up to 15 years. Personal bank guarantees, property confiscation, and travel bans are routinely imposed in AML prosecutions.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department will adjust the taxable profit to the arm's length level, charge underpaid tax and interest at 3.5% per annum, and impose an administrative penalty of 50% of the underpaid tax (or 100% for gross negligence). TP documentation default penalties may be separately applied at EUR 2,000 per year.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally approve artificial pricing schemes can be held civilly liable for the tax adjustment in conjunction with the company; in deliberate cross-border manipulation cases, revenue authorities may obtain personal injunctions, travel bans, and criminal conviction for participating in a fraudulent scheme.

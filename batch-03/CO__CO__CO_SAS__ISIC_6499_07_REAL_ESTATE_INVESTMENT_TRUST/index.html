Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Colombian SAS; the bylaws may provide for deferred payment terms. No escrow account is required for cash contributions. For in-kind contributions, a sworn inventory and appraisal must be annexed to the incorporation instrument; if real estate is contributed, title must be transferred by public deed before registry filing. For regulated real-estate collective investment activities, capital subscriptions are held through a fiduciary administration contract under Decree 2555 de 2010.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax and Customs Directorate of Colombia (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales – DIAN), operating the MUISCA electronic portal (https://www.muisca.dian.gov.co) and the institutional portal (https://www.dian.gov.co).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a Colombian Tax Identification Number (NIT) by appointing a legal representative resident in Colombia and submitting the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) pre-registration through the MUISCA portal, attaching certified incorporation documents, a valid power of attorney, and the representative's identity documentation. The DIAN assigns the NIT and simultaneously classifies the taxpayer for income tax and VAT responsibilities; a SAS could obtain its NIT directly at formation under the DIAN's procedural rules with digital authentication.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must obtain a Colombian Tax Identification Number (NIT) by appointing a legal representative resident in Colombia and submitting the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) pre-registration through the MUISCA portal, attaching certified incorporation documents, a valid power of attorney, and the representative's identity documentation. The DIAN assigns the NIT and simultaneously classifies the taxpayer for income tax and VAT responsibilities; a SAS could obtain its NIT directly at formation under the DIAN's procedural rules with digital authentication.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not have a check-the-box or hybrid election regime. A SAS is a resident entity taxed at the entity level on its worldwide income. No transparent or pass-through election is available; taxpayers may only choose between the general corporate regime and the small-business 'Simples' regime when satisfying quantitative eligibility thresholds, which does not alter entity-level character for income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

COP 164,727,500 (3,500 UVT for tax year 2024; UVT value COP 47,065). Mandatory registration as a VAT-responsible person results from exceeding this prior-year gross income threshold out of taxable activities, or from importing goods, holding taxable inventory, or performing categories of services regardless of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible goods qualify for a 0% (excluded) VAT rate where they are physically shipped and covered by a registered customs export declaration. Services qualify for 0% VAT where the beneficiary has no domicile or establishment in Colombia, the service is effectively used outside Colombia, and a service-export declaration (declaración de exportación de servicios) is filed. Real estate leases and sales of Colombian real property are statutorily excluded from VAT (Art. 476, E.T.), not export-zero-rated, so REIT rental income is VAT-exempt but requires RUT registration for income tax withholding duties.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX)
  • Transport Document (Bill of Lading or Air Waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Operation Report (Informe de Cambio y Certificado de Liquidación de Divisas)
  • Validated Electronic Export Invoice (Factura Electrónica de Exportación with CUFE)
  • Service-Export Contract and Supporting Performance Evidence (for service export zero-rating)

Mandatory electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) applies to all VAT-responsible and income-tax-registered entities: each invoice must be generated by DIAN-certified software and transmitted to DIAN in near real-time via authorized web-service providers for automated fiscal validation and the issuance of the unique electronic billing code (CUFE/CUDS). Export invoices must bear the validated CUFE and reference the underlying customs documentation; digital stamping and QR verification are integral.

DIAN may audit export files retroactively for up to five tax years and claw back refunded VAT plus default interest when the documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or electronically untraceable; sanctions include (i) a 10% penalty for improper refund claims (Art. 647 E.T.), (ii) late-payment interest and monetary sanctions of up to 160% of the assessed tax (Art. 648 E.T.), and (iii) criminal liability of 48–108 months imprisonment plus fines of 260%–400% of the value fraudulently obtained (Art. 371-2 E.T.).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a free-float, market-based convertibility regime in which neither current-account nor capital-account operations require prior administrative exchange authorization. Current-account convertibility (dividends, profit remittances, service fees, interest and royalties) is unrestricted provided that the underlying foreign investment is registered in the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales (RII) of Banco de la República, the corresponding Declaración de Cambio (Form 1/Form 2) is submitted through an authorized intermediary ('intermediario del mercado cambiario', IMC), and local tax obligations (dividend withholding at source, ICA duties and corporate income tax) have been satisfied. Capital-account convertibility (equity injections, loans, unit redemptions, and property disposals) is likewise open but requires contemporaneous or ex-post RII registration and mandatory channeling through an IMC. The key remittance hurdles are procedural rather than discretionary: the investor must evidence the registered investment certificate, the corporate distribution minutes, and the tax payment/withholding certificates before the bank releases the wire; there is no ceiling on dividend conversion or prior approval quota.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Circular DCIN-83, exporters of goods and services must 'channel' export proceeds through an IMC or into a Banco de la República-registered foreign-currency compensation account (cuenta de compensación) within the following statutory windows: goods exports, six months from the customs shipment (fecha de embarque) date; services exports, six months from the contractual payment due date, or if none is stated, from service completion. For the nearshore software R&D and digital-customer-service revenue streams of the REIT, 100% of the foreign-currency consideration must be repatriated within that window. There is no fixed mandatory surrender ratio of USD to COP; the exporter may elect to sell the currency to the IMC (standard route) or deposit it in a registered compensation account, subject to quarterly reporting in the Declaración de Cambio. Failure to channel within the deadline, use of unregistered intermediaries, or late filing gives rise to administrative sanctions and pecuniary fines of up to 200% of the uncanalized value under the Régimen Cambiario.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia) is the monetary and exchange-control authority, exercising prudential and exchange regulatory powers jointly with the Comité de Cambios (Foreign Exchange Committee) and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia as the banking and AML supervisor. The binding exchange framework is set out in Ley 31/1992 and the comprehensive 'Régimen de Cambios Internacionales' implemented by Circular Reglamentaria Externa DCIN-83, administered and enforced by Banco de la República and DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) in their respective capacities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury or clearing rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Mercury, Payoneer, Airwallex, N26-type platforms) are not licensed as Colombian IMCs and cannot lawfully execute the 'canalización' of export proceeds, capital contributions, or cross-border payments required by DCIN-83. Using them for equity injections or service-export revenue would constitute an unregistered foreign-exchange operation with DIAN and Banco de la República enforcement exposure. Their permissible role is limited to de minimis operational subscriptions (e.g., low-value SaaS and platform overhead) funded from IMC-cleared COP or USD balances; such usage should be expressly excluded from the bank covenant list to avoid inadvertent exchange-control violations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Each foreign equity contribution to the REIT follows a strict five-step protocol: (1) pre-funding the entity opens an operational COP current account and, optionally, a registered USD foreign-currency compensation account with an IMC; the foreign investor pre-files Form 3 'Declaración de Registro de Inversión Internacional' with Banco de la República, including the projected capital amount and property acquisition plan; (2) wire transmission the investor sends a SWIFT MT103 from a non-resident bank account in the investor's own name to the IMC, quoting purpose code 2111 (direct investment for the acquisition of real estate) and referencing 'Registro de Inversión Internacional – REIT S.A.S.'; funds must not be labeled as a loan or as a third-party collection; (3) conversion and credit the bank purchases the USD at the official TRM of the business day, credits the net COP proceeds to the operational account, and files the Declaración de Cambio (Form 1) on T+0/T+1; if a USD account is used, the funds are credited to the compensation account and reported quarterly; (4) registration the IMC issues the Certificado de Registro de Inversión Internacional and the final RII registration must be completed within twelve months of settlement, failing which dividend remittances and capital exits are blocked; and (5) utilization the COP balances are transferred to the property-purchase escrow or paid directly to the notary/title vendor via domestic transfer, preserving an audit trail that connects the specific inbound wire to the asset acquisition.Outbound treasury flows settle through four principal rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 wires for dividends, intercompany management fees, software subcontracting, and vendor payments; each disbursement requires a Form 10/Form 11 remittance declaration, the underlying investment-registration certificate, distribution minutes, and withholding-tax certificates, with the bank selling COP against USD at the TRM through the IMC channel; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit (MT700 issuance, MT707 amendment) and standby LCs for imported fit-out, hardware, and equipment, settled via MT103/MT202 cover payments from the issuing bank's USD account; (iii) ACH and CUD (Cámara de Compensación Urbana) domestic clearing for COP distributions to resident Colombian shareholders, rent remittances, payroll for the digital-customer-service center, and tax payments to DIAN and municipal authorities; and (iv) compensation-account sweeps and telegraphic transfers for overseas property managers and foreign suppliers, subject to quarterly Banco de la República reporting. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary treasury or clearing rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Mercury, Payoneer, Airwallex, N26-type platforms) are not licensed as Colombian IMCs and cannot lawfully execute the 'canalización' of export proceeds, capital contributions, or cross-border payments required by DCIN-83. Using them for equity injections or service-export revenue would constitute an unregistered foreign-exchange operation with DIAN and Banco de la República enforcement exposure. Their permissible role is limited to de minimis operational subscriptions (e.g., low-value SaaS and platform overhead) funded from IMC-cleared COP or USD balances; such usage should be expressly excluded from the bank covenant list to avoid inadvertent exchange-control violations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance); official portal: Taxisnet (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy/taxportal/).

E-government filing portal

For the Cypriot private limited company (CY_LTD), the Registrar of Companies automatically issues a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon incorporation of the entity. For a foreign entity, the TIN is obtained by filing a written application (Form TIN 101) with the Tax Department, accompanied by legalized certificate of incorporation, memorandum/articles of association, proof of registered address, passport/ID/Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) declarations and Cyprus contact/tax representative details. VAT registration is conducted through Taxisnet on Form VAT 101 (or VAT 102 for non-established traders); the deductible VAT registration threshold does not apply to non-residents, and a VAT number in the format CY + 9 digits is issued. Foreign companies without a Cyprus establishment must appoint a fiscal representative (tax agent) where required to ensure VAT compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (i) group loss relief election for 75% group companies; (ii) tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping companies; (iii) IP Box regime election allowing an 80% deduction of qualifying IP profits (effective 10% rate); (iv) foreign permanent establishment profit exemption election subject to conditions; (v) dividend transfer pricing notional interest deduction (NID) election; and (vi) consolidation of VAT group registration election for closely linked entities. Hybrid mismatch treatment follows EU ATAD Directive (Law 35(I)/2019) and operates by default neutralization rules, not elective check-the-box.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover in Cyprus for supplies of goods/services; EUR 35,000 for intra-Community distance sales into Cyprus. For non-established foreign suppliers there is no local registration threshold if the supply is not otherwise subject to VAT in another Member State.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union qualify for 0% VAT under Article 44 VAT Law if the seller obtains and preserves evidence showing that the goods were dispatched or transported outside the EU. Conditions include: goods physically leave the EU within the statutory period (normally within 90 days of supply), exporter retains production, purchase, inventory and transportation documentation, a valid Cyprus customs export declaration has been accepted, and the sale is to a foreign buyer for consolidation in the export records. Intra-Community supplies to VAT-registered persons in another EU Member State may also be zero-rated upon valid EU VAT number and VIES declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD - Single Administrative Document) with Cyprus Customs Electronic Export System release confirmation
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill or other transport document evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercially executed invoice, packing list, and purchase/production records
  • For EU intra-Community supplies: EC Sales List (VIES) declaration and buyer's valid VAT registration number

As of 2025 Cyprus does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for B2B invoices. Invoices must be issued in compliance with VAT invoicing rules (Articles 59-66 VAT Law), and suppliers must submit periodic recapitulative statements (EC Sales List / VIES), Intrastat declarations, and VAT returns through Taxisnet. E-invoicing to Cypriot public authorities is mandatory via the Cyprus National Peppol Authority using the Peppol BIIIC/European e-invoicing format in accordance with EU Directive 2014/55/EU. Following the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) reform, accredited e-invoicing and transaction-level reporting will become mandatory for intra-EU supplies once implementing legislation is in force.

Cyprus does not maintain exchange controls, but export zero-rating is audit-scrutinized against the 180-day inward remittance benchmark and the complete SAD-transport-payment trail. If zero-rated exports are found to be unsupported or fraudulent, the Tax Department issues an additional VAT assessment at 19%, applies statutory interest (currently 1.50% per month or proportionate annual rate) and may impose administrative penalties up to EUR 20,000 under VAT Law; criminal prosecution for fraud, VAT evasion or false statements may result in imprisonment and unlimited fines. Directors' personal liability attaches to fraudulent VAT claims and clawback defenses require production of contemporaneous customs approval, transport and FX remittance evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Cyprus is a full Eurozone member; the euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no restrictions, prior approvals, surrender requirements, or repatriation timelines applicable to cross-border payments by a Cyprus private limited company. Full liberalisation has applied since euro adoption (1 January 2008) and all residual crisis-era capital restrictions were definitively abolished on 6 April 2015. Current-account payments (trade, services, royalties) and capital-account movements (equity injections, intercompany loans, dividend distributions) are unrestricted and aligned with Article 63 TFEU and IMF Article VIII obligations. Dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are free from Cypriot withholding tax and are not subject to any Central Bank clearance, although the paying bank will complete External Transactions Reporting for balance-of-payments statistics. Interest, royalty and services payments to non-residents are likewise free of Cypriot withholding tax. The only practical constraints are AML/KYC documentary duties and, for non-EU counterparties, sanctions-screening obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio in force in Cyprus. Exporters of ISIC 3240 silicone tactile fidget goods may retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds indefinitely in any licensed credit institution or EU/EEA bank, may freely convert proceeds to euro at market rates, and may maintain USD, GBP, CNY or multi-currency accounts without Central Bank notification or approval. The legacy Exchange Control Law regime that historically governed retention of export earnings has been abolished; only standard commercial record-keeping (invoices, bills of lading, customs export declarations, and platform settlement statements) is required as evidence of genuine trade for AML review.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), which concurrently functions as the exchange-control statistician and as the prudential supervisor of credit institutions and Cyprus-licensed Electronic Money Institutions under the Business of Credit Institutions Law and the Electronic Money Law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for EU/EEA Electronic Money Institutions that have lawfully passportised into Cyprus under the E-Money Directive and PSD2; such EMIs may onboard a Cyprus corporate for euro-denominated payment services and virtual IBANs. Low for offshore non-EU EMIs (e.g., UK, US, Singapore, Hong Kong digital banks) that possess no Cypriot EMI licence or EU passport; they cannot legally maintain settlement accounts for a Cyprus limited company and should only be used as Platform_Pay collection gateways (PayPal, Stripe, Amazon Pay) rather than primary treasury depots. A Cyprus-licensed EMI regulated by the Central Bank of Cyprus offers a higher compliance profile but remains inferior to a tier-1 credit institution for deposit safety, given the EUR 100,000 deposit-guarantee coverage applicable to banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections are executed via SEPA credit transfer for EU-based shareholders or SWIFT MT103 for non-EU shareholders, with remittance information reading: 'Share capital subscription – CY_LTD – Form HE32 allotment; BoP Code F6 Direct Investment – Equity Capital'. The funds are credited to the Operational Account or the dedicated Share Capital Deposit Escrow Account, and CY_LTD files Form HE32 (Return of Allotment) with the Cyprus Registrar of Companies within 60 days, attaching the board resolution and updated share register. No Central Bank of Cyprus approval is required, but non-EU shareholder transfers above EUR 50,000 will trigger source-of-funds documentation (bank statements, audited accounts of the shareholder, evidence of origin of wealth). Euro clearing is frictionless over TARGET2; receipts are immediately available.Outbound trade payments run through SWIFT MT103 for T/T supplier settlements in USD or CNY, SEPA Credit Transfer for EU vendors, and irrevocable commercial letters of credit (UCP600, via MT700/MT707) for high-value mould-tooling or bulk silicone procurement from Asia. Each disbursement is supported by a commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, certificate of origin, and customs declaration, with BoP purpose code S101 (imports of goods) or B008 (imports of goods for processing). Platform_Pay rails (PayPal, Stripe, Amazon) settle directly into the Operational Account via payout cycles and require no additional clearing infrastructure.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legally fixed minimum paid-in capital. The S.A.S. structure permits freely agreed capital; paid-up capital must be effectively represented by the subscribed shares and may be paid in cash, chattel (including container fleets), or other assets. No statutory escrow account is required for cash capital, but corporate bank account opening is required to operationalize funds.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and civil-defense certification (Certificado de Condiciones de Seguridad Humana) issued by the local Cuerpo de Bomberos; structural load capacity inspection of storage racks and loading zones by a licensed architect or engineering inspector; occupational health and safety clearance (Ministerio del Trabajo) through registration of the company's ARL affiliate status and compliance with Resolución 0312 de 2019 on minimum safety standards; municipal occupancy permit (Licencia de Ocupación) and, if employees are hired, a NSC-risk assessment and workplace medical evaluation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating exchange rate with full convertibility of the Colombian peso (COP) for current-account transactions and for capital-account operations that are channeled through an authorized foreign-exchange intermediary (intermediario del mercado cambiario). Profit remittances (dividends) are freely transferable once the underlying foreign investment has been registered with DIAN and the corresponding Declaración de Cambio is filed at the time of channeling; no central-bank approval is required for ordinary dividend flows. The practical hurdles are mandatory channeling of proceeds arising from cross-border container leasing (treated as exports of services), full documentary traceability of each FX operation, the six-month repatriation timeline, and the dividend withholding and income-tax obligations that must be settled before external transfer. There are no quantitative convertibility ceilings for service exports; limitations are administrative and documentary rather than volumetric.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For exports of goods, the entire export proceeds must be surrendered through the Colombian foreign-exchange market within six (6) months counted from the date of the numbered customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación). For exports of services, including cross-border container leasing, the same six-month rule runs from the completion of the service as evidenced by the lease invoice and the master lease agreement. A mandatory 100% surrender requirement applies, with no statutory percentage exemption; all proceeds must be credited to a local account of the S.A.S. through an authorized intermediary and reported on the corresponding Declaración de Cambio (service-export code). Unsurrendered balances, late channeling, or channeling through unauthorized vehicles constitute exchange infractions (infracciones cambiarias) which DIAN sanctions with fines proportional to the amount involved. An exporter may apply to DIAN for a justified extension of the six-month term or may maintain an authorized compensation account (cuenta de compensación) allowing temporary offshore retention subject to eventual repatriation and surrender.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Bogotá) as the central bank and monetary authority, together with the Unidad Administrativa Especial Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) as the enforcement and registry authority for the Colombian Foreign Exchange Regime (Régimen de Cambios Internacionales). The regime is compiled in the Banco de la República Circular Reglamentaria Externa DCIN-83 (as amended), and is supplemented by Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, Decree 1065 of 1999, and Decree 1191 of 2023 with respect to the filing of foreign investment registrations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail and Medium only as a supplementary corridor. Colombia has no standalone electronic-money institution (EMI) licence assimilable to a European EMI; non-resident digital banks and FX platforms (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, and similar) cannot act as authorized intermediaries of the Colombian foreign-exchange market and cannot open COP clearing accounts for a Colombian S.A.S. unless they operate through a licensed local bank under a sponsoring arrangement. Their practical utility is limited to collecting USD lease receivables from foreign lessees and centralizing funds offshore, provided each underlying flow is later formally channeled and documented in Colombia. A Colombian S.A.S. must not rely on an EMI for the statutory Declaración de Cambio or for inbound capital contributions; such funds must pass through a bank supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) is a non-resident entity domiciled in a jurisdiction with low tax transparency or in the Colombian and global enhanced-risk watchlist, such as certain Caribbean or Central American financial secrecy centers.

Root cause: Ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) is a non-resident entity domiciled in a jurisdiction with low tax transparency or in the Colombian and global enhanced-risk watchlist, such as certain Caribbean or Central American financial secrecy centers.

Defense evidence: Apostilled and sworn-translated corporate registry extracts of every entity in the ownership chain; tax residence certificates issued by the respective tax authorities; passport pages and proof of residential address of each UBO; a notarized declaration of source of wealth and source of funds; the S.A.S. bylaws evidencing a single, clearly traceable ownership chain; and a written memorandum explaining the economic rationale of the cross-border container leasing activity.
Shell indicators: no local payroll, no physical office, registered-agent address only, and initial capital proposed to be funded by third parties, family trusts, or an unrelated offshore account.

Root cause: Shell indicators: no local payroll, no physical office, registered-agent address only, and initial capital proposed to be funded by third parties, family trusts, or an unrelated offshore account.

Defense evidence: Registered commercial office lease in Colombia plus proof of occupancy; container fleet register with serial numbers, purchase invoices, and insurance certificates; master lease agreements with international lessees and depot suppliers; accountant-reviewed or audited financial statements evidencing operational substance; Colombian tax-filing receipts (RUT, ICA, VAT) demonstrating taxable activity; and a shareholder resolution confirming that the initial capital contribution will be remitted from the UBO's own same-named bank account.
Trade-based money-laundering red flags: round-trip USD flows, unusually high container purchase prices from offshore vendors, or payments to third-party forwarding agents unsupported by transport documentation.

Root cause: Trade-based money-laundering red flags: round-trip USD flows, unusually high container purchase prices from offshore vendors, or payments to third-party forwarding agents unsupported by transport documentation.

Defense evidence: Original purchase contracts, LC or MT103 wire evidence, Bills of Lading and delivery notes, container handover certificates identifying box numbers and geographic locations, independent valuation documentation for the leased equipment, KYC/AML questionnaires for each foreign vendor, SWIFT/OFAC sanctions screening results, and a treasury policy requiring all fleet acquisition payments to be made only to the stated supplier's same-named bank account.
Sanctions exposure through counterparties in Venezuela-linked shipping, depot, or transport supply chains, or any party subject to US, EU, UN, or Colombian restrictive measures.

Root cause: Sanctions exposure through counterparties in Venezuela-linked shipping, depot, or transport supply chains, or any party subject to US, EU, UN, or Colombian restrictive measures.

Defense evidence: Automated counterparty screening against OFAC, UN, EU, and Colombian lists; sanctions-negative confirmations from every lessee and vendor embedded in the lease agreements; a formal denial-of-service policy of the S.A.S.; a compliance manual approved by the bank; and the provision of the latest sanctions screening reports to the onboarding compliance officer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder must remit the initial capital or a capital increase by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's same-named bank account to the Colombian S.A.S. capital escrow account held at a Superintendencia Financiera supervised bank. The payment reference in SWIFT field 70 must state 'Aporte de capital social en sociedad SAS - Inversión directa internacional' (share capital contribution - foreign direct investment). The receiving bank channels the inward remittance through the foreign-exchange market, assigns the statutory direct-investment operation code (Direct Investment in Colombia), issues the corresponding Declaración de Cambio, and records the funds in the escrow account until registration formalities are completed. The S.A.S. must file the foreign investment registration with DIAN pursuant to Decree 1191 of 2023 and evidence the registration before any dividend or capital redemption remittance is made. All inward wires must include the S.A.S. NIT and the shareholder's identification number, and local counsel should ensure the capital amount is reflected in the statutory books and in the annual report filed with the Cámara de Comercio.For procurement of ISO shipping containers, container depot charges, and lease payments to foreign fleet owners, the operative clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 (gpi) wires in USD or EUR for direct supplier and lessor settlements, with each payment converted from COP through the authorized bank and covered by a Declaración de Cambio; and (ii) commercial letters of credit (MT700) for container purchases above established thresholds, with documents comprising invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and the container purchase contract. The S.A.S. may maintain a USD-denominated foreign-currency account (cuenta en divisas) at its local bank to settle trade liabilities originally contracted in USD without repeated conversion. Domestic operating expenses in COP are executed through the ACH/PSE rails. For recurring lease payments to an offshore fleet manager or related party, funds must flow through authorized intermediaries with proper operation codes, and complete transport, service, and transaction documentation must be retained to satisfy DIAN audits and transfer-pricing rules.Deliverable USD/COP forward contracts (contratos a plazo) executed exclusively for hedging the S.A.S. commercial receivables and payable exposure with a local authorized bank within the Mercado Cambiario.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) with offshore dealers, settled in USD outside Colombia to avoid forced COP conversion, with documentary linkage to the underlying lease and fleet contracts and appropriate reporting to Banco de la República.USD/COP call and put options (opciones a plazo) with authorized Colombian banks or international dealers, used to cap conversion costs on non-USD lease income while retaining operational flexibility.Cross-currency interest rate swaps and plain currency swaps matched to USD-denominated container financing facilities, subject to external-debt or derivative registration requirements when the swap entails principal exchange or collateral obligations.Natural hedge strategy through a USD-denominated cuenta en divisas and matching of USD receivables against USD payables, minimizing FX conversion frequency and regulatory channeling friction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: COP 2,118,000,000 approx (45,000 UVT; 2025 UVT = COP 47,065), triggering Local File and Master File obligations.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) code of the UAE type. However, the SAS must demonstrate actual business substance, office presence, container fleet control, and local personnel. Economic substance is also relevant under Colombia's anti-abuse rules and DTA beneficial-ownership clauses; empty legal structures cannot invoke treaty protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Omission of income triggers a penalty up to 100% of omitted tax plus correction interest; criminal tax evasion carries imprisonment from 5 to 18 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable under the S.A.S. veil-piercing rule for manipulated corporate assets; travel bans, restitution orders, and imprisonment are available for intentional evasion.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failures

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the transaction, disallow the deduction, impose fines for missing documentation (up to 15,000 UVT per document), and recalculate taxable profit at arm's-length fair rental value.

Director personal liability: Directors sign TP documentation under penalty of perjury; knowingly facilitating false documentation leads to civil restitution and can be prosecuted as aiding tax evasion.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Violation

Statutory sanction: Colombian AML regulations impose fines, account freezing, corporate dissolution, and disqualification from public procurement; non-filing of beneficial ownership information triggers repeated enforcement actions.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal civil liability; serious money-laundering facilitation carries imprisonment up to 20 years, asset confiscation, and prohibition from managing commercial entities.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus (Tax Department); operating portal is the Cyprus Tax Portal, https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy (My Cyprus Tax / Taxisnet e-submissions). The VAT Service is integrated within the Tax Department. Exports are administered by the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department, Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate a Private Limited Company (Ltd, HE77) with the Registrar of Companies (ROC, https://www.companies.gov.cy) and obtain Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, plus a registered office address in Cyprus. 2) Upon incorporation the Tax Department automatically issues the statutory 8-digit Tax Identification Code (TIC / TIN), which serves as the corporate tax number. 3) For VAT, file Form TFC 101 (Application for VAT Registration) electronically through the Tax Portal attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles, director passports, registered office lease, and a business plan with projected turnover. 4) A non-resident company establishing a branch must first register the branch under Part XI of the Companies Law and obtain a branch TIC before any VAT or income tax registration. 5) Non-established foreign suppliers have a zero registration threshold and must register for VAT before the first taxable supply in Cyprus; a fiscal representative is required where the applicant is not established in an EU Member State and no administrative cooperation agreement exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Cyprus-resident Ltd is a company-level taxpayer on worldwide income at 12.5% under Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002; foreign permanent establishment profits are exempt under s.6 subject to conditions; dividends and capital gains from foreign participations benefit from the participation exemption; non-resident companies are taxed on Cyprus-source income only, giving the system a territorial-scope overlay; no pass-through or transparent classification exists for an Ltd).

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Statutory elections available to a Cypriot Ltd include: the Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital under s.9B (with 5-year carry-forward of unutilized amounts); group loss relief election under s.19 (donor surrenders up to 75% of taxable profit); the IP Box nexus election (80% of qualifying IP profit may benefit); election to exempt foreign permanent establishment profits under s.6(1)(c); unilateral Foreign Tax Credit election under s.32 rather than exemption; and VAT partial exemption annual adjustment election. Cyprus has enacted ATAD-aligned hybrid mismatch rules that constrain dual-inclusion and double-dip structures, so no hybrid mismatch election is available for imported mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 of taxable turnover in the preceding 12 months for Cyprus-established businesses (2025). Non-established taxable persons have a nil threshold and must register before the first taxable supply. The threshold for intra-EU distance sales to final consumers is EUR 35,000; the annual threshold for cross-border B2C telecommunications, broadcasting and e-services under the OSS scheme is EUR 10,000. Voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated treatment is available for a supply of goods dispatched or transported by the supplier, or by another person acting on the supplier's behalf, to a destination outside the EU, per s.15(v) of the VAT Law 95(I)/2000 (Art. 146(1)(a) of Directive 2006/112/EC). For dried goji berries and herbal botanicals the exporter must prove that the goods physically left the EU customs territory within 90 days of the time of supply (extensions possible where justified); the supplier must obtain and retain the customs export declaration and contemporaneous transport evidence within 3 months of supply; the commercial documentation must evidence the offshore recipient, payment terms and transfer of ownership; export-related services (freight, insurance, handling performed in Cyprus) may also be zero-rated where directly connected to and contractually embedded in the export supply; goods merely kept for exhibition or intended to be brought back into the EU do not qualify.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Single Administrative Document (SAD) EX/A with Movement Reference Number (MRN) lodged in the EU Export Control System (ECS)
  • Commercial invoice in the 10 mandatory VAT fields, sequentially numbered, referencing the export contract
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) / Airway Bill (air) / CMR or rail consignment note (land) proving physical dispatch from Cyprus to a non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advise evidencing offshore payment and FX conversion
  • Packing list and weight/list-of-contents statement
  • Phytosanitary certificate (EU plant health certificate under Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/2072) for dried Lycium barbarum (goji) and herbal botanicals
  • Certificate of Origin (recommended for buyer customs clearance and documentary credit banks)
  • Proof of export identity (exporter EORI number and customs party role)

No real-time B2B fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control or digital stamping regime is in force in Cyprus. Mandatory electronic filing applies: VAT return (Form TFC 211), VIES lists and Intrastat declarations must be submitted via the Cyprus Tax Portal by the 10th day of the second month following the tax period. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory under EU Directive 2014/55/EU using the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 standard. Export declarations are lodged electronically with Customs through the EU Export Control System (ECS) using the Single Administrative Document (SAD) EX/A, producing the Movement Reference Number (MRN). VAT invoices must satisfy Articles 122-126 of the VAT Law (sequential numbering, date, supplier and customer identification, description, quantities, net and VAT data); records must be retained for 6 years.

The Tax Department and Customs conduct post-clearance audits and cross-check the MRN, movement records and payment flows. If the 90-day export deadline or the proof-of-export bundle is missing, the zero-rating is retrospectively overturned and the exporter is assessed to VAT at 19% plus statutory late interest at 1.5% per month and default-assessment penalties. Fraudulent export zero-rating or false customs declarations constitute criminal offenses under s.39 of the VAT Law 95(I)/2000, punishable by a fine of up to EUR 50,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years, and under the Customs and Excise Law 94(I)/2004 false declarations expose the exporter to penalty assessments of up to three times the customs debt, forfeiture or sale of the goods, loss of EORI status and personal liability of directors; repayment claims and input VAT credits are clawed back where the underlying export is invalidated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a Eurozone member state; the euro is fully convertible and there are no residual exchange controls. Both current account and capital account transactions are liberalised under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the free movement of capital regime. Non-resident dividends, profit remittances, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred once standard corporate tax withholding and anti-money laundering verification are satisfied. The only substantive friction is the mandatory EU sanctions and beneficial ownership screening applied by Cypriot banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or fixed repatriation timelines for export receipts in Cyprus. Exporters may hold foreign currency proceeds in designated accounts without conversion into euros. For external statistics and balance of payments data, the Central Bank of Cyprus requires banks to report the purpose and counterparty of export inflows, but no permission or approval is needed for the receipt or disposal of the funds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border digital EMI accounts are legally viable in Cyprus only where the provider is an authorised Electronic Money Institution under E-Money Directive 2009/110/EC, transposed nationally through the Electronic Money Law 122(I)/2012. EEA-licensed digital EMIs that passport into Cyprus are rated Medium viable, as they may open segregated e-money accounts and issue GBP, USD, or EUR IBANs with pass-through settlement. Offshore, non-EEA digital banks and unlicensed EMIs are Low viability and cannot lawfully carry on payment services or establish bank accounts in Cyprus. Any offshore EMI arrangement would require a Cypriot commercial bank intermediary and would trigger enhanced correspondent due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is remitted through standard SWIFT MT103 messages from the overseas corporate bank to the Cyprus beneficiary account. The payment narrative in field 70 must explicitly state 'Share Capital Contribution' and reference the company's Memorandum and Articles of Association; the bank will request a board resolution and share allotment certificate as verification. For EUR transfers from EU/EEA members, SEPA-eligible wires are used. No Central Bank approval is required for third-country capital inflows; the applying bank classifies the funds as direct investment equity for balance of payments reporting and confirms the economic purpose before the account is credited.Outbound trade settlement is executed predominantly via SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for supplier invoices. Confirmed irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued under UCP600 are used for high-value bulk GOJI consignments where documentary risk must be mitigated. For euro-denominated payments to European counterparties, SEPA Credit Transfer is accepted. 12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Cyprus does not impose withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents, regardless of corporate shareholder status, provided the recipient does not maintain a permanent establishment in Cyprus. Cyprus-resident individual shareholders may be subject to Special Defence Contribution at 17% on dividend income, but non-residents are exempt.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate controlled transactions per tax year triggers mandatory preparation and retention of Local File documentation. A Master File is required when the Cyprus entity belongs to a multinational group whose consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code, but applies economic-substance requirements through the tax-residency test (management and control in Cyprus), the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD), and the Cyprus IP Box regime. Sufficiency of premises, employees, revenues, and business direction is assessed by the Cyprus Tax Department; artificial arrangements without genuine substance are disregarded for taxation and treaty eligibility.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at full 12.5% with 100% penalty on the under-assessed tax, default interest at 3.5% per annum, and criminal prosecution under the Cyprus Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law carrying a fine up to EUR 10,000 and imprisonment up to three years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or participate in concealment are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, exposed to civil recovery proceedings, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced when fraud is established.
Shell Company / Sham Conduct

Statutory sanction: Statutory strike-off or reversal of the Cypriot corporate identity, denial of treaty benefits on dividends/interest/royalties, retroactive taxation of profits at the ultimate parent shareholder level, and substantial administrative penalties for failure to maintain proper corporate records.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell entity are personally assessed for the underlying tax liabilities if the company is shell-struck, and the Cyprus Court may issue a declaration of fraud or misfeasance, leading to disqualification from directorship, asset seizure within Cyprus, and restraint orders.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Deficient Documentation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income to the arm's length price, additional Corporate Income Tax at 12.5%, penalty of 5% per month on the additional tax due, and fixed fines ranging from EUR 500 to EUR 5,000 for missing documentation; continued non-documentation attracts criminal fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve intentionally distorted pricing may be cited for aiding tax evasion, jointly liable for the adjusted tax, and subject to monetary penalties in cases of gross negligence.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law, financial and non-financial entities face fines up to EUR 500,000, temporary suspension of the license to operate, confiscation of transaction proceeds, and criminal penalties against natural persons including imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial-owner nominees face personal criminal indictment; individual fines can be up to EUR 50,000, with imprisonment for conduct committed with intent; the regulatory authority may also issue travel bans pending criminal investigation and void corporate transactions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: www.dian.gov.co; transactional portal: muisca.dian.gov.co. DIAN is simultaneously the national tax and customs authority.

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders incorporate the S.A.S. by private deed and file with the local Cámara de Comercio. Obtaining the Certificado de Existencia y Representación Legal is followed by enrollment in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) through the MUISCA portal, after which DIAN issues the definitive NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria); the same NIT is used as the VAT identification. Non-resident shareholders may act through a general power of attorney in Colombia, no minimum capital is required, and no Notary protocol is mandatory unless real property or regulated assets are contributed.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign shareholders incorporate the S.A.S. by private deed and file with the local Cámara de Comercio. Obtaining the Certificado de Existencia y Representación Legal is followed by enrollment in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) through the MUISCA portal, after which DIAN issues the definitive NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria); the same NIT is used as the VAT identification. Non-resident shareholders may act through a general power of attorney in Colombia, no minimum capital is required, and no Notary protocol is mandatory unless real property or regulated assets are contributed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; a S.A.S. is always a separate income-tax taxpayer. Available statutory elections include opt-in to the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) for small taxpayers with gross revenues at or below statutory UVT thresholds, registration as a Zona Franca user where statutory export activity conditions are met, and the ordinary corporate income-tax deductions subject to documented productive asset investment, accelerated depreciation, and hybrid mismatch limitation rules under the E.T.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold applies to a juridical person: S.A.S. VAT registration in the RUT is compulsory from the first taxable supply. For natural persons, the non-responsible trader exclusion remains available only below 3,500 UVT of annual gross taxable income (approximately COP 174.3 million for tax year 2025).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for zero-rated VAT if the goods physically leave the Colombian customs territory within the statutory deadline, the RUT records the entity as an exporter, the buyer is a foreign resident, and the foreign currency proceeds are repatriated through an authorized Colombian intermediary. For NVOCC ocean freight-forwarding services, zero-rating applies to the international ocean transportation and directly related offshore logistics segments, provided the service is contracted by a non-resident client and used or exploited exclusively outside Colombia; pre-carriage, terminal handling and local warehousing inside Colombia remain subject to 19% VAT.

  • Declaración Aduanera de Exportación (DAE/DEX) accepted by DIAN Customs through the SIDUNEA/MUISCA electronic platform
  • Commercial invoice, Master or House Bill of Lading, and cargo manifest evidencing the international movement of goods
  • Inward remittance certificate or Declaración de Cambio issued by an authorized Colombian banking intermediary evidencing collection of foreign currency
  • Service export contract and supporting documentation demonstrating utilization and economic exploitation outside Colombia for zero-rated offshore NVOCC services

All VAT-responsible S.A.S. entities must issue the Factura Electrónica de Venta using DIAN-authorized software or an authorized Operador Tecnológico. Each invoice is generated in XML, signed digitally, assigned a CUFE/CUDE unique code and QR code, transmitted to DIAN for validation before delivery to the buyer, and acknowledged through DIAN web-service APIs. Export invoices must be cross-linked with the customs export declaration and the corresponding foreign-currency exchange documentation.

DIAN may re-examine zero-rated export claims within the five-year limitation period; improper refunds are clawed back with interest, full indexation, and, in certain cases, a penalty for devolución improcedente. Sanción por inexactitud reaches 200% of the tax difference where the zero-rating position is based on inaccurate documentation. Fraudulent export invoicing and false customs declarations additionally expose the legal representative and the company to criminal liability, including customs-contraband offences and document falsification under the Código Penal, and may lead to exclusion from the DIAN refund program.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a free-floating, market-regulated convertibility regime. Current-account transactions (including service exports such as NVOCC freight routing) are freely convertible through the official foreign exchange market (Mercado Cambiario). Capital-account transactions are subject to mandatory registration and periodic reporting; however, profit remittances and the repatriation of registered foreign investment are permitted without a surrender requirement provided all filings are in order. Conversion outside the regulated market is prohibited and treated as a violation of the Régimen Cambiario.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Circular DCIN 83, service-export proceeds including freight-forwarding invoices must be received through an authorized local intermediary or, if credited to a foreign account, repatriated to Colombia within six (6) months from the date of service performance. Upon effective receipt, the foreign currency must be converted into COP within three (3) business days (or by the next business day if credited in a local foreign-currency account). Proceeds must be reported using Formulario No. 6 ('Declaración de cambio por exportación de servicios') and supported by the NVOCC invoice issued to the foreign principal.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit, under the statutory foreign exchange regime set out in Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN 83 (Sistema de Cambios Internacionales).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Revolut Business) are not chartered in Colombia and have no legal capacity to receive FX declarations or interact with Banco de la República. Their practical use is limited to contingency collection of small, non-core receivables from foreign freight clients. Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary settlement channel for sub-USD 10,000 transactions, but Low as a primary or statutory compliant treasury rail. Colombian AML Circular Externa 009 of 2011 places responsibility on the entity to identify and report transactions routed through unregulated channels, so all significant operational and tax-obligation proceeds must be settled via local authorized intermediaries.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from non-resident shareholders into the Colombian NVOCC must be routed to a local authorized intermediary via SWIFT MT103. The wire must reference the NIT (tax ID), the nature of remittance as 'Foreign Direct Investment', and assign the correct DIAN purpose code - C46 for foreign capital investment or C13 for foreign investment in local currency. For proceeds exceeding USD 500,000, a copy of the Foreign Investment Registration (Formulario No. 3) must be provided to the receiving bank, and the funds must be booked into a dedicated capital account before any operational use. Amounts up to USD 50,000 may be classified as ordinary service income and included in the obligation report; larger sums trigger separate registration with Banco de la República through the local bank.Outbound trade settlements for carrier fees, vessel charter, port terminals, and international logistics costs must be executed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for intermediary bank instructions) citing the underlying freight contract and Incoterms. Commercial documentary letters of credit (MT700) are viable when the NVOCC purchases a prior shipment or when a principal requires a trade-backed payment guarantee; however, the Colombian entity's primary disbursement rail remains SWIFT wire because most counterparty carrier payments are pre-agreed per booking and not LC-mediated. Each outbound wire exceeding the USD 10,000 threshold requires the filing of the corresponding foreign exchange declaration (Formulario No. 6 for service-related payments) and is subject to anti-money laundering screening under SAROFAC. 10% on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders where the profits distributed have been taxed at the corporate level at 35%. No further Colombian income tax applies for the foreign recipient. If the distribution is made out of tax-exempt or insufficiently taxed profits, the statutory rate may rise to 35% or be adjusted under the applicable DTA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year fiscal year ending December 31, the return must be filed during the DIAN’s annual filing schedule, generally falling in September-October of the following year. The exact deadline is fixed by the final digits of the taxpayer’s NIT, and in no case may it be earlier than 180 calendar days after the fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Colombian SAS must prepare a Local File and a Master File, and file the annual Transfer Pricing Informative Return (Form 120), if in the prior tax year it had gross assets or gross income equal to or exceeding COP 5,000,000,000 (approximately 100,000 UVT), or if it is classified by DIAN as a large taxpayer, or if it executes transactions with foreign related parties of any amount and falls into the threshold. Even below the threshold, all NVOCC transactions with related parties must be documented on an arm’s-length basis.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. Nonetheless, DIAN applies substantive anti-abuse principles, substance-over-form rules and the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) contained in the Colombian Tax Code. An NVOCC SAS is expected to demonstrate real economic presence: a physical office in Colombia, operational staff, active P&L and bank account flows, contractual booking documentation, and actual management decisions taken in Colombia. Pure captive or shell forwarding structures risk re-characterization, loss of deductions, and piercing of the corporate veil.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: DIAN inexactness penalty of 100%-200% of the omitted tax, plus moratory interest and criminal sanctions under Article 434-A of the Colombian Criminal Code: imprisonment of 48 to 108 months and monetary fines.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives, financial officers and directors with signatory power face personal criminal prosecution, travel bans, asset embargos, and civil liability for fraudulent management under Law 1258/2008.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalty of up to 15,000 UVT (approx. COP 750 million); mandatory re-pricing with an understatement penalty of 20% of the additional tax; prohibition of related-party deductions and default-interest charges.

Director personal liability: Administrators may be jointly liable for unpaid tax and penalties where they signed flawed documentation; DIAN can pursue director bank accounts and assets under solidary responsibility.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DIAN and the Comisión de Sociedades may disregard the separate legal personality and pierce the corporate veil under Article 42 of Law 1258/2008, imposing CIT and penalties on the beneficial owner; penalty up to 200% of under-declared tax.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and administrators become personally, jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts, fines, and anti-money-laundering non-compliance; a criminal finding of tax fraud may add detention and travel restrictions.
AML and Shadow-Freighting

Statutory sanction: Loss of the SIC/RUNT operational license and the DIAN import/export authorization; fines under Colombian AML law (UIAF) up to COP 1.2 billion; criminal money-laundering penalties of 8-22 years imprisonment; possible confiscation of the very vessel cargo.

Director personal liability: Directors, AML compliance officers, beneficial owners and customs representatives face investigation by the UIAF and Fiscalía; personal property can be seized; travel bans and imprisonment are possible for those who knowingly facilitated the illegal cargo flow.

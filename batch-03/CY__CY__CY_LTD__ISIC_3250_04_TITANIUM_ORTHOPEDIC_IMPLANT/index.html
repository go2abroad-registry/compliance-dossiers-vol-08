Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), headed by the Commissioner of Taxation; VAT is administered by the VAT Services Sector; company incorporation is handled by the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DROCOR). Digital infrastructure: TaxForAll portal (https://taxforall.mof.gov.cy) for VAT registrations, returns and filings; ARIADNE integrated tax-information system; THESEAS customs declarations system for export MRNs.

E-government filing portal

STEP 2: Upon incorporation the company is automatically issued a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN) in the format 12345678A (8 digits plus one alpha check character). STEP 3: If actual or projected Cyprus taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600 in any 12-month period, file VAT registration Form TFC 101 with the VAT Services Sector within 30 days from the end of the month in which the threshold is exceeded; an EU-established person may register voluntarily from the first euro. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Cyprus-resident company (managed and controlled in Cyprus) is taxed on worldwide income at a 12.5% corporate rate; a non-resident Cyprus-incorporated company is taxed only on Cyprus-source income. No pass-through rules apply to an LLC. A foreign permanent establishment of a Cyprus company is exempt from Cyprus tax if subject to a foreign tax of at least 12.5%; dividends from foreign subsidiaries are exempt under the participation exemption. No tax-exempt zone applies in the Republic of Cyprus. Branch operations of foreign companies are taxed at 12.5% on Cyprus-source income only; no branch remittance tax is imposed.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections relevant to a medical-device manufacturer: (a) group loss relief election for Cyprus companies linked by 75% direct or indirect ownership; (b) IP Box election granting an 80% deemed deduction on qualifying intangible property income (effective tax rate of 2.5%); (c) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity subscribed on or after 1 January 2015, calculated by reference to the 10-year Cyprus government bond yield plus 3 percentage points (capped at 11%); (d) VAT group election for closely linked taxable persons; (e) special VAT partial-exemption pooling election for mixed business/personal use; (f) tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping (not applicable to ISIC 3250); and (g) Special Defence Contribution (SDC) exemptions for dividends and interest paid to non-resident shareholders, subject to filing Form T.D. 615. Hybrid mismatch rules under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD, transposed via Law 34(I)/2020) restrict deductions for hybrid financial instruments and reverse-hybrid structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for supplies of goods or services by a Cyprus-established taxable person and for intra-Community acquisitions of goods; EUR 35,000 annual threshold for EU distance sales (remote sales) of goods imported into Cyprus; EUR 10,000 aggregate EU threshold for cross-border B2C electronic services under the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating for exports of titanium orthopedic implants applies under Article 59 of Law 94(I)/2000. Conditions: (i) the supplier is a Cyprus-established taxable person holding an EORI number; (ii) the goods must be physically dispatched to and removed from the European Union territory within an accepted evidentiary window of 90 days from the tax point; (iii) the supply must be evidenced by the customs export declaration (EX-A/SAD) released by Cyprus Customs and by transport documents (CMR, bill of lading, or air waybill); (iv) title and risk must pass to the non-EU buyer; and (v) the goods must not be diverted for EU consumption. For ISIC 3250, CE marking of the medical device is a regulatory rather than a VAT precondition. Input VAT directly attributable to the zero-rated export is fully deductible; where input VAT exceeds output VAT on a monthly filing, a refund is available through the VAT return without a separate refund application for Cyprus-established taxpayers.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX-A) with Movement Reference Number (MRN) issued through THESEAS
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill / Air Waybill / CMR transport document evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank account statement) evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export contract and Letter of Credit or other payment instrument
  • EORI registration certificate and, where applicable, CE technical documentation for the titanium implant

Cyprus does not operate real-time per-invoice fiscal clearance. The mandate is structured as follows: (a) B2B and B2C invoicing: a VAT invoice must be issued at the time of supply and, in any event, within 30 days of the supply for domestic transactions and within the same time frame for intra-EU supplies; (b) EBR/SAF-T mandate: the Tax Department requires submission of digitized 'Electronic Books and Records' (EBR) in SAF-T XML format using an accredited digital-signature certificate, with phased filing via ARIADNE/TaxForAll: for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2023 for taxpayers with turnover above EUR 2 million; 1 April 2024 above EUR 500,000; and 1 April 2025 above EUR 150,000 (other taxpayers may file voluntarily); (c) B2G e-invoicing: suppliers to the Cypriot Government must send structured e-invoices through the 'Kupo' Government e-invoicing portal (PEPPOL BIS 3.0); (d) customs exports: export declarations are filed electronically in THESEAS with Movement Reference Number (MRN) clearance; no digital stamping of export invoices is required, but SAF-T records must reference the MRN.

The Tax Department and Cyprus Customs jointly verify zero-rating substantiation in refund audits and ex-post compliance reviews. Export evidence must be retained for 6 years from the end of the accounting year; the retention period extends to 10 years where fraud, wilful neglect, or gross negligence is suspected. If the export documentation is deficient, the Tax Department recharacterises the supply as a standard-rated (19%) domestic sale, claws back output VAT together with input VAT deductions, and imposes late-payment additional tax (administrative surcharge of up to 10% per annum on the unpaid VAT) plus statutory interest. Fraudulent VAT evasion is a criminal offence under Article 63 of Law 94(I)/2000, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years and/or a fine of up to EUR 10,000; negligent misdeclaration may additionally attract civil penalties under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, including an assessment of up to 100% of VAT evaded. Retrospective assessments are permissible for up to 10 years in cases of fraud or omission. With regard to the 180-day currency repatriation convention, Cyprus abolished exchange-control restrictions upon euro adoption in 2008 and imposes no statutory forfeiture or clawback for late remittance of export proceeds; the 180-day benchmark nonetheless operates as a banking-covenant and anti-money-laundering surveillance standard integrated into SWIFT/L/C payment structuring for Cyprus exporters.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full member of the Eurozone; the Euro is the legal tender. Both current and capital account transactions are fully liberalised under the EU Treaty framework and the OECD Code of Liberalisation. There are no general exchange control restrictions on inward or outward capital movements, dividend distributions, loan repayments, or profit repatriation for non-resident shareholders. The residual emergency capital control measures adopted during the 2013 banking crisis were fully lifted by April 2015 and have not been reinstated. A Cyprus private limited company may freely receive share capital, shareholder loans, trade payments, and intra-group funding in EUR or any convertible foreign currency. Profit remittance via dividends is not subject to Cyprus withholding tax for non-resident shareholders, provided the company is tax resident in Cyprus, and no additional Central Bank approval is required. In practice, commercial banks apply standard anti-money-laundering (AML) and know-your-customer (KYC) checks to the underlying transaction purpose and source of funds, but no monetary convertibility cap or administrative approval applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation under Cypriot or EU law to surrender export proceeds or to convert export receipts into EUR within any timeline. A Cyprus-domiciled exporter of titanium orthopedic implants may retain export earnings in the invoiced currency (e.g., USD, GBP, CHF) or convert them to EUR at market rates through any authorised banking institution. Export proceeds must be declared for VAT, customs, and statistical balance-of-payments purposes via the Intrastat/Extrastat reporting system and the CBC’s external statistics reporting, but no forced conversion ratio exists. The exporter may also maintain multi-currency operational accounts with Cypriot banks or EU-based credit institutions, provided the accounts are reported for tax and annual financial statement purposes.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou, CBC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High with legal constraints. Cyprus recognises EU-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the Electronic Money Directive (EMD2). A Cyprus Cyrpus (CY) Ltd may use a licensed EMI as an additional liquidity or payment gateway for low-value trade transactions, contingent on the EMI’s passporting status into Cyprus through the European Banking Authority (EBA) register. Offshore EMIs not licensed or passporting into the EEA cannot provide regulated payment services in Cyprus and must not be used as the primary clearing rail. High-value orthopedic implant trade settlements (typically above EUR 50,000 per invoice) and any capital injection should be routed through a full-service Cypriot or EU credit institution to preserve banking relationships, Swift access, and LC issuance capability. Due diligence may require the EMI to demonstrate a valid Cyprus/EU licence, a local or EU branch presence, transparenttransaction monitoring, and the ability to provide audit trails compatible with the enhanced KYC obligations of the orthopedics sector.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection from a non-resident shareholder should be wired in EUR or USD via SWIFT MT103 to the Cyprus company’s designated capital escrow or operational account. The remitting bank must include a clear payment purpose code corresponding to the underlying transaction. For foreign direct investment in a Cyprus Ltd, common codes include: (a) EU Balance of Payments (BoP) classification for direct investment equity (share capital) – use the BOP code for 'Direct investment - Equity capital’ (EC) or the ECB’s specific code; (b) ISO 20022 purpose code 'COST' is not appropriate; instead use purpose 'AIA' or 'CFIN' depending on interbank practice; more granularly, banks require a narrative such as 'CY Ltd Share Capital Injection – ISIN not applicable – Private Placement – Direct Investment'. The Cyprus receiving bank will perform a source-of-funds KYC release and may request the corporate resolution authorising the share issue, a directors’ resolution approving the escrow account, and proof of the shareholder’s identity. Where the shareholder is a corporate body, the bank will require its constitutive documents, certificate of good standing, and a list of UBOs.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — official portal: www.dian.gov.co; virtual taxpayer office (Oficina Virtual) for RUT/NIT administration: muisca.dian.gov.co; electronic invoice validation and consultation environment: cfe.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian S.A.S.: (1) incorporate before the Cámara de Comercio and file the digital mercantile registration; (2) apply for the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) and the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through the DIAN Oficina Virtual or a physical service point, authenticating the legal representative's identity and contact data; (3) upon issuance, the RUT/NIT confers the status of IVA-responsible party, electronic invoicing subject, potential withholding agent, and foreign-trade operator. For a non-resident foreign shareholder or a foreign enterprise without a Colombian branch, a NIT may be obtained through a Colombian fiscal representative by filing the RUT with apostilled constitutional documents, board resolution appointing the representative, and a local notification address; no minimum capital applies to the S.A.S. All registrations are free of charge and the RUT/NIT is typically issued within one business day after incorporation records are integrated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the S.A.S. is a juridical person and Colombian tax resident assessed separately from its shareholders; there is no pass-through eligibility. The company is taxed on world-wide income, and non-resident shareholders are taxed only at source, including statutory withholding on distributed dividends. The S.A.S. is simultaneously an IVA-responsible party for consumption tax purposes and a withholding agent when statutory thresholds are met.

Available election forms

Colombia does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime; an S.A.S. is mandatorily taxed as a corporation. Available statutory elections include: (i) opt-in to the simple combined taxation regime 'Régimen Simple de Tributación' (SIMPLES) for qualifying small businesses (Arts. 903 et seq. E.T.); (ii) accelerated depreciation and elective 1-3 year expensing of productive fixed asset investments (Art. 104 E.T.); (iii) the exporter's election to obtain periodic refund or offset of VAT input credits under Art. 855 E.T.; and (iv) compliance with the domestic anti-hybrid and BEPS Action 2 rules, which disallow deduction/double-deduction mismatches without leaving taxpayer-beneficial hybrid elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

COP — No economic turnover threshold for juridical persons: the S.A.S. must register as an IVA-responsible party from its first taxable act regardless of revenue (Art. 437 E.T.). Only natural persons may qualify for the simplified non-responsible regime where gross annual income does not exceed 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 165 million for FY2024) and the remaining statutory conditions are met; this regime is not available to an S.A.S.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exclusion) applies under Art. 481 E.T. to: (i) goods physically exported from Colombia under a valid customs export declaration; and (ii) services rendered in Colombia to non-resident customers which are used exclusively outside Colombia, at arm's length consideration, with proceeds repatriated under foreign-exchange regulations. For the equity-crowdfunding S.A.S., platform fees invoiced to non-resident issuers/investors qualify for 0% only when the service is contractually and effectively consumed abroad (foreign platform users, foreign servers, foreign contractual counterparties); fees charged to Colombian residents generally attract 19% IVA unless a specific statutory exemption, such as the financial-services exemption under Art. 476(4) E.T. where the operator is subject to financial supervision, is available. Exporters may further claim refunds of VAT embedded in export-related purchases under the Art. 855 E.T. procedure.

  • Electronic export invoice (FEV with CUDS code) validated by DIAN evidencing the 0% rate
  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación - DEX) where physical goods are part of the transaction
  • Signed service agreement with the non-resident customer specifying scope, place of use, and market consideration
  • Inward remittance certificate and bank statement evidencing payment in foreign currency
  • Declaración de Cambio (foreign exchange declaration, export category, Form No. 5) filed with the authorized foreign-exchange intermediary
  • Substantiating records of exclusive foreign use (platform access logs, IP geolocation, client written attestation)

All IVA-responsible taxpayers must issue electronic invoices (Factura Electrónica de Venta - FEV) through DIAN-authorized technology providers or DIAN's free platform. The FEV is transmitted in real time to DIAN's web-service API using X.509 digital signature; DIAN validates the document and returns a unique traceability code, 'CUFE' for ordinary invoices or 'CUDS' for export operations, which is embedded as a QR code on the printable representation. No invoice is fiscally valid until DIAN acceptance is obtained; batch or offline issuance is limited to narrow statutory exceptions, and credit/debit notes and 'documento soporte' records are equally subject to electronic validation.

DIAN's assessment power expires after the statutory 5-year prescription period. Where the export tests are not met, the 0% rate is disregarded and 19% IVA is assessed on the gross consideration, plus monthly default interest under Art. 635 E.T. The 'sanción por inexactitud' under Art. 648 E.T. is imposed at 100% of the understated VAT; deliberate falsification of export documentation triggers criminal liability under Law 1762/2015, including imprisonment. VAT refunds previously obtained under Art. 855 E.T. on export inputs are clawed back with interest and the underlying input credits are disallowed; repeated improper zero-rating may result in suspension from DIAN's electronic invoicing and refund programs and disqualification of the legal representative.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a floating exchange rate and full convertibility for current-account operations. Capital-account inflows, including foreign direct investment and equity contributions from crowdfunding investors, are subject to registration with Banco de la República and must be channelled through an authorised foreign-exchange intermediary (closer 'mercado de cambios con intermediario'). Profit and dividend remittances are freely permitted when the original foreign investment has been duly registered and Colombian income tax obligations have been satisfied; no additional withholding tax is levied solely for remittance, but a fiscal certificate from DIAN and the relevant Banco de la República registration (Formulario 2 for dividends) are required. Practical hurdles include mandatory intermediation, strict documentary time windows, source-of-funds verification, and the need for local representation for tax and FX filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia does not impose a universal percentage surrender obligation for all exporters. Exporters of goods and services must 'reintegrate' (repatriate) the foreign-currency proceeds into Colombia through the regulated foreign-exchange market within six (6) months from the date of exportation or delivery of the service, except for exports listed in exception clauses (e.g., energy, coal, coffee) that carry special conversion rules. The equity-crowdfunding portal's service fees on foreign platforms are service-export proceeds and are subject to the six-month repatriation term; they may be held in a foreign-currency deposit account in a local bank if prior authorisation is obtained. During the current regime, there is no mandatory conversion of export proceeds into COP; however, the proceeds must appear in the exporter's bank records and in the corresponding foreign-exchange declaration.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) and Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (regulatory oversight), with exchange control administered under Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Colombian law does not recognise the EU/UK-style e-money institution (EMI) licence. A non-resident fintech or EMI cannot directly open settlement or operating accounts with Colombian banks or the national clearing/correspondent network unless separately regulated. Feasibility is rated Medium only where the foreign EMI partners with a Colombian licensed commercial bank, acts as a registered payment service provider under Superintendencia Financiera, and signs a formal agent/service agreement; otherwise, the operation is considered unauthorised money transmission and will be blocked by Colombia's banking system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections to the SAS or its escrow must be routed via SWIFT MT103 through a Colombian authorised intermediary (CAM). The wire must be addressed to an account held with a licensed local commercial bank and must include the foreign-investment concept code '310' (Principal of Foreign Direct Investment) or the specific code prescribed by Banco de la República for capital contributions made through equity crowdfunding. The sender and beneficiary must be identified with their full legal names, tax identification number (NIT if local, or foreign document), and the reason for payment referencing the registered Foreign Investment Application Form (Formulario 1). Prior to first receiving any foreign equity funds, the SAS must register the external investment with Banco de la República. The bank will apply enhanced KYC and may request the escrow agreement and SFC licence before accepting the inbound transfer.Disbursements from the platform to investee start-up business are cleared through (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wires to foreign beneficiaries and (ii) the local CUD (Cuenta Única del Sistema de Pagos) or ACH-ACH Colombia for domestic disbursements. Given that ISIC 6499 is an equity crowdfunding intermediary and not an import/export goods trader, traditional commercial letters of credit are not the standard instrument; funds are released to investee companies by direct wire after validation of disbursement triggers, using the appropriate FX concept code for investment abroad or 'disbursement from escrow'. If a particular investee requires equipment imports, a commercial LC may be issued through a Colombian bank, but such LC issuance will be subject to the underlying import declaration and the relevant Banco de la República indirect forex registration. A non-resident fintech or EMI cannot directly open settlement or operating accounts with Colombian banks or the national clearing/correspondent network unless separately regulated. Feasibility is rated Medium only where the foreign EMI partners with a Colombian licensed commercial bank, acts as a registered payment service provider under Superintendencia Financiera, and signs a formal agent/service agreement; otherwise, the operation is considered unauthorised money transmission and will be blocked by Colombia's banking system.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding rate on distributions to non-resident shareholders and foreign companies when dividends are paid out of profits already subject to the 35% corporate income tax (Art. 408-1 E.T.). Distributions from untaxed accounting profits are subject to a 35% withholding at the time of distribution. Resident individual shareholders pay a progressive surtax on dividend income above 300 UVT; however, the cross-border rate for the crowdfunding portal foreign investors is the 10% rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File, Master File, and the annual transfer pricing information returns (individual and consolidated e-forms designated by DIAN) are triggered when the S.A.S. carries out cross-border transactions with related parties and, in the preceding year, had either gross income or gross assets exceeding 100,000 UVT. For 2025, 100,000 UVT is approximately COP 5.1 billion (about USD 1.2 million). Transactions with entities resident in tax havens trigger documentation regardless of the monetary threshold, and a separate domestic related-party regime applies to transactions with Colombian related parties above 60,000 UVT.

Economic substance scope: Colombia has not enacted a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation regime, but equivalent substance requirements operate through: (i) the general anti-abuse rule under Art. 869 E.T., which authorizes DIAN to recharacterize transactions lacking genuine business purpose; (ii) the substance-over-form doctrine and legal personality disregard under Art. 42 of Law 1258/2008 for S.A.S. entities used for fraud or evasion; and (iii) the licensing and ongoing substance obligations imposed by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia on regulated crowdfunding platforms under Decreto 1357/2018, including the need for physical presence, qualified staff, governance, capital, and auditable operational records in Colombia. A crowdfunding portal styled as a mere invoice conduit or mailbox company will fail license review and be challenged by DIAN.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Gross Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment with moratorial interest; inexactness penalty up to 100% of the tax difference (Art. 647 E.T.); late-filing penalty of 5% per month capped at 100% of the tax (Art. 641 E.T.); criminal tax fraud charges under Art. 434A of the Código Penal carrying prison from 48 to 108 months and a fine of up to 100% of the assessed evaded amount; and asset seizure to secure collection.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and statutory auditor who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable with the company for the unpaid taxes (Art. 794 E.T.). Where fraud is proven, the DIAN criminal referral triggers personal criminal prosecution of the directors, freezing of personal bank accounts, travel restrictions while the process is pending, and, upon conviction, imprisonment. Piercing of the corporate veil under Art. 42 of Law 1258/2008 renders shareholders' personal assets liable for investor restitution as well as tax debts.

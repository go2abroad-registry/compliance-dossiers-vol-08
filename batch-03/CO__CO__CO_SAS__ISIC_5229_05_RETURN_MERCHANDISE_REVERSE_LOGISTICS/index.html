Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum subscription or paid-up capital applies to an S.A.S. under Ley 1258/2008. A token capital of approximately COP 1,000,000 (about USD 240) may be declared. Payment may be deferred up to a maximum of two years as provided in the bylaws. No escrow bank account is legally mandated; cash contributions are paid into the company's corporate bank account or contributed in kind and recorded in the books.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: Certificado de Cumplimiento de Normas de Seguridad Humana issued by the Cuerpo de Bomberos, covering extinguisher placement, emergency exits, and signage. Structural: engineering certificate or plan indicating load-bearing capacity and seismic compliance under NSR-10 (Reglamento Colombiano de Construcción Sismo Resistente). Occupational: implementation and maintenance of the Sistema de Gestión de Seguridad y Salud en el Trabajo (SG-SST) under Resolución 0312 de 2019, including ARL risk classification and annual work-plan reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box or hybrid pass-through election; the S.A.S. remains a separate taxpayer. Elective mechanisms include: (i) an election to the consolidated Régimen Simple de Tributación for gross revenue ≤ 80,000 UVT; (ii) Zona Franca / Operador Logístico certification conferring a 20% corporate rate and deferred VAT/customs treatment; (iii) designated Colombian Holding Company (CHC) election for foreign investors; (iv) foreign-tax-credit and advance-payment/installment elections; and (v) VAT refund acceleration via DIAN authorization as a 'calificado' exporter. Anti-hybrid rules under Law 2010/2019 override mismatching deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based registration threshold: every person engaged in taxable sales of goods or services in Colombia must register for IVA. A simplified-regime de minimis of annual gross income ≤ 49,000 UVT (approx. COP 2.44 billion for 2025) exists, but cross-border reverse-logistics operators issuing export invoices and claiming refunds are required to register in the full common regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible-goods exports are zero-rated under Article 481 E.T. upon valid customs exit of the goods. Cross-border services, including reverse-logistics, quality grading, and returned-goods management rendered from Colombia, are exempt at 0% where (i) the beneficiary is not Colombian-domiciled, (ii) the service is effectively used and benefited abroad, (iii) the provider is a VAT-registered common-regime taxpayer, and (iv) the service is not designated locally taxable. Services on goods in transit or located outside Colombia qualify if the offshore-use test is met.

  • DIAN export declaration (Declaración de Exportación/DEX) or the applicable simplified exit document for the returned goods
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or courier consignment note evidencing physical exit from Colombia
  • Electronic export sales invoice referenced to the DEX
  • Forex reintegration certificate issued by the authorized foreign-exchange intermediary
  • Service agreement/P.O. with the foreign beneficiary evidencing foreign domicile and offshore economic use

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance under DIAN's Facturación Electrónica de Venta framework: tax invoices must be generated as signed XML, transmitted via DIAN-certified web services/API or DIAN's free tool, and validated by DIAN in real time; acquirers must accept or reject each invoice within 48 hours. Unique CUFE/CUDS codes evidence fiscal authenticity; no physical stamping is used. Failure to transmit carries per-invoice fines and invalidates VAT deductions.

DIAN and Banco de la República systems cross-match export declarations, port/SIGEP logistics data, and FX reintegration records. Improper or fraudulent zero-rated exports are retroactively clawed back with monthly default interest and a 200% inaccuracy penalty (Art. 648 E.T.); fraudulent refund schemes additionally trigger criminal customs-fraud prosecution under Criminal Code Arts. 358/358-A (imprisonment 4–9 years), loss of export status, and exclusion from future VAT refund programs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed-float convertibility model. Current-account operations — trade-related payments, logistics services, profit remittances, and other services — are freely convertible and channelable through IMCs without prior administrative authorization, but each foreign-currency inflow or outflow must be settled via the mercado cambiario and evidenced by a Declaración de Cambio submitted by the IMC. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment (FDI) in the SAS, cross-border shareholder loans, and foreign guarantees, are also freely convertible but require registration with Banco de la República through an IMC as a precondition to effective profit-repatriation and capital-disinvestment rights. For a reverse-logistics SAS, profit remittance hurdles are procedural rather than prohibitive: the entity must hold a valid FDI registration, file annual financial statements, demonstrate distributable post-tax earnings, and comply with transfer-pricing documentation if flows involve related parties. The Colombian peso (COP) has a floating exchange rate; the official reference rate is the TRM (Tasa Representativa del Mercado).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Resolución Externa 8 of 2000 (arts. 79 et seq.), all proceeds from exports of goods must be channeled through the foreign-exchange market within six (6) months from the date of shipment; proceeds from exports of services, which include cross-border reverse logistics, return-merchandise quality grading, and related digital operations, must be channeled within six (6) months from the date the service is rendered/completed. Colombia imposes no fixed mandatory conversion ratio or compulsory surrender to the central bank: the exporter may convert the proceeds to COP at an IMC or retain them in a Banco de la República-registered cuenta de compensación (foreign-currency compensation account held abroad). However, the full and timely entry of the contractual export value into the regulation is obligatory; late, partial, or non-channeling triggers DIAN-imposed fines, loss of currency-market access, and potential criminal exposure for exchange-control evasion.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), exercising exchange-control authority through the Junta Directiva under the Estatuto Cambiario (Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, and Resolución Externa 8 of 2000, as amended). The Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia supervises the authorized foreign-exchange market intermediaries (Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario - IMCs), and the DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) enforces the exchange-control disciplinary regime, fines, and registration obligations.

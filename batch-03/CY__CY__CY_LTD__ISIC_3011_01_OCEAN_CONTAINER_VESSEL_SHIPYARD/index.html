Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; official Tax Portal at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company first incorporates with the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver and obtains a unique HE/company registration number. Following incorporation, the entity must register with the Cyprus Tax Department at the Tax Portal for corporate tax, VAT if taxable supplies will be made in Cyprus, and employer registration. Tax identification is unified under the VAT Identification Number / Tax Registration Number issued by the Tax Department; VAT registration requires submission of the company certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, details of directors, registered office, identity documents of shareholders and directors, and a completed VAT application form. Registration is processed by the VAT District Office on the basis of the declared business activity and projected turnover.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus law applies an entity-level corporate income tax without domestic check-the-box classification. Cyprus-resident ship management and ship-owning entities may elect the tonnage tax regime under the Merchant Shipping (Fees and Tax Provisions) Law, which replaces profits tax with a tonnage-based charge. For qualifying intellectual-property assets, an 80% qualifying profit exemption may be elected. A notional interest deduction on new equity may be elected. Group relief is available to Cypriot tax-resident companies with at least 75% direct or indirect ownership, and VAT grouping is available under the Cyprus VAT Law. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are mandatory and do not permit elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 in any 12-consecutive-month period for Cyprus-established taxable persons; non-resident and foreign-owned Cyprus Ltds engaged in taxable supplies within Cyprus generally require VAT registration irrespective of local turnover threshold, while distance sales of goods to Cyprus consumers trigger registration based on the EU-wide €10,000 threshold for cross-border traders or the specific supply-specific threshold set by EU/EEA destination rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available for the export of goods to third countries outside the EU, provided the goods leave Cyprus and the EU within the statutory period, the exporter holds the export declaration authenticated by the Cyprus Customs authorities, and the transaction is registered in the export records. For vessel and shipyard operations involving ocean-going container vessels, zero rating may apply to supplies of qualifying vessels operated commercially on international voyages and to services directly associated with the construction, repair, modification, or equipment of such vessels, provided the vessel is intended for commercial international use and sufficient documentary evidence is retained. The recipient company must be the owner or charterer, and the vessel must remain outside private pleasure use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / ECS export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent maritime transport evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and tax-compliant VAT invoice
  • Packing list and cargo manifest
  • Shipyard delivery certificate or vessel acceptance certificate
  • Evidence of the vessel’s registration for international commercial operation

Cyprus does not currently mandate real-time electronic fiscal clearance or digital stamping for VAT invoices. VAT returns and VIES listings are filed electronically through the Tax Department portal. Businesses are required to issue VAT invoices in a timely manner, retain electronic records, and comply with SAF-T (Standard Audit File for Tax) data requirements. The Cypriot authorities are aligning with the EU VAT digital reporting framework, but as of this dossier issuance there is no mandatory B2B e-invoicing clearance or real-time transaction-level stamping obligation beyond normal electronic VAT return filing.

The Cyprus Tax Department and Customs authorities scrutinize export zero-rating claims through document-based audits, cross-border vessel transaction reviews, and verification of the physical export and receipt of consideration. Where zero-rating is claimed fraudulently or without the required customs and maritime documentation, the VAT authority may claw back the zero-rated VAT, impose interest from the original date of supply, and levy assessments of up to twice the amount of VAT undercharged. Criminal penalties may include fines up to €50,000, imprisonment, and public debarment, especially for false declarations or falsified bills of lading and inward remittance certificates. Additionally, under the Cyprus Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, inaccuracies leading to tax loss carry penalties plus statutory interest and potential criminal prosecution for fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible in Cyprus for both current and capital account transactions. Cyprus applies free capital movement under Article 63 TFEU and the EU Capital Movements Directive (Directive 88/361/EEC), with no central bank prior-approval, quantitative limits, or withholding constraints on foreign direct investment inflows or outbound profit/dividend remittances. Practical conversion obstacles arise solely from bank-level AML compliance: any receivable or payment above EUR 10,000 is subject to source-of-funds and counterparty due diligence, and cross-border payments above EUR 50,000 trigger enhanced scrutiny, documentary evidence of the underlying commercial transaction, and ECB Balance of Payments statistical reporting. Profit and dividend remittance is frictionless for non-resident shareholders: Cyprus imposes no dividend withholding tax; a 30% Special Defence Contribution may apply only where the recipient is a Cyprus tax-resident individual or a Cyprus-resident company outside the exempt holding-company criteria, and the dividend must be declared through the Cyprus Tax Department portal for cross-border clearing. Corporate income tax on the shipyard entity is a flat 12.5%, and the vessel construction/export income may qualify for the tonnage tax regime under Cyprus Merchant Shipping (Tonnage Tax) Law, further simplifying treasury conversion modelling.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists in Cyprus. Shipbuilding export proceeds (including marine installment receipts under commercial letters of credit and escrow releases) may be retained indefinitely in EUR, USD, or any freely convertible currency without mandatory conversion to the local currency and without a statutory holding period. The only administrative requirement is statistical: the Central Bank of Cyprus mandates international transactions reporting (Balance of Payments / ITRS) for individual receipts or payments exceeding EUR 12,500, typically due within six business days after the value date, and banks will request the underlying contract reference. There are no mandatory conversion ratios and no repatriation timeline for export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating as the national competent authority within the Eurosystem, the European System of Central Banks (ESCB), and the Single Supervisory Mechanism for matters of banking supervision and monetary policy; the Exchange Control Act (Cap. 96) administered by this authority has been legally dormant since EU accession, with Cyprus having adopted the euro on 1 January 2008.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW-MEDIUM; not viable as a primary treasury or clearing rail, only as an ancillary seed-capital channel. Legal constraints: under Cyprus Electronic Money Law L.83(I)/2012 (transposing PSD2 and E-Money Directive 2009/110/EC), any entity issuing e-money or providing payment services to Cyprus clients must hold CySEC authorization or an EU/EEA passport endorsed by CySEC. Offshore digital EMIs lacking an EU passport have zero lawful onboarding pathway, and Cypriot Tier-1 banks will reject fund transfers from unlicensed EMIs due to money-laundering and sanctions exposure. Even EU-passported EMIs are operationally unsuitable as clearing rails for shipbuilding-scale payments because Cyprus correspondent banks treat high-volume EMI-conduits as elevated-unknown-origin channels, subjecting stage payments from non-EEA shipowners to prolonged KYC holds; EMIs also cannot issue letters of credit or escrow settlement structures. Recommended structure: use a licensed EMI solely for minor operational expenses and payroll in EUR, while escrow, LC settlement, and FX hedging remain with Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank.

Even EU-passported EMIs are operationally unsuitable as clearing rails for shipbuilding-scale payments because Cyprus correspondent banks treat high-volume EMI-conduits as elevated-unknown-origin channels, subjecting stage payments from non-EEA shipowners to prolonged KYC holds; EMIs also cannot issue letters of credit or escrow settlement structures. Recommended structure: use a licensed EMI solely for minor operational expenses and payroll in EUR, while escrow, LC settlement, and FX hedging remain with Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat corporate income tax rate under Article 21(1) of the Cyprus Income Tax Law, L.118(I)/2002, as amended; no municipal or local income surtax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Cyprus does not impose withholding tax on outbound dividends under domestic law.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year fiscal year-end, the TD2004 and self-assessment are due by 30 June of the following year. For a non-calendar accounting period, the due date is within six months of the accounting-period end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 – Company identity Legal name, registration number (HE), Tax Identification Number (TIC), VAT number, registered office address, contact details and authorized tax representative. A non-resident company with a Cypriot permanent establishment must quote the Cyprus registration/P.E. number and provide the local P.E. address; a non-resident without a Cyprus P.E. does not file TD2004 unless it has other Cyprus-source chargeable income. Late filing attracts a fixed penalty of EUR 100 plus EUR 50 per month or part of a month, up to EUR 500; late payment accrues statutory interest at 9% per annum (daily accrual) and may trigger additional recovery proceedings, asset attachments and criminal prosecution for persistent non-compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate value of controlled transactions triggers mandatory Local File preparation and annual TP disclosure; EUR 500,000 for related-party financing transactions; Master File may be required where the multinational group exceeds the relevant consolidated turnover threshold.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations filing regime analogous to the UAE, but economic substance is embedded in Cypriot tax residence, the 'management and control' test, CFC/ATAD rules, OECD BEPS substance requirements, and transfer pricing documentation. A shipyard entity must maintain physical premises, marine production assets, qualified personnel, local decision-making and genuine operational risk in Cyprus; otherwise the entity risks being treated as a sham or shell, with inherent tax adjustments and cross-border anti-avoidance consequences.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent income tax filing

Statutory sanction: Additional assessment of tax evaded, statutory interest at 9% per annum, civil penalties of up to 100% of the undercharged tax, and criminal prosecution under the Cyprus Income Tax Law and Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of a false return face criminal fines, imprisonment, confiscation orders, travel bans, director disqualification and civil restitution; the corporate veil may be pierced where the company is used as a façade for fraud.
Shell company / sham substance arrangement

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of expenses, re-attribution of profits to the real economic owner, TP adjustments, withdrawal of tax residence if management and control are absent, and penalties for incorrect returns; foreign shareholders may also be subject to CFC or anti-hybrid rules in their home jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors of an artificial Cyprus shell may be held personally accountable for wrongful trading and fraudulent invoicing; courts may impose restitution, asset freezes, travel bans, director disqualification and imprisonment where offshore structures are used to evade foreign tax or defraud creditors.
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The Tax Commissioner may adjust profits to the arm's-length level, disallow deductions for non-compliant transactions, impose civil penalties and interest, and issue assessments up to the statutory time limit; late TP documentation attracts separate fixed and daily penalty measures.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for knowingly incorrect TP positions are personally exposed to fines and prosecution for assisting in tax evasion; travel bans and seizure of personal assets may be ordered where artificial pricing caused significant tax loss.
AML and trade-based money laundering

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law, L.188(I)/2007, as amended: imprisonment and fines up to the statutory maximum for money laundering, asset forfeiture, and freezing of bank deposits and vessel-related proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal liability, monetary penalties, travel bans, international extradition risk, disqualification, and civil recovery; the corporate veil is disregarded where individuals knowingly facilitate laundering through the shipyard entity.

E-government filing portal

Step 2: Incorporation of the S.A.S. by private deed before a notary and registration with the Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce) of the port city, obtaining the Certificado de Existencia y Representación Legal. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The Colombian S.A.S. is a tax-resident corporation subject to national corporate income tax (Impuesto sobre la Renta y Complementario) on worldwide income at the 35% standard rate (2024/2025); there is no fiscal-transparency or check-the-box pass-through for an S.A.S.; foreign-source income is taxable with a foreign-tax credit; no territorial exemption applies. Dividends paid to non-resident shareholders bear 10% withholding tax (or 35% when the distributing profit was not subject to entity-level tax). If the operator were established inside a Zona Franca, a preferential 20% CIT rate and VAT-free imports would apply, but the CO_SAS is analyzed as a standard national port operator outside such zone.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists; entity-level CIT is mandatory. Available elections and statutory options: (a) 'Régimen Simple de Tributación' (ET Arts. 905-916) for qualifying taxpayers with gross income ≤ 100,000 UVT (COP 4,706,500,000 in 2024); stevedoring is in principle eligible but the option is barred if group turnover exceeds the ceiling or if the activity is excluded by law. (b) Export-of-services zero-rating framework under ET Art. 481 and Decreto 1625 de 2016: services rendered to non-residents and used exclusively abroad are treated as excluded from VAT with entitlement to input-VAT refund; the taxpayer reflects the election through the RUT responsibility codes and the bimonthly refund/offset application. (c) Election of 'devolución y/o compensación' of VAT 'saldo a favor' on each bimonthly Formulario 300 filing. (d) Election among DIAN free e-invoicing software, in-house software, or certified technology provider. (e) No hybrid-mismatch election: anti-hybrid rules of Law 2277 de 2022 apply mandatorily. (f) Tax Stability Contracts are closed to new applicants since 2019.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a market-based foreign-exchange system with full convertibility for current-account transactions, including trade in goods and services. Capital-account transactions, such as equity injections and long-term financing, are likewise freely convertible but must be registered with the Banco de la República (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales) within twelve months of the operation. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted once the S.A.S. files its annual income tax return and settles the 35% corporate income tax (at the rate effective for the applicable fiscal year) and the complementary gains and withholding taxes. The principal convertibility hurdle is not quantitative rationing but the strict channelling mandate: every foreign-currency receipt or payment must pass through an authorized intermediary using the official Declaración de Cambio system. Non-channeled operations, late registrations, or the use of offshore fintech wallets in lieu of local clearing expose the company to fines, regularization orders, and adverse tax treatment on unreported FX gains.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Resolución Externa No. 1 of 2018 of the Junta Directiva del Banco de la República, export proceeds for both goods and services, including stevedoring, terminal-handling, and marine cargo services supplied to international shipping at Colombian ports, must be received and fully channeled through an authorized foreign-exchange intermediary within a six-month statutory term measured from the loading/discharge completion date or service issuance date. The term is extendable to up to twelve months for justified operational causes with prior central-bank notice. There is no proportional surrender ratio; the full 100% of the invoiced value must be channeled, meaning it must be sold into Colombian pesos on the exchange market or credited into a permitted foreign-currency deposit account in Colombia. Failure to comply triggers administrative fines from the DIAN, mandatory regularization filings, delayed access to export financing, and a negative reputational record under the national AML/CFT regime.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), acting through its Junta Directiva, is the supreme foreign-exchange authority under Law 9 of 1991 and Decree 1735 of 1993. The Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) supervises declaration filing, penalties, and enforcement; the Ministerio de Comercio, Industria y Turismo and its Registro de Inversiones Internacionales coordinates foreign investment approval and registration, while the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia licenses all Authorized Foreign Exchange Intermediaries (Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Cross-border Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) headquartered outside Colombia, such as Wise, Payoneer, or Mercury, are not recognized as Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario and therefore cannot lawfully receive export proceeds, inject foreign capital, or disburse USD/COP trade payments on behalf of a Colombian S.A.S. Their viability for core treasury functions is rated LOW-FAIL. They may serve only as ancillary invoicing/billing concentrators with zero touch on the final peso clearing, and any attempt to route stevedoring revenue through such entities without a Colombian bank intermediary would constitute a breach of the régimen cambiario, subjecting the company to fines and reputational damage.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders wire USD or EUR via SWIFT MT103 from their own bank to the S.A.S. operational account at a Colombian authorized intermediary. The funds must be described in the payment message as 'Capital contribution to Colombian S.A.S. for stevedoring terminal operations'. Upon receipt, the local bank generates the Declaración de Cambio for foreign investment (Form for Inversión Extranjera Directa) and the investor must complete the Banco de la República Registro de Inversiones Internacionales within the statutory deadline. Clearance into pesos occurs at the market rate; the proceeds cannot be parked in an unregistered offshore wallet, and any use of an intermediary EMI voids the channelling requirement. Where the port concession requires a Patrimonio Autónomo (autonomous trust), the capital injection routes to a segregated escrow account at the trust's bank, which then funds the operating account based on the approved disbursement schedule.Trade settlement for imported machinery, spare parts, fuel, and subcontractor stevedoring services is executed through SWIFT MT103 wires, each matched to the applicable Declaración de Cambio for imports or services rendered. High-value equipment purchases (cranes, conveyor systems, hoppers) are settled through irrevocable commercial documentary letters of credit (LC at sight) advised via BANCOLDEX or a local commercial bank, combining supplier protection with end-to-end FX-regulatory traceability. Port-authority dues (dockage, wharfage, storage, terminal infrastructure fees) that remain inside Colombia are cleared through the local CUD (Cuentas y Depósitos) real-time gross-settlement system or standard ACH transfers to the concessionaire's account, with each disbursement cross-referenced against the port tariff invoice to preserve audit integrity.Deliverable USD/COP FX forwards (forwards) executed with authorized local intermediaries, limited to underlying trade inflows and outflows of the terminalCross-currency swaps (USD/COP) aligned with the tenor of the port concession loan or long-term equipment financingUSD/COP options and collar structures purchased from licensed banks for hedging volatility of stevedoring fee receipts denominated in USDFX forward-linked rate agreements to stabilize service revenue and personnel cost conversion ratiosOffshore NDFs permitted solely for non-resident counterparty transactions under a demonstrated underlying Colombian trade exposure; onshore COP NDFs are not lawful, and any NDF must be reported in the central bank's FX declaration for derivative operations.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Cross-border Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) headquartered outside Colombia, such as Wise, Payoneer, or Mercury, are not recognized as Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario and therefore cannot lawfully receive export proceeds, inject foreign capital, or disburse USD/COP trade payments on behalf of a Colombian S.A.S. Their viability for core treasury functions is rated LOW-FAIL. They may serve only as ancillary invoicing/billing concentrators with zero touch on the final peso clearing, and any attempt to route stevedoring revenue through such entities without a Colombian bank intermediary would constitute a breach of the régimen cambiario, subjecting the company to fines and reputational damage.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% (FY2024 onward under Law 2277 of 2022 for Colombian resident corporations)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders where the dividends are derived from Colombian-source profits already taxed at the 35% CIT rate. Where the distributed profits originated from tax-free or preferentially taxed income at the entity level, the additional dividend withholding is 20% on the untaxed portion. Dividends paid to resident corporate shareholders are generally not subject to additional withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return is due by the date assigned by DIAN according to the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT, typically falling between early April and early June of the year following the fiscal year-end (31 December). Filing schedules are legally published each year and must be checked for the exact due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Colombian Local File and Master File are mandatory when the taxpayer's gross income or gross assets exceed 100,000 UVT, or when controlled transactions individually or in aggregate exceed 20,000 UVT. For FY2024, 1 UVT = COP 47,324; therefore 100,000 UVT ≈ COP 4,732,400,000 and 20,000 UVT ≈ COP 946,480,000. The annual transfer pricing information return (Form 120) is filed when these thresholds are met or when DIAN triggers a formal notice. Transactions with parties resident in non-cooperative low-tax jurisdictions trigger documentation at a lower threshold of 10,000 UVT.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply the Anglo-Caribbean Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) to domestic S.A.S. entities. However, Colombian tax law contains a robust general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under Article 260-10 of the Tax Code and the anti-arbitrage rules introduced by Law 1819 of 2016. A non-resident parent holding a Colombian stevedoring S.A.S. without substantial board direction, operational staff, physical assets at the port, or demonstrated business activity may be disregarded or have transactions recharacterized for Colombian tax purposes. A terminal operator must maintain audited financial statements, board minutes, payroll, lease contracts for cranes and machinery, and evidence of day-to-day operational management in Colombia. CFC rules may also attribute Colombian terminal profits to the parent if the Colombian entity is treated as passive or lacks substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: DIAN assessment for the omitted tax plus a 100% inaccuracy penalty (sanción por inexactitud) of the tax difference and late interest. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Colombian Criminal Code provisions as amended by Law 1762 of 2015 carries imprisonment of 48 to 96 months and fines of up to 1,000 SMMLV, plus the confiscation of assets derived from evasion.

Director personal liability: A director who signs, approves or cooperates with a false tax return is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid CIT and penalties. Criminal proceedings trigger automatic travel bans and possible preventive detention; conviction carries custodial imprisonment and disqualification from managing any legal entity in Colombia.
Transfer Pricing Compliance Failure / Shell-Interposed Structure

Statutory sanction: DIAN may apply an upward adjustment to taxable income recharacterizing the transaction, decline deduction of the related-party expense, and assess additional 35% CIT. Administrative fines for failure to meet transfer pricing documentation obligations are applied under Article 260-15 of the Tax Code, in addition to the 100% inaccuracy penalty where the return misstates related-party results. Transactions with no commercial purpose may be entirely disregarded.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve knowing intercompany sham arrangements may be subjected to veil-piercing under Law 222 of 1995, personal liability for the unpaid tax debt, and civil restitution to the terminal operating company and minority shareholders. If criminal fraud is established, imprisonment and travel bans apply.
Unlawful Dividend Distribution and Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: The Colombian entity as withholding agent is personally liable for the withheld amount; failure to practice withholding triggers a fine equal to the amount not withheld (100%), plus interest and possible late-payment surcharges. DIAN may also reverse the deduction for the disguised fees and apply the 100% inaccuracy penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize unlawful distributions may be held personally liable to reimburse the company and the tax authority; in proven fraud they face criminal sanctions for illicit dividend distribution, travel restrictions, and seizure of personal assets.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Article 323 of Law 599 of 2000 (money laundering), conviction carries imprisonment of 6 to 15 years and a fine of 1,000 to 50,000 SMMLV. The port operating license is administratively revocable by the Superintendencia de Puertos y Transporte; vessel and terminal equipment involved in the crime is subject to seizure and forfeiture. Trade embargo procedures can freeze all bank accounts and operational funds of the S.A.S.

Director personal liability: Directors participate in money-laundering offenses are subject to criminal arrest, preventive travel bans, confiscation of personal assets, removal from the board, and civil tort liability for all losses suffered by shipping clients and the State.
Corruption / Bribery of Port Officials

Statutory sanction: Bribery of Colombian public officials under Law 1474 of 2011 and Criminal Code provisions is punishable by imprisonment of 5 to 8 years, fines, and permanent disqualification from contracting with state-owned port entities. The terminal operator may also be placed on the DIAN and Superintendencia Lists forbidding public procurement and port concession renewal.

Director personal liability: Directors implicated in corruption bear direct criminal liability without the possibility of limited-liability defense; they may face immediate arrest, travel ban, asset freezing, personal monetary fines, and civil damages claims by the Colombian port authority under Law 222 of 1995.

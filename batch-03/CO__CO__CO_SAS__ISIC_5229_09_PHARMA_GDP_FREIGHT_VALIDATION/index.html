Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for SAS; capital shall be freely fixed in the bylaws, subscribed and paid in cash or in kind within two (2) years from incorporation; no escrow or deposit account is legally mandated for SAS formation.

Funding & unlock path: Certificado de Condiciones de Seguridad y Prevención de Incendios issued by the Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos (fire brigade) under Ley 1575 de 2012; mandatory structural load inspection certificate issued by a qualified engineer for pallet and racking systems; workplace health and safety (SG-SST) program described in Decreto 1072 de 2015, Chapter 6, requiring the employer to file a workplace risk assessment, emergency evacuation plan, and occupational hazard profile; municipal usage certificate (Certificado de Uso del Suelo) confirming logistics use is permitted under the POT (Territorial Ordering Plan); and health conditions certificate (Certificado de Condiciones Sanitarias) from the local Secretaría de Salud for the operation and premises of a pharmaceutical good distribution practice warehouse.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – portal Muisca (muisca.dian.gov.co)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity must appoint a legal representative domiciled in Colombia, execute a notarized Spanish-language incorporation deed for the S.A.S., file for foreign investment registration (if applicable), and submit the registration application through DIAN's Muisca portal using an appointed registered tax attorney or public accountant. Obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), RUT (Registro Único Tributario), and then register for VAT and electronic invoicing prior to commencing taxable activities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entity must appoint a legal representative domiciled in Colombia, execute a notarized Spanish-language incorporation deed for the S.A.S., file for foreign investment registration (if applicable), and submit the registration application through DIAN's Muisca portal using an appointed registered tax attorney or public accountant. Obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), RUT (Registro Único Tributario), and then register for VAT and electronic invoicing prior to commencing taxable activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Colombia. A local S.A.S. is mandatorily taxed as a corporate entity under the general income tax regime; no unilateral pass-through election is available. Hybrid mismatch and aggregated-outcome rules under Law 2277 of 2022 and OECD Pillar Two implementation may recharacterize structures involving transparent foreign entities, but the Colombian S.A.S. remains an entity-level taxable person.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for corporate entities (S.A.S.); any entity carrying out taxable sales, services, or imports within Colombian territory must register in the RUT and become a VAT responsible (Régimen Común). The simplified regime threshold of 3,500 UVT per year applies exclusively to natural persons meeting all statutory conditions, not to corporations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are constitutionally zero-rated for VAT purposes. To claim the 0% rate on pharma freight logistics services, the service must be ancillary or directly connected to the physical export of goods, performed on behalf of the exporter, and rendered from Colombia. The exporter must demonstrate the goods physically departed Colombia under a valid export authorization and that the foreign currency proceeds were transferred through the Colombian foreign exchange market. Freight, storage, and GDP validation services supplied without proof of connection to an actual export remain dutiable at the standard 19% VAT rate.

  • Declaración de Exportación (Formulario Único de Exportación – FUE)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure
  • Commercial invoice issued as DIAN electronic invoice with CUFE code
  • Certificado de Liquidación de Divisas / Reintegro de Divisas issued by an authorized Colombian forex intermediary
  • GDP compliance evidence (SIC authorization or health ministry waiver, as applicable)

Mandatory electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta) with real-time DIAN validation. Invoices must be generated through DIAN-authorized software or the DIAN free web portal, contain a unique validation code (CUFE) or digital stamp (CUDE), and be transmitted to DIAN within the regulated timelines. For cross-border services, a non-resident electronic invoicing mechanism applies through DIAN's 'Régimen de Facturación Electrónica para No Residentes' where technically required.

DIAN can deny zero-rating and retroactively assess VAT at 19% with default interest if the export documentation is missing, the FUE is not correlated with the physical dispatch, or forex repatriation exceeds the statutory 180-day term without a valid extension. Fraudulent export claims attract fines of up to 100% of the omitted tax, increased to 200% for repeated or simulated export operations; criminal prosecution under Articles 402 and 424 of the Colombian Criminal Code applies for intentional tax evasion, with imprisonment of 5–9 years, and the responsible legal representatives of the S.A.S. are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian Peso (COP) is convertible on a managed floating exchange rate basis through the Regulated Foreign Exchange Market (MCR). Current account transactions, including cross-border freight, logistics, GDP clinical freight validation services, insurance, and financing fees, are freely convertible but require formal canalización via a Declaration de Cambio submitted through an authorized exchange intermediary. Capital account transactions (foreign direct investment, external loans, portfolio inflows) are convertible without prior exchange-control authorization but must be declared and registered with Banco de la República through the applicable Foreign Investment Registration system (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales). Profit repatriation is legally unrestricted subject to the completion of the registrations; dividends remitted to foreign shareholders are subject to Colombian withholding and dividend surtax but not to any exchange surrender or conversion tax. Tax clearance at source may apply to branch remittances (impuesto de remesas for permanent establishments), while a Colombian SAS distributing foreign-source earnings must observe the incremental dividend surtax rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Decreto 1735 de 1993, Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83 (and subsequent amendments), all exporters of goods and services, including ISIC 5229 GDP pharma freight and logistics validation services, must channel 100% of export proceeds into the MCR through an authorized local exchange intermediary. The proceeds must be credited to a local foreign-currency Cuenta de Compensación or a registered Cuenta del Exterior held abroad, within six (6) months from the date of collection; Banco de la República may extend the term up to twelve (12) months for documented working capital or receivables reasons. No mandatory percentage conversion into COP is imposed: foreign currency may be retained in a foreign-currency account, but the full amount must be brought under the Colombian exchange umbrella and reported. Failure to canalize exposes the exporter to fines under the Colombian foreign exchange enforcement regime (potentially up to COP 500,000,000 or a multiple of the unreported amount) and criminal/customs collateral consequences before DIAN.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia, acting through its Board of Directors and the Subgerencia de Estudios Económicos, together with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC), the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Tourism (Comité Nacional de Inversiones Extranjeras), and DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) as the enforcing agencies of the Colombian Exchange Control Regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a standalone treasury solution. A foreign electronic money institution (EMI) not registered and supervised by the SFC cannot legally offer transactional accounts, custodial services, or FX clearing services to a Colombian resident company; the reception of export proceeds in an unlicensed EMI account creates compliance risk under the exchange-channeling regime. Offshore EMIs can only be used auxiliary tools, and only when the underlying foreign-currency proceeds are subsequently repatriated and channeled through a licensed local exchange intermediary; the foreign EMI account itself may need to be declared to Banco de la República as a Cuenta del Exterior. The only fully compliant e-money structure is an SFC-authorized SEDPE (Sociedad Especializada en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos) or a licensed financial institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) The foreign investor wires USD/EUR via SWIFT MT103 to the Colombian SAS's debited account at the selected authorized exchange intermediary using the beneficiary NIT and account number; (2) the remittance must identify the purpose as 'capital subscription to Colombian SAS' (e.g., in the remittance reference field / CMND / 70 tags); the local bank will register the inflow in the Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional (Formulario No. 3) assigning the corresponding concept code for new direct foreign investment in foreign currency (not a loan); (3) Banco de la República processes the Foreign Investment Registration (Registro de Inversión Internacional) and the issuer must preserve the registration certificate for future dividend remittances, capital reductions, and liquidation payouts; (4) as a pre-condition, the bank will file a KYC dossier containing the notarized bylaws of the SAS, the Chamber of Commerce certificate, and apostilled identity documents of all UBOs. Procedure is completed within three to ten business days depending on the bank's compliance queue; proceeds may be converted to COP at the market TRM or held in a foreign-currency compensation account.Clearing rails permitted: (a) for services import and operational expense payments (e.g., sub-carrier freight, co-loading fees, software fees) — SWIFT MT103 via the authorized local bank, filing the applicable Declaración de Cambio por Servicios (Formulario No. 9) at the moment of the transfer; the underlying invoice must identify the INCOTERM, the GDP service description, and the freight/temperature validation deliverables; (b) for commercial import of goods (pharmaceutical inputs, packaging, medical devices) — Irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) called up in favor of the foreign supplier; the documentary package must include a commercial invoice, original Bill of Lading/AWB, certificate of pharmaceutical Good Distribution Practice compliance, certificate of analysis, certificate of insurance, and INVIMA-issued import sanitary permit; (c) domestic COP disbursements (labor, tax, local contractors) are cleared via ACH/Red CEDEC at Bancolombia or the corresponding bank, and for payroll, the compulsory 'cuenta de ahorro' mechanism must be used. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends paid from profits already taxed at 35% at the SAS level. Distributions out of exempt or non-taxed profits are subject to final 35% withholding plus 10% surcharge, resulting in up to 45% aggregate burden.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Calendar-year SAS must file and pay between the months of April and June of the following year, or per the DIAN annual schedule determined by the last two digits of the NIT; non-calendar year filers follow the fiscal year-end with the term set by DIAN.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and supporting TP documentation are mandatory when gross income or total assets exceed 100,000 UVT (approximately COP 4,241 million in 2023) and the SAS enters related-party transactions exceeding 45,000 UVT in an annual period. Master File and CbCR apply to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of €750 million or equivalent; all thresholds are inflation-indexed to UVT.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a separate ESR regime, but the GAAR, CFC rules and the constitutional 'sustancia sobre forma' doctrine require real premises, personnel, management, and risks in the SAS. A pharma GDP freight vehicle with no employees, no warehouse, no insurance, and only intercompany invoices will be re-characterized, deductible payments disallowed, and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion punishment under Article 434A of the Colombian Penal Code: imprisonment of 4 to 9 years and a fine of USD equivalent of 20,000 to 100,000 UVT; civil sanction from DIAN of up to 200% of the omitted tax, plus default interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives may be jointly liable for tax debts under Article 794 of the Tax Statute when the evasion is done with intent or gross negligence; civil restitution and travel restrictions apply; criminal conviction triggers imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Entity

Statutory sanction: DIAN disallows all related-party expenses, re-characterizes the entire income to the beneficial owner, applies 35% corporate tax plus recovery of VAT input credits, and imposes a 100% accuracy penalty; registration on DIAN risk list.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil permits fiscal agents, shareholders and managers to be sued for unpaid corporate taxes; prohibitions on leaving Colombia and disqualification from holding public contracts or directorship are imposed for serious tax fraud.
AML / Public Health Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under Law 1751 and Penal Code provisions on unlawful pharma trafficking: administrative fines of up to 1,000 SMMLV, license disqualification for 10 years, cancellation of INVIMA health registration, and money-laundering charges if the proceeds are laundered.

Director personal liability: Managers who knowingly approve false GDP certifications are criminally liable as principals; personal fines and civil restitution to the health regulator; prison terms of 6 to 12 years for trafficking of adulterated or falsified pharmaceutical products.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 10% of the value of related-party transactions not reported, plus a fixed penalty of up to 20,000 UVT for missing documentation; DIAN also adjusts taxable income by the unreported arm's length variance.

Director personal liability: The legal representative must certify the filing information; in cases of willful omission, directors face personal liability equal to the resulting tax deficiency and can be barred from serving as officers in regulated sectors.

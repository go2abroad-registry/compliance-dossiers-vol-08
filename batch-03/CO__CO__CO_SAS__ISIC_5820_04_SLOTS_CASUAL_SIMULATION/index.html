Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – Portal: https://www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

Foreign or locally incorporated S.A.S. entities must obtain a RUT (Registro Único Tributario) and NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by registering with DIAN. Registration is performed electronically via the DIAN portal using the 'Registro Único Tributario' application. For foreign remitters without a Colombian establishment, the foreign entity must appoint a legal representative in Colombia and submit certified corporate documents, proof of authorized signatory, and a completed RUT form. The NIT is the definitive tax identification for VAT, income tax, and customs purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign or locally incorporated S.A.S. entities must obtain a RUT (Registro Único Tributario) and NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by registering with DIAN. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Colombia. S.A.S. companies are per se corporate income tax payers under Article 12 of the Tax Code. No hybrid mismatch elective classification is available; the entity is directly subject to national corporate income tax at 35% (2023-2024 period), plus complementary taxes. The only election mechanism relevant to the structure is the option to be classified as a 'Régimen Común' VAT responsible party and to voluntarily register for electronic invoicing prior to mandatory thresholds.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general fixed turnover threshold for S.A.S. VAT registration; any entity engaged in the sale of goods or services, including digital gaming services, must register as a 'Responsable de IVA' in the RUT and in the 'Régimen Común' irrespective of turnover if the activity is taxable. The simplified regime threshold (UVM-based) applies only to natural persons, not juridical entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of software services and digital content published on foreign platforms qualify for zero-rated (0%) VAT in Colombia if the service is performed within Colombia but the benefit or use is realized abroad, the client is a non-resident entity or person, and the payment originates from abroad. It is also necessary to document that the income is considered an 'export of services' under the Customs law, and the supplier must prove that the contract, invoice, and payment evidence comply with Article 481 of the Tax Code.

  • Export Customs Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – only for physical media, if applicable)
  • Commercial Invoice issued under facturación electrónica with export marking
  • Exit proof for goods (Manifiesto de carga / Bill of Lading for physical media)
  • Service contract or service order demonstrating cross-border provision
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/commercial bank credit memorandum evidencing foreign-currency or external bank payment
  • RUT and NIT certification of exporter and importer

Mandatory electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta) is enforced by DIAN through Resolution 000165 of 2023 and Resolution 000012 of 2024. B2B and digital-service invoices must be transmitted to DIAN in real-time via authorized software or through the DIAN's free 'Facturación Electrónica' portal. Validation occurs through DIAN's 'Validación previa' API; the invoice receives a CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica). Digital stamping ('QR code' and CUDE for debit notes) and export invoices must integrate the software-type validation code. All gaming-service transactions to final consumers via IAP/Google Play are also subject to the general electronic invoice obligation unless an equivalent fiscal equivalent ('tiquete de máquina registradora') is authorized by DIAN.

DIAN has the power to deny or recover VAT refunds for exports if the required documentary evidence is incomplete or if the zero-rating claim is deemed fraudulent. Penalties for false export declarations or simulated exports include a penalty equal to 160% of the amount of any disallowed refund, plus moratory interest, and possible criminal liability for tax fraud under Law 1943 of 2018 (as amended by Law 2010 of 2019). For services, failure to repatriate foreign-currency proceeds within the 180-day term or failure to prove receipt of payment abroad can result in full reversal of the zero-rate benefit, application of 19% VAT, and VAT surcharges. DIAN also applies the 'anti-abuse rule' of Article 869 of the Tax Code to disregard transactions lacking economic substance, triggering retrospective clawback of refunds and fines up to 200% of the unpaid incremental tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is fully convertible on current transactions, including trade in goods, services, and profit remittances, subject to mandatory channeling through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, and financial credits, are freely permitted after registration with Banco de la República and compliance with tax and AML/CFT formalities. Profit remittance is lawful provided the underlying foreign investment is registered under the 'Registro de Inversiones Internacionales', the right to remit is evidenced in the company's accounts, and all corporate and withholding taxes applicable to remittances (including the 7.5% remittance tax where relevant) have been settled. No express prior exchange control authorization is required for dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders once the investment registration and tax clearances are in place.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombian law imposes a mandatory channeling requirement, not a full surrender requirement. All export proceeds in foreign currency must be channeled through the foreign exchange market within six months from the date of shipment for goods, or from the legally stipulated accrual date for services. The exporter may maintain the foreign currency in a domestic foreign-currency account or sell it to the market. There is no fixed statutory conversion ratio mandating that a certain percentage of COP must be obtained; however, proceeds must be repatriated to Colombia and credited to an authorized local financial institution before the deadline. Failure to channel within the term triggers fines, tax recomputation, and loss of FX benefits. For the software services and digital content publishing sector (ISIC 5820), the relevant term commences on the invoice date or contractual service completion date, whichever comes first under the provisions of External Circular DCIP-83.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit and the National Planning Department (Departamento Nacional de Planeación) for foreign exchange policy; operational FX control instruments are implemented by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia and the Comité de Inversiones Extranjeras.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium, structurally constrained. Foreign digital EMI or neo-bank providers are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Colombia and cannot directly access the local clearing system, ACH, or CIFIN/FINCA SAS AML clearinghouse. They may be used merely as a pre-funded disbursement layer for ease of API integration, but the primary treasury account of a Colombian S.A.S. with non-resident shareholders and export activities must reside with a locally authorized credit institution. Any entity performing local payment solicitation or collection must be licensed or registered with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia; unlicensed operation would violate the Colombian Financial Regulation framework. Use of EMI rails is viable only as a secondary, non-clearing treasury channel for outbound low-value operational expenses, with a maximum legal rating of 'Medium' if supplemented by a local commercial bank settlement account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment must be routed through a bank authorized to operate the Colombian foreign exchange market. The remitting entity should execute an international wire in USD or EUR using SWIFT with a beneficiary bank located in Colombia, beneficiary being the S.A.S., and a purpose-of-payment code corresponding to 'Inversión extranjera directa de capital en efectivo' (FDI equity injection) as defined in the Balance of Payments manual and Circular DCIP-83. The Colombian receiving bank will require a 'Formulario de Declaración de Cambio' on the date of the transfer, and the entity must present the foreign investment registration certificate issued by Banco de la República within the mandatory calendar time limits. Funds must not be fragmented into small payments; each wire must be individually declared and supported by the shareholder resolution, subscription of shares, or capital increase documentation. If the contribution is made by way of share premium, it must be identified as such and recorded to a capital surplus account.The primary outbound clearing rail for trade and operational settlement is the SWIFT network via MT103 wire transfer from the Colombian commercial bank, using the dedicated 'Formulario de Declaración de Cambio' and, where applicable, the payment code for import of software or IP licensing. For larger acquisitions, a commercial letter of credit (UCP 600) may be opened in favor of the seller; the L/C must be issued in COP or USD with SWIFT confirmation, and the Colombian bank will process the documentary presentation and settlement. Where the outbound payment is a royalty or services remittance to a non-resident, the taxpayer must obtain the corresponding withholding tax certificate and furnish it to the bank to evidence compliance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus — Cyprus Tax Portal (ARIADNE): https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: Incorporate the Private Limited Company under the Cyprus Companies Law, Cap. 113, by filing the Memorandum and Articles of Association with the Department of Registrar of Companies and Intellectual Property; upon incorporation the Registrar automatically assigns the Corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the company is entered in the Tax Department's taxpayer register. STEP 2: Register on the Ariadne e-tax portal and submit the company particulars (certificate of incorporation, registered office, directors, shareholders and correspondence address) to enable electronic income tax filing and future VAT administration. STEP 3: File Form VAT-101 (Application for VAT Registration) electronically through the Tax Portal when annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600, or when the company undertakes intra-Community acquisitions or distance sales of goods exceeding EUR 35,000, or where a VAT registration number is required to substantiate export refund claims or customs procedures; the District VAT Office issues the Cyprus VAT Registration Number in the format CY + 8 digits + 1 letter. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Cyprus permanent establishment, the usual procedure is direct VAT registration via a fiscal representative or direct registration at the Tax Department if a fixed establishment or customs import/export activity in Cyprus exists; where the foreign entity merely supplies B2B services or goods on a reverse-charge basis, no Cyprus VAT ID is required. The company must retain the TIN/VAT certificate and notify the Tax Department of any change in particulars within 30 days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. Cyprus resident companies (managed and controlled in Cyprus) are taxed at entity level at a uniform 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income; non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-sourced income and gains from immovable property located in Cyprus. No territorial remittance election and no tax-exempt zone regime applies to onshore commercial entities. Reduced effective taxation may arise from statutory deductions such as the Notional Interest Deduction (NID) and the IP Box regime, but the legal classification is always opaque entity-level taxation.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not provide a US-style check-the-box or pass-through election. Under Law 118(I)/2002 the available statutory elections include: (a) joint election for group relief; (b) election to claim a unilateral foreign tax credit instead of a tax deduction; (c) election for the Notional Interest Deduction on new equity; (d) election for the 80% IP Box notional deduction on qualifying intangible assets; (e) election to use losses against profits of another group company. Under the Special Defence Contribution Law 117(I)/2002 there is an election to deem profits as distributed and an election for tax credit on underlying dividends received. Hybrid mismatch limitations under Law 33(I)/2021 (EU ATAD) disallow deduction/non-inclusion outcomes; no elective hybrid entity classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover from supplies of goods and services; EUR 35,000 for intra-Community acquisitions of goods and distance sales of goods

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 40 VAT Law for: (i) exports of goods dispatched or transported by the supplier or on his behalf to a destination outside the European Union, provided the goods physically leave EU customs territory within the statutory proof period and the supplier retains the accepted Customs export declaration; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered customer in another Member State, provided the customer's valid EU VAT identification number is quoted on the invoice and the recapitulative EC Sales List is filed; and (iii) directly connected transport and handling services attributable to the export. For Chinese baijiu liquor exported from Cyprus bonded warehouses or free-zone storage to non-EU customers, 0% applies only upon documented physical exit from EU territory; retention of a customs-approved warehouse status pending export is not by itself sufficient unless re-addressed to export. The supplier must demonstrate the goods were exported unchanged in the same contractual state, the foreign purchaser is identifiable, and the export proceeds are traceable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD electronic export entry lodged in Cyprus Customs ECS)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or AWB/CMR (air/road) showing destination outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT wire confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and sales contract/purchase order
  • Proof of physical exit from EU customs territory (e.g., outward transit document T1, exit notification in ECS, or container/vehicle tracking report)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is currently in operation in Cyprus. E-invoicing is legally recognized and equivalent to paper invoicing. Statutory reporting is executed through periodic electronic VAT returns (Form F4 detailed return; Form F5 summary return) filed exclusively via the Ariadne Tax Portal by the 10th day of the month following each taxable month or quarter. Export declarations and INTRASTAT filings are submitted electronically through Cyprus Customs (TAXISnet). The Republic's Recovery and Resilience Plan contemplates phased mandatory B2B and B2G e-invoicing from 2025, but no centralized real-time clearance system (e.g., Italian SDI) is yet operational for the current compliance cycle.

The Tax Department and Cyprus Customs may retroactively verify export substantiation, and the legal burden of proving the export lies entirely with the supplier. Where zero-rating is disallowed or falsely claimed, VAT at 19% is assessed on the full consideration together with statutory interest at 1.25% per month or part of a month and a late payment penalty of up to 10%, with further accruals. Tax assessments may be raised within five years of the end of the relevant taxable period, extended to ten years in cases of fraud or wilful default. Criminal conviction for VAT evasion or fraudulent VAT return may result in a fine of up to EUR 50,000 and/or imprisonment. Additional consequences include withholding or offset of VAT refunds, customs administrative penalties, revocation of the required alcoholic beverages wholesale licence, denial of input VAT deduction for goods diverted to domestic consumption, and corporate income tax recomputation for misreported turnover.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a member of the European Union and the Eurozone. The euro is the sole legal tender and is fully convertible in both the capital account and current account. There are no foreign-exchange controls, exchange-rate restrictions, capital movement restrictions, or advance-approval regimes applicable to inward equity contributions, shareholder loans, operational trade payments, or outward profit remittances under normal circumstances. Profit and dividend repatriation is not subject to Central Bank of Cyprus approval; however, the paying entity must satisfy the Cyprus Companies Law distributable-reserves test, perform applicable tax compliance (including withholding taxes, if any, under domestic law or an applicable double tax treaty), and provide the receiving bank with adequate documentary evidence of source of funds and underlying corporate purpose. EU-level capital movement safeguards permitted under TFEU Article 66 may be introduced only in exceptional systemic circumstances, and none are currently in force.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation for a Cyprus tax-resident exporter to surrender, convert, or reinvoice export proceeds, nor is there a statutory timeline for repatriation of foreign-currency export earnings. Being within the Eurozone, Cyprus abolished all exchange-control surrender requirements upon EU accession in 2004 and upon euro adoption in 2008. A company may lawfully retain export proceeds denominated in USD, CNY, or any freely convertible currency in a designated foreign-currency account with a licensed Cypriot credit institution, or convert such proceeds into euros at the market exchange rate. The sole administrative obligation is statistical: exports and related receipts must be reported to the Central Bank of Cyprus in accordance with the EU Balance of Payments (BoP) reporting framework within the applicable monthly or quarterly reporting periods and thresholds prescribed by the Central Bank. Misreporting or gross under-reporting may trigger penalties under the relevant statistics and banking laws.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) / Kendraki Trapeza tis Kyprou

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Limited Payload Viability / High-Risk Concentration. Fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed or passporting into Cyprus under the Cyprus Electronic Money Law (Codification of Laws 2020) and the EU PSD2 framework (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, MultiPass, or Cyprus-licensed EMI rails) can provide efficient Euro-denominated online payments, immediate FX conversion, and fast interbank clearing via SEPA/TARGET2. However, EMIs are neither credit institutions nor banks in the full legal sense; they may not issue commercial letters of credit, provide supplier invoice financing, issue bank guarantees, accept term deposits, or hold client funds in a manner capable of supporting a capital-escrow relationship for the underlying Chinese baijiu purchase lifecycle. Therefore, an EMI can be used as a conventional payment overlay for operational expenses or minor supplier payments, but the export/import clearing spine must be a licensed Cyprus commercial bank. Offshore non-EMI payment providers and crypto-backed rails are not legally acceptable in Cyprus for cross-border trade settlement or for satisfying tax/audit historical transaction evidence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: A Chinese parent or Neobank shareholder shall transfer equity capital or shareholder subordinated debt to the Cyprus company's Capital Escrow Account through the SWIFT network using MT103 (single customer credit transfer) or MT202 (if passing through an intermediary). Prior to transfer, the Cyprus bank must receive a board resolution, opening application, and the UBO declaration in corrected format. After receipt, bank compliance will release escrowed funds to the operating account only after the funds have been screened and the purpose statement is matched against the underlying share register. Offshore non-EMI payment providers and crypto-backed rails are not legally acceptable in Cyprus for cross-border trade settlement or for satisfying tax/audit historical transaction evidence.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum statutory capital exists for an S.A.S. The capital is freely fixed. However, the subscribed shares must be paid in at least one-third (1/3) of their nominal value upon subscription. Contributions in cash are deposited in a Colombian financial institution and may be used after inscription. No escrow account is legally required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety clearance from Cuerpo de Bomberos (NFPA-adapted local inspection), structural load verification for office floors per NSR-10 (minimum live load 500 kg/m² for office use), and occupational health and safety registration with an ARL (e.g., ARL Sura) covering mandatory 5-day post-incident risk coverage. These clearances are triggered when a physical office is occupied.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum statutory capital exists for an S.A.S. The capital is freely fixed. However, the subscribed shares must be paid in at least one-third (1/3) of their nominal value upon subscription. Contributions in cash are deposited in a Colombian financial institution and may be used after inscription. No escrow account is legally required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — Colombia's national tax and customs administration. Main portal: www.dian.gov.co. Operational portal: MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) at muisca.dian.gov.co, used for RUT registration, NIT issuance, electronic invoice validation, export-of-services registration (RES), and periodic VAT/CIT filings.

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian S.A.S.: (1) Execute a private incorporation document ('documento privado de constitución') before a notary or via Chamber of Commerce digital channels; non-resident shareholders appoint a local legal representative. (2) Register the company with the Cámara de Comercio; obtain the certificate of incorporation and 'pre-RUT' (preliminary RUT). (3) Finalize the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) electronically in MUISCA within 10 business days of incorporation; DIAN then assigns the definitive NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria). (4) Obtain a digital signature ('firma electrónica') from a DIAN-authorized certification provider to enable e-invoicing and RUT updates. (5) Update the RUT to reflect status as 'Responsable del IVA' (VAT taxpayer), ICA obligations with the respective municipality, and, if applicable, the 'Registro de Exportación de Servicios' (RES). (6) Maintain a registered local address ('domicilio principal') and notify DIAN of any changes within the statutory term. Foreign shareholders are permitted without special licensing, but the local legal representative must hold a valid Colombian residence or visa status for RUT authentication.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the S.A.S. is a separate capital company and a Colombian tax resident by incorporation, taxed at the 35% corporate income tax rate (tax year 2025) on worldwide income, including foreign-source SaaS and market-research revenue. No pass-through or US-style check-the-box election is available. Territorial_Scope: residents are taxed on global income; non-resident shareholders are taxed only through Colombian-source withholding (e.g., dividends, capital gains). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the S.A.S. is a separate capital company and a Colombian tax resident by incorporation, taxed at the 35% corporate income tax rate (tax year 2025) on worldwide income, including foreign-source SaaS and market-research revenue. No pass-through or US-style check-the-box election is available. Territorial_Scope: residents are taxed on global income; non-resident shareholders are taxed only through Colombian-source withholding (e.g., dividends, capital gains). Tax_Exempt_Zone is not a default; a reduced 20% CIT applies only upon an explicit Zona Franca grant.

Available election forms

Colombia lacks a check-the-box regime; classification is statutory. Available elections: (i) opt-in to 'Régimen Simple de Tributación' (SIMPLE) — a consolidated presumptive tax for entities with gross revenue ≤ 100,000 UVT (≈ COP 4,979.9 million for 2025), integrating income tax, VAT, ICA and other contributions; (ii) application for 'Zona Franca' status (20% CIT for 25 years) for eligible technology/export operations; (iii) voluntary early registration as a VAT responsible even before the first taxable transaction; (iv) waiver of VAT refunds in favor of crediting against future tax liabilities; and (v) selection of annual versus bimonthly VAT filing cycles based on volume. Hybrid-mismatch rules (thin capitalization and CFC 'transparencia fiscal internacional') apply automatically with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for S.A.S. legal entities — the company becomes a VAT 'responsable' from the first taxable sale, import, or service; RUT registration is mandatory before commencement of operations (Art. 437 E.T.). The 3,500 UVT threshold (COP 174,296,500 for tax year 2025, with UVT 2025 = COP 49,799) applies exclusively to qualifying natural persons claiming the 'no responsable' exception, not to corporations. Late registration triggers penalties under Arts. 641 and 653 E.T.; for a foreign platform selling digital services to Colombian consumers, nexus is established separately by the digital-services regime (Art. 420 literal c) E.T.) requiring local VAT registration irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT with refund rights) for export of services is available under Art. 481 literal e) E.T. and Art. 1.1.1.1. of Decree 1625/2016, as interpreted by DIAN Concept No. 0232 of 2023. Criteria: (i) the direct beneficiary is a non-resident entity without Colombian tax domicile; (ii) the service is used or exploited exclusively abroad ('utilización y aprovechamiento exclusivo en el exterior') — for the consumer-panel market-research SaaS, the data, surveys, and research deliverables must be hosted, processed, and delivered abroad for the foreign client's benefit; (iii) payment is received in foreign currency through an authorized FX intermediary or a registered 'cuenta de compensación' maintained with Banco de la República; (iv) the exporter holds an active DIAN 'Registro de Exportación de Servicios' (RES) in MUISCA; and (v) the export proceeds are repatriated within the legal FX term. B2B digital/research services meeting these tests qualify for 0% with input-VAT refund. Caution: platform revenue derived from advertisements or services 'used' by Colombian-resident panelists may be treated as taxable domestic services and must be ring-fenced to avoid partial disqualification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación — DEX/SISCION Export) for physical goods; for the digital SaaS/consumer-panel service, the functional equivalent is the DIAN-approved 'Registro de Exportación de Servicios' (RES) entry in MUISCA and the FEV marked 0%.
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Electronic Delivery Certificate — transport document for physical media; for digital services, the signed international service contract plus platform delivery/log access evidence identifying the foreign client and the foreign use/benefit.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — 'Declaración de Cambio por Servicios' (Form No. 1) or 'Comprobante de Ingreso al Mercado Cambiario' issued by an authorized FX intermediary, evidencing foreign-currency repatriation within the 180-day regulatory term; or a certificate of remittance to a registered 'cuenta de compensación'.
  • Commercial Invoice (Spanish/English) cross-referenced to the FEV (CUNE + QR), together with the DIAN 'documento soporte' for export-related local purchases where input VAT is claimed.

Mandatory 'factura electrónica de venta' (FEV) with real-time pre-validation ('validación previa') through DIAN's MUISCA web services, governed principally by Resolution 000165 of 2023 and Resolution 000012 of 2021. Each FEV requires: a certified digital signature, a unique CUNE (Código Único de Numeración de Factura Electrónica) generated by DIAN's algorithm, an XML payload transmitted via API in near real-time, DIAN's acceptance/rejection response, and a human-readable PDF/format bearing a QR code. Colombia does not use physical fiscal stamps; clearance is entirely electronic. Contingency 'validación posterior' is permitted only for certified technical outages. Exporters must also issue electronic 'nota crédito/nota débito', transmit the 'documento soporte' for purchases from non-VAT-responsible parties, and file annual 'información exógena' reporting of export transactions.

DIAN cross-matches the RES, FEV e-invoices, FX remittance forms, and 'información exógena' declarations through specialized export-audit models, and PayPal/Wise payment rails not operating through authorized intermediaries will be flagged. Consequences of false or non-substantiated zero-rating: (i) 'sanción por inexactitud' (Art. 648 E.T.) of up to 200% of the additional tax determined, plus default interest ('intereses moratorios', approximately 1.5% monthly); (ii) 'sanción por devolución o compensación improcedente' (Art. 670 E.T.) of 20% of the refund improperly received, plus indexation; (iii) retrospective clawback of all input VAT credits linked to the disqualified export invoices; (iv) cancellation of the RES/RUT export status and up to 5 years' exclusion from export-refund procedures, with public disclosure on DIAN's infringement register; (v) criminal tax evasion ('evasión fiscal' — Art. 434A Criminal Code) with imprisonment from 48 to 108 months and fines where the evaded VAT exceeds the statutory threshold; and (vi) personal liability of the S.A.S. legal representative and shareholders for forged FX documents or false certificates under 'falsedad en documento privado' (Art. 289 Criminal Code).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a free-market managed floating exchange rate for the Colombian peso (COP) with full current-account convertibility for trade, services, and ordinary capital transactions. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment (FDI), portfolio investment, and external credits, are generally unrestricted but must be routed through authorized intermediaries of the Colombian foreign-exchange market and registered with the central bank in the Registro Internacional de Inversiones; the inward FDI registration (Formulario No. 4) must be completed through the local bank before or upon receipt of funds. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without central-bank exchange approval once the foreign investment is registered and the company has complied with local corporate and tax prerequisites; the main legal hurdle is not FX convertibility but documentary compliance, including the dividend-withholding regime applicable to non-residents (currently generally 10 percent plus any applicable surcharge combined with the corporate income-tax rate on distributable profits) and proof that the capital injection was previously registered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds, including receipts from the export of market-research services, are subject to a mandatory channeling obligation ('canalización por el mercado cambiario') under Banco de la República rules: the full 100 percent of the export value must be brought through an authorized intermediary, either by conversion into COP, deposit into a registered local foreign-currency account, or deposit into an authorized compensation account (cuenta de compensación), within six months from the date of export declaration for goods or, for services, within six months of service performance or the corresponding contractual maturity date of the invoice. There is no mandatory fixed quota requiring conversion into pesos; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in approved accounts, but the applicable declaración de cambio (Formulario No. 1 for goods, Formulario No. 3 for service receipts and intangibles) must be submitted to the central bank through the commercial bank, and extensions are available only in justified circumstances. Failure to channel export proceeds is an exchange infraction exposing the company to supervisory sanctions, so the platform should systematize the reconciliation of every PayPal/Wise payout batch into the local bank treasury account within the regulatory term.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Board of Directors) is the central bank and foreign-exchange authority, with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia as the banking and FX-intermediary supervisor; the foreign-exchange regime is codified primarily in Resolution 8 of 2000 of the Banco de la República, Circular Reglamentaria Externa DCIN-83, and implementing rules issued under Decreto 1735 of 2020 and related decrees.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility: Conditional High as a disbursement rail only, not as a treasury substitute. Wise, PayPal Mass Payout, and analogous electronic-money institutions are not authorized to operate as full deposit-taking banks in Colombia; they must operate through a licensed local payment-entity arrangement (SEDPE) or through correspondent arrangements with a Colombian Tier-1 bank, and all US-dollar flows related to the platform must ultimately be channeled through the regulated local banking system to satisfy the channeling obligation. Legal constraints include the prohibition on cross-border EMIs maintaining corporate treasury balances in COP without Colombian banking or deposit authorization, the requirement that the local entity keep its principal operating account with an authorized intermediary, and the need to document each financial-flow leg (platform respondent payout, aggregation, and settlement) to avoid characterization as an unauthorized money-transmission or electronic-money business under the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia regulatory perimeter.

Root cause: Payments routed through third-country aggregation platforms (Wise/PayPal) creating a perceived gap in the regulated FX channeling chain

Defense evidence: Banking compliance manual and flow diagram showing that EMIs are only front-end disbursement rails; corporate treasury policy requiring periodic consolidation of all funds into the local authorized-intermediary account; exact trace of transaction codes and declaración de cambio filings; opinion of the statutory auditor (revisor fiscal) confirming no unregistered FX operations; and subscription of the fintech entity to local AML and privacy frameworks applicable to payment aggregators.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder's capital contribution must be sent by SWIFT wire transfer (MT103) to the S.A.S.'s Colombian operating account held at an authorized intermediary, with the message containing the beneficiary S.A.S. legal name and NIT, the reference 'FOREIGN DIRECT INVESTMENT', and the FDI registration number previously obtained or simultaneously filed through the bank's Formulario No. 4 electronic registration; the funds should never be sent directly to a PayPal or Wise wallet because such routing would not satisfy the declaración de cambio and FDI-registration requirement. The bank will validate the consignment against the Cámara de Comercio certificate, evidence of the capital call, and the board minutes allocating the capital contribution, and will issue the corresponding channeling certificate for later capital repatriation and dividend remittances.Outbound settlements should be executed through: (a) SWIFT MT103/MT202 payments for supplier invoices, cloud infrastructure, royalties, and marketing services, each accompanied by the relevant tax-withholding certificates and the service-payment declaration (Formulario No. 3) or import-of-goods declaration (Formulario No. 2) filed through the bank's FX platform; (b) commercial documentary letters of credit governed by UCP 600 for material procurements when suppliers require bank-payment security; and (c) domestic peso-clearing rails (CUD direct-clearance, ACH, and the CENIT electronic clearing house) for local Colombian disbursements to survey participants and vendors. The platform must maintain a consolidated treasury calendar to cover the bank's FX trading cut-off times and avoid late channeling declarations; payments to related parties or tax-haven jurisdictions must be supported by transfer-pricing documentation because the Colombian tax administration applies OECD-aligned transfer-pricing rules to cross-border intercompany services.COP/USD deliverable currency forwards (forwards de tasa de cambio) traded over-the-counter with local banks, permissible only when backed by an underlying commercial receivable or payable of the S.A.S.Cross-currency swaps (permutas de divisas) for medium-term hedging of USD-denominated capital or operating liabilities, subject to the same underlying-exposure requirement under Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia regulations.FX options (opciones sobre divisas) used for asymmetric risk protection on the USD/COP exposure of the survey platform's cross-border invoicing, requiring evidence of the hedged transaction.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) with offshore counterparties entered into through the local treasury or through an international bank's Colombian branch, permissible for risk management but subject to bank confirmation and underlying-operation documentation, since the COP is a deliverable currency and NDF structures must not be used to circumvent channeling rules.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Conditional High as a disbursement rail only, not as a treasury substitute. Wise, PayPal Mass Payout, and analogous electronic-money institutions are not authorized to operate as full deposit-taking banks in Colombia; they must operate through a licensed local payment-entity arrangement (SEDPE) or through correspondent arrangements with a Colombian Tier-1 bank, and all US-dollar flows related to the platform must ultimately be channeled through the regulated local banking system to satisfy the channeling obligation. Legal constraints include the prohibition on cross-border EMIs maintaining corporate treasury balances in COP without Colombian banking or deposit authorization, the requirement that the local entity keep its principal operating account with an authorized intermediary, and the need to document each financial-flow leg (platform respondent payout, aggregation, and settlement) to avoid characterization as an unauthorized money-transmission or electronic-money business under the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia regulatory perimeter.

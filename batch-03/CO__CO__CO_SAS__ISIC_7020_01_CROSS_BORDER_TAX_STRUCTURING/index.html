Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official web portal: www.dian.gov.co; electronic services via the MUISCA platform (Sede Electrónica).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Foreign incorporators obtain the 'pre-RUT' provisional registration through the DIAN MUISCA site (www.muisca.dian.gov.co). Step 2: Execute the S.A.S. constitutive document (private instrument, no minimum capital; foreign ownership fully permitted) and record it with the Cámara de Comercio to obtain the matrícula mercantil (business registration). Step 3: Within thirty days of incorporation, file the definitive RUT with DIAN to be assigned the NIT and to assume fiscal responsibilities (income tax, VAT or IVA, source withholding, electronic invoicing; ICA municipal tax if applicable). Step 4: Register non-resident shareholders or a legal representative receiving Colombian-source payments in the RUT with character 'No Domiciliado' for the NIT; if a foreign parent receives dividends or fees, that NIT supports the remittance and transfer-pricing documentation. Step 5: Activate the electronic invoicing habilitation (software provider ID and digital certificate) and maintain the annual RUT update obligation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the S.A.S. is a separate income-tax payer (no pass-through treatment) under Colombia's worldwide-taxation regime for resident entities; foreign non-resident shareholders are taxed on remittances/dividends only at source rates (0%-10% additional rate) with separate 'no domiciled' filing obligations. — Step 1: Foreign incorporators obtain the 'pre-RUT' provisional registration through the DIAN MUISCA site (www.muisca.dian.gov.co). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the S.A.S. is a separate income-tax payer (no pass-through treatment) under Colombia's worldwide-taxation regime for resident entities; foreign non-resident shareholders are taxed on remittances/dividends only at source rates (0%-10% additional rate) with separate 'no domiciled' filing obligations.

Available election forms

No general check-the-box election exists in Colombia. Specific elections available to the S.A.S. include: (i) SIMPLE (Simplified Taxation Regime) election, replacing income, VAT and industry-and-commerce tax with a presumptive single payment, limited to gross annual income not exceeding 100,000 UVT (tax year 2024 UVT = COP 47,065); and (ii) election to form a consolidated tax group ('grupo económico con consolidación') with related Colombian entities, subject to prior DIAN authorization. Hybrid mismatch treatment is mandatory (BEPS Action 2 transposed by Law 2010 of 2019); there is no elective hybrid-mismatch regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable sale, and a corporation (S.A.S.) cannot benefit from the 'no responsable de IVA' status, which is reserved for natural persons whose gross income is below 3,500 UVT (tax year 2024: approx. COP 164.7 million). Because consulting services under ISIC 7020 are taxable, the S.A.S. must register as a VAT responsible in the RUT immediately after incorporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating is conditioned on physical exit from Colombian customs territory under a definitive export declaration ('Declaración de Exportación' - DEX), sale to a non-resident, and collection of proceeds through the Colombian foreign-exchange market. Services: a consultancy service (ISIC 7020) qualifies as an 'export of services' only if it is rendered to a recipient not domiciled in Colombia, is used exclusively abroad, does not relate to assets or activities physically located in Colombia, and the price is paid by the non-resident in foreign currency remitted from abroad. The zero-rated nature must be reflected in the FEV invoice, and the resulting 'saldo a favor' (excess VAT credits) is recoverable against income tax or refundable at DIAN's discretion.

  • Goods/merchandise only: Customs Export Declaration (DEX - Declaración de Exportación) registered in DIAN/VUCE
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR transport document evidencing physical exit (goods), or delivery-and-acceptance protocol (services)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign-exchange settlement certificate (Certificado de Venta de Divisas or Certificado de Cambio) proving receipt from abroad
  • Services only: signed international service contract and, where available, the Certificado de Exportación de Servicios issued to the foreign client
  • VAT return (Form 300) and e-invoice export event in which the zero-rated operation and export exemption are reported

MANDATORY REAL-TIME BILLING: Under DIAN Resolution 165/2023, the S.A.S. must obtain an electronic fiscal certificate (firma electrónica), be habilitated by DIAN to issue the Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV), and transmit the standardized XML to DIAN's MUISCA web services (SOAP/API) so that the invoice is pre-validated before legal issuance. DIAN returns an acceptance/acknowledgment and the sealed invoice carries the unique electronic document code (CUDE); all post-issuance events (credit notes, transport events, export operations) are transmitted to RADIAN, DIAN's mandatory e-archive platform. No physical pre-printing or retrospective stamping is allowed; issuance to a foreign export client is filed under the special 'export operation' invoice event.

DIAN risk rules automatically match DEXs, e-invoice export events, transport documents and FX settlements; a false or unsupported export claim leads to (i) reversal of the zero-rating and collection of the 19% VAT, (ii) late payment interest under Art. 634 E.T., (iii) a 200% penalty on the difference for inexact returns when documentation is false (Art. 648 E.T.), and (iv) suspension or refusal of the refund claim; criminal exposure under Law 1762 of 2014 for smuggling ('contrabando') and fiscal fraud ('defraudación fiscal') includes imprisonment, fines and confiscation, with penalties aggravated when structured shells or sham service contracts are used.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% under Article 240 E.T. for taxable years 2023 onward; S.A.S. entities in consultancy have no reduced statutory corporate rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Pursuant to Article 245 E.T., dividends paid to non-resident shareholders out of profits already taxed at the corporate level are subject to a final 5% withholding tax. Dividends distributed from profits not taxed at the corporate level are subject to a 35% withholding tax. Treaty rates may reduce these amounts if the beneficial owner meets DTA conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Under the annual DIAN Resolution, the Form 210 filing window opens in the ninth month after the fiscal year-end and closes in the tenth month; for FY ending 31 December 2024, deadlines are set by the last two digits of the NIT between 9 September 2025 and 9 October 2025. No general extension exists.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Form 120 are mandatory for FY2024 when prior-year gross income is at least COP 4,706,500,000 (100,000 UVT) or gross assets are at least COP 7,059,750,000 (150,000 UVT), with reportable related-party transactions. Master File is required for members of multinational groups that meet the Colombian CbC threshold, aligned to the OECD EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold. Contemporaneous TP documentation remains advisable for all cross-border consultancy arrangements below these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not operate an Emirates-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. However, DIAN applies general anti-avoidance rules, form-over-substance doctrine, beneficial ownership requirements, DTA limitation-on-benefits clauses, and the substantive permanent establishment tests. A Colombian S.A.S. must demonstrate real business purpose, local management, payroll, office or infrastructure and substantive execution of advisory functions; otherwise treaty benefits may be denied and the entity may be disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Revenue Omission

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Article 648 E.T. plus criminal liability under Article 434 A of the Colombian Criminal Code: imprisonment of 48 to 108 months, fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax, and mandatory publication of the sanction.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders may be personally liable under Law 1258/2008 for the S.A.S. where the entity is used as a façade. Civil restitution, asset attachment, exit/travel restrictions imposed by the criminal process, disqualification from corporate management and imprisonment are available.
Transfer Pricing Noncompliance

Statutory sanction: DIAN makes transfer pricing adjustments increasing taxable income; the underpayment carries a 20% inaccuracy penalty plus default interest. Failure to file the TP return triggers article 651 E.T. penalties computed on gross income or assets, capped by UVT limits.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and revisor fiscal who certifies false TP information may face civil liability for the additional tax, professional sanctions, disqualification, and criminal prosecution for false documentation.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a private company limited by shares under Cap. 113; a single subscriber share of EUR 1.00 must be issued and paid up at par; no escrow or banked capital is legally mandated, but the Registrar requires a filed Statement of Capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued under the Fire Safety in Buildings Regulations; structural load inspection certificate by a licensed civil engineer for the cleanroom and assembly floor; Occupational Safety and Health clearance by the Department of Labour Inspection, Ministry of Labour and Social Insurance, under the Safety and Health at Work Law 89(I)/1996, covering sharps handling, machine guarding, chemical hygiene and emergency egress.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a private company limited by shares under Cap. 113; a single subscriber share of EUR 1.00 must be issued and paid up at par; no escrow or banked capital is legally mandated, but the Registrar requires a filed Statement of Capital.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Τμήμα Φορολογίας – Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; operational portal: Cyprus Tax Department ARIADNE e-services platform (https://taxrefund.mof.gov.cy, VAT and CIT filing via Tax Department online services).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Cypriot LTD: (1) Incorporate with the Cyprus Registrar of Companies through the online Registrar platform or a licensed Corporate Service Provider; (2) Obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, which records the sole company registration number (HE/HE number) that concurrently serves as the company Tax Identification Number (TIN) in the Republic of Cyprus; (3) If the company is managed and controlled from Cyprus, it is automatically treated as tax-resident and must file annual income tax returns; no separate TIN application is necessary. (4) For VAT registration, the company (including non-established foreign owned entities) must submit the VAT-101 Application for VAT Registration to the relevant District VAT Office; non-resident entities without a Cyprus establishment must appoint a tax representative under Article 60 of the Cyprus VAT Law. (5) Upon acceptance, the Tax Department issues a nine-character VAT registration number using prefix 'CY' followed by eight digits/letters, and the entity is enrolled in the VIES system for intra-Community supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a check-the-box pass-through regime; every Cyprus private limited company is taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Law (L.118(I)/2002). Available statutory elections include: (a) group loss relief election under Article 15 of the Income Tax Law for group members, allowing intragroup set-off of tax losses; (b) election for a Cyprus tax resident company to be treated as non-resident if central management and control is exercised abroad, subject to OECD transfer pricing and substance scrutiny (administrative practice); (c) elections under the Cyprus holding company regime for the use of notional interest deductions on new equity (Article 9F); (d) no hybrid mismatch election exists, as Cyprus anti-avoidance aligns with EU ATAD and hybrid mismatch rules via the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law and interpretation of OECD nexus rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The general registration threshold for a Cyprus-established taxable person is €15,600 of annual taxable turnover. A non-established taxable person making taxable supplies in Cyprus has no threshold and is mandatorily registrable from the first euro of taxable turnover; a non-resident supplier solely making distance sales into Cyprus must register when intra-Community distance sales to Cyprus exceed €35,000 per calendar year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to the supply of goods dispatched or transported by the supplier or on its behalf to a destination outside the European Union, as well as to certain intra-Community supplies to VAT-registered EU counterparties. For the surgical suture assembly scope: the Cypriot manufacturer must retain valid evidence of export, act as the VAT-owning exporter, prove that the goods left Cyprus and the EU, and hold the HMRC-31-equivalent / Customs SAD C88 exit certificate, transport contract, and payment records. Offshore services, such as assembly services performed in Cyprus on non-resident-owned inputs with final export, can also be zero-rated as ancillary transport/processing services under Articles 79–85 of the VAT Law if the direct destination is outside the EU. Zero-rating cannot be claimed when goods are consumed in Cyprus or if the exporter merely makes a domestic transfer of goods to a fiscal warehouse.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document – SAD/C88) showing exit of goods from Cyprus and the EU
  • Signed commercial invoice in EUR/USD referencing the surgical suture needles and ISIC 3250 scope
  • Bill of Lading (ocean freight) or Air Waybill (airfreight) evidencing physical dispatch from Cyprus to the non-EU consignee
  • Packing list and attestation of origin (CNZ/UTR or Certificate of Origin, as requested by importing country)
  • Proof of export/exit authorization from Cyprus Customs (validation stamp in the electronic system)
  • Intra-Community exemption support if final transit is via another EU member state (e.g., Transit Accompanying Document TAD or Exit Summary Declaration)

Cyprus currently has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance system, no government-controlled e-invoice authorization database, and no digital stamping regime for B2B invoices. E-invoicing is permitted and legally equivalent to paper where authenticity, integrity and legibility are preserved via business controls or qualified electronic signature. VAT returns and VIES recapitulative statements must be filed electronically through the Cyprus Tax Department electronic portal. For export transactions, a standard invoice plus supporting Customs SAD/C88 export declaration and transport documents remain mandatory. The EU e-invoicing common standard (EN 16931) may be used voluntarily; a mandate will only crystallize if the EU VAT in the Digital Age package is adopted and implemented.

Export zero-rating is strictly scrutinized by the Cyprus Tax Department and Cyprus Customs. For disallowed false export claims, the VAT authority issues an assessment requiring immediate payment of the reserved VAT at 19%, plus accruing interest from the date of the under-declared output tax. Civil penalties can reach 2% per month on unpaid tax. Fraudulent claims, false documentation or collusive customs declarations carry criminal penalties under the Cyprus VAT Law L.95(I)/2000 and the Criminal Code, including imprisonment up to three years for tax fraud and unlimited fines; professional license revocation may follow for medical device manufacturing entities. Export VAT credits are also subject to retrospective clawback for up to six years, meaning the exporter must preserve the original customs export declaration, BOL/AWB, and payment receipts for at least six years in line with the Cyprus VAT record-keeping obligation of Article 66 of the VAT Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a member of the Eurozone; the euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions within the EU framework. There are no exchange control permissions required for capital injections, trade payments, dividend distributions, or loan repayments. Profit remittances are unrestricted from an FX perspective, subject only to corporate tax compliance (12.5% standard rate), anti-money laundering checks on the beneficial owner, and the EU Regulation on transfers of funds (recast) record-keeping duties. The Central Bank of Cyprus does not impose convertibility restrictions, but banks may request supporting documentation to satisfy AML/KYC and sanctions obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain repatriated proceeds in EUR or other permitted convertible currencies in their commercial bank accounts. Balances of export receipts are reported for statistical purposes to the Central Bank of Cyprus under the Balance of Payments reporting regime, but no compulsory conversion into EUR applies.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with significant legal constraints. Cyprus-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) authorized by the Central Bank of Cyprus may offer e-wallet and payment accounts, but they are generally less suitable as primary treasury hosts for large cross-border manufacturing settlements due to lending restrictions, transaction volume monitoring, and the requirement for clear underlying trade documentation. EEA-licensed EMIs may passport into Cyprus under EU rules after CBC notification, but non-EEA offshore digital EMIs cannot operate unless separately licensed; clearing through offshore EMIs is disfavored by tier-1 banks and by qualified counterparties for LC flows. For ISIC 3250 surgical consumables trade flows, an EMI should be used only as a secondary collection rail, never as the main liquidity account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections from shareholders must be executed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from a verified bank account in the shareholder's legal name to the Cyprus company's IBAN account held with a CBC-authorized credit institution. The payment reference must state 'Share capital contribution' or 'Subordinated shareholder loan' and cite the board resolution authorizing the transaction. Under EU anti-money laundering rules and EU sanctions regulations, the sending bank must provide corresponding account details, beneficial owner information, and the reason for the transfer in structured fields (field 70 / structured remittance information). No pre-approval from the Central Bank of Cyprus is required, but the receiving bank is obliged to verify the identity of both parties and to report any transaction above thresholds to the relevant authorities under the prevention of money laundering framework. The company should retain all corporate resolutions, updated shareholders register, capital increase amendments, and tax filing records to prove the nature of the influx and to avoid potential misclassification as taxable income.Trade settlements for raw material purchases and machinery imports for surgical suture manufacturing shall be staged through SWIFT MT103 wire payments and irrevocable documentary letters of credit (Commercial LCs) issued by a Cypriot commercial bank in favor of the supplier and confirmed by a first-class European or Asian correspondent bank when required. For regular purchase orders below EUR 500,000, MT103 telegraphic transfers with attached commercial invoice and bill of lading references are acceptable. For larger transfers, direct bills of lading collection (D/P and D/A) and standby LCs may be used. Payments shall be denominated in EUR where possible; when supplier settlement requires USD or CNY, the Cypriot bank may apply a spot FX conversion with full documentation of underlying trade exposure. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions (triggering preparation of the Local File and the Summary Information Table filed with Form TD 4); Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to offshore financial centres; substance is instead enforced through (i) the central-management-and-control test for tax residence, (ii) EU ATAD rules (CFC regime, GAAR, EBITDA interest limitation), (iii) the OECD BEPS nexus condition in the IP Box, and (iv) the Cyprus Tax Department's administrative practice of requiring physical premises, local staff, payroll, board decision minutes, bank accounts and audited financial statements before issuing certificates of residence. A CY LTD engaged in surgical suture assembly with no real operational footprint in Cyprus is exposed to recharacterization, treaty-benefit denial, and CFC reattribution abroad.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: On conviction: fines of up to EUR 50,000 (or an amount up to three times the evaded tax where higher) and/or imprisonment of up to five years; the tax authority additionally levies 4.5% annual interest, late-payment surcharges, and demands full restitution of the tax difference.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct concealment face personal criminal prosecution, disqualification as directors of any Cyprus company, civil restitution from personal assets under the Companies Law fraudulent-trading provisions, and court-imposed travel bans during tax collection proceedings.
Shell Company / Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Refusal of the Cyprus Certificate of Tax Residence; loss of DTA benefits and of the participation exemption; downward TP reallocation of profits by the foreign tax authority under OECD PE and beneficial-ownership rules; EU ATAD CFC taxation of undistributed Cyprus profits in the shareholder's state; administrative penalties on incorrect filings.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly maintaining an artificial letterbox arrangement may face civil claims from tax authorities and minority shareholders, personal collection orders, departure prohibition orders, and, in egregious cross-border schemes, criminal prosecution for conspiracy or money laundering with imprisonment exposure.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Cyprus AML Law (188(I)/2007 as amended): administrative fines for legal persons up to EUR 500,000 or 10% of annual turnover; criminal conviction for money laundering carries imprisonment of up to 14 years and confiscation of the proceeds; banking facilities may be frozen by court order and the company's statutory license for medical-device manufacturing suspended.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally prosecuted as directing minds for willful blindness to suspicious transactions, incurring imprisonment, personal confiscation orders over bank accounts, real estate and passports, plus travel bans pending investigation; directors also face civil liability for losses caused to the company by facilitating illicit payment flows.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Tax Commissioner may re-determine taxable profits at arm's length, extend the reassessment period, charge 4.5% annual interest on the additional tax, impose penalties for non-production of documentation when requested, and deny deductions for unsubstantiated cross-border payments; reassessment windows reach six years and twelve years in cases of fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or instruct a non-arm's length pricing policy that causes a tax loss may face breach-of-fiduciary-duty suits by the company, disqualification under the Companies Law, and, where the arrangement is used to evade tax, joint personal liability with the company for the tax difference plus criminal fines.

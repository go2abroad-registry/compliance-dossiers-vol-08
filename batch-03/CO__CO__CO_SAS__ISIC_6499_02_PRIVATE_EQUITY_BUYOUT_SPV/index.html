Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — National Tax and Customs Directorate; operational portal: MUISCA (https://www.muisca.dian.gov.co); institutional portal: https://www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: S.A.S. is a standalone juridical taxpayer under Art. 12 of the Tax Statute; resident by incorporation and principal office; taxed on worldwide income at the 35% corporate income tax rate (2025). No pass-through or fiscally transparent status is available.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box: entity-level taxation is mandatory for all S.A.S. Statutory elections available: (i) election to consolidate affiliated entities as a single VAT taxpayer under Art. 53 E.T.; (ii) opt-in to the SIMPLE regime where turnover and wealth thresholds are met; (iii) accelerated depreciation of 20% per annum for eligible productive capital goods; (iv) optional protective treaty positions before DIAN for CFC/hybrid mismatch exposures, as the anti-avoidance rules themselves are non-elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover de minimis for entities: VAT responsibility arises on the first taxable supply in Colombia or first import. The optional Monotributo formalization applies below 3,500 UVT (COP 174,296,500 in 2025); non-resident digital-service suppliers must register where prior-year Colombian consumer receipts exceed 40,000 UVT, except under platform-based simplified collection regimes imposed by DIAN decree without a minimum threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Colombian customs territory qualify for 0% VAT with full input-VAT recovery (Art. 481 E.T.). Export services, including software development and BPO/call-center services performed in Colombia for non-resident recipients, qualify when the service is economically 'exported', used abroad, and the corresponding proceeds enter Colombia through the foreign-exchange channel. A PE buyout SPV holding passive equity investments will normally generate no export turnover; management-fee and acquisition services are outside the VAT taxable scope or constitute VAT-exempt/denied inputs.

  • Electronic export declaration (DEX/SIEX filing approved by DIAN customs)
  • Original commercial invoice / export invoice (factura de exportación) pre-validated by DIAN with CUFE
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing exit from Colombian customs territory
  • Bank inward-remittance certificate / foreign-exchange channel declaration (Formulario de Declaración de Cambio)
  • Certificate of origin and any sector-specific permits where required

Mandatory electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta) with DIAN pre-validation: invoices must be issued through DIAN-authorized invoicing software and transmitted in real time to MUISCA via web-services API (SOAP/REST), receiving a unique CUFE/CUDE code before delivery to the buyer; export invoices must be validated before submission of the export customs declaration. Software providers require DIAN authorization; failure to comply invalidates the invoice for input-VAT credits and deductions and triggers monetary penalties.

DIAN may audit export refunds within the general 5-year review term (Art. 705 E.T.). False exports, inflated refunds, or unrepatriated proceeds attract: (i) the 200% inexactness penalty on the excess refund claimed (Art. 647 E.T.); (ii) compulsory reversal with default interest; (iii) criminal tax fraud sanctions under Law 2277/2022, including imprisonment up to 9 years and disqualification of the legal representative; and (iv) removal of the exporter from the DIAN registered-exporters list with consequential blacklisting of the NIT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian Peso (COP) is convertible for current-account transactions (trade in goods, services, and ordinary remittances) subject to submission of a foreign-exchange declaration through an authorized intermediary within the statutory deadlines. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment (FDI) and outward investment, are fully convertible only after prior registration with Banco de la República and after channeling funds through the official foreign-exchange market. Dividends and capital repatriation can be remitted abroad without further FX approval if the investment was duly registered and tax obligations have been settled; the remittance of earnings is subject to the withholding and remittance tax under the Colombian Income Tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Decree 1198 of 2016 and External Regulation No. 1 of 2018, exporters of goods must channel export proceeds into the foreign-exchange market and surrencer the equivalent of 100% of the collectable export amount within 6 months from the date of the export customs declaration. For services exports, the surrender period is 6 months from the accrual date. The conversion to COP is mandatory unless the exporter is eligible for an exemption (e.g., free-zone status, certain technological services with special regimes). Foreign-exchange intermediaries must receive the proceeds in a foreign currency account or exchange them directly and file the corresponding foreign-exchange declaration.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Banrep) as the central bank and foreign exchange authority, acting jointly with the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia in administering the exchange control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore fintech EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, etc.) do not hold any banking or financial license from the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia and are not authorized to open local corporate accounts or to act as local foreign-exchange intermediaries. Their use may cause violations of the Colombian FX channeling rules and expose the SPV to unauthorized intermediation. A locally licensed commercial bank or a licensed branch of a foreign bank (e.g., Citibank Colombia) is required for compliant treasury operations.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque Ultimate Beneficial Ownership (UBO) structure, e.g., a trust or holding in a secrecy jurisdiction preventing identification of natural persons holding ≥10%.

Root cause: Opaque Ultimate Beneficial Ownership (UBO) structure, e.g., a trust or holding in a secrecy jurisdiction preventing identification of natural persons holding ≥10%.

Defense evidence: Organisational chart identifying all UBOs and their controlling percentages; certified articles of incorporation of each SPV and fund entity; registry extract from the Colombian Chamber of Commerce for the local S.A.S.; sworn declaration and source-of-funds statement from the fund's AML officer; apostilled passports and utility bills of all UBOs.
Non-resident ownership with minimal physical substance, indicating potential shell or conduit entity.

Root cause: Non-resident ownership with minimal physical substance, indicating potential shell or conduit entity.

Defense evidence: Detailed business plan and investment memorandum evidencing the private-equity buyout mandate; executed acquisition agreement or letter of intent; audited financial statements of the fund and the SPV (if any); evidence of a genuine local registered address and active Colombian legal representation; client profile questionnaire (CPQ) completed by the fund's compliance department.

Defense evidence: Enhanced due diligence report approved by the parent fund's AML committee; certification that the PEP's funds originated from a regulated financial institution; declaration that no Colombian public funds are involved; additional background check and legal opinion from a Colombian law firm.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital injections into the Colombian S.A.S. must be routed through an authorized foreign-exchange intermediary (the local commercial bank) using SWIFT MT103. Prior to the transfer, the foreign investment must be registered with Banco de la República via the 'Formulario de Inversión Internacional' and assigned a Caribbean Clearing House (CCH) Code or an internal foreign investment code. The wire reference field must include the NIT (tax identification number) of the S.A.S. and the registration number assigned by Banco de la República, and must indicate the transaction purpose as 'Foreign Direct Investment – Equity Contribution'. The receiving bank will prepare the corresponding Declaración de Cambio and issue an impresión of the foreign investment registration, which constitutes legal proof of the capital inflow for future profit remittance and capital repatriation.For syndicated-loan disbursements and acquisition payments abroad, the S.A.S. must instruct its local bank via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank transfers) with the standard commercial description and the relevant outward foreign-exchange declaration. Payments for the acquisition of shares or assets in a foreign target are classified as outward investment and require prior registration with Banco de la República if the investment exceeds the applicable de minimis threshold (currently equivalent to USD 100,000.00 as updated by external regulation). Where the counterparty requires a payment guarantee, a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) at sight or usance may be opened through the local bank's trade desk, subject to underlying transaction documentation. The L/C must comply with Colombian tax withholding rules and be reported in the FX declaration if the intended settlement is in foreign currency.Deliverable Foreign Exchange Forward (DF) between the S.A.S. and a Colombian authorized bank, with mandatory delivery of COP against USD at maturity.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) settled in USD, permissible when the underlying exposure relates to a future liability denominated in COP that cannot be fully covered by an onshore deliverable forward.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CCIRS) between USD and COP, used to hedge both currency and interest-rate risk on long-term acquisition financing.Foreign Exchange Options (spot and OTC call/put) on USD/COP, provided the contract is linked to a genuine commercial or investment operation and is documented in the files of the local intermediary.

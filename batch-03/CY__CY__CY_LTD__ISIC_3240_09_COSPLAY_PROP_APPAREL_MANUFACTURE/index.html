Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DROC), Ministry of Energy, Commerce and Industry — Central Registry, Nicosia; electronic formation accepted through the official DROC e-filing portal.

Governing statute

Cyprus Companies Law, Cap. 113 (as amended), ss. 3–9 (incorporation by registration), s.8(1) (application in Form N1), s.47 (contents of the Memorandum), Part XII/XIII (directors, secretary and registered-office duties); Companies (Beneficial Owners) Law L.21(I)/2021. Capital duty is abolished in line with Directive 2008/7/EC.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form N1 — 'Application for Registration of a Company Limited by Shares', filed together with the executed Memorandum and Articles of Association (Cyprus Companies Law, Cap. 113, s.8).

Incorporation processing time

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Cap. 113. A private limited company is standardly formed with an authorised share capital of EUR 1,000 (1,000 shares × EUR 1.00). Issued shares may be fully or partly paid in cash or in kind; no escrow, bank deposit, or notarial capital deed is required. A Statement of Capital must accompany Form N1, and subscriber subscriptions must be paid before collection of the Certificate of Incorporation.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance — official digital portal 'Tax For All' (ARIADNE) at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy; auxiliary export administration: Cyprus Customs and Excise Department operating the THESEAS electronic customs declaration system.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus private limited company (CY_LTD, ELF code HE77) obtains its statutory identifiers as follows: (1) incorporate with the Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver in Nicosia to obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and Registry Number (HE-format registration number); (2) register with the Tax Department through the ARIADNE Tax For All portal using the TD2001-series registration forms to receive the company Tax Identification Number (TIN) — an 8-digit numeric plus one Latin check-letter code (format NNNNNNNNL) used for corporate income tax, social contributions, and employer obligations; (3) file a VAT registration application when taxable supplies in Cyprus reach €15,600 in any consecutive 12-month period, or immediately when any intra-Community acquisition, intra-Community supply, or taxable import requiring deduction is planned; the resulting VAT registration number is formatted 'CY' + 8 digits + 1 check letter (e.g., CY10259033P) and the same identifier is extended into an EORI number after activation by Customs; (4) register in the THESEAS customs module to activate the EORI and obtain digital access for lodgement of export declarations (electronic Single Administrative Document and Movement Reference Number); (5) where the entity is managed from abroad or has no Cyprus permanent establishment, appoint a tax representative established in Cyprus and complete registration within 60 days of commencing any Cyprus taxable activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Cyprus-resident company (central management and control exercised in Cyprus) is taxed at 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income; a non-resident company is taxed only on Cyprus-source income; no sub-national corporate income tax or local business tax applies in this unitary national jurisdiction. — A Cyprus private limited company (CY_LTD, ELF code HE77) obtains its statutory identifiers as follows: (1) incorporate with the Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver in Nicosia to obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and Registry Number (HE-format registration number); (2) register with the Tax Department through the ARIADNE Tax For All portal using the TD2001-series registration forms to receive the company Tax Identification Number (TIN) — an 8-digit numeric plus one Latin check-letter code (format NNNNNNNNL) used for corporate income tax, social contributions, and employer obligations; (3) file a VAT registration application when taxable supplies in Cyprus reach €15,600 in any consecutive 12-month period, or immediately when any intra-Community acquisition, intra-Community supply, or taxable import requiring deduction is planned; the resulting VAT registration number is formatted 'CY' + 8 digits + 1 check letter (e.g., CY10259033P) and the same identifier is extended into an EORI number after activation by Customs; (4) register in the THESEAS customs module to activate the EORI and obtain digital access for lodgement of export declarations (electronic Single Administrative Document and Movement Reference Number); (5) where the entity is managed from abroad or has no Cyprus permanent establishment, appoint a tax representative established in Cyprus and complete registration within 60 days of commencing any Cyprus taxable activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Cyprus-resident company (central management and control exercised in Cyprus) is taxed at 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income; a non-resident company is taxed only on Cyprus-source income; no sub-national corporate income tax or local business tax applies in this unitary national jurisdiction.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election or a general fiscal-transparency election; companies remain mandatorily subject to entity-level taxation. Statutory elections relevant to the indirect/export framework include: (i) group loss-relief election available to companies with a 75% shareholding relationship, electable per assessment year; (ii) group VAT registration election enabling related parties to operate under a single VAT number; (iii) IP Box election for qualifying intangible assets giving an effective 2.5% tax rate on net qualifying profits; (iv) tonnage-tax election for qualifying shipping businesses; (v) fixed-asset capital-allowances election prescribing the depreciation programme per asset class; (vi) One-Stop-Shop (OSS) / Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) registration election for EU cross-border distance sales; and (vii) mandatory fixed-rule compliance under the ATAD hybrid-mismatch package with no elective disapplication.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 of taxable supplies made in Cyprus in any consecutive 12-month period; €10,000 Union-wide threshold for distance sales of goods to EU consumers; zero threshold for non-established traders supplying goods or services in Cyprus and for intra-Community acquisitions of goods by a private company; exporters may, and generally must, voluntarily register for VAT to effect input-VAT recovery on export-related purchases.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(1) Export of tangible cosplay/prop apparel and synthetic wigs to a non-EU destination qualifies for the 0% rate under Article 19(2) of L.94(I)/2000, provided the goods physically leave Cyprus and the EU within 90 days of the supply and the exporter retains the customs-validated export declaration (SAD/MRN released by THESEAS), transport evidence naming the exporter and overseas consignee, the commercial contract, and bank evidence of foreign consideration received. (2) Intra-Community supply to a VAT-registered EU customer qualifies for 0% when the customer's VIES VAT number is validated at the time of supply, the goods are dispatched to the destination member state, and the supply is declared in the periodic EC Sales List. (3) Services directly related to the exported goods — packing, inland transport, freight, insurance, pre-shipment inspection and warehousing — may also be zero-rated. (4) Input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports is recovered by claiming an excess refund in the periodic VAT return; non-established exporters recover through the 13th Directive refund route (Form VAT 362) or the EU electronic refund portal.

  • Customs Export Declaration — electronic Single Administrative Document lodged via THESEAS, evidencing the Movement Reference Number and customs release
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (or CMR consignment note) proving physical removal of the goods from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice confirming receipt of export proceeds into a Cyprus bank account
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List matching the export declaration contents
  • EORI number confirmation and Certificate of Origin where required by the destination customs authority
  • Intra-Community EC Sales List record for EU zero-rated dispatches

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous-transaction-control (CTC) e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime is in force in Cyprus as of the 2024–2025 assessment cycle. Structured e-invoices compliant with EN 16931 / UBL 2.1 are accepted, while conventional paper/PDF invoices remain lawful. Statutory digital filing obligations — VAT returns, VIES/EC Sales Lists, Intrastat declarations, and customs export declarations (SAD/MRN) — are compulsory through the ARIADNE Tax For All portal and THESEAS customs system. Cyprus is transposing the EU's VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package, under which mandatory e-invoicing and near-real-time digital reporting for intra-EU supplies is scheduled no earlier than 1 January 2030.

Cyprus maintains no exchange-control or statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is applied by the Tax Department and commercial banks as the documentary substantiation norm for export-proceeds reality. Audit scrutiny: export zero-rating and refund claims are verified against the SAD/MRN trail, transport documents, and banking evidence; records must be retained for the 8-year VAT retention period, extended where fraud is suspected. An unsupported or fictitious export zero-rating claim is clawed back in full: VAT is assessed at 19% from the date of supply, with statutory late-payment interest and civil penalties generally reaching up to 200% of the under-declared VAT where intent or gross negligence is established, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion under L.94(I)/2000 and the Union Customs Code (Reg. (EU) 952/2013) carrying imprisonment of up to three years and fines. Fraudulent export declarations may also trigger a customs debt and additional customs penalties proportionate to the evaded duty.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Cypriot economy is fully integrated into the Eurozone and the EU Single Market. The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital transactions, and there are no statutory exchange controls under EU capital-movement rules. Repatriation of profits and payment of dividends require no Central Bank approval, but practical hurdles exist through bank-level AML/KYC reviews, tax clearance obligations, audited financial statements, and the availability of auditable revenue records. Non-resident shareholders may freely receive dividends after the 12.5% corporate income tax (or applicable WHT under treaties) is assessed and documented.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no legal requirement to surrender export proceeds to the Central Bank, nor any mandatory conversion ratio into euros. Export receipts may be held in foreign-currency accounts with licensed Cypriot banks. However, all inward trade receipts above the statistical reporting threshold must be reported to the Central Bank for Balance of Payments (BoP) purposes, typically through the bank's electronic BoP submission system. The exporter must retain shipping, customs, invoice and payment records to support the underlying trade transaction during AML and tax audits.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. EU/EEA licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may legally passport into Cyprus under the Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the E-Money Directive. Near-seamless cross-border euro-denominated account opening and payout can be achieved through EMIs such as those with a Maltese, Lithuanian, or Irish license. However, Cypriot banking and the Central Bank's AML enforcement discourage reliance on unregulated or non-EU offshore EMIs. A non-EEA EMI cannot lawfully service Cyprus clients without a Cyprus branch license or passporting certificate. EMIs are also frequently rejected for high-value trade settlements involving export invoices because of the inability to provide a full-bank, irrevocable payment guarantee; therefore an EMI account should complement, not replace, a tier-1 domestic bank relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from non-resident shareholders should be routed via SEPA/COR1 (euro) or SWIFT MT103 using the ISO 20022 purpose code 'INVE' / 'A01' (Direct Investment) or 'GIFT' where applicable. The remitting bank must include the Cypriot company's registration number and a board resolution approving the capital increase. For a shareholder loan, the MT103 field 70 should state 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN AGREEMENT DATED [DD/MM/YYYY]'. The receiving bank will require a notarised board resolution, an updated register of shareholders, audited financial statements of the lender if a legal entity, and a tax/legal opinion confirming no debt-pushdown calculation is being triggered. The declared source of funds must be consistent with the shareholder's net-worth profile and previously observed bank activity. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Within 15 months after the end of the fiscal year. For a 31 December year-end, the return is due by 31 March of the second subsequent year; provisional tax instalments are due 31 July and 31 December of the relevant tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate value of controlled related-party transactions in a tax year triggers mandatory Summary Information Table (SIT) filing with the TD4. A full Local File must be prepared and maintained when controlled transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000; a Master File is mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated global turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not issue a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations decree, but the Cyprus Tax Department enforces substance requirements under section 5 of the Income Tax Law and the OECD BEPS construct. A cosplay costume and wig manufacturer must demonstrably carry out core value-adding functions — procurement, manufacturing cost control, inventory risk, marketing decisions, and policy execution — through its own Cyprus office, employees, and bank accounts. A letterbox company or a captive service entity that merely invoices foreign principals without local operational substance will be re-domiciled or denied treaty benefits, and may be subject to GAAR reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Upon conviction, a fine of up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 3 years, together with recovery of full tax underpaid, statutory interest at 10.5% per annum, and a civil penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or procure false returns face criminal prosecution, personal civil liability for the tax loss, travel bans, and court orders freezing their personal assets; the corporate veil may be pierced where the company is operated as a sham.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Tax Department may disregard the Cypriot entity, reclassify the commercial income as Cyprus-source and assess it at 12.5%, impose a 10% GAAR adjustment penalty, and deny foreign tax credits or treaty benefits. A tax amnesty ceases to apply after criminal prosecution begins.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for the full amount of tax plus interest and penalties where the insolvent entity's structure constitutes fraudulent trading under Section 305 of the Companies Law, and may be subject to disqualification orders and domestic travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profit to the arm's-length value; underpaid tax plus 10.5% interest and an additional penalty of up to 100% of the adjusted tax for intentional failure; late filing fees for the TP return accrue at EUR 500 per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or recklessly approve non-arm's-length pricing may be held jointly and severally liable for the TP adjustment, and may face disqualification as directors if the Cyprus Tax Department certifies the conduct as gross negligence.
Money Laundering and Non-Compliant Payment Processing

Statutory sanction: Under Cyprus AML Law (L.188(I)/2007), individuals face imprisonment up to 14 years and/or fines up to EUR 200,000; bodies corporate are liable to fines of up to EUR 350,000 and confiscation of assets; a regulatory penalty of up to 5% of annual turnover may be imposed by CySEC or the FIU for compliance failures.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are criminally accountable for laundering offences; civil confiscation orders extend to their personal assets; travel bans and passport suspensions are routinely issued during investigative proceedings against senior management.
Failure to Maintain or Submit Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Automatic administrative fine of EUR 1,000 plus EUR 200 per month until rectification (maximum EUR 10,000); the tax return is deemed incomplete, allowing the Tax Commissioner to estimate profits using default margins and impose a 20% estimation surcharge.

Director personal liability: Key officers responsible for the accounting records can be penalised individually, reconstituted tax assessments may be charged to directors personally, and for persistent non-compliance a court order may declare the director personally responsible for the company's tax debts.

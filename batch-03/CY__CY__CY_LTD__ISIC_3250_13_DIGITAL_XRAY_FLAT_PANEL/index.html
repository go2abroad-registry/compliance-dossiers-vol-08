Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance; portal: Tax for All (https://www.taxdepartment.mof.gov.cy) / ARIADNE system

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must register via the Cyprus Tax Department using Form TD031 (Certificate of Registration / VAT form) or electronically through the ARIADNE portal. Non-resident companies with a permanent establishment or taxable supplies in Cyprus must submit the company incorporation certificate, memorandum and articles of association, proof of registered address and local agent, and director/shareholder declarations. The Tax Department issues a Taxpayer Registration Number (TRN) and, where applicable, a VAT number with prefix 'CY' followed by 8 digits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a check-the-box regime. All Cyprus-incorporated companies are per se taxable entities under the Income Tax Law (L. 118(I)/2002). Available statutory elections include: group relief election under section 31, notional interest deduction election on new equity, intellectual property box election (80% profit exemption), and tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping companies. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD (L. 58(I)/2021) apply with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable supplies for goods/services; EUR 7,200 annual intra-EU acquisitions; voluntary registration allowed below thresholds; non-resident suppliers of electronically supplied services must register regardless of threshold under the OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cyprus zero-rates the export of goods directly shipped from Cyprus to a non-EU destination, including medical diagnostic equipment such as digital X-ray flat panel detectors, provided the supplier retains documentary evidence of dispatch/transport out of the EU within 90 days of supply. Intra-EU domestic acquisitions in Cyprus from other Member States are not zero-rated but are VAT-exempt with right to deduction if all conditions of section 43 of the VAT Law are met. Services directly related to exported goods (transport, loading, commission) may also be zero-rated based on the 12th Directive principle.

  • Home Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / ECS system) containing exact HS code (e.g., 9022.10 for X-ray systems) and final destination out of Cyprus
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing actual physical movement out of EU territory
  • Commercial export invoice referencing the Customs Export Declaration and ISO country code of non-EU buyer
  • Proof of payment and FX settlement (SWIFT MT103 / TT Wire copy or Inward Remittance Certificate)
  • Packing list and medical device certificate/CE documentation as supporting evidence for ISIC 3250 scope

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime currently applies in Cyprus. Electronic invoicing is permitted and legally equivalent to paper invoices under Article 8 of the VAT Law (L. 95(I)/2004). Cyprus is preparing for the EU ViDA framework; businesses must store e-invoices in a secure, readable format for 6 years. No API-based clearance obligation is in force as of the reporting date.

The Tax Department may scrutinize export zero-rating claims ex post through documentary audit, cross-checks with the Cyprus Customs electronic system, and third-party bank data. Where zero-rating is applied fraudulently or in excess of substantive documentary proof, the Tax Department may issue an additional VAT assessment equal to 100% of the under-declared VAT, plus interest at 1.5% per month or part thereof, and an administrative penalty of up to 200% of the tax due in cases of intent to evade. Criminal sanctions for using forged export documents or falsely claiming zero-rating include imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines of up to EUR 5,000 per offence; the Minister of Finance may additionally issue a retrospective clawback order under section 114 of the VAT Law.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus has fully liberalised current and capital account convertibility since joining the euro area on 1 January 2008; the old Exchange Control Law has been repealed and the temporary March 2013 crisis restrictions were fully abolished by April 2015. The Euro is freely convertible and unrestricted; there is no central bank prior-approval requirement for dividend remittance, branch profit transfer, intercompany loan repayment, royalties, or investment capital repatriation. Free movement of capital is anchored in Article 63 TFEU with only EU-sanctioned derogations (AML, financial sanctions, national security) applicable. The 12.5% corporate income tax applies to the accounting profit of CY_LTD; dividend income received by CY_LTD is exempt; and Cyprus imposes no dividend withholding tax on non-resident shareholders - the Special Defence Contribution (SDC) of 17% applies only where the ultimate recipient of the dividend is a Cyprus tax-resident individual, never to a non-resident parent or an offshore corporate. A CY_LTD manufacturing digital X-ray flat-panel detectors can invoice, hold, and settle in EUR, USD, GBP, CHF, JPY, or CNY without any exchange control approval and without any obligation to route settlement through the domestic banking system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender ratios, mandatory conversion timelines, or forced repatriation rules for export proceeds in Cyprus. A resident exporter may retain 100% of export receipts in any freely convertible currency in any authorised bank account. The only binding requirement is statistical and anti-money-laundering reporting: the Central Bank of Cyprus, through the authorised banks, records all cross-border payments of EUR 12,500 or above (and any series of related transfers) in the CBC e-Prodata reporting system for BoP statistics, and requires the resident exporter to submit trade declarations (INEX-Q1 / CBC BoP forms) for goods exports. Each payment instruction on SWIFT or SEPA must carry the ECB/ISO20022 purpose category - for exports of goods the code 'M120' (or ISO 'GDDS'); for imports of goods 'M130' (or ISO 'GDDS') - so that the CBC can classify the trade flow without touching currency convertibility.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou, 80 Kennedy Avenue, Nicosia), which operates inside the Eurosystem and the ECB Single Supervisory Mechanism. It is the sole national competent authority for licensing and supervising credit institutions, electronic money institutions, and payment institutions; for balance-of-payments statistical reporting it applies ECB Regulation (EU) No 2013/26 and the CBC e-Prodata reporting framework; for AML/CFT it enforces the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law L.188(I)/2007 (as amended) and CBC Directive 141/2021.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate related-party transaction value per tax year triggers mandatory Cyprus Transfer Pricing (TP) documentation. A Master File is required when the multinational group has consolidated turnover exceeding EUR 50 million and the total cross-border related-party transactions of the Cyprus entity exceed EUR 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus enforces Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) aligned with the EU ATAD and the Code of Conduct for Business Taxation. For a private limited company engaged in ISIC 3250 OEM sales of digital X-ray flat-panel detectors and related IP exploitation, the company must demonstrate adequate premises, full-time qualified employees, actual decision-making management, and incur real operating expenditure in Cyprus. Pure IP-holding, treasury, or distribution activities without local substance will invite adjustment and denial of tax incentives.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to EUR 50,000 per offence, plus tax surcharges of 5%, interest at 5% per annum, loss of treaty benefits, and criminal sanctions including imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors, or beneficial owners who cause or consent to the false return may be personally assessed for unpaid tax under anti-avoidance provisions, subject to travel bans, asset seizure, and imprisonment for up to three years.
Artificial Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of IP Box benefits, recharacterization of intercompany contracts, TP adjustments, additional tax at 12.5%, penalties and interest, and cancellation of the tax residence certificate.

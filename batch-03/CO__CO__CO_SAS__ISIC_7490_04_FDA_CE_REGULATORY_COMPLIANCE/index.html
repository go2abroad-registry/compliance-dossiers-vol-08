Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an S.A.S.; subscribed capital may be paid in installments provided that full disbursement is completed within two years from incorporation. No escrow account is mandated; cash contributions are held in the company's corporate bank account following registration. In-kind contributions must be described and valued in the incorporation document.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification (Certificado de Bomberos) obtained through the official fire brigade inspection of the office premises; structural load and electrical installation verification by the property landlord or municipal civil defense, as required by local building code; occupational health and safety clearance through enrollment in a licensed ARL with a risk classification suited to professional desk-based activities; mandatory and complete social-security registry prior to the start of any personnel activity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — official portal: www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities without a legal presence in Colombia must appoint a local fiscal agent or legal representative, execute a power of attorney before a notary, obtain a specific tax residence certificate, and apply for NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through DIAN’s cleartax / Muisca portal. The application requires corporate documentation apostillized and translated into Spanish, financial statements, and identification of the effective beneficial owner. Upon approval, DIAN issues a NIT with a unique 11-digit code, valid indefinitely, and the taxpayer must be registered in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT), which constitutes the single statutory tax ID for all VAT, income tax, and customs operations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities without a legal presence in Colombia must appoint a local fiscal agent or legal representative, execute a power of attorney before a notary, obtain a specific tax residence certificate, and apply for NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through DIAN’s cleartax / Muisca portal. The application requires corporate documentation apostillized and translated into Spanish, financial statements, and identification of the effective beneficial owner. Upon approval, DIAN issues a NIT with a unique 11-digit code, valid indefinitely, and the taxpayer must be registered in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT), which constitutes the single statutory tax ID for all VAT, income tax, and customs operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombian law does not provide a check-the-box pass-through regime. A Colombian S.A.S. is an entity-level taxpayer subject to national corporate income tax on worldwide income. No deemed partnership election is allowed for domestic purposes; hybrid mismatch elections may be analyzed by treaty partners, but the default Colombian classification is a taxable corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Colombian VAT registration is mandatory without a local threshold for foreign entities that have a permanent establishment, perform taxable events in Colombia, or sell digital services. For domestic SMEs, the simplified regime applies to taxpayers with annual gross income of up to COP 3,500 per million; however, a foreign MedTech regulatory consulting company exporting services will require VAT registration only if it has Colombian customers or performs taxable activities within Colombia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Colombian taxpayer may apply a 0% VAT rate only for (i) tangible goods physically exported from Colombian customs territory, evidenced by a valid single export declaration (Declaración de Exportación), or (ii) services rendered exclusively to a foreign recipient and effectively used or exploited outside Colombia, as specified under Articles 481 and the complementary provisions of the Colombian Tax Code. Consulting, advisory, and regulatory compliance services performed for foreign medical device manufacturers are zero-rated if the results are delivered to a nonresident customer, the intellectual property and economic utilization of the service is realized abroad, and the invoice is issued to a foreign commercial domicile.

  • Apostilled commercial contract with export intention
  • Electronic single export declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DEX)
  • Certified bill of lading (or air-freight airway bill, where applicable)
  • Customs exit stamp and security manifestation
  • Foreign entity’s tax residency certificate
  • Commercial invoice with 0% VAT footnote
  • Bank automatic exchange-rate certification for foreign currency inflow
  • Inward remittance certificate (Certificado de Negociación de Divisas) issued by the Colombian intermediary bank

Colombia mandates real-time electronic invoicing (factura electrónica) for all VAT-registered taxpayers through DIAN. The electronic invoice must be issued via an authorized software provider using the factura electrónica de venta (FEV) format, signed with a DIAN-issued digital certificate, and validated telematically before delivery to the purchaser or the receipt is sent within 48 hours. For each invoice, the supplier must transmit the XML to DIAN over the webservice API, receive a validation response, and stamp the electronic invoice. There is no separate excise-stamping regime; compliance is achieved exclusively through the telematic clearance system.

Export zero-rating is subject to forward and backward audit scrutiny by DIAN. The taxpayer must prove actual physical export or offshore provision; for intangible services, the burden includes templates, service deliverables, and communication logs to prove extraterritorial use. If export documentation is incomplete, falsified, or the foreign exchange is not repatriated within 180 calendar days, DIAN will retroactively claw back the full IVA not collected, apply statutory interest (1.5 times the current usury rate), impose a penalty equal to 100% of the omitted tax under Article 648 of the Tax Code, and may initiate criminal proceedings for tax fraud when documents are falsified, with prison sentences of up to 9 years in aggravated cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a regulated liberalized convertibility regime under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, Decree 2245 of 2015 and circulars of the Banco de la República. Current-account transactions (imports, exports, services, interest, dividends) are freely convertible but are subject to mandatory declaración de cambio through authorized intermediaries. Capital-account transactions, foreign direct investment (FDI), portfolio investment and external credit, require registration in the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales (RII) of the Banco de la República. Dividend and profit remittance for a duly registered S.A.S. is permitted without prior administrative authorization, provided the income has been recognized in audited financial statements and the local withholding tax on dividends (currently 5% for resident entities, 10% as a treaty minimum in certain holding structures) has been settled. Remittance impediments arise principally from unregistered FDI, incomplete RII disclosures, unresolved tax liens, or failure to present the Formulario de Declaración de Cambio prior to the outflow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods exporters must channel 100% of the FOB value of export proceeds through the Colombian foreign exchange market, through an authorized bank, financial corporation or exchange house, within six months from the date of export registration; the Banco de la República may extend, restrict or individualize this term in line with Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83. De minimis operations equal to or below USD 10,000 per individual export event are exempt from mandatory channeling. For service exports the channeling period is generally six months and the threshold is USD 50,000 aggregate per calendar month. Natural persons and services exporters must surrender exports of services via declaración de cambio; there is no fractional surrender ratio applicable to standard S.A.S. exporters, so the effective conversion ratio is 100% for canalizable receipts. Non-channelized proceeds are treated as a foreign-exchange infraction and expose the exporter to fines issued by DIAN and restrictions on subsequent exchange operations.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), operating jointly with the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia for the administration of the international exchange regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-Medium viability. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Mercury, N26 and similar do not hold Colombian banking licenses and cannot serve as vehicles for exchange declarations, statutory capital escrow, RII registration, or profit-remittance corroboration. Their practical utility is restricted to satellite or contingency clearing for non-regulated low-value disbursements (subcontractor fees, cloud software licensing, tokenized R&D expenses) if the supporting service contract and VAT invoice are produced. Using an EMI as the principal treasury rail without a Colombian exchange declaration poses a substantial regulatory risk of un-channelized currency under the régimen cambiario and can be challenged during AML/KYC due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a foreign equity contribution to the S.A.S., the remitter executes an SWIFT MT103 transfer to the company's COP or USD account (if dual-currency authorized) at a Colombian commercial bank, quoting the company NIT and the payment reference 'Aporte de capital / FDI registration'. Prior to receipt, the company must present to the intermediary bank the pro-forma registrational dossier: board resolution approving the investment, updated bylaws, RUT certificate, identification of each shareholder, and the bank's own foreign-investment questionnaire. The intermediary bank then prepares and files the Declaración de Cambio using the standard capital-account purpose code for direct foreign investment and submits the registration to the Banco de la República's RII system within the statutory deadline. Registration in RII is mandatory to authorize future profit repatriation and capital liquidation dividends. Funds arriving without these supporting instruments may be rejected under anti-corruption and cross-border inbound-risk controls.Primary clearing rails for the S.A.S. are (i) SWIFT MT103 for service invoices, software license royalties, contractor payments and tax/social-security disbursements; (ii) domestic ACH (Cenit operated by ACH Colombia) for local Colombian peso payments; and (iii) corporate commercial letters of credit exclusively for physical equipment purchases, issued via MT700 and confirmed with transport and inspection documents. For every outbound foreign-exchange operation equal to or above the de minimis threshold, the bank must receive a declaración de cambio with the appropriate import-of-services purpose code, supported by the service contract, invoice, and, where applicable, transfer pricing policy. Use of a commercial LC for pure MedTech consulting is atypical and should be limited to a client requirement; the company should rely on SWIFT/ACH as the cost-effective statutory-compliant rails. 10% withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders when the distributed profits have been subject to Colombian corporate income tax at the entity level; no additional national taxation at the shareholder level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing return (Form 120) and supporting documentation (Local File and Master File) required when taxpayer's gross income reaches or exceeds 135,000 UVT (approx. COP 6.3 billion for 2025) or when total equity or gross net worth is equal to or greater than 70,000 UVT AND related-party transactions exceed 45,000 UVT (approx. COP 2.1 billion).

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime for ordinary operating companies; however, general anti-avoidance provisions (Art. 869 Tax Statute) and CFC rules require that the S.A.S. maintain substantive commercial operations, physical or virtual presence, and a legitimate business purpose to support treaty benefits and avoid denial of deductions or recharacterization of transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud penalty under Article 434B of the Colombian Tax Statute: imprisonment of 5 to 9 years and a fine of 200% of the evaded amount. Additionally, civil tax settlements may impose penalty of up to 200% of the omitted income and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt if they participated in the fraud or failed to exercise due diligence; personal assets may be seized, travel bans imposed, and criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Application of anti-abuse rules may cause full disallowance of deductible payments, reassessment of income at the hands of the Colombian entity, and penalty of up to 200% of the omitted tax; the DIAN may also cancel the NIT and statute-limitation periods remain open indefinitely.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the entire reassessed tax and penalties under liability-shifting rules (Art. 794 Tax Statute); in addition, a declaration of a sham company allows creditors and the tax authority to pierce the corporate veil and seek recovery from personal assets.
AML / FIU Reporting Breach (Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under Law 526/1999 and external regulation 2018 per UIAF guidelines: up to 1,000 current legal monthly minimum wages (approx. COP 1.3 billion) for financial reporting breaches; criminal money-laundering charges under Article 323 of the Criminal Code carry imprisonment of 10 to 30 years for directing or through negligence allowing laundering of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally accountable for the entity’s AML compliance deficiencies; they may be fined individually, prohibited from leaving the country, and subject to criminal indictment for the failure to prevent laundering activities, including extradition in serious cross-border cases.

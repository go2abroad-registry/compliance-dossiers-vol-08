Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Unidad Administrativa Especial Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); electronic portal: www.dian.gov.co; transactional platform: MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado)

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: an S.A.S. is a separate juridical person and Colombian tax resident subject to worldwide taxation for income tax purposes; no pass-through or fiscal transparency is available. The corporate income tax rate is 35% for 2025 for service companies; the S.A.S. is always treated as a responsible VAT taxpayer (régimen común) irrespective of revenue level.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election and no voluntary hybrid-mismatch election exists under Colombian law. Relevant statutory elections: (a) Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE), available to service companies whose prior-year gross revenue does not exceed 100,000 UVT (~COP 4,706,500,000 in 2024), provided the company has at least 10 employees and a monthly payroll above 360 UVT, and filed via RUT/MUISCA; (b) fiscal consolidation election by a controlling parent with at least 51% direct or indirect control of the subsidiary, subject to DIAN authorization; (c) exporter status election (calidad de exportador) necessary to issue zero-rated export invoices and claim VAT refunds; (d) preferential CIT treatment for income from the exploitation of qualifying I+D intangibles (Art. 236-2 ET), which is in principle unavailable for execution-only patent prosecution fees and requires actual ownership, licensing, or transfer transactions with qualifying intangibles. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory anti-abuse provisions introduced by Law 2277 of 2022 and are not subject to taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold for juridical entities: an S.A.S. performing taxable services must obtain VAT registration (NIT/RUT) from inception and charge IVA on every taxable service from the first peso. The 3,500 UVT threshold (~COP 164,727,500 in 2024) exclusively determines eligibility of natural persons for the simplified IVA regime and is unavailable to an S.A.S. Non-resident suppliers of digital services must register for IVA with zero threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

PCT patent-related and trademark prosecution services qualify for the 0% export VAT rate under Article 481 of the Estatuto Tributario provided: (i) the beneficiary is a non-resident of Colombia with no Colombian establishment receiving the service; (ii) the service is used or exploited exclusively abroad, for example filings before WIPO or foreign patent offices; (iii) the S.A.S. holds exporter status (calidad de exportador) and issues the factura electrónica de exportación; and (iv) the consideration is received through the Colombian foreign exchange market (canalización). Refund of upstream VAT on related inputs is substantiated via DIAN Form 106 and the complete export documentation bundle.

  • Customs and FX Export Declaration of Services (registration filed in the DIAN-VUCE export module and the corresponding service-export foreign exchange declaration under Banco de la República rules)
  • Factura Electrónica de Exportación (electronic export invoice at 0% VAT, with CUFE digital stamp)
  • Underlying service agreement with the foreign patent applicant identifying the foreign residence and absence of Colombian establishment of the recipient
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or equivalent intangible-delivery evidence for the services, namely WIPO or foreign patent office filing receipt and electronic transmittal proof
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (certificado de canalización / comprobante de pago) issued by the Colombian foreign exchange intermediary

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance: all taxable services must be invoiced through the Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV). The S.A.S. or its authorized Proveedor Tecnológico (PT) transmits each invoice as a digitally signed XML document to DIAN's MUISCA web-services interface; DIAN performs pre-validation (validación previa) and issues acceptance or rejection in real time. Accepted invoices are authenticated with the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica), which functions as the digital stamp; the XML and CUFE must be embedded in the PDF delivered to clients. No physical wet stamp is required. Exports require the special factura electrónica de exportación variant referencing the foreign exchange declaration.

DIAN audits export zero-ratings with a formal burden of proof on the exporter; failure to substantiate the bundle triggers the inaccuracy penalty (sanción por inexactitud, Art. 647 ET) of up to 200% of the tax difference, retroactive clawback of any VAT refund obtained, plus default interest at the statutory moratorium rate (interés moratorio, quarterly adjusted, recently above 40% per annum). Persistently false export claims can result in cancellation of the exporter registration, customs fraud proceedings (contrabando) with imprisonment up to 6 years, and Central Bank penalties for late or non-channeled repatriation of export proceeds beyond the 180-day term. All export documentation, including WIPO receipts, must be preserved for at least 5 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a market-based exchange system in which the Colombian peso (COP) is freely transferable for current-account transactions: professional fees, PCT patent prosecution services, trademark work, annuities, and profit remittances are legal and do not require prior Central Bank exchange-control approval. All inflows and outflows involving non-residents must nevertheless be channelled through an authorized IMC and supported by a 'Declaración de Cambio' identifying the underlying transaction class. Capital-account operations (equity injections, foreign loans) are convertible once the international investment is registered through Formulario No. 5. Dividend and branch-profit remittances are permitted but are subject to Colombian withholding tax at the tax-compliance stage and, on the local side, to the financial-movement tax (GMF, currently 0.4% '4 por mil') applicable to accountable account debits. The practical hurdles are documentary and timing-driven (timely filing of exchange declarations, correct purpose-code mapping, beneficial-owner declarations, and the registration of any 'cuenta de compensación' used abroad) rather than substantive; there is no exchange-control license or bona fide test for remitting legitimate service-export earnings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

(1) Statutory timeline: service-export proceeds, which include the S.A.S. fee income from PCT applications and trademark filings invoiced to foreign clients, must be received in Colombia and channelled through the exchange market within six (6) months from the invoice or service-performance date as the general commercial deadline; extensions are available only with Banco de la República authorization for justified non-speculative causes. (2) Mandatory conversion ratio: 100% of the proceeds must be sold to the local exchange market (or, as an allowed alternative, deposited into a foreign-currency account with a local IMC or into a formally registered 'cuenta de compensación' abroad whose residual balance must be periodically channelled); there is no partial surrender percentage or exemption for services. (3) Documentary medium: the 'Declaración de Cambio' (Formulario No. 3 – Exportación de Servicios) is filed through the IMC at the moment the COP credit is booked, and the bank issues a 'certificado de canalización'. Booked receivables aged beyond the statutory window constitute an exchange-control infringement subject to monetary sanctions and create adverse tax-audit and SARLAFT risk.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (the Central Bank of the Republic of Colombia), acting through its Board of Directors (Junta Directiva) and the foreign-exchange regime enacted under Law 9 of 1991 and Decree 1068 of 2015, jointly with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC), which licenses the commercial banks authorized to act as 'intermediarios del mercado cambiario' (IMCs); DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) verifies the underlying export/import-of-services documentation.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 35 Gross income: report all Colombian-source income from patent prosecution, PCT application services, trademark registration agency fees, and associated administrative charges. Understatement penalties range from 20% to 200% of the omitted amount; failure to file may trigger additional penalties up to 200% of the assessed tax.
Box 45 Costs and deductions: deductible operating expenses, official patent-office filing fees, professional service charges, depreciation of software and specialised research equipment, payroll, rent, and regulatory licensing costs. Expenses paid to foreign service providers are deductible only if supported by invoices, evidence of withholding tax at the statutory rate, and proof of economic benefit to the Colombian entity. Late filing attracts a penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction of month of delay, capped at 100% of the tax due. Interest accrues at the DIAN benchmark lending rate. Understatement penalties range from 20% to 200% of the omitted amount; failure to file may trigger additional penalties up to 200% of the assessed tax.
Box 55 Net operating losses: offset permissible prior-year tax losses within the 12-year carryforward period. Loss offsets cannot be claimed by non-resident shareholders personally; the deduction belongs exclusively to the Colombian S.A.S. and cannot reduce withholding obligations on profit distributions. Late filing attracts a penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction of month of delay, capped at 100% of the tax due. Interest accrues at the DIAN benchmark lending rate. Understatement penalties range from 20% to 200% of the omitted amount; failure to file may trigger additional penalties up to 200% of the assessed tax.
Box 70 Tax determination: apply the 35% headline CIT to the liquidated taxable income, after deductions, exempt income, and approved tax incentives. Non-resident investors should note that this box determines the tax base for subsequent dividend distributions; only profits already taxed at the entity level attract the 10% shareholder withholding. Late filing attracts a penalty of 5% of the tax due per month or fraction of month of delay, capped at 100% of the tax due. Interest accrues at the DIAN benchmark lending rate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: COP 5,800,000,000 (approximately 155,000 UVT for FY 2023) of gross income or net assets; related-party cross-border transactions above this threshold trigger mandatory Local File and Master File documentation. Transactions with tax-haven jurisdictions require sworn transfer pricing statements even below this threshold.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a separate registration-based Economic Substance Regulation regime; however, DIAN applies OECD BEPS-aligned substance doctrines, anti-abuse rules, and general statutory anti-fraud concepts under Law 2010 of 2019. The S.A.S. must maintain actual business infrastructure, local management, and substantive patent-prosecution operations in Colombia to defend the application of treaty benefits and to avoid recharacterisation as a conduit or shell entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 200% of the understated tax, plus monthly default interest; criminal prosecution under Colombian tax criminal provisions may result in imprisonment for 3 to 9 years and fines measured in minimum monthly wages.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or authorize fraudulent returns may be held personally liable for the evaded tax, face civil restitution, asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: DIAN may recharacterize transactions, deny treaty benefits, apply the full 35% rate plus withdrawals/inconsistencies penalties under the patrimonial increment regime, and impose penalties ranging from 30% to 200% of the tax recalculated.

Director personal liability: Colombian corporate veil-piercing under Law 222 of 1995 exposes directors to personal and joint liability for tax debts and interest when the entity is used as a mere shell to evade tax or mislead foreign authorities.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Mandatory tax adjustment to an arm's length result, a penalty of 30% of the omitted tax, plus interest, and an additional 30% penalty when the Local File, Master File, or sworn declaration is not timely filed.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve defective transfer pricing documentation can be jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax adjustment and penalties; repeat violations may trigger loss of regulatory licences for the patent agency.
AML / Sanctions Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, freezing of accounts, suspension of the patent-prosecution agency licence, and criminal money-laundering charges with custodial sentences up to 10 years and substantial fines.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for AML governance may face personal criminal prosecution, travel bans, disqualification from corporate management, and seizure of personal assets where the corporate structure was used as a laundering conduit.

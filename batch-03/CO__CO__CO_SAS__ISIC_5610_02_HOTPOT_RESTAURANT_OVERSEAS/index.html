Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official electronic portal: MUISCA (muisca.dian.gov.co) with institutional site www.dian.gov.co; e-invoicing validation platform: facturaelectronica.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A Colombian S.A.S. obtains its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) automatically upon notarized incorporation and Chamber of Commerce registration. The foreign shareholders must appoint a Colombian legal representative with a notarized power of attorney, who then completes the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) enrollment with DIAN, declares the economic activities (CIIU 5610 for restaurant services together with condiment ISIC 1075/4790 as appropriate), selects the IVA responsibility regime, and registers each local establishment. A non-resident enterprise without a Colombian entity must first obtain a NIT through a Colombian agent; however, for this overseas hotpot direct-operated chain the operating arm is the resident CO_SAS, which must register before issuing any invoice. Statutory licenses from municipality and sanitary authority must precede the DIAN RUT finalization.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the S.A.S. is a resident juridical person subject to corporate income tax (CIT) on worldwide income at 30% for tax years beginning 2025 (35% for tax years 2023-2024 under Law 2277/2022). Colombia does not recognize US-style check-the-box pass-through for a domestic SAS; the entity itself is the taxpayer. The source-based territorial rules apply only to non-residents and permanent establishments, while the Colombian SAS must include both Colombian-source and foreign-source income with a foreign tax credit. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the S.A.S. is a resident juridical person subject to corporate income tax (CIT) on worldwide income at 30% for tax years beginning 2025 (35% for tax years 2023-2024 under Law 2277/2022). Colombia does not recognize US-style check-the-box pass-through for a domestic SAS; the entity itself is the taxpayer. The source-based territorial rules apply only to non-residents and permanent establishments, while the Colombian SAS must include both Colombian-source and foreign-source income with a foreign tax credit. A 9% preferential CIT rate may apply to certain newly created companies in agro-industrial or creative export activities under Law 1429/2010 and Law 1955/2019 conditions.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Colombia. Available statutory mechanisms include: (a) the SIMPLE regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación), an optional consolidated substitution tax covering income tax, VAT and municipal taxes for eligible small businesses below the UVT-indexed threshold under Law 1943/2018; (b) election of the 9% preferential CIT regime for qualifying new export-oriented creative/agro-industrial companies subject to minimum employment and net-worth conditions; (c) application of free-trade-zone (Zona Franca) treatment at 20% CIT with prior DIAN authorization; (d) advanced pricing agreements and mutual agreement procedures for transfer pricing; and (e) mandatory anti-hybrid neutrality rules disqualifying deduction of hybrid mismatch payments under Law 2277/2022.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for full VAT-registered responsibility: every person selling taxable goods or rendering taxable services in Colombia must register in the RUT as Responsable de IVA. Persons whose gross income in the prior calendar year is below 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 154,000,000 in 2024) may be treated as 'No Responsables', but this exception does not exempt a hotpot restaurant because on-premise meal service and beverage sales are expressly taxable activities; thus the CO_SAS must obtain IVA responsibility status from inception. Businesses under the SIMPLE regime are also considered VAT registrants for invoicing purposes. Exporters remain registered even if their export-only turnover would otherwise fall below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Colombian VAT territoriality rule, exports of goods are excluded from VAT but confer the exporter the right to recover input VAT (exporters' VAT refund mechanism). The CO_SAS may zero-rate: (i) physical exports of hotpot condiments, sauces, packaged meal kits and related tangibles, evidenced by customs export declarations; (ii) offshore services, franchise management, know-how, software and BPO services rendered to an overseas franchisee or related foreign entity, provided the beneficiary is tax-resident abroad, the service is wholly used and economically consumed outside Colombia, and the contract and invoicing clearly identify the foreign user; and (iii) servicing of overseas direct-operated restaurants where the consideration is paid in foreign currency and no taxable activity occurs in Colombian territory. Goods and services sold to local Colombian customers are taxable at the general 19% rate.

  • Export Customs Declaration (Declaración de Exportación - DEX), filed electronically through SIGO/VUCE and validated by DIAN customs authorities
  • Bill of Lading for maritime shipments or Air Waybill for air cargo, evidencing physical departure of the goods from Colombian customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Cambio y Liquidación de Divisas) issued by the local intermediary bank confirming that the export proceeds have been repatriated and sold in the Colombian foreign exchange market

All Colombian taxpayers, including restaurant operators, must issue electronic sales invoices (Factura Electrónica de Venta) using DIAN-authorized software or the free DIAN app. Each invoice is transmitted in real time to DIAN's web service using a digital certificate; DIAN validates the XML, assigns a unique electronic invoicing code (CUFE/CUDS), and returns the authorization status before the invoice may be delivered to the customer. The restaurant POS must sync directly into the e-invoicing engine, and each emitted invoice must include the mandatory QR code. No paper stamp or physical pre-printed invoice may be used. Export invoices require a specific 'export status' annotation to obtain zero-rated or excluded treatment, and customs clearance is cross-matched electronicaly with DIAN's SIGO and VUCE platforms.

DIAN reconciles the DEX, the electronic export invoice status, customs exit records and the foreign-exchange certificate; failure to remit export proceeds within the statutory 180-day term or absence of admissible transport documents triggers automatic reversal of the zero-rating. DIAN assesses the omitted IVA at 19% plus daily default interest and imposes a penalty of up to 100% of the tax omitted under Estatuto Tributario Article 648; fraudulent invoicing, fake export declarations or misused export refunds expose the SAS to criminal liability for tax evasion under Law 2277/2022, retroactivity up to the 5-year limitation period, disqualification from the 9% export incentive regime, and joint personal liability of the legal representative and tax preparer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: COP 6,353,775,000 (135,000 UVT for the 2024 taxable year)

Economic substance scope: Colombia has not enacted a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) as seen in Caribbean jurisdictions. However, the DIAN applies the doctrine of substance through the general anti-abuse rule in Article 120 ET, the restriction on fictitious transactions, and the requirement of a valid business purpose under Article 658 ET. A foreign-owned local hotpot chain must demonstrate operational substance—employees, managerial oversight, physical restaurant premises, accounting records, and commercial-trading control—before claiming treaty benefits or counterpart documentation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (cash-skimming)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 671 ET, an omission entailing underdeclared income attracts a fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax, plus default interest and criminal exposure under Articles 330-332 of the Criminal Code; direct criminal consequence for the local statutory representative.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives and sole shareholders of a SAS may face personal civil liability for all unpaid corporate taxes and fines; criminal conviction can result in imprisonment of four to nine years, travel bans, and extradition proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DIAN is authorised to increase the SAS's taxable income to the arm's length price; Article 260-11 ET imposes penalties of 1% to 5% of the value of each unreported related-party transaction, plus a specific fine of COP 1,380,000 for each omitted local file and interest on the primary adjustment.

Director personal liability: The statutory agent is personally liable for the accuracy of transfer pricing declarations; knowingly submitting false certificates constitutes procedural fraud under Article 434 of the Criminal Code, carrying imprisonment of twenty-four to sixty months and disqualification from holding board positions.
Shell Company Abuse / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: DIAN disallows the entire deduction under the GAAR and Article 120 ET, recharacterises the transaction as fraudulent, applies a 35% penalty on the corrected tax, and imposes the interest accruing from the original due date; if the structure triggers a treaty benefit denial, the entire foreign income remains taxable in Colombia and the foreign intermediary becomes liable for the withheld tax.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil protection is removed under Law 222 of 1995 when the entity is used as a mere instrument to evade taxes; directors and shareholders become jointly and severally liable for all national taxes, interest, and sanctions, and can be barred from leaving Colombia pending resolution of the offence.
AML and Illegal Cash-Flow Structuring

Statutory sanction: Under Law 526 of 2021 and the National Code of Compliance, a merchant accepting cash must report cash transactions above COP 12,900,000 (2024) on the Formación SARLAFT; failure to file or structuring transactions constitutes an administrative penalty of up to 5,000 SMLV or suspension of the commercial registration and revocation of the operating license.

Director personal liability: The legal representative is criminally liable for money-laundering under Articles 323-333 of the Criminal Code; if the restaurant is held as a legal fiction to channel illicit capital, directors may be personally fined an amount equal to 150% of the laundered source, receive a travel ban and asset freezing, and serve a prison term of ten to twenty years.

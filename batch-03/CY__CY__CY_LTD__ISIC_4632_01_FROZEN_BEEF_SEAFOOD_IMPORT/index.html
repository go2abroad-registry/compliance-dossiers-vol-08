Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus (official e-portal: Tax For All)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity seeking to carry on taxable activity in Cyprus must file the relevant registration form (e.g., TD Form for VAT and/or employer registration) with the Department of Taxation through the Tax For All portal. A Cyprus private limited liability company incorporated under the Companies Law is automatically registered for corporate income tax upon incorporation and receives a TIN. Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment must apply for a tax registration number and may be required to appoint a tax representative for VAT compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation, territorial scope: Cyprus resident companies are taxed on worldwide income; non-residents are taxed on Cyprus-sourced income. Cyprus does not operate tax-exempt zones; a special IP Box regime applies an effective 2.5% rate on qualifying intangible property income.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Cyprus applies the EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules. The IP Box regime allows an 80% notional exemption from taxable profit derived from qualifying intangible assets, subject to election and filing requirements. Companies may elect group taxation under common control conditions, and voluntary VAT registration is available where the turnover threshold is not met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable supplies of goods and services by an established taxable person; EUR 10,000 for intra-Community distance sales by a non-established supplier (general EU distance selling threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of goods, including cold-chain frozen meat and seafood, where the seller retains conclusive evidence that the goods have been physically removed from the Republic of Cyprus and delivered to a destination outside the EU within the statutory time frame. Services related to export shipments, such as transportation and handling of exports, may also qualify under the VAT Directive conditions. The zero rate is claimed via the VAT return with supporting documentation.

  • Single Administrative Document (SAD) customs export declaration for the relevant customs office in Cyprus
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or CMR/Airway Bill (road/air transit) evidencing dispatch to a non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bank SWIFT/TT or inward remittance certificate confirming receipt of the export purchase price on a Cyprus bank account
  • Licensing and slaughter/cold-chain compliance documents for frozen beef and seafood exports (e.g., veterinary health certificates) where demanded by customs or the Veterinary Services
  • Transport evidence such as vessel lists, container tracking, and port departure stamps
  • A copy of the import customs declaration at the destination country if available

Cyprus does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoicing, or digital stamping for B2B VAT supply chains. VAT invoices remain mandatory for B2B transactions, and an electronic VAT return (Form VAT 101) must be submitted monthly or quarterly via the Tax For All portal. E-invoicing is permitted but not cleared by the tax authority before transmission.

The Cyprus Tax Department will disallow zero-rating and issue a VAT assessment for the standard rate plus statutory interest if export documentation is unavailable, incomplete, or inconsistent. Cases of fraudulent export zero-rating or false customs declarations are subject to substantial administrative penalties, criminal fines of up to hundreds of thousands of euros, and imprisonment for officers of the company. Retrospective clawback remains open for a seven-year period under the VAT (Amending) provisions for deliberate concealment and for up to ten years where tax fraud or misrepresentation has occurred; customs authorities may also impose separate penalties for inaccurate SAD declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a eurozone Member State and applies no exchange control restrictions on current or capital account transactions among EU Member States or with third countries. The euro is fully convertible; profit remittance is unrestricted for non-resident shareholders, and no Cyprus withholding tax applies to dividends remitted to non-residents, subject to documentary evidence of beneficial ownership. Banks may impose standard AML/CFT documentation requirements for large outgoing transfers, but no permit or conversion obligation is required under Cypriot law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds. Exporters may hold foreign currency receipts in external or domestic foreign-currency accounts. All cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 15,000 must be reported by banks to the CBC through the International Transaction Reporting System (ITRS) for balance-of-payments and ECB statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC); CySEC regulates FX brokerage and payment services where applicable, but banking and monetary authority remains with the CBC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate as an ancillary collection or disbursement vehicle only; it is not a substitute for a CBC-licensed credit institution as the primary clearing rail. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) must hold an EEA passport or a CySEC/CBC authorization under the Electronic Money Law L. 140(I)/2018; unlicensed third-country EMIs cannot legally provide settlement services to a Cyprus corporate treasury without a branch or agent in Cyprus.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 in aggregate controlled related-party transactions per tax year, as applied by the Cyprus Tax Department in administrative practice; multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more are additionally subject to EU CbCR and Master File expectations. Even below the threshold, the Cyprus company must keep contemporaneous arm's length evidence for all related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not administer a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing, but applies a functional substance doctrine based on the OECD 'place of effective management' test and the general prohibition of tax avoidance. A Cyprus Ltd importing deep-frozen beef and seafood must maintain in Cyprus: a real local office, independent local management, actual board meetings or equivalent decisions, appropriate employees or outsourced service providers, bank accounts, and transaction records. Without such substance, tax residence may be challenged, TRCs refused, the IP box and notional interest deductions voided, and treaty benefits blocked by the Principal Purpose Test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Assessment to tax on the true profits plus a penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax; late-payment interest; criminal prosecution before the criminal court with fines equal to the amounts evaded and/or imprisonment. The Cyprus courts may impose a custodial sentence of up to five years for tax fraud depending on value and intent.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or intentionally cause the company to evade tax may be made personally liable for the tax loss through the fraudulent trading provisions of the Companies Law, Cap. 113. The court may also issue civil restitution orders, disqualification from serving as director for up to five years, travel bans pending recovery, and imprisonment upon conviction.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of the Cyprus tax residence certificate; retrospective reallocation of taxable profits to the jurisdiction of effective management; administrative fines under the Companies Law for defective registered office and misleading filings; loss of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Nominee and inactive directors are at risk of civil restitution for facilitating tax evasion, disqualification proceedings by the Registrar of Companies, and personal criminal liability where they knowingly permitted the corporate façade to be used for unlawful tax avoidance.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Enforcement action under Cyprus AML laws and E.U. AML Directives; corporate fines, freezing and confiscation of cargo or bank balances, revocation of import/trading licences, and publication of AML enforcement notices. Depending on the scale, fines can run into hundreds of thousands of euros.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, and compliance officers qualify as 'accountable persons' and may be personally criminally liable. Assets can be seized, travel bans imposed, and imprisonment of up to 14 years can be imposed for wilful money laundering.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Cyprus Tax Department adjustment to the arm's length profit under the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines; assessment of additional tax at 12.5%; interest on the overdue tax; administrative penalties equal to a percentage of the adjustment; disallowance of the inflated deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign grossly excessive related-party payments without economic justification may be personally liable in a misfeasance or fraudulent preference claim, required to repay lost corporate assets, and disqualified from acting as directors.
Late Filing / Underpaid Provisional Tax

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of EUR 100 per return, EUR 10 per day for continued default, late-payment interest on any balance, and refusal of a Cyprus tax clearance certificate. Underpaid provisional tax is assessed with an additional charge or interest and can result in a full withholding of refunds.

Director personal liability: In a serious or prolonged default, the Tax Commissioner can seek a personal assessment against directors who were responsible and cannot demonstrate that the failure was beyond their control. Travel restrictions may also be ordered in tax collection procedures.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A Cyprus private company must maintain a registered office address in Cyprus at all times. In practice, a virtual office or service-provider accommodation address at the premises of a licensed corporate service provider is permitted for registration purposes, provided that a physical address is maintained for service of process, statutory records, and inspection by the Registrar. For an e-commerce and custom-manufacturing operation, the tax authorities and AML supervision expect that actual business activity — invoices, records, management meetings and decision-making — be demonstrably linked to Cyprus; a purely virtual office without local substance will create an elevated tax-residence and economic-substance audit risk.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver, Ministry of Energy, Commerce and Industry, Republic of Cyprus (Nicosia headquarters); all filings are executed electronically via the Cyprus Business Portal e-incorporation service (businessportal.com.cy).

Governing statute

Companies Law, Cap. 113 of the Laws of Cyprus, as amended, governing incorporation of private limited companies, principally Sections 6 (mode of incorporation), 7 (memorandum of association), 9 (form of memorandum), 10 (signing of memorandum) and 72 (return of allotments); e-incorporation implemented through the Companies (Amendment) Law 135(I)/2021 and the Cyprus Business Portal; beneficial owner registration under the Register of Beneficial Owners Part of Cap. 113 (inserted by Law 13(I)/2021); Stamp Duty Law 19/1963 applies to share capital.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum and Articles of Association of a Private Limited Company (bilingual English/Greek), submitted with the online Incorporation Application (e-form), signed Consents to Act of directors and secretary, and the integrated Beneficial Owner Declaration.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Cyprus private limited company under Cap. 113; at incorporation the subscribers may take one or more shares of €1.00 each (typical authorized capital €1,000–€2,000 fully subscribed). No escrow account or bank deposit of capital is required to incorporate; the Return of Allotment (Section 72) must be filed within one month of each allotment. The absence of a pre-funded capital requirement does not affect the directors' duty to maintain sufficient capital for operating obligations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate for industrial occupancy issued by the Cyprus Fire Service after site inspection (extinguishers, emergency exits, evacuation plan); structural adequacy certificate by a registered civil engineer for floor loading of injection moulding presses; electrical installation inspection under the Electricity Law; and workplace clearance under the Factories Act, Cap. 134 covering machine guarding, noise control, ventilation, and welfare facilities, issued by the Department of Labour Inspection before operations commence.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Cyprus private limited company under Cap. 113; at incorporation the subscribers may take one or more shares of €1.00 each (typical authorized capital €1,000–€2,000 fully subscribed). No escrow account or bank deposit of capital is required to incorporate; the Return of Allotment (Section 72) must be filed within one month of each allotment. The absence of a pre-funded capital requirement does not affect the directors' duty to maintain sufficient capital for operating obligations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Tax Department (Τμήμα Φόρων), Ministry of Finance — official Tax Portal: https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy. VAT administration is handled by the Tax Department while customs and import/export controls are handled by the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department (https://www.mof.gov.cy/cec).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first incorporate a Cyprus private limited company at the Registrar of Companies or register a Cyprus branch of the foreign parent company. The incorporation or branch registration particulars are transmitted to the Cyprus Tax Department, which issues the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN). For VAT, the entity must submit a VAT registration application in the statutory form prescribed by the Tax Department, attaching the certificate of incorporation, registered office confirmation, constitutional documents, directorship and ownership identification, and a description of the intended export activity. A non-EU-established company must appoint a Cypriot VAT representative. The Tax Department normally issues the permanent VAT registration certificate within one to three weeks after the filing has been validated.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must first incorporate a Cyprus private limited company at the Registrar of Companies or register a Cyprus branch of the foreign parent company. The incorporation or branch registration particulars are transmitted to the Cyprus Tax Department, which issues the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN). For VAT, the entity must submit a VAT registration application in the statutory form prescribed by the Tax Department, attaching the certificate of incorporation, registered office confirmation, constitutional documents, directorship and ownership identification, and a description of the intended export activity. A non-EU-established company must appoint a Cypriot VAT representative. The Tax Department normally issues the permanent VAT registration certificate within one to three weeks after the filing has been validated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus has no US-style check-the-box election. A Cyprus private limited company is per se treated as an entity subject to Cyprus income tax at the corporate rate of 12.5% on worldwide profits, irrespective of the residence of its shareholders. Tax-transparent treatment is available only for partnerships and limited partnerships under the Cyprus Income Tax Law. No pass-through election or hybrid mismatch election exists for a private limited company, but cross-border arrangements are screened for EU ATAD hybrid mismatch rules. Specific elective regimes (e.g., tonnage tax for shipping or the IP box) are not applicable to custom mechanical keyboard cap manufacturing and export.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover excluding VAT for taxable supplies in Cyprus. For intra-Community distance sales made to customers in Cyprus, the registration threshold is EUR 35,000 per calendar year. Non-established taxable persons are generally required to register from the first relevant supply where the reverse-charge mechanism does not apply; no turnover threshold benefits them.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of custom mechanical keyboard caps to a customer located outside the EU qualifies for the 0% VAT rate if the goods physically leave the EU through a Cypriot or other EU customs office, title passes to the non-EU buyer, and the exporter holds a complete documentary trail of the export, including the customs export declaration, commercial invoice, transport documentation, and proof of receipt by the foreign buyer. Supplies of services ancillary to the exported goods (e.g., design services, packing, freight insurance) may be treated as directly related and eligible for zero rating to the extent required by the applicable VAT rules. Offshore services supplied to a foreign taxable person are generally outside the scope of Cyprus VAT and subject to reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Cyprus Single Administrative Document (SAD) or the electronic export declaration lodged through the EU Automated Export System
  • Bill of Lading or other international transport documentation (CMR waybill, airway bill, seaway bill, or delivery proof with package ID)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice from a licensed Cyprus bank evidencing that export proceeds have been received in Cyprus
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and purchase order records that are fully consistent with the customs declaration

No mandatory national e-invoicing system and no real-time tax clearance / Continuous Transaction Controls are currently enforced for B2B or B2C invoices. Electronic invoices are accepted provided the authenticity, integrity, and legibility of the invoice are maintained. EU B2G e-invoicing standards under Directive 2014/55/EU apply to the supply of goods to Cypriot public authorities. Cyprus has not yet implemented a national API reporting or digital stamping mandate; future EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' e-invoicing obligations may impose CTC-style clearance in later years.

Export zero-rating is an intensely scrutinised area. Cyprus VAT authorities may request the export documentary bundle during audit and impose short deadlines for its production. If the exporter fails to provide satisfactory proof of physical export or shows evidence that export proceeds were not repatriated within the administrative 180-day benchmark, the 0% rate is retrospectively clawed back and the full VAT output tax becomes due. Statutory interest and administrative penalties of up to 200% of the tax assessed may be imposed, and criminal proceedings may be instituted for forged export declarations, false inward remittance certificates, or fraudulent evasion of VAT. Export records and supporting documentation must be retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible across both current and capital account lines. Cyprus imposes no general exchange control law, and no prior central bank approval is required for inbound equity injection, dividend remittance, interest payments, or trade-related transfers. Profit repatriation by a wholly foreign-owned Cyprus Limited company to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, subject solely to standard corporate tax, withholding tax (if applicable), anti-money-laundering verification, and the filing requirements of the Accountant services provider and the Cyprus Tax Department. Access to ECB/market FX liquidity is uninhibited, and the practical hurdle is not convertibility but bank counterparty compliance and proof of economic substance for the underlying commercial activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio to euro. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, converted at the bank's commercial rate, or used directly to pay overseas suppliers. The only obligations are evidentiary: the receiving bank must ensure the transaction is consistent with the client's declared business activity, and resident companies must maintain auditable commercial records for VAT, corporate income tax, and Central Bank of Cyprus balance-of-payments statistical reporting. Intra-EU exports are not subject to customs documentation, while extra-EU exports must be evidenced by customs declarations for VAT zero-rating purposes.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) acting within the Eurosystem and the European Central Bank's Single Supervisory Mechanism; foreign exchange policy for euro-denominated Cyprus entities is governed by EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital rather than by national exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High for commercial settlement but not for statutory capital escrow. Cyprus recognises EU Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions passported under PSD2 and EMD2; a non-Cyprus EMI such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Multitude/Leonteq-backed rails may open accounts remotely for a Cyprus Ltd and provide EUR IBANs, SWIFT/SEPA clearing, and multi-currency current accounts. However, leading Cypriot clearing banks and the Central Bank of Cyprus regard passported EMIs as lower-assurance counterparties for corporate lending, trade finance, and high-value inbound equity remittances. Therefore EMIs should be used only as sub-rails for Shopify/Stripe payouts and supplier payments while a domestic tier-1 account retains primary liquidity.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Non-resident sole shareholder without EU tax residence

Defense evidence: Certified passport copies, proof of tax residence, source-of-wealth statement, bank reference from the shareholder's home institution, audited financial statements of parent, and a signed declaration confirming the Cyprus entity's genuine commercial substance.
High transaction velocity relative to declared sales for a low-risk e-commerce keyboard accessories company

Root cause: High transaction velocity relative to declared sales for a low-risk e-commerce keyboard accessories company

Defense evidence: Shopify and Stripe settlement reports, order-level invoices, customs declarations, logistics tracking, supplier purchase orders, shipping carrier bills of lading, and a cash-flow forecast reconciled to the expected e-commerce revenue cycle.
Inbound capital contribution from a jurisdiction on FATF or EU listing processes

Root cause: Inbound capital contribution from a jurisdiction on FATF or EU listing processes

Defense evidence: Notarised proof of lawful origin, the underlying commercial contract, share register and board resolution authorising issuance, a formal tax opinion confirming the funds are not criminal proceeds, and a confirmation that the Cyprus entity has an economic employer and real production/warehousing arrangements, not merely letterbox activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a Cyprus Ltd receiving share capital from a non-EU shareholder: instruct an outgoing SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank with transparent purpose code describing 'equity capital contribution' and reference the Cypriot company's registration number. Cyprus does not require clearance from the Central Bank of Cyprus for the initial injection, but the receiving bank will conduct a source-of-funds inquiry and may require the signed board resolution, shareholder resolution, and updated share allotment form. Funds should arrive in EUR at a Cypriot bank account via SWIFT or be converted from USD to EUR using the receiving bank's dealing desk. For amounts above €50,000, the bank's AML transaction monitoring will require the legal basis of the payment to be stated in the credit advice. Same-day construction is not available for cross-border; normal RFS/SEPA arrival is T+1 to T+2.Outbound supplier settlements for PBT resin and keyboard-cap tooling are executed primarily by SWIFT MT103 for USD/CNY payments and by SEPA Credit Transfer for euro transactions. For large or repetitive raw-material purchase commitments, the Cyprus entity is advised to open a commercial letter of credit (sight or usance) with its domestic bank using standard UCP 600 terms, especially when suppliers require bank confirmation. Shipping documents required under the LC include commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and certificate of origin. Direct MT103 transfers are acceptable for smaller payments and prepayments, but require strong underlying contracts. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR – a Simplified TP File is mandatory for tax years from 2022 where aggregate related-party transactions are between EUR 750,000 and EUR 5,000,000; a full Local File is mandatory where aggregate related-party transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000; documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and produced within 60 days of a Tax Department request.

Economic substance scope: Applicable – Cyprus applies economic substance requirements consistent with EU Code of Conduct Group guidance and OECD standards (Tax Department Circular on economic substance and Section 33A of the Income Tax Law). For this ISIC 3240 manufacturer, substance is evidenced by a physical production facility in Cyprus, double-shot injection moulding machines and tooling, local payroll for operators and designers, Cypriot bank accounts receiving Shopify and Stripe settlements, and management-and-control exercised through Cyprus-resident directors holding board meetings in Cyprus; a letterbox entity with all production, sales and decision-making abroad will be re-domiciled for tax purposes, stripped of treaty benefits and subject to TP challenges under the DEMPE analysis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Income Tax Law and the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, conviction carries a fine of up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; the Tax Department may additionally assess the evaded tax with a penalty uplift of up to 100% plus statutory interest of 1.75% per annum, and may enforce seizure of bank accounts and assets.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil is pierced under Companies Law Cap. 113, section 305 (fraudulent trading) and section 306 (wrongful trading): directors, shadow directors and the non-resident UBO can be held personally liable for the company's unpaid tax and damages; courts may issue travel bans, asset-freezing orders and disqualification orders of 2 to 12 years, and criminal custodial sentences of up to 5 years.
Shell Company and Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Tax Department may treat management and control as exercised offshore, reclassify the company as non-Cyprus tax resident, reject the Cyprus registration for VAT and tax purposes, make transfer pricing adjustments based on OECD DEMPE analysis, and impose penalties of up to 100% of the undercharged tax together with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal assessment for taxes where the company is struck off, disqualification orders under the Companies Law, and personal liability in winding-up for fraudulent trading; the foreign principal who controls the sham structure may be pursued under mutual assistance and the European Arrest Warrant for tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Tax Department may adjust the taxable profit to the arm's-length position, disallow the intercompany expense in full, impose the 5% per month late payment surcharge (cap 25%) on the additional assessment, and levy administrative fines for non-production of documentation under Section 33A; TP documentation is treated as mandatory and its absence shifts the burden of proof to the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the TD4 without verifying TP documentation expose themselves to personal liability in insolvency proceedings for losses caused to creditors, and to criminal liability if the mispricing was intentional in evading tax; group CFOs and foreign finance officers who construct artificial pricing can be prosecuted as accomplices.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (L.188(I)/2007) exposes legal persons to fines of up to EUR 500,000 for serious breaches and unlimited fines in aggravated cases; the Registrar of Companies applies daily penalties of EUR 100 to EUR 200 for BO register non-compliance, can strike off the company, and the Tax Department may suspend the VAT number.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who sign declarations without knowing the UBO commit a criminal offence punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment; the foreign principal who directs concealment is liable for money laundering as a principal offender, with confiscation orders over the proceeds of crime and personal asset seizure.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permissible for incorporation and general correspondence; Colombia recognizes virtual or address service offices for S.A.S. purposes if the registered address serves for law-mandated notifications, judicial service (notificaciones judiciales), and UIAF/Superintendencia formal notices. However, for ISIC 6201 software and cloud operations, the tax authority (DIAN) may disallow a pure virtual address for VAT invoicing registration and permanent establishment risk avoidance if actual management is performed abroad. Given vertical ERP/CRM cloud operations with Colombian data processing, the company must demonstrate at least one physical management office, a permanent employee or a rent contract, plus local telecom infrastructure to sustain the 'effective place of management' doctrine under Article 12-1 of Law 1819 of 2016 (anti-tax-haven rules). Virtual office alone is not sufficient for banking onboarding with local Colombian banks (e.g., Bancolombia, Davivienda), which typically require a physical inspection of commercial premises by their risk department before opening corporate account.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for an S.A.S. The shareholders freely determine paid-up and subscribed capital. If subscribed capital is stated, at least 20% of the subscribed shares must be paid up at execution. No escrow or bank account is mandated for the paid-in amount unless the bylaws voluntarily require one for management of contributions.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

DIAN - Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (National Tax and Customs Directorate); official portal www.dian.gov.co; electronic taxpayer portal Sede Electrónica (MUISCA/ Nueva DIAN) for registrations, filings, e-invoicing and refund proceedings.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute and apostille a corporate power of attorney appointing a Colombian resident legal representative and tax agent. Step 2: Formalize the S.A.S. deed before a Notary and register it with the local Cámara de Comercio, obtaining the Certificado de Existencia y Representación Legal. Step 3: File the electronic Pre-RUT through the DIAN Sede Electrónica using the representative's digital signature. Step 4: Receive the statutory NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and definitive RUT (Registro Único Tributario), which assigns the taxpayer as responsible for IVA (VAT) under the Régimen Común, income tax at entity level, withholding agency, and electronic invoicing. Step 5: Activate the Firma Electrónica and the CUFE/CUDS stamping credentials and contract an authorized proveedor tecnológico for facturación electrónica. Step 6: Register the ISIC 6201 activity and, if goods are exported, obtain the export registration in the customs and exchange systems.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Colombian S.A.S. is a resident juridical person taxed on worldwide income (Articles 12 and 22 E.T. and Law 2277/2022), with a headline corporate income tax rate of 35% for 2024; Pass_Through_Eligible: No, no fiscal transparency to shareholders; Territorial_Scope: Worldwide for a Colombian-resident S.A.S.; Tax_Exempt_Zone: N/A unless the entity obtains a DIAN-recognized Zona Franca user status, which is elective, not default.

Available election forms

Colombian tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (i) the SIMPLE regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación, Ley 2010/2019, Article 905 E.T.), elective for S.A.S. with gross revenues up to 100,000 UVT in the prior year, replacing income tax with a combined progressive rate of approximately 1.8% to 14.5%; (ii) application for Zona Franca status with reduced 15% income tax for qualifying software exporters; (iii) optional selection of the bimonthly VAT filing and refund period based on export volume; (iv) election to keep exchange-rate accounting gains under specific rules; and (v) no hybrid mismatch deductions are available to a Colombian resident S.A.S., and BEPS Action 2 limitations are applied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de-minimis turnover registration threshold for corporate entities: VAT registration in the RUT is mandatory from the first taxable act or service provision by a S.A.S. (Articles 437 and 555-2 E.T.). The only relevant threshold is the SIMPLE regime eligibility ceiling of 100,000 UVT (~COP 4,706,500,000 for 2024), which does not waive VAT registration obligations for export-oriented software suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Article 481 E.T., Decree 1073/2017 and Decreto 1625/2016 to exported goods and to exported services, including vertical ERP/CRM and SaaS software (ISIC 6201), when all conditions are met: (i) the service is covered by the statutory list comprising software development, maintenance, technical support, licenses and cloud services; (ii) the acquirer is a foreign resident without a Colombian permanent establishment and the service produces no economic use or benefit inside Colombia; (iii) the contract, FEV, technical reports and cloud usage logs evidence service delivery and foreign consumption; (iv) payment is received in foreign currency through the Colombian foreign exchange market; (v) the exporter files the Declaración de Cambio and, for supporting inputs, maintains full VAT credits, which become refundable as saldos a favor.

  • DEX (Declaración de Exportación) for goods or, for services, the service-export contract and technical certificate of execution abroad
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill for goods; for software services, the electronic delivery log and cloud consumption report
  • Factura Comercial (Commercial Invoice) and FEV - Factura Electrónica de Venta de Exportación with CUFE stamp and explicit 0% VAT clause
  • Declaración de Cambio (FX declaration, Form 1 / DC-1) and Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Colombian authorized intermediary
  • Foreign client corporate identification and a written statement confirming the non-existence of a Colombian permanent establishment

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta, FEV) under Resolución DIAN 000165/2023 and the unified Anexo Técnico: the S.A.S. or its authorized proveedor tecnológico must transmit XML invoices to DIAN web-services before issuance, incorporate the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) or CUDS digital stamping, exchange acceptance/rejection events with the buyer, and report export invoices under the 'Exportación de servicios' modality; export invoices cite the DEX or the service export contract and zero-rate VAT. Refund claims and the VAT return (Form 300) are cross-validated against the electronic invoice database.

DIAN reconciles the FEV/CUFE e-invoice database, the VAT return Form 300, the DEX customs statements and the Banco de la República foreign-exchange database to verify that export proceeds entered Colombia within the 180-day period. If the zero-rating claim fails, DIAN may: (i) reassess the 19% IVA on the disputed export invoices with default interest under Article 635 E.T. and inflation adjustment; (ii) impose the imprecision penalty of 100% to 200% of the omitted VAT under Article 643 E.T.; (iii) levy electronic-invoicing fines of up to 1% of gross revenue under Article 652 E.T.; (iv) deny or retroactively claw back the VAT refund balance (saldos a favor) and disallow the corresponding input credits; and (v) refer the case for criminal tax fraud under Article 402 of the Colombian Criminal Code if forged export invoices or exchange declarations are detected; ordinary audits may extend beyond three years in cases of omitted declarations, with longer limitation periods in criminal and penalty proceedings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating exchange rate with full convertibility on current account transactions, including trade in goods, services, royalties, and profit remittances, subject to mandatory channeling through authorized exchange market intermediaries for receipts and payments with non-residents. Capital account operations, including foreign direct investment and cross-border loans, remain generally free but require ex post registration with the Banco de la República and must be channeled through local banks. Dividend and profit remittances to foreign shareholders are lawful, provided the S.A.S. is tax-compliant, the payment is supported by certified financial statements, and the funds are routed via a Colombian bank through the exchange market using the appropriate CAMALEIS declaration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from the sale of software and technology services, when classified as goods or services under Colombian export rules, must be channeled through an authorized local financial intermediary within the statutory term of six months from the date of shipment or service completion, unless an extension is expressly approved by the Banco de la República. The mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts is 100% of the invoiced foreign currency amount, meaning the funds must be brought into Colombia and converted into Colombian pesos, or held in a foreign currency deposit account at a local authorized bank that reports the position to the central bank. Early repatriation is permitted, and documentary evidence of invoicing, invoice number, and customs or service export declaration must be submitted to the intermediary to complete the foreign exchange declaration.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia, acting through its Exchange Operations Department and the regulations of the Comisión de Cambios; prudential supervision is exercised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore electronic money institutions and non-resident EMIs. Colombian law does not recognize foreign EMIs as authorized exchange intermediaries; only institutions supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia may open local accounts, receive FX channeled funds, or access the interbank clearing infrastructure. Offshore EMIs can be used only as auxiliary billing or client-payment software interfaces, provided they are not used for custody of COP funds or for settlement of export proceeds without a local partner bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment for the Colombian S.A.S. must be registered with the Banco de la República using the foreign investment registration form and the associated CAMALEIS line, and the capital injection must be wired as a clean international transfer via SWIFT MT103 to an operational or escrow account held by an authorized Colombian bank. The wire must include the investor's legal name, the company's NIT, the purpose code corresponding to foreign direct investment in software and technology services, and a reference to the notarized share subscription agreement. The receiving bank will require a copy of the registration confirmation before crediting the funds to the company's account; failure to register within the statutory timeline may trigger exchange regime penalties.Outbound trade settlements for vendor payments, licensing royalties, and subcontractor disbursements shall be executed through SWIFT MT103 instructions from the Colombian operating account or, when transaction volume and risk justify it, through irrevocable documentary commercial letters of credit issued by a Colombian bank. For service and software subscription payments, clean payment transfers under the foreign exchange intermediary must be accompanied by the relevant import declaration or service contract, pro-forma invoice, and exchange declaration to satisfy the Banco de la República's periodic reporting duties.Delivery-based forward contracts between COP and USD entered into with a Colombian authorized bank or broker, covering a specific trade invoice or committed capital flow.Non-deliverable forwards entered into with offshore counterparties, provided the settlement difference is paid outside Colombia and the contract is not settled in COP within the national territory; such instruments are used for board-approved hedging of foreign-exchange risk.Cross-currency swaps between COP and USD executed through a local authorized intermediary, limited to matching cash-flow positions in the ERP subscription and capital expenditure cycle.Exchange-traded or OTC options on USD/COP for income protection and for the management of contingent balance-sheet risk arising from non-collection of foreign receivables.

Annual Corporate Income Tax and Complementary Return (Declaración de Renta y Complementario) Annually in the DIAN-fixed filing window for legal persons covering the preceding calendar year; exact days are announced by DIAN Resolution and are allocated by the last two NIT digits, typically in March-April $0 USD Failure to file or pay by the deadline triggers late-filing fines under ET Arts. 641 and 642, default interest, inability to obtain tax clearance certificates, and, after official assessment, enforced collection by DIAN.

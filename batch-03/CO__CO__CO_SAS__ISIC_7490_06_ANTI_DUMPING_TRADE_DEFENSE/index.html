Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Ley 1258 de 2008; capital is freely determined by the shareholders. Subscribed shares must be paid within two (2) years from incorporation; no escrow account is mandated for cash contributions, but contributions in kind must be itemized, valued, and delivered as recorded in the deed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos for the registered office; structural load and occupancy inspection by the municipal planning authority confirming office-use compliance; workplace safety and health workplace clearance registered with the contracted ARL; compliance with minimum occupational hygiene standards under Resolution 2400 de 1979.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official electronic portal: https://www.dian.gov.co and DIAN's 'Sede Electrónica' taxpayer virtual center for RUT application, NIT issuance, electronic invoicing validation, and export declaration management.

E-government filing portal

A non-domiciled foreign entity must first obtain a DIAN-issued electronic signature ('firma electrónica') for online authentication, then register in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) to obtain or update a Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT). For an Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.) with non-resident shareholders, the registration is performed by the legal representative (or appointed domiciliary agent) through DIAN's RUT en línea platform, submitting apostilled and Spanish-translated constitutional documents, proof of legal existence in the home jurisdiction, a notarized power of attorney evidencing the representative's appointment, and, where applicable, a Colombian branch or agency license issued by the Superintendencia de Sociedades. After validation of RUT data, DIAN issues a single NIT number that simultaneously covers entity status, VAT, income tax, and customs/foreign trade registration obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (resident Colombian S.A.S. corporations are separate taxpayers subject to corporate income tax on their worldwide income; no pass-through regime applies). For a foreign parent with a Colombian permanent establishment through an S.A.S. branch context, the territorial scope is limited to Colombia-source income, and the S.A.S. itself remains the VAT and customs taxable person. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident Colombian S.A.S. corporations are separate taxpayers subject to corporate income tax on their worldwide income; no pass-through regime applies). For a foreign parent with a Colombian permanent establishment through an S.A.S. branch context, the territorial scope is limited to Colombia-source income, and the S.A.S. itself remains the VAT and customs taxable person. Tax-Exempt_Zone benefits are not automatic and require specific Zona Franca registration.

Available election forms

Colombia does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' or elective hybrid classification mechanism. A Colombian S.A.S. may however elect: (i) the 'Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE)' under Articles 903-915 of the Estatuto Tributario for eligible entities below the 100,000 UVT gross revenue threshold; (ii) consolidated or group taxation election for entities forming a business group ('grupo empresarial'); (iii) the option to appear before DIAN for permanent or bimonthly export VAT refunds under Decreto 1625 de 2016; (iv) Zona Franca developer or user regime elections, if applicable; and (v) the ancillary election to request extension of the foreign-exchange repatriation deadline before Banco de la República. Hybrid mismatch rules under Law 2010 of 2019 are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general monetary turnover threshold triggering IVA registration for S.A.S. entities: any legal person habitually supplying taxable goods or services in Colombia must register in the RUT and take a category as IVA responsible ('responsable del IVA'). The 'Régimen Simplificado' small-trader exception is available only to natural persons/artisans and does not apply to an S.A.S.; however, for reference, simplified taxpayers are limited to gross income below 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 170 million per year) and other statutory conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are eligible for the 0% VAT rate when the exporter is an IVA-responsible entity, the goods permanently leave Colombian customs territory under an accepted DEX, and documentary proof of physical exit and foreign payment is maintained. Export services, including legal and trade-defense consulting, qualify for 0% VAT only if the beneficiary is a non-domiciled entity, the service is used exclusively abroad, the service is rendered from Colombia but not connected to the production or marketing of taxable goods within Colombia, and the foreign client receives formal invoices plus service certificates. Inbound payments must be channeled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market, and a written contract describing the offshore use must be kept.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DEX) accepted by DIAN
  • Bill of Lading (ocean transport) or Air Waybill (air cargo) evidencing physical exit of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Canalización de Divisas) issued by an authorized Colombian foreign-exchange intermediary
  • Final commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export contract or foreign purchase order containing Incoterms and payment terms
  • Certificate of origin and any applicable export permit or license (sanitary, environmental, or customs broker authorization)

All VAT-responsible entities must issue invoices through DIAN's mandatory electronic invoicing system ('Facturación Electrónica'). The invoice XML must be transmitted in real time to DIAN's validation platform, either by an authorized 'Proveedor Tecnológico' or through DIAN's free software application, before the invoice is legally delivered. Once validated, each invoice is assigned either a 'Código Único de Factura Electrónica (CUFE)' for standard sales invoices or a 'Código Único de Documento Soporte (CUDS)' for purchase/support documents. The generated PDF or XML must carry a QR code linking to DIAN repositories, the issuer's digital certificate, and the DIAN electronic stamp. Export invoices and supporting declarations (Declaración de Exportación / DEX) are also transmitted through the same system and must match the customs exit records.

DIAN may audit any export operation where the DEX, shipping documents, service contract, or foreign-exchange proceeds are missing, inconsistent, or backdated. If zero-rating is disallowed, DIAN will retroactively assess the unpaid 19% IVA with monthly default interest and applies the 'sanción por inexactitud' under Article 647 of the Estatuto Tributario, generally equal to 160% of the greater amount of tax due or refund obtained. Improperly obtained export VAT refunds are clawed back with retroactive effect together with penalties of up to 20% of the refunded amount, and the taxpayer may be suspended from operating or invoicing for up to ten days. Where fraud or criminal intent is shown, DIAN and the Fiscalía General de la Nación may bring charges under Articles 402 and 403 of the Código Penal, carrying imprisonment, disqualification, and fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is generally convertible for current account operations, including payments for services, royalties, and profit remittances, subject to mandatory foreign exchange channeling through authorized intermediaries and registration in the Declaración de Cambio system. Capital account operations, including foreign direct investment, require prior registration with Banco de la República through a local authorized intermediary using Form 5 (Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales). Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted once the underlying investment is registered, corporate tax clearances (e.g., the parafiscal and tax compliance certificate issued by DIAN) are obtained, and the remittance is channeled through the exchange market. Bucket operations, offshore settlement outside the regulated exchange market, and invoicing practises that avoid exchange channeling constitute strict violations under Colombian foreign exchange regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of goods and services must channel foreign currency proceeds through the Colombian foreign exchange market via authorized intermediaries (banks or financial corporations) within the statutory timelines: goods exports must be collected and channeled within six months from the date of the export declaration (declaración de exportación), and service exports must be collected and channeled within five months from the maturing or accrual date of the corresponding operation, as established in Banco de la República External Regulations and Decree 1068 of 2015, Book 3. There is no mandatory full surrender ratio; the exporter may keep foreign currency in a foreign currency deposit account in a local authorized bank, but the initial receipt and channeling must occur through the intermediario del mercado cambiario. Failure to channel export proceeds within the term triggers corrective declarations, fines, and potential anti-money-laundering scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), with foreign exchange administration partially delegated to the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) for enforcement and customs-related control; the Junta Directiva del Banco de la República issues foreign exchange regulations under Decree 1068 of 2015.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and neobanks not licensed by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia have limited legal viability for receiving Colombian-source trade or service export revenue. Any cross-border virtual account that seeks to replace mandated exchange market channeling is non-compliant. Locally regulated financial technology companies (e.g., authorized Sociedades Especializadas en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos – SEDPEs) and banking-as-a-service platforms integrated with tier-1 banks are viable for treasury operations, but they cannot replace the intermediario del mercado cambiario for FX channeling. Offshore EMIs may be used solely as auxiliary payment rails for low-value operational expenses, provided all export and capital receipts are separately channelled through a Colombian authorized intermediary.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment into a Colombian S.A.S. must be routed through an authorized foreign exchange intermediary (a Colombian commercial bank) using the Declaración de Cambio for foreign investment (Form 5). The investor or the company must register the investment with Banco de la República within the information system before or after the transfer, as applicable. Wire instructions must include the CITI (Código de Inversión Territorial e Internacional) or the investor identification code, the foreign investment registration code (when available), and a payment reference stating 'Registro de inversión internacional bajo Decreto 1068 de 2015'. The incoming wire must be in USD, EUR, or COP, and the receiving bank will issue a declaración de cambio that simultaneously records the capital injection. The account should be a dedicated operational or capital escrow account, and the 'concepto cambiario' must be coded as 'Inversión directa en el exterior – establecimiento permanente – suplemento de capital' or equivalent for equity contributions.Outbound trade disbursements for anti-dumping defense services (e.g., payments to foreign legal counsel, economic consultants, expert witnesses, and official filing fees) must be executed via SWIFT MT103 for wire transfers, or through documentary commercial letters of credit (UCP600) when the counterparty requires restricted or sight-payment modalities. All outbound service payments require a Declaración de Cambio Form 6 (servicios) submitted through an authorized intermediary, with supporting invoices, contracts, and tax clearance from DIAN confirming no outstanding tax or customs liabilities. The company may hold USD-denominated or EUR-denominated accounts in a local authorized bank to reduce conversion cost, but any payment to a non-resident must be reported to the exchange market within the applicable three-day correction window. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% on dividends paid to non-resident beneficiaries when the profits distributed were previously taxed at the 35% corporate income tax rate. If profits originated from exempt or non-taxed income, the dividend becomes subject to final tax at the rate of 33% as a non-qualified distribution. Resident individual shareholders are additionally taxed at 7.5% on annual dividends above 300 UVT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation applies when the taxpayer's gross income or total gross assets in the preceding taxable year equal or exceed COP 100,000,000,000 (approximately USD 25 million). In addition, taxpayers with related-party cross-border transactions exceeding COP 50,000,000,000 must file the Transfer Pricing Informative Return (Form 120). The thresholds are annually adjusted for inflation by DIAN.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime analogous to the UAE. However, the SAS must maintain real economic substance in Colombia to prevent adverse transfer pricing adjustments, the application of the Colombian General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, Article 658-3 of the Tax Code), and the Controlled Foreign Corporation (CFC) rules (Article 882 of the Tax Code). Substance indicators include a physical office in Colombia, full-time qualified employees, board meetings held in Colombia, execution of contracts and invoices from Colombian territory, and commercial risk assumed by the SAS. Absence of such substance will trigger reinterpretation of the entity as a shell and may lead to denial of treaty benefits and recharacterization of intercompany flows.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Omission of Foreign-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion is punishable with imprisonment of 48 to 108 months (Article 434 of Law 599 of 2000) plus a fine equal to 100% of the evaded tax. Administrative sanctions include a fine of 50% to 100% of the understated tax for an inaccurate return, moratory interest, asset seizure, and commencement of criminal investigation by the Fiscal General.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for restitution of the evaded tax when they signed or ordered the fraudulent return. Colombian courts may pierce the corporate veil under Law 1429/2010 and personal bankruptcy of the directors may be requested. Travel bans, prohibitions on leaving Colombia, and extradition-related proceedings may be imposed while criminal liability is investigated.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule permits DIAN to recharacterize the arrangement and apply a 35% tax on the concealed economic income. Intercompany deductions are disallowed, transfer pricing penalties of up to 20% of the underpaid tax may be imposed, and the taxpayer can be declared a defrauding company with cancellation of the NIT and loss of fiscal rights.

Director personal liability: Directors, shareholders and incorporators may be held jointly and severally liable for the recharacterized taxes and penalties under the doctrine of dominating shareholder liability. Colombian supervisory courts can impose asset attachments, travel bans, and a prohibition on managing any commercial company for up to five years. The veil is pierced when the entity was used to commit or facilitate tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Failure to present the required transfer pricing documentation carries a fine of 10% of the aggregate value of the cross-border related-party transactions (5% if filed late after announcement but before final resolution). Underpaid tax from a transfer pricing adjustment is additionally subject to a 35% re-determined tax plus interest, and the penalty for artificial structures can reach 20% of the assessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the transfer pricing forms or participated in preparing inaccurate supporting studies can be personally liable for reckless misstatement. The DIAN and Superintendencia de Sociedades may order civil restitution, disqualification from serving as a director for two to five years, and a travel ban in cases involving hidden foreign-controlled income.
Money Laundering and Sanctions / Embargo Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines for AML violations can reach COP 1,000,000,000 or more, and the entity can be closed temporarily. Criminal money laundering under Article 323 of Law 599 of 2000 carries a prison sentence of 10 to 30 years, and the funds, assets and instruments are subject to mandatory forfeiture. Additionally, OFAC or COLCIS sanctions may result in exclusion from the global anti-dumping legal market.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and compliance officers who knowingly benefit from laundered funds are jointly liable for the criminal offense and civil restitution. Colombian criminal courts can impose travel bans, extradition-adjacent precautionary measures, and seizure of personal assets. The corporate veil is pierced to recover assets laundered through the entity, and an individual director may be deemed criminally responsible and personally barred from holding any corporate office in Colombia.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio con jurisdicción en el domicilio social (e.g., Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá) through the Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES) portal (www.rues.org.co).

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 (Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada), Arts. 1-6, 17-21, 25-29; Código de Comercio de Colombia (Decreto 410 de 1971), Arts. 98-126, 353-375; Decreto Único Reglamentario 1074 de 2015, Libro 2, Parte 2, Título 1.

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución y Estatutos (private incorporation instrument and bylaws), authenticated before a notary only when aportes en especie (in-kind contributions) of real property are involved; registered in the Registro Mercantil.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required for a S.A.S. (Law 1258/2008, Art. 6). The subscribed capital may be paid in cash or in kind within installments not exceeding two years from incorporation. No escrow account is mandated; the payment schedule must be recorded in the bylaws and certified by the legal representative.

Funding & unlock path: Office occupancy in Colombia requires a fire safety certificate (Certificado de Condiciones de Seguridad Humana contra Incendios) from the Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos for the rented office; the Certificado de Uso de Suelo from the municipal planning office or Curaduría Urbana validates zoning compliance; the employer must affiliate the company with an Administradora de Riesgos Laborales (ARL) and implement the Sistema de Gestión de Seguridad y Salud en el Trabajo (SG-SST) prior to hiring staff. No structural load inspection or industrial plant clearance is required for a software office environment.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is required for a S.A.S. (Law 1258/2008, Art. 6). The subscribed capital may be paid in cash or in kind within installments not exceeding two years from incorporation. No escrow account is mandated; the payment schedule must be recorded in the bylaws and certified by the legal representative.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – portal: https://www.dian.gov.co (transactional portal: https://muisca.dian.gov.co)

E-government filing portal

A foreign person or entity obtaining Colombian-source activities must procure a Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT) and an Registro Único Tributario (RUT) before commencing business. The procedure requires appointing a Colombian legal representative or attorney-in-fact, completing the pre-registration via DIAN's Muisca portal using the assigned institutional credential, submitting the RUT form (Formulario de Registro Único Tributario) with the constitutional documents (charter and bylaws of the SAS), proof of foreign entity existence and apostilled certification, and powers of attorney. DIAN issues the NIT immediately in most cases; the RUT is the single certificate of tax registration and contains the NIT, address, activity code (ISIC 6201), and applicable tax obligations. Non-resident owners do not require local minimum capital, but the local legal representative must maintain valid identification and a local tax domicile for service of process.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (a Colombian S.A.S. is treated as a separate juridical and tax resident entity subject to Colombian corporate income tax on worldwide income; there is no automatic pass-through classification and no check-the-box mechanism under Colombian national law); 'Territorial_Scope' applies only to certain Colombian offshore service regimes under free-trade-zone rules, which are not default. — A foreign person or entity obtaining Colombian-source activities must procure a Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT) and an Registro Único Tributario (RUT) before commencing business. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a Colombian S.A.S. is treated as a separate juridical and tax resident entity subject to Colombian corporate income tax on worldwide income; there is no automatic pass-through classification and no check-the-box mechanism under Colombian national law); 'Territorial_Scope' applies only to certain Colombian offshore service regimes under free-trade-zone rules, which are not default.

Available election forms

None for pass-through classification. Available statutory elections include: (i) elective inclusion in the Régimen Simple de Tributación for qualifying small-activity taxpayers under Law 2010 of 2019, replacing regular corporate income tax and IVA duties with a single consolidated payment on gross income, subject to turnover thresholds and activity restrictions; (ii) voluntary application for Zone Franca status (autorización de usuario de zona franca) conferring preferential territorial 20% CIT treatment on export-related income, subject to DIAN approval, minimum investment and employment commitments, and permanent establishment conditions; (iii) for VAT purposes, an election to be treated as a non-responsable or exempt operator only if the entity's sole transactions are excluded or non-taxed, otherwise registration is mandatory. No hybrid mismatch election is available because Colombian tax law does not apply entity classification check-the-box rules; foreign hybrid instruments remain governed by the mismatch-restriction articles of Colombian tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

COP 0 (no economic threshold): any legal entity, irrespective of turnover, carrying out taxable sales of goods or services within Colombian territory is required to register in the RUT as a 'responsable' and to charge IVA. Solely exempt or non-taxable operators may remain unregistered but must still obtain an RUT and NIT for identity and payroll compliance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods and exports of services (including software development, database management, API monitoring, PaaS/SaaS cloud tools) are zero-rated (0% VAT) when the services are utilised exclusively outside of Colombia, the recipient is not resident in Colombia, the service is not connected to tangible goods or real property located in Colombia, and the exporter qualifies as a 'usuario acogido' to export treatment. The service must be performed from Colombia in the sense that the developer tools export contract, the technical specification, and the billing arrangements support offshore beneficial use. Compliance requires a written export services contract, electronic export service documentation (for foreign exchange purposes), and compliance with DIAN's export service registration procedure.

  • Electronic customs export declaration (Declaración de Exportación Electrónica) validated by DIAN through the VUCE / Exporta Facíl system, including the SAS exporter's NIT and ISIC 6201 activity code
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (transmitted as the electronic FEV export invoice with CUFE and export complement)
  • Bill of Lading (for goods mode) or air waybill / courier airway bill and master waybill where applicable
  • Supporting export contract and technical specification proving the offshore beneficiary, the delivery mode (SaaS/cloud), and absence of Colombian in-country consumption
  • Certificate of foreign exchange repatriation (Certificado de reintegro de divisas) issued by the local bank or authorized payment processor, evidencing receipt of the export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT MT103 evidence if payment is received by a Colombian bank or a foreign account disclosed to DIAN under the foreign-exchange regimen (when using Stripe Billing or Mercury ACH rails)

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta - FEV) applies to all taxable persons, including SAS entities, from 2023 onwards. Invoices must be generated as XML validated in near-real-time through DIAN's web services; the issuance process requires digital signature (certificado digital), assignment of a single digital stamp or equivalent (CUFE/CUDE), transmission of the invoice before the applicable deadline (generally within 1 day), and receipt of DIAN validation/acceptance. Every export invoice must also be transmitted with complemento of goods or services export, and the export transaction must be backed by the electronic export declaration. The failure to obtain DIAN validation renders the invoice legally non-compliant and disallows input IVA credits where applicable.

Export zero-rating claims are scrutinized under Articles 485 and 487 of the Colombian Tax Statute. If the exporter fails to prove the export nature of the transaction or the foreign exchange repatriation deadline is missed, DIAN may retroactively reclassify the sale as a domestic transaction subject to 19% VAT, eliminate input VAT credits, and assess the unpaid IVA together with default interest (current rate of approximately 1.5% per month or fiscal equivalent). The inaccuracy penalty under Article 647 can amount up to 200% of the difference between the tax declared and the correct tax liability, and knowingly fraudulent export invoices attract criminal penalties under the Colombian criminal tax offence regime (delitos contra el orden tributario), including imprisonment, fines, and loss of tax registration. The exporter also risks cancellation of periodic VAT returns and refunds, and, in respect of false export documentation, adverse reference in DIAN's risk-scoring system for a period of up to ten years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

COP is convertible under a managed-floating regime with a daily TRM fixing. Current-account operations (software export proceeds, royalties, profit remittances) are liberalized but remain subject to mandatory channeling, exchange declarations, and documentary compliance. Capital-account inflows (FDI) are freely accepted but require registration with the Banco de la República as a precondition for future dividend remittance and capital repatriation. Practical hurdles include the six-month repatriation window for export receipts, the obligation to prove the CIT-paid status of distributable earnings before remitting dividends offshore, and IMC operational control over declaration forms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory channeling ratio is 100%: all proceeds of ISIC 6201/NAICS 511210 software-service exports must be canalized through an IMC within six (6) months from the invoice/collection-note date, either by conversion into COP at the TRM or by credit to a Banco de la República-registered foreign-currency or compensation account. Conversion into COP is elective; however, neither holding funds abroad without prior registration nor late channeling is permitted. Extension requests must be filed before expiry of the statutory window, and breach triggers fines by the DIAN and the Banco de la República.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia; the operative FX regime is established by Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, Resolution 8 of 2000 of the Junta Directiva, and the comprehensive Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83 of 2018. All cross-border treasury flows must be channeled through authorized exchange-market intermediaries (IMCs), which are Colombian commercial banks and financial corporations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury locus; Medium as an ancillary collection layer. Offshore digital EMIs (Mercury, Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) are not authorized IMCs and cannot legally substitute the channeling obligation. Receipts parked in such accounts must be repatriated to an IMC within the statutory window and reported in exchange declarations. A Colombian SAS may lawfully hold a foreign-currency account abroad (cuenta de compensación) only after prior registration with the Banco de la República; an unauthorized offshore account triggers administrative sanctions.

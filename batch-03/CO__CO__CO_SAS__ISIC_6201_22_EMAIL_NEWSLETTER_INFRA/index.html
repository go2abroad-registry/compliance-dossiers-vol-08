Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Unidad Administrativa Especial Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: www.dian.gov.co; all registration, filing and clearance operations are executed through the MUISCA platform (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicios y Trámites).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporation: execute the S.A.S. bylaws before a notary or in a private written document and register with the Cámara de Comercio of the registered office to obtain the business registry certificate (certificado de existencia y representación legal). Step 2 — RUT/NIT enrolment: the legal representative files the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) at DIAN through MUISCA or in person, attaching the certificate of existence, the representative's identification, and, for foreign shareholders, corporate resolutions and powers of attorney duly legalized; the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) is issued immediately upon RUT validation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the S.A.S. is an independent capital company taxed at entity level on its worldwide income (with the statutory exemptions for certain foreign dividends and controlled-foreign-company rules), and separately subject to VAT, excise and payroll taxes in Colombia; no pass-through or fiscal-transparency election is permitted for companies, and tax consolidation with parent companies is unavailable.

Available election forms

Colombia does not operate a domestic check-the-box or self-classification regime; the S.A.S. is mandatorily treated as a corporation for all tax purposes. Relevant elective mechanisms: (i) the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE), an elective combined income/VAT/consumption regime open to qualifying S.A.S. taxpayers with prior-year gross turnover at or below 80,000 UVT (approximately COP 3.98 billion for 2025), subject to statutory exclusions (e.g., financial and public entities); (ii) the ordinary corporate regime, which is the default (35% corporate rate for 2025); (iii) the enterprise income tax credit and the option to offset VAT balances; and (iv) Colombian anti-hybrid provisions under BEPS Action 2 targeting double-deduction and deduction-without-inclusion mismatches, which require factual and documentary substance; a foreign check-the-box election on the S.A.S. has no Colombian domestic effect.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold exists for corporate taxpayers: every S.A.S. undertaking taxable acts must be registered as a VAT-responsible party (RUT with 'responsable de IVA' characterization) before commencing operations, regardless of projected revenue. By comparison, the statutory de minimis of 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 174.3 million for 2025, with 1 UVT = COP 49,799) is limited to natural persons in the 'régimen simplificado' and is unavailable to companies. Foreign digital-service providers selling to Colombian residents must also register with DIAN and collect 19% VAT from the first transaction, unless the local payment rails or card schemes act as substitute responsible parties.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated (excluded from VAT) and carry the right to recover input VAT through refunds or offset of the balance in favor. A service qualifies as an export under Articles 481 and 591 ET and Decreto Único Reglamentario 1625 de 2016 when: (i) the recipient is a non-resident that maintains no permanent establishment in Colombia; (ii) the service is used, exploited or enjoyed exclusively outside Colombian territory; (iii) the consideration is paid and canalized through the foreign exchange market; and (iv) the exporter retains a complete documentary file. The S.A.S. email/newsletter/transactional-email platform may zero-rate API and subscription fees invoiced to foreign subscribers if the rendering servers, data processing and exploitation occur outside Colombia; domestic Colombian subscribers are always subject to 19% IVA.

  • DIAN-validated Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación, DEX) filed in the MUISCA/VUCE customs single-window system for physical goods; for services, the equivalent export-of-services registration and the exchange declaration 'Declaración de Cambio por Exportación de Servicios' (Formulario No. 6)
  • Transport documentation (Bill of Lading or Airway Bill) together with the commercial invoice, packing list and insurance documents evidencing the physical departure of exported goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (comprobante de canalización) issued by an authorized intermediary of Banco de la República, showing receipt in foreign currency and timely repatriation within the 180-day statutory deadline

Electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta, FEV) is compulsory for all DIAN-registered business taxpayers as of 2022. FEVs must be generated through DIAN-enabled technology providers using the MUISCA web-service APIs (REST/SOAP with digital-certificate authentication), must carry the DIAN validation code CUDE and a QR code, and must be transmitted to DIAN for real-time (or near-real-time) clearance before delivery to the client, per Resolution DIAN 000165 of 2023 and amendments. Complementary mandates include the electronic 'documento soporte' for acquisitions from non-responsible persons, electronic payroll support documents, and bimonthly consolidated VAT returns (Formulario 300) integrated with the periodic electronic information declarations.

DIAN routinely audits zero-rating claims through documentary and substantive checks; if export requirements are not met, the excluded nature of the transaction is retrospectively reversed, the taxpayer is assessed for 19% IVA on the gross invoice plus monthly arrears interest from the original due date, and the 'sanción por inexactitud' of 100% (reaching 200% in aggravated or fraudulent cases) is imposed under Articles 647–648 ET, with refunds and credited balances clawed back and input credits disallowed. Export proceeds not channeled into the Colombian foreign exchange market within 180 days (or an authorized extension) trigger Banco de la República exchange penalties of up to the unrepatriated amount. Fictitious exports, forged customs declarations or knowingly false invoices additionally constitute customs smuggling offenses (Customs Statute, Decree 1165 de 2019) and tax fraud under Colombian criminal law, exposing executives to imprisonment and pecuniary fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is freely convertible for current-account operations (trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, and remittances) through the Mercado Cambiario, which is composed solely of authorized intermediaries (commercial banks and exchange houses regulated by the Financial Superintendency). Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment into an SAS, external credit, and investment in foreign assets, require prior or subsequent registration with the Banco de la República via a local authorized intermediary. Profit remittance and capital repatriation are lawful for registered foreign investment, but dividend outflows, royalties, and service fees are subject to local tax withholding and must be channeled through the exchange market with a valid foreign exchange declaration (Declaración de Cambio). No blanket quantitative controls exist on conversion, but informal, parallel, or crypto-based conversion channels are prohibited and trigger severe AML and FX enforcement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Decree 1735 of 1993 and Banco de la República Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83 (as amended), exporters of goods and services must channel 100% of export proceeds into the Colombian foreign exchange market through an authorized intermediary within six (6) months from the date of the export declaration (goods) or from the date the service is rendered (services). The proceeds must ultimately be converted into COP and credited to a domestic bank account; however, exporters may temporarily hold foreign currency in a registered foreign-currency account abroad during that period for the payment of imported inputs or foreign debt, provided such use is previously disclosed to the central bank. A mandatory conversion ratio of 100% applies to the net export value after deducting authorized external financing, and any non-compliant holding or delayed surrender is subject to exchange violation penalties (sanciones cambiarias).

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) in coordination with the Comité de Cambios (Foreign Exchange Committee) and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (Financial Superintendency of Colombia)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) do not constitute regulated banking venues for Colombian treasury purposes. They can serve only as ancillary settlement rails for third-party card or marketplace collections, but cannot legally hold the SAS's own capital, disbursements, tax balances, or SARLAFT-mandated records. To operate in Colombia as an electronic payment provider or EMI, a fintech must obtain a license from the Financial Superintendency as a 'sociedad especializada en depósitos y pagos electrónicos' (SEDPE) and comply with full banking and AML/KYC regulations. Feasibility for core treasury use: Low. Feasibility for low-value international payout routing: Medium, provided the SAS maintains its primary account with a Colombian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from foreign shareholders must be routed as a direct SWIFT MT103 transfer to the SAS's local authorized bank, referencing the company's NIT (tax ID) and the legal corporate name. The wire must be marked with the appropriate foreign-exchange purpose concept code - typically Código de Concepto 4010 ('Inversiones de capital a través del Mercado Cambiario') - and the bank shall issue a Declaración de Cambio (Form No. 1) evidencing the foreign direct investment. The SAS must then ensure the investment is registered in the national registry of foreign investment within the statutory deadline. To avoid misclassification as debt or current-account flow, the remittance must be denominated in a major convertible currency (USD or EUR), must indicate 'CONCEPT: APORTE DE CAPITAL - INVERSION DIRECTA' on the wire instructions, and must not be intermingled with service fees or trade payments. For injections exceeding the bank's KYC limit, the bank may require the shareholder's audited financial statements, a board resolution approving the injection, and proof of the parent company's lawful source of funds.Trade and operating disbursements are cleared through the local bank's SWIFT MT103 network for international vendor payments (cloud hosting, software licensing, data-center costs) and through the Colombian automated clearing house (CCE, PSE) for domestic payments and payroll. For physical-goods settlement, the SAS may optionally use commercial letters of credit (LC at sight) or documentary collection issued via the same local bank, subject to mandatory foreign-exchange declaration. All outbound service payments must carry a valid foreign-exchange concept code (e.g., Código de Concepto 3010 for software licensing and Código de Concepto 3060 for technical services) and must be accurately declared in the quarterly/annual exchange report submitted through the authorization intermediary. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) do not constitute regulated banking venues for Colombian treasury purposes. They can serve only as ancillary settlement rails for third-party card or marketplace collections, but cannot legally hold the SAS's own capital, disbursements, tax balances, or SARLAFT-mandated records. To operate in Colombia as an electronic payment provider or EMI, a fintech must obtain a license from the Financial Superintendency as a 'sociedad especializada en depósitos y pagos electrónicos' (SEDPE) and comply with full banking and AML/KYC regulations. Feasibility for core treasury use: Low. Feasibility for low-value international payout routing: Medium, provided the SAS maintains its primary account with a Colombian bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File for fiscal 2024 if: (i) related-party transactions exceed 10,000 UVT (approx. COP 424 million); or (ii) the taxpayer's net worth or gross income exceeds 100,000 UVT (approx. COP 4.2 billion). A Master File is additionally required when the group's consolidated revenue exceeds 1,000,000 UVT (approx. COP 42 billion).

Economic substance scope: Colombia has no separate Economic Substance Regulation, but the General Anti-Abuse Rule (Art. 869 ET) enables DIAN to disregard shell entities with no local operational substance. The S.A.S. must have real employees, active software infrastructure management, contractual authority, and a local business bank account to support treaty benefits and avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-declaration

Statutory sanction: Additional tax plus penalty of 100% of omitted tax (Art. 648 ET); criminal imprisonment of 5 to 9 years and fine of 100% to 300% of evaded tax (Law 599/2000, Art. 402).

Director personal liability: Civil joint liability with the company; asset seizure and travel embargo; directors may be arrested and imprisoned for criminal tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 15,000 UVT (approx. COP 636 million); disallowance of the missing documentation's tax deduction; recharacterized profit attracts 35% income tax plus withholding tax on the perceived distribution.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives who sign false transfer-pricing studies face criminal penalties for tax fraud, personal fines equal to the adjustment, and potential asset embargo.
AML / Digital Payments Compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Law 1762/2015: imprisonment from 10 to 20 years and fines up to 1,000 times the illegal benefit; bank account freezing and asset confiscation; directed company dissolution.

Director personal liability: Officers who fail to implement AML/KYC or file suspicious transaction reports with UIAF are personally liable; travel bans, disqualification from financial sector roles, and imprisonment.

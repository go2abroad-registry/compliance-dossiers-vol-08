Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Cyprus Companies Law, Cap. 113 imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a private limited company; the authorized share capital is fixed in the M&A (e.g. EUR 25,000) and shares may be paid up by cash or non-cash consideration. No escrow deposit is required for statutory incorporation, but the defence/strategic goods licensing authority may require proof of paid-up capital or escrowed bank funds equivalent to 24 months of payroll and facility obligations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Cyprus Fire Service based on structural fire plans, hydrants, suppression systems and evacuation routes; Civil Engineer's Structural Stability Certificate for production halls and ballistic test bays; Occupational Health and Safety clearance under Safety and Health at Work Law 89(I)/1996 including noise, welding fumes and medical surveillance; Civil Defense registration for emergency preparedness and shelter plans.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus (official portal: taxportal.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first be registered with the Registrar of Companies in Cyprus. Following incorporation, the Cyprus Tax Department issues the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). VAT registration is applied for using Form VAT-101, supported by the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director/shareholder details, registered office address, and, where no local establishment exists, the appointment of a Cypriot tax representative for VAT purposes. The VAT certificate is issued after verification and approval.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Cyprus-resident limited companies are separate taxable persons subject to Cyprus corporate income tax at 12.5%; no pass-through treatment; non-resident entities are taxable only on Cyprus-source income) — A foreign entity must first be registered with the Registrar of Companies in Cyprus. Following incorporation, the Cyprus Tax Department issues the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). VAT registration is applied for using Form VAT-101, supported by the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director/shareholder details, registered office address, and, where no local establishment exists, the appointment of a Cypriot tax representative for VAT purposes. The VAT certificate is issued after verification and approval.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Cyprus-resident limited companies are separate taxable persons subject to Cyprus corporate income tax at 12.5%; no pass-through treatment; non-resident entities are taxable only on Cyprus-source income)

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a check-the-box equity classification regime; legal personality determines tax treatment. EU ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules apply, requiring taxpayers to align treatment with EU anti-hybrid principles. Elective regimes available include the Cyprus IP Box (80% qualifying income deduction), tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping activities, VAT grouping, and group loss relief elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The mandatory annual taxable turnover threshold for VAT registration is EUR 15,600. Foreign businesses making taxable supplies in Cyprus are required to register from the first taxable supply when no local establishment is maintained; distance-selling and EU OSS thresholds are applied in cross-border e-commerce scenarios.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of goods dispatched or transported from Cyprus to a destination outside the EU, or to another EU Member State as an intra-Community transaction, qualifies for the zero-rate provided the supplier retains adequate documentary evidence of export or intra-Community movement and complies with statutory time limits. Defense and tactical military hardware exports are zero-rated only when supported by a valid export licence, end-user undertaking, and customs release. B2B services supplied to a taxable person established outside Cyprus are generally outside the scope of Cyprus VAT under place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document – SAD)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR consignment note / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit evidence confirming export proceeds repatriation
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Export Licence and End-User Certificate obtained from the Cyprus Ministry of Commerce / national arms export control authority

Cyprus has not implemented mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, Government API tax stamping, or a CTC digital clearance platform. Electronically issued invoices are accepted subject to authenticity and integrity controls. VAT e-filing and digital submissions are mandatory through the Cyprus Tax Portal. Cyprus is monitoring EU VAT in the Digital Age initiatives, with mandatory e-invoicing/CTC to follow EU-determined timelines.

Where zero-rating is claimed without sufficient documentary justification, the Tax Department assesses VAT at 19% with statutory interest and penalties. Fraudulent zero-rating claims or forged export documentation are subject to criminal sanctions, including imprisonment of up to 10 years and/or fines, retrospective clawback of VAT, denial of deduction rights, and enhanced audit scrutiny. The limitation period for tax assessments generally does not exceed five years, but fraud or deliberate default may extend the look-back period and increase exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus has been a full Eurozone member since 1 January 2008; the euro is the sole legal tender, and no exchange-control statute or foreign-exchange approval framework remains in force. Current-account settlements and capital-account transfers are fully and irrevocably convertible under TFEU Articles 63-66, including profit remittances, dividend distributions, interest, royalties, branch profits and liquidation proceeds. There is no corporate withholding tax on outbound dividends or interest to non-resident shareholders under domestic law, and the 17% Cyprus Special Defence Contribution applies to the narrow historical category of Cyprus-resident individual recipients, with double-tax-treaty relief available. For an ISIC 3040 defence-hardware manufacturer, the residual remittance hurdles are procedural rather than statutory: (i) banks apply a mandatory enhanced-due-diligence file before executing any profit transfer; (ii) the remittance must be traceable to audited, unencumbered reserves supported by board-resolved dividend distribution; and (iii) the recipient must not appear on EU, UN, OFAC or UK consolidated sanction lists. Conversion of EUR into USD, GBP, CHF or other freely convertible currencies is unrestricted and unlimited, and foreign-currency balances may be held indefinitely in Cyprus-resident accounts without Central Bank of Cyprus approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Cyprus. There is no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio and no prescribed repatriation deadline for export receipts; exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in any convertible currency in resident bank accounts and convert to euro at freely negotiated market rates. The only binding regulatory obligation is the Central Bank of Cyprus Balance of Payments reporting regime under its External Statistics Directive: every export receipt above EUR 5,000 per transaction must be reported through the receiving bank on a monthly basis using IMF BPM-6 purpose code S011 for trade in goods, and defence programme receipts are cross-referenced to the export licence and customs export declaration. The recommended treasury control is to reconcile each export receipt against the underlying Military Goods Export Licence and customs declaration within 30 days to avoid Enhanced Customer Due Diligence review under the CBC statues and the Eurosystem reporting instructions.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating inside the European System of Central Banks and the Eurosystem as the domestic prudential supervisor and sole licensing authority for Cypriot credit institutions, electronic-money institutions and payment institutions under the Banking Law of 1997 (as amended) and the AML Law 188(I)/2007. The Cyprus Ministry of Energy, Commerce and Industry acts as the statutory export-control licensing authority for defence-related material under Law 182(I)/2002, and EU Dual-Use Regulation 2021/821 applies directly for dual-use components.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: LOW, permissible only for incidental non-trade purposes. Cyprus-licensed EMIs and Payment Institutions under the Electronic Money Laws 2012-2018 and PSD2 can open accounts rapidly and provide multi-currency IBANs, but ISIC 3040 all-terrain military-tactical hardware is an automatic exclusion in the risk committees of nearly all European EMIs because their tier-2 and tier-3 correspondent banks refuse to process military-goods transactions. A foreign EEA EMIs passporting into Cyprus under EU passporting rules does not supersede the CBC AML Directive, and the passporting firm must add a Cyprus branch filing and negative-screening limitations. The viable use is limited to payroll, utility, tax-office and administrative expenditure; it is not an acceptable rail for Government_Direct_Wire, diplomatic settlement, import letters of credit or export proceeds collection. A licensed Cyprus commercial bank or a regulated credit institution remains the exclusive clearing counterparty for treasury-grade operations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Cyprus imposes no withholding tax on dividends, interest, or royalties paid to non-residents. The Special Defence Contribution (SDC) at 17% applies only to dividends paid to Cyprus tax-resident shareholders and does not affect non-resident owners.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

IR501 due within 15 months after the end of the tax year (e.g., TY2024 return due 31 March 2026). Provisional tax installments: 75% of tax liability, split equally, due 31 July and 31 December of the tax year (new companies: 25% of estimated tax in each installment). Final tax settlement due 1 August of the year following the tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required for tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2022 when controlled transactions exceed de minimis thresholds: EUR 750,000 for sales/purchases of goods; EUR 250,000 for services; EUR 100,000 for financing transactions; EUR 100,000 for intangible/royalty transactions; where the Cyprus company's turnover is below EUR 750,000 or aggregate controlled transactions are immaterial, the Local File is not mandatory. Master File required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least EUR 750,000,000. Country-by-Country Report required for ultimate parent entities resident in Cyprus with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not maintain a separate Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations statute. Substance is enforced through: (i) the central management and control test for tax residence, requiring board meetings, strategic decisions, employees, premises, and records in Cyprus; (ii) the 2019-2022 Cyprus Tax Department economic substance guidance requiring real, proportionate personnel and premises for companies claiming IP Box, NID, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, or Interest & Royalties Directive benefits; (iii) ATAD GAAR and the principal-purpose test in DTAs; and (iv) audit practice requiring production, supply-chain contracts, board minutes, and local management records. For a defense tactical vehicle manufacturer, high-risk AML classification and defense-license supervision make documentary proof of physical operations, order management, and contract execution in Cyprus critical.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Omission of Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Cyprus Income Tax Law and Assessment & Collection of Taxes Law: fines up to EUR 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years; civil reassessment at 100% penalty on the evaded tax plus interest at 0.5% per month; seizure of assets and freezing orders; revocation of export and defense trading licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false IR501 returns or knowingly cause non-declaration are personally liable for the penalty assessment where the company is insolvent or dissolved; criminal prosecution of the 'directing mind'; court-ordered exit bans and travel restrictions for unpaid tax debts; potential disqualification as director for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of tax residence by 'central management and control' to the jurisdiction of actual management; denial of Cyprus treaty benefits and EU directives; application of ATAD GAAR with full re-assessment, late-payment interest at 0.5% per month, and penalties up to 100% of underpaid tax; deregistration of the HE77 company and administrative fines for false declarations.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face criminal liability for false statements under the Companies Law (Cap. 113) and the Criminal Code; joint and several liability for reassessed taxes under the 'responsible person' provisions; travel bans and confiscation orders; prohibition from acting as a director in any EU jurisdiction.
AML / EU Sanctions Breach (High-Risk Defense Sector)

Statutory sanction: Cyprus AML Law (L.188(I)/2007): imprisonment up to five years and fines up to EUR 100,000 per offense; freezing and confiscation of the proceeds of crime; revocation of statutory defense trading licenses; EU sanctions enforcement with asset seizures and criminal prosecution; possible inclusion on FATF/international watchlists resulting in de-risking and liquidation of banking relations.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally criminally liable where AML failures are intentional or reckless; seizure of personal assets and travel bans; imprisonment up to five years; disqualification from defense-sector licensing permanently; civil liability for losses of the Cyprus entity and its creditors.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment by the Cyprus Tax Commissioner under Section 33 of the Income Tax Law; additional tax at 12.5% on the adjusted profit, plus interest at 0.5% per month and penalties of 10%-100% of the adjustment depending on cooperation; administrative fines up to EUR 20,000 for TP documentation failures; cross-border MAP and joint audit exposure, potentially engaging pillar-two rules for large groups.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign inaccurate TP documentation face fines for false statements and, in cases of deliberate understatement, personal liability up to EUR 50,000; where the company cannot pay the reassessed amount, directors are joint and severally liable if they knowingly permitted the non-arm's length arrangement; travel bans may issue pending settlement of the tax debt.
Dividend Repatriation / Directive Abuse

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of the payment as a deemed distribution or non-deductible expense; disallowance of interest or royalty deductions under ATAD interest limitation (30% EBITDA) and the GAAR; recomputation of tax with a 100% penalty and interest at 0.5% per month; denial of Directive or treaty relief and revocation of the 0% withholding position; potential SDC assessment on deemed distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors authorizing unlawful distributions under Cap. 113 are jointly liable to creditors for the distribution amount; in fraudulent trading, unlimited personal liability and imprisonment up to five years; cross-border enforcement of EU arrest warrants for financial crimes; disqualification and asset freezing where disguised repatriation is proven.

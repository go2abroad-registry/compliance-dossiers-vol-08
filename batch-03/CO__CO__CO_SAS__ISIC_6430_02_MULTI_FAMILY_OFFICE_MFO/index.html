Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — national tax and customs authority; official taxpayer portal: www.dian.gov.co (MUISCA platform for registration, filing, and electronic invoice validation).

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors forming a S.A.S. must obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), which serves as the general tax ID and VAT ID, together with an enrolment file in the RUT (Registro Único Tributario). Procedure: (1) execute the S.A.S. bylaws before a notary in Colombia or abroad with apostille; (2) appoint a legal representative with local residence; (3) submit the pre-registration and RUT form to the DIAN office or through the MUISCA portal with supporting documents — certificate of existence of foreign shareholders, passport/IDs, bylaws, powers of attorney, and proof of local address; (4) receive the NIT and RUT certificate, which automatically registers the entity for income tax, IVA, and electronic invoicing obligations. A foreign parent that holds shares in the Colombian S.A.S. but has no Colombian permanent establishment does not need its own NIT solely for holding purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Colombian S.A.S. is a resident corporation taxed on worldwide income as a separate taxable subject; no pass-through election is available and territorial scope is limited to foreign-source income exemptions under domestic law) — Foreign investors forming a S.A.S. must obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), which serves as the general tax ID and VAT ID, together with an enrolment file in the RUT (Registro Único Tributario). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Colombian S.A.S. is a resident corporation taxed on worldwide income as a separate taxable subject; no pass-through election is available and territorial scope is limited to foreign-source income exemptions under domestic law)

Available election forms

Colombian income tax law does not provide a check-the-box regime; the S.A.S. is irrebuttably treated as a separate taxable entity. Available statutory elections include: (i) optional consolidated income tax filing for qualifying economic groups satisfying 95% direct or indirect control and other tests under Law 2277 of 2022 (effective from tax year 2024, mandatory for the largest groups from 2025); (ii) election between foreign tax credit and deduction for foreign income taxes; (iii) election for the export-of-services regime to be recognized by DIAN as an exporter of services for IVA purposes; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules operate automatically under Law 2277 of 2022 implementing BEPS Action 2 — no elective opt-out or deferral is available for hybrid mismatch arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For legal entities such as the S.A.S. there is no de minimis turnover threshold: the entity must enrol with DIAN as an IVA-responsible person from commencement of any taxable or excluded activities. A simplified regime (régimen simplificado) is reserved for natural persons with annual gross income below 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 174.3 million for 2025, UVT = COP 49,799) plus other qualifying limits. Multi-Family Office advisory and portfolio management fees are generally taxable services at 19%, except where a specific exclusion or exemption under the financial services provisions of the Estatuto Tributario applies, e.g., income from the administration of collective investment funds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0% IVA) applies to the export of goods physically leaving Colombia and to services that meet the statutory export-of-services tests under Estatuto Tributario Art. 481 and Decree 2245 of 2011: (i) the recipient is a person without tax residence or domicile in Colombia; (ii) the service must be performed and its economic benefit consumed or exploited exclusively outside Colombian territory; (iii) the service is not linked to tangible assets physically located in Colombia or to real property situated in Colombia; (iv) the service must be evidenced by a written contract and e-invoice in foreign currency; and (v) the exporter must be registered in DIAN's export-of-services module and obtain the Certificado de Exportador de Servicios. Discretionary portfolio advisory services to non-resident multi-family structures can qualify where decision-making, substantive advice, and economic use occur abroad; fees performed through a Colombian fixed establishment or benefitting Colombia-linked assets will not qualify.

  • DIAN Export-of-Services Declaration (declaración de exportación de servicios) registered in the VUCE/DIAN module
  • Written service agreement with the non-resident family or wealth-structure beneficiary evidencing foreign-currency consideration
  • E-invoice (factura electrónica) bearing the CUFE, digital signature, and explicit narration of the 0% IVA export treatment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (certificado de ingreso de divisas) issued by an authorized foreign exchange intermediary confirming repatriation through the Colombian exchange market
  • Bank statements evidencing payment in lawful foreign currency from the non-resident beneficiary
  • Certificado de Exportador de Servicios (DIAN recognition certificate) and, where tangible goods are embedded, the customs export declaration (DAE) with bill of lading or air waybill

Mandatory national electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) applies to all IVA-responsible parties. E-invoices must be produced through DIAN-approved software and transmitted in real time to the DIAN/MUISCA platform for validation and fiscal clearance; DIAN assigns a unique electronic invoicing code (CUFE) and responds with acceptance or rejection within seconds through the prescribed API/Web-Service interfaces. Documents must be digitally signed and include the CUFE, invoice number, and full tax data. For family office fee billing, the e-invoice must be issued within 5 calendar days of each billing event, and IVA returns are filed bimonthly or quarterly depending on gross income level.

DIAN may reopen zero-rated export claims within 5 tax years (extended up to 6 years in cases of discovered fraud). Consequences of unsupported, mispriced, or fictitious export declarations include: (i) reversal of the 0% IVA treatment with full 19% IVA plus default interest recalculated from the original billing period; (ii) the inexactitude penalty between 100% and 200% of the tax omitted under Law 2277 of 2022; (iii) penalties of 1% of the invoice value for defective electronic invoices (Art. 652 E.T.) and customs documentation penalties under Decree 1165 of 2019; (iv) criminal liability for fraudulent tax maneuvers (maniobra fraudulenta) with imprisonment up to 9 years where forged contracts or false FX certificates are used; and (v) joint and several liability of the legal representative for the recovery of clawed-back IVA, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a hybrid regime under Decreto 1735/1993, Resolución Externa 2/2018, and the Estatuto Orgánico del Sistema Financiero. Current-account transactions, including cross-border payments for goods, services, royalties, and family-office fees, are generally freely convertible through authorized foreign-exchange market participants without prior central-bank approval. Capital-account inflows, notably foreign direct investment in the Colombian S.A.S., must be registered with Banco de la República to secure later profit remittance and capital repatriation rights. Profit remittance is admissible for registered foreign investors after verified audited retained earnings, confirmed tax-deductibility or withholding compliance, and completion of the relevant Formulario de Declaración de Cambio; there are no administrative restrictions on conversion into COP other than transaction evidence requirements. The practical hurdle is documentary: pre-registration of foreign investment, certified financial statements, proof of tax standing, and a local legal representative are necessary to avoid blocked wires or exchange-control penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Proceeds from Colombian goods exports must be channeled into the domestic foreign-exchange market through an authorized intermediary within six months from the date of customs exit or shipment, in accordance with Circular DCIN-83 and Resolución Externa 2/2018. In general, 100% of the export value must be repatriated. Mandatory conversion into Colombian pesos is not automatic: exporters may hold foreign currency in local foreign-currency accounts, but any settlement of Colombian-peso obligations or conversion for local disbursement must be executed through the authorized market. For services and know-how exports, funds must similarly be channeled through an authorized intermediary. Failure to repatriate within the statutory period triggers exchange-surrender fines and DIAN enforcement.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia, acting as the central bank and monetary authority, with foreign-exchange administration overseen jointly by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) for licensed entities and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) for enforcement of exchange declarations and anti-money-laundering controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability as a stand-alone rail. Offshore electronic-money institutions and fintech EMI providers do not hold Colombian banking licenses and cannot clear Colombian-peso payments through the Banco de la República and ACH Colombia clearing system. They may support low-value cross-border receipts and FX conversion in a pre-banking phase, but they cannot serve as a regulated custody, treasury, or capital-repatriation infrastructure for a licensed Colombian multi-family office. Colombian law deems deposit-taking and market-making without SFC authorization as illegal; therefore offshore EMI accounts must be ancillary only, with final liquidity legalized through onshore supervised commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from foreign family-office shareholders should be sent via SWIFT MT103 to the Colombian S.A.S. dollar account or directly to a COP account at the chosen onshore bank. The remittance beneficiary must match the exact NIT and legal name of the S.A.S., and payment reference must uniquely identify the share-subscription or capital contribution. Within one business month, the company must file the corresponding foreign-investment registration with the Banco de la República through the authorized intermediary, using the appropriate exchange declaration and concept code for direct foreign investment. The bank will require proof of remittance, board/shareholder resolution, corporate incorporation certificate, and UBO confirmation before accepting the funds. For a multi-family-office business, any custody or client-money receipt must be strictly segregated from the operational capital account and may require an SFC trust or separate safeguarding structure.Outbound client disbursements, professional fees, software-development payments, and cross-border settling to family beneficiaries should clear through SWIFT MT103 from the Colombian treasury account to the beneficiary bank. Intercompany and operating expense payments above regulatory thresholds require exchange declaration submissions. Where a physical trade settlement is involved, a commercial letter of credit, documentary collections, or SWIFT MT103 with attached shipping invoices is acceptable. Local Colombian peso payments to providers must be executed through ACH Colombia, CENIT, or bank-specific cash-management platforms; same-day high-value payments can be made through Banco de la República-operated real-time gross settlement. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation thresholds are based on the taxpayer's gross income or net equity exceeding 1,200,000 UVT (for the 2023 tax period, approximately COP 43,854 million) or when aggregate transactions with related parties exceed 150,000 UVT (approximately COP 5,482 million). The Informative Transfer Pricing Return (Formulario 227) is filed under the same thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation analogous to Caribbean ESRs. However, anticompratsomory Article 869-1 of the Tax Code, the Controlled Foreign Company rules, and treaty abuse provisions require that the S.A.S. maintain real staff, physical premises, operational functions, and risk control in Colombia. A multi-family office lacking local substance risks being disregarded for tax purposes, resulting in the reallocation of income to its non-resident shareholders and the denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax plus interest; criminal imprisonment of 108 to 360 months under Article 426 of the Colombian Criminal Code; disqualification from contracting with state entities.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax, interest, and penalties. Criminal proceedings may lead to imprisonment, travel bans, and freeze orders on personal assets.
Shell Company / Abusive Interposition

Statutory sanction: Administrative dissolution and cancellation of the commercial registration under the Código de Comercio; nullification of acts performed under the corporate veil; fines imposed under DIAN powers for breach of substance requirements.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil removes the limitation of liability, rendering directors and controlling shareholders personally liable for corporate debts, unpaid taxes, and fines. Directors may also be subject to asset forfeiture and criminal charges for illegal enrichment.
Anti-Money Laundering / CFT Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to 22,000 minimum monthly legal wages (approximately COP 2.4 billion) per infringement; suspension or revocation of the operating license; money laundering convictions under Law 1908 of 2018 carry imprisonment up to 30 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face criminal prosecution for money laundering, personal fines, disqualification, and travel prohibitions. Assets derived from illicit activity are subject to forfeiture.
Fraudulent Withholding and Remittance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 100% of the amount not withheld plus interest; failure to remit withheld taxes constitutes criminal offense punishable by imprisonment of 36 to 108 months and a fine equal to the unpaid amount.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers are personally liable for the unremitted withholdings and associated penalties. Criminal liability can lead to imprisonment, disqualification from holding public or corporate offices, and seizure of personal assets.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); portal: www.dian.gov.co; transactional platform: MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned S.A.S. is formed by filing a private incorporation document or public deed with the Chamber of Commerce in the relevant jurisdiction. Once the Chamber registers the company, DIAN issues the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria). The legal representative must complete and validate the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) in MUISCA, record ISIC 8299 as the principal economic activity, mark the company as a VAT-responsible electronic-invoicing taxpayer, and obtain DIAN web-services credentials and a digital certificate. Non-resident shareholders may lawfully own the S.A.S.; the S.A.S. is itself the taxpayer for income tax, VAT, and ancillary taxes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not provide a check-the-box election or any elective hybrid-mismatch classification. An S.A.S. is always taxed as a separate Colombian-resident entity. The principal statutory election is the Régimen Simple de Tributación (RST), an optional integrated tax regime for eligible small and medium service enterprises; the election is made through the RUT and, once in force, replaces income tax, VAT, and municipal industry-and-commerce tax with a consolidated charge subject to annual revenue limits and DIAN oversight.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

COP 0 for corporate taxpayers. An S.A.S. performing habitual taxable activities must be registered in the RUT as a VAT responsible person before beginning operations; no minimum turnover exemption exists for companies. Under the individual non-responsibility rule, a natural person may avoid VAT responsibility only if prior-year taxable gross income did not exceed 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 164.7 million for 2024); this is not available to an S.A.S.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Colombian customs territory are IVA-exempt at 0% and the exporter may reclaim VAT borne on inputs. Services, including ISIC 8299 virtual-office mail scanning and digital document services, qualify for the 0% export rate when the beneficiary is a non-resident without a Colombian permanent establishment, the economic use and benefit of the service occurs outside Colombia, the service is not an activity supplied for consumption in Colombia, and the exporter channels the fee through the Colombian foreign-exchange market. The exporter must maintain evidence of the residence status of the foreign client and the actual performance of the service abroad.

  • Goods exports: Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) filed in MUISCA/SIIS and the DIAN-approved export document showing departure from Colombian customs territory.
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading, House Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing dispatch to the foreign consignee.
  • Commercial invoice and, for service exports, the signed service contract describing the ISIC 8299 virtual-office or mail-scanning services and the non-resident beneficiary.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (certificado de negociación de divisas) issued by an authorized bank evidencing settlement of the export proceeds through the Colombian FX market.

Mandatory. Every VAT-responsible S.A.S. must issue electronic sales invoices (factura electrónica de venta – FEV) using DIAN-authorized software or DIAN web services. Each FEV must include the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) and a QR code, and must be transmitted to DIAN electronically; DIAN responds with an acceptance or rejection online on the MUISCA platform when the invoice is submitted. Invoices, credit notes, debit notes and supporting export documents must be stored in the DIAN-designated XML format with sequential numbers.

DIAN cross-validates export refunds against customs declarations, service contracts, beneficiary residency documentation, electronic invoices, and FX records. Any zero-rating claimed without a genuine export, without receipt and 180-day repatriation of foreign currency, or using fraudulent documentation is retroactively disallowed; DIAN will assess full IVA at 19% plus default interest, impose the Article 647 E.T. inaccuracy penalty equal to 100% of the overstated refund, and may order administrative suspension or closure measures. Fraudulent refund schemes may also be referred for criminal tax fraud prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) operates under a managed free float with a two-tier convertibility framework. Current-account transactions such as the export and import of services, royalty payments, and operating remittances are freely convertible when channelled through the organised foreign exchange market, which is composed of commercial banks and foreign exchange intermediaries supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia and regulated by Banco de la República. Capital-account operations, including foreign direct investment in a Colombian S.A.S., must be recorded through an exchange declaration (Formulario 11) and registered on the Banco de la República international investment register; registration is a condition precedent for the future outflow of dividends, capital reductions, or share redemptions. There are no express quantitative caps on profit remittances of a compliant foreign investor, but the remittance is subject to standard Colombian dividend withholding taxation and the investor must maintain an updated registration of the investment. For an ISIC 8299 virtual office and mail scanning enterprise, the main practical hurdle is procedural: all inbound equity and outbound operational settlements must be traceable through the regulated banking circuit so that the central bank's exchange declaration and the tax administration's NIT records remain consistent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Colombian foreign exchange regime, exporters of services, including mail scanning and virtual office services classified under ISIC 8299, must collect their export proceeds within twelve months counted from the issuance of the corresponding export invoice or from the filing of the export declaration with the bank (Formulario 14), whichever is applicable. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into COP; the exporter may lawfully retain the proceeds in a foreign currency account abroad, provided that any such account is reported to Banco de la República as a cuenta de compensación and that periodic declarations are filed. If the exporter elects to convert the proceeds, the conversion must be executed through the formal foreign exchange market at a bank, and the bank will record the operation. Where payment is received in domestic currency from an international card settling house such as Stripe, the service export must still be reported through Formulario 14, and the company must retain the merchant settlement statements, card network reports, and underlying client invoices as evidence of the export transaction. Failure to comply with the twelve-month repatriation or collection term, or failure to file the corresponding exchange declaration, may trigger graduated fines and the potential exclusion of the operation from the tax benefit of service exports.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for collections-only auxiliary use, Low for use as a primary treasury account. An offshore electronic money institution (EMI) such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex is not a supervised foreign exchange intermediary in Colombia and cannot open an onshore COP account in the name of the S.A.S. Holdings in such an EMI are characterised as funds maintained abroad and therefore fall within the cuenta de compensación reporting regime if the account belongs to a Colombian tax resident entity. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing protocol for inbound foreign equity for the Colombian S.A.S. requires the foreign shareholder to send a SWIFT MT103 transfer from its own corporate bank account to the S.A.S. bank account held in Colombia, naming the S.A.S. NIT in the beneficiary field and stating in the payment narrative the purpose as aporte de capital, inversion directa de no residentes en Colombia. The receiving tier-1 bank, for example Bancolombia S.A. with SWIFT BIC COLCCOBM, records the operation and produces the exchange declaration Formulario 11 under the direct investment concept codes contained in Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, Annex 1. If the S.A.S. is capitalised in COP, the bank converts the foreign currency at the market exchange rate and the exchange declaration evidences the capital contribution. If the contribution is maintained in a foreign currency account, the account must be flagged for foreign investment registration purposes. The company then files the formal international investment registration with Banco de la República within twelve months of the wire, and the bank remains the channel for the registration because it has the operative exchange declaration. Payments received through online EMI platforms, cryptocurrency, or third-party card processors are not accepted as valid evidence for the foreign direct investment registration and must not be used for capital injections. After registration, the S.A.S. may lawfully remit dividends, distributions, and the proceeds of capital reductions to the foreign shareholder subject to Colombian tax withholding.Outbound disbursements for trade and operating purposes, including software licensing, cloud hosting, overseas subcontractors, and international marketing services, should be executed through the SWIFT MT103 clearing rail via the company's local bank. The bank files the corresponding exchange declaration Formulario 14 under the service import concept code of Circular DCIN-83 Annex 1. Each payment must be supported by a commercial invoice issued to the S.A.S., a signed service agreement, and when the payment is made prior to delivery, the advance payment clause of the agreement. Payment against a commercial letter of credit is not typical for service disbursements under ISIC 8299, but a standby letter of credit may be used as a performance or rent guarantee for the premises of the virtual office. For recurring small-value payments to foreign vendors, the company may aggregate them into a monthly treasury run to minimise bank fees and facilitate reconciliation. Every payment instruction must repeat the invoice number in the remittance information field, and the company must retain the bank's foreign exchange liquidation and the corresponding MT103 as the audit trail for the DIAN and Banco de la República. The bank's designated corporate treasury portal should be used exclusively; outgoing payments from an offshore EMI remain reportable and should be avoided where possible.Deliverable USD/COP foreign exchange forward contracts (OTC) entered into with a Colombian credit entity authorised to operate the foreign exchange market.Non-deliverable USD/COP forwards (NDFs) with an onshore bank or an offshore counterparty where the reference rate is the Colombian Central Bank's representative market rate (TRM).USD/COP cross-currency swaps allowing the company to swap fixed or floating USD receipts into COP liabilities and manage tenor risk on subscription cash flows.Plain-vanilla USD/COP call and put options and zero-cost collar or corridor structures executed through a local brokerage or bank treasury desk.Listed USD/COP futures and unified TRM settlement contracts traded on the Colombian Stock Exchange or through its authorised clearing houses for corporate hedging positions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for collections-only auxiliary use, Low for use as a primary treasury account. An offshore electronic money institution (EMI) such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex is not a supervised foreign exchange intermediary in Colombia and cannot open an onshore COP account in the name of the S.A.S. Holdings in such an EMI are characterised as funds maintained abroad and therefore fall within the cuenta de compensación reporting regime if the account belongs to a Colombian tax resident entity. Receipts from international card networks through Stripe, when settled directly to a Colombian bank account, remain the preferred legal and operational rail for the recurring subscription model. The S.A.S. may use an EMI for small-disbursement vendor payments outside Colombia, but all proceeds must be reconciled with Formulario 14 filings, and the core operating liquidity must sit in a tier-1 local bank to preserve exchange-control compliance and future dividend remittance capacity.

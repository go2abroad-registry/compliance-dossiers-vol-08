Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus (TAXISnet portal at taxisnet.mof.gov.cy and taxdepot.mof.gov.cy), acting as the competent national tax authority for corporate income tax, VAT (VAT Service), withholding taxes and related filings; customs administration, export clearance and import VAT collection are administered by the Department of Customs and Excise (Ministry of Finance) under the EU Union Customs Code.

E-government filing portal

For a Cyprus-incorporated Private Limited Company (HE77): the Tax Identification Number (TIN) is issued automatically upon incorporation by the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver and is identical to the Certificate of Incorporation number; the company must complete formal taxpayer registration at the competent District Tax Office (Form TIN 151) attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, registered-office address, directors and shareholders registers, and beneficial-ownership (UBO) declarations. A foreign-incorporated entity with a Cyprus establishment or fixed place of business must apply for a non-resident TIN and, where it makes taxable supplies, intra-Community acquisitions or chargeable imports in Cyprus, must additionally submit Form VAT 101 to the VAT Service of the Tax Department; non-established taxable persons may be required to appoint a fiscally resident tax representative under Art. 23 of the VAT Law (Law 94(I)/2000) before a VAT registration number (format CY + 8 digits + 1 check letter) is granted and the entity is onboarded to the TAXISnet electronic filing system.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a Cyprus Ltd is an opaque, separate legal taxable person under the Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002), subject to corporate income tax at a 12.5% headline rate; tax residence follows the place of management and control, with resident companies taxed on worldwide income and non-resident companies taxed on Cyprus-source income only (territorial scope); no pass-through election applies to companies and Cyprus has no tax-exempt zone outside the statutory shipping tonnage tax regime. — For a Cyprus-incorporated Private Limited Company (HE77): the Tax Identification Number (TIN) is issued automatically upon incorporation by the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver and is identical to the Certificate of Incorporation number; the company must complete formal taxpayer registration at the competent District Tax Office (Form TIN 151) attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, registered-office address, directors and shareholders registers, and beneficial-ownership (UBO) declarations. A foreign-incorporated entity with a Cyprus establishment or fixed place of business must apply for a non-resident TIN and, where it makes taxable supplies, intra-Community acquisitions or chargeable imports in Cyprus, must additionally submit Form VAT 101 to the VAT Service of the Tax Department; non-established taxable persons may be required to appoint a fiscally resident tax representative under Art. 23 of the VAT Law (Law 94(I)/2000) before a VAT registration number (format CY + 8 digits + 1 check letter) is granted and the entity is onboarded to the TAXISnet electronic filing system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Cyprus Ltd is an opaque, separate legal taxable person under the Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002), subject to corporate income tax at a 12.5% headline rate; tax residence follows the place of management and control, with resident companies taxed on worldwide income and non-resident companies taxed on Cyprus-source income only (territorial scope); no pass-through election applies to companies and Cyprus has no tax-exempt zone outside the statutory shipping tonnage tax regime.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election, no hybrid-mismatch election and no pass-through election are available to a Cyprus Ltd. Statutory elections that do exist include: group loss relief surrender between Cyprus group companies; the Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital; the Nexus-compliant intangible/IP (patent box) regime election; the shipping tonnage-tax election for qualifying vessels; the election to claim foreign tax credit rather than deduction; VAT grouping election; optional small-enterprise VAT accounting; and the approved deferred-accounting arrangement for import VAT under VAT and Customs rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600: a Cyprus-established taxable person must register for VAT once taxable supplies exceed EUR 15,600 in any rolling 12-month period, or upon making any taxable supply from a Cyprus establishment (non-established persons have no turnover exemption); a parallel EUR 10,251.61 annual threshold applies to intra-Community acquisitions of goods and EUR 35,000 to intra-Community distance sales; exporters and persons making only zero-rated supplies must still register in order to exercise the right to deduct and recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exports applies under Art. 46 of Law 94(I)/2000 where goods are supplied to a customer outside the EU and are dispatched or transported out of the EU by the supplier, the customer, or a third party on behalf of either, provided objective export evidence is held: an accepted EU customs export declaration with IE-599 exit confirmation, transport documents showing physical departure normally within 90 days of the time of supply, and a valid EORI registration. Intra-Community (EU-to-EU) supplies are effectively taxed at 0% where the customer has a valid EU VAT number confirmed in VIES and the EC Sales List is duly filed. Offshore procurement and lithium-carbonate trading facilitation services rendered by the Cyprus company to non-EU principals are outside the scope of Cyprus VAT or zero-rated where the general B2B place-of-supply rule locates the supply at the customer's business establishment, provided the supplier retains sufficient due diligence on customer status, the underlying contractual chain, and payment flows. Imported battery-grade lithium-carbonate consignments entering Cyprus for onward trading may be processed under the approved import-VAT deferral (VAT declared and deducted on the VAT return rather than paid in cash at customs), customs warehousing, or inward processing arrangements, preserving working capital.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD accepted in THESEAS with IE-599 export movement/exit confirmation), Bill of Lading (or equivalent transport document evidencing physical departure of the lithium-carbonate consignment from the EU), Inward Remittance Certificate (bank MT103/foreign-currency credit records evidencing actual repatriation of the export proceeds to the Cyprus bank account)

Cyprus does not currently operate a real-time fiscal-clearance continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate or a digital stamping system (no Italian/Spanish-style immediate clearance and no Hungarian-style digital marking). B2G e-invoicing is mandatory under EU Directive 2014/55/EU via PEPPOL BIS 3.0 for invoices to Cypriot public authorities. A domestic B2B e-invoicing obligation has been enacted and is being phased in by taxpayer turnover bands, with structured electronic invoices to be issued and exchanged through the Tax Department approved interoperability/clearing environment once each phase is operative. VAT returns, EC Sales Lists and Intrastat declarations must be filed electronically via TAXISnet; customs declarations are electronic through the THESEAS/EU UCC systems; approved traders may account for import VAT on the VAT return under the deferred import VAT arrangement.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to substantive audit; the Tax Department cross-checks EC Sales Lists, THESEAS customs movement data, transport documents and FX inflows. A claim lacking complete export/FX documentation within the statutory six-year record-retention period is recharacterized as a taxable local supply and output VAT at 19% is assessed from the original due date, with default interest, an administrative fixed penalty of EUR 100 per defective filing and, for fraudulent or grossly negligent claims, penalties of up to twice the tax involved; fraudulent evasion additionally exposes directors and agents to criminal fines of up to EUR 5,000 and imprisonment and extends the assessment window to up to 11 years (six years in standard cases). The Tax Department also disallows corresponding input VAT recovery on clawback amounts, and circular or non-repatriated invoicing arrangements may trigger joint Customs/Tax investigations, director liability, and money-laundering reporting obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full eurozone member of the European Union. The euro is freely convertible with no prior-authorisation requirements for current or capital account transactions. EU capital movement liberalisation under Article 63 TFEU is fully implemented, together with Article 13 of the Cypriot Constitution. The temporary capital controls imposed during the 2013 banking crisis have been fully lifted across all sectors. Profit remittances from a Cyprus Limited liability company to non-resident shareholders are permitted without restriction, and no dividend withholding tax, interest withholding tax, or royalty withholding tax is imposed on outbound payments to non-residents. Banks apply documentation checks for source of funds and AML/Counter-Financing of Terrorism clearance, but these do not restrict currency convertibility. Cash and precious-metal transaction reporting thresholds under the Cyprus AML Statutory Framework may trigger enhanced scrutiny but do not create forex controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no mandatory export proceeds surrender, compulsory conversion, or statutory repatriation timeline. Exporters may retain receivables in foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts. However, for the trade flow of battery-grade lithium carbonate powder, each export or settlement transaction must be supported by underlying commercial contracts, invoices, transport documents (e.g., bill of lading, CMR, or warehouse warrants), and a Balance of Payments purpose code for goods trade. Where foreign exchange receipts exceed EUR 10,000, the EU Wire Transfer Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/1113) requires the payer's bank to transmit originator information, and Cyprus clearing banks apply enhanced trade finance monitoring for dual-use and metals commodity flows.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou) acting within the European System of Central Banks; the European Central Bank has jurisdiction over euro monetary policy, issuance, and systemic payment system oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cyprus-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions supervised by the Central Bank of Cyprus under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) are legally viable for low-value ancillary payments, virtual account currencies, and API-driven treasury operations. However, for main settlement of battery-grade lithium carbonate procurement involving large invoice amounts, documentary letters of credit, and trade finance workflows, traditional tier-1 commercial banks are overwhelmingly preferred. Offshore digital EMIs (for example, UK, Lithuania, or Singapore-licensed fintechs) do not automatically benefit from EU passporting rights into Cyprus post-Brexit unless the EMI is incorporated and licensed in a European Economic Area (EEA) state. Reliance on unlicensed offshore EMIs for primary trade settlement is a known AML/CTF red flag and may lead to account termination, sanctions scrutiny, and correspondent bank refusal. Overall viability rating: Medium for ancillary and treasury operations; Low for primary cross-border trade settlement whereas LC and reconciliation controls are paramount.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Inconsistency between declaring corporate substance in Cyprus and actual operational activity in a third country, such as Asia-Pacific lithium refiners or South American mining counterparties.

Defense evidence: Defense package must include: (1) signed commercial agency/procurement office lease or service agreement in Cyprus; (2) payroll and social insurance records for local staff; (3) board minutes reflecting decision-making in Cyprus; (4) full chain of title documents for lithium carbonate purchase offers and sale/purchase contracts; (5) audited or management accounts showing real domestic activity; and (6) a substance memorandum prepared by a Cyprus-licensed accounting firm.

Opaque beneficial ownership structure with multiple shell layers, nominee shareholders, or non-resident trust arrangements that obscure the ultimate beneficiary of the Cyprus limited company.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection routes for the Cyprus buyer entity are: (a) equity subscription for paid-up share capital, remitted by SWIFT MT103 with narrative 'Share Capital Subscription CY Ltd' and Balance of Payments direct-investment purpose code; (b) unsecured or secured shareholder loan supported by a written loan agreement and evidence of funds, carrying arm's-length interest and CRS/FATCA relevance; and (c) a trade/bridging advance from parent against signed lithium carbonate purchase contracts. All inbound transfers are executed without Central Bank of Cyprus prior approval, but the receiving bank must complete sanctions screening, UBO confirmation, and source-of-funds verification. For transfers above EUR 10,000, originator and beneficiary data are collected under EU Regulation 2023/1113, and the bank may require an amended corporate resolution and invoice or contract to align the remittance purpose with underlying trade operations.Outbound disbursements for battery-grade lithium carbonate procurement use the following clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for direct correspondent-bank wires in USD or EUR; MT700/MT710 series for documentary credits (Commercial Letters of Credit) and standby credits; SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for euro payments within EEA under a shorter settlement execution cycle; and SWIFT MT101/MT900/MT910 messages for treasury liquidity sweeping and standard settlement. For a commercial LC disbursement, the issuing Cypriot bank requires the conforming presentation of the supplier's commercial invoice, full set of clean-on-board bills of lading (or other transport documents), insurance certificate, certificate of origin, weight list, and lithium carbonate quality/analysis certificate. Non-euro payments to suppliers in USD, CNH, CLP, AUD, or other currencies must be settled through the bank's global correspondent network; the bank will execute only after ensuring the payment does not breach EU restrictive measures, UN sanctions, or Central Bank of Cyprus directives. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under Cyprus Companies Law Cap. 113, an independent statutory audit is required unless the company qualifies as a 'small entity' under Section 9A (total assets not exceeding EUR 350,000, net turnover not exceeding EUR 700,000, and average number of employees not exceeding 10). Audit exemption is not available for public-interest entities, banks, insurance companies, or entities falling under CySEC-regulated scope. In practice, a cross-border trading company with ongoing annual financial statements and tax submission will retain an auditor to support the TD4 filing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 aggregate annual connected-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File preparation in Cyprus administrative practice. Master File obligations arise where the multinational group meets EU TPD thresholds (consolidated turnover of at least EUR 750 million) and the Cyprus entity has aggregate related-party transactions above the Local File threshold.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not maintain a separate codified Economic Substance Regulations statute, but economic substance is essential for tax residency, DTA treaty access, and the substance-over-form doctrine. A Cyprus LTD engaged in cross-border battery-grade lithium carbonate procurement must demonstrate genuine local board meetings, commercial decision-making, contract execution, freight coordination, procurement operations, and bank account administration in Cyprus. Where substance is absent, the entity may be re-characterised as a shell, profits may be re-attributed to the jurisdiction of management, and treaty benefits can be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to twice the tax under-assessed coupled with statutory interest on the overdue tax; criminal conviction carries a fine not exceeding €10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years; seizure of bank accounts and assets may be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the company to under-declare tax may be personally liable for the unpaid liability and civil restitution; a court can impose travel bans, disqualification from directorship, disgorgement of unlawful gains, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Cypriot and foreign tax authorities may reclassify the company as non-resident, reallocate profits to the real management jurisdiction, add interest and penalties, impose late-filing penalties, and deny DTA relief for the artificial structure.

Director personal liability: Nominee and de facto directors can be held personally liable for false statutory declarations and for hidden liabilities; courts may lift the corporate veil where the company is a mere façade, exposing personal assets and subjecting directors to civil recovery orders, travel bans, and criminal sanctions.
Trade-Based Money Laundering / AML Infringements

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Law (L. 188(I)/2007) and EU AML/CTF rules can reach €1,000,000 for serious entities; criminal conviction for money laundering carries imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines up to €100,000; freezing and confiscation of trade proceeds are mandatory for serious offences.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners have personal criminal exposure for knowingly executing trade-based laundering; they are subject to prosecution, disqualification, travel bans, freezing orders against personal assets, and confiscation proceedings.
False Beneficial Ownership / Transparency Filing

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €30,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years for false declarations; an administrative penalty of up to €100 per day of continuing non-compliance may be imposed; the particulars may be transmitted to EU beneficial-ownership registries.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or knowingly permit false UBO filings are personally liable for false statements, subject to disqualification, travel bans, and orders for restitution; banks may restrict or freeze corporate accounts after a regulatory finding.

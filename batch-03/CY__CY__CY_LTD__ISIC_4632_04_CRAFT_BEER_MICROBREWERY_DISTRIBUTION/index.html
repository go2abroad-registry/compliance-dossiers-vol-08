Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Tax Department (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance; digital service portal: TaxForAll (https://taxforAll.mof.gov.cy); customs/excise clearance: Customs and Excise Department (https://www.mof.gov.cy/ce); corporate registry: Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (https://www.brs.gov.cy).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the Private Limited Company (ELF HE77) on-line with the Registrar of Companies to obtain the corporate Registration Number. Step 2 - The Tax Department automatically issues the statutory 8-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon incorporation; no separate application is required for a Cyprus-resident company. Step 3 - For VAT, file Form VAT-101 with the District VAT Officer within 60 days of the day the cumulative taxable turnover in any consecutive 12-month period reaches EUR 15,600, or file immediately where the company expects to exceed that threshold or intends to make zero-rated supplies (e.g., export of craft beer) and wishes to recover input VAT on export-related costs. Step 4 - A foreign (non-EU or EU non-established) entity making taxable supplies in Cyprus must register for VAT directly or appoint a Cyprus fiscal representative approved by the Commissioner of Taxation; a non-resident company with a Cyprus permanent establishment must also apply to the Tax Department for a TIN (Form TD-2001) and register with the Social Insurance Services for payroll obligations if it engages local staff in its distribution warehouse.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections and mechanisms: (i) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital introduced in an accounting year, claimed by submitting the audited financial statements and a supporting computation with the corporate income tax return (Article 9(10) of Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002); (ii) Group relief election for losses surrendered between Cyprus-resident group companies with at least 75% common ownership (Article 25), made in writing with the tax return; (iii) Election to exempt foreign permanent establishment profits for non-listed tax-resident companies (Article 14(2)(b)); (iv) VAT group election (Article 16 of the VAT Law) allowing affiliated persons established in Cyprus to be treated as a single taxable person, subject to Commissioner approval; (v) Hybrid mismatch disclosure and optional alignment elections under the Cyprus ATAD implementation provisions (Law 34(I)/2019), including the double-deduction and deduction-non-inclusion reversal mechanisms; (vi) Intra-community (VIES) supplier verification is procedural rather than elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General domestic supplies of goods and services: EUR 15,600 in any consecutive 12-month period; cross-border EU distance sales of beer (B2C) and other electronically-supplied services: EUR 10,000 pan-EU threshold under the OSS regime; voluntary registration is available below the threshold to exporters and zero-rated suppliers in order to recover input VAT; non-established businesses have no minimum threshold and must register when a taxable supply nexus arises in Cyprus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) is available under Article 23 of the VAT Law for: (i) the direct export of craft beer physically despatched or transported by the supplier or by a third party on the supplier's behalf to a destination outside the EU; (ii) indirect exports where the goods are shipped to an overseas customer via a Cyprus forwarding agent or export house, provided the evidence bundle is retained by the supplier; (iii) intra-Community supplies (B2B) of beer to a customer in another Member State holding a valid EU VAT number confirmed on VIES; and (iv) ancillary services such as international transport, customs handling and cold-chain logistics supplied directly in connection with the export. Eligibility requires that the goods leave the territory of the EU in the same unaltered condition within the commercially accepted period (Cypriot administrative practice: dispatch and physical exit within 90 days of the supply), that the supplier holds complete documentary evidence in the 8-box format, and that the invoice clearly states 0% VAT, the contractual export clause, and the customer's EU VAT number where applicable. Where any documentation element is missing or the deadline is missed, the supply must be reclassified as standard-rated at 19% with input VAT restoration reversed.

  • SAD - Single Administrative Document / Customs Export Declaration (e-Export Summary) lodged in the Cyprus e-Customs platform with the Electronic Export Accompanying Document number
  • Bill of Lading (ocean freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and Export Contract / Purchase Order supporting the stated transaction value

Cyprus does not currently operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping system for B2B/B2C invoices. Mandatory electronic invoicing applies exclusively to B2G supplies: every invoice issued to the Cyprus Government and public bodies must be transmitted through the Cyprus National E-Invoicing System (MyCyProof), a PEPPOL-based platform, in EN 16931-compliant format via a certified PEPPOL access point, using the Cyprus-specific e-invoice schema and Business Interoperability Interfaces. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary and subject to customer consent; the Tax Department accepts all legally valid invoices irrespective of format. Cyprus is aligning with the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package; real-time e-invoicing or digital reporting obligations are expected to be phased in progressively by 2030-2035, at which point API-based transactional clearance may be introduced.

The Tax Department and Customs and Excise Department cross-match the SAD export declarations with VIES listings, bank FX receipts, container manifests and cold-chain transport logs. If the zero-rating is disallowed due to missing proof, late exit or fraudulent documentation, the VAT is clawed back retrospectively at 19% together with statutory interest at 9% per annum from the original due date, plus late-payment penalties of up to 100% of the tax under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law (Law 4/1978). Fraudulent claims, including purchase of false export confirmation documents or circular shipment schemes, carry criminal liability under Section 55 of the VAT Law and Law 4/1978: a fine of up to EUR 17,086 or double the tax evaded (whichever is greater) and imprisonment of up to 5 years; the assessment period is extended to 10 years for fraud or gross negligence; directors are jointly and severally liable, and the debt is published in the Tax Department's defaulter registry with revocation of VAT registration and withdrawal of the excise duty warehouse/kegging approval where relevant.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a euro-area member; the euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account transactions. Under Article 63 TFEU, all restrictions on capital movements and payments between member states and third countries are abolished, subject only to EU financial sanctions and tax reporting. Profit remittance is unrestricted in principle: dividends may be paid freely after audited financial statements, corporate tax clearance, and presentation of applicable Residence Certificate/Form IR.616 where treaty relief is required. There is no withholding tax on dividends under Cyprus domestic law, and no deposit/transaction currency controls. Bank practice requires a distribution justification pack comprising audited financials, tax certificate, and UBO confirmation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists. Since EU membership and euro adoption, exporters may retain foreign-currency receipts in dual-currency/foreign-currency accounts and convert to EUR at commercial market rates at their discretion. The only legal obligations are statistical: export declarations (customs for non-EU; Intrastat/EC Sales List for EU), plus balance-of-payments reporting via the commercial bank for trade credits, collection terms, and pre-payments. Commercial documents should evidence typical export collection cycles to support transfer pricing and VAT/excise arrangements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) — the national competent authority integrated in the Eurosystem/ECB, responsible for banking supervision, exchange control statistics, and balance-of-payments reporting under the Central Bank of Cyprus Law of 2002.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating for an EU-licensed EMI (PSD2) is Medium as a complementary rail, Low as an exclusive treasury route. Legal constraints: EMIs may provide payment accounts, issue payment instruments and execute payment transactions, but may not lend money; they are not protected by the Cyprus Deposit Protection Scheme, and CBC-regulated banks generally decline to treat EMI balances as equivalent to a commercial bank relationship. An EMI may be used for pass-through collection/disbursement to low-risk EU distributors, but must be backed by a primary licensed banking relationship for capital injections, escrow, trade finance and foreign-exchange analytics.

A small-company audit exemption applies when at least two of the following criteria are satisfied for two consecutive years: (a) balance-sheet total does not exceed EUR 350,000; (b) annual net turnover does not exceed EUR 700,000; (c) average number of employees does not exceed 10. The exemption is unavailable for public-interest entities or if shareholders holding at least 10% of the paid-up capital request an audit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions (Local File); Master File mandatory only where the consolidated group turnover equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus enforces economic substance through the Income Tax Law, Tax Department circulars, EU ATAD and GAAR principles; a craft beer distributor claiming Cyprus residency must demonstrate local board management, employees, warehousing and commercial decision-making; artificial conduit structures are recharacterized and denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law (L.2/1987): fine up to EUR 50,000 or three times the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years; additional interest of 3.5% per annum.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for evaded tax, civil restitution, court-imposed travel bans and disqualification from board positions.
Shell Company / Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of Tax Residency Certificate, retroactive re-assessment of profits, cancellation of NID and IP Box reliefs, GAAR adjustments, and loss of treaty protection.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil under Companies Law Cap. 113, s. 305; directors personally ordered to restore diverted profits, prohibited from leaving Cyprus and liable for damages.
AML/CFT Breach in Distribution Payments

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 100,000 per contravention of the AML Law (L.188(I)/2007), revocation of the distribution license, and criminal money laundering penalties.

Director personal liability: The MLRO and directors face imprisonment up to 5 years, confiscation of proceeds, travel bans and personal fines.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment, interest at 3.5% per annum, and administrative penalties up to EUR 25,000 for documentation failure.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties; referral to the Tax Department TP audit unit and travel ban pending settlement.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Ministry of Finance) — official portal: https://taxportal.cy; website: https://www.mof.gov.cy/taxdep

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned private company limited by shares is first incorporated under the Cyprus Companies Law with the Registrar of Companies (HE prefix). After incorporation, the entity must submit tax registration via the Tax Portal using the prescribed tax registration forms, attaching the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, registered-office details, and beneficial ownership particulars. The Tax Department then allocates an income tax identification number (TIN) for corporate tax and social insurance purposes. A separate VAT identification number must be applied for when taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold or where non-established suppliers commence local taxable activity; a tax representative may be required for non-resident entities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. The Cyprus tax law provides for group-loss relief election between resident companies linked by a 75% voting/ownership chain; an election may be made to apply the Notional Interest Deduction (NID) on new equity introduced into the company; shipping and tonnage-tax elections are available for qualifying vessels; hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD automatically neutralise double deduction or deduction-without-inclusion arrangements. Cyprus tax-resident companies are taxed on worldwide income, but the territoriality rules exempt foreign permanent establishment profits and foreign-sourced dividend/interest passive income in defined conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for suppliers established in Cyprus; non-established taxable persons must register from the first taxable supply, without a turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Cyprus VAT-registered exporter may apply the 0% rate to a supply of collectible die-cast scale models only if the goods are physically sold and transported out of the EU within the required timeframe and the exporter holds full documentary evidence of export. Services rendered to a non-EU business customer can be treated as zero-rated where the business customer is established outside the EU and the service is directly linked to that customer; if evidence is absent, VAT at 19% must be accounted for.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / EXS declaration with Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR evidencing physical movement of goods to the non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer record showing receipt of foreign sales proceeds

Cyprus does not currently impose general real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for B2B/B2C invoices. E-invoicing is mandatory for public-sector buyers under EU Directive 2014/55/EU using the national PEPPOL-compliant platform. VAT returns and EC sales lists are filed electronically through the Tax Department's e-services portal; no separate API clearance stamping is required for die-cast model export supplies.

Export zero-rating is closely audited. If the exporter fails to retain or produce the required export documentation, the Tax Department may reassess the supply at 19% VAT with statutory interest from the date of supply. Refunds already granted may be clawed back through an assessment. Fraudulent or forged export documentation is a criminal offence under Cyprus VAT law and may result in court-imposed fines, imprisonment, and disqualification of the exporter; the standard assessment limitation period is up to 6 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible. Cyprus joined the eurozone on 1 January 2008 and applies the complete liberalisation of capital movements mandated by Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union; there are no exchange controls, prior authorisations, investment licensing gates, or profit-remittance quotas applicable to current or capital account transactions. Dividends and other profit distributions to shareholders, whether resident or non-resident, are subject to zero Cypriot withholding tax, and no central-bank approval is required for repatriation. The only residual obligations are statistical balance-of-payments reporting (CBC BoP returns filed by the receiving bank) and AML/CFT declaration duties for cash and cross-border transfers of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion of foreign currency into EUR, and no prescribed repatriation timeline. Export receipts in USD, GBP, CNY or other currencies may be retained indefinitely in designated multi-currency accounts at a licensed Cypriot credit institution. However, all export revenue must be recorded for CBC BoP reporting under the Eurostat trade-in-goods codes, the underlying transaction documents (commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation) must be retained for a minimum of five years for both tax and AML inspection, and any physical cash component above EUR 10,000 is prohibited under the Cyprus Cash Payments Restriction Law 90(I)/2023.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating as a member of the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments supervision is exercised jointly with the Cyprus Statistical Service (CYSTAT) and the Ministry of Finance under the Central Bank of Cyprus Law 1963-2023 and the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law 188(I)/2007.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: 7/10 as a supplemental collections rail; 3/10 as the primary treasury settlement rail. EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may passport into Cyprus under Directive 2009/110/EC and PSD2, and reputable digital EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer) can onboard CY_LTD for low-value outward disbursements and PayPal balance sweeps. However, EMIs are not licensed deposit-taking institutions, cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit, and generally terminate manufacturing/import-export profiles unless a 12-month invoice history and an existing primary bank relationship are demonstrated. The Central Bank of Cyprus does not license foreign offshore EMIs for domestic clearing, so any offshore EMI must rely on an EEA passport notification and a Cyprus-registered agent; the resulting account cannot receive inbound equity capital injection or serve as the CBC BoP reporting channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital is cleared exclusively by SWIFT MT103/MT202 wire in EUR to the segregated Capital Escrow Account at Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank. The remitter field must match exactly the name of the shareholder in the Cyprus share register; the remittance reference must cite the ECB Balance-of-Payments purpose code 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' (Eurostat BoP Item 8.1.1). The receiving bank files the CBC statutory BoP return on the value date, and the company must lodge the allotment return (Form SH02) with the Cyprus Registrar of Companies within one month of allotment. For injections exceeding EUR 15,000 from outside the EEA, the bank additionally requires a funds-trace summary (notarised shareholder source-of-funds statement, 12-month remitter bank statements, and the signed share-subscription agreement) before operational usage is permitted.Operational outflows settle through the EUR-denominated operational account: SEPA and SWIFT MT103 for international wires; SWIFT MT700-issued irrevocable documentary letters of credit (sight or 90-day usance) with Hellenic Bank or Eurobank Cyprus for recurring zinc-alloy ingot imports from Asia; and documentary collections for smaller consignments. Export receivables from EU, UK and US collectors' markets are received by SWIFT MT103 and cleared through the bank's trade desk, with CBC/Eurostat BoP trade-in-goods coding applied automatically; every disbursement or collection requires the backup file of commercial invoice, purchase order, customs Single Administrative Document (SAD), and bill of lading. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cyprus TP documentation rules apply to tax years commencing on or after 1 July 2022. Mandatory documentation exists where controlled transactions exceed: €1,000,000 for goods, €250,000 for services, and €100,000 for royalties, interest, or financial transactions. A Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least €750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not maintain Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but genuine economic substance is mandatory under: (i) OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines as incorporated by Cyprus TP rules; (ii) ATAD-implemented CFC and general anti-abuse rules (GAAR); (iii) the management-and-control test for Cypriot tax residency; and (iv) the nexus requirement of the IP Box regime. A Cyprus Ltd functioning purely as an invoicing shell without staff, premises, or real decision-making is exposed to reassessment, TP adjustments, and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Additional tax equal to the evaded amount, administrative penalties of up to 100% of the tax underpaid, statutory interest, and criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Courts may lift the corporate veil under Cyprus Companies Law (Cap. 113) for fraudulent trading; directors face civil restitution of unpaid taxes with interest and costs and may be barred from leaving Cyprus through departure prohibition orders.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Non-Arm's-Length Pricing

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment increasing taxable income, penalty of 25% on the additional tax arising where documentation is absent, and statutory interest at 3.5% p.a. on the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve inaccurate TP disclosures may be personally assessed for negligence and face director disqualification proceedings and civil claims from shareholders or creditors.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement Lacking Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR/CFC challenge: the arrangement is disregarded, income is reallocated to the ultimate parent, additional tax plus interest is imposed, and treaty benefits are denied under anti-abuse provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax where the arrangement is adjudged a sham; criminal liability arises where artificiality masks fraud or deliberate tax evasion.
AML / Money Laundering Through Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Offence under Cyprus AML law (L.188(I)/2007): imprisonment of up to 14 years and/or a fine of up to €500,000; the Unit for Combating Money Laundering (MOKAS) may freeze and forfeit the assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or knowingly fail to prevent suspicious transactions face personal criminal liability, confiscation of personal assets, directorship prohibitions, and travel bans in cross-border investigations.
Late Filing / Failure to Maintain Books and Records

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of €100 plus monthly surcharges capped at €1,200 per return, estimated assessments, revocation of tax clearance certificates, and statutory interest on all unpaid amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be sued by the Tax Department and creditors for losses flowing from the company's non-compliance; courts may issue winding-up orders and director disqualification orders of up to 3 years.

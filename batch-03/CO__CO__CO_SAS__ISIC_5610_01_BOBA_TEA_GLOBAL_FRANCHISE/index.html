Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital for S.A.S.; capital may be subscribed in authorized shares and paid in cash or non-cash assets. No escrow account is legally required, but the bylaws must stipulate the term for payment of subscribed shares, and any actual disbursement must be deposited in a Colombian bank account as documented by bank certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the Cuerpo de Bomberos (fire brigade) certifying fire extinguishers, emergency exits, smoke detectors, and evacuation plan; structural load inspection certificate and uso de suelo (land use compatibility) from the local Alcaldía/Planeación; and workplace clearance from the ARL (Aseguradora de Riesgos Laborales) registering employees and mandatory occupational risk prevention program

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – National Tax and Customs Directorate of Colombia; taxpayer portal: MUISCA (https://muisca.dian.gov.co); official site: https://www.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

A Colombian S.A.S. obtains its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) at incorporation through the Chamber of Commerce Single Registry (RUE). The NIT is also the VAT ID; no separate VAT-ID suffix exists. The company must then complete/update its RUT (Registro Único Tributario) before DIAN and activate the applicable tax responsibilities: income tax (declarante del impuesto sobre la renta), IVA responsible (responsable de IVA), withholding agent (agente de retención), and electronic invoicing (facturador electrónico). For a foreign entity itself acquiring Colombian tax registration, the non-resident must appoint a local agent/representative and file the RUT/NIT application before DIAN using apostilled or legalized constitutional documents (translated to Spanish by an authorized translator); registration must precede the first invoice or tax return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No Colombian check-the-box or US-style fiscal-transparency election is available; an S.A.S. is mandatorily taxed at entity level and cannot be treated as a pass-through/partnership. The only elective regime of practical relevance is the 'Régimen Simple de Tributación' (SIMPLE), a turnover-limited consolidated income/IVA/municipal tax regime for qualifying small businesses; it is not an entity-classification or hybrid-mismatch election. No hybrid mismatch elections exist for outbound royalty or syrup-export structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None in Colombian pesos for legal entities; the S.A.S. is a mandatory IVA responsible from the first taxable act. There is no economic-turnover registration threshold for companies. The statutory de-minimis non-responsible status, where still applicable, is limited to natural persons under the UVT-based turnover limits and is unavailable to a corporate franchise/supply-chain entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods (bubble-tea syrups, packaging, ingredients) qualify as 0% IVA exports when legal ownership passes to a non-resident buyer and the goods are physically exported from Colombian customs territory. Services (franchise royalties, brand licensing, technical assistance, software development, digital customer service) qualify as 0% IVA export services when rendered from Colombia to a non-resident, economically used outside Colombia, and not embedded in Colombian taxable transactions. The Colombian S.A.S. retains full right to recover input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports. For royalty flows from foreign franchisees, the zero-rating is available only where substance, transfer-pricing, and export documentation demonstrate a true cross-border export service.

  • Validated Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX/Formulario Único de Exportaciones) transmitted via DIAN/VUCE
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Colombian exporter
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exit from Colombian customs territory
  • Transport/customs documentation endorsed to the Colombian exporter and foreign buyer records
  • Declaración de cambio (foreign-exchange declaration) and inward remittance certificate/bank collection evidence showing repatriation of export proceeds within the mandatory 180-day period

Mandatory end-to-end electronic invoicing via the 'Factura Electrónica de Venta' regime: every local B2B/B2C invoice must be generated in DIAN-authorized software, digitally signed, assigned the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica), and transmitted in real time to DIAN's MUISCA/Sede Electrónica platform for prior or posterior validation. B2B sales require electronic acceptance (acuse de recibo); export invoices must match the validated customs export declaration. Physical fiscal stamping is not used in Colombia; DIAN's digital validation operates as the clearance and control mechanism.

DIAN audits export zero-rating refund claims by matching the export declaration, transport documents, customs exit validation, foreign buyer identification, FX repatriation, and books/records. Missing or inconsistent documentation causes outright rejection of the 'saldo a favor' (VAT refund balance). If a refund was previously paid, DIAN will claw it back retroactively plus default interest and may impose tax-inaccuracy penalties (sanción por inexactitud), improper-refund penalties, and corporate debarment from electronic-refund programs; fraudulent schemes trigger criminal tax-fraud liability under Colombian law. The general assessment/statute-of-limitations window is five years from the statutory filing deadline, with extended exposure where criminal fraud or related-party offshore concealment is alleged.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed-float exchange regime in which the COP is fully convertible within the 'mercado cambiario' (authorised foreign-exchange market). Current-account transactions—including franchise royalties, brand-license fees, syrup imports, and dividend payments—are freely channelable through licensed commercial banks and authorized exchange houses without prior administrative approval, provided each transfer is documented in the applicable Declaración de Cambio filed by the intermediary. The capital account is open for unrestricted sectors, and the retail food-and-beverage franchise activity is unrestricted; however, foreign direct investment must be registered in the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales maintained by Banco de la República to preserve the statutory right to repatriate capital and remit profits. Profit remittance by the Colombian S.A.S. to non-resident shareholders is legally unblocked once (i) the corporate income tax (35% headline rate) is settled, (ii) the non-resident dividend withholding (7.5% under Article 242 E.T.) is discharged, and (iii) the foreign-exchange operation is declared under the appropriate causal code. There are no FX quotas, priority lists, or auction mechanisms restricting convertibility for legitimate franchise-treasury flows; the practical hurdles are documentary (apostilled corporate records, Spanish translations, UBO declarations) rather than regulatory. Derivative operations are permitted solely for genuine commercial hedge purposes; speculative merchant-level positions are subject to SFC sanction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Resolución 8 de 2000 (Articles 78–84 for goods; Article 106 and related for services), as simplified by Decree 1192 of 2020, the Colombian S.A.S. exporting franchise services, know-how licensing, and syrups must channel 100% of the export proceeds through the authorised 'mercado cambiario' within 6 months from the date of shipment (goods) or from the invoicing/service-provision date (services). The statutory obligation is 'canalización y declaración'—routing hard currency through a licensed intermediary and filing the exchange declaration (Form 1 for goods exports; Form 2 for services/royalties)—not compulsory conversion to COP: the exporter may retain USD/EUR in a foreign-currency account at a local commercial bank or, in specifically authorised cases, in a 'cuenta de compensación' abroad. The intermediary must lodge the declaration with Banco de la República within 5 business days of settlement, generating the Certificado de Cambio that constitutes the legal evidence of channeling. Extended collection terms (up to 18 months for certain goods) require prior application to Banco de la República or the authorised intermediary under the general regime. For the boba-tea franchise, each royalty and franchise fee receivable from foreign franchisees must appear in the S.A.S. receivables ledger with a matching commercial invoice, and the related FX must be channeled and declared within the 6-month window; failure triggers the exchange-regime sanctioning framework (multas cambiarias) applied by the SFC or DIAN.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), acting in coordination with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and the DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, and Resolución 8 de 2000 (Régimen de Cambios Internacionales y de Comercio Exterior), as updated by Circular Reglamentaria Externa DCIN-83 and Decree 1192 of 2020.

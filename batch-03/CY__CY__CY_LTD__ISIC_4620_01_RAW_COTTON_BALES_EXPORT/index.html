Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department (Φορολογικό Τμήμα) of the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; official e-services portal: 'Tax For All' (TFA) integrated digital platform at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy.

E-government filing portal

Foreign and domestic entities must first incorporate with the Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver to obtain an HE company registration number. The company then applies to the Tax Department for a Cyprus Tax Identification Code (TIC) via the Tax For All portal by submitting the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, and details of directors and ultimate beneficial owners. If taxable supplies are made or are intended, the entity files VAT Form TD 2001 to obtain an 8-digit Cyprus VAT number (format CY + 8 digits). Registration as an employer under the Social Insurance Scheme is additionally required where employees are engaged. Non-resident controlling owners may file the non-resident declaration and, if relevant, elect tax residency under Section 2(1b) of the Income Tax Law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus has no check-the-box or general pass-through election under domestic law. Statutory elections available to a Cyprus Limited entity include: (i) Section 2(1b) election to be treated as a Cyprus tax resident through the TIC registration mechanism; (ii) election for the Notional Interest Deduction (NID) on new equity introduced after 2014 (Sections 9B-9G of the Income Tax Law); (iii) election for group loss relief and the transfer of losses between group companies under Section 19; (iv) election to apply straight-line or written-down-value depreciation for capital assets; and (v) ATAD-aligned hybrid mismatch self-assessment and disclosure elections where cross-border arrangements risk double non-taxation. All elections are filed through the annual corporate income tax return or the Tax For All digital process.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 — cumulative taxable turnover in any trailing 12-month period; no threshold for non-established suppliers, who must register regardless of turnover; EUR 10,000 intra-Community distance-sales and distance-sales-of-services threshold for cross-border B2C transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically removed from the European Union qualify for zero-rating (0% VAT) under Article 44 of the Cyprus VAT Law where the goods leave EU customs territory within 90 days of the supply and are evidenced by customs export documentation. Intra-EU B2B dispatches to a customer holding a valid VIES VAT number are zero-rated under Article 45, provided the goods are transported to another Member State. Ancillary transport and logistics services directly linked to the exported goods and supplied to the exporter are zero-rated under Article 46. For the brokerage business model, commissions charged to a non-EU principal or to a non-EU buyer are outside the scope of Cyprus VAT on place-of-supply grounds (place of supply is the customer's establishment), while domestic services supporting the physical export must evidence a direct link to the export transaction. Zero-rating is unavailable, and VAT at 19% is due, where the required export documentation is not held by the supplier at the time of the audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A/SAD) — electronic CusDEC acceptance printout with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical removal of the goods from EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation or payment ledger evidencing settlement of the export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Sales or brokerage contract and Incoterms evidence demonstrating the transfer of risk and transport responsibility
  • Certificate of Origin or non-preferential origin proof where required by the destination market or commodity-specific rules

Cyprus does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, SDI-style digital stamping, or an API-based clearance mandate. E-invoicing is mandatory only for business-to-government (B2G) procurement under EU Directive 2014/55/EU using the PEPPOL format, and remains voluntary for B2B. VAT compliance is return-based via the Tax For All portal: electronic VAT returns (Form VAT 300/301), VIES recapitulative statements, and mandatory Intrastat/EXSTAT declarations filed to the Cyprus Statistical Service above dispatch and arrival movement thresholds. Under the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) Directive adopted in 2025, Cyprus will transpose compulsory structured e-invoicing and digital reporting for intra-EU B2B supplies effective from 1 July 2030.

The Tax Department may retrospectively claw back wrongly zero-rated VAT within the general five-year assessment window, and without time limit where the claim is fraudulent. Retrospective assessment converts the export to a taxable domestic supply at 19% VAT, with interest accruing at 1% per month (approximately 12.7% effective annual) from the date the VAT would have been payable, plus late-return penalties of EUR 50 per month and administrative fines of up to 10% of the unpaid tax. Where forged export proof, phantom exports, or deliberately false VIES declarations are detected, criminal sanctions apply under the VAT Law, including fines up to EUR 42,699 and/or imprisonment of up to three years. Customs penalties under the Union Customs Code may be applied cumulatively, and the exporter may lose refund priority, be moved to the high-risk verification lane requiring 100% documentary collateral review, and be barred from zero-rating future consignments until outstanding assessments are settled. Cyprus banks also apply a 180-day inward-remittance benchmark for documentary collection and letters of credit; unsupported forex repatriation is treated as non-export domestic turnover by the Tax Department.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a member of the Eurozone, and the Euro is freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions under the EU Treaty and the Cypriot Exchange Control Law (Cap. 199) as liberalized. No general exchange control approval is required for cross-border payments, including dividend remittances, royalty fees, trade settlements, or capital repatriation, provided the transactions comply with EU anti-money-laundering (AML), sanctions, and statistical reporting obligations. Profit remittances from a Cyprus Limited Company are subject to no withholding tax under domestic law and no capital controls are in force as of the reporting date; the temporary emergency capital controls imposed in March 2013 were fully phased out by April 2015. For select categories such as loans exceeding €100,000 between residents and non-residents, or direct investments outside the EU, the Central Bank of Cyprus may require statistical or balance-of-payments reporting forms (e.g., Form CAC/Investment) for registry purposes only; these do not constitute authorization hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements for export proceeds in Cyprus. A Cyprus exporter may retain 100% of export receipts in any currency and holds full discretion over the timing and currency composition of any conversion. The only obligations are documentary: exporters must present proof of export (Customs export declaration, SAD or EX-A form, or electronic equivalent) to their licensed bank for the completion of the underlying transaction for balance-of-payments statistics, and file monthly or quarterly ITRS (Integrated Transaction Reporting System) returns for monetary amounts exceeding the Central Bank of Cyprus reporting thresholds, currently set at €10,000 per individual transaction or aggregate reporting on a monthly basis. No mandatory conversion ratio has ever been applied to cotton bale exporters under the Euro regime.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Trapeziki Kyprou)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legally feasible with a Medium feasibility rating for a cotton exporter, but operationally constrained in practice. Under the EU Electronic Money Directive (2013/36/EU as transposed by the Cyprus Law on Electronic Money Institutions, L.81(I)/2018), EMIs authorized in Cyprus or licensed in another EEA state may operate passporting rights in Cyprus and can provide payment accounts, cross-border PAYOUT/PAYIN services, and FX conversion without a banking license. However, mainstream Cyprus banks often de-risk against corporates who maintain a fintech EMI account as a primary treasury rail, and for documentary trade operations (LC issuance, documentary collections, goods inspection procedures), a full commercial bank relationship is effectively required because Cyprus's clearing and settlement infrastructure—the Cyprus Automated Clearing House (JCC/CCS) and the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission CySEC-regulated investment ecosystem—does not accept EMIs as settlement banks for SWIFT MT103 credits through the Bank of Cyprus/Hellenic Bank nostro network. Its recommended use is as a secondary liquidity overlay for supplier payments below €10,000 or for disbursements to non-bankable cotton suppliers in low-banking-penetration markets, but not as the principal clearing rail for a cross-border raw cotton export operation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital for a Cyprus Ltd cotton exporter generally takes the form of share capital subscriptions, shareholder loans, or trade advance payments, and is routed via SWIFT MT103 messaging with the mandatory EU exclusion code and Cyprus purpose codes. For equity injections, the participant code and the purpose code must reflect Bank of Cyprus / Hellenic Bank's Balance of Payments statistical form—specifically code 101 (direct investment in Cyprus by resident enterprise) or code 103 (equity share capital), with an accompanying board resolution and share registry filing at the Cyprus Registrar (Form HE33). For shareholder loans, the SWIFT MT103 beneficiary field must be the Cyprus entity's corporate account, with the field 50K disclosing the lender and the remittance information containing 'LOAN-AGREEMENT-DATE [DD/MM/YYYY] AND ISIC-4620' to ensure full traceability under the Central Bank of Cyprus reporting threshold of €10,000. The bank will require a signed loan agreement bearing economic substance (arm's-length interest rate) and a verification of the lender's own banking history to prevent suspected circular financing. Trade advances for raw cotton exports are coded under purpose code PR0101 (Inward payments for exports of goods) for advance payment from the overseas cotton mill or textile buyer; the proof of the underlying contract (proforma invoice, sale and purchase agreement, or letter of credit) must be submitted before the funds are posted, and if the goods are exported more than 12 months after the receipt of funds, the Central Bank of Cyprus may reclassify the receipt as a financial liability.For a cotton bale exporter using a Cyprus intermediary trading entity, outbound trade disbursements are mainly executed over three compliant clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct credit — for supplier confirmations, ginning services, freight, and commissions; the MT103 must be structured with field 50F/ordering institution, field 59/beneficiary institution, and field 70/remittance information, including the invoice number, commodity code 5201/5202, and the phrase 'TRADE-PAYMENT-UNDER-ISIC-4620-EXPORT-CONTRACT'; (ii) Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) — for high-value cotton transactions above €250,000 from the Cyprus exporter to the end-buyer, using a confirmed irrevocable usance L/C drawn on the buyer's international bank, with freight and insurance covered under Incoterms CIF or FOB; and (iii) Documentary Collection (D/P or D/A under URC 522) — for cash-against-documents settlements where title documents (bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, cotton quality certificate) are released to the remitting bank in Nicosia through the SWIFT MT798 network against payment or acceptance. For cash disbursements to cotton farms in non-EU countries where a physical bank account is absent, the Cyprus entity should utilize money transfer operators only when the counterparty amount is below the Anti-Money-Laundering Regulation threshold of €2,000, and every aggregate payment above that level must be routed through the entity's primary Cyprus commercial bank to maintain clean audit trails.Outright FX Forward Contract (sale of EUR / purchase of USD) with a maturity date matched to the cotton L/C settlement date, transacted under the 2006 ISDA Master Agreement or a Cyprus Law (Cap. 113) netting confirmation, available from Bank of Cyprus Markets or Eurobank Cyprus dealing desk for notional values above €25,000.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) for exotic currencies (e.g., TMT, PKR, BDT, or EGP, based on the cotton origin market), under Central Bank of Cyprus authorization as a type of financial derivative permitted to corporates dealing in physical trade, with cash settlement in EUR on a specified fixing source.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CCIRS) for transforming a floating-rate USD import finance facility incurred to pay cotton bale suppliers into a fixed-EUR liability, permissible under the Cyprus Markets in Financial Instruments Law (CySEC) and executed with a licensed Investment Firm that also holds a Banking and Investment Services license from the Central Bank of Cyprus.Currency Option (Call/Put on USD/EUR) providing an asymmetric hedge on the exchange rate for the cotton export receivables, permitted under EU MiFID II optionality for non-retail corporate clients, with valuation and collateral governed by the 1992 and 2002 ISDA Equity Derivatives Definitions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Legally feasible with a Medium feasibility rating for a cotton exporter, but operationally constrained in practice. Under the EU Electronic Money Directive (2013/36/EU as transposed by the Cyprus Law on Electronic Money Institutions, L.81(I)/2018), EMIs authorized in Cyprus or licensed in another EEA state may operate passporting rights in Cyprus and can provide payment accounts, cross-border PAYOUT/PAYIN services, and FX conversion without a banking license. However, mainstream Cyprus banks often de-risk against corporates who maintain a fintech EMI account as a primary treasury rail, and for documentary trade operations (LC issuance, documentary collections, goods inspection procedures), a full commercial bank relationship is effectively required because Cyprus's clearing and settlement infrastructure—the Cyprus Automated Clearing House (JCC/CCS) and the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission CySEC-regulated investment ecosystem—does not accept EMIs as settlement banks for SWIFT MT103 credits through the Bank of Cyprus/Hellenic Bank nostro network. Its recommended use is as a secondary liquidity overlay for supplier payments below €10,000 or for disbursements to non-bankable cotton suppliers in low-banking-penetration markets, but not as the principal clearing rail for a cross-border raw cotton export operation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; e-filing via ARIADNE Tax Portal (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

For a Cyprus private limited company, the Registrar of Companies issues the Certificate of Incorporation. The company must then apply to the Tax Department (through ARIADNE or the local District Tax Office) for a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN). The application requires the incorporation certificate, Memorandum and Articles of Association, registered office address, details of directors and secretary, and a description of intended business activities. Upon approval, a 9-digit TIN is issued; this serves as the corporate tax identifier and will become a VAT registration number once the relevant VAT registration is completed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available for Cyprus limited companies; they are per se taxable as separate entities. Hybrid mismatch election mechanisms operate under EU ATAD implementing rules, allowing adjustments for blended-taxation and double-deduction arrangements in cross-border structures. No statutory tax-residence election exists; a Cyprus incorporated company is resident if managed and controlled in Cyprus.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for a Cyprus-established business; non-established foreign suppliers have no turnover threshold and must register from the first taxable supply; for intra-EU distance sales, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 OSS threshold applies to sales to consumers in other Member States.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Cyprus exporter of dietary supplements and nutraceuticals may apply the zero rate to supplies of tangible goods exported to a destination outside the European Union, provided the exporter obtains and retains proof that the goods physically left the EU within 90 days from the date of supply. Also zero-rated are services directly linked to exported goods and certain B2B offshore services. For each export, the supplier's VAT registration number and evidence of shipment are required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading (or other transport document confirming physical export)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank receipt substantiating receipt of payment from the overseas buyer

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based clearance mandate applies to Cyprus VAT. E-invoicing is permitted and must satisfy the EU VAT invoice authenticity and integrity requirements; public procurement invoicing under the Peppol framework is encouraged but not a universal private-sector fiscal mandate.

The Cyprus Tax Department and Customs closely audit export substantiation; missing or fraudulent documentation triggers de-zero-rating and assessment of 19% VAT, interest at the statutory rate, civil penalties of up to EUR 17,000 or double the tax loss, and potential criminal liability for corrupt or fraudulent claims. The clawback assessment period is generally six years from the end of the relevant tax period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Cyprus is a Eurosystem member state. The euro is freely convertible for both current account transactions and capital account operations. There are no exchange control restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds for Cypriot corporate entities. The only administrative hurdles are documentary: audited financial statements, tax compliance/certificate of good standing, corporate resolutions, and standard EU anti-money-laundering declarations for cross-border transactions. Capital restrictions historically imposed during the 2013 financial crisis were fully abolished. Non-resident shareholders may freely receive dividend distributions; Cyprus does not impose withholding tax on ordinary dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, provided the company is not an inward-investment vehicle deriving income from immovable property in Cyprus subject to special defence contribution rules. Dividends paid out of profits derived from permanent establishments abroad are also generally exempt. In practice, commercial banks may request a formal board resolution, source-of-funds evidence, and confirmation of beneficial ownership before effecting large outbound profit transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender requirement exists in Cyprus. Exporters are not obligated to convert foreign currency proceeds into EUR or to repatriate proceeds within a statutory timeline. Foreign currency proceeds may be retained in external or domestic foreign currency accounts. All physical and financial flows are subject to EU and national statistics/reporting obligations under the IMF Balance of Payments reporting framework and the Central Bank of Cyprus administrative orders. For transactions exceeding certain thresholds, banks require documentary evidence (sales contracts, invoices, customs declarations, or transport documents) for the purpose of completing BOP/CPC reporting, but no conversion ratio or surrender timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for a high-risk nutraceuticals e-commerce entity. Cyprus-registered EMIs and EU-authorised electronic money institutions (e.g., Revolut Business, N26 Business, Mistertango) may provide multi-currency IBANs and API-driven payment rails. However, the high-risk pharmaceutical/dietary supplement vertical triggers enhanced due diligence by PSPs and acquirers. Most mainstream EMIs either restrict the sector outright or impose monthly volume caps, mandatory prescriptive drug-license checks, and strict notification of product-class approvals. Legacy-type EMIs incorporated in Cyprus are not authorised to offer credit, and their available clearing rails are limited to SEPA and internal wallet networks. The entity should maintain a tier-1 commercial bank as the primary settlement anchor and use EMIs only as secondary receiving rails after full documentation disclosure and merchant-contract approval. Offshore EMIs with no Cyprus/EU passporting rights are not legally viable for serving a Cyprus-resident company without breaching the EU PSD2 / Cyprus Electronic Money Law regime.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound subscription or shareholder loan capital must be wired to the entity's Euro-denominated corporate account through SWIFT MT103 from the investing entity's bank. Standard EU/ISO 20022 purpose codes are used: for equity injection, code 'COST' (capital contribution) or 'MALA' for convertible loans; for non-resident investment under EU capital freedom, code 'CIR' is not appropriate. More precise applications use purpose code 'G2' on the beneficiary side where required by international banking customs. The bank will demand a board resolution authorising share issuance or debt financing, a notarised declaration of the ultimate source of funds, and a capital reduction/repayment plan for loans (if applicable). All inbound payments are screened against EU sanctions lists and negative news; amounts above EUR 10,000 automatically trigger enhanced due diligence. The compliance verification bundle comprises: subscription agreement, bank statement of the remitter, board minutes, and latest audited balance sheet. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Cyprus does not impose a domestic dividend withholding tax on distributions by a Cypriot private limited company to non-resident shareholders; dividends received by non-domiciled individuals and foreign corporate investors are also exempt from the Special Defence Contribution (SDC).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The IR1 covering each calendar tax year must be filed within fifteen months from the end of the tax year (for example, for the tax year ended 31 December 2023, the statutory deadline is 31 March 2025); provisional tax installments are payable earlier during the tax year, generally by 31 July and 31 December, with any balancing payment due at the filing deadline.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Corporate identification Legal name, Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN), Company Registration Number (HE77), VAT number (if registered), business activity code (NACE/ISIC), and fiscal year-end date. A Cyprus branch of a foreign enterprise must obtain a Cyprus TIN and register with the Tax Department under the branch registration certificate; report the branch as the taxpayer while identifying the foreign parent as the legal owner. Late filing of the IR1 exposes the taxpayer to a best-of-judgment assessment by the Tax Commissioner, a 10% surcharge on the assessed tax, default interest at 1.75% per month (21% per annum) from the statutory due date, and fixed administrative penalties of approximately EUR 100 to EUR 300 per delayed return; continued non-filing can trigger seizure of bank accounts, enforcement against assets, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion.
Box 3 - Gross business revenue Total turnover attributable to the Cyprus entity's online retail activity in dietary supplements and nutraceuticals, converted into EUR; reconciliation with credit-card merchant statements and third-party e-commerce platforms. Include only Cyprus-attributable turnover when the taxpayer is a non-resident; turnover processed through foreign payment gateways must be fully declared regardless of where the payee's bank account is held. Late filing of the IR1 exposes the taxpayer to a best-of-judgment assessment by the Tax Commissioner, a 10% surcharge on the assessed tax, default interest at 1.75% per month (21% per annum) from the statutory due date, and fixed administrative penalties of approximately EUR 100 to EUR 300 per delayed return; continued non-filing can trigger seizure of bank accounts, enforcement against assets, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR; Local File applies to any category of controlled transaction (goods, services, intangibles, financing) whose aggregate annual value exceeds EUR 750,000; a Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000; small and medium enterprises (fewer than 50 employees with annual turnover or balance-sheet total not exceeding EUR 10,000,000) are exempt unless the transaction involves a covered high-risk arrangement.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not adopt a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations code such as those of the UAE or BVI; however, a de facto substance doctrine operates through (i) the central management-and-control test for corporate residence; (ii) the OECD transfer-pricing paradigm requiring the people functions and real risk to be located in the entity claiming profits; (iii) the Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD), including controlled foreign company rules requiring genuine economic activity in Cyprus; (iv) the Principal Purpose Test and Limitation on Benefits clauses in double tax treaties; and (v) eligibility conditions for the Notional Interest Deduction and IP-box which presuppose an actual Cyprus business. A Cyprus retail vehicle reselling pharma supplements online must prove substance through board meetings held in Cyprus, decision-making on merchant contracts and payment routing, local personnel, a local business bank account, and records maintained in Cyprus; failure to demonstrate such substance leads to disregarded entity treatment, denial of treaty benefits, and CFC liability in the parent state.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent under-declaration

Statutory sanction: Cyprus tax law (Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law 4/1978, Section 12) punishes fraudulent evasion with fines of up to EUR 20,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; the tax authority additionally imposes a 10% late-payment penalty, statutory interest at 1.75% per month, and seizure of bank accounts and assets; a criminal recovery order can be issued against the evader's property.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false returns or directed the concealment of merchant revenue are jointly and severally liable toward the unpaid tax; the Tax Commissioner may additionally order a travel ban and departure prohibition order, freeze the personal bank accounts of the director, and institute company-law disqualification proceedings; imprisonment of the responsible director can apply concurrently with the corporate fine.

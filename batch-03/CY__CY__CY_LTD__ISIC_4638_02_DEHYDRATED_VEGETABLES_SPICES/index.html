Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up share capital or escrow requirement for a Cyprus private limited company. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας); electronic filing and VAT administration are conducted through the ARIADNE e-portal (https://ariadne.mof.gov.cy) and the Cypriot Tax Portal (https://www.mof.gov.cy/taxportaltc).

E-government filing portal

A private limited liability company is first incorporated with the Department of Registrar of Companies, obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation under HE77 registration. With the certificate, articles of association, registered office address, and directors/shareholders evidence, the company applies to the Cyprus Tax Department for a Tax Identification Code (TIC). For VAT purposes, the company submits Form VAT-1 (or equivalent ARIADNE online application) supported by the certificate of incorporation, proof of registered office, identity documents of officers, and a declaration of taxable activity. Compulsory VAT registration is triggered when taxable turnover exceeds the statutory threshold; immediate voluntary registration is recommended for an export-oriented trading company so that input VAT on procurement and logistics can be reclaimed in the same period as zero-rated export turnover.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Cyprus-resident private limited company is a separate legal taxpayer in its own right, neither fiscally transparent nor eligible for pass-through treatment. The company is taxed at the corporate income tax rate of 12.5% on worldwide income, subject to statutory exemptions such as the dividend participation exemption and foreign permanent establishment exemption. Cyprus applies a unitary national tax system with no sub-state corporate tax distinctions; however, a company managed and controlled outside Cyprus may be treated as non-resident and taxed only on Cyprus-source income.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime and does not permit limited liability companies to be treated as partnerships. The available statutory elections relevant to a Cypriot trading company include the Notional Interest Deduction election on new equity, the Intellectual Property Box election, and the VAT group election under which two or more related Cypriot resident companies may be treated as a single taxable person. A company may also elect to use the simplified VAT cash accounting scheme and to voluntarily register for VAT before the compulsory threshold is exceeded; all such elections are made through the Tax Department in the prescribed form and are generally irrevocable for a specified period.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 annual taxable turnover for a resident taxable person supplying goods or services in Cyprus; for non-established foreign entities making taxable supplies in Cyprus there is no general threshold and registration may be required at the first taxable supply, except where the reverse charge or distance sales rules apply; an exporter of dehydrated vegetables and spices is advised to obtain voluntary VAT registration immediately to preserve the right to deduct input VAT against zero-rated export turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available to a VAT-registered Cypriot supplier on the direct export of goods physically transported from Cyprus out of the European Union to a destination outside the EU. In the case of bulk dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chilli pepper sold FOB from Cyprus to non-EU buyers, the sale is a supply of goods and qualifies for 0% VAT provided that ownership in the goods passes to the overseas customer, the goods are consigned to a non-EU destination, and the supplier holds contemporaneous documentary evidence sufficient to satisfy the Tax Department. Offshore services supplied to non-EU customers may be outside the scope of Cypriot VAT rather than zero-rated under the place-of-supply rules; for this goods-driven export model, zero-rating applies on the export sale itself.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document with the relevant customs office confirmation, or electronic Export Control System record) reflecting the Cypriot exporter and the non-EU destination
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note showing physical departure from Cyprus/EU and delivery to the destination country outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued in the name of the Cypriot supplier and the overseas buyer, clearly marked as zero-rated export supply
  • Certificate of origin or supplier's declaration where required by the destination customs authority
  • Bank telegraphic transfer, SWIFT confirmation, or Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing payment received from the overseas buyer
  • If goods are sold ex-works or through an intermediary, written contract evidence and freight instructions demonstrating that the goods left the EU at the risk of the overseas customer

Cyprus currently does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or State-certified e-invoicing for B2B/B2C commercial invoices. Invoicing must nevertheless comply with the EU VAT invoice rules: each invoice requires a sequential number, date, supplier name and VAT number, recipient VAT number where applicable, full description and quantity, taxable amount, VAT rate, VAT amount, and the statutory zero-rating reference for exports. VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and annual recapitulative statements must be submitted electronically through the ARIADNE e-portal. Periodic VAT returns use the standard nine-box format and are due by the 10th day of the second month following the tax period; export invoices must be retained digitally or on paper for at least ten years and must be capable of being transmitted to the Tax Department upon audit.

Cyprus Customs and the Tax Department conduct risk-based and post-clearance audits of export zero-rating claims. If the taxpayer cannot prove that goods physically left the EU or that the supply was made to a non-EU customer, the full 19% VAT is retroactively assessed on the export consideration, input VAT claimed against the export is clawed back, and the company loses any bad debt relief for the disputed periods. Late assessments attract interest at 9% per annum and an additional tax charge of 10% of the unpaid VAT; inaccurate returns or documents intended to mislead may lead to administrative fines of up to €500 per offence and, on indictment, to criminal penalties including imprisonment. Personal liability of directors and officers may arise where the zero-rating arrangement is found to be an abusive or artificial scheme lacking economic substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full eurozone convertibility since 1 January 2008; both current-account and capital-account flows are liberalised and cross-border transfers to third states are unrestricted pursuant to Article 63 TFEU. Profit remittance for a CY private limited company is unimpeded: outbound dividends, interest and royalties are exempt from Cyprus withholding tax provided the beneficiary is non-resident, is not a Cyprus tax resident, and does not maintain a permanent establishment in Cyprus. The only procedural hurdles are statutory balance-of-payments reporting to the Central Bank of Cyprus, declaration of physical cash carriage above EUR 10,000 under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672, and standard bank-level AML/KYC documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no surrender obligation and no statutory conversion timeline for export proceeds; the capital liberalisation legal framework in force since euro adoption abolished mandatory conversion of export receipts. Foreign-currency export collections may be retained in multi-currency accounts or converted at the bank's commercial FX rate. Statutory compliance is limited to balance-of-payments statistical reporting by the receiving bank to the Central Bank of Cyprus, retention of the underlying trade file (contract, invoice, bill of lading, customs declarations) for at least five years, and the EUR 10,000 cash declaration threshold under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) acting as the eurozone national central bank and the competent foreign-exchange administration under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the Cypriot Exchange Control liberalisation framework

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Cyprus-licensed e-money institutions under the EMI Law (Law 17(I)/2015, transposing Directive 2009/110/EC) and EU-passported foreign EMIs may operate payment-account and currency-conversion services without a banking licence, but EMIs cannot take deposits and depend on their sponsor bank for settlement. Non-EU offshore digital EMIs are viable only as a secondary rail because they require a sub-structure licence or a Cypriot credit institution partner; they are not recommended as the sole treasury route for documentary letter-of-credit transactions because they cannot issue or settle commercial LCs.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity or intercompany loans are injected via SWIFT MT103 wires from the UBO's or shareholder's home account into the Cyprus entity's euro operational account, coded under the ECB Balance-of-Payments classification E.1 (direct investment — equity) or F.4 (intercompany loans). The receiving bank requires a certified board resolution authorising the capital contribution, an apostilled share subscription agreement, updated MHE/company registry documentation, source-of-funds evidence (bank statements, tax returns, sale agreements), and the Central Bank of Cyprus quarterly BOP return is filed by the bank as the reporting agent.Trade settlements for bulk dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper export run on two priority rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for prepayment T/T or open-account terms, and (2) commercial documentary letters of credit under UCP600 for jurisdictionally risky or first-time buyers. Every disbursement instruction is accompanied by the export contract, proforma or commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, certificate of origin, and phytosanitary certificate where required; the bank performs UN/EU/OFAC sanctions screening and edge-of-network correspondent routing before debiting the euro account.Spot FX conversion (T+2) with a licensed Cypriot credit institution or a CBC/EU-regulated payment institution for daily trade settlements.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forward contracts with a licensed Cyprus bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement, where an underlying export receivable in USD, CNY or INR is evidenced.FX swaps (e.g., EUR/USD and USD/CNH) executed under ISDA and reported under EU EMIR, subject to clearing-threshold and margin rules.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permissible strictly as a hedge on non-deliverable or restricted-market currencies (e.g., INR or certain African corridors) where no deliverable interbank market exists, with genuine hedging need evidenced against the trade receivable.

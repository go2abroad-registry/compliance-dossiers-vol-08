Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

DIAN - Direccion de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (National Tax and Customs Directorate); official portal: www.dian.gov.co; electronic tax filings via MUISCA (Modelo Unico de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado); electronic invoicing clearance via SIE (Sistema Informatico Electronico) web services; single tax identification registry: Registro Unico Tributario (RUT).

E-government filing portal

The SAS is incorporated before the local Camara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce). The issued certificate of existence and legal representation (certificado de existencia y representacion legal), together with the RUT application form, notarized bylaws, and the legal representative's identification, is filed with DIAN. DIAN assigns the NIT (Numero de Identificacion Tributaria) and activates the RUT for income tax (impuesto de renta), VAT (responsable de IVA), and national consumption tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Colombian resident commercial company (sociedad nacional) taxed on worldwide income at the 35% corporate rate; SAS is not a pass-through; dividends to non-residents are subject to 10% withholding; foreign entities and branches are taxed only on Colombian-source income; film-licensing royalties sourced from non-resident payers are treated as foreign-source service export earnings; no tax-exempt free-zone designation applies unless a specific Zona Franca authorization is separately procured.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists under Colombian law; entity classification is mandatory under the Codigo de Comercio and Estatuto Tributario (E.T.). Available elections relevant to a film-licensing SAS: (i) voluntary adhesion to the SIMPLE unified tax regime when gross income does not exceed 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 174.3 million for 2025); (ii) optional consolidated tax-group treatment (consolidacion fiscal); (iii) election of cinematography incentives under Ley 1556/2012 (35% tax credit for investors and exempt income for film producers) subject to Stage 4 compliance; (iv) filing of advance pricing agreements (APAs) for cross-border royalty arrangements; (v) hybrid mismatch neutrality rules under Ley 2010/2019 disallowing deductions where counterparty tax classification differs, without a unilateral election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold applies to legal entities: any corporate actor performing VAT-taxable supplies in Colombia must register as a 'responsable del IVA' from day one, irrespective of volume. Natural persons not considered merchants are 'no responsables' only up to 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 174,296,500 in 2025). Non-resident digital service providers must register when taxable sales to non-responsible Colombian individuals exceed 60,000 UVT (approx. COP 2,987,940,000 in 2025), with voluntary early registration permitted. A Colombian SAS engaged in independent film licensing is mandatorily registered for VAT from inception.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of services is excluded from VAT (0% with full credit for input VAT) under E.T. Article 481(2) and DUR 1625 Article 1.3.1.23.5. The service must be rendered to a non-resident foreign entity or individual having no domicile or permanent establishment in Colombia, the service must be used or exploited exclusively abroad, and the provider must issue the electronic invoice with the 'Exportacion de servicios' exclusion and file the informative export declaration. International film-licensing royalty remittances from non-resident distributors qualify when the licensed exhibition territory and the exploitation benefit are located entirely outside Colombia. Licensing to a Colombian resident or for local theatrical/digital exploitation in Colombia is subject to 19% VAT unless the statutory exclusion for a definitive copyright assignment (cesion definitiva de derechos de autor) applies.

  • Formulario 710 Declaracion Informativa de Exportacion de Servicios (DIAN/MUISCA filing clearance for zero-rating)
  • Factura Electronica de Venta with CUFE digital stamp annotated 'Excluido - Exportacion de Servicios' (or DEX Declaracion de Exportacion when tangible media is shipped)
  • Written film license agreement evidencing foreign beneficiary and territorial scope, accompanied by the counterparty's certificate of tax residence
  • Declaracion de Cambio por Exportaciones de Servicios (Inward Remittance Certificate) issued by an authorized foreign-exchange intermediary in the Banco de la Republica market
  • Proof of actual remittance of royalties, including SWIFT MT103 payment evidence or Letter of Credit documentation

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturacion Electronica de Venta) with real-time DIAN validation: invoices are issued through an authorized technology provider or DIAN's free web service, transmitted via JSON/API web services (SIE) under Resolution 000165/2023, and must carry the unique CUFE (Codigo Unico de Facturacion Electronica) or CUDS (Codigo Unico de Documento Soporte) as a de facto digital stamp and QR code. Export transactions require the invoice annotation 'Excluido - Exportacion de Servicios', the informative export declaration (Declaracion Informativa de Exportacion de Servicios) or DEX customs declaration for physical media, electronic transmission to DIAN within 48 hours of issuance (practical real-time clearance), the electronic IVA ledger (libro de IVA), and the annual exogena information return.

DIAN verifies the full documentary chain (contract, e-invoice, informative export declaration, FX surrender). Failure to substantiate zero-rating results in assessment of 19% VAT on gross royalties plus moratory interest, Sanción por Inexactitud of 100%-200% of the omitted VAT (E.T. Art. 648), a 20% penalty for false or non-filed informative declarations (E.T. Art. 643), and, for customs fraud, penalties up to 200% of the goods' value under Ley 1762/2015. Improper VAT refund claims (compensacion indebida de saldos a favor) are clawed back with a sanction up to 100% of the amount (E.T. Art. 670), legal representatives may face criminal liability for tax evasion with imprisonment of 6 to 12 years, the RUT may be suspended or reactivated, and assets may be embargoed; the statute of limitations for DIAN assessments is generally 2 to 5 years, extended to 10 years for unregistered obligations or criminal conduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is fully convertible for current-account operations (royalties, dividends, services, profit remittances) provided the flows are channelled through an authorized intermediary of the exchange market (intermediario del mercado cambiario, IMC) and supported by an electronic exchange declaration. Capital-account traffic is free from quantitative ceilings but requires registration of the foreign investment with Banco de la República (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales, R-II) to secure lawful access to the exchange market for future repatriation. Dividend and capital remittances are legally remittable once the S.A.S. is tax current, transfer-pricing documentation is in place for related-party licensing, and the applicable non-resident dividend withholding (generally 10% absent treaty relief) has been settled. Remittance hurdles are documentary, not substantive, provided the R-II record is updated and each wire carries the correct FX purpose code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Banco de la República External Resolution No. 8 of 2000 and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, service-export proceeds, including film copyright licensing and royalty income, must be repatriated and channelled exclusively through an IMC. The statutory collection period for service exports is a maximum of six months from accrual of the invoice; the collected foreign currency must then be surrendered and converted in full (100% of the receipt) at the prevailing market TRM rate, with the IMC filing the corresponding Declaración de Cambio. Late reintegration triggers daily surcharges and fines imposed by Banco de la República; retention of export proceeds abroad is permitted only with express authorization of Banco de la República.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Bogotá, Colombia), exercising monetary and exchange-control authority; jointly enforced with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and the National Tax and Customs Directorate (DIAN).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign capital must enter via the regulated exchange market: (i) the sender issues a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent to the IMC correspondent account (usually via New York) with the mandatory reference line Foreign Direct Investment, R-II registration number, and the Colombian company RUT; (ii) the IMC receives the USD or EUR funds and converts them to COP at the day TRM rate; (iii) the bank files the electronic Declaración de Cambio with Banco de la República and returns the authentication receipt; (iv) the S.A.S. must update the R-II registration within the regulatory window and provide the updated certificate before any outbound flow is processed. Practical verification triggers: board resolution approving the capital injection, matched invoice, RUT, power-of-attorney matrix, and the exact exchange-declaration sub-code agreed with the IMC prior to transfer.Outgoing disbursements for acquisition of film prints, DCP encoding, dubbing, subtitling, and licensing fees are cleared either by (i) a direct SWIFT MT103 issued by the IMC with the exchange declaration registered against the underlying invoice or service contract, or (ii) a commercial letter of credit (UCP 600) opened by the IMC where the supplier requires documentary protection for physical deliveries. Each disbursement must be linked to a valid exchange-declaration purpose code for royalties or copyright licensing and must be pre-registered when payment precedes the service (advance royalty or prepaid service imports). Withholding taxes on foreign royalties or technical fees, VAT, and transfer-pricing reporting obligations must be settled before the bank processes the remittance; otherwise the IMC will block the transfer.Forward FX contracts (COP/USD and COP/EUR) with an SFC-licensed IMC, subject to an underlying current-account or investment exposure test.Nondeliverable forwards (NDFs) on the COP/USD TRM with OECD-resident investment-grade banks, used by non-resident investors to hedge dividend streams and capital repatriation without local delivery.Cross-currency swaps (COP/USD) executed with an IMC, matching the principal amount of the registered foreign investment, with periodic interest flows and final principal exchange.Plain vanilla COP/USD FX options (both calls and puts) purchased from a local IMC to cap worst-case conversion rates on known licensing inflows.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Non-Colombian fintech EMIs and digital wallets (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Mercury) are not licensed by the SFC and cannot file Banco de la República exchange declarations, issue authenticated TRM conversions, or provide compliant channeling evidence. They cannot serve as the primary receipts or disbursements rail for a Colombian S.A.S.; maximum permissible use is as a secondary auxiliary collection tool for small invoiced amounts, provided the funds are subsequently transferred to the IMC and properly declared.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% for taxable years 2022 and thereafter; assessed on the worldwide net income of Colombian tax-resident companies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends paid by the Colombian SAS to non-resident shareholders are subject to a final withholding tax of 7.5% when the profits distributed have already been taxed at the 35% corporate income tax rate. If the distribution is made out of profits that were not taxed at the entity level, the withholding tax rate is 35%. Treaty rates may reduce these rates.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax year ends on 31 December. DIAN issues an annual calendar establishing a filing window, normally between July and September of the following year. The specific due date is assigned by the last digit of the taxpayer's NIT; for most companies the deadline falls in August of the following year. The return must be filed and the tax balance paid by the assigned deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File, Master File, and annual Transfer Pricing Information Form (Formulario 160) are required when the Colombian SAS's gross income or net worth exceeded the annual UVT threshold established by DIAN, approximately COP 10,650 million for fiscal year 2024 (250,000 UVT), or when cross-border related-party transactions exceeded 150,000 UVT. Even below the threshold, the SAS must apply the arm's length principle and maintain contemporaneous supporting evidence. Independent film-licensing entities with material related-party royalty flows should assume the obligation applies.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a dedicated offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. However, the Colombian Tax Code's general anti-abuse rule (Article 869-2) permits DIAN to disregard or recharacterize transactions or structures that lack economic substance, business purpose, or commercial rationale. For treaty purposes, the OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI) and specific limitation-of-benefits clauses require the Colombian SAS to demonstrate substantive activity, such as actual film rights acquisition, active licensing negotiations, management control, and local personnel, before accessing reduced treaty rates.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of up to 200% of the tax deficiency plus default interest; criminal tax evasion under Law 1762/2015 carries imprisonment of 66 to 120 months and fines up to 1,500 statutory minimum monthly wages. DIAN may also order seizure of bank accounts and disqualification from contracting with the State.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who knowingly prepare or authorize fraudulent tax returns are personally liable for criminal sanctions, civil restitution, and asset forfeiture. DIAN can impose travel bans and request an arrest warrant precisely to secure collection of the assessed tax.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: DIAN adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 200% of the under-declared tax caused by the adjustment, and late-filing penalties for omitted documentation. The underpayment must be paid with interest; repeated violations may be classified as fraud.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and chief financial officer may be jointly and severally liable for resulting tax liabilities if they signed the return or approved the intercompany pricing knowing it was artificial. Criminal proceedings may follow, with travel restrictions and freezing of personal assets.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Applying Article 869-2 GAAR, DIAN may disregard the SAS's separate tax personality, reclassify income at the foreign shareholder level, deny treaty benefits, and impose penalties of 100%–200% of the tax avoided plus interest. Financial transactions through the shell may also be reported as potential money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors who structure or maintain a sham Colombian entity may be criminally prosecuted for aiding tax evasion, held personally for unpaid corporate taxes, and subjected to asset embargoes, travel bans, and imprisonment, because Colombian courts may pierce the corporate veil when the company is used as a fraud instrument.
Withholding and Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: The Colombian SAS, as withholding agent, is personally liable for the uncollected tax, plus a fine equal to 100% of the withheld amount not retained and default interest. Failure to file the corresponding withholding informative returns also results in fixed penalties per form; repeated defaults can trigger license revocations for the SAS.

Director personal liability: The president and legal representatives are jointly liable for the corporation's withholding obligations and may face criminal sanctions for misappropriation of tax funds. DIAN can issue a travel restriction order under Article 768-1 and ultimately request extradition if the director is abroad.
AML and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Articles 323 and 324 of the Colombian Criminal Code carries imprisonment of 10 to 30 years and fines of up to 1,000 statutory minimum monthly wages. The proceeds and related assets may be forfeited; the SAS may face corporate criminal liability under Law 1904/2018.

Director personal liability: Each officer, compliance officer, or director who participates in, facilitates, or omits to report suspicious transactions is individually criminally liable. Personal assets may be confiscated, travel bans may be imposed, and the corporate veil will be pierced to recover laundered funds.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department (Tmima Forologias), Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus, operating the ARIADNE Tax Portal (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy); VAT administration is exercised through the VAT Service division of the Tax Department; customs export controls are handled by the Customs and Excise Department via the THUNDER electronic declaration system.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the Private Limited Company (HE77) with the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DRCOR) and obtain the company registration number. 2. The Tax Department automatically issues an 8-digit Tax Identification Code (TIC) upon incorporation notification; a non-resident entity without a Cyprus incorporation must file Form TD2001 with the District Tax Office. 3. Register for VAT under Value Added Tax Law N.95(I)/2000 by submitting Form VAT 101 via the ARIADNE portal when taxable turnover reaches EUR 15,600 in any 12 consecutive months, or on a voluntary basis for a refund claim. 4. Obtain an EORI (Economic Operators Registration and Identification) number from the Customs and Excise Department to lodge export declarations electronically. 5. Register the employer with the Social Insurance Services (SIS). TIC, VAT number (format CY+8 digits+1 letter) and EORI must be aligned in the taxpayer's master file before the first export shipment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a check-the-box election; a Private Limited Company (Ltd) is opaque and subject to 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income, with territorial exemptions for foreign dividends and capital gains. Available statutory elections: (i) VAT group registration election under Article 15 of N.95(I)/2000 allowing jointly liable group VAT accounting; (ii) group relief election for transfer of tax losses among companies held at 75% or more (Income Tax Law N.118(I)/2002, Articles 19-25); (iii) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital; (iv) IP Box election granting 80% deduction on qualifying intangible income (effective 2.5% rate); (v) tonnage tax election (shipping only, not applicable); (vi) Pillar Two Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax (15% QDMTT) registration and filing election for MNE groups within scope.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for supplies of goods and services; EUR 10,251.61 for intra-Community acquisitions; EUR 35,000 for distance sales into Cyprus. Mandatory registration applies within 30 days of the end of the month in which the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% applies to the supply of goods, including canned boba tapioca pearls, dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union by the supplier or a third party acting on the supplier's account (Article 5(1)(a), Cyprus VAT Law N.95(I)/2000). Proof that the goods physically left the EU within 90 days of supply is mandatory. For intra-EU customers, zero-rating requires a valid EU VAT registration number of the buyer and an EC Sales List filing. Related offshore services (warehousing, palletizing, quality control) supplied to a non-EU buyer qualify for zero-rating if directly linked to the exported goods and the recipient is established outside the EU. Input VAT on packaging, sterilization, canning and freight is fully recoverable through the periodic VAT return when the export zero-rating is substantiated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD) electronically validated and stamped by the Customs and Excise Department through the THUNDER system
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) or CMR consignment note, signed and dated, proving dispatch to a destination outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or TT credit advice) evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in a Cyprus bank account in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing HS 1903.00 (tapioca preparations) or HS 2106.90 (flavored boba pearls), net/gross weight and destination country
  • Phytosanitary certificate, health or halal certificate where required by the importing country's customs authority

No real-time B2B fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate is in force. Cyprus applies EU Directive 2014/55/EU e-invoicing (PEPPOL format) for supplier-to-government (B2G) invoices only, through the national e-invoicing platform. VAT returns must be filed electronically quarterly (monthly for large taxpayers) via the ARIADNE portal; EC Sales Lists (VIES) are filed monthly/quarterly for intra-Community supplies. Customs export declarations are lodged electronically in the THUNDER system with automatic validation by the Customs and Excise Department; no separate live-fiscal stamping API is currently required.

The Tax Department may raise retrospective assessments within 6 years (extended to 10 years for fraudulent intent) to claw back input VAT recovered on export zero-rating supported by fabricated transport documents, false B/Ls or circular banking flows. Under N.95(I)/2000 Article 48, fraudulent export zero-rating is a criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to EUR 5,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil consequences include additional tax up to 100% of the understated VAT, monthly interest of 1% on unpaid VAT, permanent forfeiture of the input VAT credit, and cancellation of the EORI number. Non-repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days without bona fide commercial or exchange-control justification triggers a presumption that the goods were not exported, leading to reversal of the 0% rate and imposition of 19% VAT plus penalties and retrospective clawbacks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared and filed when the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions exceeds EUR 5,000,000; a Master File is additionally required when the group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000 and the annual value of controlled transactions exceeds EUR 5,000,000. Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to Gulf jurisdictions, but administrative practice, the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD), and the OECD BEPS rules require that a Cyprus tax-resident company have real management and control, physical premises, appropriately qualified personnel, and substantive business decision-making in Cyprus. For a canned boba manufacturing entity, economic substance is evidenced by the aseptic production facility in Cyprus, production staff, packaging and sterilisation activities, engagement of local managers, board meetings in Cyprus, and the allocation of commercial risk and inventory to the Cyprus entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Assessment of omitted tax plus interest at the statutory rate; administrative penalty of 100% of the underpaid tax; criminal prosecution for fraudulent tax evasion with fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment of up to five years, together with seizure of assets and charging orders on bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or authorise fraudulent returns become personally liable for the underpaid tax, civil restitution, and criminal penalties. The Cyprus courts may issue travel bans, asset freezing orders, and imprisonment from two to five years; the corporate veil may be pierced where the company is an instrumentality of fraud.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of the favourable tax treatment and tax residency certificate; retroactive rejection of treaty benefits; mandatory tax reassessment at effective rates of the foreign jurisdiction or at Cyprus corporate income tax as re-allocated profit; penalty of up to 50% of the additional charge; possible strike-off of the company for non-viability.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax where they knowingly caused the company to trade without legitimate economic substance. Cyprus insolvency law imposes personal liability for wrongful trading, and criminal courts may issue disqualification orders, travel restrictions, and civil restitution orders against directors who abuse the separate legal personality doctrine.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's-length profit; additional corporation tax at 12.5% on the adjustment; interest from the due date; administrative penalty of 10%–20% of the upward adjustment or a fixed fine of up to EUR 20,000 for failure to prepare documentation; in egregious cases, anti-avoidance penalties of up to 50% of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign an inaccurate IR4 accompanied by false TP documentation may be jointly liable for the tax adjustment and penalties. Personal civil liability may arise under Cyprus company law for misfeasance and breach of fiduciary duty, and criminal prosecution for making a false return can result in disqualification as a director and imprisonment.
Anti-Money Laundering / Sanctions and Trade Financing Abuse

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law of up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10 percent of annual turnover; seizure of the proceeds of crime; criminal imprisonment of up to five years; revocation of the required statutory manufacturing license and denial of trade financing approvals from Cypriot banks.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable if they knowingly participate in money laundering or wilfully blind conduct in structuring trade transactions. The court may impose civil restitution, asset confiscation, travel bans, and imprisonment up to five years; directors may also face separate civil suits by creditors and regulatory debarment.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio del domicilio social (Registro Mercantil), with subsequent registration before the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) for tax purposes.

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 (Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada), Articles 1, 3-5, 11, 12, 16, and 46; Código de Comercio, Articles 110-119, 129-131, and 142.

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (Private Instrument of Incorporation) registered before the Chamber of Commerce; a notarial Escritura Pública is required if real property or specialized in-kind contributions are part of the share capital.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum subscribed or paid-up capital is required by Law 1258 of 2008. Shareholders determine the capital amount in the bylaws. If contributions are in cash, funds must be received and acknowledged by the legal representative; no escrow account is mandatory, but unpaid subscribed capital must be fully paid within two years from incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Before commencing operations, the premises must obtain a fire-safety certificate from the Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos, verify structural load-bearing conditions through an engineer's certificate for office occupancy, and comply with workplace safety and health obligations under Resolución 0312 de 2019, including evacuation plans, emergency brigades, and registration with a licensed Administradora de Riesgos Laborales (ARL).

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum subscribed or paid-up capital is required by Law 1258 of 2008. Shareholders determine the capital amount in the bylaws. If contributions are in cash, funds must be received and acknowledged by the legal representative; no escrow account is mandatory, but unpaid subscribed capital must be fully paid within two years from incorporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); transactional portal: MUISCA via www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must (1) appoint a Colombian legal representative or attorney-in-fact; (2) obtain or update the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by filing the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) before DIAN; (3) register the precise economic activities, responsible VAT status, and e-invoicing class; (4) maintain the RUT data in force for all customs, foreign-exchange and tax filings. The Colombian S.A.S. itself must complete the same RUT/NIT procedure upon incorporation before starting taxable operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Colombian tax law. A qualifying small taxpayer may elect the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE), which replaces income tax, national consumption tax, and VAT in a unified regime, subject to gross-income ceilings and excluded activities; otherwise the S.A.S. remains an ordinary income-tax taxpayer at entity level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general COP threshold: VAT registration through RUT is compulsory before engaging in taxable supplies. For the optional SIMPLE regime, the prior-year gross ordinary income ceiling is approximately COP 3,765,000,000 (80,000 UVT for 2024); taxpayers above that ceiling must remain in the ordinary VAT/income regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for 0% VAT when the customs export declaration is lodged and the goods physically leave Colombian customs territory. Services qualify as 'exported services' when rendered in Colombia but economically used or exploited abroad for the direct benefit of a foreign beneficiary and payment is received from a non-Colombian source; the supporting evidence must be available before claiming the zero rate on the VAT return.

  • Formulario de Declaración de Exportación (DEX)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / multimodal transport document
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matched to the DEX
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Canalización / Reintegro de Divisas) issued by an authorized Colombian foreign-exchange intermediary

VAT-responsible taxpayers must use DIAN-registered invoicing software or API connectors, transmit electronic invoices in XML/UBL format to DIAN's validation service, obtain and embed the unique generation code (CUFE/CUNE) and QR, and report invoice events including acceptation, rejection and credit/debit notes. Transmission is required in near-real-time before the invoice is considered valid for VAT and customs purposes.

DIAN cross-checks zero-rated export sales against customs (DEX), banking (foreign-exchange channeling) and VAT-return databases. If the zero rate is applied without substantiated export documentation or timely FX repatriation, DIAN may reclassify the revenue as taxable domestic supply and assess 19% IVA, plus default interest, price-level adjustment, and an inaccuracy penalty that can approximate 100-200% of the tax omitted. Intentional document falsification or reliance on shell foreign beneficiaries is criminally punishable and triggers retrospective clawback of VAT refunds under the statutory audit period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) operates within a managed floating exchange-rate regime. Current account transactions, including cross-border legal services, are freely convertible through the authorized foreign-exchange market, subject to documentary declarations and the availability of Form 52 (Declaración de Cambio). Capital account transactions are restricted only by registration and reporting gates: foreign direct investment into a Colombian S.A.S. must be registered with Banco de la República through the receiving local bank to secure subsequent dividend repatriation and capital-reduction remittances. Profits and dividends are remittable abroad after local tax settlement and presentation of the corresponding foreign-exchange declaration. There is no statutory quantitative limit on profit remittance, but the repatriation must match the net after-tax amount and be supported by an annual shareholders' meeting resolution and DIAN tax-attestation where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For services exports such as cross-border litigation and arbitration fees, the foreign currency inflow must be canalizado through a Colombian authorized bank within six months from the date of the invoice, service completion, or collection milestone. Banco de la República's Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83 requires the export proceeds to be fully channeled through the Mercado Cambiario; the mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% for services exports unless the exporter has received a specific foreign-currency account authorization to maintain a working balance. Exporters of goods may retain up to 20% of the proceeds in foreign-currency accounts; services exporters generally must negotiate the full collected amount into COP. Late canalización triggers graduated fines and automatic adjustments to the tax cost base.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) in coordination with the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) and the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Tourism, which jointly enforce Colombia's Regime of International Foreign Exchange (Mercado Cambiario).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for direct non-resident EMI onboarding. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer) cannot offer Colombian IOLTA/trust-account capabilities because Colombian regulation reserves deposit-taking of client funds to licensed credit institutions and to Sociedades Especializadas en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos (SEDPEs) supervised by the Financial Superintendence of Colombia. Offshore EMIs may be used only as pre-funding front-end collection rails provided the ultimate settlement lands in a local bank account; they are not a legally compliant custody or escrow vehicle for cross-border litigation proceeds. A medium-viability local workaround is partnering with a certified SEDPE or licensed remittance company that maintains a local nostro/trust ledger.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a private limited company. Authorized capital may be nominal, commonly EUR 1,000 divided into ordinary shares. No escrow account or capital deposit certificate is required at incorporation. Subscribers may take up partly paid shares, with the unpaid balance recorded in the Statement of Capital as a debt payable on call.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the Cyprus Fire Service after inspection of exits, extinguishers, alarm systems, and storage of flammable materials; structural load inspection and building-use approval from the District Administration confirming the premises may be used for light industrial printing; occupational health and workplace clearance from the Department of Labour Inspection covering machine guarding, dust/ventilation control, and safe chemical storage.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance – Anexartisias 67, Nicosia; online portal: Tax Portal (taxportal.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity seeking to establish a Cyprus Private Limited Company (HE77) must apply through the Registrar of Companies for incorporation, obtain a Company Registration Number (CRN), and submit Form T.D. 2001 for Tax Registration to the District Tax Office. The VAT authority then issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and, where required, a VAT registration number (VAT ID) under the Cyprus VAT Law. A local registered agent must be appointed and a Cyprus address maintained for notification purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity seeking to establish a Cyprus Private Limited Company (HE77) must apply through the Registrar of Companies for incorporation, obtain a Company Registration Number (CRN), and submit Form T.D. 2001 for Tax Registration to the District Tax Office. The VAT authority then issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and, where required, a VAT registration number (VAT ID) under the Cyprus VAT Law. A local registered agent must be appointed and a Cyprus address maintained for notification purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus applies a uniform corporate income tax rate of 12.5% on a worldwide basis for tax-resident companies. No general check-the-box election exists; however, subject to substance and anti-avoidance rules, a Cyprus company may elect to be treated as a transparent partnership for tax purposes if it is formed as a partnership or a limited liability partnership. Hybrid mismatch rules under the ATAD (EU Directive 2017/952) apply, restricting deduction/non-inclusion outcomes and denying double-dip structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 for taxable supplies during any 12-month period; EUR 11,200 for intra-Community acquisitions of goods

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Cyprus to non-EU destinations or delivered to another EU member state for export qualify for 0% VAT, provided that proof of export is retained and the goods leave the EU within statutory deadlines. Offshore services to non-EU customers, including digital publishing services for collectible trading cards, qualify for zero-rating only where the place of supply rules under Article 59 of the VAT Directive are properly applied, considering the customer's business status and location. Intangible card-related services are taxable at 0% where the effective use and enjoyment is outside the EU and documentary evidence establishes receipt by a non-EU taxable person.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/C88) bearing the certification stamp of the Customs Authority of the Republic of Cyprus
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill showing exporter as the Cyprus entity and overseas consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a licensed Cyprus bank evidencing settlement of export proceeds within the statutory repatriation window

Cyprus has introduced mandatory electronic invoicing (e-invoicing) for B2B transactions effective progressively from 2024, using the national PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format and a national clearing platform. Real-time fiscal clearance and digital stamping are not yet uniformly required, but e-invoicing must be transmitted to the Tax Department through certified ERP interoperability APIs. All invoices exceeding EUR 20,000 must carry a cryptographic QR code and be validated within the central Eurydokia repository.

Export zero-rating is subject to intensive Customs and Tax Department audit. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims trigger the default VAT rate of 19% plus accrued interest, civil penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded, disqualification of the entity from zero-rating schemes, and criminal sanctions including imprisonment of up to five years for knowingly making false declarations. Retrospective clawbacks extend to all unsubstantiated supplies within the preceding six years under Article 45 of the Cyprus VAT Law and EU Council Regulation (EU) No 904/2010 exchange of information procedures.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully liberalized current and capital accounts under EU Treaty provisions (TFEU Articles 63-66) and Eurosystem law. The euro is freely convertible; profit remittances, dividend distributions, loan servicing, and equity repatriations by non-resident shareholders do not require any Central Bank approval. Cyprus's emergency capital controls (2013-2015) were fully lifted; no residual authorization, quota, or 'prior approval letter' regime exists today. Outbound dividends to non-residents are generally exempt from Cyprus withholding tax, subject to general anti-abuse, CFC, and substance safeguards. Foreign-currency accounts with Cyprus banks are permitted without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline because the euro is the domestic legal tender. Export receivables from trading card sales (EUR, USD, GBP, or other currencies) may be held indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts. Banks nonetheless require documentary support (invoices, shipping records, customs declarations, and payment references) for transaction monitoring under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law of 2007 (L.188(I)/2007) and relevant EU AML Directives.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Greek: Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating inside the European System of Central Banks and the Eurosystem; the European Central Bank holds final monetary and foreign reserves authority for the euro. The Central Bank of Cyprus also supervises credit institutions, EMIs, and payment institutions under the Business of Credit Institutions Law of 1997 and PSD2; CySEC separately licenses investment firms and FX brokers but has no currency-control mandate.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. EEA EMIs authorized under the E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and payment institutions under PSD2 (EU) 2015/2366 may passport into Cyprus, offering multi-currency IBANs and faster remote onboarding. Legal constraints: (i) safeguarding with an EU credit institution is mandatory; (ii) USD clearing depends on an EMI's correspondent banking network and is therefore operationally narrower than a direct Eurozone bank; (iii) EMIs routinely reject merchants with high third-party payouts or high refund velocity; collectible trading card e-commerce is low-risk but Stripe/Authorize.Net settlements must flow through an EU acquiring bank, making the EMI account a supplemental disbursement rail rather than a primary treasury account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Non-resident shareholders domiciled in an elevated-risk jurisdiction or with cryptocurrency-derived wealth

Defense evidence: Apostilled passports and proof of address; 12 months of personal and corporate bank statements from an established regulated bank; certified source-of-wealth narrative documenting accumulation of funds; audit trail from crypto exchange to fiat account with exchange statements and withdrawal confirmations; UBO declaration under the Cyprus Beneficial Owner Register.
High-volume e-commerce card sales with elevated chargeback risk and global refund exposure

Root cause: High-volume e-commerce card sales with elevated chargeback risk and global refund exposure

Defense evidence: Stripe and Authorize.Net merchant statements showing 12-month processing history and volume trend; chargeback ratio summary (target below 0.5%); published refund policy; signed distribution agreements for sealed product; evidence of card asset ownership or licensed IP for holographic and cold-foil printing.
Cross-border outbound payments to Asia-based security printing equipment and foil suppliers, exposing the account to customs-vulnerable trade flows

Defense evidence: Pro-forma invoices and purchase orders; executed supply contracts incorporating Incoterms 2020; shipping bills of lading and airway bills; customs import/export declarations; third-party goods-inspection certificates; supplier KYC pack including beneficial owner passports and business licenses.
Thin substance in Cyprus: virtual office, absent management, or reliance on foreign back-office assistance

Root cause: Thin substance in Cyprus: virtual office, absent management, or reliance on foreign back-office assistance

Defense evidence: Commercial lease agreement for a physical or co-working office in Cyprus; tax residence certificates for resident directors; employment contracts, social insurance filings, and payroll records for local personnel; board minutes documenting strategic decision-making performed in Cyprus; engagement letters with a licensed ACSP/fiduciary.
Lump-sum inbound capital injection with insufficient provenance and no apparent link to declared business value

Root cause: Lump-sum inbound capital injection with insufficient provenance and no apparent link to declared business value

Defense evidence: BOard resolution approving the share issuance; statutory return of allotment filed with the Department of Registrar of Companies; signed share certificates; UBO's verified bank statements evidencing the funds origin; written capital introduction declaration; contemporaneous valuation or business plan supporting the share price.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route via SEPA for EEA shareholder transfers or via SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA corridors. Protocol: (1) Board resolution approving the share subscription and allotment; (2) file the statutory return of allotments with the Cyprus Department of Registrar of Companies within one month; (3) remit funds to a clearly designated 'Share Capital' sub-account or label the payment reference 'Share subscription capital contribution, [Company Name], invoice/allotment [Date]'; (4) credit is verified against the signed share certificate and the capital contribution letter, not against future receivables; (5) no Central Bank exchange approval or prior approval letter is required; (6) register any UBO ownership change with the Cyprus Beneficial Owner Register and provide a post-injection beneficiary declaration to the bank within the contractually agreed KYC refresh cycle.Primary rail is SWIFT MT103 for cross-border supplier settlements, with full beneficiary bank details, invoice number, Incoterms, and goods description included in field 70/remittance information. For high-value security-foil and printing-equipment purchases, issue a commercial letter of credit at sight, preferably confirmed by a top-tier correspondent bank, with documents against payment to protect against supplier default in Asian manufacturing corridors. SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) is used for EEA suppliers within seconds. Staggered liquidity buffers per payment cycle: maintain 60 days of operational cash in EUR, convert USD receivables monthly to avoid gap exposure, and keep a dedicated USD clearing sub-account for US e-commerce settlements from Stripe and Authorize.Net. Banks may require underlying commercial contracts to release any payment above EUR 50,000.Outright FX Forward Contracts between EUR and USD or GBP, executed OTC on a bilateral master agreement; permitted without Central Bank of Cyprus authorization, but transactions are subject to EMIR reporting obligations to an authorized trade repository when entered with a licensed financial counterparty.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) - legally available in Cyprus only for currencies with convertibility restrictions in the underlying market; for freely convertible USD and GBP, deliverable forwards are the market-standard instrument and NDFs are not recommended.FX Swaps and Cross-Currency Swaps (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP) - permissible for hedging trade receivables and matching future supplier liabilities; requires ISDA/CSA collateral documentation where applicable and compliance with EMIR clearing thresholds for non-financial counterparties.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá acting as the commercial registry delegate integrated with the Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES); authoritative corporate supervision by the Superintendencia de Sociedades.

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 (Sociedades por Acciones Simplificadas, Articles 1, 4, 5, and 17); Código de Comercio (Decreto 410 de 1971) Articles 110-114; and Decreto 1074 de 2015 (regulatory commerce compilations), supplemented by Ley 2069 de 2020 for entrepreneurial facilitation.

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (private incorporation instrument), elevated to notarial escritura pública only when real property or public-instrument contributions are made; filed through the RUES registration platform with the Matrícula Mercantil application.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital; the share capital and the fixed value of shares are freely determined by the incorporator. Contributions may be cash or in-kind and may be deferred for a term not exceeding two (2) years. No mandatory escrow or blocked-account requirement exists; paid-in contributions are credited to the company's corporate bank account.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety and human-life protection certificate issued by the Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos; structural adequacy for the office occupancy under NSR-10; and registration in the Sistema de Gestión de Seguridad y Salud en el Trabajo (SG-SST) with the corresponding ARL once staff are engaged.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

DIAN - Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales; official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co (including Muisca electronic tax filing platform)

E-government filing portal

For a local S.A.S., the NIT is generated in the RUT registration process after Chamber of Commerce incorporation; foreign entities must also register through DIAN's Muisca portal. The procedure requires appointment of a local legal representative/agent, notarized and apostilled incorporation documents, power of attorney, proof of taxable nexus or representative office, and submission of the RUT form online. DIAN issues one unified 'Número de Identificación Tributaria' (NIT) used for income tax, IVA registration, customs and international trade.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a local S.A.S., the NIT is generated in the RUT registration process after Chamber of Commerce incorporation; foreign entities must also register through DIAN's Muisca portal. The procedure requires appointment of a local legal representative/agent, notarized and apostilled incorporation documents, power of attorney, proof of taxable nexus or representative office, and submission of the RUT form online. DIAN issues one unified 'Número de Identificación Tributaria' (NIT) used for income tax, IVA registration, customs and international trade.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists for a Colombian S.A.S.; classification is mandatory. Colombia applies OECD/Hybrid Mismatch rules rather than elective entity classification, and there is no domestic election to treat a S.A.S. as fiscally transparent. Optional mechanisms are limited to group consolidation (optional for groups qualifying under Law 1819/2016) and the simplified taxation regime for small business (not applicable to a licensed-vehicle SFO).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

COP 0 (no monetary threshold); any foreign or local juridical person performing taxable transactions in Colombia must complete RUT registration and obtain IVA status before invoicing. Simplified small-taxpayer status only affects invoicing, not the zero threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% IVA applies to exports of goods physically leaving Colombian customs territory, as well as to qualifying exports of services rendered from Colombia but used exclusively abroad by non-resident clients. For single-family office and investment custody services, the provider must evidence a contract with a foreign counterparty, beneficial use and value exclusively outside Colombia, and no relation to tangible goods delivered in Colombia. The exporter must present complete supporting documentation to DIAN and apply for refund of input IVA attributable to export sales.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) validated in DIAN's customs system, or service export declaration/electronic record where the 'export' is a service transaction.
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading or Airwaybill for goods; for offshore services, a signed contract with the foreign principal showing scope, fee, delivery, and exclusive use abroad.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a Colombian bank or foreign exchange intermediary evidencing collection and repatriation of export proceeds.
  • Certificates of supplier-to-exporter for zero-rated local input purchases used in the exported goods or services, if applicable.

All IVA-registered persons must issue electronic invoices through DIAN-authorized platforms (Facturación Electrónica de Venta). DIAN validates each XML invoice in near-real-time via HTTPS/API, assigning a unique CUFE/CUDS cryptographic code and publication status; no physical fiscal stamp is used. The invoice and all transaction events must be sent electronically to DIAN and delivered to recipients through approved channels.

DIAN conducts direct audits of every zero-rated export, looking for fake invoices, unfilled exemptions and absent FX repatriation. A clawback or disallowed credit may occur when the export declaration is invalid, documentation incomplete, or proceeds not repatriated by the 180-day term. The exporter will be assessed 19% IVA on the full export value, plus inflationary adjustment, default interest, and the inaccuracy penalty under Tax Code Art. 648 (up to 200% of the evaded tax). Falsified export documentation or sham cross-border flows can trigger criminal tax/customs proceedings, disqualification of NIT, and monetary sanctions against the legal representative.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains an administered exchange-control regime, not a full capital-control prohibition. Current-account convertibility for trade, services, interest, royalties, and ordinary income is broadly free, but all such transactions must be canalizada through local authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries. Capital-account operation is conditional: foreign direct investment and foreign loans must be registered with Banco de la República through an authorized local bank or financial intermediary. Dividend and profit remittances to non-resident investors are legally available for registered investments once Colombian corporate income tax and applicable non-resident dividend withholding tax have been settled. The main remittance hurdles are not exchange rationing or prior administrative approval, but rather mandatory foreign-investment registration, documentary proof of tax and accounting basis, tax clearance, and the domestic bank's AML/CFT source-of-funds review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombian goods exporters are required to channel and negotiate in the local foreign-exchange market 100% of the FOB/negotiated value of their export proceeds through an authorized intermediary within the statutory deadline set by Banco de la República, generally six months from the shipment or loading date. If the foreign currency is not collected and surrendered within that period, the exporter must submit a justification and request an extension through its intermediary. Service-export receipts are equally subject to canalización under the service contract terms, and no discretionary right exists to retain export proceeds indefinitely in offshore accounts without local exchange-market surrender.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia, operating jointly with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia and the Comité de Cambios Internacionales for the interpretation and enforcement of Colombia's foreign-exchange regulations (Régimen Cambiario).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs have low viability as the primary treasury account for a Colombian tax-resident SAS single-family office. Colombian supervisory law reserves deposit-taking and local payment intermediation to entities authorized by the Superintendencia Financiera, and an unlicensed foreign electronic-money institution cannot lawfully hold client funds or process regular COP-denominated third-party payments without a licensed local bank partner. Domestic SEDPEs (Sociedades Especializadas en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos) have medium viability only as a supplementary expense-management or wallet rail; they cannot provide correspondent SWIFT clearing, securities custody, capital escrow, or the full private-banking treasury infrastructure required by an SFO.

Defense evidence: Complete beneficial-ownership chart through to natural persons, certified trust deeds and SPV constitutional documents, family council or investment committee minutes, audited financial statements, a written no-nominee/no-bearer-shares certification, and a sworn source-of-wealth declaration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be routed through a locally authorized Colombian foreign-exchange intermediary. The compliant protocol is: (i) non-resident shareholder wires USD or other foreign currency to the SFO's designated capital-escrow account at the tier-one bank using SWIFT MT103 or MT202 with a purpose code identifying foreign direct investment/equity subscription; (ii) the remitter completes Banco de la República's foreign-investment registration and the local bank files the corresponding exchange declaration form (Declaración de Cambio Formulario 1) in the investor's name; (iii) the receiving bank verifies the beneficial owner, source of funds, and corporate authorization before any conversion to COP; and (iv) the SFO maintains a monthly reconciliation between the capital account, registered foreign investment file, and tax basis to avoid late-registration penalties.Outbound trade and investment disbursements must be canalizada through the authorized Colombian intermediary. The preferred clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for final commercial payments, SWIFT MT202 COV for correspondent clearing, and documentary/commercial letters of credit subject to UCP600 for physical trade settlements. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends remitted to non-resident shareholders from profits already taxed at the 35% corporate income tax rate; 35% withholding if dividends are distributed from profits not subjected to corporate income tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: COP 4,706,500,000 (100,000 UVT for fiscal year 2024) in gross income or total assets, or related-party transactions equal to or exceeding COP 470,650,000 (10,000 UVT), triggering Local File and Master File obligations and the TP return annex.

Economic substance scope: Colombia enforces economic substance through general anti-avoidance rules, CFC legislation under Law 2277 of 2022, and controlled-transaction analysis. A single family office must demonstrate real physical presence, active management, payroll, and commercial rationale for each transaction to avoid re-characterisation as a shamming arrangement. The SFO's Medium-Risk Enhanced AML profile further requires an active corporate, banking, and compliance footprint.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty for tax evasion under Article 447-A of the Colombian Criminal Code, as amended by Law 2277 of 2022: imprisonment of 4 to 9 years plus fines and asset forfeiture. Administrative sanction is equal to 200% of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors and the ultimate beneficial owner of the SFO become jointly and severally liable for the civil tax debt and may face travel bans, asset restraint, and detention pending trial if the misuse of the corporate veil is proven.
Shell Company / Sham SFO Structure

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the entity for tax purposes and impose the fine under Article 647 E.T. equal to 3% of the shareholder equity stated in the return or 1% of the improperly deducted amounts. Judicial nullity of the sham vehicle's contracts can follow.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal civil liability for all omitted corporate taxes and interest, plus criminal liability for fraudulent simulation with possible imprisonment of 48 to 108 months and property confiscation.
AML / SARLAFT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Law 526 of 1999 and Superfinanciera Circular 004 of 2021, penalties may reach COP 3.4 billion for severe violations, cancellation of the SFO's authorisation to operate, and seizure of funds linked to illicit assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives are personally subject to administrative fines, disqualification from commercial activity for up to 10 years, and criminal prosecution for money laundering under Article 323 of Law 599 of 2000, which carries prison penalties of 9 to 15 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Filing / Thin Capitalisation Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Article 260-10 E.T., DIAN may apply a fine equal to 10% of the non-deductible adjustment and 20% of the omitted tax, plus penalties for incomplete documentation. Unjustified thin capitalisation may result in the disallowance of interest deductions.

Director personal liability: The legal representative signing the disclosure is personally liable for penalties arising from deliberate non-cooperation with DIAN, and could face disqualification as an administrator and personal tax debt attachment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – MUISCA electronic portal

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned S.A.S. must incorporate by private document recorded with the Chamber of Commerce, obtain the Certificate of Existence and Legal Representation, and then register in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) electronically through DIAN's MUISCA portal. A domiciled legal representative (and, for e-invoicing, an electronic signature) is required. DIAN issues the NIT upon completion of RUT registration, without requiring a prior physical visit for entities with non-resident shareholders. For regulated brokerage services, the legal representative must also present the Financial Superintendency authorization to DIAN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the S.A.S. is a separate Colombian income tax resident; no pass-through status is available for foreign or domestic shareholders.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists under Colombian law. Tax classification is mandatory. Anti-hybrid and hybrid mismatch rules (ET Article 124-3) are imposed automatically; entities may elect the use of accelerated depreciation and VAT exemption criteria only in the sense permitted by the Tax Statute. For VAT export services, taxpayers must request in the RUT the condition of 'export service provider' to claim zero-rating.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

3,500 UVT per calendar year (approximately COP 174 million for fiscal year 2025). For securities brokerage activities, registration is nevertheless mandatory if any taxable or exempt activity is carried out, because financial service providers are treated as obligated VAT subject.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies only to exports of tangible goods and to services expressly listed in Article 481 of the Tax Statute (e.g., software, call centers, tourism and certain BPO/KPO services). For an offshore securities brokerage, commission and intermediary fees are outside the statutory list. As a result, the service is either exempt under Article 476 (if rendered by a supervised financial entity) or standard-rated at 19% when provided by a commercial S.A.S. without Financial Superintendency consolidation. To claim zero-rating, a taxpayer must first be recognized as an 'export service provider' on the RUT, demonstrate that the service is received and used wholly abroad, and submit a formal request to DIAN before carrying out the activity. A prior favorable case-by-case ruling is the only safe route for brokerage fees.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación F500) applicable to goods-linked transactions
  • Commercial Invoice / Export Service Contract executed with the non-resident client
  • Bill of Lading (for goods) or Equivalent Service Use Certificate (Certificado de uso en el exterior) signed by the foreign recipient
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de canalización de divisas) issued by a local bank
  • Power of Attorney / Certificate of Legal Representation for the S.A.S. and the DIAN export authorization (VUE - Registro de Vendedores de Servicios) if relying on Article 481

Electronic invoicing ('factura electrónica de venta') is mandatory for all VAT registrants. The taxpayer must use an authorized web service, transmitting each invoice in XML to DIAN in real time; DIAN validates and returns a unique code (CUFE) which must appear on the invoice. Clearance must be completed before the invoice is issued. Since 2022, digital stamping is also required for travel and export documentation, and each transaction must be serviced through DIAN's 'Facturación Electrónica' and 'Documento Soporte' applications.

DIAN will claw back zero-rated VAT if the exporter does not retain and present the customs or service documentation, fails to prove actual inbound FX remittance within the 180-day window, or the destination/offshore use is not credible. The penalties include an 'inexact penalty' of up to 160% of the underpaid VAT, 36% interest, and, in fraudulent cases, criminal sanctions for tax evasion under Article 402 of Colombia's Criminal Code. Additionally, the taxpayer may lose the RUT export-provider status and be barred from claiming VAT refunds on its input costs for the subsequent fiscal year.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a floating exchange system with an active domestic forward market. Current-account operations (including brokerage fee settlements, commission remittances, and related service payments) are freely convertible, but every physically exchanged currency transaction must be routed through an authorized intermediary del mercado cambiario and supported by the appropriate Declaración de Cambio (currently Formulario No. 5). Capital-account operations, including capital injections, foreign loans, and the establishment of foreign investments in a Colombian entity, are likewise convertible but conditioned on registration with Banco de la República and ongoing compliance with the investment registration certificate (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales). Profit remittance is permissible without quantitative ceilings, provided the underlying investment has been registered, Colombian withholding taxes (including dividend withholding tax at source) have been settled, local financial statement audits are up to date, and the foreign exchange proceeds are channeled through a Colombian authorized bank using the allocated investment liquidation purpose code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For services export proceeds (which include brokerage and agency commissions earned by the S.A.S from foreign clients), the statutory channeling obligation requires the Colombian exporter to channel the foreign currency through the authorized foreign exchange system within six months from the date of receipt of the payment. For goods exporters the analogous deadline runs from the shipment/export declaration date, normally six months unless a longer payment term beyond six months is contractually justified and declared. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the exporter may maintain 100% of the channeled foreign currency in a local foreign-currency account with an authorized intermediary, may sell it voluntarily to the banking system, or may use it within Colombia for legal current-account obligations. The obligation is to 'canalizar' (route) the proceeds, not to surrender them to the central bank or government; nevertheless, non-channeling or late channeling gives rise to exchange-regime fines and adverse AML/CFT reputation flags.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Banca Central de Colombia); foreign exchange administration is exercised by the same central bank under Resolución Externa No. 1 de 2018, with supplementary enforcement by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is LOW. Colombian law and SFC prudential rules require securities brokerage, escrow, and client-money segregation to be handled by local Colombian licensed credit institutions. Offshore electronic-money institutions (EMIs), foreign virtual account providers, and cross-border fintech rails cannot lawfully open segregated client accounts for Colombian resident brokerage operations, and Banco de la República channeling rules do not recognize them as authorized mercados de cambio. An offshore EMI account may be used only as an auxiliary treasury console outside Colombia for purely administrative inflows that are not subject to Colombian exchange-channeling, but it cannot serve as the primary operational or client-money custody rail for the Colombian S.A.S. without regulatory non-compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to a Banco de la República-authorized local bank, with the beneficiary account being the S.A.S. capital-escrow account. The sender must be a non-resident entity in the banking system and must include the precise RUT (Colombian tax ID) of the S.A.S., the corporate resolution, and the Banco de la República purpose code applicable to 'Inversión directa de no residentes'. After receipt, the S.A.S. compliance officer must file the foreign-exchange declaration (Formulario No. 5) on the local bank’s channel, attaching the investment registration certificate issued by the Banco de la República (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales). The bank will verify the SWIFT remittance against the motif 'Aportes de capital' and will not allow further allocation until the registration is complete.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements and commission payments shall be made through the S.A.S.’s authorized Colombian clearing bank using SWIFT MT103 messages for ordinary value transfers (e.g., foreign broker-dealer commissions, clearing fees, custody charges), with the corresponding Banco de la República exchange registration code for 'Pagado de comisiones' / 'Servicios de intermediación'. If physical commodity flows are intermediated alongside the securities brokerage, the commercial-letter-of-credit route (MT700 with MT707 amendment) may be used; all documentary credits require the underlying trade contract, the invoice, and the exchange declaration at the time of negotiation. Turnover: A statutory independent auditor (Revisor Fiscal) is mandatory for any Colombian S.A.S. meeting at least one 2025 threshold: gross assets exceeding 3,000 SMMLV (~USD 1.16M), gross revenue exceeding 3,000 SMMLV, or more than 20 employees. Additionally, a S.A.S. holding an SFC securities-brokerage license is always subject to mandatory audit regardless of size due to the public-interest nature of the regulated activity.

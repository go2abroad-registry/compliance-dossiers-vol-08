Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-in capital is required under Law 1258/2008. The full subscribed capital must be paid within two years after incorporation; no escrow account is mandated for cash contributions, and contributions in kind may be made without prior appraisal unless otherwise required by law.

Funding & unlock path: Office premises must obtain a fire safety inspection certificate from the local fire department (Cuerpo de Bomberos) if a physical office is occupied. Structural compliance with the national earthquake-resistant building code (NSR-10) applies to leased or owned office space. If employees are engaged, the company must register with an ARL and implement the minimum occupational health and safety program under Resolution 0312/2019.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — the national tax and customs authority of Colombia; online portal: www.dian.gov.co, with transactional services via the MUISCA platform and the SIRE electronic invoicing system.

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian S.A.S., incorporation is effected by executing a constitutive private document under Law 1258 of 2008, filed with the Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce) through the Ventanilla Única Empresarial (single business window). Once the company is registered, the S.A.S. must enroll in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) maintained by DIAN. The process is performed using the online pre-registration module on the DIAN portal, followed by in-person biometric validation of the legal representative at an authorized DIAN service office or through an accredited intermediary. Upon completion, DIAN assigns the Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT), a permanent ten-digit tax identification code, and simultaneously activates the S.A.S. as a common-regime IVA taxpayer, income-tax filer, customs declarant, and withholding agent. A Colombian resident legal representative with full tax filing powers is mandatory; non-resident shareholders are permitted and hold no ban on ownership, but the entity must maintain a valid resident fiscal representative to keep the NIT active and to receive DIAN notifications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not provide a US-style check-the-box elective regime; entity classification follows commercial form, and an S.A.S. is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to entity-level corporate income tax. The principal statutory elections available are: (1) option to be taxed under the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLES) established by Articles 903-916 of the Estatuto Tributario, available to an S.A.S. with gross income not exceeding COP 3,500,000,000, replacing ordinary CIT with a unified turnover-type tax; (2) designation as a Zona Franca (Free Trade Zone) taxpayer, where eligible, resulting in a reduced 20% CIT rate and special VAT treatment for the development of export-oriented software services; (3) formal election of the 0% VAT export treatment for services under Article 481 E.T., by registering the service-export activity in the RUT, filing the corresponding export declarations, and maintaining qualifying evidence; (4) application of the CFC and hybrid-mismatch transparency rules under Law 1819/2016, including the option to demonstrate effective substance for foreign-controlled entities; and (5) conclusion of advance pricing agreements (APAs) with DIAN for cross-border related-party service transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

An S.A.S. must register as an IVA responsible taxpayer (declarante) when, in the immediately preceding taxable year, its gross common taxable income exceeded COP 100,000,000 (approximately USD 23,000) or its year-end gross assets exceeded COP 350,000,000 (approximately USD 82,000). Because a cybersecurity SaaS business habitually supplies taxable digital services in Colombia, the S.A.S. will in practice be obliged to register as a common-regime IVA responsible regardless of those monetary thresholds. Registration is also a precondition for claiming the 0% export rate and the refund of input VAT credits on export activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The cybersecurity SaaS offering (ISIC 6201 — cloud vulnerability scanning, penetration testing, and related compliance SaaS) qualifies for the 0% IVA rate as an export of services under Article 481(4) of the Estatuto Tributario and Decree 1625/2016, Article 1.2.1.1.24, where the following cumulative conditions are met: (1) the customer is not domiciled in Colombia and is not a Colombian permanent establishment or resident related party; (2) the service is used or commercially exploited exclusively outside Colombian territory — for cloud SaaS, the software platform must operate from servers and data nodes located outside Colombia, and no Colombian user may benefit from the vulnerability scans or penetration-testing deliverables; (3) any associated professional/implementation services are rendered from Colombian offices to the foreign customer without interposition of a Colombian intermediary; (4) the consideration is received in freely convertible foreign currency (USD/EUR via Stripe or wire transfer) and is brought into the Colombian foreign-exchange market within the statutory repatriation period; and (5) the S.A.S. maintains a complete evidentiary file demonstrating offshore utilization, including IP-geolocation logs, server-location records, customer delivery receipts, and the contractual terms identifying the foreign recipient. The S.A.S. must formalize its service-export status in the RUT, file the corresponding service-export declarations, and may be required to obtain the exporter-of-services qualification certificate ('certificado de exportador de servicios') from the relevant authorities.

  • DIAN Declaración de Exportación de Servicios (service export declaration) filed through VUCE (Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior), or DIAN form 4840 where applicable
  • Factura Comercial / Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV) evidencing the export transaction, including the SaaS subscription or penetration-testing services agreement, ISIC 6201 description, the foreign customer's legal identity and foreign VAT/GST registration number, and USD/EUR consideration
  • Signed SaaS master subscription agreement, order form, or marketplace procurement record containing exclusive offshore-delivery terms and the customer's foreign-domicile certification
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) when physical hardware, token media, or on-premise scanning appliances are shipped; otherwise, the digital delivery log capturing the encrypted download, customer consumption, and hosted-data-center location outside Colombia
  • Declaración de Cambio / Form ECO (inward foreign-exchange declaration), also known as the Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas, issued by the Colombian intermediary bank evidencing the USD/EUR inward remittance received through the Colombian foreign-exchange market
  • Cámara de Comercio / ColombiaProductiva certificate of exporter-of-services qualification (when issued) and the updated RUT evidencing the service-export activity code
  • Technical service-delivery evidence pack: penetration-test report transmittal receipt, vulnerability-assessment delivery emails, cloud provider region metadata, SIEM or API access logs, and user geolocation analytics demonstrating exclusive offshore usage
  • Supporting bank statements and payment reconciliations linking each invoice to the foreign-exchange repatriation certificates

Electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta — FEV) is fully mandatory for all VAT-responsible taxpayers, with the obligation phased to completion for all registered businesses by fiscal year 2024. The S.A.S. must contract with a DIAN-authorized technology provider (proveedor tecnológico) that transmits invoices in real time over SOAP/REST APIs to DIAN's validation service (Servicio de Validación Previa). Each FEV must bear the DIAN-assigned CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) digital stamp, a QR code, and the standardized XML/XAdES structure set out in the Anexo Técnico, currently version 1.6, with migration underway to the SIRE document-electrónico platform (versions 2.0/2.1). DIAN performs real-time pre-clearance validation before an invoice reaches definitive status; rejected files must be corrected within the same business day. In addition, the S.A.S. must transmit the electronic invoicing data set to the 'Activo en Comercio' report, file monthly/quarterly form 300 (IVA return), submit form 2241 fiscal reconciliation, and, for services exports, coordinate with the VUCE (Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior) for the export declaration.

DIAN subjects zero-rated service exports to intense documentary scrutiny, cross-matching the 0% claims against electronic invoices, VUCE declarations, RUT records, and bank foreign-exchange forms. Failure to substantiate any element of the export treatment results in: (a) retrospective reversal of the 0% rating, recharacterization of the income as locally taxable services, and assessment of the full 19% IVA on the export consideration, together with mora interest at the certified Banco de la República rate and the inaccuracy penalty under Article 648 E.T. of 130% of the assessed differential, increased to a higher punitive bracket — commonly 200% — where DIAN finds sham transactions, concealed facts, or deliberately fraudulent documentation; (b) clawback of all input VAT credits previously claimed as saldo a favor, disallowance of refunds, and exclusion from the automatic-refund regime for up to three taxable years; (c) criminal exposure under Law 1762 of 2015 for customs and tax fraud, including imprisonment and monetary fines for statutory tax evasion; (d) customs penalties under Decree 1165 of 2019 for inaccurate export declarations, potentially escalating to fines of up to 300% of the export value and suspension of the exporter's customs authorization; and (e) foreign-exchange sanctions imposed by the Central Bank for failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days, including fines equal to up to 200% of the un-repatriated amount, compulsory reintegration, and, in serious repeat cases, exclusion of the company from the Colombian foreign-exchange market.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% general corporate income tax for Colombian Sociedades por Acciones Simplificadas with domestic tax residence.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding on gross dividends paid from Colombian taxable profits to a non-resident individual or entity; an additional 5% withholding applies on distributions made out of profits that have not effectively borne corporate income tax. The combined withholding on excess distributions may reach 35% where the underlying profits were wholly untaxed at the entity level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Deadline falls within the ninth calendar month after the fiscal year-end. The exact date is fixed annually by DIAN based on the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT, generally between 9 and 17 September of the following year.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall

The non-resident S.A.S. owner must use the same Colombian NIT of the entity. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation is triggered when the Colombian SAS meets any of: (i) gross income ≥ 100,000 UVT (approximately COP 4.71 billion for tax year 2024); (ii) gross assets ≥ 150,000 UVT; (iii) gross equity ≥ 150,000 UVT; or (iv) transactions with related parties located in tax havens or preferential regimes, regardless of thresholds. TP documentation and informational return are filed through Form 120. The UVT value for 2024 is COP 47,065.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations framework. However, substance is mandatory under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule of Article 869 E.T., the beneficial-ownership requirement of tax treaties, permanent-establishment definitions, and the statutory conditions for R&D and Orange Economy exemptions. A cybersecurity SaaS enterprise must document real employees, physical or virtual office substance, strategic decision-making in Colombia, ownership of intellectual property, assumption of operational risk, and genuine pre-sales/post-sales activity. An empty corporate shell with back-to-back foreign contracts will be recharacterized or disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misstatement

Statutory sanction: DIAN may impose the inexactness penalty of up to 200% of the tax understated, joint liability for unpaid taxes, and forfeiture of tax benefits. Criminal prosecution for wilful tax fraud can result in imprisonment of up to 9 years, criminal fines, confiscation of proceeds, and disqualification from government contracts.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives are jointly liable under Article 794 E.T. when they knowingly participate in the tax fraud. Courts may pierce the S.A.S. veil to seize personal assets; travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition procedures may be ordered once a criminal investigation begins.
Shell Company and Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the transaction or the entity under Article 869 E.T., reassess taxable income at 35%, deny VAT refunds on export services, impose literal penalties up to 200% of the difference, and refer the case for criminal investigation. The company may be declared a mere façade and liquidated.

Director personal liability: Directors who permit a zero-substance shell to accept foreign revenue become personally liable for the resulting tax debt and civil restitution. A formal finding of fiscal fraud may also trigger personal criminal responsibility, restrictions on leaving Colombia, and disqualification from holding corporate office.
Anti-Money Laundering and Foreign Exchange Violations

Statutory sanction: UIAF and fiscal authorities may impose monetary fines per violation, block bank accounts, suspend the company's tax registration number, and order confiscation of funds. Money-laundering offences carry imprisonment from 6 to 15 years in Colombia, depending on aggravation, and the corporate entity may be dissolved.

Director personal liability: Directors who control payment rails and ignore AML controls face personal criminal liability, civil restitution to victims, and asset forfeiture. Colombian courts may issue preventive travel bans, notification to Interpol, and broad seizure orders over foreign bank accounts.

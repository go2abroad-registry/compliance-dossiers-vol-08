Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — Colombia's national tax and customs authority. Official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; MUISCA e-services portal (RUT registration, NIT management, VAT filing): https://muisca.dian.gov.co; foreign-trade single window (VUCE/SIEX): https://vuce.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

The S.A.S., even if 100% non-resident owned, is a Colombian domestic entity and must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), which serves simultaneously as the income-tax ID and the IVA/VAT registration number. Procedure: (1) execute the S.A.S. bylaws before a Colombian notary, register with the Cámara de Comercio, and appoint a legal representative domiciled in Colombia; (2) apostille or legalize all foreign corporate documents and supply official Spanish translations; (3) file Form 50 (Registro Único Tributario, RUT) via DIAN's pre-registration web module or at the DIAN office, stating ISIC/CIIU 5820 (software publishing / sporting platforms) and the obligation to invoice with IVA; (4) upon approval, DIAN assigns the NIT and generates the RUT certificate; (5) update the RUT to add the export-service status ('exportador de servicios') and, if desired, the SIMPLE-regime election; (6) obtain a DIAN-authorized electronic signature (firma electrónica) through a certified certificate authority for MUISCA and e-invoicing access. Payment gateways (Stripe, PayPal) and Colombian banks will require the NIT/RUT as part of account onboarding.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Colombian S.A.S. is a resident juridical entity taxed at the entity level on worldwide net income (35% corporate rate for 2025); there is no pass-through eligibility. Income derived from the esports platform is ordinary business income subject to full assessment, while certain passive foreign-source income may qualify for territorial-reach exemptions. Shareholders bear additional dividend and/or remittance taxation at the beneficiary level.

Available election forms

Colombia has no check-the-box election; entity-level taxation is non-electively mandatory. The material elections and special-rule mechanisms available to an S.A.S. engaged in software/esports exports are: (i) the optional Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) for qualifying small taxpayers whose prior-year gross income does not exceed COP 3,500 million, which consolidates income and IVA remittance mechanics; (ii) the ZOMAC reduced-rate election for new companies located in conflict-affected municipalities (subject to its statutory eligibility window); (iii) the 'economía naranja' enhanced deduction for software and creative-industry investment where the statutory conditions are met; (iv) export-service IVA zero-rating classification declared in the RUT and applied through the FEV; and (v) anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Colombian law that, without any election, deny or defer deductions or tax credits to the extent that hybrid instruments or hybrid entities produce a mismatch in the counterparty's jurisdiction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a single free-floating exchange market rather than a fixed-rate system. The Colombian peso is generally convertible for current-account transactions and for capital-account transactions, provided the funds are channeled through an authorized foreign-exchange intermediary and properly reported under the national exchange regime. Capital-account transactions, including equity contributions, foreign loans, and foreign direct investment, are subject to registration requirements with Banco de la República. Profit remittance by a Colombian S.A.S. is legally permissible after compliance with corporate and tax obligations, including audited financial statements where required and distribution minutes adopted by the shareholders; remittances must be made through an authorized intermediary and supported by the underlying international investment registration. Colombia does not impose multiple exchange rates, quantitative exchange rationing, or prior exchange-license requirements for ordinary trade and investment remittances, but the use of non-channelized offshore balances violates the foreign-exchange regime and may trigger DIAN sanctions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods exporters must channel 100% of their export proceeds through the authorized Colombian foreign-exchange market within six (6) calendar months following the date of the export customs declaration (DEX). Exporters of software and cross-border digital services must channel service-proceeds within six (6) months from the date the income is accrued or invoiced under the applicable service contract, unless a longer term is authorized by the foreign-exchange regime. The statutory mandatory channeling ratio is 100%: the foreign currency must be sold to an authorized intermediary, deposited into a Colombian foreign-currency account, or used for permitted external obligations through an authorized intermediary. There is no separate legal obligation to convert into Colombian pesos if the exporter elects to maintain a foreign-currency account. Revenue collected by offshore payment processors such as Stripe or PayPal is not exempt and must ultimately be repatriated into the Colombian treasury account and reported through the applicable Declaración de Cambio.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia) as the monetary and foreign-exchange authority; Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia as the financial supervisor and licensing authority for authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries; Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) as the customs and tax authority for merchandise trade declarations and underlying export/import operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium as a primary treasury rail; Medium as an ancillary collection-only facility. Offshore digital EMI entities are not recognized as authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries under the Colombian exchange regime and therefore cannot legally serve as the sole channel for the S.A.S.'s export proceeds, foreign investment capital, or FX payments. Colombian law permits specialized electronic deposit and payment institutions, known as SEDPEs, to operate payment services under the supervision of Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia, but an offshore EMI must either partner with such a licensed local institution or use a local commercial bank clearing agreement to collect and deposit funds in Colombia. A Colombian S.A.S. may maintain ancillary accounts at foreign EMIs for e-commerce convenience, but those accounts must be transparently connected to the local treasury and the funds must be channelized into Colombia according to the mandatory surrender rules. Direct use of an unlicensed foreign EMI as the main operational account creates a legal red flag under AML/KYC and foreign-exchange compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign equity capital must be transferred from the non-resident investor's bank by SWIFT MT103 to the S.A.S.'s designated capital-receiving account at an authorized Colombian bank. The wire must uniquely identify the beneficiary S.A.S. NIT and the investor legal entity, and the payment purpose code in the SWIFT field 50/59 or remittance information must clearly indicate Foreign Direct Investment - Equity Capital Injection. The receiving authorized financial intermediary is required to prepare and file the applicable Declaración de Cambio for international investment on, or immediately after, the transaction date. The investor or the S.A.S. must subsequently register the investment in the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales of Banco de la República under the applicable rules, and the assigned file number must be maintained for all future dividend repatriation and capital-disinvestment transactions. Equity funds may not be routed through an unregulated offshore EMI or crypto-fiat conduit as a substitute for this channelized banking route. If the S.A.S. uses a foreign currency account, the capital declaration will reflect the maintained foreign-currency position, while any local operational funding requires conversion into COP at a free-market rate negotiated with the authorized intermediary.Outbound trade flows should be cleared through the Colombian S.A.S.'s operating account subject to the bank's FX desk and foreign-exchange declarations. For supplier payments, SWIFT MT103 is the appropriate single-currency wire clearing rail; for documentary trade, a commercial letter of credit opened by the Colombian bank through MT700 and subsequently settled via MT103/MT202 is preferred. All outbound payments for imported goods or services must be supported by the relevant DIAN import declaration, commercial invoice, service contract, license agreement, or similar underlying transaction documentation, and the local bank will complete a Declaración de Cambio by import classification at the time the client purchases FX. Tournament-winning disbursements to foreign players should be made through the same regulated banking channel, as direct offshore payment from Stripe/PayPal balances to third-party prize recipients may inflate the underwriting risk of the platform and create complicated FX-compliance records. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% general corporate income tax (CIT) applicable to Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.) for fiscal tax periods from 2023 onward per Law 2277 of 2022.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For dividends distributed to foreign shareholders: 5% withholding on the portion representing profits already subject to CIT; 35% withholding on profits that were exempt such as some Orange Economy earnings; effective combined CIT+WHT on ordinary operating profits is 38.75%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transfer pricing Local File and Master File are mandatory when the taxpayer's gross income exceeds COP 4,232 million (90,000 UVT in 2024) or gross assets exceed COP 9,405 million (200,000 UVT in 2024), and related-party transactions exceed COP 1,270 million (27,000 UVT in 2024). The separate TP Informative Return (Form 120) is due annually under those thresholds, with deviations in November of the following year.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a dedicated Economic Substance Regulation statutory act, but the General Anti-Abuse Rule (Article 658-bis E.T.) allows DIAN to disregard a S.A.S. as fiscally transparent or as an abusive shell if it lacks real premises, employees, decision-making activity, and actual it-platform infrastructure in Colombia. For an esports platform, substance evidence must include server logs, developer payroll, Colombian bank accounts, tournament prize-flow contracts, and meeting minutes of the board of directors.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion / hidden digital revenues

Statutory sanction: The tax authority re-determines taxable income and imposes a deficiency penalty of up to 200% of the evaded CIT (Article 779 E.T.); criminal prosecution under Article 434-A of the Colombian Criminal Code (evasión fiscal) carries imprisonment of 48 to 108 months and fines equal to 100%–200% of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives of the S.A.S. are jointly liable for the evaded tax and interest, and in criminal tax cases may receive travel bans issued by DIAN, asset embargoes, personal restitution orders, and terms of imprisonment of up to nine years if their tax noncompliance is deemed intentional.
Shell company / abusive economic substance structure

Statutory sanction: DIAN rejects the corporate structure under Article 658-bis (abuso de la naturaleza jurídica), disallows deductible expenses, recasts revenue at the level of the real foreign owner, denies Orange Economy exemptions, and levies a general tax fine of 5%–200% of the adjusted CIT.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under Law 222 of 1995 when the S.A.S. is used for fraudulent, abusive, tax-avoidance purposes; directors can be personally ordered to pay the full tax deficiency, face a ban on international travel until the debt is secured, and be prosecuted for document falsification when signatures on annual returns are deemed materially inaccurate.
Transfer pricing noncompliance for related party cross-border fee flows

Statutory sanction: DIAN adjusts the price to the arm's length value and charges a TP penalty of up to 20% on the resulting CIT adjustment, plus late-filing fines of 5% per month up to 200% of unpaid tax and interest; failure to file Form 120 carries an additional fine of 10–15,000 UVT.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incorrect TP declarations face personal liability for the adjustment and penalized tax; in aggravated cases, they may be named in criminal charges of 'falsedad en documento privado' with imprisonment of up to six years and be subject to anti-money-laundering investigation under the predicate offense of tax fraud.
AML / terrorism funding / unauthorized payment rails around tournament prize payouts

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Article 323 of the Colombian Criminal Code (lavado de activos) is punishable by 9 to 20 years of imprisonment and fines of up to 50,000 statutory minimum monthly wages; the Financial Information and Analysis Unit (UIAF) may freeze accounts, and the S.A.S. may lose its passport-style banking license privileges.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Chairman/CEO are treated as 'personas expuestas políticamente' where control failures appear; they may receive a travel ban, company asset seizure, and personal fines up to 20,000 SMMLV, in addition to criminal imprisonment in connection with predicate crimes of tax evasion or illicit enrichment through the platform.

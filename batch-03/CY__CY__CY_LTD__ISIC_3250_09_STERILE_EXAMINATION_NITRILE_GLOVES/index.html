Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; official portal: TAXISnet (https://tax.mof.gov.cy/), aided by the Department of Registrar of Companies and Intelligence on company incorporation.

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor establishes a Cyprus Private Limited Company via a local registered agent, obtaining an HE company registration number from the Registrar of Companies. Then the company applies to the local Tax District Office of the Department of Taxation for a Tax Identification Code (TIC) using the prescribed registration forms. If taxable supplies will exceed EUR 15,600 per year or if the company is a non-established supplier making supplies in Cyprus, VAT registration (Form VAT 101, or VAT 101 for non-established persons) is filed through TAXISnet. The TIC serves as the base tax reference; the VAT registration number is issued separately after the application and appears on the VAT certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus has no US-style 'check-the-box' or general pass-through election for a Private Limited Company. A Cyprus tax-resident Ltd is automatically taxable at corporate level on worldwide income; non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. Statutory elections available include group loss relief under the Group Relief provisions (for groups of collectivest investment undertakings and corporate groups with at least 1% shareholding), tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping operators, and specific 'deemed distribution' rules. Hybrid mismatch provisions (EU ATAD) are mandatory and cannot be opted out of; no hybrid entity classification election is available under Cypriot law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full euro-area member, so the euro is freely convertible on both current and capital account. No foreign-exchange control license, ring-fenced FX approval, or central bank registration is required for capital inflows, trade settlements, or profit remittance. Dividend, interest, royalty, and service-payment remittances to non-residents are unrestricted; the only breakage points are Cypriot tax documentation (generally zero outbound withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties outside IP royalty carve-outs), the Central Bank of Cyprus balance-of-payments statistical reporting on a BPM6 basis through the local reporting banking agent, and normal bank anti-money-laundering verification of the underlying beneficial ownership and source of funds. There is no convertibility bottleneck or repatriation queue.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio applies in Cyprus; exporters may hold EUR or other freely convertible currencies in any Cypriot bank account. Compliance for a manufacturer of sterile medical nitrile gloves consists of (i) maintaining the export-commercial dossier (invoice, packing list, bill of lading or CMR, Certificate of Origin EUR.1, CE Certificate under EU Medical Device Regulation (EU) 2017/745) for the dealing bank, (ii) filing Intrastat/Extrastat trade-statistical declarations, and (iii) disclosing the trade receipt in the Central Bank of Cyprus BOP direct-investment and trade-credit survey when the bank reporting threshold applies. Proceeds can be netted, converted, hedged, or reinvested without central bank consent.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), the national central bank within the European System of Central Banks; the Central Bank of Cyprus holds licensing, supervision, balance-of-payments statistical reporting, and sanctions-compliance authority over credit institutions and electronic money institutions in Cyprus

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for collections and wallet concentration; Low for trade finance and capital escrow. A Central Bank of Cyprus-licensed or EU-passported EMI (operating under Cyprus Electronic Money Law 31(I) of 2012 implementing EU EMD2, and PSD2 payment conduct) can provide fast EUR collection rails such as SEPA and SEPA Instant for EU distributor receipts. However, an EMI is legally prohibited from granting credit, issuing or confirming commercial letters of credit, discounting documents, or acting as a capital-injection escrow agent; its safeguarding account structure is not a universal banking account and carries balance-sub-ceiling restrictions. Therefore the core treasury architecture must retain one Tier-1 Cypriot bank for LC operations and share-capital escrow, using EMIs only as an auxiliary receivables sweep vehicle.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses SWIFT MT103 or MT202 cover (for EUR from EU counterparties, SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit is acceptable) directed to the CY Ltd operational EUR account at the selecting Tier-1 bank. The wire-reference field on the instruction should state 'equity capital contribution / issue of shares / share premium' and carry the bank's reason-for-payment code for equity capital under the Central Bank of Cyprus BOP reporting matrix. The bank verifies the share-issuance board resolution, the certified allotment register, and the certified source-of-funds declaration before crediting; since Cyprus imposes no pre-approval on capital inflows, the entire process is a bank-compliance and statistical disclosure exercise rather than a licensing exercise. Where the shareholder remits USD, the receiving bank freely performs spot conversion to EUR at market rates plus margin; conversion is unrestricted. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing is due within 12 months after the end of the accounting period; for a 31 December year-end the IR1 must be submitted by 31 December of the following year (e.g., FY2024 return by 31 December 2025). The self-assessment tax liability is settled in three instalments on 31 July, 31 December, and 31 March of the subsequent tax year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The provisional self-assessment is payable in three instalments: 31 July (first instalment), 31 December (second instalment), and 31 March (final settlement instalment) for companies with a calendar-year accounting period; the instalment schedule adjusts for non-calendar-year accounting periods.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Company Identification Tax Identification Number (TIN), Companies Registry HE number, registered office, permanent establishment address of the glove factory, accounting period start and end dates, and name of the appointed tax representative. The Cyprus company files as a Cyprus tax resident regardless of the foreign residence of shareholders; non-resident directors must still disclose their full details, and the registered office in Cyprus must be active and verifiable for service of process. Late submission of the IR1 attracts an administrative fine of EUR 100, plus EUR 100 for each month of delay, capped at EUR 500; unpaid tax accrues interest at the statutory official rate (currently 1.75% per annum) compounded monthly, together with a 5% surcharge if settlement is not made within 30 days of the due date and an additional 5% if default extends beyond six months; wilful failure with intent to evade tax exposes the company and its officers to criminal prosecution.
Box 2 - Trading Results and Income Gross turnover from sterile examination nitrile glove sales (ISIC 3250), export revenues, medical tender commissions, inventory changes, cost of raw materials, consumables, and direct production overheads; exempt dividend income and non-taxable capital items are excluded. Report all amounts in EUR; zero-rated export sales to non-EU buyers must be segregated from local Cyprus supplies; intra-group export sales to related distributors must be booked at arm's-length prices supported by transfer pricing evidence. Late submission of the IR1 attracts an administrative fine of EUR 100, plus EUR 100 for each month of delay, capped at EUR 500; unpaid tax accrues interest at the statutory official rate (currently 1.75% per annum) compounded monthly, together with a 5% surcharge if settlement is not made within 30 days of the due date and an additional 5% if default extends beyond six months; wilful failure with intent to evade tax exposes the company and its officers to criminal prosecution.

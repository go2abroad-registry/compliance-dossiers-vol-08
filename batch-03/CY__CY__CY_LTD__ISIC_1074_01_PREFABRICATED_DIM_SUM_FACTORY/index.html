Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Tax Department (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance; electronic portal: TAXISnet (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy); VAT administered under the Value Added Tax Law, Law 94(I)/2000; customs and export control administered by the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department (Τελωνεία) under the EU Union Customs Code (Regulation (EU) 952/2013).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Cyprus Private Limited Company (HE77) must: (1) incorporate with the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver and obtain the registered company number (HE number); (2) file Form T.F. 200 (Application for Registration of a New Company) with the District Tax Office within 60 days of incorporation to obtain the 8-digit Tax Identification Code (TIC); (3) register for VAT via Form VAT 101 within 30 days of entry into force of taxable activity or when annual taxable turnover exceeds 15,600 EUR, or immediately before making taxable supplies if a non-established supplier; (4) obtain an EORI (Economic Operator Registration and Identification) number from Customs for export declaration processing; and (5) if exporting outside the EU, open an AES (permitted) export clearance account and appoint a customs representative where required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Cyprus tax resident company (management and control test under the Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002) subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide income, plus 17% Special Defence Contribution (SDC) on deemed dividend distributions, and a 30% deemed dividend distribution tax reform effective for undistributed profits; the entity is not a pass-through vehicle and is ineligible for UK/US-style check-the-box flow-through treatment. — A foreign-owned Cyprus Private Limited Company (HE77) must: (1) incorporate with the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver and obtain the registered company number (HE number); (2) file Form T.F. 200 (Application for Registration of a New Company) with the District Tax Office within 60 days of incorporation to obtain the 8-digit Tax Identification Code (TIC); (3) register for VAT via Form VAT 101 within 30 days of entry into force of taxable activity or when annual taxable turnover exceeds 15,600 EUR, or immediately before making taxable supplies if a non-established supplier; (4) obtain an EORI (Economic Operator Registration and Identification) number from Customs for export declaration processing; and (5) if exporting outside the EU, open an AES (permitted) export clearance account and appoint a customs representative where required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Cyprus tax resident company (management and control test under the Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002) subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide income, plus 17% Special Defence Contribution (SDC) on deemed dividend distributions, and a 30% deemed dividend distribution tax reform effective for undistributed profits; the entity is not a pass-through vehicle and is ineligible for UK/US-style check-the-box flow-through treatment.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Cyprus. Available statutory elections: (1) group loss relief election between 75% group companies under Art. 25 of the Income Tax Law; (2) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on newly introduced equity capital; (3) IP Box regime election limiting taxable IP income to 80% of qualifying costs; (4) VAT group registration election for closely linked persons (Art. 14E VATA); (5) cash-basis VAT accounting election for the frozen food manufacturing entity where turnover does not exceed 15,600 EUR; (6) election to use the special OSS/IOSS schemes for distance sales; all elections remain subject to EU Hybrid Mismatch Directive (ATAD 2) restrictions and the arm's length standard.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

15,600 EUR annual taxable turnover for Cyprus-established businesses (Art. 13 VATA); 35,000 EUR intra-Community distance selling threshold; no threshold for non-established suppliers of services or for importers of goods; voluntary registration permitted without threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available for (1) export sales of goods to destinations outside the EU (Art. 59 VATA) where the goods physically leave the EU within an accepted timeframe and the exporter retains proof of export; (2) intra-Community supplies to a VAT-registered business in another EU Member State (Art. 60 VATA), contingent on the customer's valid VAT ID and correct VIES reporting; (3) international transport and ancillary services (Art. 63 VATA). For the dim sum factory, all qualified export invoices are returned on the quarterly VAT return as Box 8/9 zero-rated outputs, and input VAT on raw materials, packaging, utilities and freight can be reclaimed via the standard periodic refund mechanism.

  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document through the EU AES/Cyprus Customs) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill/Airline Cargo Manifest evidencing physical export out of the EU
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the frozen dim sum products and HS code
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 payment proof evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Signed Sale and Purchase Contract with the foreign buyer
  • EU VAT-registered customer VAT ID proof (for intra-Community supplies)
  • Cold-chain logistics documentation (reefer container notes, temperature logs) supporting delivery evidence

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance for B2B e-invoicing is currently in force in Cyprus; no national digital stamping or API-verified clearance obligation applies. Mandatory electronic invoicing (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) applies to all B2G public procurement invoices since April 2020. Intra-EU recapitulative statements (VIES) must be submitted electronically; EU ViDA framework proposals requiring real-time transaction clearance are not yet transposed.

The Cyprus Tax Commissioner may retrospectively disallow zero rating and re-assess 19% VAT, plus statutory interest, where export evidence is deficient; the assessment period is 6 years (extended to 10 years in cases of negligent or fraudulent conduct). Administrative penalties under Art. 47 VATA may reach 100% of VAT evaded. Fraudulent export subsidy or false declaration practices attract criminal prosecution under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law and the Customs Law, with imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fines, and are reportable under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Act 188(I)/2007. In addition, failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may cause the unpaid balance to be re-characterised as a deemed dividend distribution, triggering 17% SDC, and exposes the foreign parent to transfer pricing and thin-capitalisation challenges under the Cyprus Income Tax Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus, as a Eurozone Member State, maintains full capital and current account convertibility under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There is no domestic foreign-exchange control statute, no prior approval requirement for foreign direct investment into CY_LTD, and no restriction on the repatriation of share capital, dividends, interest, royalties, or liquidation proceeds. Cyprus does not impose withholding tax on ordinary dividend distributions, interest, or royalty payments to non-residents. The only practical remittance hurdles are ordinary AML/KYC checks, payment transparency Regulation (EU) 2015/847, sanctions screening, and customer due-diligence confirmation that the source of funds and underlying commercial purpose are lawful. All temporary administrative capital-control measures introduced during the 2013 banking crisis have been fully abolished.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no Cypriot or EU requirement to surrender export proceeds, no statutory conversion period, and no mandatory conversion ratio. 100% of export receipts may be retained in any freely convertible currency, held in foreign-currency bank accounts in Cyprus, used for offshore payments, or sold in the spot market at freely negotiated rates. Banks submit statistical balance-of-payments reports to the Central Bank of Cyprus for external transactions, including export receipts and capital transfers, but this reporting creates no royalty, remittance, or conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza Kyprou), acting as the national monetary authority, single banking and e-money licensing supervisor, and Eurosystem member; the European Central Bank (ECB) exercises primary monetary authority in euro-area matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EEA-authorized electronic money institutions (EMIs) validly passporting into Cyprus under the Electronic Money Law of 2012 and the Payment Services Law (PSD II). Such EMIs may open euro and foreign-currency virtual IBAN accounts, execute SWIFT/TARGET2 deposits, and support trade payments with reduced account-opening friction. Feasibility rating: Low for non-EEA or offshore digital EMIs. A third-country EMI may not lawfully provide payment or e-money account services in Cyprus without a Cyprus/EU licence or an approved passport arrangement. Use of unlicensed offshore EMIs as treasury cash-management platforms creates AML/CFT, sanctions, and tax reporting exposure and will be rejected by Cypriot auditors, banks, and regulators.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from the non-resident parent or shareholder must be routed by SWIFT MT103 (or TARGET2 / Instant Payment if in euro) to the Cyprus operational account, with remittance information clearly identifying the transaction as 'Capital Contribution / Share Subscription' and referencing the corporate shareholder resolution. The sending bank must apply the correct purpose-of-payment code under its local reporting system; in Cyprus there is no restrictive FX licensing, but the beneficiary Cypriot bank will require source-of-funds evidence and the ultimate beneficial owner identity for AML purposes. Foreign-currency injections may be settled immediately into EUR at market rate under a spot sale, or held in a multi-currency account; no surrender or conversion authorization is required. For debt injections, a formal loan agreement, Cyprus interest withholding tax exposure analysis, and board approvals must be provided; for equity injections, the company's issued capital must be updated in the Cyprus company registry as appropriate.Trade settlements for imports of quick-freezing machinery, packaging, ingredients, and foreign raw materials should be executed via SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers, or through commercial letters of credit issued by a Cypriot bank using SWIFT MT700/MT707 subject to ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP600). EUR disbursements clear over TARGET2; USD disbursements clear via CHATS/CHIPS/Fedwire correspondent banking; GBP via CHAPS. The commercial invoice should reference Incoterms 2020, LC number, shipping documents, health certificates, and sanctions classifications. Cyprus banks apply full EU sanctions screening, trade-finance document checking, and can require the exporter/importer to provide EORI and customs evidence before releasing funds.

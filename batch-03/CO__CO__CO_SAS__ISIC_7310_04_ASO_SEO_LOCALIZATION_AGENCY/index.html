Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum authorized capital; each subscribed share must be paid at least 25% of its nominal value at incorporation, in cash or in kind, with the balance payable according to the bylaws, generally within 24 months. No escrow account requirement applies.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the official fire department (Cuerpo de Bomberos) confirming emergency exits, extinguishers, and evacuation signage; structural load inspection verified through the leasehold's existing building safety certificate; and workplace compliance under Resolution 312 of 2019 requiring an occupational health and safety management system (SG- SST) with risk assessments and employee training records.

Digital taxpayer portal: 'Sede Electrónica DIAN' at www.dian.gov.co; transactional e-services are operated on the MUISCA platform. Foreign-owned entities interact through a Colombian resident legal representative ('apoderado') recorded in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: Worldwide for resident S.A.S.; Pass_Through_Eligible: Not available (no check-the-box or partnership flow-through election); Tax_Exempt_Zone: Not applicable (ordinary national regime; a 20% Zona Franca rate requires separate DIAN Free-Zone authorization). Concretely, the S.A.S. is a Colombian taxpayer resident under ET art. 12-1 and is taxed on worldwide net income at the general corporate rate of 35% for fiscal years 2024-2025; the same entity is the IVA taxpayer for services rendered; non-resident shareholders are not themselves taxed at entity level but dividends distributed abroad are subject to separate Colombian dividend withholding rules.

Available election forms

Colombia has no US-style check-the-box election. Statutory elections relevant to this S.A.S. include: (i) the 'Régimen Simple de Tributación – SIMPLE' (Law 1943 of 2018, as amended), an elective consolidated income-VAT-ICA regime for entities with annual gross income not exceeding 85,000 UVT (approx. COP 4,233 million for FY2025; 2025 UVT = COP 49,799), with combined progressive rates of 0%–14.5% and simplified e-filing; (ii) election to enter an established Zona Franca or obtain 'Usuario Industrial de Bienes y Servicios' status, giving a 20% corporate income tax rate and certain VAT exclusions (not assumed here); (iii) election to claim input VAT refunds on a periodic basis instead of offsetting them against future VAT liability; and (iv) transfer-pricing documentation elections under OECD-aligned Colombian rules. Anti-hybrid and Colombian CFC provisions apply, so hybrid-branch/check-the-box mechanisms are unavailable; an S.A.S. always remains a corporation for tax purposes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian Peso (COP) is fully convertible for current account transactions but subject to mandatory channeling through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment and outbound profit remittances, require prior registration with Banco de la República and are generally free of quantitative restrictions, provided that tax obligations and annual corporate filings are current. Dividend remittances are permitted after statutory financial statements adoption and tax payment, but the corresponding central bank registration and withholding payment proof must be presented to the local commercial bank. No general prior approval is required for routine service export collections, yet all proceeds must enter the national exchange market in compliance with channeling rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters, including digital service providers generating foreign-currency revenue from SEO and localization projects for a Colombian S.A.S., must channel all export collections through the domestic foreign exchange market via an authorized local commercial bank within a maximum term of 13 months from the date of the invoice or the service provided. The mandatory conversion ratio is 100% of the foreign-currency proceeds, unless the Banco de la República has expressly authorized the exporter to hold foreign-currency bank accounts abroad or indefinitely offshore funds. The sale to the local market must occur within 5 business days of collection if the money is received directly into a foreign account, or immediately if credited to a local USD settlement account. Compliance is evidenced through the monthly foreign-exchange declaration (Formulario No. 14) submitted to the Central Bank by the commercial bank.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia, acting through the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia as executing regulators for the Colombian foreign exchange regime (Régimen Cambiario).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderate. Wise B2B and Payoneer are not authorized deposit-taking banking institutions in Colombia; they cannot issue a Colombian NIT or provide a local account for mandatory FX channeling, nor can they act as managers for the Banco de la República foreign-exchange registration. Their lawful use is as auxiliary collection and disbursement rails for low-value cross-border payments, provided that the underlying services are not part of Colombian export proceeds requiring surrender to the official exchange market. For an SEO localization agency receiving global performance-based fees, WISE/Payoneer balances must be swept in-country on a monthly basis to an authorized commercial bank account and reported in the FX declarations. As a primary treasury account, these EMIs are deemed non-compliant with Colombian capital controls and a local bancarized account is mandatory.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Single-member foreign-owned S.A.S. with no physical office, no local employees, and no Colombian revenue.

Root cause: Single-member foreign-owned S.A.S. with no physical office, no local employees, and no Colombian revenue.

Defense evidence: Provide a notarized lease agreement in Bogotá, the individual tax ID (RUT) of a Colombian resident general manager, six months of banking statements from a foreign parent, detailed global commercial contracts with APAC/LatAm clients, and a sworn statement that the entity will engage local bilingual personnel for ASO and localization work.
High-throughput cross-border payments flowing from payment aggregators (Wise/Payoneer) without identifiable underlying invoices.

Root cause: High-throughput cross-border payments flowing from payment aggregators (Wise/Payoneer) without identifiable underlying invoices.

Defense evidence: Submit a fully reconciled transaction ledger matching each inbound payment to a signed client purchase order or localization invoice, the corresponding SWIFT/payment settlement statements, and a record of the monthly Formulario No. 14 FX declaration filed through the designated commercial bank, together with a signed AML representation letter certifying that all counterparties are free from OFAC and EU restrictive measures.
Rapid creation of corporate structure followed by high foreign capital injection with a shareholder located in a high-risk jurisdiction.

Root cause: Rapid creation of corporate structure followed by high foreign capital injection with a shareholder located in a high-risk jurisdiction.

Defense evidence: Deliver a comprehensive beneficial ownership chart (Régimen de Propiedad Beneficiaria) certified by the Colombian notary, a bank reference letter from the foreign source bank issued within 30 days, a tax affidavit of the parent company clarifying its commercial activity, and an independent economic substance report prepared by a local auditor confirming the S.A.S. has sufficient operational capacity to execute the SEO market strategy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections for the Colombian S.A.S. must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for large interbank transfers) with the correspondent bank and the local commercial bank as final beneficiary, using the official Banco de la República foreign-exchange purpose concept 3618 (invexes from the parent company) or concept 2005 (capital contributions). The sender must include the NIT (tax ID) of the Colombian S.A.S., the concept word 'INVESTMENT - REGISTRO INVERSIONES INTERNACIONALES', and the reference to the foreign investment registration number issued by Banco de la República. The receiving bank will assign an exchange declaration form (Formulario No. 14) for the transaction, which must be closed before any COP disbursement. For subscription of shares by a non-resident, the paid-in capital is initially not subject to FX surrender, but the equivalent COP proceeds disbursed from the USD account must be tracked and registered as an investment against the account statement.Trade settlements for supplier disbursements (e.g., outsourcing of localization, ASO development, linguistic quality assurance) must be executed through the authorized local bank via SWIFT MT103 under the official 'Outbound Services' purpose code corresponding to the nature of the service (e.g., 4730 – technical services, 4740 – marketing/advertising services). Commercial letters of credit (LC) are permissible but generally unnecessary and administratively expensive for intangible service contracts; open account MT103 with 30/60/90 days terms is the standard clearing rail. All outbound payments require an approved binding contract, proof of prior month-end FX declaration, and the appointed bank's validation that the payment qualifies under the risk management rules. Turnover: An S.A.S. must appoint a Revisor Fiscal and obtain a statutory audit opinion when total gross assets or gross income exceed the equivalent of 3,000 current monthly legal minimum wages (SMMLV) as of the applicable fiscal year-end; the Revisor Fiscal is also compulsory when required by law, by sector-specific regulation, or by the shareholders' agreement. Below the threshold, no independent statutory audit is mandatory, but shareholders may voluntarily designate an auditor or revisor fiscal.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% statutory withholding tax for dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders from earnings already subject to Colombian corporate income tax. Where dividends are paid out of exempt or otherwise untaxed profits, the withholding rate is 35% (the corporate headline rate) at the time of distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally due in the 4th to 9th month following the fiscal year-end, depending on taxpayer classification and the last digit of the NIT, as published annually in a DIAN deadline resolution. Calendar-year entities face a filing season that runs from April to September of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is required for corporate taxpayers that have related-party transactions and whose gross income exceeds approximately 61,000 UVT (approx. COP 3.1 billion / USD 700,000) or whose total related-party transaction value exceeds approximately 10,000 UVT (approx. COP 500 million). A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required only for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above COP 100 billion. Questions about compliance threshold: even when documentation is optional, an annual Transfer Pricing Information Return (Form 120) must sometimes be filed where any of the income, asset, or transaction filters are met.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not adopt a standalone 'Economic Substance Regulations' regime as found in some offshore centers. However, Colombian tax law and DIAN administrative doctrine apply the substance-over-form principle to challenge artificial structures and shell companies: the entity must demonstrate actual premises, personnel, administrative capacity, and decision-making in Colombia. A SAS with no local substance but only a registered PO Box risks having its payments to non-residents recharacterized as dividends or royalties subject to higher WHT, CFC attribution under controlled-foreign-company rules, and denial of treaty benefits for lack of beneficial ownership or permanent establishment exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / intentional understatement

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine under DIAN can reach up to 160% of the evaded tax, plus moratory interest at the official rate. Criminal prosecution under Colombian Criminal Code Article 434 for tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment of 36 to 108 months (approximately 3 to 9 years) and a fine equivalent to the evaded tax amount.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives can be personally bound for the unpaid corporate tax where they signed fraudulent returns or participated in the concealment; criminal judges may impose civil restitution, disqualification from managing companies, travel bans pending proceedings, and custodial imprisonment for the directors actually charged.
Money laundering and unexplained asset growth

Statutory sanction: Under Colombian Criminal Code Article 323, money laundering carries a prison sentence of 10 to 30 years and fines of up to 1,000 times the current statutory minimum wage (SMMLV). Funds and assets connected to the offence may be confiscated.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability falls on any director, shareholder, or compliance officer who knowingly participates in laying or disguising illicit proceeds. Convictions lead to immediate prison terms, travel bans, bank account freezes, and civil liability for all laundering losses suffered by third parties.
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disallow expenses for lack of documentation, levy fines of up to 1% of the value of unreported related-party transactions per infraction, and charge interest on any adjusted tax underpayment. Understating the tax base substantively can trigger the same evasion sanctions as classic tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Signing false compliance certificates or knowingly filing incomplete transfer-pricing documentation exposes the CFO and legal representative to civil damages and to criminal liability for fraud against the tax administration if the underpayment exceeds criminal thresholds; can include prohibition from serving as directors and personal seizure of assets.
Failure to withhold and remit cross-border dividend and royalty WHT

Statutory sanction: DIAN imposes on the withholding agent a sanction equal to 100% of the tax not withheld, plus moratory interest and late-payment surcharge. Additionally, recurring failures may be treated as criminal nonpayment of withholding tax, generating imprisonment for the responsible officer.

Director personal liability: The managing director and accountant who execute an unauthorized tax-free remittance become personally liable for the withholding shortfall; criminal cases can bring preventive detention, travel bans, disgorgement of economic benefit, and direct civil reparation to the tax authority.

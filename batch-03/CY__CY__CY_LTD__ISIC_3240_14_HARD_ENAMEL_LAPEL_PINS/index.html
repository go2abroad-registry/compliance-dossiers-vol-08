Governing statute

The Companies Law, Cap. 113 (as amended by Law 153(I)/2018 and aligned with EU directives); key provisions applied: Sections 6 (incorporation), 7-8 (memorandum), 12 (registered office), 26 (no par value shares prohibited), 120 (registered office retreat), and 176 (register of directors and secretary).

Formation / constitutional filings

Form HE01 (Application for Registration of a Private Company) together with the executed Memorandum and Articles of Association and statutory declarations of compliance under Section 6 of Cap. 113.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum issued or paid-up capital for a Cyprus private limited liability company; one ordinary share of EUR 1.00 is standard. Capital may be subscribed and fully or partly paid post-registration; no escrow account or capital duty is mandated by Cypriot law for private companies.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum issued or paid-up capital for a Cyprus private limited liability company; one ordinary share of EUR 1.00 is standard. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance; electronic registration and filing via TaxisNet / Cyprus Tax Portal (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy); physical filing at the District Tax Office having geographic competence over the registered office.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus Private Limited Company (Companies Law Cap.113, registration prefix 'HE') automatically receives a Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon incorporation, using an 8-digit base plus one check character; the same TIN is later used as the VAT number with the EU prefix 'CY' if VAT registration is granted. A foreign entity becoming taxable in Cyprus must submit the prescribed TIN/VAT registration application to the competent District Tax Office (in person or via TaxisNet), attaching: certificate of incorporation or equivalent foreign registration, constitutional documents and translations, proof of registered office or appointed agent in Cyprus, identity details of directors/shareholders/signatories, and a description of intended Cyprus activity. Processing takes approximately 30 days from receipt of a complete file. A non-established (foreign) taxable person making any taxable supply in Cyprus has no turnover threshold and must register from the first euro of sales; businesses established in Cyprus must register within 30 days of exceeding EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' or similar pass-through election exists under Cyprus law; Cypriot-resident limited companies are taxed at entity level on worldwide income at a 12.5% corporate rate. Available statutory elections include: (i) VAT group registration election under section 12 of the VAT Law N.95(I)/2000 for related Cyprus-established persons; (ii) notional interest deduction (NID) election on new equity introduced after 1 January 2015 (section 9(l) Income Tax Law); (iii) qualifying intellectual property box election under section 24A achieving an effective 2.5% rate on qualifying assets; (iv) group relief election for transfer of tax losses under section 24; and (v) anti-hybrid ordering choices under ATAD implementing legislation for structured arrangements, limited to mandatory rule matching rather than elective pass-through treatment. Tonnage tax is optionally available for eligible shipping but is not applicable to ISIC 3240 (enamel lapel pin manufacturing).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 of annual taxable turnover for Cyprus-established suppliers of goods or services. Non-established taxable persons supplying goods or services taxable in Cyprus must register without any threshold from the first euro of turnover. Intra-Community distance sales into Cyprus by an EU supplier require registration once annual distance sales exceed EUR 10,000 (Electronically taxable: the Union-wide threshold applies), unless the supplier voluntarily opts to register earlier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of hard enamel lapel pins from Cyprus to any destination outside the EU is zero-rated at 0% VAT when the goods physically leave the EU within 90 days of the time of supply and the exporter possesses preserved evidence of export (customs export declaration SAD/AES, transport documents and payment/settlement records). Intra-Community supplies to EU business customers (e.g., EU-based distributors) are zero-rated where the customer provides a valid EU VAT number, the goods are transported to another Member State, and the supplier files the required VIES recapitulative statement and any applicable Intrastat return. For B2C sales made via Etsy, the EU e-commerce VAT package treats the marketplace (Etsy) as the deemed supplier for intra-EU distance sales and for imports of consignments up to EUR 150; consequently, the Cyprus seller's supply of pins to Etsy is treated as a taxable B2B supply to a VAT-registered marketplace, preserving the exporter's ability to zero-rate the underlying shipment with proof of dispatch and Etsy's EU VAT number. Sales to non-EU end consumers via Etsy/Stripe are zero-rated using the same customs-export evidence (SAD/AES and carrier tracking). Input VAT on imported brass blanks, enamel, backing clutches, packaging and freight is recoverable through the periodic VAT return to the extent attributable to zero-rated export sales.

  • Customs Export Declaration – Single Administrative Document (SAD) / AES clearance record with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading, House Air Waybill, or CMR international consignment note evidencing dispatch to and arrival outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice identifying the goods (HS 8306, 7326, 3926 or 7117), quantities, unit values and Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a Cyprus bank, or an equivalent SWIFT/merchant settlement statement from Etsy Direct Pay or Stripe evidencing foreign-currency receipt of export sale proceeds
  • Customer's valid EU VAT number and VAT-validation printout (for intra-Community B2B zero-rating)
  • Carrier delivery confirmation or tracking status showing the final destination address outside the EU (third-country exports)

As at the current reference stage, Cyprus does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based invoice submission to tax authorities. E-invoicing is legally accepted using PDF, XML (including the European standard EN 16931) or EDI formats, and manual paper invoices also remain valid if they satisfy the VAT Law content requirements. VAT returns (VAT 400), VIES recapitulative statements, and Intrastat declarations must be filed electronically via TaxisNet. Cyprus is implementing the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) package, which is expected to introduce mandatory e-invoicing and digital transaction reporting for intra-EU supplies in the 2030s; no live digital clearance mandate exists at this Stage 3 reference date.

Cyprus abolished exchange-control restrictions, so no statutory 180-day repatriation deadline is imposed by law; however, the Tax Department and banking practice treat 180 days as the accepted commercial audit benchmark for repatriation of export proceeds, and unexplained delays may trigger transfer-pricing adjustments (section 33 Income Tax Law) or a deemed distribution/CFC enquiry. Where the exporter fails to produce the required export evidence, the Tax Department withdraws the zero-rating and re-assesses VAT at 19% on the gross sale consideration, plus interest at the statutory annual rate (currently 2% p.a.) and administrative penalties up to 200% of the VAT shortfall. Fraudulent or forged export documentation (e.g., fake customs declarations, fictitious bills of lading or unreported cancellations of export) constitutes a criminal offence under VAT Law N.95(I)/2000 and the Customs and Excise Law, punishable with fines up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years (up to 5 years for serious evasion of VAT). The assessment limitation period is extended from 6 years to 10 years in cases of fraud, willful misstatement or intentional non-disclosure, and all supporting export records must be retained for at least 6 years following the date of the return to which they relate.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full liberalization of both current-account and capital-account transactions has applied since Cyprus's EU accession on 1 May 2004; the domestic exchange-control regime was repealed and no prior central-bank approval is required for non-resident shareholding, equity injections, loan servicing, royalty payments, branch profit extraction, or dividend remittances. The euro is legal tender in Cyprus, and the treasury may freely invoice, hold, and convert EUR, USD, GBP, and other EEA/third-country currencies. The remaining constraints are indirect: CBC Balance-of-Payments reporting imposed through resident banks, EU/UN sanctions screening, and AML/CFT obligations under Law 188(I)/2007 (implementing Directive (EU) 2015/849 as amended). For a low-risk hard-enamel-lapel-pin exporter using Etsy Direct Pay and Stripe, no convertibility licensing or profit-remittance hurdles exist, and outbound dividends are not subject to Cyprus withholding tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Cyprus imposes no statutory repatriation timeline and no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio for export receipts, because the euro is the domestic legal tender; proceeds may be retained in EUR or foreign currency at the exporter's discretion. In practice, the CBC-supervised account bank requires export commercial invoices, transport or courier evidence, and Etsy/Stripe payout reports for AML screening and Balance-of-Payments coding, and exporters are expected to repatriate proceeds within the invoiced commercial payment terms (typically 30–180 days) to demonstrate bona fide trading activity for Cyprus tax-residence and transfer-pricing purposes.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου, 'CBC'), constituted under the Central Bank of Cyprus Law 2002 (Law 138(I)/2002, as amended), acting as the national monetary authority and banking supervisor within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank; the Cyprus Ministry of Finance retains statutory authority under Articles 64–66 TFEU to impose temporary capital-movement safeguards in emergency circumstances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability rating: High as an ancillary settlement channel, Medium as the exclusive or primary treasury rail. EU/EEA-authorized Electronic Money Institutions operating under the free-passporting rights of PSD2 may lawfully open e-money/payment accounts for a Cyprus private limited company without a separate Cyprus license; UK-based (post-Brexit) or other offshore EMIs outside the EEA may lawfully service a Cyprus corporate client only on a client-initiated basis or through a Cyprus branch/agent and are not recommended for core treasury. Practical constraints include the CBC's statistical reporting obligations, the bank's duty to identify the full settlement flow, and the Cyprus Tax Department's transfer-pricing position on treasury functions; therefore the primary operating and capital-escrow accounts must remain with a CBC-supervised credit institution, while EEA EMIs may be layered as liquidity buffers for supplier payouts.

TD4 must be filed within 12 months after the end of the tax year (31 December for calendar-year companies, i.e., by 31 December of the following year). Provisional tax is due in two equal installments on 31 July and 31 December of the tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions. Specific sub-limits apply: EUR 2,000,000 for tangible goods, EUR 750,000 for financial transactions (loans, guarantees), and EUR 500,000 for intangible services or intellectual property.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Cyprus tax residence requires effective management and control exercised in Cyprus. For a manufacturer and seller of hard enamel lapel pins, substantive operations (production, inventory, sales, invoicing, and decision-making) must occur in Cyprus. The company must hold regular board meetings in Cyprus, maintain its statutory registers, employ local staff, lease or own premises, and ensure that commercial decisions are made in Cyprus. EU anti-abuse rules (ATAD/DAC6) and the Cyprus-specific 'economic substance' guidance require that a pure shell company with no real activity will be denied tax residence, penalised, and may face CFC recharacterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Online Sales Suppression

Statutory sanction: Civil: additional tax of up to 100% of the evaded tax, default interest at 1.25% per month, and fines up to €1,500 per offence. Criminal: imprisonment for up to 5 years and fines up to €10,000 or more, depending on the amount evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or cause the company to suppress revenue can be held personally liable for the company's tax debts. Courts may issue travel bans, freezing orders, and property seizures against directors. In criminal proceedings, directors risk individual imprisonment and disqualification from acting as company directors.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department will deny the Tax Residence Certificate and retrospectively assess profits at 12.5% with interest and penalties of up to 10% of undisclosed amounts. Under EU DAC6/ATAD, such shell entities may be required to produce additional evidence; failure can lead to administrative fines of €10,000 and the invalidation of tax benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for the company's unpaid taxes if they knowingly tolerated a shell structure. Courts can impose civil damages, freezing orders, travel bans, and in serious cases, a penalty of imprisonment for contravention of the Companies Law in declaring false declarations of registered office or management.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 6% of the adjusted transaction amount, plus 10% of the additional tax undercharged, and interest at 1.25% per month. The tax authority may make a discretionary upward adjustment to profits if the taxpayer fails to provide adequate documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for TP penalties if they failed to ensure the documentation was prepared and filed, and the company cannot pay. They may also face disqualification orders and, in flagrant cases, criminal proceedings for tax fraud if they deliberately conceal related-party pricing.

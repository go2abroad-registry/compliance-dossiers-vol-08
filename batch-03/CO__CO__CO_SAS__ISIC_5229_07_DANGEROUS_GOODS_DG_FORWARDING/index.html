Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Unidad Administrativa Especial Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — Colombia's National Tax and Customs Authority; primary portals: www.dian.gov.co, MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado), and VUCE (Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior) for customs and export-related filings.

E-government filing portal

The statutory tax identification number is the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria). A foreign-owned S.A.S. must complete RUT (Registro Único Tributario) registration through DIAN's MUISCA e-portal (e-RUT) using the legal representative's certified digital signature, or in person at a DIAN office presenting Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio) certified incorporation documents. Non-resident shareholders require no local presence, but the S.A.S. must maintain a Colombian-domiciled legal representative, register the ISIC 5229/dangerous-goods forwarding activity in the RUT, update the RUT upon any change in address, legal form, or tax regime, and appoint a licensed customs broker (SAI - Sociedad de Intermediación Aduanera) for customs and DG forwarding operations. The RUT serves simultaneously as the registry for income tax, VAT, withholding obligations, and foreign-exchange reporting duties.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — An S.A.S. is an autonomous legal entity taxed at entity level on worldwide income under the Estatuto Tributario at the standard 35% corporate income tax rate; no pass-through, fiscal-transparency, or check-the-box classification exists in Colombian law. Dividends distributed to foreign shareholders remain subject to shareholder-level withholding taxation.

Available election forms

Colombia has no US-style check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch election. The only available statutory elections are: (1) the optional Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) for entities with prior-year gross income not exceeding 105,000 UVT (approximately COP 4,941,825,000 at the 2024 UVT of COP 47,065), which replaces income tax, VAT, and industry-and-commerce tax with a single composite payment; (2) the consolidated income-tax filing election for qualifying economic groups (matriz and subordinadas) under Act 2010/2019; and (3) the annual inflation-adjustment election. None of these alter the fundamental entity-level taxation of the S.A.S.; SIMPLE is typically unattractive for a licensed, high-risk DG forwarding entity with substantial insured customs-brokerage revenue, so the default common regime applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold applies: any person or entity performing taxable supplies in Colombia must obtain the RUT/NIT and register as a VAT-responsible party regardless of revenue volume. The only monetary threshold conditions voluntary entry into the SIMPLE regime (prior-year gross income ≤ 105,000 UVT, approximately COP 4,941,825,000 at 2024 UVT); the former simplified VAT regime for small taxpayers was abolished by Act 1819/2016, so this S.A.S. operates under the common VAT regime in all cases.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible goods physically exported from Colombia are excluded from IVA under E.T. art. 481, and input VAT borne in producing or handling those goods generates a refundable VAT balance. Services qualify for zero-rating as export of services under E.T. art. 481 num. 8 and Decree 1625/2016 arts. 1.6.1.12.25–1.6.1.12.55 only upon cumulative satisfaction of: (i) the recipient being a foreign person or entity without Colombian domicile or a Colombian permanent establishment; (ii) the service being actually used or commercially exploited abroad; (iii) the service not being re-invoiced by the foreign recipient to a Colombian-domiciled party; and (iv) compliance with the foreign-exchange channelization regime. For this DG forwarding S.A.S., zero-rating applies to freight-forwarding and dangerous-goods logistics services rendered to foreign principals (overseas NVOCCs, offshore cargo owners) with foreign use; services sold to Colombian-domiciled exporters solely to facilitate their shipments are domestically taxable at 19%. International carriage of cargo by air or sea commencing in Colombia also qualifies for the zero-rated export-services basket when the same conditions are met.

  • Electronic Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación — DEX) filed through VUCE/MUISCA and validated by DIAN customs systems at the exit customs office
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (AWB) issued to the exporter/shipper evidencing the physical export movement
  • Electronic Export Invoice (Factura Electrónica de Exportación) bearing the DIAN-generated CUFE and explicitly evidencing the zero-rated supply
  • Cargo Manifest (Manifiesto de Carga) endorsed by customs at the seaport or airport of departure
  • Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas — the foreign-exchange declaration for exports (Formulario No. 1) issued by the authorized exchange-market intermediary, proving timely repatriation and channelization of export proceeds
  • Dangerous-goods compliance file: IMDG/IMO, IATA-DGR or ICAO-TI DG declaration, competent-authority permits for regulated hazardous chemicals (Ministry of Mines, Ministry of Health, IDEAM, or DIMAR as applicable), and the DG packing/container-vehicle certificate

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta — FEV) is in force for all taxpayers under Resolution DIAN 000165/2023. Invoices must be generated through DIAN-authorized software or DIAN's free e-invoicing channel, digitally signed with a DIAN-validated electronic certificate, and transmitted to DIAN in near real time for fiscal clearance; DIAN responds with a unique validation code (CUFE or QR CUDE) without which the invoice has no fiscal legal effect. Export invoices (Factura Electrónica de Exportación) are transmitted under the same regime. Failure to issue, or issuing irregular, invoices triggers fines under E.T. art. 652 (up to 1% of prior-year gross income for systematic non-issuance, plus per-invoice penalties) and can entail temporary closure of the establishment.

DIAN cross-validates the DEX, CUFE-verified export invoices, customs exit records, and Banco de la República foreign-exchange settlement data before any VAT refund is released. Suspected zero-rating abuse triggers: (a) a material reassessment reinstating 19% IVA on the proceeds plus moratory interest (E.T. art. 634); (b) a 10% penalty where the refund was improperly granted (E.T. art. 670); (c) an inexactness penalty of 100% of the over-refunded amount, increased to 200% when fraudulent documents or simulated transactions are shown (E.T. art. 648); (d) exchange-regime fines under Resolución 8/2000 where export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline (extensions to a maximum of 12 months may be authorized), ranging up to 200% of the un-channelized amount; and (e) criminal tax fraud under E.T. art. 671, punishable by 48–108 months of imprisonment, where the improper refund exceeds 100 SMLMV (approximately COP 142,350,000 at the 2025 minimum wage). DIAN's assessment period is extended to 10 years in fraud cases under E.T. art. 714, making retrospective clawbacks of fraudulently obtained export zero-rating refunds highly probable.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a unified managed-float foreign-exchange market with no dual or tiered rate. The Colombian peso (COP) is fully convertible for current-account operations: cross-border freight-forwarding service revenue for the CO_SAS DG entity must be channeled through an authorized exchange intermediary and reported via the Declaración de Cambio, but no prior authorizations or quantitative limits apply. Capital-account transactions, specifically foreign-direct-investment capital contributions into the S.A.S., are freely permitted but must be channeled through the Mercado Cambiario and registered with Banco de la República under the foreign-investment registration rules (Formulario No. 2 - Inversiones Internacionales). Profit remittance is legally available once the foreign investment has been properly registered and after DIAN withholding taxes on dividends have been settled; the practical hurdle is documentation integrity, because the bank and the Central Bank require a complete underlying file (contract, commercial invoice, airway bill or bill of lading, DG declaration, insurance certificate, and proof of payment) before the Declaración de Cambio is accepted. Misclassification or incomplete documentation can result in rejected filings, frozen funds, administrative fines, and loss of tax deductibility for the underlying costs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia imposes a channeling and repatriation obligation rather than a forced conversion or surrender obligation. For export of goods, proceeds must be received in the Mercado Cambiario within six (6) months from the date of the export customs declaration (DEX), with certain extensions available under Decree 1951 of 2020. For export of services, including cross-border freight-forwarding fees of the CO_SAS, the statutory channeling term is six (6) months following the accrual or receipt date of the service compensation, as evidenced by the contractual terms. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%, meaning the exporter may elect to keep foreign currency in a foreign-currency account with an authorized local bank (cuenta en moneda extranjera), sell it on the spot market, or, if holding funds abroad, use a previously authorized compensation account (cuenta de compensación). All inflows must be declared on the applicable Declaración de Cambio form, and the underlying evidence must be retained for the statutory file period of five (5) years.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (central bank), acting through its Junta Directiva and the Comisión de Cambios (Foreign Exchange Commission), with technical administration by the Departamento de Cambios Internacionales. The applicable framework comprises Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, Resolution 8 of 2000 of the Junta Directiva, Decree 1951 of 2020, and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83. Authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries (intermediarios del mercado cambiario) are supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC), while customs-linked flows and export/import documentation are overseen by DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales).

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividend distributions made to non-resident legal entities or foreign resident individuals out of profits that have already been taxed at the entity level. Colombian-resident corporate shareholders are generally exempt from dividend withholding. The total blended tax burden on distributed Colombian-source profits to a non-resident shareholder is approximately 41.5% (35% corporate + 10% dividend withholding).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The statutory ceiling is six months after the fiscal year-end. DIAN publishes an annual filing calendar by taxpayer NIT ending digit; for a 31 December fiscal year this calendar normally runs from April 10 through June 24 of the following year. Large taxpayers are assigned earlier dates in April.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File documentation under Articles 260-1 and 260-5 E.T. is triggered when the Colombian taxpayer's gross income or net assets exceed 100,000 UVT (COP 4,706,500,000 for tax year 2024, where UVT = COP 47,065), or when intercompany operations with foreign related parties exceed 10,000 UVT. Multinational groups with consolidated revenue above 600,000 UVT (COP 28,239,000,000) must also prepare a Master File. Country-by-Country notification applies where group revenue exceeds EUR 750 million. For FY2023 and FY2024 these amounts are annually indexed by DIAN.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime like Caribbean or Gulf jurisdictions, but the law contains powerful substance-over-form and anti-avoidance doctrines. Article 36 of Law 1258 of 2008 allows courts and DIAN to 'pierce the veil' of an S.A.S. when it is used as a mere instrumentality for tax evasion or shareholder fraud. DIAN may also disregard transactions lacking commercial rationality under the general anti-evasion clause introduced by Law 1819 of 2016. A DG freight-forwarding SAS must maintain actual personnel, office facilities, transport contracts, DG permits, and documentary proof of operational decision-making in Colombia; a nominal office recording invoices for an offshore principal will be recharacterized as shell-company profit shifting.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines and imprisonment under Article 434 Bis of the Colombian Criminal Code (Law 599/2000): 48 to 108 months imprisonment when evasion exceeds 250 current monthly legal minimum wages. DIAN also imposes a 100% inaccuracy penalty on the omitted tax amount, plus default interest, and may order asset embargoes to secure the tax obligation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct the fraudulent declaration become jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt plus penalties. Travel bans, precautionary detention, and prohibition from commercial activity may be imposed during the criminal investigation. Civil restitution and disqualification from serving as director of regulated transport or logistics entities also apply.
Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Veil-piercing under Article 36 of Law 1258 of 2008, with DIAN disregarding the entity's separate existence and reallocating income to the beneficial shareholder or parent. In addition, the taxpayer faces the 100% inaccuracy penalty, default interest, and forced liquidation of the shell for failing to obtain and keep the required dangerous-goods transport license. Regulatory authorities may impose fines equivalent to 10,000 current legal monthly minimum wages.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors may be personally liable for the entire tax deficiency, unpaid social security, and creditor claims. A court may order asset forfeiture, exit bans, and direct restitution to the treasury. Criminal prosecution for money laundering is possible when the shell is used to obscure charges for illegal hazmat shipments.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: DIAN recharacterization of the non-arm's-length transaction, upward adjustment of Colombian taxable income, application of the 100% inaccuracy penalty on the additional tax assessed, plus default interest. Failure to present transfer-pricing documentation or to file the mandatory annex triggers autonomous fines of up to 20,000 UVT or 1% of the related-party transaction value, whichever is lower.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve a sham intercompany fee schedule are jointly liable for the resulting tax deficiency and penalties. For wilful manipulation, criminal sanctions for evasion or conspiracy apply. The commercial registry may also be suspended after an administrative declaration of non-compliance.
High-Risk Hazmat License and AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Immediate suspension or cancellation of the DG transport authorization, fines of up to COP 1,900,000,000 under transport regulations, and seizure of the involved cargo or vessels. Criminal liability arises under Articles 323 and 340 of the Criminal Code for financing illicit transport of hazardous substances, with sentences from 8 to 20 years and asset-expense seizure under Law 1708 of 2014.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face personal criminal indictment, travel bans, prolonged pretrial detention, and civil liability to third parties damaged by a DG incident or AML breach. International mutual legal assistance may trigger Interpol red notices. In addition, directors become jointly liable for fines imposed on the SAS when they knowingly operated without authorization.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up share capital for a Cypriot private limited company; incorporation may proceed with one issued share of €1.00. For bankability and operational scale a paid-up capital of €2,000–€10,000 is recommended. No escrow account or capital deposit requirement applies at incorporation under Cap. 113.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Tax Department (Tmima Forologias, Ministry of Finance), acting through the Commissioner of Taxation; taxpayer portal: TAXISnet (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy); export clearance matters: Department of Customs and Excise (https://www.mof.gov.cy/ce).

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus HE77 Private Limited Company is incorporated via the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (https://efiling.drcor.mof.gov.cy). Upon incorporation, corporate data is transmitted to the Cyprus Tax Department, which automatically issues a Tax Identification Code (TIC). VAT registration is a separate mandatory step: a taxable person supplying goods or services in Cyprus must register once taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600 in any rolling 12-month period or when such exceedance is expected; optional registration is permitted from EUR 10,000. A non-resident company making taxable supplies in Cyprus must register without any turnover threshold and, if it has no EU/EEA establishment, must appoint a fiscal representative approved by the Commissioner and may be required to provide security. The VAT number issued is verified electronically via VIES for intra-EU transactions.

Available election forms

Cyprus has no US-style check-the-box election; a Cyprus LTD is by law the CIT and VAT taxable entity. Available statutory elections and claims include: (i) group relief loss surrender within a 75% group; (ii) notional interest deduction (NID) on new equity contributions; (iii) IP Box 80% deduction for qualifying intangible income; (iv) tonnage tax election for eligible shipping entities; (v) VAT group registration and option to tax certain exempt supplies such as immovable property; (vi) ATAD hybrid mismatch neutralizing rules in the Income Tax Law (L.118(I)/2002), as amended, apply automatically without an elective blocker.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to: (1) direct exports of multilayer aligner sheets by the Cyprus manufacturer to non-EU destinations, provided the goods physically leave the EU customs territory within 3 months of the time of supply and the supplier obtains and retains proof of export; (2) intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered customers where the customer's VAT number is valid under VIES and the goods are transported to another member state within 3 months, with the supply declared on the EC Sales List; (3) certain export-related services whose place of supply is outside Cyprus. Zero-rated exports carry full input VAT deduction and any excess credit is refunded in the periodic VAT return. Records supporting the zero-rating claim must be retained for at least 6 years.

  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document lodged via Cyprus Customs REF-ICS/AES)
  • Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), or CMR (road) evidencing that the goods left the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 confirming receipt of export proceeds from the non-EU buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • VAT invoice issued at 0% with a zero-rating annotation and, for intra-EU supplies, the customer's validated VIES VAT number

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, or continuous transaction controls are in force as of 2026; Cyprus operates a post-audit e-invoicing model. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory through the PEPPOL-compliant Cyprus National e-Invoicing system per Directive 2014/55/EU (Law 109(I)/2021). B2B e-invoicing is voluntary. Under the EU VAT-in-the-Digital-Age (ViDA) package agreed in 2025, Cyprus will be obliged to mandate structured e-invoicing and digital reporting for intra-EU B2B supplies by 2030. VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and Intrastat declarations are filed electronically via TAXISnet.

The Tax Department subjects export zero-rating to strict audit scrutiny, requiring proof of export within 3 months, VIES validation for intra-EU supplies, and functional supplier substance. A failed zero-rating claim is recharacterized as a taxable supply at 19% from the time of supply, with interest at 2% per month (24% per annum) and additional tax of 10% of the assessed VAT. Negligent or fraudulent claims attract a special additional tax of up to 100% of the tax shortfall plus criminal penalties (fines up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years) under Law 95(I)/2000. Assessments may be issued within 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or wilful omission, producing retrospective clawback of VAT, denial of input VAT credits, and adverse VIES/export flagging. Note: Cyprus has no statutory FX-repatriation deadline following euro adoption; the 180-day benchmark is the customary audit standard that distinguishes genuine trade receivables from deemed loans or distributions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus adopted the euro on 1 January 2008 and maintains full current and capital account convertibility under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, IMF Article VIII obligations and the Cypriot Exchange Control Law framework, which is effectively deregulated. No exchange-control licences, prior authorisations, repatriation permits or conversion quotas apply to trade settlements, dividends, interest, royalties or capital movements. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is unrestricted; Cyprus does not impose withholding tax on outbound dividends under domestic law (subject to substance and anti-abuse rules, e.g., the 12.5% corporate income tax regime and transparent Cypriot tax-residence structures). Residual hurdles are bank-level AML/KYC source-of-funds checks, not statutory FX controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios or repatriation deadlines for export proceeds in Cyprus or the wider euro area. Exporters may retain 100% of export receipts in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency in Cyprus-based or offshore bank accounts for an indefinite period. The only binding obligations are (i) threshold-based balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the Central Bank of Cyprus under ECB statistical regulations, and (ii) documentary retention for AML/trade-compliance purposes, including commercial invoices, customs export declarations, transport documents and SWIFT credit advices. Treasury practice of converting surplus USD export receipts into EUR is discretionary and commercially driven, not legally mandated.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC), the national monetary authority of the Republic of Cyprus and an integral member of the Eurosystem, jointly with the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy; CBC also acts as the competent supervisory authority for credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic money institutions in Cyprus.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. CySEC-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the Electronic Money and Payment Services Law of 2018 (Law 31(I)/2018) and EU-passported fintech EMIs (e.g., Lithuanian or Estonian-licensed virtual IBAN providers) can legally open multi-currency settlement accounts for CY_LTD without physical presence and provide card acquiring, FX conversion and API-based payment rails. Such EMI rails are viable as auxiliary clearing layers for Stripe/platform credit-card capture, vendor pay-outs and small-value operational disbursements. However, EMIs are not a substitute for a Tier-1 credit institution: they lack commercial letter-of-credit capacity, trade finance product lines and regulated custody-grade treasury services, and most Cypriot banks apply de-risking that prevents an EMI from acting as the sole account intermediary for a manufacturing exporter. The optimal blueprint is a primary direct account at Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank plus one EMI for digital payment agility, subject to a EUR 50,000 per-transaction ceiling and daily sweep to the primary account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder equity or intra-group funding must be routed as a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from the shareholder's own bank account in the shareholder's country of tax residence, using ISO 20022 purpose code INTC (intra-company payment) for shareholder loans or SHAR (share capital) for paid-in equity, with the company registration number HE77 and a board-resolution reference in the remittance narrative. The receiving Cypriot bank applies no exchange-control approval but performs AML source-of-funds verification for single transactions above EUR 50,000; the standard verification pack includes a notarised board resolution, register-of-members extract, source-of-funds dossier (audited shareholder statements, salary records or asset-disposal legal contracts) and a completed CBC customer risk questionnaire. USD-denominated injections should be credited to a segregated USD settlement account and converted to EUR using an outright spot or forward contract above EUR 100,000 equivalents to protect against adverse FX fluctuation and preserve clean audit provenance for dividend payments.The governing clearing rails are: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for intra-EU supplier payments, warehouse costs and platform refunds; (ii) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers with purpose code TRAD for non-EU trade invoices below USD 100,000 equivalent; (iii) irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit governed by ICC UCP 600 for high-value imports of PETG/PU/thermoformable multilayer film from Asia, with the documentary set comprising bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin and pre-shipment inspection certificate; (iv) direct card-scheme settlement of consumer e-commerce sales through Stripe and the platform credit-card aggregator into the Bank of Cyprus EUR operating account, followed by an automated monthly treasurer conversion of USD card receipts into EUR; and (v) SEPA Direct Debit standing instructions for fixed operating expenses. Annual Return (Form HE32) under Section 114, Companies Law Cap. 113 Within 42 days after the anniversary of incorporation; filing required at least once in each calendar year $0 USD Fines on the company and each officer in default; persistent default entitles the Registrar to commence administrative strike-off and dissolution proceedings.
Annual Company Levy (annual fee for all registered Cyprus companies) Due every year by 30 June; no pro-rating for newly incorporated companies $382 USD Late payment attracts statutory penalties and loss of good standing; continued arrears for five successive years exposes the company to Registrar-initiated strike-off and dissolution.
Annual Beneficial Ownership Confirmation (UBO declaration) Annual confirmation due by 30 June; changes in beneficial ownership, including share transfers, must be filed within 30 days of the change $0 USD Non-compliance is a criminal offense; the company and responsible officers are liable to fines and imprisonment, and the Registrar may strike off the company after statutory notice.
Corporate Income Tax Return (Form TD4) and annual audited financial statements under the Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002 Within six months after the end of the tax year; for calendar-year accounting periods the statutory deadline is 30 June of the following year $0 USD Late submission subjects the company to surcharge and interest on unpaid tax, specified fixed penalties for non-filing, and potential criminal sanctions for fraudulent or negligent default.

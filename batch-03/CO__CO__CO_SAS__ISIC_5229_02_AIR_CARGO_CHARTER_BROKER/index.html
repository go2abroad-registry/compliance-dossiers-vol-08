Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – official portal: www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain a local tax representative or appoint a legal representative resident in Colombia, then register through DIAN's online portal (Museo Virtual / PIDE) to obtain the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) and the Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT). The registration requires documentary proof of the company's existence, power of attorney, and the representative's identification. The RUT is the single tax identification document for all tax, customs, and exchange purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must first obtain a local tax representative or appoint a legal representative resident in Colombia, then register through DIAN's online portal (Museo Virtual / PIDE) to obtain the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) and the Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT). The registration requires documentary proof of the company's existence, power of attorney, and the representative's identification. The RUT is the single tax identification document for all tax, customs, and exchange purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not provide a check-the-box election; all SAS entities are treated as separate taxable persons. However, certain hybrid mismatch rules and group taxation (consolidation) electable in limited circumstances may apply. A SAS may choose to operate under the standard corporate income tax regime without pass-through eligibility.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for corporations; every legal entity, including SAS, must register for IVA purposes through the RUT before commencing any taxable activity. Natural persons under the simplified regime may have a threshold, but corporate entities are always registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and certain international services are zero-rated (0% IVA). To qualify, the taxpayer must be an IVA-registered exporter, evidence the actual departure of goods from Colombian customs territory (or the provision of services used exclusively abroad), and file the corresponding export declaration with DIAN and the customs authority. For air cargo charter brokerage services, the service must be performed for overseas principals or related to goods destined for export.

  • Declaración de Exportación (Customs Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (issued by the carrier)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas)

Colombia operates a mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) system administered by DIAN. All IVA-registered taxpayers must issue invoices through DIAN-authorized technological providers or DIAN's free invoicing application. Invoices must be transmitted in near-real-time using XML/CUFE/CUDE formats, include a QR code and digital signature, and comply with the API technical integration requirements. Customs and export documentation must also be processed through DIAN's electronic systems.

DIAN maintains strict audit scrutiny over export zero-rating claims. If the export documentation or foreign-currency repatriation requirements are not satisfied or are found fraudulent, DIAN may retroactively claw back all IVA credits claimed, impose fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax, apply default interest, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution for tax fraud. The limitation period for DIAN assessments is generally five years from the date the tax return was filed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is fully convertible for current-account transactions including trade in services, royalties, interest, dividends, and cross-border broker commissions; Colombia operates a unified free-exchange regime without separate capital/current-account convertibility tiers. Profit remittances are freely permitted after Colombian-source withholding taxes are settled (dividend remittance WHT at the general 5% rate for non-residents under applicable treaty/domestic rules, plus any additional surtaxes per Law 2277 of 2022), and no prior central-bank authorization is required provided the proceeds are channeled through an authorized intermediary and the underlying foreign investment is duly registered. Capital-account entries (equity injections, loans, guarantees) require registration with Banco de la República via the applicable foreign-exchange declaration forms (e.g., Formulario 3 for international investment); currency convertibility is not rationed or restricted by exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender ratio and no compulsory conversion percentage (0%); exporters may lawfully retain and hold foreign currency. However, 100% of export proceeds arising from goods and services, including air cargo charter brokerage commissions, must be channeled through Colombia's regulated foreign-exchange market within statutory deadlines: for goods, within 6 months from the date of the export declaration; for services, within 6 months from the date the service is performed or the payment obligation accrues; extensions are subject to Banco de la República authorization. Valid channeling methods are (i) sale of the foreign currency to an authorized intermediary, (ii) deposit into a foreign-currency bank account maintained in Colombia, or (iii) application against authorized foreign-currency obligations; each operation must be evidenced by the corresponding exchange declaration (Formulario 1 for exports of goods/services).

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), jointly with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (Financial Superintendency) as prudential regulator and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales - DIAN (National Tax and Customs Directorate) as exchange-control administrator; the regulated foreign exchange market (mercado cambiario) may only operate through locally authorized intermediaries.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary corporate treasury rail; MEDIUM only as an ancillary collection arrangement. Foreign digital EMI/neobanks (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Revolut Business) are not authorized intermediaries of Colombia's regulated foreign-exchange market; routing export proceeds, trade settlements, or capital injections through such entities instead of a Colombian authorized intermediary constitutes an exchange-control infraction (Decree 1730 of 1991, External Resolution 1 of 2018) and may result in fines imposed by DIAN/Banco de la República. These EMIs also cannot issue the Formulario 3 registration for foreign direct investment or provide TRM-referenced COP conversion certificates. Colombian native digital banks (Lulo Bank, Nu Colombia, and Bancolombia's digital corporate onboarding) may serve remote account-opening needs, but all statutory FX channeling and cross-border clearing must remain with tier-1 authorized intermediaries.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends paid to non-residents from profits already taxed at the Colombian entity level are subject to a 10% withholding tax. If the distribution exceeds taxed retained earnings and is sourced from untaxed or specially exempt profits, the distribution is subject to an additional 35% withholding tax on the non-taxed portion, in combination with the basic 10% dividend WHT. A DTA may reduce the domestic 10% rate, historically to 0%–5%, depending on the treaty partner.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation is required when the Colombian taxpayer’s gross income or gross equity at the end of the previous tax year equals or exceeds 100,000 UVT (approx. COP 4,700 million for tax year 2024). Transactions with related parties below this threshold still must be documented for reasonableness, and an abbreviated transfer-pricing annex applies when cross-border transactions exceed 50,000 UVT. Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational groups consolidating more than COP 4 trillion in annual revenues.

Economic substance scope: There is no formal Economic Substance Regulations regime. However, DIAN and the Superintendencia de Sociedades apply the doctrines of simulation, sham transaction, abuse of corporate form, and anti-fragmentation to disregard a charter broker that lacks employees, premises, aircraft contracts, bank accounts, commercial invoices, and decision-making in Colombia. A foreign entity claiming treaty relief must show substance in its jurisdiction and pass the OECD/Latin American beneficial owner test; otherwise the dividend, royalty, or interest payment is recharacterised and the WHT benefit is denied with reverse penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Underreporting Charter Brokerage Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under articles 434A and 434B of the Colombian Criminal Code, as amended by Law 1819 of 2016, carries imprisonment of 48–108 months and fines equal to 20% of the evaded tax. The DIAN may also apply an administrative inexactitude penalty of up to 160% of the omitted tax, plus daily default interest and compulsory collection.

Director personal liability: Under Law 1258 of 2008, the corporate veil can be pierced if the SAS is used as an instrument of fraud. Shareholders, sole director, and legal representative who ordered or knew of the evasion become personally liable for the corporate tax debt, face travel bans, submission of passports, and possible extradition arrest in international money-laundering investigations.
Shell Beneficial Ownership – Nominee Shareholders and Hidden Principals

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the corporate structure and reallocate the charter and brokerage income to the real beneficiary, applying the 35% corporate tax plus penalty of up to 160%. Under Colombian AML law, failure to identify and report the ultimate beneficial owner to the UIAF is an autonomous administrative offence with fines reaching thousands of UVTs.

Director personal liability: Directors and notaries signing false beneficial-ownership documentation commit the criminal offence of falsedad en documento privado, punishable by imprisonment of 5–10 years. Courts may freeze the director's personal assets and impose an Interpol red notice or travel ban if the director leaves the jurisdiction.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation – Intercompany Charter Markups

Statutory sanction: DIAN adjusts the transaction to the arm's length price, collecting the 35% income tax on the adjustment, default interest, and transfer-pricing penalties. The penalty for failure to file the required Local/Master File or TP annex can be up to 5% of the transaction value, in addition to the inexactitude penalty on the adjusted income.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally negotiated or approved the inflated intercompany rates are joint debtors for the tax debt if the SAS was used to defraud creditors. The Superintendencia de Sociedades can impose administrative fines, and criminal liability may arise when fraudulent documentation was used to support the abnormal prices.
Money Laundering and Embargo Violations in Air Cargo Charter

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under article 323 of the Colombian Criminal Code imposes imprisonment of 9–15 years and a fine of 500 to 50,000 minimum monthly wages for the individuals found criminally responsible. Administrative fines by the UIAF can exceed COP 10,000 million and the Aeronáutica Civil may revoke the required air cargo brokerage licence.

Director personal liability: A director who authorised the wire transfer or knowingly signed cargo documents participates personally in the offence. Corporate veil protection is expressly unavailable for fraud and gross negligence; the director faces extradition risk, individual bank account sealing, and cancellation of residency if a foreign national.
Repatriation Evasion through Disguised Loans or Fees

Statutory sanction: DIAN recharacterises the transaction as a dividend or taxable royalty and collects the avoided withholding tax plus a 100% penalty for fraud or an inexactitude penalty up to 160%. Default interest is computed daily on the unpaid amount from the statutory due date.

Director personal liability: The foreign parent's officers who caused the Colombian manager to route payments in a fraudulent manner are liable under Colombian administrative law for unpaid withholding taxes; the Colombian director may be prevented from leaving Colombia through a judicial travel ban and may be personally fined under the company law duties of loyalty and diligence.

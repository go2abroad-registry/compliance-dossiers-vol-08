Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); online portal: MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) at www.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

A Colombian SAS must complete (i) incorporation before the Cámara de Comercio, which electronically transmits the registral data and obtains automatic issuance of the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), and (ii) formal registration in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) via DIAN's MUISCA portal or an authorized service center. Required items: certified articles of incorporation (certificado de existencia y representación legal), legal representative identification (valid Colombian cédula or foreign passport with local immigration status), local registered office address, description of economic activity (ISIC 7020 accepted), and evidence of non-resident shareholder status for foreign owners. RUT must be kept current and may be completed online for standard cases; no minimum capital is required for the SAS.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Colombian SAS must complete (i) incorporation before the Cámara de Comercio, which electronically transmits the registral data and obtains automatic issuance of the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), and (ii) formal registration in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) via DIAN's MUISCA portal or an authorized service center. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not operate a check-the-box or US-style disregarded entity election. The SAS may irrevocably elect taxation under the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) if annual gross income does not exceed 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 174,296,500 for tax year 2025) and the activity qualifies; it may also elect the special VAT regime provisions applicable to exported services. No hybrid mismatch elections are available; BEPS Action 2 outcomes are addressed through domestic CFC and permanent establishment rules under Laws 1943/2018 and 2010/2019, which impose mandatory disclosure on aggressive tax structures rather than elective treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for companies: every SAS engaged in taxable transactions in Colombia must obtain NIT/RUT and register as a VAT-responsible taxpayer ab initio, irrespective of volume. The only proportional threshold is the SIMPLE regime opt-in limit of 3,500 UVT for small taxpayers, not a VAT registration exemption; VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable operation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods physically dispatched from Colombian customs territory qualifies for 0% VAT (exclusion from the taxable base). Cross-border M&A advisory services (ISIC 7020) qualify as export of services excluded from IVA when: (i) the service is rendered to a non-resident beneficiary without domicile in Colombia, (ii) the service is effectively used or exploited outside Colombia, and (iii) the SAS maintains documentary evidence of both facts in accordance with Decree 1625 of 2016, Article 1.6.1.13.2 et seq. The SAS must retain VAT-responsible status and continue filing periodic returns even when all transactions are zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX) filed through DIAN/SICE customs system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exit of goods from Colombian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and foreign currency payment proof (SWIFT MT103 from the overseas client)
  • RUT and the relevant VAT return (F-300) demonstrating zero-rating treatment
  • Advisory services contract / engagement letter linking the service to the foreign beneficiary and to use/exploitation outside Colombia

Mandatory, statute-driven electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta). All invoices must be transmitted in real time to DIAN for 'validación previa' (prior validation) before delivery to the client; DIAN assigns a CUDE (Código Único de Documento Electrónico) and a machine-readable digital stamp (QR code). Taxpayers must use DIAN-authorised software providers or DIAN's free electronic invoicing application; API/SFTP integration is compulsory for taxpayers exceeding DIAN's volume thresholds. An invoice that fails DIAN validation is legally ineffective for cost deduction and input VAT credit purposes, and late or non-transmission triggers daily fines under Articles 615 and 652-13 of the Estatuto Tributario.

DIAN subjects zero-rating claims to rigorous documentary and transactional audit. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating triggers: retroactive VAT assessment on the full invoice amount, monthly default interest (currently 1.5% per month), inaccuracy penalty (sanción por inexactitud) of up to 200% of the tax involved under Articles 647 of the Estatuto Tributario as amended by Law 2277 of 2022, customs fines, and potential seizure of goods for false export declarations. Where intent is proven, criminal prosecution under Articles 434B and 435 of the Código Penal and anti-smuggling Law 1762 of 2015 may result in imprisonment, disqualification of the legal representative, cancellation of export benefits, and clawback of any VAT refunds previously received, with interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account operations, but every cross-border flow must be 'canalizada' through authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries registered with Banco de la República. M&A advisory fees and success fees arising from cross-border services are treated as services-export proceeds and must be channeled back into Colombia through the foreign exchange market. Profit remittances are permissible after corporate income tax has been satisfied, but require proof of distributable earnings, shareholder approval, filing of the applicable foreign-exchange declaration, and compliance with dividend withholding tax rules. Capital contributions by non-resident shareholders are classified as foreign direct investment and require registration with Banco de la República; such registration is a precondition for subsequent dividend remittance and exit transactions. No blanket prohibition on profit repatriation exists, but delays or procedural omissions in FX declarations can trigger sanctions and loss of access to the official exchange market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds, including M&A advisory fees from foreign clients, must be repatriated through an authorized Colombian foreign-exchange intermediary within the statutory six-month term prescribed under the Colombian exchange regime measured from the invoice or completion milestone for services. There is no mandatory fixed-percentage conversion into Colombian pesos; the exporter may retain the funds in a foreign-currency-denominated bank account located in Colombia provided the proceeds have been properly channeled and the corresponding electronic 'Declaración de Cambio' has been submitted through the receiving bank. Failures to channel, late repatriation, or monetization outside the authorized foreign-exchange market constitute exchange-control violations subject to fines and administrative penalties. The Colombian banking intermediary is required to verify the export nature of the proceeds and assign the correct purpose code before accepting the remittance.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (the Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit and the Financial Superintendence of Colombia (Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia) as the competent authority under the Colombian Foreign Exchange Regime (Régimen de Cambios Internacionales), principally contained in Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, and Banco de la República Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability as the primary treasury rail. A Colombian S.A.S. may legally open and use accounts with offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, or Dukascopy, but such entities are not authorized Colombian foreign-exchange intermediaries and cannot process exports, capital injections, or regulated FX declarations. Offshore EMIs may serve only as auxiliary cards or disbursement channels for incidental expenses, provided all underlying Colombian-source operations are documented and mirrored through a local regulated bank. Heavy reliance on an EMI for inbound client fees or outbound success-fee payments will be treated as an AML red flag by Colombian banking partners and may constitute a breach of the mandatory channeling regime under Decree 1735 of 1993.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign share capital injections into the Colombian S.A.S. must be received as SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the shareholder's foreign bank account to the entity's USD or COP operating account maintained at a local commercial bank. The local bank must classify the remittance using the appropriate foreign-exchange purpose code for 'foreign direct investment in companies' and issue the corresponding electronic foreign-exchange declaration ('Declaración de Cambio') to Banco de la República. If the incoming amount constitutes a registered foreign investment, the S.A.S. must complete the foreign-investment registration with Banco de la República using the proper investment-registration form and maintain proof for future remittances. The instructing bank must include a clear payment narrative referencing the entity's NIT (tax identification number), the corporate purpose, the shareholder's name, and the capital subscription agreement. Inbound amounts below the minimum registration threshold may be received through the simplified channel of the local bank, but the forming documentation and board resolutions must still evidence that funds are equity, not debt, and not proceeds needing export-channeling treatment.Outbound disbursements for cross-border M&A advisory engagements, including commissions to sub-agents, external legal counsel, valuation firms, or the return of client monies, must be cleared through the entity's local regulated bank using SWIFT MT103 or equivalent correspondent banking rails. The payment must be classified under the correct foreign-exchange service code and accompanied by a signed commercial invoice, engagement contract, or disbursement instruction demonstrating the purpose of the transaction. The bank will generate the corresponding foreign-exchange declaration for exit reporting. Withholding taxes and the bank debit tax (Gravamen a los Movimientos Financieros) must be settled at the time of payment when applicable. Commercial letters of credit are not a viable or appropriate clearing rail for M&A advisory fees; they should be used solely for goods transactions. For deterministic milestone payments, SWIFT MT103 provides real-time traceability, payment confirmation, and matching with the invoice reference.FX forward contracts with authorized Colombian foreign-exchange intermediaries, limited to hedging of documented current-account exposures arising from cross-border M&A advisory receivables or payables.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in COP/USD referencing the official Colombian peso exchange rate, provided the underlying exposure is genuine, quantifiable, and evidenced by an outstanding service agreement or receivable.Currency swaps executed with licensed Colombian banks or foreign banks through an authorized intermediary, used exclusively to hedge currency mismatches on cross-border transactions and not for speculative trading.FX options and cross-currency interest-rate swaps where expressly authorized by Banco de la República and executed under a duly documented hedging relationship with an underlying trade or investment exposure.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident beneficial owners of shares or quotas in a Colombian SAS. The rate is applied to distributions made from profit subject to corporate income tax. Dividends paid to shareholders residing in a non-cooperative / low-tax jurisdiction may be disqualified from the statutory treatment and subject to higher withholding and administrative re-characterization.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return is due according to the DIAN resolution-calendar, which for calendar-year taxpayers falls generally between mid-April and mid-May of the following fiscal year, based on the last two digits of the NIT. Taxpayers with a non-calendar fiscal year end use the deadlines set for such periods by DIAN, generally no later than three months after the close of the corresponding tax period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Colombian Transfer Pricing Documentation (Local File and, for groups, Master File) is triggered when, in a given fiscal year, related-party transactions exceed 10,000 UVT - COP 470,650,000 based on the 2024 UVT of COP 47,065 - or when the taxpayer's gross income and/or net wealth exceeds 125,000 UVT (approx COP 5,883,125,000). A separate declaration annex is due for taxpayers that engage in cross-border related-party transactions and satisfy the minima above.

Economic substance scope: Colombia has no independent Economic Substance Regulation in the BVI/SAMOA sense, but the tax authorities apply the general anti-abuse provisions (Articles 669-679 E.T.), under which an SAS engaged in M&A advisory must have real premises, qualified personnel, actual management decisions, and a rational functional profile in Colombia. A letter-box entity, a nominee board, or a contract routed solely through a foreign holding structure without operational substance will be disregarded or re-characterized for Colombian income tax purposes, DTA benefit denial, and potential CFC / transfer pricing adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False or Manipulated Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Imposition of a 100% monumento inaccuracy penalty, increased to 220% where an abusive tax advantage is obtained, plus moratory interest. Criminal conviction for tax evasion can lead to imprisonment of four to eight years and a fine calculated on the understated tax, under Colombian anti-evasion legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign a knowingly false return become jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax debt, interest and penalties. The tax administration may seek preventive embargo of assets, interrogation, and judicial travel bans; a criminal conviction triggers debarment from corporate management and forfeiture of ill-gotten proceeds.
Shell Company / Artificial Split of Activities

Statutory sanction: DIAN can apply the abuse-of-legal-form doctrine, disregard the entity, re-characterize revenues as the parent's income and dividend payments as repatriation subject to the 10% withholding plus the 35% corporate tax at the entity level, or impose the 100%-220% penalty. Failure to file or filing a fraudulent TP report adds an additional 25% incomplete-documentation fine.

Director personal liability: Under Colombian Tax Code provisions on liability of administrators, directors and shareholders who receive the diverted value can be held liable for the entity's unpaid tax and penalties. Director passports can be restricted, and civil lawsuits for restitution may attach because the corporate veil is pierced in cases of artificiality, contempt, or fraud.
AML / Cross-Border Bribery and Money Laundering through M&A Fees

Statutory sanction: Failure to adopt and execute an adequate SAGRILAFT/AML system by an obligated entity or individual can generate administrative fines of several thousand UVT and cancellation of required licenses (including the statutory M&A activity license). If the activity is prosecuted as money laundering, the criminal sanction includes substantial imprisonment (generally more than eight years), asset confiscation and fines proportional to the laundered value.

Director personal liability: Officers of the boutique who designed or ignored the AML protocols face direct criminal liability, personal fines, sequestration of personal assets, bans on leaving the jurisdiction and, upon conviction, imprisonment. The corporate form does not shield the managers from the prohibited conduct, and each officer may be personally ordered to disgorge any fees obtained from the illegally structured transaction.

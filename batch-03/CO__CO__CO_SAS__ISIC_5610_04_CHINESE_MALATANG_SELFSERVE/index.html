Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co and Sede Electrónica (MUISCA platform).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Colombian S.A.S. must: (1) execute and notarize the bylaws, then register the company with the Cámara de Comercio to obtain the commercial registration and the electronic 'firma electrónica' certificate; (2) appoint a legal representative domiciled in Colombia with apostilled and protocolized power of attorney; (3) file the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) pre-registration through the MUISCA portal, attaching the apostilled certificate of incorporation, the power of attorney, and any foreign investment registration with the Banco de la República; (4) obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) from DIAN and select the applicable tax responsibilities on the RUT, including 'Responsable de IVA' status for taxable sales and services; (5) complete the electronic invoicing habilitación process and issue invoices exclusively in the DIAN-approved electronic system before the first transaction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists for a Colombian S.A.S.; the entity is always taxed at entity level under Article 4 of Law 1258/2008. Available statutory elections include: (i) the elective Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) for eligible small taxpayers under Articles 905-915 ET, generally unsuitable for a royalty-licensing fast-casual franchise model; (ii) optional alignment of accounting closing and CIT filing to the calendar year; (iii) the election to treat qualifying offshore franchise fees and support services as zero-rated 'servicios exportados' under the special VAT export rules; and (iv) transfer-pricing simplification elections consistent with Colombian transfer-pricing documentation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold applies to VAT registration in Colombia; the statutory trigger is the habitual performance of taxable transactions (sale of goods or provision of services) within the national territory, irrespective of revenue volume. A foreign entity with a fixed place of business or performing taxable activities in Colombia must register in the RUT as 'Responsable de IVA' before the first taxable transaction. Non-resident digital service suppliers have a separate registration obligation under Decree 358/2020.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical exports of goods are excluded from IVA at the 0% rate and carry the right to obtain a refund of input VAT. Intangible services, including franchise royalties and software-support services, qualify as zero-rated 'servicios exportados' when cumulatively: (i) the service is rendered to a foreign recipient not established in Colombia; (ii) the benefit is realized and consumed outside Colombia; (iii) the service is not physically performed in connection with movable or immovable property located in Colombia; and (iv) the exporter maintains contractual, performance, transfer-pricing and foreign-exchange repatriation evidence. For the Malatang self-serve fast-casual franchise, local franchise royalties on Colombian store sales remain taxable at 19%, while qualifying offshore licensing and management-support fees may be zero-rated if the statutory export-service criteria are met.

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX) validated by DIAN customs for goods, or the documented service-export dossier for intangible services
  • Bill of Lading (maritime) or Airway Bill (air) evidencing the physical exit of goods, or the service delivery/consumption certificates for export services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / certificado de canalización de divisas issued by a Colombian intermediary financial institution evidencing repatriation through the foreign-exchange market

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing ('factura electrónica de venta') applies to all VAT-responsible businesses. Invoices must be generated in DIAN-approved XML format containing CUF and CUDE codes, transmitted immediately for online validation clearance to the DIAN through authorized software providers' SOAP/REST APIs, and display a scannable QR code. The DIAN must issue the acceptance ('acuse de recibo') or rejection ('tachamiento') event before the invoice acquires fiscal validity; deadlines for daily transmission are prescribed by DIAN. Purchases from non-obligated suppliers require the electronic 'documento soporte en adquisiciones a no obligados' filed and stored in the RADIAN archive.

DIAN audits export zero-rating claims strictly. The taxpayer inaccuracy penalty under Article 648 ET is 100% of the understated VAT, escalating to 200% for recurring or documentary-fraud patterns; improper refunds face the Article 674 ET penalty of 20% plus default interest. Retrospective clawbacks extend through the standard 4-year statute of limitations for filed returns, expanded to 6 or 8 years where a return was not filed or foreign assets/income were omitted. Criminal prosecution under Article 402(2) of the Colombian Criminal Code may apply for aggravated tax evasion involving forged export evidence. The taxpayer carries the burden of proving effective exportation, foreign consumption and FX repatriation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP, ISO 4217 code COP) is freely convertible for current-account transactions, including royalties, service fees, dividends, and profit remittances, provided the transactions are channeled through an authorized financial intermediary registered with Banco de la República. Capital-account convertibility is subject to a prior declaration of foreign direct investment (FDI) through the 'Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales' with the applicable statistical category codes. For an SAS engaged in a fast-casual Chinese malatang self-serve franchise, inbound equity or shareholder loan injections may be treated as FDI capital inflows, requiring registration with the Central Bank's 'Registro de Inversiones Internacionales' (Foreign Investment Register) within the statutory timeframe. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation to non-resident shareholders are generally unrestricted once taxes are paid and compliance formalities are met; however, remittance must be executed via an authorized FX intermediary and reported using the appropriate 'Declaración de Cambio' form. Repatriation obstacles may appear if the company fails to submit annual or semi-annual 'CERO' forms (offshore foreign investment updates) or if the original FDI registration was incomplete.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Colombia's foreign exchange regime (Régimen Cambiario), export proceeds from goods and services must be 'canalized' (surrendered or collected) through authorized FX intermediaries. For services exports, including franchise fees, royalties, and licensing income received by a Colombian entity from outside, the proceeds must be received in Colombia and reported via the 'Declaración de Cambio' within the general settlement deadline of six (6) months from the date of issue of the export invoice or the service billing, unless a specific extension is granted by Banco de la República. For goods exports (if any ancillary merchandise supplied to the franchise), the exporter must surrender 100% of the export value within the statutory term, normally six months, but DIAN/Banco de la República may apply shorter periods for perishable or certain identified operations. Mandatory conversion of COP from export proceeds into foreign currency is not required; the obligation is to channel funds through the controlled FX market and declare them, not to monetize into COP automatically. Failure to timely canalize triggers fines calculated by Banco de la República and potential adverse tax treatment under Colombian transfer pricing rules.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) together with the Comité de Cambios (Foreign Exchange Committee) and the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit. Operational enforcement is delegated to the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for a newly formed Colombian SAS. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) and virtual account providers are not recognized as authorized FX intermediaries under Colombian law and cannot settle 'Declaración de Cambio' obligations. To receive inbound FDI or pay cross-border trade disbursements in a compliant manner, funds must flow through a supervised financial institution (credit establishment or exchange house) registered before Banco de la República. Local Fintechs licensed as Sociedades Especializadas en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos (SEDPE) may provide operational payment accounts and POS aggregation, but they cannot independently satisfy statutory FX reporting for shareholder loans or royalty remittances. Therefore, the treasury architecture must combine a regulated Tier-1 bank for all FX and cross-border flows with a local licensed Fintech only for domestic low-value settlement (e.g., POS payments and small supplier payments).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound invested capital must be structured as direct foreign investment (inversión extranjera directa) received via SWIFT MT103 in COP or USD from the Chinese parent. The remitter must use a clear wire purpose: 'Shareholder Loan to Colombian Subsidiary' or 'Equity Contribution for Working Capital' as appropriate. On receipt, the local bank will require the submission of the 'Declaración de Cambio' using concept code '191' for equity capital, '192' for loan in foreign currency, or '193' for loan in legal tender from abroad, whichever corresponds. The bank credits the funds into a central operational account; at the same time the company must register the investment in Banco de la República's Investment Register within 30 calendar days of receipt. To avoid over-yielding against the 'capital escrow' classification, the account should clearly flag that the net proceeds are earmarked for approved expenditure only, and a redemption request (for liquidation) will require a 'Certificado de Devolución de Capital' from the registrant. For contracts, the parent should execute a zero-interest or market-rate shareholder loan agreement, evidencing term, principal amount, and repayment mechanics; all documentation must be apostilled in China and available in Spanish upon request.Outbound disbursements for equipment, spices, condiments, and commercial capital goods purchased from China or other suppliers will be executed through (1) standard SWIFT MT103 payments for advance payment terms, open account, or post-shipment settlement; (2) irrevocable and confirmed Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C at sight or usance) for significant production runs, where the Colombian SAS opens an import LC at Bancolombia or Banco de Bogotá and China counterparties negotiate against documents; and (3) documentary collections (D/P, D/A) for medium-risk trade. For intercompany royalties, disbursements are made via SWIFT MT103 using the payment description 'Royalties under License Agreement' and the relevant 'Declaración de Cambio' concept code for patent/trademark royalties (category 'Residenciados en el exterior – Regalías'). Cross-border clearing rails must always include a 'Tramitación de Operación' through the local bank's foreign trade desk, and each transaction must be paired with the corresponding DIAN import declaration (Declaración de Importación) or an exemption certificate when only ingressing software (as a service). Domestic outbound payments for rent, wages, utility, and POS settlements flow over the local ACH (CENIT), check, or bank debit routes, using the same COP account as the operational treasury account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: COP 7,766,000,000 (approximately USD 1.9 million) for fiscal year 2024; the Colombian threshold is 165,000 UVT (Unidad de Valor Tributario). A taxpayer whose total gross income or total assets exceed this threshold and who has intercompany transactions must prepare and file Local File documentation. Master File is also mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding the higher threshold.

Economic substance scope: Colombia applies a General Anti-Abuse Rule and the economic-substance doctrine. Franchise royalty structures that lack substantive local management, physical operations, actual employees, and a commercial purpose may be recharacterized by DIAN, and treaty benefits may be denied when the Colombian licensee or the foreign licensor lacks beneficiary ownership. The OECD/G20 minimum standards are also mirrored in Colombia's administrative rules, requiring a clear corporate purpose and real activity for any intra-group licence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine equal to 200% of the tax deficit (non-compensable with subsequent losses) plus statutory interest; criminal sanction of 6 to 9 years in prison under Article 426 of the Colombian Criminal Code for willful non-declaration of taxable income.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the defective return are jointly and severally liable for the fine. In criminal proceedings, they are subject to travel bans, disqualification from managing any legal entity, and up to 9 years of imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may make a secondary adjustment by applying the arm's-length price and impose a penalty equal to 200% of the understated tax, or a monthly penalty equal to 5% of the value of the transactions not disclosed in the required transfer pricing return, capped at 100% of the unreported amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve intercompany pricing without a compliant benchmarking study, or who sign a false transfer-pricing certificate, are personally liable for the adjustment and corresponding fine. They also face administrative disqualification and criminal prosecution for facilitating tax fraud.
Shell Company / Fiscally Transparent Conduit

Statutory sanction: DIAN applies the General Anti-Abuse Rule to disregard the conduit entity and reattributes the income to the true foreign recipient. The remittances are then recharacterised as dividends or royalties taxed at the regular 10% and 15% withholding rates, plus a 100% to 200% penalty on the withholding differential. All treaty relief is denied.

Director personal liability: The directors who facilitated the sham structure are subject to corporate-veil piercing for the unpaid withholding taxes in the full amount, restrictive travel measures to secure collection, and, if laundering criminal proceeds through the entity is proven, up to 14 years in prison on money-laundering charges.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) – Cash-Intensive Franchise

Statutory sanction: Under Law 526 of 1999 and UIAF regulations, fines of up to 100,000 legal monthly minimum wage units (about COP 116 billion for 2024) per violation per franchisee, plus a criminal sentence of 5 to 10 years for wilful concealment of illicit financial operations.

Director personal liability: Each director has an independent statutory obligation to institute an AML compliance programme for the SAS and its franchise network. Failure to comply, or deliberate structuring to hide the true transaction volume, subjects the director to civil liability for the total assets moved through the system, a court-issued travel ban, and criminal extradition risk in cases involving foreign parties.

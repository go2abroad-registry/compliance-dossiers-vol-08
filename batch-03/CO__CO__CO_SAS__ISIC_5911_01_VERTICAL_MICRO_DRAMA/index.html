Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum capital; the incorporation instrument must state the subscribed and paid-in capital, which may be zero (sólo capital diferido). Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

DIAN - Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (National Tax and Customs Directorate). Portal: www.dian.gov.co; transactional e-services: MUISCA platform (muisca.dian.gov.co); mobile RUT enrollment via the DIAN app; foreign trade declarations via VUCE/DIAN.

E-government filing portal

All taxpayers, including 100% foreign-owned Colombian S.A.S., must obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by enrolling in the RUT (Registro Único Tributario). Procedure: (1) Incorporate the S.A.S. by private document registered with the Cámara de Comercio of the registered office; foreign shareholders need no Colombian residence, but a local legal representative (representante legal) must be appointed. (2) File RUT registration online through the DIAN RUT en línea module or an authorized gestor, submitting the certificate of existence and legal representation, apostilled and officially translated corporate documents of any foreign corporate shareholder, and the representative's identity documentation. (3) DIAN validates in real time and assigns the NIT with the applicable responsibility codes for income tax (renta), VAT (IVA), and withholding agent (agente de retención). (4) Obtain a DIAN-authorized digital signature certificate for electronic invoicing. (5) Register the invoicing software (software propio or proveedor tecnológico) with DIAN. RUT issuance is typically completed within 1 to 5 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Not applicable for check-the-box: Colombia has no pass-through or US-style entity classification election; an S.A.S. is by law a taxable resident corporation subject to 35% corporate income tax on worldwide income (2024 and 2025 rates), with a foreign tax credit for foreign-source income taxes. Relevant statutory elections available: (1) opt for the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) for qualifying micro/small merchants with gross ordinary income up to approximately 100,000 UVT (inflation-indexed), which replaces income tax, VAT and municipal industry/commerce tax on a single return; (2) choose bimonthly (rather than annual) VAT refund filing as an exporter; (3) apply for the ProColombia Certificado de Exportación de Servicios to document service exports; (4) elect standard accelerated depreciation methods within DIAN limits; (5) hybrid mismatch limitation rules of Law 1819/2016 are mandatory disallowances, not elective reliefs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and annual TP filing obligations arise for the S.A.S. if gross income or gross assets exceed 45,000 UVT (approx. COP 2,117,925,000 for FY2024), or if aggregate transactions with foreign related parties exceed 10,000 UVT (approx. COP 470,650,000); a Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above 250,000 UVT. A micro-drama S.A.S. should nevertheless prepare contemporaneous TP analysis if any cross-border related-party services or IP royalties are paid.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but DIAN enforces substance-over-form under Article 28 E.T., the general anti-avoidance rule (Article 869-2 E.T.), and the beneficial-owner requirement in DTAs. For a micro-drama platform, actual human operations, local IP development, board control and management decisions must be in Colombia; a sham S.A.S. or purely passive conduit with no local substance will be disregarded and its payments recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Omission of Colombian-Source Income

Statutory sanction: DIAN can assess the omitted tax plus an increased-return penalty of up to 200% of the understated liability, apply moratory interest, and impose criminal fines; tax evasion has statutory penalties of up to 9 years' imprisonment under Colombian criminal tax law when fraudulent intent is shown.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil protects shareholders only if the S.A.S. is validly constituted and administered. Administrators who knowingly sign fraudulent returns or retain funds knowing there is no capacity to pay tax are jointly liable for unpaid tax, subject to embargo of personal assets, travel bans, and disqualification from managing Colombian companies upon criminal conviction.
Transfer Pricing and Unreported Related-Party Transactions

Statutory sanction: DIAN may recharacterize the transaction, disallow the deduction, assess 35% CIT on the adjustment together with interest, and impose a TP penalty of 100% of the understated tax or 4% of the transaction value if required documentation is not delivered; in serious cases, criminal evasion penalties apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve TP documentation known to be false can be held personally liable for resulting tax deficiencies and disgorgement of benefits. Foreign directors are not immune; DIAN may issue joint liability notices and coordinate with overseas authorities.
Disguised Profit Repatriation and Dividend Withholding Evasion

Statutory sanction: The full amount is reclassified as non-deductible and taxable as a distribution; the negligent payer must remit the 10% dividend WHT, plus penalties of 100% of the withheld amount for failure to withhold, moratory interest, and possible criminal liability for breach of withholding duties.

Director personal liability: Colombian company law requires administrators to ensure withholding and tax obligations are fulfilled. Failure to retain and pay dividend WHT makes the current administrator personally liable for the unremitted withholding, and a director may face economic embargo while the shareholders remain liable for corporate tax.
AML/CTF and Digital Payment-Rails Compliance

Statutory sanction: Colombia's UIAF and financial/administrative regulators can impose fines in UVT and freeze assets; money-laundering convictions carry 10-30 years' imprisonment. Deficient AML programs expose the S.A.S. to regulatory revocation of authorizations, reputational downgrade, and seizure of bank accounts used by payment aggregators.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and legal representatives who knowingly execute or omit AML controls face personal criminal liability, patrimonial forfeiture of illicit gains, and travel restrictions during investigation; non-resident beneficial owners cannot shield themselves by using nominee directors in the S.A.S.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio of the registered-office district operating through the Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES) portal, with regulatory oversight by the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio (SIC).

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 (Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada), arts. 1, 4, 5, 11, 14, 25 and 44; Código de Comercio (Decreto 410 de 1971), arts. 98-113 and 137-156; Decreto 1074 de 2015, arts. 2.2.1.1.1-2.2.1.9.1; Ley 1429 de 2010, arts. 50-53 (registration formalities simplification); Decreto 1625 de 2016, art. 1.6.1.1.1 (tax registration and RUT rules).

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución - private incorporation document executed by the founding shareholder(s) and filed for registration with the Chamber of Commerce; if the subscription is paid with real property, the instrument must be elevated to public deed before a Notaría.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 19 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum subscribed or paid-up share capital (art. 5, Ley 1258 de 2008); if paid in instalments, the full payment term may not exceed two years from incorporation (art. 14). No escrow or capital deposit account is legally required for cash subscriptions; however, a bank deposit certificate evidencing paid capital and an expert appraisal for non-cash contributions must be annexed whenever the company obtains bank financing or transfers shares.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certificate (Certificado de Bomberos) is mandatory for the leased office; structural load clearance is checked via the land-use certificate and the building's construction/occupancy license (licencia de construcción - uso oficinas); occupational clearance requires implementation and registration of the SG-SST (minimum standards, Resolución 0312 de 2019), ARL affiliation for the company (Risk Class I for software), first-aid and fire warden training, and an ergonomic evaluation of workstations for the office and telework model if applicable.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum subscribed or paid-up share capital (art. 5, Ley 1258 de 2008); if paid in instalments, the full payment term may not exceed two years from incorporation (art. 14). No escrow or capital deposit account is legally required for cash subscriptions; however, a bank deposit certificate evidencing paid capital and an expert appraisal for non-cash contributions must be annexed whenever the company obtains bank financing or transfers shares.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); portal: Muisca (transacciones electrónicas), RUT (Registro Único Tributario) and Factura Electrónica services at www.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Colombian S.A.S. must appoint a legal representative resident in Colombia, obtain a Colombian Tax ID (NIT) through a pre-registration process before DIAN, submit the RUT form, provide notarized constitutional documents (certificate of incorporation and by-laws), evidence the representative's identity/address, and obtain the RUT defintivo. The S.A.S. must also apply for a digital signature certificate and enroll in the electronic invoicing regime (resolución de facturación) before commencing taxable operations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (S.A.S. is a Colombian resident corporation subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income; branch/PE activities are source-taxed on a territorial basis). — A foreign entity establishing a Colombian S.A.S. must appoint a legal representative resident in Colombia, obtain a Colombian Tax ID (NIT) through a pre-registration process before DIAN, submit the RUT form, provide notarized constitutional documents (certificate of incorporation and by-laws), evidence the representative's identity/address, and obtain the RUT defintivo. The S.A.S. must also apply for a digital signature certificate and enroll in the electronic invoicing regime (resolución de facturación) before commencing taxable operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (S.A.S. is a Colombian resident corporation subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income; branch/PE activities are source-taxed on a territorial basis).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available. Colombian law permits limited special elections: (i) the Régimen SIMPLE de Tributación (SIMPLO) for small taxpayers not exceeding 100,000 UVT gross income, replacing income tax, VAT, and municipal tax with a single bracket; (ii) irrevocable classification as a 'national corporation' for tax purposes; and (iii) hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized. Foreign tax credit election and CFC rules are mandatory rather than elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold. Any entity or individual carrying out taxable acts in Colombia (including remote/external service providers with a tax nexus) must register with DIAN in the RUT. Small taxpayers with gross income below 100,000 UVT (approx. COP 4,732 million for 2024) may optionally adopt the SIMPLE regime, which absorbs VAT and income tax obligations, but they remain VAT-required for invoicing purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualifying exports of goods and certain services are excluded or exempt from Colombian VAT rather than subject to 0%. For a PropTech/SaaS vendor supplying services from Colombia, zero-rating is available when: (i) the service is rendered to a foreign entity without a permanent establishment in Colombia; (ii) the service is 'exported' in economic terms and the beneficiary has its tax domicile abroad; (iii) the revolution from the contract is effectively used or exploited exclusively outside Colombia; and (iv) the invoice indicates the special 'Export' nature. Digital platforms providing real-estate SaaS to non-resident clients must evidence the off-shore usage and the foreign residence/recipient data in the invoicing system; otherwise the supply is subject to 19% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DEX) or authorized equivalent electronic filing
  • Bill of Lading (BL) or Airway Bill (AWB) evidencing physical movement when goods are involved
  • Commercial contract and invoice indicating the service destination, scope, and beneficiary
  • Certificate of payment channeling from a local financial intermediary (Certificado de Canalización de Divisas / Formulario de Declaración de Cambio)
  • RUT and export registry validity for the exporting entity

Mandatory electronic invoicing for all taxpayers. Invoices must be issued electronically through a DIAN-certified technological provider and transmitted in XML to DIAN's Muisca platform in real-time for validation, acceptance, and digital stamping. Each invoice must include the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) or CUDS for exports, a QR code, and the electronic invoice state used. The recipient must accept or reject the invoice within 48 hours. Exports require an 'Export Operator' electronic invoice and prior export registration in the VUCE or customs declaration system.

DIAN audits export transactions and may retroactively reinstate the 19% IVA, plus pop-up interest (currently 1.5% per month) and penalty for inaccuracy (sanción por inexactitud) equal to 100% of the outstanding tax difference when the export exemption/zero-rating is unsupported. Fraudulent claims may also trigger criminal tax fraud (defraudación fiscal) penalties and the denial of future export invoices. Additionally, failure to channel export proceeds within the required term by the authorized foreign exchange intermediary may lead to central bank fines and loss of export benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombian peso (COP) is partially convertible. Current-account operations (service export revenue, trade payments, profit and dividend remittances) are freely permissible but must be settled through the authorised exchange market (mercado cambiario) via an IMC; no prior authorization is required, yet each flow must be captured in the applicable Declaración de Cambio. Capital-account operations (foreign equity investment into the SAS, cross-border loans, capital repatriation) are liberalised but require mandatory ex-post registration with Banco de la República through Formulario 4 (Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales); a registered foreign investment is a strict legal precondition for future dividend remittance and capital repatriation. Profit remittance hurdles: dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to Colombian corporate income tax, dividend withholding tax (generally up to 10% depending on treaty), and, for profits exceeding the applicable taxable amount, an additional remittance surtax; the treasury must evidence payment of such taxes through certificates of tax withholding (certificados de retención) before the bank executes the FX transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All foreign-currency proceeds derived from the export of PropTech software services must be reintegrated (reintegro): remitted to Colombia and effectively negotiated/sold through the Colombian exchange market via an IMC. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% of export receipts (no discretionary retention percentage applies). The reintegro term is governed by Resolución Externa No. 8 de 2000 and Circular DCIN-83; the standard maximum window is six (6) months counted from the contractually established collection date/receipt of funds, provided the underlying service contract supports the term, extending beyond the general threshold only under documented commercial reasons accepted by the IMC. Each operation must be declared in the exchange declaration applicable to service exports, supported by invoice, contract, proof of collection, and the platform settlement reports. Alternative structure: the SAS may apply ex ante to Banco de la República for an authorised Cuenta de Compensación (foreign compensation account), allowing the temporary offshore retention of export revenue collected via Stripe or ACH aggregators; balances must comply with the authorised maximum holding period and annual reporting. Failure to reintegro within the statutory term constitutes an infracción cambiaria (exchange violation) with monetary fines calculated on the unrepatriated amount and potential loss of tax evidence.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), acting through its Board of Directors (Junta Directiva), is the sole authority for foreign exchange policy, supported by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia for supervision of IMCs (Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario). Legal pillars: Ley 9 de 1991 (Estatuto Cambiario, Foreign Exchange Statute), Resolución Externa No. 8 de 2000, and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, as amended. The DIAN also exercises a complementary control function for trade and service flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Restricted / Low for primary treasury operations. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Mercury, Payoneer) cannot substitute for a Colombian IMC because only authorised local banks and financial corporations may execute foreign-exchange operations, receive Formulario 4/6 exchange declarations, or register foreign investment with Banco de la República. An EMI is compliantly usable only as an intermediate USD aggregation account for Stripe Billing and ACH Direct payouts, provided the account is fully owned by the SAS, is reported under the Cuenta de Compensación regime (if used for export proceeds), and funds are swept to the local IMC within the reintegro term. The EMI must never be used for equity injection, profit remittance, or COP treasury conversion; its existence must be disclosed annually in the exchange information filing to Banco de la República and in the applicable DIAN exogenous information. Feasibility rating: Medium as a pass-through rail; Low as a treasury spine.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant sequence for foreign equity injection into the Colombian SAS: (1) the foreign investor transmits USD or other foreign currency by SWIFT MT103 to the SAS's USD account held at the local IMC, inserting the exchange purpose code for international investment in field 72/70 rather than a generic goods or service code; (2) on the settlement business day the IMC captures and files Formulario 4 (Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales) with Banco de la República, identifying the transaction as direct foreign investment (inversión directa), the NIT of the SAS, and the subscribed capital; (3) the IMC converts the funds at the official TRM and credits the COP operational account; (4) Banco de la República issues the investment registration record in the name of the SAS, which becomes the legal anchor for future dividend and capital remittances. The treasury must archive the MT103, the FX contract/ticket, and the Formulario 4 acceptance, and reconcile the registration with the equity section of the financial statements. For capital injections above USD 1 million the bank will require a written use-of-proceeds statement and may run enhanced sanctions and negative-news screens; for shareholder loans above statutory thresholds, an external indebtedness registration (Formulario 5) would be required instead of Formulario 4. Capital contributions below the local threshold do not require prior authorisation, but never allow the wire to arrive without pre-agreed purpose codes, as re-remediation is materially slower.For outbound trade, software license fees, cloud infrastructure, marketing services, and cross-border contractor payments: the standard clearing rail is a clean SWIFT MT103 issued from the SAS's COP account, with the IMC purchasing foreign currency and filing the exchange declaration for service imports (Formulario 6/Giros al Exterior). Supporting evidence: commercial contract, vendor invoice, and where the annual threshold applies, the official DIAN declaration of service imports to substantiate tax deductibility. For physical PropTech hardware, beacons, or point-of-sale devices, the recommended rail is a sight Commercial Letter of Credit (confirmed or unconfirmed) issued by the Colombian bank's trade desk on documents-against-payment terms, accompanied by the DIAN goods import declaration; the LC operation also triggers the exchange declaration by the IMC. Attention: all disbursements linked to the export service chain should retain the full MT103 trail, because Colombian tax and exchange auditors cross-match outbound spending to the reintegro of export receivables. International payroll for foreign contractors may be executed through the same MT103 route or via a payroll-specialist EMI, but the local bank still expects the exchange declaration. Statutory minimis: service imports below the threshold may proceed under general rules, yet treasury policy should formalise every operation with the bank's granted FX form.COP/USD deliverable FX forwards (Forwards) with the Colombian IMC: authorised solely to hedge genuine commercial exposures such as USD-denominated SaaS receivables, future equity injections, and dividend remittance obligations; the hedge documentation must reference the underlying invoicing/settlement reports and match the reintegro or remittance horizon; no naked speculative forward positions are permitted for residents.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/COP: usable in the offshore market and mirrorable through Colombian banks where the resident has a demonstrable underlying currency exposure; the operation must satisfy Banco de la República's risk-hedging conditions and be reported by the IMC; NDFs cannot be used to avoid mandatory reintegro of export proceeds.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) COP/USD: suitable for hedging medium-term intercompany shareholder loans, dividend flows, or future capital repatriation; must be transacted through an IMC, with both the FX rate and interest legs included in the relevant exchange declaration; economic substance documentation and board approval are required.Listed COP/USD futures traded on the Colombian derivatives exchange (Derivex/BVC) and cleared through a central counterparty: appropriate for systematic quarterly hedging of recurring Stripe and ACH receivable flows across 3-12 month tenors; margin variation payments must be forecast in the COP liquidity plan; futures are explicitly recognised under Colombian exchange regulations as permitted hedging tools when linked to real exposure.FX options and zero-cost collars with authorised IMC counterparties: permissible for protection against abrupt TRM depreciation on committed cross-border disbursements or when protecting the COP value of the investment; premiums must be paid through the IMC and the underlying operational exposure evidenced at inception.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Restricted / Low for primary treasury operations. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Mercury, Payoneer) cannot substitute for a Colombian IMC because only authorised local banks and financial corporations may execute foreign-exchange operations, receive Formulario 4/6 exchange declarations, or register foreign investment with Banco de la República. An EMI is compliantly usable only as an intermediate USD aggregation account for Stripe Billing and ACH Direct payouts, provided the account is fully owned by the SAS, is reported under the Cuenta de Compensación regime (if used for export proceeds), and funds are swept to the local IMC within the reintegro term. The EMI must never be used for equity injection, profit remittance, or COP treasury conversion; its existence must be disclosed annually in the exchange information filing to Banco de la República and in the applicable DIAN exogenous information. Feasibility rating: Medium as a pass-through rail; Low as a treasury spine.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – National Tax and Customs Directorate; headquartered in Bogotá; principal online portal is MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicios y Trámites) at https://www.dian.gov.co, with the Sede Electrónica and Carpeta Ciudadana used for taxpayer registration, e-invoice validation, tax returns, and payment proceedings.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the SAS (Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada) by private written instrument filed through the Cámara de Comercio / RUES (Registro Único Empresarial y Social); no minimum statutory capital is required and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted. (2) Obtain the Matrícula Mercantil and the Certificado de Existencia y Representación Legal. (3) Within 30 days of incorporation, complete the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) registration through MUISCA; DIAN then assigns the NIT (9-digit tax identification number, format XXX.XXX.XXX-X) and activates the applicable tax responsibilities: CIIU 7490 activity code, IVA 'Régimen Común', income tax filer, retention-at-source agent, and information return markers. (4) Appoint a local legal representative (resident individual) and obtain the DIAN digital signature ('Firma Electrónica') from an ONAC-accredited certification authority to sign e-invoices and tax declarations. (5) Enable the Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (either DIAN's free web portal or an authorized 'Proveedor Tecnológico') and pass the initial e-invoice validation test. (6) Open a Colombian current-account bank facility for compulsory foreign-currency channeling ('canalización de divisas') and maintain the supporting exchange records (Formulario No. 5 – Declaración de Cambio) for export proceeds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Colombian SAS is a separate corporate taxpayer resident in Colombia by incorporation test (E.T. arts. 12, 118), taxed on worldwide net income at the standard corporate rate: 35% for FY2024 and 30% from FY2025 pursuant to Law 2277/2022. No pass-through or fiscal transparency is available to the non-resident shareholder; profits distributed abroad are subject to final dividend withholding (art. 245 E.T.) and, in applicable cases, the additional 3-point or 10-point dividend surcharge on excess distributions. Export service income is not exempt from income tax; only the VAT treatment is zero-rated.

Available election forms

(a) SIMPLE – Régimen Simple de Tributación: optional consolidated income/VAT/ICA gross-receipts regime available to SAS whose shareholders are natural persons and whose annual gross income does not exceed 100,000 UVT (approx. COP 4.98 billion for 2025); service activities are taxed on a progressive scale from 1.8% to 6.8% of gross receipts; election is made at initial RUT filing or at the beginning of the fiscal year. (b) Zona Franca (Free-Trade Zone): optional authorization from the Comité de Zonas Francas for exporters of qualifying digital services, granting a 20% corporate income tax rate and 0% VAT on exports of services; entails minimum investment commitments and job creation benchmarks. (c) Economía Naranja: historically available 5-year income-tax exemption for newly incorporated SAS devoted to certified creative/cultural projects (Mincultura certification under Law 1943/2018 and Law 2010/2019), subject to quantitative caps and high compliance scrutiny. (d) No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Colombia; entity classification is rigid under the Código de Comercio and E.T., and hybrid mismatch rules, CFC rules, and OECD Pillar Two provisions must be observed in cross-border structuring.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold: a Colombian SAS performing taxable services is mandatorily categorised as IVA 'Régimen Común' from the date of its first taxable transaction, regardless of turnover. The simplified IVA regime is restricted to natural persons with prior-year gross income below 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 174.3 million for 2025) and is unavailable to an SAS. The 'Régimen Común' SAS must file monthly Form 309 (IVA returns) and, where applicable, Form 310 for export services, with payments made through DIAN's MUISCA platform.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under E.T. art. 481 and Decree 2242/2015 (as amended by Law 2010/2019), exported services are classified as 'exempt' and invoiced at 0% IVA where all of the following cumulative conditions are met: (a) the supplier is a Colombian-resident SAS registered in the RUT as an IVA Régimen Común taxpayer; (b) the recipient is a non-resident entity without domicile in Colombia (foreign AAA game studio or publishing principal) and is not a Colombian state enterprise; (c) the service is performed in Colombia but exclusively used outside Colombia, meaning the 3D sculpted assets, rigs, textures, and intellectual property are consumed, embedded, licensed, or exploited in the foreign principal's game production and not allocated to Colombian-sourced products; (d) the service is not directly connected with tangible goods located in Colombia at the time of performance; and (e) the exporter maintains contemporaneous evidence of the offshore destination, including an executed export contract, DIAN-validated Factura Electrónica de Exportación with CUFE, delivery/acceptance certificates for digital files, and inward remittance records. Digital delivery of game art by file transfer or cloud repository does not disqualify an export; however, DIAN will pierce the arrangement if the foreign client is a conduit, a related party without substance, or the final beneficiary is Colombian.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX / DEX Int.): compulsory only when physical media containing master game-art files are shipped; for pure digital transmission, the DIAN-validated Factura Electrónica de Exportación (with CUFE code) serves as the operative export document and must be referenced in the monthly Form 310.
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (Guía Aérea o B/L): required for tangible carriers of the 3D asset packages; for digital delivery, the equivalent is the signed digital delivery or acceptance certificate (Acta de Entrega y Aceptación) from the foreign studio, including hash values or version control identifiers.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Canalización de Divisas / Comprobante de Compensación): issued by the Colombian commercial bank through which the foreign-currency proceeds were negotiated, evidencing SWIFT/wire-transfer receipt from the offshore client within the statutory 180-day term.
  • Export Service Contract (Contrato de Exportación de Servicios): executed between the Colombian SAS and the foreign game developer, identifying the parties, IP ownership and licence transfer, deliverables, milestones, payment currency, and transfer-pricing documentation consistent with E.T. arts. 260-1 et seq. (arm's length analysis for related-party clients).
  • Offshore-Use Certificate (Carta de Uso Exclusivo en el Exterior): signed declaration from the foreign recipient confirming that the 3D assets, rigs, and renders will be exploited solely outside Colombia and not resold, rendered, or utilised in any Colombian-domiciled game project.

Mandatory for all IVA taxpayers since 2021. Every sale of services, including exports, must be documented as a Factura Electrónica de Venta (FE) in XML format and submitted for real-time validation to DIAN through the authorized Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (DIAN web portal or certified Proveedor Tecnológico). Each FE must contain the CUFE or CUDS unique code, the DIAN digital stamp/QR code, the CIIU activity code, and the NIT of the issuer; transmission is generally required within 48 hours for domestic invoices, while export invoices are subject to pre-validation rules and, for services, must be transmitted within the applicable tax period before the export benefit is recognised. Late, incomplete, or non-transmitted e-invoices trigger penalties under E.T. art. 652 (1% of the invoice value) and the suspension of the e-invoicing authorization, with automatic rejection of the VAT zero-rating claim if the invoice has not been validated by DIAN.

DIAN exercises a retrospective audit window of five years (E.T. art. 705) over export zero-rating claims. Where the export conditions are disproved, DIAN will assess the full 19% IVA on the exported service revenue, plus monetary moratory interest and the 'sanción por inexactitud' (100%–200% of the omitted VAT exposure) under E.T. art. 648. Additional consequences include: (i) a 1% penalty on the value of each e-invoice issued without full statutory requirements or untimely transmission (art. 652); (ii) late-filing and late-payment penalties on the reassessed VAT; (iii) fines of up to 200% of the non-canalised foreign currency under the Banco de la República foreign-exchange regime for failure to satisfy the 180-day repatriation obligation; (iv) retroactive clawback of the CUFE/CUDS validation and any previously refunded export VAT credits, with consequent recharacterisation as Colombian-source service revenue; and (v) criminal liability for aggravated tax fraud (Law 2277/2022) if fictitious contracts or inflated export proceeds are used, exposing the legal representative to imprisonment and disqualification. In practice, DIAN also applies transfer-pricing scrutiny where the foreign game studio is an affiliate, and any related-party export invoice outside the arm's length range will be adjusted with the same VAT, interest, and penalty consequences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia applies a mixed convertibility regime: current-account transactions (export service receivables, profit remittances, dividends, interest) are fully convertible with no quantitative restrictions, provided settlements flow through the official 'mercado cambiario' using an authorized intermediary (IMC). Capital-account transactions (foreign direct investment into the S.A.S., shareholder loans, equity increases and external credit) are open but must be registered with Banco de la República when the operation reaches or exceeds the regulatory threshold, generally USD 10,000 equivalent; an unregistered capital inflow makes subsequent profit and capital remittance legally unenforceable and exposes the investor to FX infringement fines. Profit remittance hurdles for the non-resident shareholder are documentary rather than exchange-control: an up-to-date FDI registration, audited or reviewed financial statements, corporate distribution minutes, evidence of payment of Colombian corporate income tax and dividend withholding tax (generally 5% for treaty-protected or low-rate jurisdictions under the 2023-2025 framework; 15% for non-residents otherwise), and processing of the exact 'Declaración de Cambio' through the IMC. There are no currency surrender restrictions on current account proceeds, but funds failing to enter the official channel are treated as a foreign-exchange offence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Colombian FX regime, 100% of export service proceeds (ISIC 7490 digital creative services) is subject to mandatory 'canalización': the exporter must collect and channel the foreign currency through an authorized intermediary and file the corresponding 'Declaración de Cambio por Exportación de Servicios'. Statutory channeling window: six months from the date of the service-export declaration, extendible by a further six months upon justified request; proceeds received earlier may be registered as advance export payments ('pago anticipado'). There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio into Colombian pesos: the exporter may lawfully retain funds in a registered foreign-currency compensation account (Cuenta de Compensación) at a local bank or in an abroad account disclosed to Banco de la República, provided all receipts are declared in the FX market. Persistent collection outside the banking system, such as personal PayPal accumulation or foreign-currency custody without declaration, constitutes an exchange-control infringement subject to fines of up to 200% of the unreported amount.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) acting as the national foreign-exchange regulatory authority; operational application governed by Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1068 of 2015 and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, with enforcement by the Financial Superintendence of Colombia (SFC) and tax-validation oversight by DIAN.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Rating: LOW-MEDIUM as a standalone primary treasury rail; MEDIUM-HIGH as a front-end collection gateway. Offshore EMIs (Payoneer, Wise, Mercury, Airwallex) are not authorized FX intermediaries under Colombian law and cannot perform the 'canalización' obligation; funds held with them sit outside the official FX market and must be repatriated to an IMC and declared for each export receivable. For the AAA game-art outsourcing commercial model, PayPal and Wise are commercially required as client-facing collection rails; the recommended structure is a tier-1 Colombian USD/COP treasury account as the principal rail, with PayPal/Wise functioning as a collection gateway swept weekly into the IMC, each sweep backed by an export declaration. Legal constraints: DIAN treats fintech balances as cash equivalents for tax accounting and demands translation into COP at the representative market rate; Banco de la República considers non-declared accumulation abroad as unreported capital; and Colombian banks may reject fintech-origin credits that lack a proper concept and invoice basis unless the client maintains an active export-registration file.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity and shareholder-loan injections must be sent by SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident investor's bank account directly to the S.A.S. account at the Colombian IMC, with the Remittance Information field containing: (a) full investor name and address; (b) the regulatory concept ('Foreign Direct Investment – Equity Subscription' or 'External Shareholder Loan'); (c) the S.A.S. NIT and registered name; and (d) reference to the constitutive resolutions and corporate approvals. The IMC will process Formulario No. 1 (Declaración de Cambio – Canalización) and, for amounts above USD 10,000 equivalent, will enforce foreign-investment registration (Registro Internacional de Inversiones) at Banco de la República. Where fast working capital is preferred, the client may instead receive advance payments under export contracts, a lower-compliance route requiring only a service-export declaration; alternatively, receivables can be capitalized by public deed and filed as an equity increase. Banks will block incoming funds if the SWIFT message lacks the ultimate originator (bank-to-bank information rule), if the sending institution is unlicensed, or if the routed funds show third-party consolidation; therefore all injections must carry full FATF Recommendation 16 and OFAC-validated originator data.Outbound treasury rails for the game-art studio consist of: (i) SWIFT MT103/M202 for payments to overseas software licensors, render-farm cloud providers and specialized subcontractors, supported by an import-of-services declaration and DIAN-certified withholding tax; (ii) MT101 batch transfers for occasional remote freelance payroll; and (iii) PayPal/Wise/Debit Rails for low-value administrative purchases below USD 2,000, aggregated into a monthly import-of-services filing. Commercial letters of credit are economically unsuitable for ISIC 7490 service flows, except where the S.A.S. acquires high-end workstations or dedicated hardware from Asian or European suppliers under documentary collections (D/P); in those cases an L/C follows UCP600 mechanics with invoice, packing list, and transport documents.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; digital portal: Taxisnet (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy); VAT registration and compliance administered by the VAT Sub-Division of the Tax Department, Nicosia.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus-incorporated Private Limited Company (HE-format, e.g., HE 77xxxx) receives its Tax Identification Number (TIN) automatically at incorporation via the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DRCOR); no separate tax registration application is required. A foreign entity without local incorporation but with a Cyprus permanent establishment must file Form TD2001 with the District Tax Office within 60 days of commencing PE activity, attaching certified constitutional documents, PE address proof, and evidence of the foreign entity's existence. For VAT: a non-established taxable person supplies-of-goods or services in Cyprus must file Form VAT 101A with the VAT Service, appoint a fiscal representative where no EU establishment exists, and will be issued a Cyprus VAT number (CY + 8 digits + X). Registration is compulsory from the first taxable supply for non-established persons (no threshold), except where the reverse charge fully applies; export-only manufacturers should nonetheless VAT-register to recover input VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: CY Ltd is a non-transparent corporate taxpayer subject to 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income managed and controlled from Cyprus. It is not pass-through; profits are taxed at entity level and distributions are exempt under participation exemption. Territorial modifiers apply: foreign PE profits and qualifying dividend/interest income are exempt; IP box regime grants an 80% deduction on qualifying intellectual property income. Cyprus is a unitary national jurisdiction with no sub-national corporate taxation.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Available statutory elections include: (1) group loss relief election (Form 111) for 75%-owned Cyprus-resident group companies; (2) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity; (3) foreign tax credit (unilateral relief) election versus expense deduction; (4) foreign PE income exemption election under Section 7(1)(b) of the Income Tax Law; (5) IP box qualifying asset election; (6) tonnage tax election (shipping only, not applicable). Hybrid mismatch: Cyprus implements ATAD 1/2 anti-hybrid rules with no elective neutrality; DAC6 disclosure applies automatically to reportable cross-border arrangements; transitional grandfathering of pre-2020 hybrid instruments requires a timely documented election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 for taxable supplies by persons established in Cyprus; €35,000 for intra-Community distance sales into Cyprus; €10,251.61 for intra-Community acquisitions; non-established taxable persons: no threshold — VAT registration required from the first taxable supply unless the reverse charge fully applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) is available for: (a) direct export of ICU mechanical ventilators and spare parts outside the EU under Section 5(1)(a) of VAT Law 95(I)/2000, provided the exporter evidences dispatch/departure from the EU within 3 months of supply and retains conclusive proof; (b) intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered customers where the buyer's VAT number is validated via VIES and the goods are dispatched within 3 months, with EC Sales List (ESL) submission; (c) offshore services directly related to exported goods (installation, biomedical calibration, training, telematics) whose place of supply is outside Cyprus under Article 58/59 of the VAT Law; (d) export-freight and inspection services. Export-only businesses must be VAT-registered to recover input VAT on raw materials, R&D, and production overheads; otherwise input recovery is only possible via the non-established taxable person refund mechanism (13th Directive).

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure of goods from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a Cyprus-authorized bank (or SWIFT MT103) evidencing payment and repatriation of export proceeds

No general mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is currently operative for B2B exporters. Mandatory B2G electronic invoicing via the Peppol network applies to invoices issued to Cypriot central contracting authorities and public hospitals (Law 44(I)/2017 as amended by Law 69(I)/2022), phased in from 2023; ICU ventilator supplies to public health entities require PEPPOL-compliant e-invoices (EN16931). The tiSYS electronic fiscal device (EFD) real-time transmission mandate applies only to designated retail/catering service sectors and is not applicable to a medical-device manufacturer. Cyprus has published a national e-invoicing roadmap aligned with the EU ViDA/VII proposals, anticipating mandatory B2B e-invoicing and transaction-based clearance by 2025-2028; no API-based continuous transaction control (CTC) is in production for exporters as of the reporting date.

Cyprus Tax Department and Customs audit export evidence on a risk-rated basis; incomplete documentation converts zero-rated supplies to 19% VAT with interest of 1.5% per month or part of a month (Law 4(I)/78). For fraudulent zero-rating: Section 49 VAT Law imposes a fine of up to €15,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years, plus assessment to double the tax evaded; false customs declarations attract penalties up to €5,000, forfeiture of goods, and criminal liability under the Customs Code (EU Reg. 952/2013). Criminal fraud conspiracy (Criminal Code Cap.154 s.300) carries up to 7 years' imprisonment, extended to 10 years in aggravated revenue fraud. Clawback includes repayment of any input VAT refunded on export-destined goods, with 100% surcharge and disgorgement of the zero-rated benefit. Limitation periods: standard 5 years, extended to 7 years for misdeclaration and 10 years in cases of fraud; records must be retained a minimum of 7 years after the transaction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus applies full current-account convertibility as a Eurozone member (currency: EUR, ISO 4217: EUR); capital-account operations are also liberalized within the EEA under Art. 63 TFEU, subject to mandatory statistical reporting to the Central Bank's Balance of Payments (BoP) system. There are no administrative limits on inbound equity, intercompany loans, or dividend repatriation for Cyprus-resident or non-resident shareholders. However, the Central Bank imposes an 'anti-money laundering gatekeeping' function: banks require a completed 'Company Economic Substance Questionnaire', audited financials, and supporting source-of-funds documentation before releasing funds to an operational account. For the target entity—an ISIC 3250 medical ventilator manufacturer—profit remittance hurdles are minimal if the entity has genuine substance (board meetings, local management, procurement/production functions), files tax returns, and pays any applicable withholding tax. Cyprus domestic law imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions to non-resident shareholders, but a 10% Special Defence Contribution (SDC) may apply to Cyprus-resident individuals receiving dividends; corporate shareholders resident in Cyprus are generally exempt. A genuine commercial manufacturing/trading operation may qualify for the 12.5% corporate income tax, making the effective blended remittance tax burden near nil if the recipient is a non-resident corporate entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds in Cyprus. Exporters may hold foreign currency denominated accounts at any authorized credit institution without converting to EUR. Nevertheless, each export transaction exceeding EUR 10,000 (or the cumulative threshold within a calendar month) must be reported to the Central Bank of Cyprus via the bank's 'International Transactions Reporting System' (ITRS) for BoP statistics. For shipments financed by export letters of credit (L/C), the negotiated documents must include the invoice, packing list, bill of lading/airway bill, and any required certificate of origin; the bank debits the L/C proceeds to a segregated foreign currency account, preserving the non-EUR balance until used. The only obligation is that any conversion to EUR is conducted at market rate through the clearing bank; no approval or 'export proceeds statement' is required for EU intra-community sales.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) acting also as the national Eurosystem central bank and reserve management authority; FX regulatory oversight derives from EU/ECB framework and the Exchange Control Law (Cap. 97) as succeeded by liberalized capital-movement regulations under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the Cypriot Statute of the Central Bank of Cyprus (2002, as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium, with distinct caveats. Cyprus-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) regulated by the Central Bank of Cyprus under the Payment Services and Electronic Money Laws (transposing PSD2/EMD2) can open virtual IBANs and provide FX conversion for trade payments, but they are not licensed to form a primary treasury relationship for high-value government or hospital 'life-support equipment' L/C settlements. Credit risk, L/C advising, and documentary processing are unsupported by most EMIs. Cross-border EMIs established in other EEA states may passport into Cyprus, yet many restrict high-risk medical-equipment invoicing to emerging-market buyers. Legal constraints include: (i) EMIs cannot grant credit; (ii) settlement of an L/C can only be performed for the account-holder's own commercial obligations, not as a confirming bank; (iii) the beneficiary bank may reject an EMI-hosted account due to inability to authenticate SWIFT/RMA keys. Therefore, use a registered Cyprus EMI as a 'collection/collection-disbursement pocket' for supplier payments under EUR 50,000, but maintain a tier-1 bank as the operating treasury and L/C clearing line.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 via a correspondent bank, with the beneficiary IBAN at the Cyprus clearing bank and the following purpose code as per CBC ITRS: 'S22B — Equity capital and investment fund shares' (or, for intercompany shareholder loans, 'S22C — Loans and advances received'). The remitting institution must execute in the 'reference/remittance information' field: '/BENEFICIARY/ CY Ltd HE77 / ISO 3250 capital equipment project / SANCTION-COMPLIANT MEDICAL MANUFACTURING / UBO: [Name] / Contract ref. [XXX]'. To avoid a false-positive export-control review, include the HS/CN code (9019.20) for medical respiratory/ventilation apparatus, the purchase order from the target distribution company, and a letter from the Cyprus auditor confirming that the funds are not derived from proceeds of crime. The clearing bank will request the source-of-funds evidence (bank statements, subscription agreement) and will release funds to the 'Capital Escrow Account' only after the CBC transaction report is filed. For intra-EU transfers, use SEPA or an instant SEPA CT if the amount is below EUR 100,000; for higher amounts or non-EUR, rely on standard correspondent SWIFT credentials.The primary clearing rail for face-to-face original equipment manufacturer (OEM) component purchases is an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (L/C) issued in favour of the foreign supplier, advised through the Cyprus bank's SWIFT network (MT700/MT710 series). For same-day settlement of parts and logistics invoices under USD 500,000, use MT103 for direct book transfer or 'MT202 COV' when a separate cover payment is required. For a delivery of ICU mechanical ventilators to a state hospital, the issuing bank will require a Purchase Order, the pro-forma invoice, a due-diligence declaration from the overseas L/C opening bank, and a Business Purpose Statement. Because the Cypriot bank acts as issuing and/or confirming bank, the reimbursement is settled through the UCP600 rules and SWIFT 'Reimbursement Claims' (MT740/742) as applicable, with the trade-finance desk monetising export invoices against the irrevocable L/C. All outbound disbursements must include the ICC Incoterms 2020 (e.g., DAP or FCA), the exact VAT/GST status, and the corrected commercial invoice value; non-EU trade settlement must comply with the EU Dual-Use Regulation 2021/821 if components are in Annex I (e.g., pressure gauges, valves, some electronic controls); the bank will freeze the payment if tampering with the HS classification is suspected.Currency Forwards (deliverable) — plain-vanilla EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF contracts with a Cyprus-authorised credit institution, to fix the conversion rate on future import/export invoices.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) — OTC NDFs for settlement in EUR where the underlying currency (e.g., TRY, NGN, PKR) has no deliverable onshore market; must be arranged with the Cyprus bank's own dealing desk, which may offset with a global counterparty.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) — permitted for treasury management to swap fixed/floating interest rate liabilities on an intercompany shareholder loan, always with maturity matching the loan tenor; subject to EMIR clearing obligation only for standard large contracts.FX Options (European-style) — permitted for eliminating upside FX risk on large tender payments, including call/put combinations; any option premium is deductible as a hedging expense if the documentation contains a formal Treasury Policy approved by the board.FX swaps for liquidity management — used to convert EUR receipts into USD inventory funding for the ventilator assembly line, with an offsetting forward contract; all swaps must be reported to the CBC under the ITRS as a 'FX and interest-rate derivative' entry.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium, with distinct caveats. Cyprus-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) regulated by the Central Bank of Cyprus under the Payment Services and Electronic Money Laws (transposing PSD2/EMD2) can open virtual IBANs and provide FX conversion for trade payments, but they are not licensed to form a primary treasury relationship for high-value government or hospital 'life-support equipment' L/C settlements. Credit risk, L/C advising, and documentary processing are unsupported by most EMIs. Cross-border EMIs established in other EEA states may passport into Cyprus, yet many restrict high-risk medical-equipment invoicing to emerging-market buyers. Legal constraints include: (i) EMIs cannot grant credit; (ii) settlement of an L/C can only be performed for the account-holder's own commercial obligations, not as a confirming bank; (iii) the beneficiary bank may reject an EMI-hosted account due to inability to authenticate SWIFT/RMA keys. Therefore, use a registered Cyprus EMI as a 'collection/collection-disbursement pocket' for supplier payments under EUR 50,000, but maintain a tier-1 bank as the operating treasury and L/C clearing line.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Superintendencia de Sociedades (oversight) and the local Cámara de Comercio (registry execution, e.g., Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá) operating the Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES).

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 (Articles 1–5, 8–13, 17–22 and 30) as the primary S.A.S. statute; supplementary Código de Comercio (Decreto 410 de 1971) and Decreto 1074 de 2015 (RUES / single business portal regulation).

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (private articles of incorporation with duly authenticated signatures) filed through the Ventanilla Única Empresarial (VUE) and recorded in the Mercantile Registry (Registro Mercantil / RUES).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum legal capital under Law 1258/2008 for S.A.S. entities; subscribed capital may be fixed at a symbolic value (e.g., COP 1 per share) and paid-in capital may be deferred for up to two years. No statutory escrow or deposit certificate is required before registration; cash contributions are payable to the corporate account as stipulated in the bylaws.

Funding & unlock path: No structural-load inspection is required for a virtual-office model. For any leased physical office, obtain the Certificado de Bomberos (fire-safety clearance) and register occupancy with the local civil-defense authority; implement the occupational health and safety system (SGSST) under Decreto 1072/2015, affiliate staff to an ARL, and maintain the 12-month workplace-risk assessment and telework protocols (Ley 1221/2008) for remote employees.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - official portal: www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned S.A.S., the entity must first execute its incorporation deed before a notary and register the company with the local Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio). Subsequently, the legal representative (including a foreign resident denominator) must obtain an electronic signature certificate from a DIAN-certified certification authority. Through the DIAN website, the representative completes the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) application, automatically assigning the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria). If the entity is liable for VAT or withholding, the corresponding tax registrations (IVA, ICA, etc.) are included in the RUT. Non-resident shareholders do not require a separate local TIN unless acting directly as taxable subjects; all registrations are executed in the name of the Colombian S.A.S.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not offer US-style check-the-box elections. The principal available elections are: (1) opting into the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) for service entities meeting applicable UVT-based gross income thresholds, which consolidates income tax, VAT, and other levies; (2) applying group taxation (bases fiscales consolidadas) for economically integrated groups; and (3) electing between national accounting standards and full IFRS for mapping book-tax differences.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exists for local businesses performing taxable services; all VAT-responsible entities are obliged to register. For non-resident digital service suppliers, a registration threshold of approximately 200,000,000 COP annual sales applies, adjusted periodically by the DIAN.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated for VAT purposes. For services (including virtual CFO services), the service must be provided to a client domiciled abroad, definitively used/exploited outside Colombia, and the export transaction must comply with DIAN rules and exchange-control regulations. If these conditions are satisfied, the S.A.S. may invoice at 0% VAT and claim offsetting input VAT credits via periodic returns.

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX) - customs export declaration
  • Bill of Lading (BL) or Airway Bill (AWB) for transport evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank proof of FX repatriation
  • Factura de Exportación - commercial export invoice
  • Service contract or purchase order executed with the foreign client

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) is required for all VAT-registered businesses. Invoices must be transmitted in real time in XML format via a DIAN-authorized provider or the DIAN’s free web/API platform. Each validated invoice receives a digital security stamp (CUDE). For exports, a special 'Factura de Exportación' must be issued. Full API integration with DIAN clearance is mandatory for all electronic invoice issuers.

DIAN may retroactively reverse zero-rating if export evidence is incomplete or fraudulent. Consequences include: surcharge of up to 200% of the VAT undercharged, default interest, administrative fines of up to 1,600 UVT per violation (Law 1819/2016), loss of input VAT credits previously recovered, and criminal prosecution for aggravated tax fraud. Failure to repatriate foreign exchange within 180 days triggers additional customs penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions, but every qualifying FX transaction must be channeled through the 'mercado cambiario', i.e., through an authorized intermediary (IMC) or a registered compensation account. Services exports (including virtual CFO and multicurrency bookkeeping services) are classified as current-account operations; profit remittances, capital contributions, and repatriation of capital are classified as capital-account operations. The principal practical hurdle is registration discipline: the foreign investment must be registered with the Banco de la República at the moment of channeling, and dividends or capital repatriations are not permitted to leave the country until such registration has been perfected and the corresponding exchange declarations have been filed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds arising from services rendered by the Colombian S.A.S. must be collected and channeled ('canalización') into Colombia through an authorized IMC within six (6) months from the completion of the service or issuance of the invoice, whichever is earlier. The full amount of proceeds is subject to the channeling obligation; although Colombia does not impose a fixed percentage-based forced conversion, the proceeds must be sold into the local market through the IMC or credited to an authorized compensation account. The IMC submits the relevant exchange declaration (Formulario de Declaración de Cambio por Exportación de Servicios) on each collection, and the exporter must retain the export contract, invoices, and proof of the effective collection for a five-year retention window.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (the Central Bank of Colombia), acting through its Departamento de Cambios Internacionales; the Colombian FX regime is codified in Decree 1068 of 2015 (Title 3, Single Regulatory Decree of the Finance and Public Credit Sector) and in Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, as amended. Day-to-day enforcement of tax and customs obligations associated with FX operations is supervised by the DIAN.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Viability: Medium (conditional). Non-resident digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not licensed by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and may not lawfully collect funds from the Colombian public or operate as the local peso settlement account; only SFC-authorized entities, including authorized SEDPE payment entities under Decree 2555 of 2010 and Law 1735 of 2014, may perform deposit-taking-like payment services in Colombia. The EMI can nevertheless be used as a lawful receiving rail for foreign-currency export proceeds provided the account is treated as a compensation account and declared under the DCIN-83 regime, and provided all funds are repatriated into the Colombian FX market within the statutory six-month window. EMI balances must not be used to compensate Colombian payroll or local vendors because such arrangements would circumvent the mandatory channeling rules and Colombian withholding obligations.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Single non-resident shareholder owning 100% of the S.A.S. with anticipated immediate cross-border profit distributions

Root cause: Single non-resident shareholder owning 100% of the S.A.S. with anticipated immediate cross-border profit distributions

Defense evidence: UBO register and SAGRILAFT documentation, chamber-of-commerce certificate of existence and legal representation, audited financial statements, evidence of Colombian economic substance (payroll, leased office or co-working space, local client contracts), and a signed business plan demonstrating ongoing Colombian operations.
High-volume multicurrency collections via third-party PSPs (Stripe Billing) with rapid payouts, flagged as a potential money-laundering or crowdfunding pattern

Root cause: High-volume multicurrency collections via third-party PSPs (Stripe Billing) with rapid payouts, flagged as a potential money-laundering or crowdfunding pattern

Defense evidence: Merchant agreements with the PSPs, monthly settlement reports, matching invoice and deliverable register, chargeback and refund ledger, treasury reconciliation policy using MT940 statements, and documented transaction monitoring under the SARLAFT framework.
Related-party management fees or intercompany commissions routed to individual foreign directors or offshore group entities

Root cause: Related-party management fees or intercompany commissions routed to individual foreign directors or offshore group entities

Defense evidence: Board minutes approving the transactions, signed intercompany service agreements, transfer pricing self-assessment and benchmarking study (Form 1126 filing, where applicable), withholding tax certificates, and proof of actual services rendered supported by time records and deliverable reports.
Attempts to withdraw cash or pay local suppliers in cash above the legal tax-deductibility threshold

Root cause: Attempts to withdraw cash or pay local suppliers in cash above the legal tax-deductibility threshold

Defense evidence: Board resolution prohibiting cash withdrawals, account instructions specifying wire-only disbursements, treasury manual, master service agreement payment terms, and a compliance training record demonstrating the anti-cash policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign equity capital intended for the Colombian S.A.S. must enter exclusively through the Colombian FX market: (i) the non-resident shareholder sends the funds via SWIFT MT103 to the S.A.S.'s foreign-currency account or the correspondent Nostro account of a Colombian authorized intermediary (IMC); (ii) at the moment of conversion to COP, the IMC prepares and files the exchange declaration applicable to foreign investment (Formulario de Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales), which simultaneously perfects the registration of the investment in the Banco de la República's foreign investment registry; (iii) the resulting COP balance is credited to the S.A.S. operational account; (iv) the S.A.S. must provide its tax identification number (NIT), the certificate of existence and legal representation, and a capital contribution resolution to support the channeling; (v) all subsequent capital repatriations and dividend remittances are contingent on this registration being valid and fully documented.For the virtual CFO services scope, disbursements are predominantly service imports and direct operational payments. The compliant clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer initiated from the S.A.S.'s peso account, with the IMC purchasing the required foreign currency and filing the Formulario de Declaración de Cambio por Importación de Servicios against the underlying invoice and deliverable evidence. Colombian source withholding tax and VAT obligations must be settled before or simultaneously with the transfer, and corresponding certificates retained. Commercial documentary letters of credit are not standard for services; where a client requires contract performance assurance, a standby letter of credit or bank guarantee issued by a Colombian bank is the appropriate instrument.

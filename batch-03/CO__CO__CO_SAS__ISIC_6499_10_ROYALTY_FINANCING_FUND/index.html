Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies under Ley 1258 de 2008, art. 5. Fully subscribed capital must be paid within two (2) years of incorporation; no escrow account is required for an S.A.S., but cash contributions must be traceable via the contributor's banking records and, where transactions exceed AML thresholds, reported to UIAF.

Funding & unlock path: Certificado de Condiciones de Seguridad Humana y Protección contra Incendios issued by the Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos covering occupancy capacity, evacuation, and firefighting equipment; structural and load inspection certificate signed by a licensed engineer; affiliation of all employees to a recognized ARL (occupational risk insurer) and implementation of the occupational safety and health management system under Chapter 6 of Resolution 1072 de 2015.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - National Tax and Customs Directorate of Colombia; electronic portal: MUISCA and Sede Electrónica DIAN at www.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian S.A.S., incorporation before the Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio) automatically creates the legal entity and DIAN assigns the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through the RUT (Registro Único Tributario). The S.A.S. must file the RUT with DIAN before carrying out taxable operations. For a foreign entity with Colombian-source income or opening a branch/PE in Colombia, the entity must appoint a legal representative domiciled in Colombia, submit apostilled/legalized constitutive documents and powers of attorney to the Chamber of Commerce, obtain registration in RUT as 'Responsable del Impuesto' under DIAN, and obtain a NIT. The NIT serves as both tax identification and VAT/ID number for all transactions, imports/exports, and electronic invoicing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not operate a check-the-box or comparable pass-through election; an S.A.S. is always fiscally opaque and is taxed at entity level. There is no hybrid mismatch election available domestically. The principal optional statutory mechanism is the SIMPLE regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación), available to eligible taxpayers with gross ordinary income not exceeding 100,000 UVT per year (approx. COP 4.7 billion for 2025), which consolidates income tax, VAT, and certain sectoral taxes into a single annual payment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover-based VAT registration threshold for corporate entities in Colombia; an S.A.S. must register in RUT as a responsible VAT taxpayer from its first taxable transaction. Natural persons in the simplified regime are subject to statutory thresholds, but a corporate entity has no de minimis. Optional SIMPLE regime threshold: 100,000 UVT per year (approximately COP 4.7 billion for 2025).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: physical exports from Colombian customs territory are eligible for 0% VAT when the goods actually leave Colombia, the exporter is registered in RUT and with DIAN as an exporter, the sale is made to a non-resident buyer, the DEX customs declaration is accepted by DIAN, and export proceeds are repatriated through the official foreign exchange market within 180 days from shipment. For services (including separately invoiced offshore services): the service must be rendered in Colombia, used or exploited exclusively outside Colombia, contracted by a foreign resident beneficiary without domicile/PE in Colombia, and paid through non-Colombian-source funds; the exporter must file the corresponding RUT registration as an exporter and keep contemporaneous evidence of the place of use/exploitation. For a revenue-based financing fund, the financing return itself is ordinarily outside the IVA taxable event; if any separate administrative, management, or collection fee is invoiced as an export service, the zero-rating substantiation rules must be satisfied.

  • Accepted Customs Export Declaration (DEX - 'Declaración de Exportación') filed through MUISCA and accepted by DIAN customs;
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or other transport document proving physical departure of the goods from Colombian customs territory;
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange dealer certificate ('Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas') evidencing collection of export proceeds by the exporter within the statutory FX period;
  • Commercial invoice and the DIAN electronic sales invoice (FEV/CUFE) issued with the corresponding export or zero-rated clause;
  • Underlying sale or service contract and, where applicable, documentary evidence of the foreign destination and place of economic use of the services.

Electronic invoicing is mandatory for all VAT-responsible taxpayers. The taxpayer must issue 'Factura Electrónica de Venta' (FEV) through DIAN-authorized software providers or DIAN-authorized in-house systems, using digital certificate-based authentication and linking each invoice to a unique CUFE (private issuers) or CUSE (official/controlled issuers). The XML invoice is transmitted to DIAN in real time for validation before the commercial transaction is completed; DIAN returns an acceptance or rejection and the invoice only becomes legally effective after acceptance. All tax returns, export declarations, and VAT documents are filed through MUISCA, with taxpayer-specific Web Service/API integration for accredited software.

DIAN cross-matches the DEX, electronic invoice, VAT returns, and foreign exchange records to verify the economic and physical substance of each export. If zero-rating is improperly claimed or documents are missing/contradictory, DIAN may issue an official assessment reclassifying the sale as taxable Colombian VAT at 19%, denying input VAT credits and refunds already paid, and demanding repayment with mora interest from the original filing date. This is accompanied by the statutory 'sanción por inexactitud' (generally 100% of the assessed tax overstatement) and, in aggravated cases, fines, suspension from the exporter's registry, and customs sanctions. Fraudulent export VAT refunds or false customs declarations also expose the entity and its legal representatives to criminal tax-fraud liability under Law 2277 of 2022, including imprisonment and fines in UVT, with clawback of the VAT benefit plus full interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed-float exchange-rate regime under the Régimen Cambiario (Decree 1068/2015). Current-account flows — including royalties, financing-service fees, and profit remittances — are convertible through authorized exchange-market intermediaries (IMCs) without a prior license, but each transaction requires a mandatory FX declaration to Banco de la República. Capital-account inflows and outflows require prior registration via the DECLARE electronic system. Dividend remittances by a CO_SAS to foreign shareholders are permissible after settlement of corporate income tax and the statutory non-resident dividend withholding tax (currently 10%); no separate central-bank remittance authorization is required, provided the outflow is channelled through an IMC and declared.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Decree 2505/2015 (goods) and Decree 1735/2014 (services), export and royalty-financing receivables must be collected through the exchange market and received by a local financial institution within 6 months of the date of service rendering or accrual. There is no mandatory peso-conversion ratio: proceeds may be sold to the exchange market or retained in registered foreign-currency accounts with a local authorized bank, in all cases declared to Banco de la República. Non-compliance triggers automatic conversion obligations, monetary penalties, and administrative FX infringement proceedings.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for offshore digital EMIs. Under Law 1735/2014 and Decree 2555/2010, only SFC-licensed SEDPEs (Sociedades Especializadas en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos) or fully supervised financial institutions may execute deposit and electronic-payment functions in COP; foreign EMIs cannot independently hold accounts or clear locally. Fintech rails are therefore limited to interoperable payment-initiation add-ons (ACH direct debit via PSE) layered onto a tier-1 bank master account.

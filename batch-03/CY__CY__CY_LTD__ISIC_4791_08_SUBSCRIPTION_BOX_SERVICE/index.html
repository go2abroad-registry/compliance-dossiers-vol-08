Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; a private limited company may be incorporated with EUR 1.00 issued capital. No escrow or blocked account is required by law for subscription-box/e-commerce retailing.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance from the Cyprus Fire Service must be obtained for any storage/fulfilment premises used by the subscription-box business, including proper means of escape and extinguisher servicing. If the floor area exceeds 200 square metres or the premises are structurally altered, a licensed civil engineer's structural safe-occupancy certificate and a municipal occupancy permit are required. The Department of Labour Inspection requires a workplace risk assessment and registration of the premises for occupational health and safety, including manual-handling and ergonomic controls for packing workers.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital; a private limited company may be incorporated with EUR 1.00 issued capital. No escrow or blocked account is required by law for subscription-box/e-commerce retailing.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; online Tax Portal (Ariadne) – https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy

E-government filing portal

For a Cyprus Private Limited Company: (1) incorporate with the Registrar of Companies to obtain the Company Registration Number; (2) submit the combined Tax Registration application through the Ariadne Tax Portal or by filing the prescribed TAX forms; (3) receive the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for corporate income tax; (4) if annual taxable supplies exceed the VAT threshold, separately apply for VAT registration with the VAT Service of the Tax Department; (5) complete registration before commencing taxable activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a Cyprus Private Limited Company: (1) incorporate with the Registrar of Companies to obtain the Company Registration Number; (2) submit the combined Tax Registration application through the Ariadne Tax Portal or by filing the prescribed TAX forms; (3) receive the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for corporate income tax; (4) if annual taxable supplies exceed the VAT threshold, separately apply for VAT registration with the VAT Service of the Tax Department; (5) complete registration before commencing taxable activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime. Cyprus Ltd is a fixed taxable entity. Available elections include VAT group registration, group relief election under the Income Tax Law, and capital allowance allocation elections. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under EU ATAD apply to deny double deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 annual taxable turnover for goods and services; €35,000 for intra-Community distance sales (subject to EU One Stop Shop union threshold of €10,000)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to territories outside the EU are zero-rated where the goods are physically removed from Cyprus within the required period and documentary proof is retained. Intra-Community supplies are zero-rated subject to the purchaser's valid VAT registration number and furnishing of compliant EC Sales Lists. Services directly linked to export movements or certain international transport services may qualify for zero-rating under the Cyprus VAT Law.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / SAD)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing removal from the EU
  • Commercial Invoice matching the customs declaration
  • VAT Invoice bearing the export exemption reference
  • Bank payment evidence or SWIFT remittance advice demonstrating receipt of consideration

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping currently in force. Cyprus is preparing for e-invoicing under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) proposals; existing rules require invoices to be issued within statutory time limits and to contain prescribed VAT content, with records retained.

The Cyprus Tax Department and Customs authorities may verify zero-rated transactions during VAT audits. False zero-rating claims trigger assessment to VAT, statutory interest, and administrative penalties of up to 100% of the VAT involved; criminal prosecution may lead to fines up to EUR 17,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Retrospective assessments may cover six years from the relevant tax period, extended to ten years in cases of fraud or deliberate non-declaration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus uses the euro, which is fully convertible. The Republic of Cyprus applies EU capital and payments liberalisation under Articles 63–66 TFEU; capital account transactions between Cyprus and third countries are unrestricted except for temporary safeguard measures and targeted Financial Action Task Force (FATF) or EU sanctions restrictions. Profit remittance by a Cyprus private limited company is not subject to exchange control approval, withholding tax on dividends is generally 0% under domestic law for resident companies and, where applicable, subject to EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or double tax treaty reductions. The only practical hurdles are commercial bank compliance procedures, beneficial ownership declarations, and anti-money laundering source-of-funds verification for inbound remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds in Cyprus. Export receipts may be retained in any currency, converted freely, or used for trade settlement without Central Bank of Cyprus approval, consistent with the euro-area foreign exchange regime and EU capital movement liberalisation. Exporters must still comply with statistical reporting obligations under the Balance of Payments and International Trade in Goods reporting framework of the Central Bank of Cyprus and the Cyprus Statistical Service, including transaction-level reporting thresholds and monthly settlement reporting windows, normally filed via the CBC reporting portal or authorised banking channels.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα Κύπρου), operating within the European System of Central Banks and the Eurosystem

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High, subject to strict licensing or passporting status. A Cyprus entity may use a Cyprus-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or a passported EU/EEA EMI under the Second Electronic Money Directive and PSD2, with the Central Bank of Cyprus exercising supervision and prudential oversight. Offshore non-EU EMIs are generally not permissible for holding client funds or making regulated payment transactions in Cyprus unless they obtain an EMI licence, establish a branch, or operate under an equivalent third-country passporting arrangement, which is not automatically available under the EU legislation. Practical viability for operational and treasury purposes is high for EU-authorized EMIs in the SEPA zone, but the subscription-box merchant should still maintain a substantive tier-1 bank account for capital escrow, trade settlement, and liquidity concentration.

Defense evidence: Corporate registry extract from Cyprus Registrar of Companies (HE77), full shareholding chain chart, UBO declaration form, certified passports and proof of residential address of all natural persons above 25%, apostilled certificate of incorporation, and signed director attestation regarding source of wealth.

Defense evidence: Executed standard-form subscription terms, sample order history and invoicing, logistics contracts with fulfilment warehouses, customs and export declarations, supplier contracts with proforma invoices, and bank statements or PSP settlement reports demonstrating matched cash flows.
Inbound capital injection from related-party lender or parent in a high-risk or non-transparent jurisdiction, particularly in the form of shareholder loans or quasi-equity

Defense evidence: Signed and notarised loan agreement, board resolution approving the borrowing, intercompany transfer pricing documentation (OECD CNCA-compliant or Cyprus TP rules), source of funds evidence from the lending bank, interest rate confirmation at arm's length, and sworn accountant certificate.

Defense evidence: Detailed transaction ledger, invoice-level reconciliation, customs declaration summaries, supplier payment records, bank confirmation of purpose codes, and a business narrative letter signed by the compliance officer or director explaining seasonality, refunds, and prepayment patterns.

Defense evidence: Notarised corporate resolutions confirming unified legal name and registered address, updated Registrar extract, bank statement in exact legal name, company statutory books, and signed indemnity where historical name changes exist.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed through a Cyprus resident bank or regulated EU EMI using SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer, accompanied by correct statistical purpose codes under the Central Bank of Cyprus Balance of Payments reporting framework. For equity capital, use BOP code E020 (or equivalent CBC reporting code for direct investment equity transactions) and in the wire reference include the corporate identification number, transaction nature, and invoice or board resolution reference. For shareholder loans, use the relevant direct investment other-capital code and attach the loan agreement and interest schedule. The receiving bank will require a Customer Due Diligence file, board resolution, and UBO declaration; funds remitted from third countries must be accompanied by a source-of-funds letter and the remitter's banking confirmation. Post-crediting, the entity must report the transaction to the Central Bank of Cyprus via the resident reporting agent, and all funds must be titled to and operationally controlled by CY_LTD (HE77) only.Outbound trade disbursements for physical subscription-box merchandise should be executed via SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments and via MT103/MT202 cover for treasury concentration. For high-value or documentary transactions, an Irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP600 ICC rules, confirmed or unconfirmed with deferred payment or sight payment, is the preferred rail. SEPA Instant Credit Transfer is permissible for EU counterparties up to the SEPA scheme transaction limit (currently EUR 100,000 per transaction), with TARGET2 and EURO1 used for large-value euro clearing. Payment purpose must be coded appropriately under the CBC statistical reporting taxonomy, typically Trade Credit or goods trade codes, and each disbursement must be matched to underlying supplier invoices, transport documents, and customs declarations to avoid treasury compliance flags.Outright FX Forwards (exchange rate agreements booked with a Cypriot authorised credit institution or EU-licensed investment firm under MiFID II)Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for restricted or less liquid emerging-market currencies, permitted where there is a genuine commercial underlying subscription-box procurement obligationCross-Currency Swaps (FX swaps and swap agreements) for managing dual-currency treasury flows, subject to EMIR clearing, margin, and reporting obligations when applicableCurrency Options (plain-vanilla FX options) permitted for hedge-only purposes when documented against anticipated trade costsFX Collars and Zero-Cost Structured Forwards, permissible only when the structure has a documented hedge rationale and is offered by a regulated Cyprus or EU counterparty

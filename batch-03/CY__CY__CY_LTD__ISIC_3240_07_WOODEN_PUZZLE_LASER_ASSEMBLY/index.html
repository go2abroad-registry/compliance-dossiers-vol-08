Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of Cyprus (Φορολογικό Τμήμα), Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus — official operational portal 'Tax For All' (https://taxforalls.cy); corporate income tax and VAT e-filing via the ARIADNE electronic filing system; customs filings via the Customs and Excise Department 'Theseas' (THΣΕΑΣ) single-window.

E-government filing portal

Stage 2: The RoC automatically transmits the incorporation certificate to the Tax Department. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Cyprus-resident private limited company is a separate taxable person taxed on worldwide income at the headline corporate income tax rate of 12.5%; Cyprus law does not confer pass-through status on an Ltd; the territorial exemption applies to qualifying dividend and foreign permanent-establishment income, and capital gains are taxed only on the alienation of Cyprus immovable property and certain securities; an exporter of wooden puzzles would be taxed at entity level with the manufacturing activities within the normal CIT net.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate US-style 'check-the-box' (Form 8832) elections; entity classification is a matter of legal form and tax residence in a unitary national system. The elective mechanisms that are available are: (1) VAT group registration election for closely related companies to be treated as a single taxable person (Article 12 of VAT Law 95(I)/2000); (2) election for the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for intra-EU distance sales and the VAT reverse-charge mechanism; (3) election between direct-attribution or pro-rata partial exemption methods for mixed VAT use; (4) the tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping activities; (5) the IP-box / patent-box election for intangible-backed manufacturing; and (6) alignment with EU anti-hybrid and ATAD / Pillar 2 rules — no local hybrid-mismatch election exists to overturn the entity-level taxation result for a Cyprus Ltd.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 (annual taxable turnover, exclusive of VAT, of a Cyprus-established taxable person) — non-resident suppliers with no fixed establishment must register with effect from the first taxable supply made in Cyprus unless covered by the EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-sales threshold or the OSS regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export supply of tangible goods (including CO2 laser-cut 3D wooden puzzle products) to non-EU destinations under Section 36 (zero-rated supplies) of the Cyprus VAT Law where the goods physically leave the European Union within the statutory timeframe, and to certain cross-border services to non-EU B2B customers under the place-of-supply rules (Section 37). Eligibility conditions: (i) the seller dispatches or transports the goods out of the EU; (ii) proof of export is secured no later than the statutory deadline for the VAT return period of the supply — the goods must actually leave the EU by the end of the month following the supply or within an extended justified period of up to 3 months; (iii) for puzzle products sold through Amazon or Stripe, B2C export evidence (customs export declaration, carrier tracker, proof of non-EU delivery address, payment confirmation) is required; (iv) for intra-EU supplies sent to Amazon FBA depots, the movement is not an export but a deemed supply/transfer requiring VAT registration in the destination member state (or OSS-based call-off arrangements), which is outside the export zero-rating route.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / SAD) lodged electronically via the Theseas system with the EX-code completing the export procedure
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing contracted physical removal from the EU to the non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a licensed Cyprus bank or evidence of foreign-currency receipt proving payment collection from the non-EU buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and the purchase/payment records of the buyer (Amazon settlement statements, Stripe payment receipts where applicable)

Cyprus at present does not operate a mandatory real-time electronic fiscal clearance system comparable to SdI (Italy) or MyDATA (Greece), and there is no digital stamping obligation for B2B export invoices. However: (a) electronic invoices are legally recognised where authenticity of origin and integrity of content are ensured; (b) all VAT-registered suppliers must file electronic periodic VAT returns via ARIADNE; (c) cross-border B2B supplies require monthly EC Sales List (VIES) reporting of EU customer VAT IDs; (d) exporters must use the Theseas single-window for electronic Single Administrative Document (SAD) customs declarations; (e) a Cypriot EORI number is mandatory; (f) PEPPOL-based B2G/B2B e-invoicing and post-2026 ViDA clearance measures are expected to be phased in but are not yet in force for CY_LTD in the 2024–2025 period.

Cyprus Customs and the Tax Department apply a strict documentary-evidence standard for export zero-rating. If export evidence is missing, late, or does not satisfy the SAD/hard-copy proof test, the 0% rate is denied and normal 19% VAT is assessed (retrospective clawback), together with (i) default interest at 1.75% per month on the VAT due, (ii) an administrative penalty of EUR 500 per infringement plus EUR 50 for each month of late return filing, and (iii) restitution of any refunded input VAT. Fraudulent zero-rating claims — knowingly false customs declarations or counterfeit inward-remittance certificates — attract criminal penalties: fines of up to EUR 5,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years under the VAT Law and the Assessment & Collection of Taxes Law, in addition to full VAT and interest recovery. Although the Cyprus tax statute does not itself impose a 180-day FX-repatriation deadline, the 180-day parameter is adopted under this dossier as the prudential banking-evidence convention; exports remaining uncollectable beyond 180 days invite intensified audit scrutiny of both the zero-rating claim and the transfer-pricing remuneration of CY_LTD.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility. As an EU/EMU Member State, Cyprus applies Article 63 TFEU free movement of capital; no foreign exchange licensing, prior approval, monetary conversion premium, or exchange control permit exists for residents or non-resident investors. The euro is the domestic legal tender, eliminating internal convertibility risk. The 2013 emergency capital restriction measures (imposed 28 March 2013 following the financial crisis) were progressively relaxed and fully abolished by CBC announcement of 4 April 2015; no residual quantitative limits on fund transfers, dividend payments, or capital repatriation remain. Profit remittance hurdles: there are no FX-specific impediments to dividend distribution or shareholder loan repayment; the only structural constraints are general corporate law solvency tests (Cyprus Companies Law, Cap.113), a 0% dividend withholding tax on distributions to non-resident shareholders (WHT exemption applies if the beneficial owner is not a Cyprus tax resident; no Cyprus Special Defence Contribution applies to non-residents), and ordinary anti-money-laundering source-of-funds verification by the remitting bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion obligation exists under Cypriot law. Exporters of goods (including CO2 laser-cut wooden puzzles) may retain 100% of export receipts in any convertible currency (USD, GBP, CNY etc.) without conversion to EUR or a forced deposit requirement. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% of export receipts. The only regulatory duty is statistical: export receipts are captured via monthly BoP reporting by the CBC-licensed credit institution under the BoP Regulation; transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported on a transaction-level basis (typically T+5 business days, using the ECB's BoP coding framework, with 'Goods — General merchandise' for the ISIC 3240 product flow). In practice the bank requires the exporter to provide the customs exports declaration (CBMER — Customs Bill of Material in the Single Administrative Document) and the underlying sales invoice to tag the receipt for BoP reporting. No forced timeline for conversion or repatriation exists.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC) [Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου], established under the Central Bank of Cyprus Law 2002 (L.138(I)/2002), operating within the Eurosystem/ESCB framework since the Republic of Cyprus adopted the euro on 1 January 2008. For the euro-denominated monetary policy environment, the CBC acts as the national competent authority for banking supervision, prudential supervision under the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM) for significant institutions, payment system oversight, and balance-of-payments (BoP) statistical collection under ECB Regulation (EU) No 1333/2019 (recast of ECB Regulation (EC) No 184/2005, 'BoP Regulation').

Feasibility Rating: Medium — eligible for secondary/collections use but NOT acceptable as the primary treasury settlement or capital injection account. Compliant protocol for non-resident owners injecting liquidity: (i) Equity capital: wire from the UBO's corporate/personal bank account in EUR to the Cyprus Ltd's EUR capital escrow account at a CBC-licensed bank; the SWIFT MT103 field 70 must read 'Direct Investment — Equity Capital (BoP FA.1)' with the company's HE77 registration number referenced; no CBC authorization is required, but the bank reports the receipt in the monthly BoP return under ECB Regulation 1333/2019; the company must file the allotment resolution with the Registrar of Companies within 3 months and provide bank confirmation of receipt to the auditor.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an S.A.S.; shares may be subscribed and paid in up to two years, and no escrow deposit is required at incorporation. The SFC P2P lending authorization, however, will impose an additional patrimonial own-funds requirement determined under Decreto 2555 de 2010, Book 2, Part 1, Title 3, as amended by Decreto 1357 de 2018.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Certificado de Bomberos) from the Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos after inspection of extinguishers, alarms, and evacuation routes; structural load and seismic safety certificate in accordance with NSR-10 (Ley 400 de 1997); land-use certificate (Certificado de Uso del Suelo) from Secretaría Distrital de Planeación; mandatory employee health and safety registration with Ministerio del Trabajo (Reglamento de Higiene y Seguridad Industrial) and ARL affiliation covering occupational risk; for critical hardware rooms, additional UPS, ventilation, and fire-suppression inspection may be required.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an S.A.S.; shares may be subscribed and paid in up to two years, and no escrow deposit is required at incorporation. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – portal: www.dian.gov.co (MUISCA and VUCE platforms for taxpayer services, registration, and electronic invoicing validation)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by registering with DIAN through a duly appointed legal representative or apoderado in Colombia. The process begins by filing the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) via the MUISCA electronic portal, submitting the certified corporate existence documents (e.g., certificate of incorporation and bylaws) apostilled or legalized, the power of attorney granted to the Colombian representative, and proof of foreign exchange authorization if required. After issuance of the NIT and RUT, the entity must register for the applicable tax regime (common VAT regime, income tax, withholding agents) and enroll for electronic invoicing. Branches or subsidiaries organized as local SAS entities automatically receive a NIT upon incorporation with the Chamber of Commerce.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by registering with DIAN through a duly appointed legal representative or apoderado in Colombia. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not provide US-style check-the-box elections. Colombian SAS entities are taxed at the corporate entity level and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) the simplified taxation regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación – RST) for eligible enterprises meeting gross-revenue thresholds (option to replace income tax and complementary taxes with a simplified levy, subject to DIAN consent), (ii) the Zona Franca regime for qualifying industrial or commercial operations (reduced CIT rate of 20% subject to employment and investment requirements), and (iii) certain hybrid mismatch adjustments under Law 2277/2022 and OECD BEPS-aligned rules that limit deductions for payments subject to no or low taxation in the recipient jurisdiction. No check-the-box election is available for the Colombian SAS; full entity-level taxation applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for corporate entities (SAS); VAT registration and collection are mandatory from the first taxable operation in Colombian territory. For resident natural persons qualifying under the simplified VAT regime, the threshold is gross income of up to 3,500 tax value units (UVT) in the prior taxable year – approximately COP 165,634,000 for tax year 2024. SAS entities are always classified in the common VAT regime regardless of revenue level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to the export of goods from Colombia and to the provision of qualifying export services. For services, including digital financial or platform services, the exporter must demonstrate that the service is rendered to a non-resident recipient with the economic and effective use of the service occurring entirely outside Colombia, and obtain and preserve the supporting documentation (International Service Export Form, Declaration of Export of Services, and proof of foreign payment). Goods exports require an exit customs declaration and compliance with free-zone or Aduana procedures as applicable. In all cases, the zero-rating leaves the exporter the right to claim refundability of related input VAT paid on costs, subject to DIAN approval.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX/Déclaration de Salida)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Truck Manifest (transport evidence issued by the carrier)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (certificado de reembolso de divisas) or certification of receipt of payment in foreign currency from the exporter's local bank

Electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta – FEV) is mandatory for all VAT-registered parties with business operations. Companies must use DIAN-authorized technological providers (proveedores tecnológicos) or DIAN's free issuing application, issuing each invoice through an authorized software system that transmits the invoice and constituent elements to DIAN in real-time. DIAN must validate the invoice before the purchaser may exercise tax credits; upon validation, a CUNE (Número Único de Facturación Electrónica) is issued. In addition, equivalent electronic documents (such as credit notes, debit notes, and export supporting documents) must be reported through the RADIAN system. Real-time digital stamping is not a separate process; instead, the mandatory pre-validation by DIAN applies. For business-to-consumer transactions, the platform must likewise issue the electronic invoice or an equivalent ticket through the authorized system, unless an exemption applies (e.g., certain no-tax-special regime transactions).

DIAN conducts strict audits on zero-rating claims. If documentation is incomplete or false, DIAN will disallow the zero rate, reclassify the transaction as a taxable local supply at 19% VAT, and also recapture any VAT refunds previously granted. The company becomes liable for the unpaid VAT, default interest, and inflation-adjusted fines calculated on the tax understatement. Under Law 2277/2022, fraudulent submission of false documents or use of fictitious export transactions may trigger criminal tax fraud charges punishable by imprisonment (up to 60 months) and larger penalties. Additionally, the authorities may suspend or revoke the exporter's authorization to issue invoices and impose sanctions of up to 1,000 UVT for failure to comply with electronic invoicing or cross-border documentation rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating exchange rate regime. The Colombian peso (COP) is fully convertible for current account transactions (imports, exports, services, debt servicing, and profit remittances), subject to mandatory filing of the Declaración de Cambio (Exchange Declaration, Form No. 10) with the Banco de la República through an authorized intermediary for transactions above the threshold of USD 10,000 or equivalent. Capital account operations, including foreign direct investment (FDI) into a Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.), require prior foreign investment registration under Resolución 1 of 2018; unregistered investment loses access to the guaranteed remittance channel. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are freely allowed after compliance with transfer pricing documentation under Articles 260-2 of the Colombian Tax Code, with a general 1.5% remittance tax on payments to foreign beneficiaries subject to treaty relief and an additional 5% withholding tax on dividends under Law 2277 of 2022 when the beneficiary is a foreign entity not benefiting from a tax treaty. The P2P micro-lending platform's revenue stream (interest spreads and loan servicing fees) is a current account item and thus eligible for full convertibility, but platform lending operations that originate borrower repayments in COP require clear tracing of funds to maintain current-account treatment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia does not maintain a forced export-proceeds surrender regime. Exporters and service suppliers must repatriate and negotiate export revenues exclusively through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries. For goods exports, proceeds must be collected within a statutory window of 120 days from customs clearance; for software and digital services exports, collection must generally occur within the term agreed in the contract but not exceeding 180 days unless an extension is approved by the Banco de la República. The P2P micro-lending platform, however, is not an exporter; its material inbound flows consist of foreign capital injections and platform operational income. Inbound foreign investment equity contributions must be reported to the Banco de la República within 60 days following the remittance date using Form 10; loan repayments from Colombian borrowers do not constitute export proceeds and are treated as local circulating funds in COP. The platform must further classify any foreign currency conversion through an authorized intermediary and ensure that each commercial transaction is matched with its corresponding exchange declaration for audit trail purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) for prudential supervision and the Unidad de Información y Análisis Financiero (UIAF) for AML/CFT supervision; foreign exchange regulation is issued under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1068 of 2015, and Circular Externa DCIN-83, with operational enforcement through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries (commercial banks and foreign exchange houses) and customs/DIAN oversight of trade and capital flows.

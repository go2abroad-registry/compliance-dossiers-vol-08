Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; electronic portal: Tax Portal (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must file the prescribed VAT registration application with the District VAT Office, supported by apostilled certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, board resolution appointing officers and the Cypriot tax representative if applicable, and proof of overseas address. For a non-EU entity, appointment of a tax representative established in Cyprus is normally required for VAT purposes. The Tax Department issues the TIN/VAT number upon approval; the same TIN is used for income tax, employer payroll, and VAT filing obligations. Completion of additional registrations (e.g. customs e-ORI number for export declarations, Intrastat/VIES enrolment) is mandatory for export traders.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Cyprus private limited company is a separate legal and taxable person; if managed and controlled in Cyprus, it is tax resident and taxed on worldwide income at 12.5%. Non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income and on income attributable to a Cyprus permanent establishment. No pass-through treatment is available for a Ltd.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Cyprus. A limited company cannot elect transparent treatment. Available statutory elections include VAT group registration, group loss relief and group tax consolidation elections, tonnage tax election for shipping, IP Box notional deduction, Notional Interest Deduction on new equity, and hybrid mismatch adjustment elections under EU ATAD transposition.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 per annum for established taxable persons; EUR 35,000 for intra-Community distance sales of goods; EUR 0 threshold for non-established/non-resident suppliers where the first taxable supply in Cyprus triggers registration

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods may be zero-rated when supplied and dispatched or transported by the supplier, the customer, or a third party to a destination outside the European Union, provided the goods physically leave Cyprus and the EU within the statutory evidence period. Services related to exported goods and certain offshore B2B services supplied to non-EU recipients are outside the scope of Cyprus VAT or zero-rated where the recipient is established outside the EU. The supplier must hold documentary proof of export and treat the supply as export zero-rated in the VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing despatch to a non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT/MT103 credit confirmation evidencing receipt of sale proceeds in Cyprus
  • Proof of non-EU customer status and export ownership (purchase order, correspondence, and, where applicable, supplier export register entry)

Cyprus does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, digital stamping, or a state-administered e-invoice API. Electronic invoicing is lawful under EU invoicing rules; invoices may be issued in PDF with valid VAT data, via EDI, or using Peppol-compatible channels. The Tax Department performs periodic risk-based audit review of VAT returns and invoice data rather than transaction-by-transaction pre-clearance.

Export zero-rating is strictly audited. If the Tax Department finds insufficient export evidence, false declarations, or diversion of the goods back into the EU/domestic market, VAT at 19% is retroactively assessed together with statutory interest, civil penalties up to the VAT evaded, potential criminal fines and imprisonment, and revocation of the VAT registration. Under anti-money-laundering and banking practice, export proceeds must be repatriated to the Cyprus entity through banking channels; unexplained failure to repatriate within 180 days may trigger AML reporting, customs audit, and retrospective clawback of export refunds even where no specific VAT law imposes a separate 180-day customs penalty.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full currency convertibility and no material profit-remittance hurdles. Cyprus has been a Eurozone member since 1 January 2008 and, as an EU Member State, guarantees free movement of capital under Article 63 TFEU; there is no exchange-control statute and no central bank approval is required for current-account or capital-account transfers. Profit remittance is unrestricted: Cyprus does not impose withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, and the Special Defence Contribution (SDC) applies only to Cyprus tax-resident corporate beneficiaries. For a CY_LTD engaged in FPV racing-drone manufacturing and retail, practical friction is limited to EUR/USD conversion spreads, correspondent-bank compliance checks on inbound/outbound wires, and anti-money-laundering documentation rather than statutory convertibility or remittance restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rule. All exchange-control restrictions in Cyprus were fully abolished in 1991; there is no statutory deadline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio into EUR, and no requirement to deposit export receipts in a designated account. Export receipts in USD, CNY, or other currencies may be retained freely in foreign-currency accounts at Cypriot banks. The only applicable obligation is statistical reporting: resident companies must report transaction details to the Central Bank of Cyprus through their bank for transactions above the EUR 12,500 threshold under EU Regulation (EC) No 184/2000 and the Central Bank's Balance of Payments directives.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), operating as the national competent authority within the European System of Central Banks and the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM) of the European Central Bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate-to-high viability for secondary collection and payout rails; low viability as the sole treasury platform. EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) passporting into Cyprus under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) may offer multi-currency IBANs, SEPA and SWIFT access, and cost-efficient FX conversion; however, they do not provide documentary trade finance, commercial letters of credit, or a direct correspondent-banking relationship for large cross-border wires. Non-EU offshore EMIs without a PSD2 passport are not permitted to operate in Cyprus and generate severe AML compliance and sanctions-exposure risk. PayPal and Stripe are accepted as practical e-commerce collection rails for the target retail business, but their balances should be swept at least daily into a Tier-1 Cypriot commercial bank operating account rather than held in an EMI wallet for any extended period.

Equity contributions from shareholders must be routed by SWIFT MT103 (for non-EEA remitters) or SEPA Credit Transfer (for EU/EEA remitters) to the Cyprus operational account in EUR, with the remittance narrative clearly stating 'Equity injection - share allotment' and using the appropriate EU Balance of Payments purpose code under Category Q for direct investment. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cyprus TP documentation rules apply to related-party transactions where the annual aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds EUR 750,000; a Master File is required if the multinational group’s consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000; transactions below EUR 750,000 are eligible for a simplified TP documentation exemption, but a contemporaneous defence file should be retained in all cases.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not maintain a framework of Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) analogous to the UAE or British overseas territories; instead, corporate residence is grounded in the central management and control test — a company is Cyprus tax resident if it is managed and controlled in Cyprus. The Cyprus Tax Department applies functional and economic substance analysis in audits, requiring adequate premises, employees, decision-making, and corporate governance. Additional substance requirements exist for the IP Box regime, where the ratio of qualifying expenditure controls the tax advantage, and for limitation of interest deductions under the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) provisions, which require a genuine economic reason for excessive levels of borrowing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years, a criminal fine up to EUR 50,000, plus Civil Penalty equal to 100% of the evaded tax and statutory interest from the due date; administrative penalty of EUR 100 plus late filing surcharges; the Tax Commissioner may also issue an assessment with anti-avoidance adjustment, which automatically overrides any treaty shelter.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the full tax debt where they have authorised, counselled, or acquiesced in the evasion; piercing the corporate veil is permitted by Cyprus courts if the company is a mere instrumentality. Directors face disqualification for up to five years, travel bans pending settlement, and personal bankruptcy proceedings; imprisonment is imposed on clinically involved directors in cases of fraudulent misrepresentation.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Loss of Cyprus tax residency; retroactive assessments at the 12.5% rate on worldwide income or, if the company is deemed managed from a higher-tax jurisdiction, additional taxes and penalties under that jurisdiction; Cypriot authorities may impose a fine up to EUR 20,000 and revoke the company’s tax identification number if disclosures are materially false.

Director personal liability: Directors become liable for the entire underpaid tax plus interest when the corporate form is disregarded as a sham; the court can order immediate appointment of a receiver to liquidate personal assets; a travel restriction order is available to prevent the director from leaving Cyprus if the tax debt exceeds EUR 5,000. Persistent violation triggers criminal sanction of up to 5 years’ imprisonment.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Infraction

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties up to EUR 1,000,000 for legal persons, suspension of the company’s registration for up to 12 months on a compliance order, forced deregistration, freezing of corporate bank accounts, and criminal prosecution of the beneficial owner for money laundering carrying imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of laundered amounts; they may be disqualified from serving in any company for up to five years; travel bans and temporary passport revocation are common interim measures pending trial. Where the corporate veil is pierced, the full benefit of the Cyprus tax rate is voided, and the director’s personal movable and immovable assets become subject to a statutory charge until all fines and confiscation orders are satisfied.

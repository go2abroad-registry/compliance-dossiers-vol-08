Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Ministry of Finance), Nicosia; district tax offices nationwide; official e-services and filing via the TAXISnet portal at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation with the Cyprus Registrar of Companies (DRCOR), the Cyprus private limited company is automatically registered for corporate income tax and receives a Tax Identification Code (TIC). The registration is completed by filing the corporate notification forms and constitutional documents with the Tax Department. For VAT, an established taxable person must submit Form VAT 101 within 30 days of exceeding the EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover threshold, or may apply voluntarily. Non-established foreign suppliers have no registration threshold and must register before the first taxable supply in Cyprus; non-EU-established suppliers generally must appoint a Cyprus tax representative unless they use the applicable EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme. The Tax Department issues a VAT registration certificate with a VAT number in the format CY########X.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the Cyprus Ltd is a separate taxable corporate person; tax residence follows central management and control in Cyprus, triggering taxation on worldwide income under the Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002). Non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Cyprus Ltd is a separate taxable corporate person; tax residence follows central management and control in Cyprus, triggering taxation on worldwide income under the Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002). Non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. No pass-through classification exists for private limited companies; limited exceptions include the foreign permanent establishment exemption under Article 8(24) ITA.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available; classification follows the Companies Law and Income Tax Law. Available statutory elections and claims include: (i) foreign PE profit exemption under Article 8(24) ITA; (ii) group loss relief surrender under Article 27 ITA between 75% qualifying group companies; (iii) optional VAT group registration under Article 17 of the Value Added Tax Law (Law 94(I)/2000); (iv) application for advance tax rulings; and (v) the tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping activities. Hybrid mismatch neutralization under Cyprus implementation of EU ATAD II applies mandatorily without an election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for established taxable persons; no threshold for non-established foreign suppliers; EUR 10,000 EU-wide cross-border distance-sale micro-business threshold for Union OSS consumption supplies to other Member States.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods to non-EU destinations qualify for 0% VAT under Article 21 of the VAT Law when title transfers to the customer abroad and the goods are dispatched or transported out of the European Union within 90 days of the supply. For automated dropshipping, each consignment must be individually evidenced as physically exiting the EU via a customs export declaration or low-value dispatch statement. Supplies of goods to EU consumers in another Member State are treated as distance sales and are taxable in the destination Member State; no Cypriot VAT applies where the Union OSS is used and the EUR 10,000 cross-border threshold is exceeded. Electronically supplied services to customers outside Cyprus and outside the EU fall outside the Cypriot VAT base under the place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic SAD) accepted by the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department or the relevant EU member state customs office
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or international courier tracking receipt evidencing physical exit from the customs territory of the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, and Incoterms confirming the delivery destination
  • Packing list matching declared goods, weights, and unit counts
  • Customs exit proof (CUS notification or equivalent export completion document)
  • Intra-Community supply evidence for EU consignments, including VIES records, CMR, and customer VAT registration certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, API approval, or digital stamping mandate is currently in force. E-invoicing is permitted under the VAT Law and EU Directive 2014/55/EU; PEPPOL-compliant B2G e-invoicing applies to central government procurement. Cyprus is actively preparing for the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) package, which will introduce mandatory standardized e-invoicing and real-time digital reporting; no national implementation date has been enacted.

The Tax Department applies heightened scrutiny to dropshipping structures where export evidence is incomplete or the goods do not demonstrably leave the EU. If export zero-rating is disallowed, the supply is reclassified as a Cyprus taxable supply and VAT is assessed retrospectively at 19%, plus default interest and penalties scaling from 10% of the tax undercharged for negligent misreporting up to 100% for deliberate or fraudulent concealment. Criminal prosecution may result in fines and imprisonment for up to 5 years. The standard assessment window is 5 years, extended to 10 years for fraudulent behavior, and documentary retention is mandatory for 6 years. Cyprus imposes no formal exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark serves as the customary evidentiary tolerance for receipt of export consideration in the substantiation bundle.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full Eurozone member and the euro (EUR) is fully convertible. Free movement of capital is constitutionally guaranteed under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), covering both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no prior approvals, and no repatriation restrictions for either capital account or current account flows. Profit remittance is unrestricted: dividends paid by a Cyprus Ltd to non-resident shareholders are exempt from Cyprus withholding tax under domestic law, subject only to EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) substance requirements and general anti-abuse rules. The only operational obligations are statistical: cross-border transactions must be reported to the ECB balance-of-payments (BoP) reporting framework through the authorized reporting agent (the receiving/paying bank) when individual transactions exceed €12,500 or annual aggregate flows exceed €125,000. EU restrictive measures (sanctions lists), FATF recommendations, and CBC AML/CFT directives apply to transaction screening and may functionally block transfers to listed counterparties but do not otherwise constrain convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export receipts may be held indefinitely in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts. For the automated dropshipping model (ISIC 4791), physical goods are typically shipped directly from third-country suppliers (e.g., China, Vietnam, Hong Kong) to end consumers, so goods never transit Cyprus customs territory; nevertheless, the Cyprus entity must retain full commercial documentation (purchase orders, supplier invoices, AWB waybills, consumer delivery confirmations) to substantiate the export-of-services/commission character of its income for VAT (EU OSS/IOSS regime) and corporate tax filings.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου, CBC) as the national competent authority for banking supervision and prudential regulation; European Central Bank (ECB) as the supranational monetary and supervisory authority within the Eurosystem. CBC licenses and supervises credit institutions, payment institutions, and electronic money institutions under the Banking Law of 1997 (Law 66(I)/1997), the Payment Services Law of 2018 (Law 31(I)/2018), and the Electronic Money Law of 2012 (Law 81(I)/2012).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for EEA-licensed EMIs that passport into Cyprus under PSD2 (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Monese, Payhawk); MEDIUM for Cyprus-licensed EMIs (Law 81(I)/2012) that offer faster onboarding but narrower trade-finance functionality; LOW for offshore unregulated EMIs or non-EEA money transmitters, which cannot lawfully provide payment services in Cyprus and create unacceptable correspondent, AML/CFT, and sanctions-screening risk. Legal constraints: (1) only EMIs holding a Cyprus license or a valid EEA passport may issue e-money and execute payment transactions in Cyprus; (2) EMIs are prohibited from granting credit or operating as treasury/credit intermediaries; (3) EMIs must maintain client funds in a safeguarding account held at an EEA credit institution, creating an operational dependency on Tier-1 domestic banks; (4) the automated dropshipping profile (high transaction velocity, global pay-ins via Stripe/PayPal, third-party supplier out-payments) will trigger enhanced due diligence and likely velocity caps at every EMI. Recommended structure: maintain the primary regulated settlement account at Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank, and use a single EMI account as a secondary collection/settlement rail for PSP payouts only.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

High-velocity e-commerce pay-ins aggregated through third-party PSPs (Stripe/PayPal) with no transparent payer identification and elevated refund/chargeback frequency

Root cause: High-velocity e-commerce pay-ins aggregated through third-party PSPs (Stripe/PayPal) with no transparent payer identification and elevated refund/chargeback frequency

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity injections must be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own bank account to the CY_LTD EUR operational account; the remittance information field must state 'Share Capital Contribution' and reference the board resolution and, where applicable, the special resolution approving the share issuance. The sending bank account must belong to the registered shareholder/UBO to avoid third-party funding red flags. ECB balance-of-payments statistical reporting applies (report through the Cypriot receiving bank if individual transaction ≥ €12,500 or annual aggregate ≥ €125,000); the bank will file the BoP return using the correct external transaction classification (e.g., C12 'Investment Fund Shares/Direct Investment'). Any issuance of shares above authorized capital must be filed with the Department of Registrar of Companies using form HE32/HE33 within the statutory period; a minimum capital is not legally required, but the bank will observe that initial capital (commonly €1,000–€10,000) is commensurate with the stated e-commerce operating costs. For PSP (Stripe/PayPal) capital inflows, activate EUR payout settlement and complete PSP KYC with bank-account micro-deposit verification; set payout currency to EUR to avoid platform FX conversion margin. Intercompany loans from a parent entity must be supported by a written facility agreement with arm's length interest under OECD transfer pricing and ATAD substance rules, routed via SEPA Credit Transfer (within EEA) or SWIFT MT103 (outside EEA) using purpose code 'Intercompany Loan'.Outbound supplier payments in the SEPA zone (EU/EEA) are executed via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) in EUR; non-EEA suppliers (China, Hong Kong, Vietnam, US) are paid via SWIFT MT103 with instruction category 'Trade payment – Goods (GDS)' and full beneficiary banking details. Each disbursement must be supported by a commercial invoice, packing list, and logistics track-and-trace record to satisfy trade-based money-laundering (TBML) checks. For high-value or recurring supplier relationships exceeding €50,000 per transaction, a Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP 600) issued by Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank is recommended, with the option of documentary collection (D/P or D/A) for mid-sized suppliers. PSP outbound settlement is limited to the PSP's payout engine: Stripe/PayPal settle gross sales net of fees to the CY bank account; the Cyprus entity then executes supplier wires from the settlement account, maintaining a documented payment-matching policy. For USD- or GBP-denominated PSP balances, instruct same-bank FX conversion into EUR rather than executing cross-currency SWIFT transfers to minimize transaction costs and avoid double FX conversion. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH for EEA-licensed EMIs that passport into Cyprus under PSD2 (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Monese, Payhawk); MEDIUM for Cyprus-licensed EMIs (Law 81(I)/2012) that offer faster onboarding but narrower trade-finance functionality; LOW for offshore unregulated EMIs or non-EEA money transmitters, which cannot lawfully provide payment services in Cyprus and create unacceptable correspondent, AML/CFT, and sanctions-screening risk. Legal constraints: (1) only EMIs holding a Cyprus license or a valid EEA passport may issue e-money and execute payment transactions in Cyprus; (2) EMIs are prohibited from granting credit or operating as treasury/credit intermediaries; (3) EMIs must maintain client funds in a safeguarding account held at an EEA credit institution, creating an operational dependency on Tier-1 domestic banks; (4) the automated dropshipping profile (high transaction velocity, global pay-ins via Stripe/PayPal, third-party supplier out-payments) will trigger enhanced due diligence and likely velocity caps at every EMI. Recommended structure: maintain the primary regulated settlement account at Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank, and use a single EMI account as a secondary collection/settlement rail for PSP payouts only.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not have a codified ESR comparable to UAE regulations, but applies a de facto economic substance doctrine through management-and-control tax residence under the Income Tax Act, OECD BEPS Action 5 substantive activity requirements, and the DTA principal purpose test; automated dropshipping companies must evidence real board oversight, physical or virtual operational presence in Cyprus, actual decision-making personnel, and management of the payment rails and supplier network from Cyprus.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Additional tax equal to up to 100% of the evaded tax, interest at 5% per annum, administrative penalties up to €10,000, and criminal conviction leading to a fine not exceeding €50,000 or imprisonment up to 5 years (or both).

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved false returns are personally exposed to criminal sanction, civil restitution, asset forfeiture, travel bans and disqualification; Cyprus courts will pierce the veil where the company was a mere façade concealing personal diversion of funds.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Loss of tax residence, denial of the Tax Resident Certificate, automatic 12.5% assessment on actual commercial profits plus 5% interest, penalties for late filing, strike-off and dissolution of the company under the Companies Law, and denial of DTA benefits under the principal purpose test.

Director personal liability: Nominee and shadow directors can be held jointly liable for taxes avoided through the fake substance arrangement; the Tax Authority may prohibit departure from Cyprus, freeze personal bank accounts, and bring perjury/civil fraud actions for signatures on false declarations.
AML / Money Laundering through Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (Cap. 188), penalties: fine up to €500,000 and/or imprisonment up to 8 years; the PSP agreement will be terminated and the company listed in Cyprus's AML registry; confiscation orders can attach company assets.

Director personal liability: The compliance officer and every responsible director are personally liable if AML systems were knowingly absent; criminal convictions carry travel bans and personal confiscation; a corporate fine does not shield directors from secondary prosecution.
Transfer Pricing / DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Section 33A transfer pricing adjustment of income at the arm's length level, additional corporate tax at 12.5%, 5% annual interest, a surcharge of up to 100% of the underpaid tax, and denial of DTA benefits where the main purpose was treaty shopping.

Director personal liability: Directors approving documented cross-border arrangements that lack economic justification are jointly and severally liable for the tax adjustment; the court can order restoration of diverted profits, travel bans, and criminal liability for fraud when the TP file was fabricated.

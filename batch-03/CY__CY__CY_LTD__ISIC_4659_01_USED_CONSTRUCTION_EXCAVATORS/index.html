Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus (Tmima Forologias); integrated e-portal 'Tax For All' (Ariadne) at https://taxforall.mof.gov.cy with legacy TaxisNet at https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy; VAT administration by the VAT Commissioner within the same Tax Department; Customs export processing by Cyprus Customs (Ministry of Finance) under the EU Customs Code (Regulation (EU) No 952/2013).

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of a Cyprus Ltd, the Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DRCOR) automatically notifies the Tax Department through the integrated register, resulting in issuance of the company Tax Identification Code (TIC). For the used-machinery trading company, the standard sequence is: (i) obtain Certificate of Incorporation (HE number) from DRCOR; (ii) confirm or amend the TIC via Tax For All using TFA forms; (iii) file VAT Form 101 (Application for Registration) with the VAT Commissioner once taxable turnover exceeds or is expected within 30 days to exceed EUR 15,600; (iv) for a foreign-incorporated vendor trading through a Cyprus PE, complete TFA1 registration, appoint a Cyprus fiscal representative under VAT Law Art. 54A, and provide certified constitutional documents, board resolution, power of attorney, bank reference, and PE evidence; a non-resident seller with no PE and no Cyprus fixed establishment is generally required to register for VAT from its first taxable supply in Cyprus.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Cyprus-resident Ltd subject to 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income with unilateral and treaty foreign tax credit relief; territorial scope applies to non-residents limited to Cyprus-source income; no pass-through election is available to a Ltd; standard capital gains taxation on Cyprus real property and certain share disposals; company is outside any special tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections: (i) IP Box regime election (80% qualifying IP income deduction under Income Tax Law Art. 9A); (ii) Notional Interest Deduction election on new equity (Income Tax Law Art. 9B); (iii) Group Relief election for intra-group loss surrender (Income Tax Law Art. 13A); (iv) VAT group registration election (VAT Law Art. 22); (v) second-hand margin scheme election for dealers in used excavators and rollers (VAT Law Art. 35); (vi) partial VAT exemption method election (VAT Law Art. 30); (vii) advance tax ruling application to the Tax Department on hybrid mismatch and export substance documentation, governed by ATAD 2 implementing Law 33(I)/2020.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 of taxable turnover in the preceding 12 months (or anticipated within the next 30 days) for established suppliers; EUR 35,000 distance-selling threshold for suppliers established in other EU member states; voluntarily registrable for persons making only zero-rated exports in order to recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% is available under VAT Law Art. 54 where used hydraulic earthmovers and rollers are physically exported from Cyprus to destinations outside the EU, provided the exporter proves removal from the VAT territory within the statutory evidence windows (in practice not exceeding 90 days from the time of supply) and retains the complete export evidence chain. Intra-EU supplies are zero-rated when the customer holds a valid VIES VAT number, the machinery is transported from Cyprus to the other member state, and the supplier files the periodic EC Sales List. For second-hand machinery, the dealer may elect the margin scheme (VAT Law Art. 35), but export zero-rating is generally preferable because it preserves full input VAT recovery on acquisition costs, spare parts, refurbishment, logistics, auction commissions and export-related fees. Offshore services ancillary to machinery export qualify for zero-rating only where the EU place-of-supply and direct-export nexus rules are documentary proven.

  • Export Customs Declaration (SAD/EX1) registered and released in the Cyprus Customs electronic system
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or CMR International Waybill (road) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical departure from Cyprus
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds under SWIFT LC or wire transfer
  • Commercial invoice with serial numbers, engine hours, tare weights and Incoterms
  • Sales contract or purchase order identifying the non-EU or EU acquirer and destination
  • Packing list and container/vehicle loading manifest
  • Pre-export ownership and registry documentation for the used machines (engine/plate photographs, CE or previous registration records)
  • EU acquirer VIES number validation printout for intra-Community zero-rated supplies

No general real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital cryptographic invoice requirement applies to domestic B2B invoices in Cyprus. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing operates through the Cyprus Government e-invoice receiving infrastructure (Peppol BIS 3.0) under EU Directive 2014/55/EU. Periodic fiscal clearance obligations include VIES/EC Sales List filings for intra-EU B2B supplies, Intrastat declarations for dispatches and arrivals, and e-filing of VAT returns and recapitulative statements through the Tax For All portal; machine-to-machine API submission is available for large taxpayers but not mandatory for private-sector issuers.

Cyprus Customs and the Tax Department audit the entire warehouse-to-destination evidence chain for zero-rated exports. Missing, inconsistent or falsified SAD/EX1, B/L, CMR, remittance or VIES data triggers retrospective reversal of the 0% rate, re-assessment at 19% VAT, denial or recapture of input VAT, statutory late-payment interest, and administrative penalties including per-day non-compliance fines. Fraudulent or sham export structures assessed under the ECJ Kittel standard (Case C-439/04) attract criminal prosecution (imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fines), penalty assessments, clawback of refunds within the full limitation period, and director disqualification procedures under Cypriot company law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full euro convertibility applies for both current and capital account operations. Cyprus abolished its exchange-control regime in 1994, joined the EU in 2004 and adopted the euro from 1 January 2008, leaving no residual approval powers for cross-border settlements, foreign-currency account holding or repatriation of profits. The only material remittance hurdle is domestic statutory withholding on shareholder distributions: 17% on dividends remitted abroad, eliminable at 0% for qualifying EU corporate recipients under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) and reducible to treaty rates for third-country investors. Because the entity's trade is wholesale remarketing of used construction excavators and rollers, commercial receipts and payments may be denominated freely in EUR, USD, GBP, JPY or other convertible currencies, and no Central Bank or Ministry of Finance authorisation is required for any payment direction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or forced repatriation deadline exists under Cypriot law for export proceeds. Used-machinery sale receipts from EU, Middle Eastern, CIS and Asian buyers may remain offshore and in foreign currency indefinitely without Central Bank approval. The applicable obligations are exclusively statistical: the bank, and where the exporter exceeds the relevant threshold, the company itself, files balance-of-payments, Intrastat or Extrastat reports with the Central Bank of Cyprus and the Statistical Service of Cyprus. The company must retain sales invoices, CMRs, Bills of Lading and customs-export documentation to evidence each transaction; these records carry no cash-conversion or monetary-policy requirement.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), functioning as the national competent authority within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as an ancillary settlement rail for low-value EUR/USD payments, FX conversion and same-day SEPA transfers; Low as the primary or sole treasury rail for this used-machinery trading business. Legal constraints: an EMI holding a Cyprus authorisation or an EU/EEA PSD2 passport may lawfully open a remote corporate account and execute MT103-equivalent cross-border credit transfers, but it cannot issue, advise or negotiate documentary letters of credit, cannot operate as an UCP 600 bank, and typically declines clients whose counterparties include high-risk non-EU buyers. Under the Central Bank of Cyprus AML directives and FATF Recommendation 16, the corporate must maintain settlement capacity only with a licensed credit institution or authorised e-money institution; UK-only FCA-authorised EMIs have no post-Brexit passporting right into Cyprus and are therefore suboptimal. The EMI should be confined to a pre-funding corridor and FX conversion while all sanctioned trade flows and LC-documentary operations are routed through the tier-1 licensed banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Euro (EUR). Full Cyprus Local File is mandatory where the aggregate value of controlled transactions with related parties in a tax year exceeds either (i) EUR 5,000,000 for transactions in tangible goods (e.g., purchase or resale of used excavators), or (ii) EUR 750,000 for transactions in services, royalties, interest or intangible property. A Cyprus Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Entities below the thresholds must still complete the transfer-pricing summary declaration attached to the annual tax return and apply the arm's length principle.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code. Instead, economic substance is enforced through the management-and-control rule for tax residency, Section 60 of the Income Tax Law (GAAR) and the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD). A used-heavy-machinery trading vehicle must demonstrate genuine commercial substance in Cyprus: physical warehousing or inspection premises, qualified staff executing purchasing/sales/re-marketing functions, actual negotiation and decision-making protocols, corporate minutes, and local bank accounts used for the core cash cycle. An arrangement found to be a 'letterbox company' used to capture the 12.5% rate without real activity will be disregarded, and profits will be reallocated on an arm's length basis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Suppressed Turnover

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the full undeclared tax at 12.5%, plus 5% late-payment surcharge, statutory interest at ECB rate + 3%, and criminal fines up to €6,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years upon conviction for false declarations to the Cyprus Tax Department.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false tax returns are criminally liable jointly with the company; civil courts may order contribution and restitution for the unpaid corporate tax; travel bans and freezing of personal assets are routinely applied in evasion cases involving cross-border wire transactions.
Artificial Arrangement / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner invokes the GAAR (Section 60 Income Tax Law) to disregard the artificial arrangement and recharacterize the profits to the real beneficiaries; a 10% additional administrative tax penalty applies on the reallocated tax liability, with treaty benefits potentially revoked.

Director personal liability: Where the company is a mere façade, courts will pierce the corporate veil; directors may be personally assessed for the unpaid corporate tax and civil damages; disqualification orders under the Companies Law may prohibit the director from serving on any Cyprus board, without prejudice to criminal sanctions.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing of Used Heavy Machinery

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment under Section 33 Income Tax Law: revision of taxable profit to arm's length, imposition of 5% late-payment surcharge, statutory interest, and a 10% GAAR penalty where the mispricing is deliberate; documentation penalties apply for failure to maintain the Local File once thresholds are exceeded.

Director personal liability: Directors approving the mispriced transactions with knowledge are subject to personal surcharge for the additional tax, liability for false declarations, travel bans, and criminal prosecution if the mispricing masks export of capital or money laundering.
AML / Sanctions Evasion in Trade Finance

Statutory sanction: Under Cyprus AML Law (L.188(I)/2007 as amended): fines up to €1,000,000 for legal entities; freezing of the company's bank accounts and assets; separate criminal conviction for money laundering carries imprisonment up to 14 years and fines up to €500,000 (or 10% of annual turnover) for the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors as 'responsible persons' are criminally liable even where the corporate body is separately fined; personal prosecution can result in imprisonment up to 14 years, travel bans, confiscation of personal assets, and a permanent prohibition from serving as a director of any Cyprus-licensed or registered company.

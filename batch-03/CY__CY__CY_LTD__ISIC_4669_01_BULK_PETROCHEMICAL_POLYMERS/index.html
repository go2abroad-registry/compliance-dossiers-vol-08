Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A Cyprus private limited company must have a registered office with a physical street address in Cyprus. A virtual-office address supplied by a licensed corporate services provider can technically satisfy the statutory registered-office requirement. However, for a bulk wholesale distributor of petrochemical polymers categorized as Medium Risk Enhanced, reliance on a virtual office without a local business desk, records, staff, or revenue-generating meetings materially increases the risk of bank account refusal, economic-substance examination by the Cyprus Tax Department, and adverse AML treatment. The effective recommendation is a retained physical serviced office at a minimum.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum issued or paid-up capital applies to a Cyprus private limited company. Shares may be denominated in any currency and issued for cash or non-cash consideration. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας); e-filing portal: https://taxportal.tax.mof.gov.cy

E-government filing portal

For a Cyprus private limited company, the Tax Identification Number (TIN) is issued automatically upon incorporation and registration with the Department of Taxation. Foreign entities without a local establishment must apply for direct tax registration via the TIN application process and are required to obtain VAT registration (Form VAT-1) before undertaking EU-eligible supplies, using a licensed tax representative where customary residence is not established.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus law does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Cyprus has implemented EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) provisions, including hybrid mismatch restrictions. Available elections include group loss relief (Section 11, Income Tax Law), deemed foreign tax credit election, notional interest deduction election, tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping operators, and VAT grouping election (Article 14A of Cyprus VAT Law).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual turnover of taxable supplies for established domestic persons; no threshold for non-established foreign suppliers, registration mandatory upon first taxable supply in Cyprus

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: exportation to a third country (outside EU customs territory) evidenced by customs export documentation and commercial movement of goods; title and risk transferred to the recipient. For offshore services/intangibles: zero-rating applies when services are supplied to a business customer (B2B) located outside the EU, or when services are directly connected to goods dispatched to a destination outside the EU, subject to the qualifying services rules and the effective use and consumption requirement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD – Single Administrative Document, EX form)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence of payment in foreign exchange
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Contract / Purchase Order

Cyprus has not implemented a mandatory real-time e-invoicing continuous transaction control (CTC/RTIR) clearance regime. Taxable persons must file VAT returns electronically through the Tax Department portal, submit EU Sales Lists (VIES) and Intrastat declarations as applicable, and maintain compliant electronic invoice archives. No digital stamping is currently mandated.

The Tax Department and Customs may conduct documentary and administrative checks requiring exporters to substantiate zero-rating claims within prescribed periods. Unfounded zero-rating results in reassessment of VAT at 19%, plus interest from the date the tax would have been due, and civil penalties up to 10% of the tax; fraudulent use of false documentation can trigger enhanced penalties of up to double the tax and criminal liability in accordance with Cyprus criminal law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility of the EUR as legal tender; Cyprus adopted the euro on 1 January 2008. Current-account transactions are unconditionally convertible, and capital-account transactions are fully liberalised for non-resident investors under TFEU Article 63. Dividends, branch profit remittances, trade settlement, principal and interest on shareholder loans, and royalties may be paid freely without prior Central Bank licensing, subject only to Cyprus AML/CFT obligations under Law 188(I)/2007 (as amended), EU sanctions screening, and the Economic Substances and CTF documentation requirements of the tax administration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies. A Cyprus-registered trading company may retain 100% of export proceeds denominated in EUR or any foreign currency in its licensed credit-institution accounts. Receipts must nevertheless be repatriated through formal Cypriot banking channels and reported in the Balance of Payments statistical filing framework administered by the Central Bank of Cyprus; cash imports or exports above €10,000 must be declared under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks; exchange-control competence is governed by the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), Articles 63–65, the Central Bank of Cyprus Laws, and the Eurosystem legal framework. Cyprus participates in the single currency area and is subject to ECB monetary policy and TARGET2-CY payment settlement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-High as an ancillary liquidity distributor only. Electronic Money Institutions licensed in Cyprus under the E-Money Law (Law 84(I)/2012, transposing EU Directive 2009/110/EC) and supervised by CySEC, or EU/EEA EMIs passporting into Cyprus, may lawfully serve open-account supplier payables, e-wallet receipts, and small-value FX conversions. They are not viable as the sole treasury spine: Irrevocable Documentary Letters of Credit (UCP 600) and high-value commodity settlement require a Tier-1 licensed credit institution with SWIFT correspondent relationships. Legal constraints include absence of the Deposit Protection Fund guarantee for EMI balances, counterparty concentration risk, and the need for board-approved treasury concentration caps of no more than 15% of operating liquidity at a single non-bank EMI.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Multi-tier non-resident holding chain (more than two intermediate companies in opaque jurisdictions) obscuring natural-person ownership

Defense evidence: Complete group organogram, certified Articles of Association and Certificates of Incumbency for each intermediate entity, register-of-beneficial-owners extract from the Cyprus or foreign corporate registry, apostilled passports and proof of residential address of all ultimate natural-person owners, and a notarised source-of-wealth statement supported by audited financial statements of the parent.
Petrochemical commodity flows touching counterparties or transport counterparties in high-risk jurisdictions (OFAC/EU/UK sanctioned risk, red-flagged ports, or unknown end-users)

Root cause: Petrochemical commodity flows touching counterparties or transport counterparties in high-risk jurisdictions (OFAC/EU/UK sanctioned risk, red-flagged ports, or unknown end-users)

Defense evidence: Full commercial dossier consisting of signed purchase orders, proforma and final invoices, warehouse receipts, Bills of Lading with the exact vessel name, certificates of origin and quality, INCOterms for each consignment, OFAC/EU/UN/UK sanctions screening reports for all counterparties and vessels, and six months of prime-bank statements evidencing the parent's commercial track record.
Immediate large EUR capital injection at incorporation followed within days by large outbound supplier wires (trade-money-laundering typology)

Root cause: Immediate large EUR capital injection at incorporation followed within days by large outbound supplier wires (trade-money-laundering typology)

Defense evidence: Executed share subscription agreement with payment schedule, board resolutions approving the capital injection and the trade disbursement programme, Central Bank of Cyprus capital-allotment filing (HE77), tax-residency and CRS/GATCA self-certifications, and pre-injection source-of-funds evidence such as the parent's audited balance sheet or a container-trade disposal contract.
Ultimate beneficial owner is a politically exposed person (PEP) or has adverse-media coverage

Root cause: Ultimate beneficial owner is a politically exposed person (PEP) or has adverse-media coverage

Defense evidence: Accredited-provider PEP and adverse-media screening reports (WorldCheck/Refinitiv), a reasoned explanation of the PEP's legitimate commercial role, employment and business-ownership history, proof that the PEP receives no public-office influence advantage, and a commitment to a clearly defined enhanced-monitoring account matrix limiting counterparties and geographies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

SWIFT MT103 from the parent company's prime-bank account with a structured payment narrative stating 'CY_LTD HE77 capital subscription — [shareholder name]' or 'shareholder loan disbursement — loan agreement dated [date]'. Cyprus does not maintain a mandatory national purpose-code classification, but the narrative must reconcile to (i) the board resolution, (ii) the share-allotment notice filed with the Registrar of Companies, and (iii) the Central Bank of Cyprus balance-of-payments direct-investment statistical category 'A.5 — equity capital'. The receiving Cypriot bank will independently verify the source of funds, and the amount must clear within a single MT103 message rather than split wires to avoid structuring red-flags.Primary clearing rails are (1) SWIFT MT103 for open-account T/T settlements (30–90 days) of low-value bulk polymer consignments executed through TARGET2-CY and the correspondent network of Bank of Cyprus/Hellenic Bank; (2) Irrevocable Documentary Letters of Credit (UCP 600) issued via MT700 for first-time or high-value counterparties, with reimbursement effected through MT202/MT202COV interbank cover payments; and (3) stand-by LC or bank-guarantee arrangements for long-term master supply agreements.

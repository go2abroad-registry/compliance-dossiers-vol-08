Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio of the corporate domicile (e.g., Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá) through the Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES), jointly with DIAN for tax pre-registration and the Superintendencia de Sociedades for beneficial-ownership reporting.

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008, Articles 1-5, 17, 22-27 and 40-49 (S.A.S. constitution, capital, governance and dissolution); Código de Comercio, Decreto-Ley 410 de 1971, Articles 110-115 and 156-161 (merchant registration and corporate records); Decreto 1074 de 2015, Titles 2.2.1 and 2.2.2 (Chamber registration rules and RUES); Resolución 100-000005 de 2023 (beneficial-owner filings); Ley 222 de 1995 (audit and minority rights).

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (private incorporation instrument) authenticated before a notary or Chamber official, registered in the Registro Mercantil and evidenced by the Certificado de Existencia y Representación Legal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 21 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up or subscribed capital applicable: an S.A.S. may be incorporated with zero or nominal capital. Subscribed capital may be paid in up to two (2) years from incorporation per Article 17 of Law 1258 of 2008; in-kind contributions must be described in the incorporation instrument, and a public deed is mandatory when real property is contributed.

Funding & unlock path: Certificado de Bomberos (fire safety inspection of exits, extinguishers and alarms); Concepto de Uso de Suelo (land-use compatibility certificate from the municipal planning authority); SG-SST implementation evidence under Resolución 0312 de 2019; binding ARL affiliation and Reglamento de Higiene y Seguridad Industrial; plus municipal acoustic-impact clearance for the recording/dubbing suite if located in mixed-use zones of Bogotá, Cali or Medellín.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up or subscribed capital applicable: an S.A.S. may be incorporated with zero or nominal capital. Subscribed capital may be paid in up to two (2) years from incorporation per Article 17 of Law 1258 of 2008; in-kind contributions must be described in the incorporation instrument, and a public deed is mandatory when real property is contributed.

Category: Not applicable. The activity is exercised through the Matrícula Mercantil and RUT with CIIU code 7490 (translation and interpretation); audiovisual dubbing for cinema exhibition optionally requires registration with the Dirección de Cinematografía under Ley 814 de 2003, but localization of non-cinema media content (e-learning, corporate video, software) requires no prior authorization.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - National Tax and Customs Directorate; official portal: www.dian.gov.co; transactional e-services platform (MUISCA): muisca.dian.gov.co; electronic invoicing clearance: fe.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

A Colombian SAS obtains its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through the RUT (Registro Único Tributario). Foreign shareholders must appoint a Colombian legal representative, formalize incorporation before the Cámara de Comercio, then apply online via MUISCA or a certified OFE (authorized e-invoicing provider). DIAN validates existence, address and economic activity codes (ISIC 7490 assigned to the RUT), issues the definitive NIT with verification digit (DV), and grants the electronic-invoicing authorization (Habilitación de Facturación Electrónica) plus the digital certificate required for monthly VAT and annual income tax filings. Non-resident entities without local activity may obtain a simplified NIT through a fiscal proxy for withholding-tax compliance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope - a resident SAS is taxed at the corporate income rate (35% for FY 2024-2025) on worldwide income, with statutory exclusions for qualifying exported services; the SAS is always a VAT responsible entity; no pass-through treatment is available. — A Colombian SAS obtains its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through the RUT (Registro Único Tributario). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope - a resident SAS is taxed at the corporate income rate (35% for FY 2024-2025) on worldwide income, with statutory exclusions for qualifying exported services; the SAS is always a VAT responsible entity; no pass-through treatment is available.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists for the SAS; entity-level taxation is mandatory. Available elective regimes: (1) Régimen Simple de Tributación (RST) - consolidating income tax, ICA and parafiscal levies at progressive rates between 2.2% and 14.5% for taxpayers with gross income below 100,000 UVT; (2) Permanent Special Free Zone (Zona Franca Permanente Especial) election - reduced 20% corporate rate for export-oriented service companies meeting DIAN investment and employment criteria; (3) Economía Naranja income-tax exemption under Art. 235-2 ET (up to 7 years) for qualifying creative and localization activities, subject to minimum job creation, local investment and export-intensity conditions as tightened by Ley 2277/2022; (4) hybrid-mismatch alignment and transfer-pricing documentation elections under Decrees 2120/2017 and 1625/2016.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary registration threshold for corporate entities: a SAS must register as an IVA responsible person from its first taxable act in Colombia. Natural persons may remain non-responsible when gross income does not exceed 3,500 UVT (COP 174,296,500 for UVT 2025 = COP 49,799) and they do not engage in import operations (Art. 437 ET). Non-resident digital service providers must register through a local agent when supplying services to Colombian users.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export services qualify for the 0% IVA rate under Art. 481 numeral 3 ET when: (a) the counterparty is a non-domiciled foreign entity; (b) the service (multilingual translation, subtitling, localization QA, media dubbing) is economically consumed outside Colombia; (c) the price is paid from abroad through foreign-currency remittance; and (d) the exporter holds an electronic export invoice and a service contract evidencing foreign use. Back-office services supporting Colombian-source production do not qualify. The resulting VAT credit balance may be refunded monthly or bimonthly through DIAN's export refund procedure.

  • Electronic export invoice (Factura Electrónica de Exportación) carrying CUFE/CUDS stamp and QR code
  • Signed localization service agreement with the non-resident client (bilingual version recommended)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas) issued by the Colombian FX intermediary, plus Wise B2B or Payoneer settlement statements when payments are received through those rails
  • Tax-residence certificate (Certificado de Residencia Fiscal) of the foreign beneficiary
  • Annual Exógena declaration (Form 2276) reporting export-service income
  • When physical master files or dubbed media are shipped: Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación/DEX), Bill of Lading or Airway Bill, and packing list

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica de Venta - FE) per Decreto 2242/2015 and Resolución DIAN 000165/2023. Each invoice is generated as XML, transmitted to DIAN's MUISCA webservice for cryptographic validation, and cleared with a unique CUFE/CUDS code and QR stamp before the transaction becomes legally effective; buyers must accept or reject the invoice event chain within 48 hours. Exporters must file the monthly VAT return (Form 300), report export-service income through the Exógena information return (Form 2276), and may claim accumulated export VAT credits through the devolución del IVA procedure (Art. 855 ET).

DIAN cross-matches e-invoices, banking/FX reports and customs data; a missing inward remittance within the 180-day term recharacterizes the zero-rated sale as taxable at 19%. Penalties: monthly moratory interest on the unremitted tax; accuracy penalty of up to 160% of the understated IVA (Art. 648 ET); 20% surcharge for improper refunds (Art. 670 ET); exclusion from the exporter refund regime; and criminal liability for fraudulent zero-rating claims under Art. 436-B of the Código Penal (imprisonment of up to 9 years) plus director disqualification.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia has a regulated convertible currency regime. Current-account transactions are generally freely convertible but must be channeled through authorized Foreign Exchange Market Intermediaries (IMCs) and reported via the exchange declaration (Declaración de Cambio). Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment, require registration with Banco de la República. Profit remittances and dividend transfers are permissible after the related foreign investment has been registered, local taxes have been paid, and the corresponding exchange declaration has been filed. There is no full on-shore capital-account liberalization; non-registered capital inflows and outflows may be blocked, penalized, or treated as illegal exchange operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For service exports, including professional multilingual translation, media dubbing, and localization services, the gross proceeds must be settled and channeled into the Colombian foreign exchange market within four (4) months of the service accrual date. In practical terms, 100% of export proceeds must be routed through an authorized IMC in COP or a foreign-currency account legally pre-registered with Banco de la República. No fixed percentage of proceeds needs to be mandatorily converted to Colombian pesos, but the full amount must either enter the local market or remain in a registered foreign-currency account; any retention outside regulated channels constitutes a violation of the exchange regime.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), in coordination with the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit and the National Directorate of Taxes and Customs (DIAN) for trade and service export documentation enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility as a primary treasury rail. Wise B2B and Payoneer are not authorized Colombian foreign exchange intermediaries and cannot issue exchange declarations, register foreign investment, or provide supporting documentation for capital repatriation. They may be used only as auxiliary collection/payment channels for minor operational amounts, provided all offshore receipts are ultimately routed to a Colombian regulated bank for proper FX settlement. Using a non-regulated EMI for significant cross-border liquidity would create unregistered export proceeds and expose the entity to exchange control penalties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign shareholders must transfer funds via SWIFT to the S.A.S.'s COP account held at an authorized Colombian bank. The receiving bank acts as the IMC and files the exchange declaration under the foreign direct investment purpose code for 'Inversión directa en el país' with Banco de la República. The entity must supply the foreign investor's identification, the local NIT (tax ID), proof of the underlying transaction, and the instruction letter stating the investment purpose. Capital must be registered with Banco de la República within the statutory period (typically within two years) to become eligible for future profits and capital repatriation. Funds received from unrelated third parties without a registered investment purpose are treated as ordinary trade financing or loans and are subject to different reporting rules.Trade settlements for service imports and operational outflows are executed via SWIFT MT103 through the Colombian IMC. Commercial letters of credit are permissible for purchasing goods and media equipment, but are not common for localization and dubbing services. For services, typical clearing rails include direct SWIFT transfers for foreign contractors, ACH and Redeban for domestic payments to Colombian vendors, and bank-draft collections for one-off royalty or license payments.

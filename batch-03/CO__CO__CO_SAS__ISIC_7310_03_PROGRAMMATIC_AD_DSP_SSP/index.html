Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá — Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES), Ventanilla Única Empresarial (VUE); oversight by Superintendencia de Sociedades, Bogotá D.C.

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 (S.A.S. law), Articles 1-5, 17, 27-29; Código de Comercio, Decreto 410 de 1971; Decreto 1074 de 2015 (RUES registration); Ley 1429 de 2010 (business formalization); Superintendencia de Sociedades regulations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (private incorporation deed) filed electronically through the VUE/RUES portal; a notarial escritura pública is mandatory only where the S.A.S. contributes real property rights at incorporation (Article 5(3), Ley 1258 de 2008).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is required under Ley 1258 de 2008; capital is freely fixed in the bylaws and may be paid in whole or in part within two (2) years of subscription. No escrow or blocked account is mandated; however, founders and the legal representative remain jointly and severally liable for unpaid subscribed capital until fully paid.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certificate from the Cuerpo de Bomberos for the registered office; land-use and structural load clearance issued by the Curaduría Urbana; occupational health and safety system (SG-SST) per Decreto 1072 of 2015 and Resolución 0312 of 2019 for workplaces with ten or more employees; computer-workstation ergonomic and medical clearance for software personnel.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; electronic services and registration through MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) at https://muisca.dian.gov.co; integrated foreign trade and customs platform: SISCOSEX.

E-government filing portal

An S.A.S. must complete, before any taxable activity: (1) execution of the constitutive private document recorded with the Cámara de Comercio of the registered seat (no minimum capital; 100% foreign ownership is permitted); (2) RUT registration (Registro Único Tributario) before DIAN through MUISCA, performed by the legal representative under digital certificate or via a notarized power of attorney for non-resident incorporators; (3) automatic issuance of the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) embedded in the RUT; (4) selection of tax attributes: IVA responsible person, exporter of services status, e-invoicing profile, and SIMPLE regime election if applicable; (5) activation of electronic invoicing software with an authorized technology operator. Standard processing takes 3 to 10 business days; the legal representative must be a natural person, though not necessarily a Colombian national, and non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the equity with no local residency or minimum capital requirement.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the S.A.S. is a Colombian tax resident (place of effective management criterion) taxable on worldwide income (renta de fuente nacional y extranjera) at the general CIT rate of 35% for tax years 2023-2025 and 30% from tax year 2026 (Law 2277 of 2022). No pass-through or partnership classification is available; dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to 10% dividend withholding tax. Optional Tax_Exempt_Zone status (single-user Zona Franca) may be authorized for qualifying software/digital service exporters, applying a 20% CIT rate and 0% VAT on exports. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the S.A.S. is a Colombian tax resident (place of effective management criterion) taxable on worldwide income (renta de fuente nacional y extranjera) at the general CIT rate of 35% for tax years 2023-2025 and 30% from tax year 2026 (Law 2277 of 2022). No pass-through or partnership classification is available; dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to 10% dividend withholding tax. Optional Tax_Exempt_Zone status (single-user Zona Franca) may be authorized for qualifying software/digital service exporters, applying a 20% CIT rate and 0% VAT on exports. Territorial_Scope rules apply only to non-resident digital service providers invoicing Colombian clients, not to the resident exporter.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists. Available mechanisms: (a) Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) - optional consolidated regime for entities with prior-year gross income below 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 164.7 million for 2024) satisfying asset and headcount tests, replacing CIT, VAT (in a simplified form), and ICA; technology/ad services are assigned specific aggregate rate bands (approximately 3%-7%); (b) Zona Franca Permanente Especial (single-user zone) - optional 20% CIT and 0% VAT for exported services under government authorization; (c) Export-services zero-rating under Art. 481 ET must be affirmatively flagged in the periodic Form 300; (d) Hybrid mismatch rules under Decree 1625 of 2016 and Law 2277 of 2022 restrict deductions on payments to related non-residents producing mismatch outcomes; no elective increases in hybrid deductions are permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary nexus threshold for legal entities: any S.A.S. selling taxable services or goods is a mandatory IVA responsible taxpayer irrespective of turnover. The de minimis exception (No Responsable) is reserved to natural persons with gross income below 3,500 UVT (COP 164,727,500 for 2024; 3,500 UVT = COP 174,296,500 for 2025) and is not available to corporations. SIMPLE regime access likewise requires prior-year gross income below 3,500 UVT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Art. 481 ET and Decree 1625/2016, a service qualifies for the 0% export VAT rate if: (i) the recipient is a non-resident person or entity without tax domicile in Colombia; (ii) the service is used or exploited exclusively outside Colombia, with no economic link to Colombian-source activities; (iii) the consideration is paid in freely convertible foreign currency, or the contract documents foreign-source payment; and (iv) the exporter maintains contemporaneous documentary evidence. DIAN doctrine (e.g., Concept 1089 of 2019) recognizes programmatic advertising intermediation, SSP platform access, and ad-auction/logistics services as qualifying export services when the advertising inventory, end-users, and final beneficiaries are located abroad. Input VAT on costs attributable to zero-rated exports (cloud infrastructure, data feeds, hosting) is creditable and periodically refundable through Form 300; services that partially benefit Colombian advertisers or local traffic must be apportioned, with the domestic portion taxed at 19%.

  • Export Declaration of Services (Declaracion de Exportacion de Servicios) filed through SISCOSEX and endorsed by DIAN (or the standard DEX customs export declaration if tangible media/equipment is included)
  • Electronic export invoice with DIAN-validated CUFE and export modality denomination
  • Signed commercial agreement with the non-resident counterparty specifying jurisdiction, foreign use, and delivery scope of the programmatic ad-exchange/SSP services
  • Inward foreign-exchange settlement certificate (Declaracion de Cambio) issued by an authorized Colombian intermediary bank (IME) evidencing receipt and sale of the foreign currency proceeds
  • Technical delivery evidence: programmatic auction logs, bid requests/responses, SSP transaction reports, IP-geolocation and traffic-origin reports demonstrating consumption of the services exclusively abroad

Mandatory electronic invoicing under DIAN Resolution 165 of 2023: all sales, including export-service sales, must be issued through an authorized technology operator (Proveedor Tecnológico) using web-service APIs (XML/JSON) and transmitted to DIAN for real-time validation (validacion previa) before issuance to the customer. Each invoice must contain the unique electronic billing code (CUFE) and a scannable 2D/QR code with the fiscal hash; export invoices use the export modality. Ancillary mandated documents: electronic payroll notes (nomina electronica), document soporte for acquisitions from non-invoicing suppliers, and SISCOSEX filings for foreign-trade transactions. Failure to obtain DIAN validation invalidates the invoice, denies input VAT credits for the recipient, and triggers account suspension and penalties.

DIAN cross-validates zero-rated export claims against SISCOSEX filings, bank FOREX records, contract terms, and platform usage logs. If exclusive foreign use is not proven, the full invoice value is reassessed at 19% VAT, plus moratory interest at the statutory monthly rate (current reference banking interest rate increased by 50%, certified monthly) and the inaccuracy penalty equal to 100% of the tax difference under Art. 648 ET, escalated to 200% where simulated transactions, non-existent export documentation, or fictitious invoicing are found. Fictitious export arrangements expose the entity and its legal representatives to criminal tax evasion prosecution under Law 1819 of 2016 (imprisonment up to 9 years and substantial fines), cancellation of exporter status, revocation of VAT refund certificates, and clawback of all refunds received within the general 5-year limitation period, extendable to 15 years where returns were not filed or fraud is proven.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with full convertibility on the current account and a liberalized but controlled capital account. Non-residents may freely invest in Colombian entities, including SAS corporations, subject to registration with the Central Bank's foreign exchange office (Banco de la República) via the Declaración de Cambio (foreign exchange declaration). Profit remittances (dividends) to non-resident shareholders require prior registration of the foreign investment with Banco de la República; once registered, remittances are permitted without prior approval, but all transactions must be channeled through authorized intermediaries (intermediarios del mercado cambiario). Capital account transactions, including inbound capital contributions exceeding USD 10,000 equivalent, must be reported within statutory deadlines; outbound capital repatriation of registered investments is free, but unregistered investments face restrictions and penalties. The Colombian peso (COP) is not an internationally traded hard currency; practical convertibility depends on the commercial banking system's access to offshore correspondent banking and the availability of US dollars in the local interbank market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia applies mandatory surrender requirements for export proceeds deriving from sales of goods and certain services, including software development and digital advertising services, to the extent classified as exports under Article 89 of Decree 2245 of 2017 and Resolution 1 of 2018. Under the current regime (Regimen Cambiario, Decreto 1068 de 2015, Libro 12), export proceeds must be surrendered to the domestic foreign exchange market within the term established by the Central Bank: for services, the term is generally six months from the date of issuance of the export declaration or invoice, but extendable to 12 months for software and technology-related services if properly justified and reported. The mandatory conversion ratio is 100% of the FOB value of the export, to be negotiated through an authorized foreign exchange intermediary (bank or financial corporation) to Colombian pesos, unless the exporter obtains prior authorization from the Central Bank to maintain foreign currency in accounts abroad or in local foreign currency accounts (cuentas de compensación) under Article 413 of Decree 1068. Non-surrender triggers exchange penalty fines under Articles 24 and 25 of Law 9 of 1991, as amended, up to 200% of the unreported amount. In practice, the Medellín and Bogotá chambers of commerce and DIAN accept programmatic advertising services as exporting services if the contract, invoice, and SWIFT payment trail evidence the provision from Colombia.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) and the Comisión de Cambio (Foreign Exchange Commission), administered operationally by the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) for exchange control registration and verification.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore EMIs as primary operating accounts. Colombian exchange control law requires all foreign currency transactions related to exports and capital investments to be channeled through authorized local intermediaries (bancos comerciales). Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) cannot act as the mandatory intermediary for surrender of export proceeds nor provide a local USD/COP settlement account that satisfies DIAN and Banco de la República reporting. They may be used only as auxiliary collection tools for inbound receipts from foreign ad counterparties, provided funds are promptly repatriated to a local authorized bank account and declared in the Declaración de Cambio. Legal constraint: Circular Reglamentaria Externa DCIN-83 of Banco de la República requires all exchange transactions to be reported by the authorized intermediary; an offshore EMI is not a permitted intermediary. Defense: use local licensed fintechs with foreign exchange authorization, such as Nequi (with Bancolombia) or Movii, strictly for domestic disbursements, never for cross-border settlement.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident directors/shareholders with no Colombian tax ID (NIT) or no local economic nexus

Root cause: Non-resident directors/shareholders with no Colombian tax ID (NIT) or no local economic nexus

Defense evidence: Apostilled certificates of incorporation from the foreign parent, passports and visa documentation for directors, audited financial statements of the foreign parent, proof of ownership chain, and a board resolution authorizing the local SAS to open the account and specifying the beneficial ownership structure.
High cross-border transaction volume with counterparties in jurisdictions perceived as low-corruption-risk but lacking local exchange regulations

Root cause: High cross-border transaction volume with counterparties in jurisdictions perceived as low-corruption-risk but lacking local exchange regulations

Defense evidence: Fully executed advertising service agreements (SSP/ad exchange contracts), commercial invoices with ISIC 7310 description, and a statement of beneficial ownership for each counterparty, plus a business rationale report confirming that all services are rendered from Colombia and that funds are for genuine commercial activities.
Rapid in-and-out flows (capital injection followed immediately by disbursements to foreign ad inventory providers)

Root cause: Rapid in-and-out flows (capital injection followed immediately by disbursements to foreign ad inventory providers)

Defense evidence: Treasury policy document setting out sequential fund usage rules, cash flow projections for the first 12 months, and a commitment letter to maintain trailing 12-month statements for spot audit. Provide proof of the underlying contracts that require prepayments to media suppliers.
Mixed USD-COP account operations with unregistered capital increases

Root cause: Mixed USD-COP account operations with unregistered capital increases

Defense evidence: Foreign investment registration certificate (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales) issued by Banco de la República, and the Formulario No. 11 (Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales) evidencing the inbound funds.
Use of shared offices or virtual workspace as the registered address

Root cause: Use of shared offices or virtual workspace as the registered address

Defense evidence: Lease agreement for a physical office space in Colombia, utility bills, photographs of the premises with signage, and an operational substance description detailing the development team's daily presence and equipment.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the CO_SAS entity must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to a local account held with an authorized Colombian bank (e.g., Bancolombia). The remitting bank should use the appropriate purpose code as per Banco de la República's exchange classification: for foreign direct investment in a SAS, the declaration code is 'STR-US' or 'US-DOC' for capital registerable as a financial loan; for equity, use the code '1401' (private non-financial sector foreign direct investment) or the generic 'Inversión directa' under Formulario 11. The physical wire must include the company's NIT number, the unique exchange registration number (código del intermediario), and the clause 'Para trámite de Declaración de Cambio No. 11'. Pre-registration with the local bank's foreign exchange desk is mandatory for amounts over USD 10,000; the bank will issue a draft foreign exchange declaration that must be validated and presented to Banco de la República within the same business week. For capital contributions in excess of USD 500,000, a preliminary letter of intent must be filed with the Central Bank's statistics department to obtain a provisional investment registration ID.Outbound settlements for advertising inventory purchases, media buys, and third-party technology costs (DSP-to-SSP payments, bidder fees, server rentals) must be executed via SWIFT MT103 (or local equivalent using CUD - Código Único de Desembolso) from the company's USD-denominated operational account. For payments to domestic Colombian suppliers, use the ACH/COP clearing system (CENIT operated by ACH Colombia). For cross-border commercial transactions exceeding USD 50,000 per transaction, the Colombian exporter/importer must use the 'Declaración de Cambio' through the intermediary bank, attaching the commercial invoice, proof of service origin, and indication of the purpose code. For letter of credit settlements, the Colombian bank will issue a Commercial LC (documentary credit) subject to UCP 600; however, for low-risk software services, a standard SWIFT wire or irrevocable LC at sight is preferred. All outbound foreign payments are subject to the 0.4% (GMF - Gravamen al Movimiento Financiero) financial transactions tax unless waived under certain technology export incentive programs (Law 1819 of 2016), and must be reported individually to DIAN under taxpayer reporting requirements.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/COP transacted with local authorized banks (e.g., Bancolombia, BBVA Colombia) only for hedging real commercial exposure under a current account or capital account obligation; no speculative positions.Forward contracts (Forwards de Tasa de Cambio) with physical settlement in COP/USD, requiring an underlying export contract or import obligation and registration with the Central Bank's foreign exchange desk.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) between COP and USD for balance-sheet hedging of the foreign investment liability or long-term receivables, subject to Banco de la República's prior notification and authorization for tenors above one year.FX options (European-style) on USD/COP, permitted for hedging the conversion of export proceeds receivables, but must be executed solely through the authorized local market.Commodity or crypto-linked derivatives are strictly prohibited for hedging purposes; only plain-vanilla instruments referencing the official TRM (Tasa Representativa del Mercado) are allowed.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility for offshore EMIs as primary operating accounts. Colombian exchange control law requires all foreign currency transactions related to exports and capital investments to be channeled through authorized local intermediaries (bancos comerciales). Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) cannot act as the mandatory intermediary for surrender of export proceeds nor provide a local USD/COP settlement account that satisfies DIAN and Banco de la República reporting. They may be used only as auxiliary collection tools for inbound receipts from foreign ad counterparties, provided funds are promptly repatriated to a local authorized bank account and declared in the Declaración de Cambio. Legal constraint: Circular Reglamentaria Externa DCIN-83 of Banco de la República requires all exchange transactions to be reported by the authorized intermediary; an offshore EMI is not a permitted intermediary. Defense: use local licensed fintechs with foreign exchange authorization, such as Nequi (with Bancolombia) or Movii, strictly for domestic disbursements, never for cross-border settlement.

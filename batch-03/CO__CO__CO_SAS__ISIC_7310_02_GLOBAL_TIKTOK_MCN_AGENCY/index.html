Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – the unified national tax and customs authority. Official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; transactional platform: MUISCA (https://muisca.dian.gov.co).

E-government filing portal

Step 2: The legal representative obtains a DIAN-recognized digital certificate (firma electrónica) from an authorized certification authority. Step 3: Through MUISCA, complete the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) pre-registration, selecting 'Responsable de IVA' and 'Factura Electrónica de Venta' obligations, and obtain the definitive NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Colombian S.A.S. is a capital-based company taxed at the entity level under the Estatuto Tributario with no pass-through transparency. Resident S.A.S. entities are taxed on worldwide income; foreign-source passive income may be exempt or taxable depending on applicable rules. The entity is a VAT responsible person for all taxable supplies and qualifies as an exporter for zero-rating purposes.

Available election forms

Colombia has no US-style check-the-box election; classification follows the Commercial Code and Estatuto Tributario. Available elections: (i) optional 'Régimen Simple de Tributación' (SIMPLES) substitute gross-receipts tax for entities with ordinary income ≤ 3,500 UVT (≈ COP 174.3 million in 2025); (ii) VAT export election to charge 0% IVA and claim monthly refund of input VAT on export-service activities; (iii) parent-subsidiary group consolidation regime (Articles 19 to 19-3 E.T.) subject to DIAN authorization; (iv) no hybrid-mismatch opt-in – OECD BEPS Action 2 rules per Law 2010 of 2019 are mandatory and disallow deductions on certain hybrid instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary de-minimis threshold for legal entities: an S.A.S. performing any taxable supply of goods or services must register as a VAT responsible person (Responsable de IVA) from the commencement of activities, irrespective of turnover. The simplified VAT regime is reserved for individuals and is not available to companies. Optional SIMPLES small-taxpayer regime applies only up to 3,500 UVT of gross ordinary income (approximately COP 174,296,500 for tax year 2025; UVT 2025 = COP 49,799).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Articles 479 and 481 E.T. and Decree 1625 of 2016 (Articles 1.3.1.6.3–1.3.1.6.5). The MCN agency's creator-management, advertising placement, and talent-representation services qualify as exported services when: (i) the beneficiary and effective recipient is not domiciled in Colombia (e.g., a non-resident TikTok/Bytedance affiliate or foreign advertiser); (ii) the beneficial use, exploitation, or result of the service occurs exclusively abroad; (iii) the service is not intermediated to or enjoyed by a Colombian taxable person; (iv) the S.A.S. issues the corresponding electronic export invoice charging 0% IVA and presents the monthly VAT return (Form 330) reflecting the zero-rated operation; and (v) input VAT (19%) paid on local acquisitions of software, cloud services, equipment, and subcontractors is creditable and refundable through the 'solicitud de devolución' process in MUISCA. The exporter must maintain the DEX (for goods), the export service contract, and proof of foreign currency collection.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX / Form 264) stamped by DIAN customs for tangible goods; for MCN services, the export service contract and the electronic export invoice replace the customs declaration
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical export of goods; for services, a dated service agreement, scope of work, and delivery reports demonstrating foreign-locale benefit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Canalización de Divisas) issued by the Colombian foreign-exchange intermediary bank, evidencing timely receipt and channelization of export proceeds
  • Electronic export invoice (Factura Electrónica de Venta Exportación) with CUFE and QR code accepted by DIAN
  • Certificate of tax residence / fiscal domicile of the foreign client (TikTok affiliate, brand advertiser, or offshore agency)
  • Substantiating documents for input VAT refund: supplier e-invoices, customs import declarations for inputs, payment evidence, and VAT ledgers

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta – FEV) with real-time XML transmission to DIAN. Each invoice is transmitted through DIAN's REST/SOAP web-service APIs or a certified 'Proveedor Tecnológico', validated within seconds, and receives a CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) plus a QR code for invoices above 3 UVT. Since 2022 all VAT responsible taxpayers operate under the e-invoicing v2 scheme; DIAN rejection of non-compliant XML invalidates the invoice for the buyer's input VAT credit. For exports, the electronic export invoice must be issued before goods leave customs or before the service-rendering invoice is recorded; the FEV must be transmitted at transaction time or within the maximum 24-hour tolerance; local point-of-sale integration is mandatory for physical retail.

DIAN conducts risk-based audits of export VAT refunds, targeting repetitive refunds, related-party offshore transactions, mismatches between exchange declarations and e-invoice values, and untimely FX channelization. If zero-rating is rejected: (i) VAT is reassessed at 19% with monthly default interest (Banco de la República benchmark rate plus 50%); (ii) 'sanción por inexactitud' of up to 200% of the additional tax (Article 647 E.T.) applies; (iii) improperly refunded amounts are clawed back with a 'sanción de devolución improcedente' of up to 100% plus interest (Article 670 E.T.); (iv) criminal liability for 'obtención fraudulenta de devoluciones' under Law 1762 of 2015 (Código Penal Articles 402–406) carries 48–108 months imprisonment and fines up to 50% of the fraudulent amount; (v) false export declarations trigger customs smuggling penalties (Articles 463–466 C.P.). The DIAN's assessment period extends to six (6) years in cases of inexactitude (Article 706 E.T.), and the refund statute of limitations is generally five (5) years. The S.A.S. may also be flagged as a high-risk taxpayer, suspended from the automated refund channel, and subjected to an administrative freeze of refunds pending full verification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) operates under a free-market, floating exchange-rate regime based on the Representative Market Rate (TRM). Current-account operations are convertible without prior authorization but are subject to the mandatory channeling of qualifying export proceeds through an IMC. Capital-account operations, including inbound foreign direct investment (FDI), require no prior license but must be declared via the relevant Declaración de Cambio and registered with Banco de la República; timely FDI registration (within 12 months) is a substantive precondition for the lawful remittance of profits and repatriation of capital. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders of an S.A.S. is permitted through an IMC subject to: (i) proof of prior FDI registration, (ii) statutory/audited financial statements evidencing distributable earnings, and (iii) withholding tax on dividends at 5% under Article 246 of the Colombian Tax Statute (subject to treaty relief). Practical hurdles include strict reconciliation of channeled funds against underlying service invoices, the 180-day channeling deadline for service export proceeds, and prudent segregation of FDI capital from operational flows to avoid misclassification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All service-export proceeds of the Colombian S.A.S. (including TikTok creator-commission income) must be channeled through an IMC within 180 days (six months) from receipt of the foreign currency; for goods exports the term runs from the date of the export declaration (DEX). No mandatory conversion ratio to COP is imposed: the exporter may either sell the foreign currency to the IMC at market rate or deposit it into an individually registered foreign-currency compensation account (cuenta de compensación). Failure to channel within the statutory period constitutes an exchange-control infraction subject to fines by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia under the Régimen Cambiario.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Comisión de Cambios (Foreign Exchange Commission) and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia, which supervises the authorized Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario (IMCs); the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) enforces supporting foreign-trade and tax declarations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a substitute for the regulated channeling route; MEDIUM as a supplemental low-value payout and collection rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise B2B, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not authorized Colombian IMCs and cannot legally discharge the foreign-exchange channeling obligation. They may be used for convenience (e.g., TikTok creator micro-payouts and aggregating receivables) provided that (i) foreign-currency balances are periodically repatriated or regularized through an IMC using Formulario 2 or Formulario 5, (ii) the Colombian S.A.S. retains full accounting and DIAN reporting traceability, and (iii) no export proceeds remain offshore beyond the 180-day channeling window unless held in a registered compensation account. Using such EMIs as the primary FX intermediary would trigger exchange-control sanctions and AML compliance deficiencies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the foreign parent wires USD via SWIFT MT103 to the Colombian S.A.S.'s COP treasury account or compensation account held with an IMC; the remittance field states 'Aporte de capital social – [Shareholder Name] – [NIT]' and must not be mixed with trade payments. The receiving IMC files the Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional (Formulario 4) using the exchange concept code for direct investment in the country; registration of the FDI with Banco de la República must be completed within 12 months of the contribution. Verification steps: (i) reconcile the credited amount against the capital contribution minutes and share subscription schedule; (ii) update the corporate capital record with the Cámara de Comercio where applicable; (iii) retain the IMC's exchange-rate (TRM) confirmation and the Formulario 4 receipt as the audit trail. A statutory capital escrow is not required in Colombia; any escrow arrangement is purely contractual.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 (Single Customer Credit Transfer) executed through the IMC for semi-annual, quarterly, or monthly settlements with foreign creators; for high-volume repetitive micro-payouts the S.A.S. may aggregate instructions via the bank's batch/payroll rail or use a fintech intermediary (e.g., Wise B2B) as an auxiliary channel. Each outbound creator payment is classifiable as an import of services and must be supported by the underlying contract/invoice and the Declaración de Cambio (Formulario 2 – importación de servicios) filed by the IMC at execution; batch schedules are acceptable for recurring payments. Payment memos should include invoice references and the SWIFT 'SI' (services) purpose-code taxonomy to prevent reconciliation flags. Commercial letters of credit are not standard for digital service/creator payouts; where documentary assurance is requested, a clean or standby L/C issued by an IMC may be used, but MT103 remains the cost-efficient operational rail.COP/USD deliverable and non-deliverable forwards (Forwards TRM) executed with authorized IMCs under master ISDA/ICOM documentation with evidenced underlying trade exposure.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps / CCS) with authorized IMCs to hedge mismatches between USD-denominated TikTok inflows and COP expenditure.Over-the-counter FX options with authorized IMCs for performance-based hedging of conversion-rate volatility.Offshore NDFs, admissible only to the extent the Colombian S.A.S. demonstrates underlying economic exposure and the domestic banking line is unavailable or insufficient.Natural hedging strategy: retaining USD receivables in a registered compensation account (cuenta de compensación) to offset USD-denominated creator payout obligations and reduce forced conversion frequency.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW as a substitute for the regulated channeling route; MEDIUM as a supplemental low-value payout and collection rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise B2B, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not authorized Colombian IMCs and cannot legally discharge the foreign-exchange channeling obligation. They may be used for convenience (e.g., TikTok creator micro-payouts and aggregating receivables) provided that (i) foreign-currency balances are periodically repatriated or regularized through an IMC using Formulario 2 or Formulario 5, (ii) the Colombian S.A.S. retains full accounting and DIAN reporting traceability, and (iii) no export proceeds remain offshore beyond the 180-day channeling window unless held in a registered compensation account. Using such EMIs as the primary FX intermediary would trigger exchange-control sanctions and AML compliance deficiencies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taxpayers with gross income exceeding 135,000 UVT (approx. COP 6,300 million for 2024, equivalently US$1.5M–1.6M depending on exchange rate) or net assets exceeding 135,000 UVT, or whose transactions with related parties exceed 45,000 UVT (approx. COP 2,100 million), are required to prepare and file the Informe de Operaciones con Vinculados (Local File generally integrated as Annex 6 / 'Anexo de TP') with the annual income tax return. A Master File (Declaración Informativa, Reporte País por País) is required for groups with consolidated revenue above 81,000,000 UVT (approx. COP 3.78 trillion) under OECD/BEPS Action 13. For a global TikTok MCN S.A.S., even below the thresholds, any cross-border transaction with a related creator entity, TikTok subsidiary, or foreign IP-owning affiliate must be benchmarked; documentation is imposed by DIAN through Resolution 000126/2021. If the company uses the SIMPLE regime, TP requirements are substantially simplified, but the standard S.A.S. remains fully subject to Arts. 260-11 to 260-17 E.T.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not have a general Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the common-law sense, but anti-abuse doctrines and 'sustancia económica' are applied through (i) the GAAR (Norma General Antiabuso, Arts. 869-2 E.T.), empowering DIAN to recharacterize transactions lacking business purpose; (ii) the beneficial ownership condition under all Colombian DTAs; (iii) the subcapitalization rules (thin capitalization limit of 3:1 debt-to-equity for payments to foreign related lenders); (iv) CFC rules (Régimen de Compañías Controladas en el Exterior, Arts. 880-1 to 880-4 E.T.) attracting foreign passive income of controlled entities to taxation in Colombia at 35%; and (v) the requirement that the MCN agency actually carry out the core creator-incubation and monetization functions—owning contracts, managing creator relationships, bearing commercial risk, paying local payroll, and maintaining premises and staff—before attributing foreign income or claiming treaty benefits or exemptions. Judicial doctrine in Consejo de Estado has consistently disregarded shell intermediaries lacking economic substance when the real transaction is directly with the foreign principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Omisión de activos / inexistencia de operaciones, Arts. 402 Código Penal; 88, 647 E.T.)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty of imprisonment 6 to 12 years and a fine of 200% to 1,000% of the evaded tax (Art. 402 Código Penal). Administrative penalty: the more severe of (i) 160% of the omitted tax; (ii) 20% of omitted taxable income; or (iii) 20% of net assets when the return omits assets. Additional default interest; dissolution/termination of the legal entity may be ordered by DIAN for repeated infractions.

Director personal liability: Directors, the legal representative, and the tax accountant (revisor fiscal if required) are jointly and severally liable for penalties and tax debt when they sign a false declaration or knowingly cause the omission (Art. 793 E.T.). Colombian courts disregard the S.A.S. limited liability veil when the entity is used as a sham to evade taxes or defraud creditors (Arts. 36-1 and 36-2 of Law 1429/2010 and judicial doctrine). Directors may face travel bans (prohibición de salida del país), asset embargo, extradition to Colombia, and criminal incarceration for evasion. Personal liability extends to fines and interest, notwithstanding the entity's dissolution.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse (Empresa ficticia, testaferro, abuse of S.A.S. limited liability)

Statutory sanction: DIAN may recharacterize the entity as a mere agent/conduit and tax the foreign principal directly in Colombia; penalties up to 200% of the tax assessment; cancellation of the commercial registry; criminal charges for money laundering (Arts. 323-325 Código Penal) if the shell is used to conceal the origin, location, or movement of funds, with imprisonment of 9 to 15 years and fines up to 50,000 SMLMV under Colombian asset-laundering statutes (Law 1762/2015).

Director personal liability: The nominee director and foreign beneficial owner are jointly liable for criminal penalties; the corporate veil is pierced, making the foreign principal directly liable for Colombian tax, interest, and penalties. Directors face personal embargo of assets, 5-to-12-year prison terms, travel bans, and disqualification from holding office in Colombia. Real beneficiary disclosure obligations under Law 2155/2021 impose personal liability on the legal representative for inaccurate beneficial ownership reporting.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Deficiencies in Cross-border Creator Payments (Riesgos LA/FT, Circular Básica Jurídica, Decree 83/2023)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines per infraction: up to 200% of the transaction value or 2,000 SMLMV (approx. COP 2,000 million); cancellation of electronic invoicing authorization; prohibition from acting as a financial service intermediary; suspension of commercial registration; in serious cases, criminal liability under money-laundering law (prison 9-15 years, fines up to 50,000 SMLMV); entity exclusion from the payment rails (TikTok and Wise may freeze or deplatform the agency’s account).

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the legal representative of the S.A.S. are criminally liable if the company's AML failures intentionally or negligently facilitate laundering or financing of terrorism. Personal asset freezes, travel bans, imprisonment, and disqualification from being a manager or director in any Colombian company for up to 10 years after criminal conviction. Additionally, civil liability for damages to creators or counterparties is not insurable for wilful misconduct.
Withholding / Collection Agent Default (Agente de retención incumplido, Arts. 371-1, 402 E.T.)

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty for not collecting and paying withholdings is 100% of the uncollected amount plus interest; late filing penalties and imprisonment 6-12 years if the amount withheld but not paid exceeds a legally defined threshold; DIAN may freeze bank accounts and seize assets of the S.A.S. to satisfy the debt.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative are personally liable for withholding amounts that were actually deducted or collected but not paid over to the Treasury (Art. 402-1 E.T.); the liability is not dischargeable by bankruptcy; the DIAN may issue a travel ban and a precautory embargo against the director's personal assets. Criminal liability applies to the financial manager or whoever signed the withholding declaration.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting (Manipulación de precios de transferencia, Art. 260-10 E.T., Decree 2178/2017)

Statutory sanction: DIAN adjustment to taxable income plus penalty of 5% on the adjusted difference (for lack of documentation/support) and potential 100%–200% of the unreported tax; formal penalty for not filing informative TP return (up to 2,000 UVT per omission); interest on deficiencies. Criminal exposure if the TP misstatement reflects fraudulent intent.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and the person signing the TP disclosure are personally liable for false statements; the corporate veil can be pierced for sham transactions. Directors may be barred from managing entities for up to 10 years and face criminal prosecution for tax fraud. The foreign parent (if indirectly controlling the S.A.S.) may be held jointly liable for uncovered tax when Colombian law attributes beneficial ownership of the S.A.S. to the foreign individual/company.

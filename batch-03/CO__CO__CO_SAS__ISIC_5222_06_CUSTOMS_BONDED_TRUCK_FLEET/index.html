Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official e-services portal: MUISCA (www.dian.gov.co); foreign-trade prior procedures are channelled through the Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior (VUCE).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The non-resident incorporator or resident legal representative obtains a digital certificate (certificado digital) from an authorized certification authority and pre-registers with DIAN through the VUR/MUISCA portal to obtain a Pre-RUT and provisional NIT. Step 2: Execute the S.A.S. articles of incorporation by private document and register the company with the Cámara de Comercio; the Chamber transmits the data to DIAN and the definitive NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) is issued within 1–3 business days; no minimum capital is required. Step 3: Update the RUT in MUISCA to activate mandatory responsibilities: IVA under 'Régimen Común', electronic invoicing issuer status, withholding agent responsibilities, and ICA retention as applicable. Step 4: Appoint a resident legal representative and file beneficial-ownership details in the RUES/Beneficiario Final registry; non-resident shareholders must obtain their own non-resident NIT for dividend and remittance reporting. Step 5: Complete the e-invoicing 'Habilitación' with DIAN before issuing any invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Colombian-resident S.A.S. is taxed at the corporate level at the 35% national income tax rate for FY2023–2025 (Law 2277/2022) on worldwide income; Territorial_Scope: resident companies are subject to worldwide taxation with foreign tax credits and CFC rules; Pass_Through_Eligible: not available in Colombia; Tax_Exempt_Zone: a Zona Franca user entity could elect the special 20% income tax rate and would enjoy certain IVA exclusions for operations physically carried out inside the free-trade zone, although ordinary bonded transits from inland warehouses to airports do not by themselves qualify for Zona Franca treatment.

Available election forms

Colombia does not recognize check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections. Available statutory elections for an S.A.S.: (i) elect the 'Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE)' — an optional combined tax on gross receipts (2%–14.5%) replacing income VAT and ICA components for taxpayers with prior-year gross revenue ≤ 100,000 UVT (≈ COP 4,979.9 million for 2025) and ≤ 50 employees; (ii) elect fiscal consolidation ('declaración consolidada') for a group holding at least 50% of a Colombian subsidiary; (iii) irrevocably claim the export-service zero-rating position under Article 481 E.T. when the service meets the statutory export criteria; (iv) elect the Zona Franca regime upon meeting DIAN/ministry investment and employment requirements; (v) use DIAN's APAs/advance pricing agreements, which are administrative elections rather than entity-classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

COP 0 for legal persons: every S.A.S. is compulsorily a 'responsable del régimen común' and must register in the RUT and obtain the NIT before starting operations, regardless of turnover. The simplified-regime ceiling of 3,500 UVT (≈ COP 174,296,500 for 2025, with UVT 2025 = COP 49,799) applies only to natural persons; corporate entities are excluded from that simplification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Eligibility under Article 481 E.T. and Decreto 1625/2016 for 'exportación de servicios': (1) The GPS-sealed road feeder service must be provided to a direct recipient that is a non-resident entity without a permanent establishment in Colombia; invoicing a Colombian freight forwarder or customs agent is exposed to DIAN challenge unless the contract clearly evidences that the service is performed for the foreign cargo owner and used exclusively abroad or in connection with the outward carriage of goods; (2) The cargo must physically leave Colombian customs territory under an approved bonded transit, evidenced by the final Declaración de Exportación (DEX) at the airport of exit; (3) The consideration must be paid in foreign currency from abroad and channeled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market within 180 days; (4) The GPS seal route must exactly match the authorized customs corridor; (5) The electronic export invoice with CUFE must be issued to the non-resident consignee. Meeting these conditions creates a 0% VAT rate and grants the right to offset and refund input IVA through quarterly/annual tax refund applications (exenta con derecho a compensación y devolución).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX)
  • Bill of Lading (Master/House Air Waybill or multimodal B/L issued to the non-resident consignee)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Certificado de canalización de divisas issued by an authorized bank or exchange house
  • Factura Electrónica de Venta with CUFE code issued as an export invoice
  • Declaración de Tránsito Aduanero (T-DTA) and electronic cargo manifest for the bonded road feeder route
  • GPS seal tracking log (recorded positions and seal integrity) matching the customs-approved corridor
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin where applicable

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta – FEV) is in force for all B2B/B2C operations; each invoice must be transmitted in real time to DIAN's electronic services (web services/API, SOAP/REST), validated online, and returned with a unique CUFE hash code and QR code; issuance is required at the moment the taxable event is completed, and for export services an export invoice must be issued before the operation is declared to customs. The bonded trucking operator must hold an e-invoicing 'Habilitación' through its own certified software or an authorized technology provider. Transport documents and the electronic cargo manifest are integrated with the Declaración de Tránsito Aduanero, allowing DIAN to cross-check the e-invoice against the customs route and the GPS seal data.

DIAN's post-clearance audits compare the DEX, the electronic export invoice, the T-DTA transit route, the GPS seal log, and the FX channeling certificate. If the physical exit of the goods cannot be verified, or the recipient is found to be a Colombian permanent establishment or agent, DIAN reverses the 0% rate and assesses the full 19% IVA on the invoice value plus monthly retroactive inflation-adjusted interest and the 20% penalty for an inaccurate return under Article 648 E.T.; an unfiled or false export declaration triggers additional customs sanctions. Failure to channel the foreign exchange within 180 days brings the operator under the Banco de la República's foreign-exchange regime (Resolución 8/2024), with administrative fines of up to double the un-channeled proceeds and mandatory surrender of the exchange through the authorized market. Fraudulent documents or knowingly fictitious export invoices constitute criminal tax evasion under Articles 434-A and 434-B of the Código Penal, carrying imprisonment of 6 to 10 years, forfeiture of VAT input credits, cancellation of the e-invoicing 'Habilitación', and disqualification of the legal representative; the limitation period for DIAN extends to 10 years if the RUT responsibility has not been updated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso operates under a managed floating exchange-rate regime. Current-account transactions, including ordinary trade, airport feeder services, and transportation payments, are freely convertible through authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries with minimal documentary friction. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity injections from the foreign S.A.S. shareholders, are also convertible but must be channeled exclusively through an authorized intermediary and registered as inward foreign investment with BANREP; there is no general prior licensing permission for a standard capital contribution. The practical profit-remittance hurdles consist of maintaining a compliant investment registration, evidencing distributable profits through statutory financial statements, satisfying Colombian corporate formalities, and completing the corresponding tax withholding and local cumulative tax reporting. Dividends and capital repatriation can be remitted through the formal market once the underlying inward investment registration and local tax obligations are fully documented.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods-export proceeds, which would include proceeds from any cross-border bonded trucking service classified as an export under the customs regime, must be canalized through an authorized foreign-exchange intermediary. The export proceeds are subject to mandatory surrender/reintegration to the Colombian foreign-exchange market in 100% of the amount evidenced by the export customs declaration. The statutory canalización window is generally three months from the shipment/export declaration date, with a possible pre-authorized extension of up to three additional months where the exporter applies to Banco de la República before the expiry deadline. Delayed surrender or non-canalization exposes the S.A.S. to administrative fines, mandatory issuance of foreign-exchange sanctions, and adverse credit classification with the local financial system.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (BANREP), with the Comité de Cambios (Foreign Exchange Committee) as the regulatory rule-setting body; foreign-exchange intermediation and anti-money-laundering compliance are supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary clearing or treasury rail. Colombia does not recognize a foreign digital electronic-money institution as a regulated financial entity for the operation of corporate bank accounts or for the mandatory canalización of foreign-currency inflows and outflows. Offshore EMIs cannot act as authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries under the Colombian exchange regime and cannot provide the legally required documentation for tax or customs reimbursement. Their only viable supplementary role is as a peripheral payment-integration layer for customer front-ends, but the actual settlement, FX conversion, and customs-related flows must sit with a supervised Colombian commercial bank or a locally licensed financial/technology entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing for an inbound equity injection begins with the corporate approval of the capital increase and the registration of the share issuance. The foreign parent must remit the funds by wire transfer, normally through SWIFT MT103, to the company's operational account at an authorized Colombian intermediary. The instruction must include the beneficiary's Colombian tax ID (NIT), the corporate name of the S.A.S., and a clearly identifiable statement of foreign-direct-investment purpose. Upon arrival, the local bank prepares the mandatory foreign-exchange declaration (Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional) and submits the corresponding data to Banco de la República for the inward investment registry. The funds are then available in COP after conversion by the intermediary or are maintained in USD in a permitted foreign-currency account. The bank will require the underlying corporate authorization documents, UBO disclosures, source of funds, RUT, and evidence of the capital injection before accepting the wire; a wire lacking the required investment purpose code will be rejected or held in suspense until the complete investment dossier is presented.Outbound trade disbursements for the bonded truck fleet should use the existing operational account for local COP payments through ACH, the local immediate-payment system (SIP), or PSE-style payment rails when paying Colombian third parties such as fuel, maintenance, toll, and terminal-handling providers. For cross-border procurement of truck assets, spare parts, and airport-related supplies, the appropriate rails are SWIFT MT103 transfers with proper trade-purpose codes and a commercial letter of credit, preferably irrevocable and confirmed, for high-value purchases. In all cases, the disbursement must be accompanied by the relevant tax document, customs import declaration, invoice, and proof of underlying transportation or asset purchase. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary clearing or treasury rail. Colombia does not recognize a foreign digital electronic-money institution as a regulated financial entity for the operation of corporate bank accounts or for the mandatory canalización of foreign-currency inflows and outflows. Offshore EMIs cannot act as authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries under the Colombian exchange regime and cannot provide the legally required documentation for tax or customs reimbursement. Their only viable supplementary role is as a peripheral payment-integration layer for customer front-ends, but the actual settlement, FX conversion, and customs-related flows must sit with a supervised Colombian commercial bank or a locally licensed financial/technology entity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portals: www.dian.gov.co, www.muisca.dian.gov.co (MUISCA virtual taxpayer office for filings, RUT, e-invoicing API and DES submission).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the S.A.S. by private deed or notarial instrument, register the company in the Cámara de Comercio of the corporate domicile, and obtain the Certificado de Existencia y Representación Legal. 2) Appoint a legal representative resident in Colombia and designate a local fiscal address; non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of equity without minimum capital. 3) Submit the Formulario de Inscripción en el Registro Único Tributario (RUT) to DIAN, identifying the entity as Responsable del Régimen Común for IVA and Régimen Ordinario for income tax; the system assigns the Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT) as the unified tax ID, normally validated within 48 business hours. 4) Request the electronic signature mechanism (Firma Electrónica via DIAN-provided certificates or certified technical operators) that is mandatory for the legal representative to sign e-invoices, DES, and periodic returns. 5) Register the S.A.S. as an exporter of services through the MUISCA 'Régimen de Exportación de Servicios' option; foreign shareholders must also register themselves in the RUT on a non-domiciled basis (NIT for foreign entities) and designate a DIAN agent for procedural notifications.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — 1) Incorporate the S.A.S. by private deed or notarial instrument, register the company in the Cámara de Comercio of the corporate domicile, and obtain the Certificado de Existencia y Representación Legal. 2) Appoint a legal representative resident in Colombia and designate a local fiscal address; non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of equity without minimum capital. 3) Submit the Formulario de Inscripción en el Registro Único Tributario (RUT) to DIAN, identifying the entity as Responsable del Régimen Común for IVA and Régimen Ordinario for income tax; the system assigns the Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT) as the unified tax ID, normally validated within 48 business hours. 4) Request the electronic signature mechanism (Firma Electrónica via DIAN-provided certificates or certified technical operators) that is mandatory for the legal representative to sign e-invoices, DES, and periodic returns. 5) Register the S.A.S. as an exporter of services through the MUISCA 'Régimen de Exportación de Servicios' option; foreign shareholders must also register themselves in the RUT on a non-domiciled basis (NIT for foreign entities) and designate a DIAN agent for procedural notifications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not apply a check-the-box regime; the S.A.S. is a separate juridical person taxed at entity level. Available elections and mechanisms: (a) optional Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) under Law 1943/2018 for gross income below 100,000 UVT (approx. COP 4,955,400,000 in 2024), combining income tax, VAT components, and industry-and-trade tax in one return, although high-risk payment processing activities are usually outside SIMPLE eligibility; (b) DIAN discretionary classification as Gran Contribuyente for large taxpayers; (c) election for the recognition of a foreign headquarters' Establecimiento Permanente under arts. 20-1 and 20-2 E.T. when the Colombian operation is only auxiliary (not relevant for a full S.A.S. booking all service income); (d) advance pricing agreements (APAs) and safe-harbor options under the Colombian transfer-pricing rules (arts. 260-10 to 260-11 E.T.); (e) CFC/hybrid rules apply mandatorily under Law 2277/2022 without elective exclusion, but a taxpayer may elect to present self-correction and extended documentation to mitigate hybrid mismatch penalties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for legal persons; a S.A.S. must register as an IVA responsible from the date of constitution and issuance of the RUT. The simplified VAT regime (permitting invoicing without itemized IVA and without monthly declaration) is reserved exclusively for natural persons with gross annual income below 3,500 UVT (COP 173,439,000 approx. for fiscal year 2024, based on UVT = COP 49,554), and is not available to the S.A.S. Consequently, VAT registration and monthly/periodic filing are mandatory irrespective of revenue from day one.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under art. 481 of the Estatuto Tributario and art. 1.6.1.12.6 of Decree 1625/2016, payment gateway and merchant acquiring services qualify for 0% VAT when: (1) the direct recipient of the service is a non-resident entity without domicile or tax residence in Colombia (e.g., a US acquiring bank, foreign PSP parent, or foreign online merchant); (2) the service is effectively used, enjoyed, or commercially exploited exclusively abroad, meaning the authorization, clearing, settlement, and gateway traffic are rendered for foreign cardholders and the benefit accrues in the foreign jurisdiction, not within Colombia; (3) the services are not performed to circumvent territorial rules designed to benefit Colombian residents; (4) the operation is documented with the Declaración de Exportación de Servicios (DES) filed in MUISCA and the export invoice is marked 'Exenta - exportación de servicios'; and (5) the consideration is received through Colombian foreign exchange channels (Declaración de Cambio or compensated accounts) within the applicable 180-day repatriation term. For cross-border acquiring on Visa/Mastercard rails and Fedwire clearing, the service is deemed an export of services because the Colombian entity operates as a back-office processing and settlement hub for foreign-originated transactions, with no Colombian end-consumer as direct beneficiary of the service.

  • Declaración de Exportación de Servicios (DES) Form 204, validated electronically in MUISCA before or within the allowed filing window, indicating the service classification under the Export Services Registry and the ISIC 6619 activity
  • Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV) with CUNE and QR code, expressly marked 'Exenta - Exportación de Servicios - art. 481 E.T.' referencing the DES number
  • Commercial contract or intercompany service agreement executed with the foreign customer, identifying the exact service scope (merchant acquiring, clearing, gateway processing), payment currency, fees, and service levels
  • Certificate of non-residence of the foreign recipient, e.g., W-8BEN-E or foreign tax authority residency certificate, plus bank account proof of the overseas PSP entity
  • Formulario de Declaración de Cambio (FX return) issued by the Colombian intermediary bank or the Foreign Exchange Worksheet for inflows representing the export settlement
  • Inward remittance certificate and bank statements evidencing receipt of USD through the Colombian financial market or the compensation account, with matching invoice numbers
  • Technical documentation of the processing flows (logs, transaction counts, route diagrams) proving that the service is exclusively for foreign counterparties and not consumed in Colombia

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing under DIAN Resolution 000165/2023: the taxpayer must issue Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV) through the Validación Previa (pre-clearance) model, transmitting the invoice dataset in near-real time via REST/SOAP API either directly or through a certified Proveedor Tecnológico; DIAN validates, assigns the CUNE unique electronic invoice code and a QR code, and returns authorization before the transaction can be delivered to the customer. The FEV is mandatory for all sales and export services, including cross-border payment gateway fees. Credit and debit notes also require DIAN pre-validation. The local technical infrastructure and datacenter must be usable by DIAN for verification, and the taxpayer must archive the XML and visual representation for the statutory retention period of five years.

DIAN audits export zero-rating rigorously and may retroactively revert the operation to domestic taxable service, assessing full 19% IVA plus monthly default interest (art. 634 E.T.) and an inexact statement penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded (art. 648 E.T.) if the DES, e-invoice, or FX settlement documentation is absent or inconsistent; failure to repatriate foreign currency within 180 days violates the Colombian foreign exchange regime and voids the export treatment, triggering immediate VAT assessment, a fine of 5% of the non-repatriated sum for non-compliance, and potential exclusion from the exporter's registry for two to five years. Willful fraudulent zero-rating, especially with fabricated documentation, constitutes tax fraud under art. 402 of the Colombian Criminal Code, punishable with 4 to 9 years of imprisonment and disqualification of the legal representative's electronic signature ('Firma'), and Law 2277/2022 extends the tax limitation window to 10 years in cases involving transfers to low-tax jurisdictions or hybrid arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

COP is fully convertible for current-account operations, but all payments for imports of goods and services, dividends, interest, and royalties must be canalized through the MCR, meaning they must be settled through an authorized local intermediary bank with an electronic Declaración de Cambio. Capital-account flows require registration rather than prior license: foreign direct investment is registered on Formulario No. 5 (Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional) and external credit on Formulario No. 6 (Endeudamiento Externo), both filed by the intermediary bank within 15 calendar days of canalización. Profit and dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted without central-bank authorization, but the remitting bank conditions disbursement on (i) valid registration of the foreign investment, (ii) evidence that the applicable dividend withholding tax was withheld and paid to DIAN, and (iii) compliance status of the S.A.S. with tax filings; no convertibility quota or repatriation approval exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombian law imposes no mandatory conversion ratio and no surrender of export proceeds to the central bank. The obligation is canalización: export proceeds must be credited through the MCR to a Colombian resident account, either in COP or in foreign currency, and the foreign currency may be retained in deposit accounts with authorized local banks or in a previously registered Cuenta de Compensación abroad. For goods exports the channeling term follows the export deadline declared in Formulario No. 2; for cross-border services, including the PSP payment-processing model, each USD settlement batch from Visa/Mastercard or Fedwire must be canalized immediately upon receipt and declared by the intermediary on Formulario No. 4 (Declaración de Cambio por Exportación de Servicios) within 5 business days; failure to canalize exposes the S.A.S. to fines and FX-return sanctions.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (central bank and foreign exchange administrator) in coordination with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) for licensing, prudential, and AML/CFT supervision, and with DIAN for tax and customs interfaces. The operative FX instruments are Resolución Externa No. 8 of 2000 (as amended) and the consolidated Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83 of the Banco de la República, which together codify the Mercado Cambiario (MCR) regime; the UIAF (Unidad de Información y Análisis Financiero) receives suspicious-transaction reports.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for outsourcing custody or MCR intermediation: Colombia does not recognize foreign EMI licenses (UK FCA or EU e-money) as a substitute for a local banking or SEDPE license. A cross-border PSP structurally must either (i) obtain SFC authorization as a Sociedad Especializada en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos (SEDPE) under Decreto 2555/2010, (ii) partner with a licensed local bank or SEDPE to hold merchant settlement funds, or (iii) operate purely as a technical acquiring switch without custody of client funds. Offshore EMIs may act only as downstream Visa/Mastercard acquiring and technology providers; they cannot open COP accounts, cannot access the MCR, and cannot hold Colombian merchant funds without triggering SARLAFT and SFC licensing obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injection must arrive by SWIFT MT103 from the registered foreign shareholder to the S.A.S. operational COP or USD account at the chosen bank, with remittance information stating 'capital contribution', the beneficiary NIT, and the ISIC code 6619. The receiving bank files Formulario No. 5 with Banco de la República within 15 calendar days of canalización; the credit is booked as inward FDI only if the remitter matches the registered shareholder and the stated activity code is consistent with the payment-gateway scope. Shareholder loans with tenor exceeding one year require Formulario No. 6 (external credit) registration before repayment; shorter intercompany loans are recorded as other operations and must be settled through the MCR. The S.A.S. must record the capital subscription in notarized shareholders' minutes and in the stock registry before funds are operational; USD funds received on an unregistered account trigger outbound re-declaration obligations.Local merchant payouts in Colombia are cleared through ACH Colombia (CENIT/Transfiya low-value clearing) or through SITRED, the Banco de la República real-time gross settlement system, in COP. Cross-border payouts to foreign merchants or suppliers are executed by SWIFT MT103 from the COP or USD account, with the intermediary bank declaring each operation on Formulario No. 3 (Declaración de Cambio por Importación de Servicios) when the outflow finances services, preserving FX symmetry with the service-export income. For commercial goods transactions, the bank can issue documentary letters of credit under UCP 600 (MT700 issue, MT707 amendment, MT202 cover) with document examination under ISBP 745. USD clearing to the United States (Fedwire target rail) runs through the tier-1 bank's U.S. correspondent network (Citibank N.A. or Bank of New York Mellon); Visa/Mastercard Direct settlement is absorbed into the PSP's USD deposit account and subsequently canalized into the MCR.Onshore deliverable COP/USD foreign-exchange forwards (contratos a plazo) executed over the MCR with authorized local banks; admissible when supported by underlying commercial exposure (settlement pipeline or cross-border investment); standard tenors up to 12 months with roll-over rights.Offshore non-deliverable COP/USD forwards (NDFs) referencing the Tasa Representativa del Mercado (TRM) and cash-settled in USD outside Colombia; usable by the foreign parent or offshore derivatives desk without MCR intermediation.Cross-currency swaps (COP/USD CCS) combining initial and final exchange of principal with COP floating legs referenced to IBR and USD floating legs referenced to SOFR; acceptable for hedging long-term FDI and intercompany loans, subject to Banco de la República derivative position reporting.Short-term domestic FX swaps executed OTC with the local intermediary bank to align settlement currencies on monthly payout cycles, delivered through the MCR and settled via SITRED in COP.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility for outsourcing custody or MCR intermediation: Colombia does not recognize foreign EMI licenses (UK FCA or EU e-money) as a substitute for a local banking or SEDPE license. A cross-border PSP structurally must either (i) obtain SFC authorization as a Sociedad Especializada en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos (SEDPE) under Decreto 2555/2010, (ii) partner with a licensed local bank or SEDPE to hold merchant settlement funds, or (iii) operate purely as a technical acquiring switch without custody of client funds. Offshore EMIs may act only as downstream Visa/Mastercard acquiring and technology providers; they cannot open COP accounts, cannot access the MCR, and cannot hold Colombian merchant funds without triggering SARLAFT and SFC licensing obligations.

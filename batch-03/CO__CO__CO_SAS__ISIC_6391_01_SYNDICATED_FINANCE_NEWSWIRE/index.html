Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio del domicilio social (Registro Mercantil), operating through the Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES) and the 'Camara Directa' online portal; coordinated with DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) for NIT issuance.

Governing statute

Law 1258 of 2008 (Ley 1258 de 2008) creating the Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada; Articles 5 and 8 (incorporation instrument requirements), Article 9 (capital subscription, minimum 10% paid-in and two-year payment term); Código de Comercio, Book II, Articles 98-125 and 134-163 (merchant registration and joint stock company rules); Law 222 of 1995 (commercial oversight); Decree 1074 of 2015 (trade regulation and registry proceedings).

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (private incorporation instrument) under Article 5 of Law 1258/2008; a public deed (Escritura Pública) is mandatory only when real property is contributed to the company.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for a Colombian S.A.S.; the founders freely determine authorized, subscribed and paid-in capital. At least 10% of the subscribed capital must be paid in at the time of subscription and the remaining balance within two years, unless the bylaws provide otherwise (Article 9, Law 1258/2008). No escrow account is legally mandated; a sworn statement of the legal representative or a bank certificate of deposit suffices for registry purposes.

Funding & unlock path: Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos fire-safety certificate verifying extinguishers, emergency exits, fire alarms, and evacuation signage; structural and seismic-safety clearance per Reglamento NSR-10 (National Building Code) for the occupied premises; SGSST occupational health and safety programme under Resolution 0312/2019; and, where the office is located in a BOMA-style commercial building, the building administrator's fire and emergency coordination certificate.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; transactional and e-filing platform: MUISCA (https://muisca.dian.gov.co); electronic invoicing clearance APIs and RUT registration services are hosted on the DIAN web-services platform.

E-government filing portal

For the S.A.S., the incorporation deed is filed with the local Cámara de Comercio, which pre-registers the company in the RUT (Registro Único Tributario); DIAN then assigns the definitive NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), which serves as the single TIN/VAT ID. For a 100% foreign-owned S.A.S., the foreign shareholder must deliver apostilled/notarized corporate resolutions and bylaws, appoint a Colombian legal representative, and the representative must submit the RUT application through the MUISCA portal, designating the entity as (i) IRC taxpayer, (ii) responsable del IVA – Régimen Común, (iii) withholding agent, and (iv) exporter of goods/services. The RUT is then updated with the ISIC 6391 activity code and the export-of-services status before the first zero-rated sale. If the foreign principal does not incorporate locally, it may alternatively register a branch (sucursal de sociedad extranjera) requiring parent-company authorization, local attorney appointment, DIAN and Chamber registration, and a separate NIT. Standard issuance is immediate for pre-validated records and up to five business days where physical documentary review is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the S.A.S. is a Colombian tax-resident corporation taxed on worldwide income at the statutory 35% corporate income tax rate, with dividends paid to foreign shareholders subject to the Colombian dividend withholding framework; Colombia does not recognize pass-through or check-the-box elections. Territorial scope applies only to non-residents: foreign beneficiaries without a Colombian permanent establishment are taxed solely on Colombian-source income. The entity is not eligible for tax-exempt-zone treatment unless specifically located in a designated Zona Franca.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box entity classification exists. Available statutory elections and special regimes include: (i) the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) under Law 1943/2018 and Law 2010/2019, electable by an eligible S.A.S. with gross revenues up to 85,000 UVT, consolidating income tax, IVA, and withholding obligations; (ii) the optional fiscal consolidation / special economic-group regime for business groups meeting DIAN qualification thresholds; and (iii) elections relating to amortization, depreciation, and inventory methods under the Tax Statute. Hybrid-mismatch neutralization (Law 1955/2019 and Law 2277/2022) is mandatory and automatic: deductions for payments producing deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction outcomes are denied to the extent of the mismatch; there is no elective hybrid adjustment, and CFC and transfer-pricing rules further restrict offshore deferral.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold for a corporation: an S.A.S. providing taxable services (including ISIC 6391 financial newswire syndication) is a responsable del Régimen Común and must register for IVA via the RUT prior to commencing taxable activity, irrespective of revenue volume. The 3,500 UVT annual-income simplification threshold under Article 437 of the Tax Statute is available only to natural persons, never to an S.A.S.; exporters claiming zero-rating must register regardless of volume to access input-VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 481 of the Tax Statute and Decree 1625/2016 Article 1.3.7.3, exports are excluded from IVA (0% VAT) with full entitlement to refund of input VAT. Tangible goods qualify upon physical departure from Colombian customs territory under an accepted export declaration. Services, including ISIC 6391 financial newswire and news-aggregation API services, qualify as exports of services only if: (i) the recipient is a natural or legal person without tax residence or domicile in Colombia (and without a Colombian PE); (ii) the service is used or exploited exclusively outside Colombia; and (iii) the consideration is paid from abroad and channeled through the authorized foreign-exchange market, unless a legal exception to exchange channeling applies. For cloud/API-delivered syndicated content, DIAN will examine the place of actual use and exploitation; routing merely through a foreign contracting party while the content is consumed in Colombia defeats zero-rating and exposes the operator to 19% IVA and penalties.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación — DEX) accepted by DIAN/Aduana with MUISCA levante record evidencing the export operation
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) proving physical departure of goods from Colombian customs territory (goods exports only)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Liquidación de Divisas) issued by the authorized Colombia intermediary evidencing receipt and channeling of foreign currency
  • Export contract, commercial invoice referencing the 0% VAT treatment, and foreign purchaser's tax-residence/proof-of-legal-existence documentation
  • DIAN service-export certificate and service-receipt statement confirming exclusive use and exploitation of the service outside Colombia (service exports)
  • RUT updated with export activity codes and Cámara de Comercio certificate evidencing the exporter's legal existence and representation

Mandatory electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta) applies to all Régimen Común taxpayers under Resolution 000165 of 2023. Each invoice must be generated as structured XML, carry the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) and a QR code linked to the DIAN verification service, and be transmitted through certified software (software propio, proveedor tecnológico, or DIAN's free facturador) via the official REST/SOAP APIs. Most taxpayers, including digital-content operators, are subject to validación previa: DIAN validates, corrects, or rejects each invoice in near-real time before the document is delivered to the buyer; accepted invoices may be disputed through the electronic rejection/acceptance event workflow. Non-compliance results in denial of input-VAT credits and deductions, per-invoice sanctions, and inclusion in DIAN preferential audit lists.

DIAN applies documentary and systems-based audits to export refund claims, with pre-refund fiscal review (fiscalización) triggered above defined materiality thresholds; the general assessment period is five years, extended to ten years where forgery or fraudulent evidence is involved. Penal sanctions include: (i) an inexactitud penalty of up to 100% of the tax difference, and up to 200% in qualified/aggravated fraud cases; (ii) mandatory repayment (clawback) of the refund received, plus moratoria interest under Article 634 of the Tax Statute computed from the date of refund receipt; (iii) an additional sanction of up to 100% of the improperly obtained refund; (iv) customs penalties for false commodity/offshore-status declarations; and (v) criminal liability for tax fraud and fraudulent procurement of refunds under Article 434 of the Colombian Criminal Code, carrying imprisonment. Adverse findings also trigger exclusion from the exporter registry, revocation of zero-rating benefits for prior periods, and designation as a high-risk audit taxpayer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed convertible exchange system. The Colombian peso is freely convertible at market-determined rates for both current and capital account transactions, but all cross-border foreign-exchange inflows and outflows must be canalized through authorized local intermediaries. Current account operations—including export of software services, newswire subscription revenue, royalties, interest, and trade settlements—require no prior central bank authorization but must be reported via the applicable cambio declaration form (Form 3 for services). Capital account operations, specifically inbound foreign direct investment, must be declared and registered with Banco de la República using the Form 4 foreign-investment declaration and the electronic Foreign Investment Registration system. Registration is a statutory precondition for the later remittance of dividends, repayment of capital, and the free transferability of the registered investment. Profit remittance is lawful after corporate income tax and dividend withholding obligations are settled; there is no general limitation on remittance amounts, but each outward flow is screened by the intermediary bank for tax, AML, and exchange-control compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For exports of services, including news aggregation and software subscription products, the entire foreign-currency proceeds amount must be channeled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market within one year of the invoice date or the contractual service completion milestone. For exports of physical goods, the exporter must settle the export proceeds within four months from the date of the export declaration (DEX). The statutory surrender rule is 100% canalization, not a mandatory conversion ratio: the exporter must bring the foreign currency into Colombia through an authorized intermediary, but may choose to hold it in a foreign-currency local account or convert it to pesos. Registered digital service exporters may apply to Banco de la República for special approval to retain proceeds abroad solely for liquidity and reinvestment, subject to complete electronic reporting to DIAN and the central bank. Late canalization or failure to canalize triggers administrative penalties under External Resolution No. 8 of 2000, including fines and suspension of foreign-exchange rights.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Banco de la República / Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Unidad Administrativa Especial de Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN), under the foreign-exchange regime of Law 9 of 1991, External Resolution No. 8 of 2000, and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN 83.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must wire the capital contribution in USD, EUR, or COP through standard SWIFT MT103 using the Colombian bank's correspondent or nostro/vostro account. The payment order must name the S.A.S. as beneficiary, include the Colombian NIT number, and the purpose field must state 'Capital increase - Registration of foreign investment under Form 4'. The receiving bank will generate the corresponding exchange-control declaration, and the company must complete the electronic Form 4 and update the Banco de la República investment register within the statutory filing window. Monetary capital contributions cannot be routed through third-party payment platforms, crypto exchanges, or offshore EMI accounts; a direct regulated banking wire is the only compliant lane. For non-monetary capital contributions, such as software licenses or intellectual property, the contribution must be documented by an independent appraiser's certificate and registered as an in-kind foreign investment with the central bank.Routine operational disbursements to foreign data vendors, journalists, technology providers and content platforms are executed as SWIFT MT103/MT202 cover payments through the Colombian bank's correspondent network. Because the business is digital news aggregation rather than physical trade, documentary letters of credit are generally neither required nor practical. If a particular counterparty insists on documentary assurance, the acceptable instrument is a standby letter of credit (SBLC, MT760) issued by a first-class bank, while the underlying cash settlement still occurs by MT103. Domestic payments to Colombian freelancers and software suppliers are cleared through ACH Colombia (ACH-C), the PSE real-time payment system, or local checks. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-medium viability. Colombian exchange-control rules require a resident Colombian legal entity to route its cross-border treasury flows through banks and financial institutions authorized by Banco de la República. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Wise, Mercury) do not hold Colombian banking or foreign-exchange intermediary licenses and cannot be used as a primary or sole treasury rail. A narrow ancillary use is tolerable for low-value card payments collected from non-Colombian subscribers via a technology platform such as Stripe, but those proceeds must be automatically or subsequently repatriated to a Colombian account and reported under Form 3. An S.A.S. relying on an offshore EMI for concentration or settlement of export receipts will create an unresolved canalization breach and a persistent AML/KYC red flag at local banks.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% for Colombian-resident corporations and S.A.S. entities (Colombian Tax Code Article 240, as amended by Law 2277 of 2022).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding tax on dividends paid from taxed profits to non-resident shareholders; 0% if the beneficial owner is a resident Colombian corporation. Additional 5% applies to dividends paid out of profits that had already benefited from a reduced corporate rate or loss carryforward.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The corporate fiscal year is the calendar year. The annual income tax return must be filed and paid between the 10th business day of April and the 14th business day of May of the following year, depending on the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT. Non-resident entities with Colombian-source income are subject to the same deadline schedule.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File if the taxpayer's gross income or total assets at the end of the fiscal year exceed COP 100,000 UVT. For tax year 2024, 100,000 UVT equals COP 4,706,500,000 (approximately USD 1,175,000). The threshold applies separately to gross income and assets; transactions with related parties must be documented if either benchmark is exceeded.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations statute like the UAE; however, Articles 260-1 through 260-10 E.T. incorporate the OECD arm's length standard, and the GAAR under Article 108 E.T. disregards transactions lacking business purpose. Additionally, Article 143 E.T. requires legal form to reflect economic substance. For financing and IP-dependent operations, the DIAN conducts a six-factor economic substance review (personnel, assets, risk control, location, contractual substance, and beneficiary ownership). An S.A.S. that is only a paper entity without operational substance in Colombia will have interposed party transactions recharacterized and may be denied treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraudulent Underreporting)

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty under Article 648 E.T.: 20% to 200% of the omitted tax amount, plus moratorium interest at the DIAN rate. Criminal conviction under Article 111 of Law 1766 of 2015 carries 4 to 8 years' imprisonment and fines up to COP 200,000,000. The penalty is non-deductible.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders who sign or direct false returns are solidarily liable under Article 793 E.T. when the company is used as a mere façade. The Colombian tax authority can impose travel bans (impedimento de salida del país) and initiate criminal action for fraud on the public treasury.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty under Articles 260-9 and 260-10 E.T.: 10% of the value of the related-party transactions, or 5% of the transactions when the documentation is incorrectly presented; taxable income may be upward adjusted based on deemed arm's-length result plus interest.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives who certify false TP documentation are subject to criminal liability for submission of false declarations under Article 290 of the Colombian Criminal Code, which carries 4 to 8 years' imprisonment.
Shell Company / Substance Deficient Structure

Statutory sanction: The DIAN may disregard the entity under Article 108 E.T., deny treaty benefits, impose the 35% corporate tax on the full book revenues, and apply a 100% penalty on the avoided tax. Additionally, if the structure is considered an improper instrument to evade taxes, a criminal charge for tax fraud can be added.

Director personal liability: The S.A.S. law (Law 1258 of 2008) permits veil piercing when the entity is used to commit fraud. Shareholders and directors may be personally liable for all unpaid taxes plus penalties; civil restitution and prohibition on serving as directors for up to 10 years apply.
AML and Cross-Border Payment Violations

Statutory sanction: Under Colombian Law 1121 of 2006 and Law 1766 of 2015, money laundering offences carry fines up to COP 48 billion and imprisonment of 8 to 15 years. If the S.A.S. is used as a conduit to move payments from illicit sources, the corporation itself may be interdicted and its operational licenses revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to implement a Simplified Risk Management System (SRMS) or who approve transactions from shell counterparties face personal criminal liability, asset seizure, and travel bans imposed by the Fiscalía General; they may be barred from commerce for life.

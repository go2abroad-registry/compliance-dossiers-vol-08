Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax and Customs Directorate of Colombia (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales, DIAN); official portal: www.dian.gov.co; transactional portal (Muisca): muisca.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent or founder obtains a Colombian NIT for the new S.A.S. by the following binding procedure: (1) register the company with the local Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio) through the 'Sociedades en Línea' or notarized deed, including corporate bylaws and appointment of a legal representative; (2) before or immediately after incorporation, submit the electronic Pre-RUT (Registro Único Tributario) to DIAN, providing notarized proof of the foreign shareholder's legal existence, full identification data, and the company's fiscal address; (3) upon DIAN's validation, the S.A.S. is permanently enrolled in the RUT and is assigned its definitive NIT and operational tax classification codes; (4) subsequent confirmations of the RUT and the digital e-signature certificate (Certificado Digital DIAN) are activated for all tax filings and e-invoicing obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Colombian S.A.S. is a per se corporation (sociedad de capital) fully subject to Colombian national corporate income tax on worldwide income, without any check-the-box pass-through regime despite the limited liability rules; a domestic S.A.S. is never fiscally transparent.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Colombian law for S.A.S. entities; however, statutory elections are available under specific tax planning rules: (i) filing as a consolidated economic group under the 'régimen de consolidación' or optional tax group regime (Majors: 90% common ownership is required), (ii) election for the preferential 'régimen de empresas cafeteras o de economía naranja' in limited software-related subsectors, and (iii) hybrid mismatch election in accordance with Colombian BEPS Action 2 rules and the statute of 'infracciones por desajustes híbridos' to reconcile Colombian and foreign entity classification mismatches; none permits pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No quantitative turnover threshold applies to corporations. A Colombian S.A.S. is required to register and charge VAT under the 'Régimen Común' from the date it starts any taxable operation, irrespective of monthly sales; the simplified VAT regime (Régimen Simplificado) is restricted to natural persons or sole proprietors with annual gross invoicing not exceeding 3,500 UVT (for 2025, approximately COP 174,296,500) and other statutory conditions; an S.A.S. may never benefit from the simplified regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are physically removed from Colombian customs territory and are excluded from IVA (effectively 0%). Services and intangibles (such as remote screen-share software and unattended IoT telemetry services) qualify for 0% VAT as 'export of services' if all of the following statutory conditions are met: (i) the beneficiary is a foreign person or entity without residence, domicile, or establishment in Colombia; (ii) the service is used or exploited entirely abroad; (iii) the service is executed in whole or in part in Colombia but provides no benefit to taxable activity in Colombian territory; and (iv) the consideration is paid in foreign currency and, when required by DIAN, accompanied by an inward foreign-exchange remittance certificate. The qualifying exporter must keep full electronic records of the contract, invoices, technical delivery, and platform logs proving remote utilization outside Colombia.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX) validated by DIAN or the customs broker
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Electronic Multimodal Transport Document evidencing physical movement, if goods are involved
  • Commercial Invoice and export contract (or PO) identifying the foreign buyer and FOB or EXW terms
  • Packing List and, where applicable, Certificate of Origin for preferential agreements
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro) issued by an authorized Colombian bank or regulated FX intermediary evidencing full repatriation of export proceeds in the name of the exporting S.A.S.

Mandatory under Resolution DIAN 000165 of 2023 (and complementary regulations): the S.A.S. must issue electronic invoices (Factura Electrónica de Venta - FEV) using DIAN-certified software or the DIAN's free web service, transmitting the invoice and its events in XML UBL 2.1 through DIAN's authorized APIs (SOAP/REST). The transaction must receive a unique CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica), be 'validated' by DIAN in real-time fiscal clearance (for large taxpayers and mandatory groups), and in all other cases be transmitted no later than 96 hours after issue. The acquirer must confirm acceptance or rejection through DIAN's event web services; the electronic invoice, debit notes, and credit notes must be stored in a fiscal archive and interchanged digitally at the purchaser's request.

DIAN audits export claims through a documentary and accounting review, cross-checking customs records, FX repatriation data, and IVA exempt regimes. If the 0% export treatment is applied without the statutory export conditions or within the deadline, the exporter must retrospectively pay the 19% IVA withheld at source plus default interest calculated from the date the invoice was issued. Fraudulent zero-rating, false declarations, or simulated exports are sanctioned as VAT evasion and may trigger: (i) correction and penalty taxes up to 160% of the amount evaded; (ii) administrative monetary fines; (iii) criminal liability under Law 1762 of 2015 for customs fraud and money laundering, leading to imprisonment of up to 14 years; and (iv) cancellation of the DIAN electronic operational registration and suspension of the exporter's customs authorization.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is the legal tender and maintains full convertibility for current account transactions, including the export of software development and remote support services, payment of royalties, contractual obligations, and shareholder profit distribution. Capital account transactions, notably foreign direct investment (FDI) into a Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.), are permitted without the need for prior administrative approval; however, the injection must be registered as a capital registration with Banco de la República under Circular Reglamentaria DCIN 83 of 2015. Profit remittance is lawful and unrestricted once the company has satisfied its Colombian corporate income tax obligations, including the applicable dividend withholding tax regime for non-resident shareholders. Dividends remitted abroad attract the statutory withholding tax on dividends plus the 'remittance tax' levy set out in Article 407 of the Colombian Tax Code, unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty. No material convertibility impediment exists for gross settlement of software-as-a-service invoicing, but the local entity must ensure its export receipts are channeled through authorized FX intermediaries to avoid breach of the exchange regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Decree 2245 of 2011 and External Resolution No. 8 of 2000 issued by Banco de la República, export proceeds from information technology and software services must be collected through a Colombian authorized foreign exchange intermediary. The statutory collection and settlement period for service exports is generally four (4) months computed from the due date or the issuance of the export declaration ('Declaración de Cambio por Exportaciones'). The total invoiced foreign currency must be surrendered into the Colombian exchange market by selling it to the intermediary; no designated percentage is exempted except for amounts lawfully used to pay substantiated imports, financial obligations, or other exposures formally qualified as 'compensaciones' under the FX regulation. Failure to comply can trigger a fine and infringement proceedings before DIAN/Banco de la República, along with reputational blocking by local banks. The S.A.S. must also file the corresponding 'Información Exógena' to DIAN in support of the export transactions.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), with foreign exchange oversight shared with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Unlicensed offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or Currencycloud are not authorized to maintain direct bank settlement accounts for a Colombian entity unless they act solely as payment facilitators through a locally regulated bank partner. Colombia's financial law reserves deposit-taking and operating-account current functions to institutions licensed by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. An S.A.S. must possess at least one conventional current account at a regulated Colombian credit institution to satisfy FX obligation rules and to permit corporate treasury and tax operations. Offshore EMIs may be adopted only as receiving rails after the mandatory local account, provided the export invoice and the final FX declaration remain traceable to the local regulated intermediary. Legal risk remains the failure to channel export earnings directly into the authorized dealer, which may constitute an exchange offense under Banco de la República rulings.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial and subsequent FDI injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 wire from the foreign shareholder to the S.A.S.'s operational account located in Colombia. The reference line in the MT103 must read 'APPORT DE CAPITAL – INVERSIÓN EXTRANJERA DIRECTA' and include the investor's foreign tax identification, the Colombian NIT of the S.A.S., and the intended use of funds. The receiving bank will generate a 'Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional' using foreign-exchange form Formulario 3, assigned purpose code 4001 for a contribution to working capital or general corporate equity participation. If the contribution is made by a related party to fund the development of remote-screen-share / IoT software, the bank may additionally require the parent's board resolution, registration of the investment in the Banco de la República FDI registry, and an updated commercial registry certificate showing the capital increase. Legal risk remains the failure to channel export earnings directly into the authorized dealer, which may constitute an exchange offense under Banco de la República rulings.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio with territorial jurisdiction at the registered office (e.g., Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá), operating through the Single Business Registry (RUES) and subject to oversight of the Superintendencia de Sociedades.

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 (S.A.S. regime), Articles 1, 5, 13–22 (formation, contents, registration, limited liability); Código de Comercio, Decreto 410 de 1971, Articles 98, 110, 113–114, 196 and 372 (corporate registration, legal representation, sole shareholder resolutions); Decreto 1074 de 2015, Article 2.2.2.31.1.2 (registration procedures); Law 2069 de 2020 (one-week company incorporation and digital filing); Law 1429 de 2010 (new-company tax benefits where applicable).

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (private incorporation instrument) filed via the Formulario Único de Registro Empresarial / RUES portal; elevated to a Notarial Deed (Escritura Pública) only where real property is contributed.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 19 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. Article 5 of Ley 1258 de 2008 allows the authorized, subscribed and paid capital to be freely fixed in the by-laws. None of the subscribed capital is required to be paid at execution, but any paid portion must be deposited into a corporate bank account opened in the name of the S.A.S.; there is no escrow account obligation. For the voice-streaming social app, a registered capital of COP 1,000,000 (approximately USD 250) is commonly declared as sufficient for the object and initial operations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety technical clearance (Concepto Técnico de Seguridad Humana y Contra Incendios) issued by the Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos for the leased office; structural and occupancy compliance certified by the Curaduría Urbana in the applicable land-use/occupancy permit; occupational health and safety clearance under Law 1562 de 2012, Resolution 2400 de 1979 and mandatory employer registration with ARL (Administradora de Riesgos Laborales), together with payroll integration with EPS and pension funds before staff commencement.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital. Article 5 of Ley 1258 de 2008 allows the authorized, subscribed and paid capital to be freely fixed in the by-laws. None of the subscribed capital is required to be paid at execution, but any paid portion must be deposited into a corporate bank account opened in the name of the S.A.S.; there is no escrow account obligation. For the voice-streaming social app, a registered capital of COP 1,000,000 (approximately USD 250) is commonly declared as sufficient for the object and initial operations.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing board mandate applies to the digital voice-streaming app. Reference authorities: MinTIC (Registro TIC for optional providers of telecommunications/network services), the municipal Alcaldía and Curaduría Urbana for land-use certification, and the Cámara de Comercio for commercial establishment registration.

Category: Not applicable — no statutory operating or manufacturing permit exists for an interactive audio chatroom/voice-streaming social application. The activity is authorized by the commercial registry certificate (Certificado de Existencia y Representación Legal); a physical office requires a municipal land-use certificate (Certificado de Uso de Suelo); a provider may optionally obtain the Certificado de Registro TIC before MinTIC if the application is characterized as a value-added communication service under Law 1978 de 2019.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – official portal muisca.dian.gov.co and www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Colombian S.A.S. (Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada) must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through the private Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio) registration process. The company must first file the 'Pre-RUT' (Registro Único Tributario) via the DIAN electronic portal (Muisca) or at a DIAN service point, selecting the 'Sociedad' category and indicating the foreign investment status. After incorporation, the S.A.S. must complete the definitive RUT registration, including the address, legal representative, economic activities (ISIC 6311 for data processing and hosting), and the applicable VAT/withholding obligations. For non-resident digital service providers without local incorporation, DIAN requires registration under the special simplified foreign digital services regime, which involves obtaining an NIT for TIN purposes and activating VAT for digital services. In both cases, the NIT is the single tax identifier used for income tax, VAT, and customs purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the S.A.S. is a Colombian tax resident company subject to national corporate income tax (Impuesto sobre la Renta y Complementarios) on worldwide income. The S.A.S. is not a pass-through entity; only dividend distributions to foreign shareholders are taxed as foreign-source dividends at the shareholder level. Nonetheless, the territorial sourcing rules apply to Colombian-source annual income earned by non-resident digital service providers when no local permanent establishment is present.

Available election forms

Colombia has no check-the-box mechanism. A S.A.S. may elect the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) under Law 1943/2018 if gross revenues are below 100,000 UVT and other statutory thresholds are met, simplifying income tax, VAT, and local taxes. A S.A.S. may also elect pension and parafiscal contributor classifications. For hybrid mismatch purposes, Colombia adopts BEPS Action 2 recommendations and allows limited anti-hybrid alignment; however, no separate transparency election exists for an S.A.S. Foreign digital service providers may elect to be treated as 'sin domicilio en Colombia' for VAT purposes and appoint a local responsible intermediary when required.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic taxable activities generally require VAT registration if gross taxable turnover exceeds 3,500 UVT per year (approx. COP 154 million in 2024). For non-resident digital service suppliers providing streaming, podcast, or audio chat services, mandatory DIAN registration arises when the annual gross consideration from Colombian users exceeds 41,000 UVT (approx. COP 1.8 billion in 2024), regardless of the absence of a local permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the S.A.S. exporting services (including voice streaming and remote social app services), zero-rated VAT applies only if: (i) the service is rendered to a non-resident person or entity who is not a VAT-registered Colombian taxpayer; (ii) the service is used exclusively abroad or is in connection with goods exported from Colombia; (iii) the service is covered by a written service export contract; and (iv) the Colombian provider obtains and retains a 'Certificado de Prestación de Servicios al Exterior' or equivalent supporting evidence. The rules for digital services require that the place of use and effective enjoyment be outside Colombia. If a Colombian resident user purchases the voice streaming app through the S.A.S. and the user consumes it in Colombia, it is not zero-rated. Export zero-rating also requires that the S.A.S. be registered as an 'Exportador de Servicios' and that the corresponding export declaration (Declaración de Cambio) is filed for foreign exchange purposes.

  • Declaración de Exportación de Servicios (Formulario de exportación de servicios) or electronic equivalent for service exports
  • Export services contract containing the nature, value, and place of effective use of the service
  • Certificado de Prestación de Servicios al Exterior signed by the non-resident customer
  • Commercial invoice in USD or other accepted foreign currency, referencing the NIT and the service contract
  • Inward remittance certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas) issued by the local bank or exchange office
  • Valid domestic electronic invoice transmitted to DIAN with export evidence
  • Customs export declaration for physical goods (for any bundled hardware or physical media) and Bill of Lading / Air Waybill

Colombia mandates full real-time electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) integrated with DIAN. All VAT registered taxpayers must issue e-invoices through a DIAN-authorized software provider using the standard XML schema and transmit to DIAN for validation before delivery. For credit notes, debits, and debit notes, the same electronic channel applies. Foreign digital service providers under the special regime may issue simplified electronic invoices through an authorized intermediary or via DIAN's web portal without the full local issuer setup. Each invoice receives a CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) or, in the case of contingency, a CUDE. No physical fiscal stamp is used; DIAN validates in real time via API-based services. In addition, all calendar monthly filed periodic VAT declarations (F-300) must reconcile electronically with transmitted invoices.

Improperly claiming zero-rating on services that are actually consumed in Colombia or without actual export substantiation triggers retroactive VAT assessment at 19%, plus monetary correction interest (intereses moratorios), and fines for inexact returns under Article 648 of the Colombian Tax Code. The penalty for inexact VAT declarations can be up to 200% of the understated tax, and for fraudulent export zero-rating claims, a criminal tax fraud penalty under Law 1943/2018 may apply, including imprisonment and disqualification from exporting for a specified period. DIAN audits export files, foreign exchange repatriation records, and customer location data for digital service enterprises; failure to meet the 180-day FX repatriation deadline may lead to exchange control sanctions, denial of export VAT exemption, and mandatory reimbursement of input VAT previously recovered.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The COP is fully convertible under a managed float. Current-account flows (service exports, service imports, dividend remittances on registered foreign investment) are liberalized and require only channeling through an IMC plus a corresponding 'declaración de cambio' (Formulario No. 5 for goods/services; Formulario No. 3 for international investments), as opposed to prior administrative approval. Capital-account inflows, including the paid-in capital of a Colombian SAS, require registration as 'inversión extranjera directa' (IED) with Banco de la República through the IMC at or immediately after the transfer date; once the investment is registered, dividends and capital repatriation are freely remittable, subject to documentary verification of the registered investment, auditable reserves, transfer pricing compliance and absence of foreign-exchange infractions. The real hurdles are administrative and evidentiary (timely registration, UBO transparency, matching wire narratives, financial statements evidencing distributable profits) rather than quantitative; there are no statutory ceilings, queues, or quota approvals on profit remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Service export proceeds (voice-streaming subscriptions, virtual-coin sales through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, in-app gifting) must be channeled through the Colombian foreign exchange market: the USD settlement received from Apple/Google must be either (a) sold to an authorized IMC, or (b) credited to a foreign-currency account at an IMC or to a previously authorized 'cuenta de compensación' (compensation account) abroad, within six months from accrual/payment. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to COP; exporters may retain USD indefinitely inside permitted accounts, but every inflow must be reported via Formulario No. 5, with the recommended treatment being (i) declaration of the gross export of services (consumer billings) and (ii) simultaneous declaration of the import of app-store distribution/commission services, so that the net USD settlement reconciles with the bank statement. Retaining Apple/Google proceeds in an unregistered offshore EMI, missing the six-month channeling deadline, or imperfectly reconciling settlement amounts constitutes a foreign-exchange infraction subject to administrative fines imposed by the exchange-control authorities and to reputational banking sanctions by the SFC.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia) holds exclusive exchange-control authority under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1068 of 2015 (Único Reglamentario Sector Hacienda y Crédito Público) and Circular Reglamentaria Externa DCIN-83 (Régimen de Cambios Internacionales). The regime is operationally administered through authorized Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario (IMCs), i.e., duly licensed commercial banks, which are supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC); the DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) administers the NIT/RUT and maintains the tax information framework that anchors the documentary records required by the IMC declarations.

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for primary treasury use. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) execute share-subscription and related corporate documents; (2) obtain the SAS NIT and Cámara de Comercio registration; (3) open the COP operational account at a designated IMC (Bancolombia preferred); (4) file Formulario No. 3 (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales) through the IMC at or immediately after the disbursement date, obtaining the bank's investment registration reference number and endosing the foreign investor's legal capacity; (5) investor wires SWIFT MT103 with beneficiary name exactly matching the registered corporate name plus NIT (the internal identifier 'CO_SAS' must not appear in the wire), account number, the bank's SWIFT/BIC, and payment narrative 'APORTE DE CAPITAL - INVERSIÓN EXTRANJERA DIRECTA'; (6) upon credit, the IMC tags the transaction with the investment registration code and generates the declaración de cambio; (7) funds may remain in USD in a linked foreign-currency account or be converted spot to COP at the IMC's reference rate; (8) any future capital increase must update the same registration file, and all future dividends and capital repatriations must cite the registration code; the SAS must maintain a permanent per-shareholder paid-in capital sub-ledger.Clearing rails: (a) domestic operating costs via COP account using ACH (Cámara de Compensación), Transfiya real-time transfers, PSE for supplier payments, and electronic payroll through 'nómina electrónica'; (b) international disbursements for cloud computing (AWS, Google Cloud), SaaS tools, and cross-border professional fees via SWIFT MT103 single payments executed by the IMC after conversion or by debiting the USD account; batch contractor remittances through MT101/MT102; (c) each outbound payment must be reported as an import of services in Formulario No. 5 (most IMCs allow aggregate monthly declarations), supported by the underlying invoice and platform contract; (d) documentary letters of credit are ordinarily irrelevant for service-only flows, but the IMC may issue a standby LC or performance guarantee if an app-store aggregator or corporate client requests deposit coverage; (e) all USD/COP conversions are executed T+0/T+1 with the IMC as principal to eliminate private counterparty FX risk.USD/COP non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) sold by authorized IMCs, settled in COP, permitted for hedging forecast Apple/Google USD receivables up to 24 months under a board-approved treasury hedging policyOTC vanilla USD/COP options (call/put) offered by SFC-licensed IMCs, with premium and exercise settlement in COP and a confirmed underlying export exposureCross-currency swaps (USD/COP and USD floating-rate vs IBR) with SFC-authorized counterparties documented under ISDA/CSAListed USD/COP futures contracts on the BVC (Bolsa de Valores de Colombia) derivatives market for granular tranche hedging if the SAS qualifies as an institutional clientNatural hedging by retaining USD proceeds in the registered foreign-currency account at the IMC and matching USD outflows (Apple/Google commissions, cloud infrastructure costs, international contractor payments) without executing unnecessary spot conversions

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for primary treasury use. Domestic digital banks (Lulo Bank, Pibank, Nu Colombia) and SEDPE payment institutions (Nequi, Movii, Dale, Addi) can open COP accounts, but none offers the required foreign-exchange declaration channel (Formulario No. 5), a linked USD account within the IMC framework, or the registered-investment (Formulario No. 3) facility; operational caps and product limitations make them unsuitable for commercial-scale Apple/Google settlement flows. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury, Currenxie) are legally usable only as an auxiliary expense rail (e.g., paying non-resident contractors, SaaS subscriptions), provided payments originate from duly channeled Colombian funds and are declared as imports of services; receiving or parking Apple/Google export settlements in an offshore EMI without a Banco de la República-registered compensation account breaches the six-month channeling rule and triggers foreign-exchange infractions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% (Ley 2277 de 2022, Article 240 E.T.)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding on dividends distributed to a non-resident shareholder out of profits that were already subject to corporate income tax (Article 242-1 and Article 408 E.T.). No additional income tax is levied on the foreign parent if the dividend was paid from taxed profits; distributions from untaxed profits are reclassified as taxable at 35% full-rate on the distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual income tax return is due in the month of February through April of the following calendar year, on a staggered schedule fixed by DIAN by reference to the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT; legal entities typically file within the period beginning 15 February and ending 30 April. For FY2024 returns, deadlines ran between 17 February and 29 April 2025.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where the Colombian taxpayer's gross income or total assets exceed 100,000 Tax Value Units (UVT) (COP 4,706,500,000 for FY2024; COP 4,979,900,000 for FY2025) and the taxpayer enters into transactions with related parties. Master File: taxpayers belonging to an MNE group that exceeds 20,000,000 UVT of consolidated group revenue must file; a full CbC Report applies to groups with consolidated revenue above 20,000,000 UVT. The informative annual TP return (Form 120) is due to DIAN in the same filing window as the CIT return for all taxpayers meeting the above economic thresholds and carrying out related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Colombia has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the Gulf-Council style; however, the S.A.S. must maintain substantive local activity to avoid (i) foreign-controlled-company (CFC) attribution to the parent under Articles 881-883 E.T., (ii) recharacterization under the GAAR (Article 869 E.T.), and (iii) lack of 'beneficial use' for treaty reduced rates. The comparative voice-streaming business requires local staff, bank accounts, server contracts, and real board oversight in Bogotá; a passive conduit with an address-only presence is exposed to DIAN reassessment and to 'abusive structure' penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Criminal) and Sophisticated Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal: 48-108 months' imprisonment and a fine equal to 50% to 200% of the evaded tax (Article 402, Código Penal, as amended by Ley 2277 de 2022). Administrative: DIAN will assess the omitted tax plus 'Sanción por inexactitud' at 100% of the difference (Article 648 E.T.), plus default interest at 1.5% monthly; criminal case must be triggered by a formal DIAN penal complaint.

Director personal liability: The legal representative is jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax if the company's assets are insufficient (Article 794 E.T.); in criminal cases, the representative's personal assets can be seized, travel bans (prohibición de salir del país) can be imposed during investigation, and a conviction carries imprisonment, plus disbarment from holding directorships for up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing Misdocumentation / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: DIAN may adjust the price, deny the deduction, apply enactment of the 'Sanción por inexactitud' at 100% of the omitted tax and recalculate transfer pricing penalties; failure to submit Documentation or Form 120 triggers the per-infraction fine under Article 651 E.T. of up to 15,000 UVT (COP 704.6 million for FY2025) and a reduction of the audit limitation period from 3 to 6 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the Form 120 with false TP information may be jointly liable; the corporate veil can be pierced under the 'abuso de la forma societaria' doctrine (Article 6 Ley 1429/2010 and explicit GAAR tax penalty under Article 671 E.T.), which lets DIAN assess the hidden taxpayer and hold the parent as joint debtor plus apply penal liability for untrue statements (Article 434-Bis Código Penal).
Shell/Circular Cash-Flow Structure and Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: DIAN can impose: (i) for the Beneficiario Final omission, fines of up to 10,000 UVT per month of delay (Article 651 E.T. applied analogically); (ii) for lack of substance, the reattribution of all income to the foreign parent under CFC rules with a 35% tax and a 10% dividend WHT at source in Colombia; (iii) a declaration of 'sociedad simulada' in the commercial registry leading to cancellation of the company's registration and joint liability of the nominee shareholder.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors of the S.A.S. are personally liable for civil restitution of the unpaid tax (Article 794 E.T.), risk criminal money-laundering liability if the circular flow is intended to conceal beneficial ownership (Article 323 Código Penal: 10-30 years), and can be subject to a preventive freezing order and a travel ban pending the investigation.

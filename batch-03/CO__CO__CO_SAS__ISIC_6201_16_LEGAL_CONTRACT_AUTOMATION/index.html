Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - Official portal: www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities without a permanent establishment must obtain a Tax Identification Number (NIT) through the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) by appointing a local fiscal agent or attorney-in-fact. Registration is completed electronically via DIAN's user portal using a digital signature certificate issued in Colombia, followed by formal filing of the RUT and issuance of the NIT certificate.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities without a permanent establishment must obtain a Tax Identification Number (NIT) through the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) by appointing a local fiscal agent or attorney-in-fact. Registration is completed electronically via DIAN's user portal using a digital signature certificate issued in Colombia, followed by formal filing of the RUT and issuance of the NIT certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available. A Colombian S.A.S. is subject to entity-level corporate income tax. The only relevant statutory election, where eligible, is the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE), which permits a combined taxation regime for qualifying small-service entities with annual gross revenue below statutory thresholds. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized as a separate mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None (zero de minimis threshold). Any person or foreign digital service provider making taxable supplies of services to Colombia, or entering into export activities, must register for VAT through the RUT process; non-resident digital suppliers have no fiscal turnover threshold triggering registration for services used in Colombia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Services (including LegalTech SaaS, cloud document automation, and e-signature authentication) qualify for the VAT 0% rate where the recipient is a non-resident entity with no domicile in Colombia, the service is effectively used or exploited abroad, and the supplier documents the export nature in compliance with Article 481 of the Colombian Tax Statute and DIAN interpretative rules. For digital services, the zero-rating applies only if the sale is not rendered for direct use within Colombia and the contract indicates offshore usage.

  • Export Invoice (Factura de Venta Electrónica) marked with VAT 0% export status
  • Signed service contract or order documentation indicating overseas recipient and offshore use
  • Foreign Exchange Declaration (Declaración de Cambio) for export services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reembolso de Divisas) issued by the Colombian bank or FX intermediary
  • Certified proof of non-resident status of the counterparty (e.g., tax domicile certificate) and CUFE-validated electronic export invoice

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica de Venta - FEV) applies. Invoices must be generated by DIAN-authorized software, contain the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) stamp, and be transmitted to DIAN in near-real time through government web-service APIs. Digital fiscal clearance is effected via DIAN online validation; export invoices also require electronic validation and the exporter's digital certificate.

Export zero-rating is subject to documentary audit by DIAN. If the export status is successfully challenged, the VAT becomes payable at 19% on the full invoice value, plus inflation-adjusted default interest and a penalty generally equivalent to 100–200% of the omitted tax where inaccuracy is attributed to negligence or fraud. Submission of forged export documents may trigger criminal liability for fraud under Colombian tax and criminal law, including imprisonment, and retrospective clawback of any VAT refunds received.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a free-market foreign-exchange regime (Régimen de Libre Cambio) under Ley 9 de 1991. Current-account operations, including service-export receipts and profit remittances, are fully convertible and freely negotiable through authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries (IMCs) without prior Central Bank authorization. Capital-account operations, including non-resident equity injections into a S.A.S., are freely convertible but subject to mandatory registration with Banco de la República (Registro de Inversión Internacional). Profit remittance hurdles are documentary only: proof of registered foreign-investment status, DIAN tax-compliance filings, dividend-distribution minutes, applicable dividend withholding at the statutory rate for non-resident shareholders on distributed taxed profits, and execution through an IMC. Compliance is declaratory and ex-post; no PRIOR approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export receipts—including software-as-a-service licensing fees under ISIC 6201—equal to or exceeding the USD 10,000 equivalent must be collected and channeled (canalizado) through the Colombian foreign-exchange market via an authorized IMC within six (6) months from the date of service performance or the corresponding receivable due date. The mandatory conversion (surrender) ratio is 100%: the exporter must sell the hard-currency proceeds to the IMC for local COP, or place them in a permitted foreign-currency account maintained with the IMC under the exchange regime. Receipts below the USD 10,000 threshold remain un-intermediated and may be collected freely. Under-reporting, late canalización, or split structuring near the threshold creates remedial fines, DIAN recategorization risk, and loss of export-benefit eligibility.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting with DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) as enforcement authority, pursuant to Resolución Externa 8 de 2000 and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83 (Régimen Cambiario). The Junta Directiva del Banco de la República issues all foreign-exchange control regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Medium as a treasury substitute; Medium as a supplementary collection rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Mercury or Wise, holding no Colombian financial license, cannot qualify as IMCs under Colombian FX law; they cannot on-ramp COP disbursements, cannot execute local payroll, cannot enter Colombian forward contracts, and cannot perform the statutory canalización of export proceeds of USD 10,000 or more. They may lawfully hold USD/EUR SaaS collections as offshore receivables and consolidate funds before a single SWIFT MT103 transfer into the Colombian IMC account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% for dividends paid out of profits not already subject to CIT at the corporate level; 10% on dividends distributed from profits already taxed in Colombia. In practice, a dividend paid by a Colombian SAS to a foreign shareholder is subject to withholding at the applicable statutory rate, and the payor must remit the tax through Form 110 and the relevant DIAN collection forms.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return is due between the 9th day of the fourth month and the 7th day of the sixth month following the fiscal year-end. The exact date is set by DIAN by resolution according to the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT; failing that date triggers penalties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For FY2024, the Local File and Master File obligations arise for taxpayers with gross income or total assets exceeding approximately COP 4,706,000,000 (100,000 UVT of COP 47,065), or for related-party cross-border transactions exceeding approximately COP 470,000,000 (10,000 UVT). The Master File is also required when the multinational group has consolidated turnover exceeding COP 75,000,000,000. The annual TP informative return (Form 120) must be filed electronically when the same thresholds are met.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulation comparable to the UAE ESR. Instead, the DIAN applies substance-over-form, commercial-rationality, and general anti-abuse principles under the Tax Code and the DTA Principal Purpose Test. A Colombian SAS engaged in LegalTech SaaS must maintain real personnel, an operational office or hub, substantive software contracts, active bank accounts, board minutes, and business decision-making in Colombia in order to support ordinary CIT treatment, TP comparisons, and treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False or Inexact CIT Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty under Article 647 ET equal to 100% of the omitted or understated tax, plus moratory interest. Criminal tax evasion carries imprisonment of 48 to 108 months and fines under Law 1778/2016 Article 434A. The entity also faces exclusion from tax amnesties and inability to obtain clean tax certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign the false return are jointly and severally liable for the tax, interest, and penalties under Article 794 ET. Where the evasion is proven as deliberate fraud, the corporate veil is pierced; directors face imprisonment, travel bans, asset embargo, prohibitions on serving as officer/manager of Colombian companies, and civil restitution to the DIAN and investors.
Failure to File / Late Filing / TP Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of 5% per month of the tax payable, capped at 100%; sanction for overdue TP documentation may be a percentage of the affected transaction value or a fixed UVT-based fine. DIAN can issue an ex officio assessment based on presumptive income, and the absence of TP documentation shifts the burden to the taxpayer. Criminal sanctions apply if the default is linked to evasion.

Director personal liability: The legal representative is personally liable for withholding taxes not remitted to the DIAN. Travel restraints, bank account freezes, and enforcement against personal assets may be ordered for persistent non-compliance. Directors who knowingly fail to disclose related-party transactions may face personal fines and criminal prosecution for aiding tax fraud.
Shell Company / Sham Transactions / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: DIAN will disregard the corporate form, recharacterize the structure, deny treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test, and apply the full statutory WHT of up to 35% on dividends and applicable rates on royalties/interest, plus penalties and moratory interest. The transactions may also be treated as inexact entries, attracting a 100% inexactness penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute simulated back-to-back agreements, phantom employment contracts, or circular funding structures are treated as parties to tax fraud; they may be held civilly liable for the full tax deficiency and face criminal indictment, veil-piercing, asset confiscation, and a ban on international travel pending criminal tax proceedings.
Anti-Money Laundering and Payment-Rails Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Colombian AML/FT legislation (Law 526/2012 and Decree 83/2017) imposes administrative fines on companies that fail to identify final beneficiaries or file suspicious-transaction reports with the UIAF. The fines can reach thousands of UVT, and if the underlying activity facilitates money laundering, the responsible parties face up to 20 years of imprisonment. Non-compliance also renders tax refunds and treaty benefits unavailable.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who approve or ignore irregular payment flows are personally liable for AML fines, may be excluded from managing financial or corporate entities, and can suffer preventive personal measure, including travel bans, asset seizures, and pretrial detention. The corporate limited-liability shield does not protect managers who knowingly participate in laundering proceeds derived from tax fraud.

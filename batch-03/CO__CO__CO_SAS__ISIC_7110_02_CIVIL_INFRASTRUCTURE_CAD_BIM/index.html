Competent corporate registry

Registro Mercantil - Cámara de Comercio del domicilio social (e.g., Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá, Medellín or Cali), operated through the RUES (Registro Único Empresarial y Social) platform, with the DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) as the counterpart authority for tax identification.

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 (Colombian Simplified Stock Corporation Act - S.A.S.), Articles 1, 4-5, 13, 19, 21, 26-31; supplementary rules of the Código de Comercio (Decree 410/1971), Articles 110-163; Decreto Único Reglamentario 1074 de 2015, Book II (Mercantile Registry); applicable tariff agreements of the Superintendencia de Sociedades for registration fees.

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (private incorporation deed) together with the Estatutos (By-Laws), filed for registration in the Registro Mercantil through the RUES registration form; notarization is optional for S.A.S. under Law 1258/2008 Article 5.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 6 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; minimum_capital_requirement is false. The shareholders set the authorized, subscribed and paid-in capital freely in the By-Laws. Under Law 1258/2008 Article 19, contributions must be paid within one year of subscription, with default interest in case of delay. Contributions in kind or foreign currency require an appraisal; an escrow/consignment account is mandatory only where the By-Laws so provide or for share premiums.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - National Tax and Customs Directorate. Main portal: www.dian.gov.co. Transactional and electronic filing platform: MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado). Electronic invoicing validation and clearance API: DIAN's Facturación Electrónica web services environment (fe.dian.gov.co).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the S.A.S. through a notarized deed or private document registered before the local Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce); no minimum paid-in capital is required for the Colombian S.A.S. Step 2: Appoint a legal representative who is a natural person resident in Colombia (national or foreigner with valid migration status) to act as the fiscal interlocutor with DIAN. Step 3: The legal representative (or an authorized registered gestor) completes the online pre-registration in MUISCA, providing the foreign parent/shareholder's incorporation documents, the S.A.S.'s by-laws, the local address, and the economic activity codes (ISIC/CIUU 7110 and the CIIU class corresponding to computer/engineering drafting services). Step 4: Obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and the RUT (Registro Único Tributario), which must include the VAT responsible status code, the income-tax responsible status, the service-export registration flag, and any applicable industry-specific codes. Step 5: Acquire a DIAN-accredited digital signature certificate (Certificado de Firma Electrónica) and register an authorized electronic invoicing software provider to enable mandatory e-invoicing. Step 6: For outbound service payments, register the S.A.S. as an exporter of services with the foreign-exchange declaration channels of the Banco de la República through a local authorized intermediary. A foreign entity cannot obtain a standalone Colombian TIN without creating a provincial branch or a local entity; the S.A.S. itself is the taxpayer for all purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The Colombian S.A.S. is a per se resident corporation under Article 12 of the Colombian Tax Code (E.T.). It pays national corporate income tax (Impuesto sobre la Renta) at the effective headline rate of 35% on its worldwide net income, with no consensus passthrough to shareholders. There is no territorial-source election for the S.A.S.; however, export income remains taxable on net profits (gross revenue less deductible costs and expenses) with an eligible foreign tax credit for overseas taxes borne on the same profits. Distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to a separate dividend withholding tax or additional taxation at 10% (or higher under the applicable timing rules of the Colombian dividend stack), not a passthrough of entity results. Taxable period is the Colombian fiscal year (January 1 - December 31).

Available election forms

Not applicable - Colombia does not recognize a check-the-box regime or any elective entity-classification rule for S.A.S.; the S.A.S. is irrevocably treated as a corporation. The only residual election for micro/small business is the 'Régimen Simple de Tributación' (SIMPLE) under Articles 903-941 E.T., but services classified under professional engineering, architectural, and BIM consulting activities are statutorily excluded from SIMPLE, so the S.A.S. cannot elect it. There are no statutory hybrid mismatch elections in Colombia: hybrid mismatch outcomes are governed by mandatory anti-abuse disallowance rules codified under the E.T. (BEPS Action 2 alignment), and a taxpayer cannot elect out of them. The S.A.S. may elect between generating financial statements in COP or a functional foreign currency for commercial purposes, but all Colombian tax filings must be expressed in COP using the certified exchange rate at the tax-basis date. No other material tax classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold applies to engineering/BIM service providers: an S.A.S. rendering CAD/BIM services is a legal VAT responsible person (responsable de IVA) from its first taxable transaction, regardless of revenue. A narrowly scoped exemption exists under the 'no responsables' category for minor retail goods sellers with gross annual revenues not exceeding 4,000 UVT (approximately COP 190,400,000 in 2024); professional technical services are always excluded from this exemption. Since the target scope consists of offshore engineering and drafting services, the S.A.S. must file the registration and periodic VAT returns even if all sales are zero-rated exports. Total VAT returns are filed electronically on a bimonthly or four-monthly basis depending on gross turnover (two-month periods for most S.A.S. taxpayers).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of services may be invoiced at 0% IVA (zero-rated with full right to claim input VAT credits and refunds) if the cumulative conditions of Articles 481 numeral 3 and 640 E.T. and Decree 2150 of 2017 are met: (i) the seller is a person or entity domiciled in Colombia (the S.A.S. with Colombian RUT); (ii) the buyer or service recipient is a person or entity without domicile or permanent establishment in Colombia; (iii) the service is effectively used or exploited exclusively abroad; and (iv) the export is supported by the required documentary chain. For offshore BIM/CAD drafting services, DIAN accepts the contract, the electronic invoice, the technical delivery/acceptance record, and the foreign remittance certificate; the governing principle is that the beneficiary of the work and the exclusive use of the drafting output are located outside Colombia. If the Colombian entity merely acts as a subcontractor for another Colombian intermediary, zero-rating fails for lack of direct export. The zero-rated treatment is not an exemption but a 0% taxed operation; therefore, the S.A.S. may claim a refund of the VAT borne on domestic inputs (e.g., software licenses, equipment, local engineering support, rent, utilities) in the same periodic VAT return, subject to DIAN's ex-post documentary audit.

  • Service Export Declaration ('Declaración de Exportación de Servicios') electronically filed with DIAN through MUISCA, using the specific export-of-services form; alternatively the export must be evidenced in the RUT profile and declared in the periodic IVA return as export transactions.
  • Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV) with 0% export code, CUFE hash, QR stamp, the non-resident client's tax identification, and an underlying written contract or confirmed purchase order specifying the offshore infrastructure/CAD-BIM scope and the foreign location of use.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas) issued by a Colombian authorized foreign-exchange intermediary (e.g., a bank) evidencing that the export proceeds were actually received in Colombia in freely convertible foreign currency within the legal deadline; a signed electronic delivery/acceptance record from the foreign client should be attached for substance substantiation.

Mandatory in full. As of 2024-2025, all VAT responsible persons must issue the Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV) using a DIAN-authorized means (own software, a DIAN-approved technology provider, or DIAN's free validation front-end). The invoice must be transmitted in real time at issuance through DIAN's Validación Previa clearance API (SOAP/JSON endpoints mutually authenticated with the taxpayer's X.509 digital certificate). DIAN replies synchronously with an acceptance/validation code before the invoice is legally delivered to the customer. Each validated invoice incorporates a CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica), a deterministic hash of the invoice data, and a mandatory QR code for digital stamping. The invoice XML must be retained and stored by the taxpayer for at least 5 years. Zero-rated export invoices must include the corresponding exportation cause code, the client's non-resident tax ID, and the DIAN service-export registration. Additionally, for cross-border service exports, the S.A.S. is required to file, when applicable, the Información Exógena (annual exogenous information return) listing export transactions and to transmit the currency-repatriation data through Banco de la República's systems.

DIAN applies a 5-year statute of limitations for assessing VAT differences; this period extends to 10 years in cases involving suppressed transactions or fraudulent documents. If the zero-rating or the foreign remittance requirement is not substantiated, DIAN retrospectively re-categorizes the operation as a domestic taxable service, assesses 19% VAT on the invoiced amount plus moratory interest (interés de mora) from the invoice date, and adds the 'sanción por inexactitud' under Article 647 E.T. equal to 100% of the underpaid tax (200% where the inaccuracy is caused by fraud or by invoicing through artificial structures). A VAT refund obtained on the strength of false export documentation is subject to clawback plus a 20% per-year adjusted interest charge and possible disqualification from refund procedures for future periods. Criminal exposure arises under Article 402 of the Colombian Criminal Code ('Violación de impuestos' / tax fraud), with penalties of 4 to 9 years of imprisonment, and under foreign-exchange regulations, failure to repatriate the export proceeds within 180 days triggers a Banco de la República 'sanción cambiaria' that may reach 100% of the non-repatriated amount and the reversal of the zero-rating from the date of shipment. Repeated or organized fake-export operations may additionally lead to suspension of the exporter's status in DIAN and the denial of further export invoice validation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia permits general convertibility for current-account transactions, including trade in engineering consultancy, technical services, and software-related royalties, provided the operation is channeled through an authorized local intermediary and properly declared under the Foreign Exchange Regime (Régimen Cambiario) established by Decree 1735 of 1993 and External Resolution of the Banco de la República. Capital-account operations, including foreign direct investment in an S.A.S., are legally open to non-resident investors but require registration with Banco de la República via the foreign-investment information system; withholding taxes and capital-gains rules apply on outbound profit remittance and liquidation proceeds. There are no general exchange-rationing measures for bona fide corporate transactions, but documentary proof of the underlying contract, invoicing, and tax standing is mandatory at the banking and regulatory level. For offshore engineering outsourcing companies, the practical convertibility risk is low if the treasury is routed through tier-1 supervised banks and every inbound/outbound wire is matched to a registered service export.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of goods and services in Colombia are subject to mandatory 'canalización' (channeling) of the resulting foreign-currency receivables through the foreign-exchange market. The statutory baseline requires goods exporters to bring the collection into an authorized intermediary within six months from the shipment date and service exporters to do so within four months from the date on which the invoice accrues, unless a specific external-resolution extension applies. The rule does not mandate a fixed ratio of conversion into Colombian pesos; the exporter satisfies the channeling obligation by selling the foreign currency to an authorized intermediary or depositing it in a foreign-currency checking/savings account (cuenta de compensación) maintained with such intermediary. Compliance is evidenced through the exchange declaration form (Declaración de Cambio), with non-compliance subject to monetary fines and sanctions imposed by Banco de la República and DIAN. For offshore BIM/CAD services, the four-month channeling window is typically favorable and can be observed through monthly mobilization of client receivables into the S.A.S.'s local account.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit (Ministerio de Hacienda y Crédito Público) and the Financial Superintendence of Colombia (Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia) for the supervision of authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries. Foreign-exchange-registration duties are administered through the Banco de la República's exchange system and the DIAN for customs-related export verification.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for ancillary treasury rail, LOW for full replacement of a supervised Colombian banking account. Colombian Law 1735 of 2014 created Specialized Electronic Deposit and Payment Entities (SEDPEs), which are Fintech-supervised companies permitted to hold transaction accounts for electronic payments; however, they are not licensed as full commercial banks and are not an authorized channel for foreign-exchange declarations to Banco de la República. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, or similar) are legally viable only for small-value non-operational flows, temporary liquidity parking, or vendor disbursements outside Colombia. They are constrained by weak function for Colombian payroll, tax retention, and FX compliance, and by the risk of bank account termination if large invoices are paid through an unregulated EMI instead of a local authorized intermediary. Corporate treasury should therefore use one tier-1 local account as the primary liquidity anchor and Wise or a similar EMI only as a last-mile disbursement rail for non-Colombian contractors.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for foreign direct investment into the Colombian S.A.S. must be built on a direct SWIFT transfer from the parent company's bank to the S.A.S.'s Colombian operational account. The wire should use MT103 format with the payment message indicating 'FDI equity contribution' and referencing the underlying shareholder meeting resolution. Upon receipt, the authorized local bank (or the company's external agent) must register the operation with Banco de la República under the international investments declaration (Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales), using the corresponding foreign-investment purpose code. For USD-denominated incoming funds, the bank will typically execute a spot sale against COP at the authorized market rate, unless the S.A.S. maintains a foreign-currency compensating account. The treasury should prepare the bank with the following documentation: certificate of incorporation and bylaws, board/shareholder resolution, legal representative identification, notarized capital contribution minutes, and the final UBO certificate. If the parent transfers through an intermediary bank, the message must be transparent and contain the beneficiary's Colombian tax ID (NIT) and account number to avoid blocking. Inbound funds should not be split into multiple wires under small amounts to avoid artificially fragmented transaction profiling; a single identified wire with full supporting documentation is more defensible under AML/KYC review.Outbound trade disbursements for the engineering outsourcing scope should be routed predominantly through SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments and employee contractor remittances, and through commercial documentary letters of credit for large-value equipment acquisitions or commissioned hardware purchases. The S.A.S. should attend to the following clearing rules: (i) all foreign-currency payments require the exchange declaration (Declaración de Cambio) where the payment involves a cross-border transfer; (ii) payments for imported technology or subcontracting services must be linked to invoices and contracts that demonstrate the import of services from the income-tax point of view; (iii) outbound transfers to related parties must be supported with transfer-pricing documentation if they exceed the applicable local threshold; (iv) bank reconciliation of each SWIFT transfer should reference the underlying invoice number and the DIAN 'exportación de servicios' classification where applicable. Direct backup settlements to foreign contractors may be transmitted via the local bank's FX desk at the reference market rate, or via the S.A.S.'s foreign-currency account if the counterparty requires payment in USD or EUR. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for ancillary treasury rail, LOW for full replacement of a supervised Colombian banking account. Colombian Law 1735 of 2014 created Specialized Electronic Deposit and Payment Entities (SEDPEs), which are Fintech-supervised companies permitted to hold transaction accounts for electronic payments; however, they are not licensed as full commercial banks and are not an authorized channel for foreign-exchange declarations to Banco de la República. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, or similar) are legally viable only for small-value non-operational flows, temporary liquidity parking, or vendor disbursements outside Colombia. They are constrained by weak function for Colombian payroll, tax retention, and FX compliance, and by the risk of bank account termination if large invoices are paid through an unregulated EMI instead of a local authorized intermediary. Corporate treasury should therefore use one tier-1 local account as the primary liquidity anchor and Wise or a similar EMI only as a last-mile disbursement rail for non-Colombian contractors.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Colombian Local File and Form 120 transfer pricing return are triggered when the S.A.S. has gross assets or gross income exceeding 100,000 UVT (COP 4,706 million for tax year 2024) and executes controlled transactions. A Master File is additionally required when the multinational group's consolidated annual gross income exceeds 500,000 UVT (COP 23,532 million for 2024). The Country-by-Country report is due for group revenue in excess of EUR 750 million equivalent. Smaller companies may nonetheless need to submit Limited or Industry Specific documentation if DIAN issues a general documentation order.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code analogous to offshore financial centers. However, DIAN's anti-avoidance doctrine, reinforced by Article 260-2 (general anti-abuse rule), the Limitation on Benefits clauses in Colombian DTAs, and the OECD BEPS multi-lateral instrument, requires a Colombian S.A.S. to maintain genuine employees, office or cloud infrastructure, management decisions in Colombia, and risk-bearing capability. A CAD/BIM outsourcing entity that is a mere invoice-passer with no real local engineering activity will be ignored; income will be attributed to the IT service provider in the place of performance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty for inaccurate or fraudulent return under Article 648 of the Colombian Tax Code: 20% of the omitted tax, increased to 200% where fraud or intentional misrepresentation is proven. Interest accrues from the original due date. Criminal prosecution under Article 297 of the Criminal Code imposes imprisonment of 4 to 9 years.

Director personal liability: The S.A.S. legal veil may be pierced where the return is used as a sham; statutory representative and signing directors are jointly liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. DIAN may request a travel ban and preliminary asset confiscation during criminal proceedings.
Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: DIAN will disregard the intermediating conduit and reallocate the taxable income to the Colombian S.A.S.; the full 35% rate applies on the reallocated income plus a 200% inaccuracy penalty. The intermediary may be deemed a sham, and any activities are treated as performed by the Colombian entity.

Director personal liability: Directors and majority shareholders who set up the conduit may be personally liable for resulting taxes. Colombian courts have held that abuse of the corporate form against the treasury triggers personal civil liability; criminal conspiracy charges are possible for deliberate tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Failure to provide transfer pricing documentation gives DIAN the right to make a primary adjustment based on its own benchmark; a sanction of 2% of the related-party transaction value may be imposed, up to 20,000 UVT. An incomplete or unusable Local File is equivalent to no documentation.

Director personal liability: Officers who approve transfer pricing calculations without reasonable review can be personally liable under the negative certificate of due diligence; in serious cases, criminal liability for fraudulent falsehood in a private deed may attach.
Dividend Withholding Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The S.A.S. is personally liable for the entire unrecovered withholding (Article 370 of the Tax Code), plus a 1% monthly fine up to 200% and late-interest charges. DIAN can deny the deduction of any related distribution expense and trigger an audit of all dividend distributions for the prior 5 years.

Director personal liability: The legal representative who authorizes payments without withholding is jointly liable for the unpaid withholding; the veil will be lifted for fraudulent non-collection. Travel restrictions may be ordered to secure the economic rights of the Treasury.
Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is criminalized under Article 323 of the Penal Code, carrying imprisonment of 5 to 15 years and fines of 100 to 66,700 SVT or UVT equivalents. The Financial Information and Analysis Unit (UIAF) can freeze accounts; DIAN can order asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve irregular cash flows become co-conspirators; provisional detention and travel bans are standard. The civil veil of the S.A.S. is disregarded, exposing directors' personal assets to restitution claims and civil damages.
Economic Substance Failure / Artificial Transaction

Statutory sanction: Under the anti-abuse rule and judicial precedent, the artificial contract is recharacterized as a capitalized dividend or hidden equity contribution; the S.A.S. loses the deduction and the foreign related enterprise is assessed with deemed Colombian-source income. Punitive interest and a 200% inaccuracy penalty follow.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign artificial contracts disregard corporate law duties of loyalty and prudence; they may be sued for the company's total tax deficiency, declared jointly liable, and a travel restraint order may be issued pending audit resolution.

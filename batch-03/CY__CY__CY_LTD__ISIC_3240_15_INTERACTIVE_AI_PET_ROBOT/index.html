Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus (official portal: www.mof.gov.cy/tax); VAT registration and filings via ARIADNE electronic system (ariadne.mof.gov.cy).

E-government filing portal

Cyprus-registered private limited companies receive their Tax Identification Code (TIC) automatically through Registrar of Companies enrolment with the Tax Department. Non-resident entities with taxable activity in Cyprus (branch/permanent establishment or direct supplies) apply to the District Tax Office via Form TIC FE-01 or ARIADNE, submitting proof of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered agent/office details, beneficial-ownership data and projected activity; TIC issuance generally completes within 15 business days. VAT registration is effected through ARIADNE VAT Form R1; local suppliers must register before the EUR 15,600 annual threshold is exceeded, while non-established distance sellers must register from the first euro or use the OSS/IOSS channels.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation / Quasi-Territorial Scope. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Quasi-Territorial Scope. A CY Ltd is a separate taxable person: corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide profits; distributions are exempt from further Cypriot taxation; foreign dividend and foreign branch profits are exempt from entity-level tax under the participation regime, giving a territorial outcome for active offshore profits.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Cyprus. Available statutory elections include: (i) VAT grouping of related taxable persons; (ii) option-to-tax on immovable-property leasing; (iii) group loss relief (75% ownership); (iv) accelerated capital-allowance elections; (v) Notional Interest Deduction on new subscribed equity; (vi) application of deemed distribution rules; and (vii) anti-hybrid mismatch adjustments under EU ATAD Code, which disallow double-non-taxation mismatches rather than electing a beneficial transparent status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 of taxable supplies in any trailing 12-month period for Cyprus-established businesses; distance sales of goods by another EU supplier become taxable in Cyprus when they exceed EUR 10,000 per calendar year unless OSS is used; non-EU suppliers have no threshold and must VAT-register in Cyprus from their first taxable sale.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies under Article 67 of VAT Law 119(I)/2000 where (i) the Cyprus-based supplier is a taxable person, (ii) the interactive AI pet robot is dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU within 90 days of supply, (iii) the export is covered by an accepted customs export declaration, and (iv) the supplier holds prescribed documentary evidence. Ancillary freight, insurance and handling services are zero-rated when linked to the exported goods; intra-EU supplies of robots to a registered business are zero-rated subject to a valid VIES VAT identification number.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/Export Accompanying Document accepted by Cyprus Customs)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note as applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit confirmation of export proceeds in EUR)

Cyprus does not operate a real-time B2B fiscal clearance or digital-stamping regime. E-invoicing is mandatory only for invoices issued to Cypriot public-sector contracting authorities (PEPPOL/EN16931 under EU Directive 2014/55/EU). Taxpayers must retain authenticated invoices with integrity of origin and content; VAT returns are submitted electronically via ARIADNE (monthly/quarterly Forms 4/8). No API-based clearance engine is applicable to the interactive AI pet robot hardware export chain.

The Tax Department may reopen VAT assessment within six years (unlimited in fraud cases), reverse zero-rated inputs/outputs if the export is not proved, and issue VAT claw-back assessments with interest (statutory rate published by the Minister of Finance, currently approx. 8% p.a.). Civil penalties reach EUR 5,000 per incorrect return; deliberate tax evasion attracts additional assessment of up to 100% of VAT evaded, fines up to EUR 20,000, and imprisonment up to five years. AI-pet-robot exporters must retain the full customs/transport/payment file for at least five years to sustain zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a member of the European Union and the eurozone; the euro is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions without prior exchange control authorization. There are no general restrictions on inward or outward payments, profit remittances, dividend distributions, loan repayments, or repatriation of liquidation proceeds. Capital movements within the EU are unrestricted under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, while transactions with third countries are likewise unconstrained except for temporary safeguards permissible under EU law in exceptional circumstances. Remittance of profits may be executable without exchange control licensing; however, practical statutory constraints arise under the Cyprus Companies Law (easily distributable profits test), tax clearance requirements for bank debiting, withholding tax obligations on dividends (17% default rate, potentially reduced under an applicable double tax treaty), and anti-money laundering verification duties imposed on Cypriot credit institutions. The Central Bank of Cyprus exercises monitoring and statistical reporting functions over foreign transactions, but does not impose convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation under Cypriot exchange control law to surrender export proceeds to the Central Bank or any authorized dealer, and no mandatory conversion of foreign currency receipts to euro. Exporters may retain balances in foreign currencies, maintain multicurrency accounts, or convert receipts at freely negotiated market rates. Nevertheless, banks operating in Cyprus apply internal currency settlement procedures and may require supporting export documentation (commercial invoice, bill of lading, customs declarations) to credit proceeds with the correct balance-of-payments statistical codes. Recipients must ensure that the inbound transfer includes accurate purpose codes (e.g., 'Exports of goods' under the ECB Balance of Payments framework) and that settlements are reported via the bank's statistical reporting to the Central Bank of Cyprus for compilation of the balance of payments.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions operating under EU passported licenses (e.g., UK FCA or Malta MFSA EMIs). Such institutions may provide quicker onboarding, virtual IBANs, and cross-border payment routing, but they generally cannot replace a full-service Cypriot banking relationship for purposes requiring Central Bank of Cyprus supervision, trade finance instruments (letters of credit), or debt/equity capital raising using traditional escrow structures. Legal constraints: an EMI passporting into Cyprus must comply with the Payment Services Law (L. 86(I)/2018), be registered with the Central Bank of Cyprus (Passport Notification), and may operate as agents/processors without obtaining a Cypriot license. For treasury operations involving large inbound capital injections or trade finance transactions, mainstream commercial banks remain the primary compliant path; EMIs are supplementary rails for low-value operational flows and client-facing payment adjacency (Stripe, card acquiring).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound wire transfers for share capital subscription, shareholder loans, or intercompany deposits must be routed via SWIFT MT103 messages using the correct balance-of-payments purpose codes under the ECB's BPM6 framework. For equity injections, the recommended purpose code is 'Equity acquisition and shares issued by the reporting enterprise' (line 5.1.1) or 'Capital in cash and deposits' for supplementary injections. Loan financing should use purpose code 7.1 (trade credits) or 7.2 (other investments). The sending bank must include the Cyprus company's name and IBAN, the investor's legal name, and the reference 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — ARTICLE OF ASSOCIATION' to reduce AML friction. The Cypriot beneficiary bank will require a supporting board resolution approving the issuance of shares, a statutory shareholders' register extract, and, if requested, a copy of the executed share subscription agreement. All funds must be received in the company's fully verified operational or capital escrow account. Amounts exceeding €10,000 have automatic CBC threshold reporting, and bank compliance teams may require documentary confirmation that the injection aligns with the company's object and the ISIC 3240 business scope.Trade settlements for inbound components (bionic actuators, speech synthesizers, and AI microprocessors) and outbound finished goods (interactive AI pet robots) shall be cleared via SWIFT MT103 (telegraphic transfer) for open account transactions up to a commercial threshold (≤ €50,000 per transaction, or when the counterparty is a long-standing supplier), and via documentary letters of credit (LCs) for larger or first-time trade relationships. For robust control, Cypriot commercial banks are able to issue irrevocable documentary LCs, stand-by LCs, and documentary collections through the SWIFT/MT700 series. All outward payments must include the appropriate EBA purpose code, such as 'Trade credit and advances' (7.1) for supplier pre-payments or 'Exports / Imports of goods and services' (2.1/2.2) for full settlement. The bank will require commercial invoice, purchase order, goods description, country-of-origin certificates, and, where applicable, customs declarations for B2B transactions. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (uniform corporate income tax rate on all Cyprus-tax-resident companies)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% under domestic Cyprus law. Cyprus does not impose withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, including non-resident individuals and companies. For Cyprus tax-resident individual shareholders, a Special Defence Contribution (SDC) of 17% applies to dividend distributions; Cyprus-resident corporate shareholders are exempt under the participation exemption.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 15 months from the end of the tax year. For a calendar-year company, the return for the year ending 31 December 2024 must be filed by 31 March 2026.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate value of controlled transactions per category (goods, services, intangibles, financing, other) in a tax year triggers the mandatory Local File under the Cyprus Transfer Pricing Decree (Regulatory Administrative Act 256/2022). A Master File must be prepared if the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000 and the Cyprus entity's controlled transactions exceed EUR 750,000. The Cyprus entity must also file a summary of related-party transactions (Form TPC-01) with the Tax Department whenever the annual value of controlled transactions exceeds the EUR 750,000 threshold.

Economic substance scope: The Cyprus Economic Substance Doctrine applies to all companies claiming tax residence in Cyprus. A company assembling interactive AI pet robots must demonstrate real, substantive operations in Cyprus: physical registered office, locally managed board meetings, a sufficient number of resident employees with technical and administrative skills, and a clearly identifiable principal source of revenue from value-adding activities performed in Cyprus. Inbound back-to-back IP licensing without active management in Cyprus is presumed unacceptable. Failure to satisfy the substance requirements results in denial of a Cyprus Tax Residence Certificate, loss of DTA benefits, and reallocation of profits to the jurisdiction of actual management.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 122 of the Cyprus Income Tax Law: a fine of up to EUR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to five years. The Commissioner may issue a best-of-judgment assessment with a penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax, plus statutory interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed or caused the filing of a false return are personally and severally liable for the tax due. Courts may issue travel bans (Departure Prohibition Orders) and freezing orders on personal assets pending prosecution; upon conviction, imprisonment for up to five years and court-ordered restitution of the evaded tax with interest.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: The Tax Commissioner re-determines taxable profits at the arm's length price, imposes a penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax, plus interest at 3.5% per annum. Alternatively, administrative fines up to EUR 20,000 for non-submission of documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors are statutory officers for tax record-keeping; failure to ensure TP documentation is prepared exposes them to personal fines, contempt-of-court proceedings, and, in cases of deliberate non-disclosure, criminal sanctions including imprisonment. In serious audit cases, departure orders have been issued against non-resident directors to compel compliance.
Profit Stripping and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the excessive interest or royalty deduction; recharacterisation of the payment as a deemed in-kind distribution (dividend) which, when paid to a non-resident, is still exempt, but when paid to a Cyprus tax resident may trigger SDC of 17%. The additional tax is due with a penalty of 100% if it arises from negligence, plus statutory interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the related-party financing without an arm's-length benchmark are jointly liable for the additional CIT and SDC. Where a distribution is deemed to have been made, unpaid SDC is a personal debt of the directors who caused the default; travel bans can be imposed until the debt is settled.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of the Cyprus Tax Residence Certificate; reclassification of the company as a foreign resident or as a 'transparent vehicle' subject to a 25% effective tax rate under the Cyprus Controlled Foreign Company regime. The company may also be struck from the Companies Registry for failing to maintain a registered office, and the Tax Department may issue a final assessed amount equal to 12.5% of all worldwide income.

Director personal liability: Directors (including foreign nominees) may be criminally liable for presenting false statements to the Tax Department. Civil remedies include piercing of the corporate veil, restitution of taxes from the directors' personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to five years for fraud-based offences. In cross-border cases, a personal liability assessment can be enforced abroad under mutual assistance provisions.

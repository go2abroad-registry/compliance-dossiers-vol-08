Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance; official taxpayer e-portal 'ARIADNE' (https://ariadne.mof.gov.cy) and auxiliary portal 'Tax For All' (https://taxforAll.mof.gov.cy); VAT administration is performed by the VAT Service of the same Tax Department; customs matters are administered separately by Cyprus Customs (Τμήμα Τελωνείων), Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

(2) File the corporate income tax registration form (TIN 101) with the Tax Department through the registered auditor; the Tax Department issues a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN) formatted as 8 digits plus a check letter (e.g., 12345678A), which also serves as the VAT registration base number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (opaque corporation). A Cyprus Private Limited Company is a legal person taxable as a separate entity under the Income Tax Law N.118(I)/2002; it is Cyprus tax resident if managed and controlled in Cyprus. Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income at a 12.5% uniform rate, with major territorial exemptions: 90% participation exemption on dividends, exemption on profits from disposal of securities, exemption for foreign permanent establishments meeting statutory conditions (foreign tax rate not below 12.5% or passive income below 20%), and non-taxation of capital gains except on immovable property in Cyprus. Non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. Pass-through treatment is not available to a Cyprus Ltd; total income is computed on the basis of the audited IFRS financial statements adjusted by the tax law.

Available election forms

Cyprus has no US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election; classification is fixed by the legal form under the Companies Law. Available statutory elections and reliefs include: (i) group loss relief election between Cyprus-resident group companies (s.24 Income Tax Law); (ii) VAT group registration election for taxpayers established in Cyprus with close financial, economic and organisational links; (iii) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital injected after 1 January 2015, provided the funds are used in the business (anti-hybrid safe harbours apply); (iv) IP box (Nexus regime) election yielding an effective 2.5% tax rate on qualifying intellectual property profits using the OECD modified-nexus ratio; (v) election for the foreign permanent establishment exemption or, instead, for taxation with foreign tax credit relief where the foreign rate is lower; (vi) elections under EU ATAD hybrid-mismatch legislation (L.115(I)/2019) to neutralize deductions where the same loss or expense is also deducted in another state or where there is double non-taxation; (vii) notional interest and other formal elections are filed with the annual VAT/income tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 of annual taxable turnover of taxable supplies of goods and services in Cyprus during the preceding 12 months (Art. 16 VAT Law); compulsory registration applies once this threshold is exceeded, while voluntary registration for exporters is permitted to recover input VAT; non-established/non-resident suppliers have no threshold and must register for any taxable supply in Cyprus; intra-Community distance-selling threshold to Cyprus consumers is EUR 10,000 (with optional OSS one-stop-shop registration); imported goods of any value trigger VAT at the customs frontier unless an onward-release or customs-warehouse regime applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to: (i) exports of goods physically dispatched or transported by the Cyprus supplier or on its behalf from Cyprus to a destination outside the EU (Art. 64 VAT Law), including supplies to hospitals, distributors and customs-bonded facilities in third countries; (ii) intra-Community supplies to a taxable customer with a valid EU VAT number verified in VIES, provided EC Sales List and Intrastat requirements are met; (iii) services directly connected to the exported goods (handling, transport, insurance and freight) when the supplier retains the customs export declaration; and (iv) qualifying offshore transactions. For Class III coronary drug-eluting stent export (HS 9021 / CN 9021.39), zero-rating requires actual removal from Cyprus within the statutory deadlines, a complete export evidence chain (customs SAD, proof of exit from the EU, bill of lading/airway bill, freight notes, payment instruments), and conformity documentation (CE Certificate, EC Declaration of Conformity, MDR Class III technical file) to secure customs release; failure to prove exportation converts the supply into a taxable domestic supply at 19%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD / ECS electronic export declaration) accepted by Cyprus Customs with the MRN
  • Outbound transport document: Bill of Lading (sea freight), Air Waybill (air freight), or CMR letter (road haulage) clearly evidencing dispatch to a third-country destination
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed Packing List with full HS 9021 classification, product description, batch/lot numbers, quantity, unit price and incoterm
  • Proof of exit of the goods from the EU (customs cancellation/arrival notification through the EU movement system or a certificate from the customs office of exit)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advice showing receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds from the overseas buyer
  • Certificate of Origin (Form A / EUR.1 / ATR or origin declaration under preference agreements, if preferential duty rates are claimed)
  • EC Sales List / VIES proof and Intrastat supplementary declaration for intra-Community supply where the goods are shipped to an EU customer
  • Free Sale Certificate, CE Certificate of Conformity and EU Authorised Representative attestation under EU Regulation (EU) 2017/745 (MDR) for Class III medical stents
  • Destination-country import/health permits (e.g., FDA import licence, TGA/Health Canada import approval, or destination customs registration) where relevant to demonstrate lawful transaction
  • Insurance certificate, phytosanitary/sterilisation certificates, and dangerous-goods declaration if the stent transport involves specialised logistics

As of tax year 2025 Cyprus does not mandate a national real-time fiscal clearance engine (no continuous transaction control such as Italy's SDI or Spain's Verifactu); electronic submission of VAT returns, EC Sales Listings, Intrastat declarations and annual recapitulative statements is mandatory through the ARIADNE online portal; B2G e-invoicing is mandatory for invoices to the Cyprus public sector under EU Directive 2014/55/EU via PEPPOL-BIS and EN 16931 formats; B2B electronic invoicing is voluntary and must comply with the authenticity, integrity and legibility requirements of Articles 138-139 of the VAT Law; the Cyprus Tax Department is preparing for the future EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) e-reporting/e-invoicing framework, expected to introduce transaction-level digital reporting in the EU from approximately 2030; medical-device supply chains should already issue structured UBL/XML invoices to ease cross-border clearance and ERP reconciliation.

Cyprus Tax Department and Cyprus Customs may conduct joint documentary audits of zero-rated exporters; the taxpayer must retain all export, transport and payment evidence for at least six years (Article 34 VAT Law record-keeping), and ten years is recommended for a medical-device Master File to survive product-liability and controlled-substance audits. Where export evidence is incomplete or false, the Tax Department retrospectively reassesses the supply at the standard 19% VAT and imposes interest at 1.0% per month from the due date of the original return until payment. For false or fraudulent zero-rating claims, civil penalties of up to EUR 10,000 per offence and an administrative penalty equal to the under-assessed VAT may be imposed, with criminal prosecution for tax evasion subject to imprisonment for a term up to three years and/or a fine up to EUR 10,000 on summary conviction; in serious or organised fraud cases, proceedings under the money-laundering and corruption statutes may apply with higher custodial sentences. Additionally, the Commissioner may deregister the VAT account, seek cross-border information exchange under EU Regulation 904/2010, extend the assessment window up to ten years in cases of fraud or gross negligence, and claw back input VAT previously recovered on goods that did not actually leave the EU customs territory.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

the euro is fully convertible in Cyprus; neither the CBC nor the Ministry of Finance imposes currency-exchange permits, surrender requirements, rationing, import-cover rules, or bilateral clearing for cross-border payments. Under Articles 63 to 66 TFEU and the EU Capital Movements Directive, current-account transactions and capital movements are liberalised as between EU Member States and between the EU and third countries, subject only to the EU sanctions regime, EU/UN anti-terrorism measures, financial sanctions, and CBC AML/CFT obligations. Profit remittance is unrestricted at the exchange-control level for a Cyprus Private Limited Company; dividends can be paid abroad without CBC approval. The practical hurdles are documentary: distributable profits must be evidenced by annual or interim financial statements, a board resolution declaring the dividend, up-to-date UBO declarations, sanctions/PRS screening of the beneficiary, and a payment purpose code consistent with the bank’s balance-of-payments reporting. For a manufacturing entity under ISIC 3250 (Class III drug-eluting stents), the remittance itself is not restrained by the Cyprus exchange-control framework, but an inbound-funding and outbound-dividend policy should be established at onboarding to avoid bank-side red flags.

Export proceeds surrender rules

none. Since the adoption of the euro on 1 January 2008, there is no Cypriot law, CBC directive, or EU regulation imposing export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion into EUR, or a minimum holding period for export receipts. An exporter may keep proceeds in EUR or in foreign currency on a multi-currency account at a licensed Cypriot credit institution. For ISIC 3250 exports, the treasury should nonetheless maintain EUR-based corporate-tax and VAT accounting using ECB reference rates; CBC balance-of-payments statistics require classification of the transaction as ‘export of goods’ but do not restrict conversion. The only practical requirement is accurate purpose/reason-for-payment information on inward MT103s so that CBC statistical data and the bank’s monitoring are consistent with the customs and commercial documents.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC) under the Central Bank of Cyprus Law 2002, operating within the European System of Central Banks as the micro-prudential supervisor of Cypriot credit institutions and the national competent authority for payment and electronic-money institutions. In respect of foreign exchange, the CBC no longer has an independent exchange-control bureau; Cyprus is a full member of the euro area and the CBC is obligated by EU law to apply the euro regime, free capital movement and ECB regulatory frameworks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary settlement and escrow; Medium for ancillary payment services. A Cyprus or EEA-licensed electronic-money institution can execute cross-border payments under the Payment Services Law (Law 141(I)/2018) implementing PSD2, and an EMII authorised in another EEA state can passport into Cyprus. However, a purely offshore (non-EEA) digital EMI has no passporting rights and would need a full Cyprus EMI licence or a Cyprus branch authorisation, which is impractical for this structure. Moreover, for a high-risk Class III MedTech manufacturing treasury, the required capital-escrow function, LC discounting, and trade settlement under UCP600 cannot be hosted by an EMI because EMIs are not banks, cannot issue guarantees indisputably, and are not accepted by confirming banks as settlement counterparties. The EMI can be used for low-value inbound collections, FX quote aggregation, or card acquiring, but all invoiced trade receipts and shareholder capital must flow through a licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The parent shareholder should effect the contribution as a same-currency EUR transfer by SWIFT MT103, where possible avoiding intermediary banks to reduce settlement risk and sanctions-screening delays. The beneficiary must be exactly ‘CY Ltd by reference to its Cyprus registered number HE77’ and the account must be the dedicated Capital Escrow Account at Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank. The MT103 Remittance Information field must state the purpose code for direct investment equity capital — e.g., ISO 20022 purpose ‘EQUI’ or the bank-specific BoP code for ‘Equity capital / direct investment’ — together with the phrase ‘Share capital contribution per board resolution dated [date]’ and the parent’s reference to the signed allotment. The CBC balance-of-payments return will be generated by the Cypriot bank from the MT103; therefore the sender should not use mixed codes such as ‘general goods’ or ‘service’ because that would distort the BoP and trigger AML queries. No central-bank approval is required, but the treasury must verify that the parent jurisdiction is not sanctioned or otherwise listed in the Cyprus PRS, and that the source-wealth bundle for the UBO is already available to the bank. Upon receipt, the capital should remain in the escrow/operational account and be recorded as issued capital in the Cyprus corporate register; intermingling with trade proceeds is acceptable only after the bank has approved the account’s purpose and the capital move has been book-kept internally.For settlements to foreign raw-material suppliers, clearing should occur by either (i) direct SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from the CY Ltd treasury account to the supplier’s bank, or (ii) a commercial letter of credit issued by the Cypriot bank under ICC UCP600 and communicated to the beneficiary’s bank by SWIFT MT700, with payment at sight or deferred payment executed via MT103/MT202 reimbursement. For outright MT103 payments, each instruction must reference the commercial invoice, order number, contract number and customs/HS code, and the purpose code should reflect ‘import of goods’ or ‘payment for goods’ under CBC/BOP reporting; the description of goods must align with the underlying customs declaration for the bioresorbable polymer stent components. Where the CY entity imports high-value materials (e.g., nitinol tubing, PTFE, absorbable polymer powder), the bank’s trade-finance desk should be engaged pre-shipment so that LC issuance terms, quarantine of funds, and compliance screening are agreed in advance. Open-account outbound wires can be used after the supplier has proven performance history, but the treasury should only release EUR after checking goods-pre-shipment inspection, transport documents and sanctions lists. For intra-group treasury sweeps to the parent, the company should use a separate purpose code for intercompany loans or current-account balances and maintain an intercompany reconciliation schedule to avoid a regulatory inference of the Cypriot entity being used as an invoicing house.Vanilla FX forward: EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, or EUR/CNH with a maximum tenor of 24 months, documented under an ISDA 2002/country FX master agreement; every forward must be backed by a demonstrable underlying export receivable or import payable from the stent manufacturing/purchase cycle.Non-deliverable forward (NDF): permissible for currencies without full deliverability in the international offshore market, such as CNY or certain emerging-market currencies, where the CY entity has an underlying invoice/LC exposure and settlement is netted in EUR through the Cypriot bank’s offshore desk.Cross-currency swap: allowed for EUR vs USD funding or balance-sheet hedging where treasury has long-term USD revenue or hard-currency supplier contracts; ICC/EMIR validation, collateralisation, or original-margin requirements may apply and must be assessed by the bank before execution.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low for primary settlement and escrow; Medium for ancillary payment services. A Cyprus or EEA-licensed electronic-money institution can execute cross-border payments under the Payment Services Law (Law 141(I)/2018) implementing PSD2, and an EMII authorised in another EEA state can passport into Cyprus. However, a purely offshore (non-EEA) digital EMI has no passporting rights and would need a full Cyprus EMI licence or a Cyprus branch authorisation, which is impractical for this structure. Moreover, for a high-risk Class III MedTech manufacturing treasury, the required capital-escrow function, LC discounting, and trade settlement under UCP600 cannot be hosted by an EMI because EMIs are not banks, cannot issue guarantees indisputably, and are not accepted by confirming banks as settlement counterparties. The EMI can be used for low-value inbound collections, FX quote aggregation, or card acquiring, but all invoiced trade receipts and shareholder capital must flow through a licensed bank.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Unidad Administrativa Especial Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (UAE DIAN); official portal: www.dian.gov.co; electronic services through the MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) and SIIC (Sistema de Información de Impuestos y Contribuyentes).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned S.A.S.: (1) execute the S.A.S. incorporation deed before a Colombian notary and register the company with the local Cámara de Comercio; (2) appoint an individual legal representative resident in Colombia; (3) complete the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) application through the DIAN portal (Form 002) using the certificate of existence from the Cámara de Comercio and the legal representative's identity documents; (4) DIAN issues the Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT) as the universal tax identifier of the S.A.S.; (5) obtain the RUT updated for VAT responsible status, electronic invoicing authorization, customs authorizations and exporter registry before commencing operations; (6) a non-resident foreign entity without a local establishment must also obtain a provisional NIT for Colombian-source tax purposes and for any customs declarations filed by the parcel hub.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – A Colombian S.A.S. is a distinct legal person subject to corporate income tax (CIT) on worldwide income at a statutory rate of 35% for fiscal year 2024 and 2025 (Law 2277 of 2022). No pass-through regime is available; the S.A.S. is a tax resident under Colombian law by reason of incorporation and place of effective management. The foreign shareholder is resident outside Colombia and is taxed only on Colombian-source income, including dividend withholding at up to 20% subject to applicable tax treaty relief. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – A Colombian S.A.S. is a distinct legal person subject to corporate income tax (CIT) on worldwide income at a statutory rate of 35% for fiscal year 2024 and 2025 (Law 2277 of 2022). No pass-through regime is available; the S.A.S. is a tax resident under Colombian law by reason of incorporation and place of effective management. The foreign shareholder is resident outside Colombia and is taxed only on Colombian-source income, including dividend withholding at up to 20% subject to applicable tax treaty relief. The activity of the parcel consolidation hub (ISIC 5229) is not located in a free trade zone and therefore has no special territorial tax exemption, although the export-related services are subject to VAT zero-rating rather than exemption without credit.

Available election forms

Colombia does not grant a US-style check-the-box election. The available statutory elections for the S.A.S. include: (a) an annual option to be taxed under the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) for small businesses where the prior-year gross ordinary income does not exceed the indexed threshold (approximately 3,500 UVT, about COP 164.7 million in 2024), which is generally not available or advisable for a parcel consolidation hub with cross-border foreign receipts; (b) an election to file consolidated income tax returns for a controlling group structure (shareholding of at least 50% directly or indirectly) under Article 29 of Law 1819 of 2016; (c) an election to apply the applicable double taxation treaty benefits by filing the relevant certificate of tax residence (CERT) with DIAN and the withholding agent; and (d) an election under foreign exchange rules to use the foreign currency proceeds repatriation system through authorized intermediaries (intermediarios del mercado cambiario) rather than a formal authorization regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis economic threshold applies to a legal entity: the S.A.S. is mandatorily a VAT responsible party (responsable de IVA) from its first taxable operation, regardless of turnover. The exemption from VAT registration applies only to natural persons whose gross income and gross assets do not exceed the statutory limits (approximately 3,500 UVT) and who are not importers or exporters. Since the parcel consolidation hub is an exporter and provider of cross-border logistics services, the S.A.S. must register as an exporter VAT responsible party in the RUT as 'Régimen Exportador' with no turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The parcel consolidation hub may zero-rate (gravamen cero) the following under Article 481 E.T. and Decree 1625 of 2016: (1) the physical export of goods (consolidated parcels that are cleared through Colombian customs and leave the country, title passing to a foreign buyer or to the foreign consignee); and (2) the rendering of export services, provided that the service is performed for a foreign recipient without domicile in Colombia, the service is used exclusively outside Colombia or is directly related to international transport of goods, the consideration is paid from outside Colombia and does not constitute Colombian-source income, and the exporter has the RUT exporter status and keeps the supporting documents. For direct-injection linehaul from the consolidation hub to a foreign gateway or destination, the international carriage segment qualifies as zero-rated export service only if invoiced to the foreign e-commerce marketplace or foreign freight forwarder and the transportation begins in Colombia and ends abroad. Domestic parcel collection, warehousing and last-mile preparatory activities remain subject to the standard 19% VAT unless separately qualified and invoiced as an integrated export service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX) transmitted electronically through MUISCA; for low-value e-commerce parcels, the Declaración de Consolidación (DC) or the new 'Exportaciones Express' procedure when applicable
  • Signed commercial invoice and detailed packing list in the name of the Colombian exporter (the S.A.S.)
  • Transport document: Air Waybill (AWB) or Bill of Lading (B/L) evidencing direct-injection from Colombia to the foreign destination, endorsed to the foreign consignee
  • Proof of effective exit of the goods: either the DIAN exit confirmation (salida de mercancías) or the mover's/freight forwarder's certificate of arrival at the foreign gateway
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (certificado de reintegro de divisas) or bank statement showing the receipt of foreign currency from the foreign buyer/forwarder through a Colombian authorized intermediary
  • Certificate of the foreign recipient's non-resident status (foreign tax registration or certificate of tax residence) for services zero-rating

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) applies under DIAN Resolutions 000042/2020 and 000165/2023. All generated invoices, export invoices and credit or debit notes must be transmitted to DIAN through authorized web services (API or the free DIAN software) before delivery to the client. Each electronic invoice must bear a valid CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) or CUDS for supporting documents, together with the DIAN QR code and the electronic signature of the issuer. Under the current pre-clearance (validación previa) protocol, DIAN performs an automatic systemic validation of the invoice, the exporter's RUT, and the customs status before accepting the document; any deviation rejects the invoice and blocks the export. The parcel hub must also use the Electronic Consolidation Manifest (Declaración de Consolidación) transmitted via MUISCA for reduced-cost small-parcel exports.

DIAN cross-matches the electronic invoices, the DEX and the foreign exchange reintegration records in the Sistema de Control de Cambios. Any zero-rated VAT refund derived from fictitious exports, false invoices, or failure to repatriate foreign exchange within the legal term triggers: (a) a penalty equal to 200% of the unduly obtained refund or balance in favor (Article 670 E.T.) plus default interest (interest corriente); (b) the compulsory clawback of the VAT credit previously returned, with retrospective recomputation of the VAT payable for the relevant period; (c) application of the 'inexactitud' penalty if the refund arises from false information (Article 647 E.T.); (d) customs fines between 100 and 10,000 UVT and suspension or cancellation of the exporter authorization (Decree 1165 of 2019); and (e) criminal liability under Article 440 of the Colombian Criminal Code for illegal refund of taxes, punishable by imprisonment of 48 to 108 months and fines. The responsible corporate officer and the current legal representative are jointly and severally liable for the clawback and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a predominantly free exchange market for both current and capital account transactions, but all foreign exchange operations must be handled through authorized intermediaries (licensed commercial banks or exchange brokers). Capital account operations, including foreign direct investment in a SAS, must be registered with Banco de la República through the sociological Form of Foreign Investment Registration and the relevant Declaración de Cambio. Profit remittances in the form of dividends are freely convertible where the foreign investment has been duly registered and the SAS has distributable retained earnings per audited financial statements. Repatriation of capital gains and partial liquidation of investment is permitted after the applicable capital reimbursement registration. The principal procedural hurdles are documentary: the investor must file the mandatory exchange declaration, retain the Certificate of Foreign Investment Registration, and ensure that all inbound and outbound flows traverse the authorized foreign exchange market. There is no exchange rate approval requirement; however, reporting failures subject the operation to fines and loss of exchange market access. Dividend remittances may also bear Colombian income withholding tax at rates between 5% and 20% depending on the applicable treaty and domestic participation thresholds, which treasury must model as a separate tax cost.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the regulatory framework issued by Banco de la República (primarily Circular DCIN-83 and unified exchange rules), exporters of goods must bring export proceeds into Colombia through an authorized intermediary within six (6) months from the date of the export customs declaration. Exporters of services, which includes cross-border e-commerce logistics and parcel consolidation and linehaul services, must repatriate proceeds within six (6) months from the date the service is rendered or the foreign client is invoiced, whichever occurs first. The mandatory conversion requirement applies to 100% of the export invoice value less funded discounts, and all proceeds must be sold into the Colombian foreign exchange market unless the exporter has received a previous authorization from Banco de la República to maintain a foreign currency account abroad for specified eligible uses. The exporter must submit to the intermediary a Declaración de Cambio containing the relevant export code (e.g., 1000 for goods, 9000 for services), together with the invoice, the collection instruction, and, where applicable, the bill of lading or airway bill. Failure to repatriate within the statutory window constitutes an exchange violation subject to administrative sanctions and restrictions on future clearing operations.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (the Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC), the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit, and the National Government's foreign exchange authorities under the Colombian Foreign Exchange Statute (Régimen de Cambios Internacionales, Decreto 1735/1993 and Law 9/1991).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Colombian law does not permit offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) or foreign virtual bank license holders to operate as licensed deposit-taking institutions or to intermediate foreign exchange directly without a local branch authorization from the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. Offshore fintechs such as Airwallex, Payoneer, and Wise may be used as auxiliary accounts for collections in foreign markets or for specific cross-border disbursements, especially in conjunction with the target payment rails indicated in the business scope. However, the Colombian SAS's statutory clearing system requires that capital contributions, export proceeds, and dividend remittances be settled through a licensed local financial institution. An offshore EMI account can therefore provide speed and lower cost at the marketplace layer but cannot replace the local bank treasury rail for regulatory compliance. Any digital EMI that seeks to hold funds for Colombian entities without a license violates financial consumer and exchange market rules, and entities using such providers as the exclusive settlement vehicle lose the ability to file valid Declaraciones de Cambio.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign capital injections to the Colombian SAS must be transmitted through the international banking system, normally by SWIFT MT103, with the remitter's bank debiting a client account and the receiving Colombian bank crediting the SAS's local account. The beneficiary bank must be an authorized exchange intermediary qualified to register the operation with Banco de la República. The wire should include the beneficiary company's NIT number (Tax ID), the investor's legal name and country, and a communication indicating the concept 'foreign direct investment in shares of a Colombian entity'. The Brazilian Peruvian? The typical purpose code for equity investment in Colombia is numbered in the exchange manual as an investment code (e.g., code 104 for direct investment by non-resident natural/legal persons). After the funds arrive, the local bank will request the SAS to submit a Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional (Form/Foreign Exchange Declaration No. 4), accompanied by a copy of the corporate minutes approving the capital increase, the bylaws, the investment certificate previously issued if the investor has an existing registration, and a certificate of inward wire. The bank then sends the registration report to Banco de la República within the term established for reconciliation. The company must also reflect the contribution in the commercial registry and in its equity account. Funds should not be mixed with operational receivables; separate escrow or dedicated capital transfer accounts are strongly recommended for audit traceability.For outbound trade and logistics-related disbursements, the SAS will use SWIFT MT103 messages for direct customer payments to foreign carriers, linehaul providers, and customs agents, with each payment identified under a valid concept (import of services, transport, or logistics support). For larger capital expenditures, such as long-term linehaul contract arrangements or aircraft lease guarantees, commercial letters of credit (L/C) are used to condition payment upon the presentation of compliant trade documents (airway bills, container bills of lading, packing lists, certificates of origin). The local clearing system (ACH Colombia and the Cámara de Compensación Electrónica) handles COP-denominated domestic disbursements to foster warehouse managers, last-mile couriers, and banding labor providers. All outbound FX payments must be executed through the authorized intermediary and reported to Banco de la República under the appropriate service or import code. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium. Colombian law does not permit offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) or foreign virtual bank license holders to operate as licensed deposit-taking institutions or to intermediate foreign exchange directly without a local branch authorization from the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. Offshore fintechs such as Airwallex, Payoneer, and Wise may be used as auxiliary accounts for collections in foreign markets or for specific cross-border disbursements, especially in conjunction with the target payment rails indicated in the business scope. However, the Colombian SAS's statutory clearing system requires that capital contributions, export proceeds, and dividend remittances be settled through a licensed local financial institution. An offshore EMI account can therefore provide speed and lower cost at the marketplace layer but cannot replace the local bank treasury rail for regulatory compliance. Any digital EMI that seeks to hold funds for Colombian entities without a license violates financial consumer and exchange market rules, and entities using such providers as the exclusive settlement vehicle lose the ability to file valid Declaraciones de Cambio.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taxpayer must prepare and file transfer pricing documentation when gross income exceeds 135,000 UVT (approx. COP 6,353,775,000 for FY2024) OR gross total assets exceed 45,000 UVT (approx. COP 2,117,925,000). Also mandatory for Large Taxpayers (Gran Contribuyente) or if controlled transactions are carried out with entities located in tax havens. Master and Local File thresholds are aligned with the OECD guidelines in Article 260-7 E.T.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime as in Caribbean jurisdictions. However, the Tax Code, Law 1819 of 2016, and case law enforce a substantive economic-substance test under GAAR and anti-abuse rules. A shell S.A.S. lacking local premises, employees, office equipment, operational decision-making capacity, or taking no entrepreneurial risk will be recharacterized by DIAN: deduction, Vat credit, and treaty benefits will be denied, and income may be attributed to the foreign parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Criminal Fiscal Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion: imprisonment from 48 to 108 months and fine of 15,000 to 75,000 minimum monthly legal wages, plus DIAN civil penalty of up to 160% of the omitted tax plus interest. The tax gap is recaptured at 35% plus annual interest accrued from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign false returns or approve fictitious invoicing are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax under Article 571 E.T. and the Colombian Penal Code. Courts may issue travel bans, asset embargos, and disqualification from managing companies for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Corporate Veil Piercing

Statutory sanction: DIAN is empowered to pierce the veil under Law 222 of 1995 and Article 123-4 E.T.: the legal entity is ignored, all Colombian-source profits are re-attributed to the foreign principal, and the effective tax becomes 35% CIT + 7.5%/35% dividend WHT + penalties (200% of the understated amount). The commercial registry may be cancelled.

Director personal liability: Directors are civilly liable to third parties and the Treasury when they use the company as a façade for fraud. They must restore all reparative damages, may be subject to asset seizure, and may face imprisonment for abuse of the corporate form.
AML / Money Laundering and Smuggling Precursor

Statutory sanction: Money laundering crime under Article 323 of the Colombian Penal Code carries imprisonment of 10 to 30 years and a fine of 20,000 to 100,000 minimum wages, with legal entity penalties under Law 1121/2006 and UIAF administrative fines up to 1,000 UVT for non-reporting. The proceeds and all assets linked to the activity are subject to extinción de dominio (asset confiscation).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate or permit cash-based laundering through the parcel hub are criminally liable as co-principals. The commercial veil is pierced to enforce confiscation, restitution of misappropriated funds, travel restrictions, and permanent exclusion from foreign trade operations and customs brokers. In extreme cases, extradition may be sought for laundering associated with narcotrafficking.

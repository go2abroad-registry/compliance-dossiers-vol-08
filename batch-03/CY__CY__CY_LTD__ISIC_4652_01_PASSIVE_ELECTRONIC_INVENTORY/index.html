Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance; online portal: Ariathos (https://ariathos.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Cyprus private limited company applies for incorporation with the Registrar of Companies; the statutory company registration number (HE-prefixed) constitutes the corporate Tax Identification Number for income tax purposes. VAT registration is obtained separately from the Cyprus Tax Department on Form VAT-101, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, registered office certificate, memorandum and articles of association, and proof of management and control in Cyprus. Non-resident taxable persons registering for VAT in Cyprus must appoint a tax representative where required by the Tax Department.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a Cyprus private limited company applies for incorporation with the Registrar of Companies; the statutory company registration number (HE-prefixed) constitutes the corporate Tax Identification Number for income tax purposes. VAT registration is obtained separately from the Cyprus Tax Department on Form VAT-101, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, registered office certificate, memorandum and articles of association, and proof of management and control in Cyprus. Non-resident taxable persons registering for VAT in Cyprus must appoint a tax representative where required by the Tax Department.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not maintain a US-style 'check-the-box' or statutory entity-classification election. A Cyprus private limited company is mandatorily taxable as a company at corporate level; partnerships and limited partnerships are fiscally transparent by statutory default, but no entity election exists to alter corporate classification. ATAD hybrid mismatch rules apply and no hybrid entity check-the-box mechanism is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for Cyprus-established taxable persons; nil registration threshold for non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Cyprus

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported by the supplier to a destination outside the EU are zero-rated if the goods are physically removed from Cyprus under customs supervision, the supplier holds a valid customs export declaration or equivalent electronic export message, and transport documentation proves the goods left Cyprus within the statutory timeframe. Services directly related to export goods and offshore supplies satisfying the place-of-supply rules may also qualify for zero-rating or exemption with input VAT recovery. Documentary proof must be retained to demonstrate the export chain.

  • Customs export declaration (single administrative document or electronic export declaration via Cyprus Customs)
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, or other proof of physical exportation
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment evidence confirming receipt of export proceeds
  • Certificate of origin and insurance documents where applicable

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime is currently mandatory in Cyprus. Standard VAT invoicing rules apply: invoices must be issued within 30 days of the chargeable supply and retained for audit. The Cyprus Tax Department maintains the Ariathos portal for periodic VAT filing, but it is not a transaction-level continuous transactional control system.

Zero-rating claims unsupported by sufficient export documentation are disallowed on audit, with VAT clawed back at the standard rate of 19% plus statutory interest. Cyprus tax law imposes financial penalties, additional assessments, and criminal sanctions for fraudulent export zero-rating or refund abuse; civil penalties may reach up to twice the tax evaded or understated, while criminal prosecution may result in fines and imprisonment. Documentary records must generally be retained for six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility guaranteed by TFEU Articles 63–66 and the EU Capital Movements Directive; exchange control was abolished upon EU accession (1 May 2004), and all residual crisis-era capital restrictions introduced in March 2013 were completely lifted by April 2015. The jurisdiction is EUR-denominated; profit remittance (dividends, interest, intercompany loans) to non-resident shareholders is free of any Cypriot withholding tax and requires no Central Bank approval. The only practical burdens are (i) CBC statistical reporting under the External Transactions Reporting framework (integrated transaction system, ITS) for every cross-border payment, (ii) EU/UN sanctions screening obligations, and (iii) enhanced due diligence triggered if counterparties or UBOs sit in grey-listed or sanctioned jurisdictions. No sector-specific restriction applies to wholesale electronics distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory conversion rules exist in Cyprus. Exporters may lawfully retain 100% of foreign-currency export proceeds in any currency, convert to EUR at the transacting bank's market rate, or re-deploy them for offshore trade settlement without permission. The only compliance obligation is real-time Balance of Payments classification: the reporting bank must log each export receipt/import disbursement under the CBC ITS using the Section 100 series purpose codes for merchandise trade (e.g., code 114 for imports of goods, corresponding export code for outward collection), and the company must attach the underlying commercial documents on request.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) as the National Competent Authority for banking supervision, payment systems, and external transactions statistics; monetary policy and eurozone FX regime governed jointly by the European Central Bank under the Eurosystem since 1 January 2008. The Cyprus unit for financial intelligence/suspicious transaction reporting is MOKAS (Unit for Combating Money Laundering) under L.188(I)/2007.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Passported EU Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions regulated under PSD2 (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise, Airwallex) can provide SEPA/global MT103-equivalent clearing and multi-currency float, but cannot issue commercial letters of credit, extend credit lines, or serve as statutory escrow holders. The Central Bank of Cyprus, through its CDD Directives and supervisory circulars, obliges Cypriot banks to apply enhanced diligence to funds originating from EMIs; transfers from third-country EMIs, or EMIs resident in non-cooperative jurisdictions, are commonly frozen pending source-of-funds evidence. Safe use is limited to operational disbursements below EUR 50,000; inbound capital injections and LC-backed trade flows must run through regulated Tier-1 banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File required where the Cyprus entity's aggregate controlled transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 per year for non-financial transactions and EUR 200,000 for financing transactions; Master File additionally applies where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not apply the offshore 'Economic Substance Regulations' applicable to BVI/Cayman, but under EU anti-tax-avoidance principles and Cyprus case law, a company must demonstrate real management, staff, premises, and decision-making in Cyprus to benefit from the 12.5% regime, to obtain treaty benefits, and to avoid being recharacterized as a shell; passive distribution operations must maintain adequate physical presence and payroll in Cyprus

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 50,000 or twice the evaded tax, plus interest at 5%; criminal conviction may impose imprisonment for up to five years

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow officers are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax if they signed a false return or directed concealment; civil recovery may extend to personal assets, and a criminal travel ban may be imposed pending trial
Shell Company Non-Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of Cyprus tax residence, imposition of tax in the shareholder's jurisdiction, and penalties for non-compliance with substance requirements under EU Administrative Cooperation Directive; Cypriot authorities may also strike off the company for failing to file audited accounts

Director personal liability: Directors can be disqualified for fraudulent trading; personal tax residency may be asserted by the offshore tax authority; travel restrictions and asset freezes in the shareholder's jurisdiction may apply
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profits to arm's-length basis, additional 12.5% tax, plus penalties of 10% of the adjusted tax amount and interest from the due date; systematic non-compliance may attract a higher penalty of up to 50%

Director personal liability: Directors can be held liable for tax adjustments attributable to their own-related party transactions; in severe cases the Cypriot tax authorities will pursue recovery against personal bank accounts
AML / Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Cyprus Companies Law and AML Law 188(I)/2007, ranging from EUR 500 to EUR 10,000 per violation; a serious offence may lead to dissolution of the company

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal fines and imprisonment for up to seven years for money laundering, and cancellation of the company's registration if the beneficial owner is unidentified

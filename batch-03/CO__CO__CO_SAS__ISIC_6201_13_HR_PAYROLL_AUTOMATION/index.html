Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum paid-up capital. Share capital is freely subscribed in the articles; payment terms may be deferred up to two years unless in-kind contributions (which require valuation). No escrow account mandated by law, but bank accounts should receive corporate funds before commencing tax obligations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Certificado de Bomberos) issued by the local fire department, verifying the office has functional extinguishers, emergency routes, and adequate occupancy load. Occupancy must comply with structural and electrical safety inspections as reflected in the SIC (Sistema de Inspección y Vigilancia) workplace clearance; if the office has more than 50 workers, a formal occupational health and safety program (SG-SST) is mandatory.

E-government filing portal

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Colombian domestic law. Available statutory elections include: (i) Colombian Holding Company (CHC) regime per Law 1943 de 2018 / Law 2010 de 2019 for qualifying holding entities, with exemption treatment for foreign dividends and capital gains; (ii) Régimen Simple de Tributación (RST) integrated tax regime for eligible SMEs with annual gross income not exceeding 100,000 UVT; (iii) Advance Pricing Agreements (APAs) with DIAN for transfer-pricing certainty; (iv) optional presumptive cost/profit percentage regimes for specified sectors; and (v) conclusive settlement mechanisms for challenged tax positions, at DIAN's discretion.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) operates under a managed-floating exchange-rate regime. Current-account transactions — trade in software services, royalties, lease payments, interest on external debt, and occasional transfers such as profit remittances and family remittances — are fully convertible absent prior authorization, but every such payment must be 'canalized' (channeled) through an Authorized Intermediary of the Exchange Market (IMC) by filing a Declaración de Cambio (foreign-exchange declaration). Capital-account inflows, including non-resident direct equity investment in the Colombian SAS, are freely permitted but must be registered with Banco de la República through Form 3 (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales); without such registration, the non-resident investor loses the legal right to remit dividends through the regulated exchange market and to repatriate capital proceeds. The principal profit-remittance hurdles are: (i) proof of a valid foreign-investment registration, (ii) corporate approval of the dividend distribution by the shareholders' meeting, (iii) certification by the registered public accountant (contador público) that the profits are distributable and have borne the applicable corporate income tax, (iv) payment of the 7.5% dividend withholding tax applicable to non-residents for profits declared from earnings subject to tax at the standard corporate rate, and (v) verification that the underlying dividend payment is declared to the IMC under the correct exchange-market concept code and accompanied by the dividend act, financial statements, and tax-cleared profit confirmation. There are no exchange-licensing restrictions on the amount of profit that may be repatriated once the aforementioned documentary obligations are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For exports of services (including the Payroll-SaaS software platform), the exporter must 'reintegrate' the foreign-currency proceeds into Colombia through an IMC within six (6) months counted from the date of issuance of the export invoice or, per prevailing Banco de la República interpretation, from the date of the respective income (devengo); goods exports are reintegrated within six (6) months from the shipment (embarque) date. The mandatory channeling obligation is effectively 100% of the export proceeds: the exporter must either sell the foreign currency to an IMC or deposit it in a Banco de la República-authorized foreign-currency account (cuenta de compensación) previously registered with the Exchange Market, and the corresponding declaración de cambio must be filed with the IMC. There is no fixed percentage split between forced conversion and retention because the full amount of proceeds must be canalized; retention in foreign currency is possible only through a registered compensation account or, during a temporary statutory window, through a 'cuenta de destinación específica' at the IMC. Failure to reintegrate within the six-month term triggers monetary fines imposed by Banco de la República and may cause Directorate of Taxes (DIAN) enforcement for customs/metrics mismatches.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia, acting with the Junta Directiva as the ultimate monetary and exchange authority; the foreign-exchange regulatory framework is principally codified in Resolución Externa No. 1 of 2018 and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83. The Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) supervises all Authorized Intermediaries of the Exchange Market (intermediarios del mercado cambiario, or IMCs), and the Unidad de Información y Análisis Financiero (UIAF) administers the anti-money-laundering reporting regime governing banking onboarding and cross-border transfers.

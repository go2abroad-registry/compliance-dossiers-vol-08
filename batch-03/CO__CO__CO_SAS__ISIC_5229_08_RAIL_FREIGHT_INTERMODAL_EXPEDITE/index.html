Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Unidad Administrativa Especial Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN), Bogotá D.C.; online portal: https://www.dian.gov.co (MUISCA self-service platform).

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian S.A.S. (foreign-owned): (1) register incorporation at the Chamber of Commerce; (2) apply for RUT inscription through DIAN's MUISCA portal using the legal representative's digital certificate; (3) obtain NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) upon approval; (4) update RUT to register as Responsable de IVA if conducting taxable activities or exports; (5) activate customs and foreign-exchange statuses, including 'exportador'. A non-domiciled foreign entity may obtain a non-resident NIT through a designated agent, but this dossier assumes the Colombian S.A.S. is the operating exporter.

(foreign-owned): (1) register incorporation at the Chamber of Commerce; (2) apply for RUT inscription through DIAN's MUISCA portal using the legal representative's digital certificate; (3) obtain NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) upon approval; (4) update RUT to register as Responsable de IVA if conducting taxable activities or exports; (5) activate customs and foreign-exchange statuses, including 'exportador'. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election is available. An S.A.S. is a Colombian domestic corporation taxed at the entity level. The only optional regime relevant to eligibility is the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE), available when annual gross revenue does not exceed 100,000 UVT and the business activity is eligible; ordinary VAT and corporate income tax otherwise apply mandatorily.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic turnover threshold: any person, including a S.A.S., performing taxable activities in Colombia must obtain RUT/IVA registration before the first taxable event. Occasional sellers of tangible goods are subject to IVA registration only above 15,200 UVT; for the optional SIMPLE regime the annual revenue ceiling is 100,000 UVT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Sales of goods that physically leave Colombian customs territory are treated as excluded from IVA (0% effective rate), provided the export is duly completed and evidenced. Export services, including international freight-forwarding and rail intermodal transport services, are zero-rated under Article 481 of the Tax Statute where the service is performed for use or consumption abroad. The exporter must be the legal owner of the goods, hold RUT status as Responsable de IVA, and maintain the physical and financial evidence of the export operation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación DEX) accepted and transmitted by DIAN
  • Bill of Lading or Multimodal Bill of Lading for rail intermodal movement
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • International rail waybill (CIM/SMGS) or combined transport bill evidencing the route to customs exit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de compensación de divisas / Formulario No. 1)
  • Certificate of origin or transport authorization if a preferential trade claim is made

Mandatory electronics invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta) via DIAN-authorised software or direct API. Each invoice must be transmitted in XML to DIAN's validation services in near-real time; the invoice must receive an accepted/valid status, include the CUDE (Código Único de Facturación), and carry a QR code. In rail-freight and intermodal forwarding, additional electronic transport documents and the electronic support document for acquisitions from non-obligated parties may be required. Failure to comply triggers penalties under Articles 652 and 652-1 of the Colombian Tax Statute.

DIAN may verify physical customs exit, delivery to the foreign acquirer, and actual repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days. Any improper VAT refund is clawed back with default interest; fraudulent requests trigger the penalty for inexactness (100% of the improper benefit), an additional 30% penalty for improper refunds, possible criminal liability for customs fraud under Law 1762 of 2015, and cancellation of export-registration status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates an officially free foreign exchange market embedded in a regulated framework. Current account payments and transfers are freely convertible and may be processed through authorized local banks without prior central bank approval, subject only to documentary verification. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, foreign loans, and portfolio investments, are freely permitted but require mandatory registration with Banco de la República for statistical, tax, and future repatriation purposes. Profit and dividend remittances are lawful once the foreign investment has been duly registered and the applicable corporate income tax (31% statutory rate) has been settled; a certificate of tax compliance from DIAN may be required. Residual restrictions exist only in respect of non-registered investments and transactions that do not pass through the formal exchange market, which would expose the company to exchange infractions. Overall convertibility risk for an operating S.A.S. is low, while documentation discipline is mandatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be channeled (canalización) through the foreign exchange market using an authorized intermediario del mercado cambiario. For goods exports, the exporter must repatriate and realize or deposit the FX proceeds within six (6) months from the date of the export declaration (Declaración de Exportación) or the shipment date, whichever applies; an extension of up to three (3) additional months may be requested before expiry. Service exports, including rail freight forwarding ancillary services, have a twelve (12) month channeling term. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into COP; the exporter may keep the proceeds in a local foreign currency deposit account, provided the funds are settled through a local bank and the respective FX declaration is submitted. Non-compliance triggers fines and the inability to deduct certain expenses connected to the export transaction until the channeling obligation is regularized.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia); foreign exchange surveillance and enforcement is exercised through the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia, while the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit issues exchange policy guidelines under Law 9 of 1991 and Decree 1735 of 1993.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low direct viability; Medium only under a local licensed partnership. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not recognized as authorized financial entities in Colombia; banking and monetary intermediation activities are reserved for entities licensed by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC). Unregulated EMIs cannot open local nostro accounts, issue payment instruments, or execute FX transactions in the official exchange market. They may provide invoice automation, treasury software, and conduit payment technology, but any settlement must occur through a Colombian licensed bank or an SFC-authorized payment company. The account-holding bank will treat such EMI as a third-party service provider subject to separate KYC and contractual review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing: the foreign investor transmits the capital contribution via SWIFT MT103 from an offshore bank account to the Colombian S.A.S.'s account at an authorized local bank. The remittance must refer to the Colombian NIT (tax identification number) and clearly indicate the investment concept, e.g., 'Foreign Direct Investment – Capital Contribution'. Upon receipt, the local bank will classify the transaction as a capital account operation and require the execution of a foreign exchange declaration (Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales). The company must register the investment with Banco de la República within one (1) year of the effective contribution, through the bank or directly via the electronic system of the central bank. The registration includes the investor's identity, the amount in COP and foreign currency, the established equity percentage, and the sector classification. Evidence required: apostilled incorporation documents, board resolution approving the capital call, bank statement of the remittance, and a sworn statement from the legal representative. Without registration, profit remittance and eventual disinvestment are unavailable.Outbound settlements for freight and intermodal rail operations are cleared through: (a) SWIFT MT103 direct bank wires for freight charges, terminal fees, storage, and ancillary logistics payments to foreign counterparties; (b) commercial letters of credit (L/Cs) issued by Colombian banks in favor of rail operators or equipment suppliers, governed by UCP600 and ISP98 rules; (c) documentary collections (D/P, D/A) as secondary support instruments. Each convertible outbound payment must pass through the exchange market if remitted in foreign currency; the bank will require underlying commercial contracts, invoices, customs evidence (where applicable), and the corresponding form for the exchange declaration. Renglón 35 Taxable net income (renta líquida gravable) after allowed costs, deductions and tax-loss offsets; the base to which the 35% corporate rate is applied. DIAN TP adjustments may increase this figure if the S.A.S. charges below-market fees to non-resident railway affiliates. The non-resident ultimate beneficiary is not taxed on this line, but dividend distributions will be separately withheld. Late filing triggers a penalty of 5% of the income tax payable per month or fraction of month, capped at 100% of the tax due; zero-tax filers attract a minimum late-filing penalty of 0.5% of gross equity or gross income, whichever is greater. Late payment additionally accrues DIAN default interest (currently approximately 1.2% per month).
Renglón 46 Corporate income tax payable at the 35% statutory rate, reduced by prepayments, withholdings and foreign tax credits validly claimed. This is the entity-level tax; it is not offset by any credits standing in the hands of non-resident shareholders. The S.A.S. must remit the balance to DIAN through authorized financial entities. Late filing triggers a penalty of 5% of the income tax payable per month or fraction of month, capped at 100% of the tax due; zero-tax filers attract a minimum late-filing penalty of 0.5% of gross equity or gross income, whichever is greater. Late payment additionally accrues DIAN default interest (currently approximately 1.2% per month).
Renglón 48 Creditable withholdings (retenciones) on income suffered during the year, including charges on railway freight contracts and bank interest. Attach DIAN withholding certificates. Withholding relating to non-resident counterparties without a permanent establishment is not credited on this line; instead, the S.A.S. must pay over such withholdings as a withholding agent. Late filing triggers a penalty of 5% of the income tax payable per month or fraction of month, capped at 100% of the tax due; zero-tax filers attract a minimum late-filing penalty of 0.5% of gross equity or gross income, whichever is greater. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation and Form 120 declaration are required when taxpayer gross income or net equity exceeds 450,000 UVT (approximately COP 21,178,500,000 for tax year 2024) and related-party cross-border transactions exceed 20,000 UVT per counterparty; documentation must include Local File and, if applicable, Master File in Spanish.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the DIAN enforces general anti-abuse and substance-based norms (E.T. Article 869), the abuse-of-law doctrine and BEPS minimum standards. The rail-freight S.A.S. must demonstrate genuine economic activity—actual subcontracts, container tracking systems, trained personnel, office premises and commercial risk—or face income re-attribution, denial of treaty benefits and characterization as an artificial intermediary.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: DIAN penalty of 100%–200% of the understated tax, plus adjusted taxable income, default interest and possible tax fraud criminal prosecution under Colombian Criminal Law (Ley 1762/2015) carrying imprisonment of 36–108 months and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign falsely or order concealment are jointly and severally liable under E.T. Article 793; civil restitution of evaded tax, personal asset attachment, travel suspensions and entry bans to Colombia, potential extradition, and disqualification as officers in any Colombian entity.
Shell Company / Sham Entity

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the corporate personality under general anti-abuse rules, reallocate income to the foreign controlling party, assess 200% penalties on non-deductible costs, and cancel the mercantile registration in the Cámara de Comercio.

Director personal liability: Nominal shareholders and founders face de facto manager liability, tort claims from creditors, criminal liability for ideological falsehood in incorporation, and a ban on holding corporate directorships in Colombia.
Money Laundering / AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Financial Superintendency fines up to COP 32 billion or 10 times the illicit transaction; asset freezing, business dissolution, and criminal money-laundering charges (Criminal Code Article 323) carrying 96–270 months imprisonment and monetary fines.

Director personal liability: Officers executing or concealing such transfers face criminal complicity, personal forfeiture of Colombian assets, restriction orders preventing exit from Colombia, Interpol-driven extradition and multi-jurisdictional asset freezing.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DIAN transfer-pricing adjustment, penalty of 5%–10% of the transaction value or 100%–300% of the adjustment, plus moral interest; the taxpayer loses the right to present reliable evidence after the inspection notice.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false transfer-pricing certifications or causing the omission may be jointly liable for the additional corporate tax; repeated non-compliance may trigger a DIAN criminal referral and a personal judicial garnishment order.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; electronic services via https://muisca.dian.gov.co and https://www.dian.gov.co/web/dian/facturacion-electronica

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.) in Colombia must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through the registration process at a Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce) and DIAN. The procedure comprises: (1) drafting and notarizing the public deed or private document of incorporation in accordance with Law 1258 of 2008 and the Código de Comercio; (2) registering the company with the Cámara de Comercio of the registered office jurisdiction to obtain the Certificado de Existencia y Representación Legal; (3) obtaining a pre-assigned NIT via the Cámara de Comercio system or directly through DIAN's MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) portal using the RUES (Registro Único Empresarial y Social) integration; (4) completing the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) enrollment before DIAN with supporting incorporation documents, proof of the registered office in Colombia, and identification of the legal representative, shareholders, and actual beneficiaries; (5) activating the electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) account and obtaining the certificado digital for electronic signature; (6) registering as responsible VAT taxpayer (Régimen Común) if taxable operations exceed the VAT threshold, or as a non-VAT taxpayer (Régimen Simplificado) where applicable and legally eligible under Decree 1625 of 2016; and (7) registering with the local municipal authorities for industry and commerce tax (ICA) and with the social security system for employee contributions if the S.A.S. hires staff. Non-resident shareholders do not need to be physically present; a resident legal representative with sufficient powers is required for all tax registration and filings. Current NIT assignment is immediate upon completion of the RUT registration in MUISCA, and a foreign-owned S.A.S. must file its registration and any subsequent changes to the RUT within the deadlines established by DIAN (generally 60 days from incorporation or from any relevant change).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia generally does not provide an elective check-the-box regime comparable to the U.S. classification election; the S.A.S. is per se a separate taxable entity subject to corporate income tax (impuesto sobre la renta) at the entity level. Nevertheless, the following statutory election and special-regime mechanisms exist: (1) the option for a S.A.S. to be taxed under the general corporate income tax rules with no formal election required; (2) the possibility to elect the SIMPLE (Sistema Impositivo Simplificado) regime under Law 1943 of 2018 and Law 2010 of 2019, as effective from 2020, for entities, including S.A.S., that are considered small and medium businesses under Article 906 of the Tax Statute and that meet the requirements specified in Article 911 of the Tax Statute, provided the entity's gross income from the immediately preceding taxable year is not more than 100,000 UVTs (approximately COP 4,758,000,000 for 2025, adjusted annually), and the entity is not in certain excluded categories (e.g., for-profit entities under the SIMPLE regime may not be subject to income tax and complementary taxes through a simplified estimation of taxable income based on gross income, while also paying VAT and other national taxes); (3) the election for entities subject to the SIMPLE regime to treat VAT payable and national consumption tax payable as part of the unified SIMPLE tax return, but the entity may not generally claim input VAT refunds where it voluntarily opts into SIMPLE; (4) the check-the-box-like classification of a foreign company with a Colombian permanent establishment (PE) because of the application of domestic PE attribution rules and tax treaty tie-breaker rules, but this is not a domestic entity election; and (5) for hybrid mismatch purposes, Colombia has adopted specific rules under Law 2010 of 2019 (Articles 118-1 and 121-2 of the Tax Statute) that disallow deductions in respect of hybrid instruments and hybrid entities when the payment is not subject to taxation in the recipient jurisdiction, or where a deduction is also obtained in the other jurisdiction; a taxpayer can elect to apply the general rules consistently but cannot make a free-standing 'check-the-box' election to ignore entity status. Because Colombia has no general elective classification regime, the default S.A.S. classification as an entity-level taxpayer is mandatory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no fixed universal VAT registration threshold in Colombia based solely on gross revenue. A person or entity performing taxable activities subject to VAT, selling taxable goods, or rendering taxable services in the national territory, must register as a responsible of the régimen común (standard VAT responsible person) and obtain the RUT, regardless of turnover, unless the entity qualifies as a small trader in the régimen simplificado under Article 437 of the Tax Statute, which historically applied only to natural persons and not to S.A.S. legal entities. As a practical and economic threshold for a newly incorporated S.A.S., the DIAN's SIMPLE regime permits taxpayer status under SIMPLE for entities with gross income not exceeding 100,000 UVTs (COP 4,758,000,000 for 2025). For VAT registration, the statutory trigger is the realization of any taxable event in Colombia, including importation of tangible goods, sale of tangible movable goods, provision of services, and the rendering of services from abroad that are taxable in Colombia; a foreign supplier without a local entity is required to register with DIAN for VAT when it carries out taxable transactions in Colombia through a permanent establishment or under the rules for digital services (Law 1819 of 2016 and Decree 1625 of 2016). For non-resident service providers, including pre-shipment inspection and offsite quality control services performed for Colombian beneficiaries, the VAT registration and remittance obligation arises when the service is rendered to a Colombian recipient and the service is taxable in Colombia under the destination principle, even if the supplier is not established in Colombia; the foreign supplier may appoint a responsible third party and must comply with electronic invoicing rules. The economically relevant thresholds include: COP 0 (no threshold) for importers and suppliers of taxable services; and for the simplified taxpayer status, restricted to individuals, so an S.A.S. has an effective registration obligation of COP 0 whenever it undertakes any taxable activity. For the anti-hoarding/wealth sector no VAT threshold applies in respect of property-related transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Colombian VAT law (Law 1819 of 2016, and previously Decree 1625 of 2016), the exportation of goods and services is taxed at a rate of zero per cent (0%) VAT, provided the operation qualifies as an 'export' under the Customs Statute (Decreto 1165 de 2019) and applicable DIAN interpretative rules. For goods: zero-rating applies to the sale and transfer of title to tangible goods departing Colombia permanently to a foreign destination, which must be evidenced by the applicable customs declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DEX or Documento Único de Aduana – DUAR) and completion of the export process. For services: zero-rating applies to services rendered for the exclusive benefit of an overseas recipient unless the service is used or consumed in Colombia, and includes the following: (i) services related to technical, scientific, and professional services (including quality control, inspection, testing and factory audit services) that are exported and effectively used abroad; (ii) services rendered to foreign persons or entities on movable goods that are physically located abroad; (iii) services that are made from Colombia and are provided for use outside Colombia, provided the recipient's operation and the economic use occur abroad; and (iv) services that are part of a value chain of an exporter, in accordance with Article 481 of the Tax Statute. The Colombian zero-rating law for services set out in Article 481, paragraph (e) includes 'servicios prestados desde el territorio nacional hacia el exterior': exported services whose recipient is a foreign entity without domicile or residence in Colombia, provided the service is not used or exploited in Colombia. An inspection and QC agency providing factory inspection, audit, testing, and certification services to a foreign buyer of goods that are physically located in Colombia, or to a foreign principal that does not have a domicile in Colombia and where the service is used solely in connection with the buyer's overseas operations, generally qualifies for zero-rating if the service is not consumed in Colombia. However, the zero-rating for services is subject to restrictive requirements: (a) the service must not be directly linked to goods physically located or consumed in Colombia, unless the goods are later exported; and (b) if the service benefits a Colombian taxpayer or is consumed within Colombian territory, it is subject to 19% VAT. The supplier must obtain, maintain and report an 'export service declaration' / certificate of exportation through DIAN's electronic platform (Formulario 120 for export services), and the exporter must satisfy the requirement of transfer of currency from abroad. For services that are 'exported services' under the law, the zero-rating is appropriate and allows the supplier to claim refund of input VAT (saldo a favor) attributable to the exported operation. For all zero-rated exports, the supplier must file the periodic VAT returns (Formulario 300), declare the zero-rated operations as exports, and file the annual income tax return including the export revenue. No prior administrative authorization is required for zero-rating, but the conditions must be demonstrated in an eventual audit.

  • Documento Único de Aduana (DUAR/DEX) for goods exports (Declaración de Exportación) accepted by DIAN and the customs authority
  • Electronic export invoice (Factura Electrónica de Venta – FE) with CUDE and QR code, transmitted to DIAN and validated

  • Commercial invoice issued to the foreign buyer and proof of payment or the recognized contract for the sale/purchase

Colombia mandates full electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) for all business taxpayers subject to VAT and income tax. The system is operated by DIAN through the MUISCA platform and requires: (1) obtaining authorization as an electronic invoicing issuer; (2) using DIAN-authorized technological providers (proveedores tecnológicos) or directly using the free DIAN web application; (3) validating and transmitting every electronic invoice, credit note, and debit note to DIAN for validation and mandatory 'acuse de recibo' (acknowledgment) before delivery to the acquirer; (4) the inclusion of a traceable CUDE (Código Único de Documento Electrónico) and a digital stamp (QR code) with verification link; (5) representing the invoice in XML format in accordance with DIAN Resolution 165 of 2023 and Annex 36 of the Technical Standard (factura electrónica de venta); (6) for an S.A.S. using a certified software system, additional real-time telematic transmission is required within a maximum of one working day after the invoice is issued, although certain sectors and taxpayers are subject to event-based or immediate electronic transmission requirements; (7) mandatory electronic transmission of invoices in real time for the Sociedad's operations, including the reporting of all electronic invoices to DIAN in the 'radian' (Registro de las Operaciones) network to support tradability of electronic invoices; (8) digital fiscal clearance for export invoices and export manifests through DIAN's export validation system, ensuring that export invoices are transmitted before customs declaration and that the respective SAD (Documento Único de Aduana – DUAR) cross-matches with the electronic invoice and export ledger; (9) compliance with the electronic invoice exportation regime under which zero-rated exports must be invoiced using the same electronic platform and must be accepted by DIAN; and (10) penalties under Article 652 of the Tax Statute for failure to transmit or improper transmission of electronic invoices (up to 5% of the value of the transactions or coins) and the general ineffectiveness of the tax document for the buyer's input VAT credit if DIAN validation is not completed. There is no separate physical 'stamping' machine for VAT; the validation act by DIAN is the fiscal clearance, evidenced by the CUDE and QR.

DIAN maintains a robust audit framework for export zero-rating claims. The following penalties and clawback mechanisms apply: (1) if the taxpayer fails to evidence the export, fails to receive payment in foreign currency from abroad, fails to repatriate the proceeds within the applicable period, or the service is later determined to have been consumed or used in Colombia, the zero-rating is disallowed and the taxpayer must pay the full 19% VAT plus interest (Article 481, Article 485 and Article 502 of the Tax Statute), and if VAT was refunded to the exporter, the refund is subject to immediate clawback (revocatoria de la devolución). (2) The DIAN may apply the 'devolución improcedente' rules under Article 855 of the Tax Statute, which impose a penalty of up to 100% of the amount improperly refunded, plus inflation adjustment and default interest, where the refund request is based on false or inaccurate information; the penalty may be up to 150% in case of subornation or gross negligence (Article 647). (3) Misclassification of an export operation to benefit from the zero-rate constitutes inexact statements in tax returns, which under Article 647 of the Tax Statute may result in a penalty equal to 200% of the greater of the tax evaded or the difference between the tax declared and the correct tax determination for international transactions, and criminal liability for tax evasion under Article 434-1 of the Criminal Code (Ley 1819 de 2016, Código Penal colombiano), which may carry prison sanctions of 6 to 12 years for aggravated evasion and up to 20 years for certain frauds through artificial operations. (4) The failure to maintain and present the complete export documentation bundle, including the inward remittance certificate, or the failure to comply with the export form regulations (e.g., submission of Formulario 120 within the applicable time) leads to the rejection of export zero-rating and the VAT assessment plus penalties under Article 642 (failure to present documents) and Article 654 (breach of invoicing obligations). (5) The use of fictitious export claims or fraudulent circular transactions to create artificial export VAT credits will trigger the 'artificiality doctrine' in Colombian tax law (Article 869 and Article 90 of the Tax Statute) and can result in the complete disallowance of the refund, monetary sanctions of up to 5,000 UVTs for non-reportable schemes, criminal sanctions, and the blacklisting of the taxpayer from future export refunds. (6) DIAN's audit scrutiny for zero-rated services includes verification of the actual foreign recipient's domicile, review of subcontracting arrangements to ensure that the service is wholly performed outside Colombia or to non-residents, proof of the transfer of the benefit, and consistency between the electronic invoices, export service forms, General Ledger, and bank records. Any deviation from the statutory six-month FX repatriation period (180 days) under Article 1.6.1.13.3 of Decree 1068 of 2015 (foreign exchange regime) exposes the transaction to review and the refund to potential clawback. Exporters are required to maintain all underlying documentation for a minimum of 5 years from the date of filing of the annual income tax return, as required under Article 632 of the Tax Statute and the customs archives regulations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed-float currency system with unrestricted convertibility for current-account trade in services and for properly registered capital-account movements. The Colombian peso (COP) is not freely convertible offshore; every designated cross-border transaction must be reported on a Declaration de Cambio (Formulario No. 1) through an authorized local intermediary bank. For a Colombian S.A.S. engaged in ISIC 7120 commercial inspection, testing and QC services, revenues are classified as service exports and are fully convertible if channelled through the Mercado Cambiario. Dividend and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are allowed after the foreign investment is registered with Banco de la República, the corresponding corporate tax on distributable earnings is paid, and the exchange declaration for the dividend remittance is filed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For commercial inspection/testing and QC service exports, 100% of export proceeds received in foreign currency must be channelled through Colombia's regulated exchange market within six (6) months from the invoice accrual date. There is no forced surrender of currency to the central bank; however, the exporter must either sell the foreign currency to an authorized bank for COP or deposit the funds into a registered foreign-currency compensation account (cuenta de compensación) maintained with a Colombian bank. The proceeds must be traced to a valid service export contract and invoice, and the operation must be filed as Declaración de Cambio 'Exportación de Servicios'. Failure to channel within the term exposes the company to fines, exchange regime penalties, and the reclassification of undistributed export earnings as presumed taxable income.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), which administers the National Foreign Exchange Regime (Régimen Cambiario) under External Resolution No. 1 of 2018 and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83. The Ministry of Finance and DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) support the fiscal and customs enforcement of exchange-control obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as the primary treasury banking rail; High as an ancillary rail for small-value cross-border payouts and vendor disbursements. Colombian law does not authorize foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) to maintain COP operating accounts or to receive export proceeds for foreign-exchange compliance purposes. Wise/Wise Business may be used as a convenient payment corridor for non-statutory transfers, but all export revenue importation, capital injection routing and official FX reporting must pass through a licensed domestic commercial bank using the appropriate Declaración de Cambio. An offshore EMI account cannot replace the statutory local bank relationship required under Colombian tax and exchange-control legislation.

Root cause: The QC inspection company has no visible operational substance, no local physical office, and expects a high turnover of report-based invoices from multiple overseas clients.

Defense evidence: Provide a lease agreement or property title for the local office, office photographs and geolocation, contracts with operational staff, payroll and social-security settlement records, health-and-safety and QHSE manuals, a full client list with executed service contracts, inspection reports with photographic evidence, and an independent auditor's letter confirming the real economic substance of the enterprise.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections into the Colombian S.A.S. must be received via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the non-resident investor to the company's local commercial-bank account in USD, EUR or COP. The remittance field must clearly state the investor identity, the beneficiary, the purpose 'Inversión Internacional', the foreign-investment registration status, and the applicable Banco de la República coding. Within twelve months of receipt, the company must file Formulario No. 1 through the local bank, and upon validation obtain the Constancia de Inversión Internacional which becomes indispensable for future dividend repatriation and capital exit. Funds may not be used for unauthorized early repayment, informal shareholder loans or operational expenses that are inconsistent with the declared investment purpose; any increase in share capital must be contemporaneously documented in a notarial minute and registered in the Chamber of Commerce.The principal clearing rail for outbound trade disbursements is the SWIFT MT103 international wire transfer executed from the local Colombian bank account to the foreign beneficiary. For the import of physical inspection and lab-testing equipment, a commercial letter of credit issued through the Colombian bank (SWIFT MT700) or a documentary collection is used to align payment with shipping documents. For payment of overseas subcontractors, independent inspectors and technical consultants, the company must issue wire transfers with a valid tax-supported invoice, apply any applicable withholding tax (for services rendered abroad the general withholding rate is 11% unless reduced by a double-taxation treaty), and maintain the supporting declaration for the sale of COP if the payment requires conversion.

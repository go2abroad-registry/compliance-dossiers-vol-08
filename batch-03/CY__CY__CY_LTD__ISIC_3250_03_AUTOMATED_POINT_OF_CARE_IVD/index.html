Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual or mailbox-only office is not considered acceptable commercial substance for this licensed IVD manufacturing scope. The Companies Law, Cap. 113, section 104 requires every company to maintain a registered office in Cyprus at a physical address where statutory records are kept and legal process may be served. For a company holding a medical-device license and seeking to establish manufacturing, registration or distribution activity under Regulation (EU) 2017/746, the Medical Devices Competent Authority, the Registrar of Companies, and admitting banks will reject arrangements that consist solely of mail-forwarding. The registered office may, however, be lawfully provided by a licensed on-island corporate services provider operating dedicated physical premises with authorized staff, provided the company maintains a genuine place of management and decision-making in Cyprus where tax residence and license oversight are concerned.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Ministry of Finance) - official e-portal 'TaxForAll' at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy; appointment/registration also handled by the Department of Registrar of Companies and Intellectual Property (DRCIP) for company incorporation and HE-number allocation; the Customs and Excise Department issues the EORI number and operates the 'Ariadni' customs declaration system for export clearances.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Cyprus Ltd in the DRCIP e-Company registry to obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and Company Registration Number (HE-XXXXX). Step 2: Within 60 days of incorporation file the Registered Office notice and Director/Beneficial Owner filings; register with the Tax Department via TaxForAll to obtain the 8-digit Tax Identification Code (TIC), the single Cypriot tax reference for Corporate Income Tax, social insurance and VAT. Step 3: Submit Form VAT-101 for compulsory VAT registration if the 12-month taxable turnover of IVD analyzers, microfluidic cartridges and reagents has exceeded or is expected to exceed EUR 15,600, or voluntarily to recover input VAT on manufacturing and R&D equipment; the VAT number is issued by the VAT Service of the Tax Department. Step 4: Apply to the Customs and Excise Department for a CY-prefixed EORI number needed for each export declaration (EX-A/SAD) filed through the 'Ariadni' customs system. Step 5: Register as an employer with the Social Insurance Services and complete statistical registration under ISIC 3250 / NAICS 334516. Step 6: Open the Cypriot operating bank account, as the TIC is required for account opening, VAT refunds and downstream CIT/GESY tax payments, and the bank will maintain the foreign-exchange remittance records used for export substantiation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Cyprus Ltd is a separate legal taxpayer resident in Cyprus when managed and controlled in Cyprus; it is taxable at the 12.5% Corporate Income Tax rate on worldwide profits, with foreign tax credit relief, participation exemption for dividends and capital gains on qualifying subsidiaries, IP Box 80% deduction for qualifying medical-device IP, and Notional Interest Deduction on new equity. No pass-through or check-the-box regime exists for a Cyprus Ltd; the VAT registration is held at entity level; no tax-exempt zone applies to this IVD manufacturer.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not apply the US check-the-box classification; the relevant statutory elections are: (i) VAT grouping election under the Cyprus VAT Law N.95(I)/2000 allowing related Cyprus companies to be treated as a single taxable person; (ii) tax group relief election permitting surrender of losses among 75%-owned Cyprus-resident group companies; (iii) IP Box election requiring timely notification to the Tax Department to claim the 80% notional deduction for qualifying IP assets; (iv) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) on fresh equity contributed after 2015; (v) DAC6 Mandatory Disclosure Rule filings for reportable cross-border hybrid mismatch arrangements; and (vi) export zero-rating compliance procedures, which require no separate election beyond maintaining the statutory proof-of-export dossier.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 taxable turnover in a 12-month period for Cyprus-domestic supplies of goods and services; the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies to intra-Community distance sales and electronically supplied services to non-taxable persons; voluntary VAT registration is permitted for pre-revenue exporters to recover input VAT on the IVD production facility; the company must hold a valid Cyprus VAT identification number on every zero-rated export invoice.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to (a) direct exports of IVD analyzers, microfluidic cartridges, immunoassay reagents and point-of-care devices physically removed from Cyprus to a destination outside the EU under Article 41 of the Cyprus VAT Law; (b) intra-Community supplies of goods transported to another EU Member State where the customer's VAT ID is valid in VIES and the goods leave Cyprus/are delivered to the other Member State; and (c) international transport and ancillary services relating to the exported goods. For direct exports, the goods must physically leave EU territory within 90 days of the time of supply and the exporter must hold full proof of export; absence of such evidence causes the supply to be re-characterised as a taxable domestic supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX-A) lodged via the Cyprus 'Ariadni' customs system quoting the company's CY-prefixed EORI number
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (master/house) evidencing dispatch from a Cyprus/EU port and naming the foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice from the Cypriot bank evidencing settlement of the export invoice in EUR or USD
  • Commercial Invoice in English stating HS 9027.80 for immunoassay analyzers, HS 3822 for IVD reagents and microfluidic cartridges, and Incoterms 2020
  • Packing List, container/gate receipt and vessel or airline manifest confirming physical loading
  • Proof of departure from EU territory: customs-stamped EX-A, T1 exit certificate or destination-country customs arrival record
  • Customer purchase order and underlying supply contract demonstrating commercial substance and arm's-length consideration
  • CE-IVDR declaration of conformity and ISO 13485 certification to support regulatory consistency of the export file

Cyprus does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls (CTC), digital stamping or SAF-T filing; mandatory e-invoicing exists only for B2G transactions under EU Directive 2014/55/EU (EN 16931/Peppol). Electronic submission of VAT returns, EC Sales Lists and VIES recapitulative statements is mandatory through the TaxForAll portal. All commercial e-invoices must contain the EU-standard mandatory fields, including seller and buyer VAT IDs, sequential invoice number, supply date, VAT-exclusive amount, zero-rating reason and export reference. The exporter's ERP should be configured to issue UBL 2.x e-invoices and to retain original XML files for the seven-year statutory retention period; API-ready datasets should be maintained for future EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' transparency requirements.

The Tax Department may audit zero-rated export transactions within the standard five-year VAT assessment period, extended to ten years where the taxpayer has acted deliberately; the exporter bears the full burden of proof. Failure of substantiation triggers a cumulative clawback: the 19% VAT is reassessed on the full invoice value; statutory interest accrues on the unpaid VAT; a late-payment/default surcharge is imposed under the VAT Law; and a misdeclaration penalty of up to 100% of the assessed VAT may be applied. Deliberate or fraudulent export claims, including forged bills of lading or fictitious EX-A declarations, are criminal offences exposing the officers of the Cyprus Ltd to fines and imprisonment of up to five years, together with the full ten-year retrospective clawback. All export records, VIES validation logs, customs declarations and bank remittance advices must be retained for at least seven years from the date of export.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate value of intercompany controlled transactions in the fiscal year; a Local File is mandatory above this threshold. A Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50 million and the Cyprus entity has controlled transactions exceeding the local threshold.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus is not an ESR-governed offshore centre, but economic substance is a substantive requirement for tax residence and treaty benefits. A Cypriot IVD manufacturing company must prove real decision-making, qualified medical-device staff, QA/manufacturing facilities, bank accounts, meetings and operational risk in Cyprus; artificial arrangements without economic substance may be recharacterised or denied treaty protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – false or misleading corporate income tax return

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Income Tax Law and Penal Code including monetary penalties equal to twice the tax evaded and imprisonment of directors/employees responsible for the false return.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or procure false returns may be criminally prosecuted, disqualified as directors, ordered to make civil restitution to the company/creditors, and prohibited from travelling abroad while the investigation or confiscation proceedings are pending.
Shell Company / absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation of the arrangement as null and void, denial of tax residence or treaty benefits, reassessment at 12.5% plus interest and penalties, and potential withdrawal of tax clearance/corporate registration benefits.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced; directors may be personally liable for taxes of a sham company, ordered to restore distributed capital, disqualified under the Companies Law, and subjected to asset freezes or departure bans.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment to taxable profits, additional corporate income tax at 12.5%, interest from the underpayment date, administrative penalties for non-documentation, and potential criminal liability for deliberately false TP declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve fictitious TP policies can be sued for breach of fiduciary duty; where the mispricing was intentional, they may face imprisonment, disqualification and personal asset recovery for amounts diverted to related parties.
Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Failures

Statutory sanction: Criminal law sanctions under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law L.188(I)/2007, including imprisonment, substantial fines, confiscation of proceeds, freezing orders, and de-registration of the corporate entity.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers can be personally investigated and prosecuted; courts may impose travel bans, passport surrender, disqualification from holding company offices, and personal confiscation of unlawfully obtained assets.
Unlawful Dividends and Insolvent Distributions

Statutory sanction: Unlawful distribution is recoverable from shareholders; the company's insolvency practitioner may compel repayment of the dividend; tax authority may treat the distribution as fully taxable income and impose penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise unlawful distributions are jointly and severally liable to repay the company and face civil recovery proceedings; in egregious cases, they may be disqualified and prohibited from leaving the jurisdiction pending restitution.

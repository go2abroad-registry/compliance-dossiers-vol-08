Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Ley 1258 de 2008; authorized, subscribed and paid capital are freely determined in bylaws. Payment terms are flexible, but subscribed capital must be paid within the term fixed in the incorporation document; no bank escrow is required for standard S.A.S. formation. However, to act as general partner of a Colombian private equity fund, the SFC imposes ongoing minimum capital/patrimony requirements verified at the fund-licensing stage (Decreto 1242 de 2013; SFC Circular Básica Contable).

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification ('Certificado de Bomberos') obtained before operation of the physical office, covering emergency exits, extinguishers, electrical network and evacuation plan; structural/occupancy clearance evidenced via the lease plus 'Certificado de Uso del Suelo' from the municipal planning authority; occupational health and safety system (SG-SST) registered with the respective ARL (Administradora de Riesgos Laborales) pursuant to Decreto 1072 de 2015, including mandatory occupational risk class selection prior to hiring staff.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - official portal: www.dian.gov.co; transactional portal (Muisca/SIE): www.muisca.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A Colombian S.A.S. obtains its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) automatically upon formal incorporation: (1) execute public deed before a Colombian notary; (2) register the company with the Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce); (3) DIAN assigns the NIT through the Chamber of Commerce registration integration; (4) the legal representative and the company must complete online RUT (Registro Único Tributario) activation and fingerprint verification via the Muisca portal; (5) non-resident shareholders must appoint a domiciled legal representative in Colombia with registered power of attorney; (6) update RUT with the 'responsable de IVA' status and any ICA registration as applicable. There is no minimum capital requirement for SAS, and 100% foreign ownership is permitted. Foreign companies without a local permanent presence that must invoice or act in Colombia obtain a separate non-resident NIT through a DIAN-registered proxy.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a Colombian S.A.S. is an autonomous corporate income taxpayer under the ordinary regime (Régimen Ordinario). No pass-through or transparent treatment is available for income tax purposes at the entity level. Corporate income tax is levied at the statutory rate of 35%, and outbound dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders may be subject to additional dividend withholding under Colombian domestic law and applicable tax treaty provisions. — A Colombian S.A.S. obtains its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) automatically upon formal incorporation: (1) execute public deed before a Colombian notary; (2) register the company with the Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce); (3) DIAN assigns the NIT through the Chamber of Commerce registration integration; (4) the legal representative and the company must complete online RUT (Registro Único Tributario) activation and fingerprint verification via the Muisca portal; (5) non-resident shareholders must appoint a domiciled legal representative in Colombia with registered power of attorney; (6) update RUT with the 'responsable de IVA' status and any ICA registration as applicable. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Colombian S.A.S. is an autonomous corporate income taxpayer under the ordinary regime (Régimen Ordinario). No pass-through or transparent treatment is available for income tax purposes at the entity level. Corporate income tax is levied at the statutory rate of 35%, and outbound dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders may be subject to additional dividend withholding under Colombian domestic law and applicable tax treaty provisions.

Available election forms

Colombia does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Mandatory corporate taxation applies to all S.A.S. entities. Limited statutory elections include: (i) the Simplified Taxation Regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación - SIMPLES) under Law 2010/2019, available for qualifying small businesses with gross income up to 105,000 UVT, which replaces ordinary income tax and VAT reporting with a combined presumptive tax; (ii) elections between annual and bimonthly/quarterly IVA filing frequencies based on gross income thresholds and responsible-party category; (iii) formal disclosure of hybrid mismatch arrangements for BEPS Action 2 purposes (Law 2277/2022) where the S.A.S. participates in cross-border hybrid instruments; and (iv) designation of the foreign-exchange intermediary bank for export proceeds repatriation. There is no consolidated income tax return regime or group relief election for unrelated venture-capital fund structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Commercial legal entities such as a Colombian S.A.S. are mandatory IVA-responsible parties (responsables del régimen común) whenever they carry out any taxable act in Colombian territory; there is no general de minimis turnover exemption for corporations. Natural persons and informal merchants are excluded from VAT-responsible status where their gross income is below 3,500 UVT per year (COP 174,296,500 for tax year 2025, UVT = COP 49,799). A venture-capital GP entity issuing management fee invoices from Colombia is therefore required to register for IVA from inception, regardless of turnover. Foreign digital-service suppliers are separately required to register for VAT on digital services when providing services to Colombian non-VAT-responsible individuals or entities, with no turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for 0% VAT when the goods physically leave Colombian customs territory under an accepted export declaration, the exporter holds a valid RUT with export status, and the foreign currency proceeds are negotiated/repatriated within the legal deadline. Services qualify as zero-rated exported services (servicios exportados, Article 481 E.T.) where: (i) the service is rendered to a foreign recipient without domicile in Colombia; (ii) the service is not deemed to be used or exploited in Colombia; (iii) the contract is executed in compliance with Colombian foreign-exchange and transfer-pricing rules; and (iv) the benefit of the service is materially received abroad. For a venture-capital GP, offshore management, administration, advisory, and carried-interest-related services performed for a fund organized outside Colombia may qualify, provided the recipient has no Colombian domicile and the service is genuinely consumed in the fund's jurisdiction. Intellectual-property and software-development services exported by a Colombian SAS also qualify if the title/beneficial use passes abroad. The VAT credit mechanism applies to input VAT for exported goods/services, allowing subsequent refund or compensation through DIAN.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) filed through DIAN/VUCE and validated in the SIGLO XXI/SOFIA customs system
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing physical exit of the goods from Colombian customs territory
  • Commercial export invoice with mandatory CUFE e-invoice stamping identifying the exporter and foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (certificado de giro de divisas) issued by the Colombian intermediary bank proving collection and repatriation of foreign currency
  • Service export contract (for offshore management fees / carried-interest / advisory services) specifying the non-resident beneficiary, scope of service, and place of effective use abroad, together with evidence that the service was not exploited in Colombia
  • Valid RUT and NIT registration certificates of the exporter and any required certificates (e.g., export marketing certificate or proof of the foreign beneficiary's non-domiciled status)

Colombia mandates real-time fiscal clearance through DIAN e-invoicing (factura electrónica de venta - FEV). All IVA-responsible parties must: (1) obtain authorization and invoice-set ranges (sets de facturación) from DIAN; (2) issue invoices exclusively through DIAN-authorized software or DIAN's free web application; (3) transmit the XML invoice to DIAN via SOAP/REST web services before or simultaneously with delivery to the client; (4) include the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) or CUDS digital stamp and QR code; and (5) process the client's digital acceptance/rejection event within 48 hours of transmission. Credit notes, debit notes, and equivalent documents follow the same real-time validation. For venture-capital GP fee billing, e-invoicing applies to every management fee, carried-interest arrangement, and expense rebate regardless of amount; the system imposes interoperability via the national RADIAN electronic-receivable infrastructure.

If DIAN determines that the zero-rating or VAT refund claim is false, inexact, or unsupported (e.g., simulated service exports, untimely repatriation, incomplete documentation, or proceeds never collected), the following consequences apply: (i) retrospective assessment of the 19% IVA differential, recalculated from the original invoice date, plus default interest (intereses moratorios); (ii) sanción por inexactitud under Article 648 E.T., as amended by Law 2277/2022, equal to 100% to 200% of the higher tax determined; (iii) additional fines for issuing irregular e-invoices or non-compliant export documentation, including cancellation of invoice-set authorizations; (iv) exclusion or suspension from exporter status and denial of future VAT refund/compensation requests until correction; (v) criminal liability for tax evasion, customs fraud, or money laundering under Law 1762/2015 if intentional falsity or smuggling is established, carrying imprisonment and substantial fines; and (vi) joint liability of the legal representative and auditors where wrongful signatures or certifications are involved. DIAN heavily audits cross-border service-export claims by venture-capital and knowledge-process entities, requiring contemporaneous evidence of the overseas benefit and full foreign-exchange trail.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso operates under a managed floating exchange-rate system and is freely convertible. Current-account transactions are generally performed without prior central bank authorisation, while capital-account operations are liberalised but must be reported and, where applicable, settled through authorised intermediaries in the Mercado Cambiario. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident investors are legally permitted after local tax compliance, including payment of income tax and any remittance tax, and are conditional on previous registration of the foreign investment with Banco de la República and submission of audited financial statements and the required tax clearance certificate. The main hurdles are regulatory registration, documentary formalities, and indirect tax costs rather than exchange-control rationing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The GP itself does not generally enter into Colombian export transactions. If the GP, a managed fund, or a special-purpose export entity invoices foreign clients for software development or digital services, Colombian exchange law requires export proceeds received in foreign currency to be repatriated and channelled through the authorized foreign exchange market. The statutory standard is collection and channeling within six months from the shipment or service-completion date, with possible Banco de la República sanctioned extensions. The mandatory conversion ratio for eligible export proceeds is 100% of the foreign-currency amount; non-compliance can trigger exchange-fine proceedings by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. Exports arising from venture-capital portfolio operations should follow the same regime when the operative exporter is a Colombian entity.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia); Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) as the supervisory authority for the financial system; legal framework consisting of Ley 9 de 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83 of Banco de la República.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs are not authorised in Colombia as deposit-taking banks or as principal custodians of venture-fund assets. They cannot effectively satisfy the foreign-investment registration requirements, FX channeling obligations, or SFC oversight expectations. They may serve only as supplementary euro or USD disbursement channels for minor operational payments, not as the main rail for capital calls, FX conversion, or profit distributions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident investors must be routed exclusively through SWIFT or correspondent banking to a segregated operational account at a Colombian authorised exchange intermediary. Each inbound remittance must reference the relevant capital-call notice and must be accompanied by the investor's foreign-investment registration filing with Banco de la República through Form No. 4 / Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional, using the appropriate DCIN-83 purpose code for contributions to private capital funds. The Colombian managed fund or GP must verify the investor's legal name, tax identification, country of residence, source of funds, and exact capital-allocation reference before crediting the account. Matching is made against the capital-call certificate; no cash payments, crypto-asset transfers, or unregulated intermediaries are acceptable. The bank will generate an FX declaration or domestic exchange-documentation record, and the investment must remain traceable from the foreign investor's account through the Colombian fund's local account to downstream portfolio disbursements.The primary clearing rail for outbound cross-border trade and investment disbursements is SWIFT MT103 for wire transfers and MT202 COV for bank-to-bank settlement, with full beneficiary and purpose references. For local peso disbursements, Colombian financial institutions clear through the Banco de la República-operated CUD system, ACH Colombia, and real-time low-value clearing rails. Commercial letters of credit remain available for portfolio companies requiring trade-finance-backed inventory or receivables, issued under ICC UCP600 via a Colombian bank using MT700/SWIFT messages. For a venture-capital GP, letters of credit are not standard capital-disbursement instruments; they are retained only for underlying portfolio trade or working-capital transactions. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Medium as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs are not authorised in Colombia as deposit-taking banks or as principal custodians of venture-fund assets. They cannot effectively satisfy the foreign-investment registration requirements, FX channeling obligations, or SFC oversight expectations. They may serve only as supplementary euro or USD disbursement channels for minor operational payments, not as the main rail for capital calls, FX conversion, or profit distributions.

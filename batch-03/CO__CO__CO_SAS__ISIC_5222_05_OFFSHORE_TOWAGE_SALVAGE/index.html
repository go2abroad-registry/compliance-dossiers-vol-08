Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – official national tax and customs authority; portal: www.dian.gov.co; electronic transactional platform: MUISCA (Modelo Unificado de Ingresos, Servicios Tributarios y Aduaneros) at www.muisca.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity intending to operate as a Colombian S.A.S. must: (1) appoint a legal representative or attorney-in-fact domiciled in Colombia; (2) execute the bylaws and register the S.A.S. in the mercantile registry of the competent Cámara de Comercio; (3) obtain the Certificate of Existence and Legal Representation; (4) enroll in the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) before DIAN, thereby obtaining the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria); (5) select all relevant tax obligations in the RUT, including IVA, ICA (industry and commerce tax) and, if applicable, national registration as responsible de IVA; and (6) obtain a valid digital signature (firma electrónica) from a DIAN-authorized certification authority to enable electronic invoicing and MUISCA filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity intending to operate as a Colombian S.A.S. must: (1) appoint a legal representative or attorney-in-fact domiciled in Colombia; (2) execute the bylaws and register the S.A.S. in the mercantile registry of the competent Cámara de Comercio; (3) obtain the Certificate of Existence and Legal Representation; (4) enroll in the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) before DIAN, thereby obtaining the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria); (5) select all relevant tax obligations in the RUT, including IVA, ICA (industry and commerce tax) and, if applicable, national registration as responsible de IVA; and (6) obtain a valid digital signature (firma electrónica) from a DIAN-authorized certification authority to enable electronic invoicing and MUISCA filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not adopt the U.S. check-the-box regime; a S.A.S. is always treated as a juridical person subject to entity-level taxation. Statutory elections available to a qualifying S.A.S. include the optional Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE, Articles 903–916 E.T.) for eligible small enterprises with gross revenues below 80,000 UVT, and optional customs/export-treatment elections such as inclusion in a permanent or U.T. zone regime when authorized by DIAN and the National Tax and Customs Directorate. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized under Colombian tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold applies to S.A.S. legal entities; registration as a responsible de IVA in the RUT is mandatory before carrying out any taxable act in Colombia. The regulatory threshold of 1,000 UVT (approx. COP 47,324,000 for tax year 2025) only relieves certain non-merchant natural persons from electronic invoicing obligations, not corporate entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Colombia under the customs regime are VAT zero-rated. For services, zero-rating applies only to services that are 'exported services' as defined by Article 481 E.T.: services rendered by a Colombian IVA-responsible taxpayer to a non-resident recipient with no domicile in Colombia, fully developed in Colombian territory, and benefitting exclusively from abroad. In the context of deep-sea tugboat towage and maritime salvage, the service must be supplied to a foreign principal or operating beyond Colombian territorial waters, and the entire economic benefit must be realized abroad. The exporter must be enrolled in RUT, issue electronic invoices, and retain contractual and payment evidence demonstrating the foreign destination of the service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) filed with DIAN under the Colombian customs statute when vessels or towed equipment physically leave Colombia
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill evidencing shipment from a Colombian port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas) or bank intermediary credit certificate evidencing foreign exchange collection
  • Offshore towage/salvage contract and documentary proof that the recipient is a non-domiciled party and the service is used exclusively abroad

Electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta) is mandatory for all S.A.S. taxpayers. Invoices must be generated through DIAN-authorized software or an authorized technology provider, transmitted in real time to DIAN via the MUISCA/RADIAN platform, validated by DIAN's fiscal control system, and accompanied by a digital stamp and electronic signature. Export invoices must include the corresponding foreign-trade documentation number; failure to obtain valid electronic acceptance renders the invoice legally ineffective for VAT deduction and export zero-rating.

DIAN may scrutinize export zero-rating claims for 5 years from the tax return due date. Unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating results in retroactive VAT collection, monetary interest, and penalties of up to 200% of the omitted tax under Article 648 E.T. Misrepresentation of an export may also trigger customs sanctions under Decree 1165/2019, loss of VAT refund rights, exclusion from trusted exporter programs, and criminal tax fraud liability under Article 434-A of the Colombian Criminal Code, including imprisonment for the legal representative in egregious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a free-floating market-based exchange system under the Régimen de Cambios Internacionales (Foreign Exchange Regime). Current account transactions, including towage and salvage service receipts, are fully convertible and may be settled freely, provided they are channeled through an authorized foreign-exchange intermediary when converted into Colombian pesos. Capital account transactions (foreign direct investment inflows, shareholder loans, and outbound investment) are also permitted but require registration with Banco de la República through the foreign-exchange declaration (Formulario No. 12). Profit remittance and dividend payout to non-resident shareholders are lawful without exchange-control approval once the local company has satisfied corporate income tax obligations and the foreign-exchange registration for the underlying FDI is current; however, remittances of pure capital gains absent adequate tax filing may be blocked by DIAN and banking compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For services exports (including deep-sea towage and emergency salvage), the statutory repatriation term is six months from the accrual/performance date. The exporter must channel the foreign-currency proceeds through the mercado cambiario by selling 100% of the collected amount to an authorized bank or broker in local currency, unless the proceeds are eligible for a permitted exception (e.g., prior-authorized maintenance of funds in an offshore operational account for coverage of voyage or salvage-related disbursements). Goods exports are subject to a four-month surrender period. Failure to repatriate or the unauthorised accumulation of foreign currency abroad triggers DIAN exercise of exchange-control surcharges and penalties.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank), with supervised foreign-exchange information reporting through the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); banking supervision by Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (conditional). Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Digital Wallet Platforms, multi-currency IBAN providers) are viable as a secondary collection layer for foreign-currency receivables and for urgent supplier payments outside Colombia, but they are not authorized local FX intermediaries and cannot legally settle Colombian peso-denominated payroll, tax, or social-security obligations. Decree 2555/2010 requires a banking or special authorization for deposit-taking and clearing; therefore any EMI must operate under a partnership with a licensed Colombian bank for COP clearing. EMI transactions trigger enhanced KYC and enhanced sanctions screening, so use them strictly for trade/advance receivables and never for escrow of salvage awards or capital injections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfers for inbound capital injection must be executed via SWIFT MT103/202 from the shareholder's foreign bank to the SAS's Operational Account at an authorized Colombian bank. The remittance instruction must cite the capital contribution purpose code under the Colombian Exchange Regime (Concept Code 1101, Inversión Internacional para Inversión Directa). The receiving bank will generate the mandatory Formulario No. 12 'Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional' prior to conversion to COP and funds availability. The bank also verifies the apostilled corporate documents, RUT, and the registered investment before issuing the 'Registro de Inversión Internacional' certificate from Banco de la República. Local law requires that the capital injection be recorded with the DIAN and the central bank within the applicable reporting term; funds shall not be released to external escrow until the registration is complete.Outbound trade settlements for salvage and towage operations shall use SWIFT MT103 for direct payments to port agents, bunker suppliers, insurers, and crew-payroll processors. SWIFT MT202 is used for interbank cover transfers when the beneficiary instructs payment through a correspondent bank. For large salvage awards or time-charter payments involving a security mechanism, a Commercial Documentary Letter of Credit (L/C) under ICC UCP 600 is acceptable, with the local bank issuing or confirming the L/C. All outbound payments must be supported by underlying invoices and, where applicable, a DIAN foreign-exchange declaration; the SAS may maintain foreign-currency accounts abroad for voyage disbursements provided that repatriation to Colombia occurs at the end of the six-month service cycle. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% general corporate income tax rate for CY2025 under Article 240 of the Colombian Tax Code (E.T.), applicable to Sociedades por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders on profits already subject to corporate income tax in Colombia (Article 242-1 E.T.). If the distributing entity has not paid the full 35% on the underlying profit due to an exemption, an additional tax of up to 35% may be assessed at distribution. A 5% treaty rate is common under an applicable double tax agreement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The filing deadline is set by an annual DIAN Resolution and is staggered according to the last digit of the taxpayer's NIT (tax identification number). For calendar-year 2025 filings, the return is due in the first filing period (generally April-May 2026) for entities with NITs ending in a low digit; large taxpayers and those with foreign parent entities are included in earlier tiers.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation applies for taxable year 2025 when the taxpayer's gross income exceeds COP 4,183 million (84,000 UVT), or when total consolidated group revenue exceeds COP 400 billion, or when cross-border related-party transactions exceed COP 230 million per counterparty. The transfer-pricing return (Form 120) and documentation forms (Form 115 Master File and Form 116 Local File) must be filed with DIAN when any of these thresholds is met.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not have a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation (ESR); however, economic substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule in Article 869 E.T., the abuse-of-corporate-form doctrine under Law 1258/2008, and the effective-beneficial-owner condition in every DTA. A Colombian S.A.S. holding foreign towage and salvage contracts must demonstrate actual business substance: vessel ownership on the Colombian flag or registered under DIMAR, substantive crew payroll, Colombian board decisions, local bank accounts, and quotidian operational management in Colombia. If the S.A.S. is wholly foreign-owned and merely passes income from foreign wreck-removal contracts without real local control, DIAN and DIMAR may re-characterize the entity as a fictious shell, reallocating its income to the foreign parent under transfer-pricing rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Understatement

Statutory sanction: Under Article 340 of Law 1819/2016, tax evasion carries imprisonment of 4 to 9 years and a fine of 100% to 200% of the evaded tax. DIAN additionally imposes a penalty of 20% or 30% of the omitted income under Article 648 E.T., plus monthly arrears interest on the additional tax assessed.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and abandonment-partner of the S.A.S. are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax under Article 794 E.T. Criminal conviction triggers personal travel bans, disqualification from serving as legal representative of any Colombian commercial company, and civil restitution for the full tax deficiency and public-credit losses. In severe cases, the director may be summoned to an extradition-proceedings precautionary detention.
Shell Company / Abuse of Corporate Form

Statutory sanction: A judge may pierce the corporate veil under the S.A.S. law (Law 1258/2008) and hold the shareholder and administrators personally liable for the company's obligations. DIAN can disregard the entity for income tax purposes, recharacterize all removed income as taxable to the foreign shareholder, and impose a penalty equal to 100% of the corrected tax. DIMAR may revoke the entity's towage and salvage operating license.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil lifting directly exposes directors and shareholders to unlimited civil liability for the S.A.S.'s tax debts, contract liabilities, and salvage-related tort claims. A director convicted of misuse of corporate form faces a five-year prohibition on managing companies, judicial embargo of personal assets, and an exit ban until all DIAN claims are guaranteed or paid.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Article 260-10 E.T., a penalty of 5% of the transaction value (minimum 10 UVT) applies for each materially incorrect or unfiled transfer-pricing declaration. Failure to submit the documentation allows DIAN to make a primary adjustment and add the 35% tax on the adjusted income plus a 20% penalty on the under-stated tax and monthly arrears interest. Manifestly false documentation can attract criminal penalties for fraudulent declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors and tax advisors who sign or endorse false transfer-pricing studies are jointly liable for the adjusted tax and penalties under Article 260-10 E.T. Criminal indictment for aggravated tax fraud can follow if the false documentation is used to shift more than COP 1,000 million of taxable income. Personal liability includes disgorgement of the tax benefit, civil fines, and disqualification from serving as director of any Colombian-regulated financial or maritime entity.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under Law 526/1999 and UIAF Circulars can reach up to 2,500 monthly legal minimum wages (over COP 3.2 billion for 2025). DIMAR may suspend the towage vessel registration and revoke the coastal towage and salvage concession. If funds are linked to drug trafficking or organized crime, the Colombian antioffshore asset-forfeiture Law 1708/2014 authorizes seizure of the S.A.S.'s bank accounts and vessels.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or recklessly ignore AML controls face a personal penalty of 5 to 10 years imprisonment, travel bans, and a lifetime prohibition on managing maritime or financial-activity companies. Additionally, the director may be subjected to asset forfeiture proceedings and required to pay civil restitution for all laundered gross proceeds attributable to the towage and salvage contracts.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας); TAXISnet portal: https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy; export customs administration: Cyprus Customs and Excise Department, Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

For a Cyprus private limited company, incorporation with the Registrar of Companies is followed by automatic allocation of a Tax Identification Code (TIC) by the Department of Taxation; no separate application is required for income tax. For VAT, an online application must be submitted on TAXISnet using Form VAT 101 where taxable supplies exceed EUR 15,600 in a rolling 12-month period or exceed that threshold at any earlier point. A foreign entity without a Cyprus establishment that has VATable supplies or income-tax obligations in Cyprus must file a non-resident tax registration request with the Department of Taxation, submitting certified copies of its foreign certificate of incorporation, memorandum/articles of association, passport details of directors, local tax representative appointment, and a statement of expected Cyprus-source activities. The resulting Cyprus TIN/VAT number must be quoted on all invoices, customs declarations, and filing correspondence.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A private limited company is an entity-level taxpayer under corporate income tax. Available statutory elections that may alter the compliance outcome include: group loss relief election under the Income Tax Law for 75% group companies; deemed dividend distribution under the Special Contribution for Defence legislation; notional interest deduction election on new equity subscribed in cash; intellectual property box election under the IP regime after 1 July 2016; and hybrid mismatch adjustment elections under the ATAD implementation where the company must disclose hybrid instruments/entities and can elect to re-characterise income or expense to neutralise a mismatch.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus applies the euro as its legal tender; capital and current account transactions are fully liberalised under the free movement of capital provisions of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, repatriation of equity, loan repayments and royalty payments by a Cyprus private limited company to non-residents are permissible without exchange control approval, provided the distribution passes the Companies Law Cap.113 solvency and distributable profits test and applicable withholding tax or deemed dividend assessments are considered. The Central Bank of Cyprus maintains only balance-of-payments statistical monitoring and anti-money-laundering reporting duties; it does not impose convertibility restrictions, currency surrender requirements or prior permitting for bona fide cross-border payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds under Cypriot or Eurosystem regulations. All proceeds from export of handmade sharp-edge epoxy resin dice and tabletop gaming accessories may be retained in any convertible currency in a Cyprus domestic banking account or in corresponding nostro arrangements, subject solely to customer due diligence thresholds and the obligation to submit statistical balance-of-payments returns to the Central Bank of Cyprus where the bank triggers reporting requirements. Receipts from online marketplaces such as Kickstarter and PayPal that are settled into a Cyprus corporate account are treated as ordinary export/online-sales collection and are not subject to any mandated conversion timeline.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) acting as the national financial supervisory authority within the European System of Central Banks and the Eurosystem

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM, with significant legal constraints. An offshore electronic money institution (EMI) holding a European Economic Area passport may lawfully provide payment services into Cyprus and open an e-money wallet for the company. That wallet can serve as a settlement rail for Kickstarter, PayPal, Stripe and direct e-commerce payments, but it is not a full bank account and cannot provide lending, overdrafts, transactional FX hedging or execution of SWIFT MT103 on the same legal footing as a credit institution. A non-EEA offshore EMI is blocked from operating in Cyprus without a Cyprus or EEA authorization; any such provider must be bypassed in the passive treasury architecture. All EMI balances must be ring-fenced under safeguarding rules, and the Cyprus corporation must maintain its primary legal-entity liquidity, corporate VAT and payroll flows through a licensed domestic commercial bank to avoid AML stigmatisation and to ensure the physical-substance expectations of both the Cyprus bank and the upstream payment processors are satisfied.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital should be injected by SWIFT MT103 or domestic instant Euro payment to the Cyprus operational account, with the bank instruction clearly identifying the sender as the non-resident shareholder and narrative field referencing: 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital Contribution to CY_LTD, HE77'. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual TD200 return for a calendar tax year must be filed by 31 March of the following tax year. For a company whose taxable year ends on 31 December, the statutory deadline is thus 31 March. Companies are also required to submit provisional tax computations and payments in two installments: 75% by 1 August and 25% by 15 December of the current tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are required for tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2022 where the aggregate value of controlled transactions with related parties exceeds EUR 1,000,000 in that tax year; a Master File is additionally required where the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds EUR 10,000,000 and the group turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000. Country-by-Country reporting applies where the consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000. The threshold currency is the Euro (EUR).

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has no separate Economic Substance Regulations filing regime comparable to the UAE ESR. However, Cyprus tax residence depends on central management and control being exercised in Cyprus. For a Cypriot limited company manufacturing tabletop dice, the company must maintain a substantive presence: Cyprus office or premises, physical inventory or operations if relevant, local management decisions, board meeting minutes in Cyprus, local employee presence, and substance aligned to income. The Cyprus IP Box regime applies the OECD modified nexus approach, requiring qualifying R&D and active decision-making in Cyprus. A shell company with address-only presence, a nominee director, no employees, and no operational activity risks losing Cyprus tax residence and having its profits treated as sourced in the shareholders' home jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imposition of additional tax up to twice the tax evaded, interest at 5.75% per annum, administrative fines up to EUR 20,000 and criminal prosecution under the Cyprus Income Tax Law and Tax Assessment and Collection Laws. The court may impose imprisonment of up to five years for fraudulent evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false returns, directed the diversion of sales proceeds, or permitted the use of the company as a sham are personally and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties and interest. Civil restitution may be ordered from the director's personal assets; travel bans may be issued by the Cyprus District Court where tax debts remain unpaid; in flagrant cases directors face criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Cyprus may deny tax residence and recharacterize the entity as managed and controlled abroad; the Cyprus Tax Department may disregard the entity for tax purposes, reassess the income in the actual shareholder's jurisdiction, and apply 12.5% plus default interest and penalties. The company may also be struck off the register and dissolved for failure to satisfy commercial substance obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell company may be personally liable on the basis of fraudulent incorporation, misrepresentation to tax authorities, or operating the company as a facade. The corporate veil may be pierced; directors owed a personal fiduciary duty to creditors and tax authorities can be subjected to civil asset freezes, disqualification orders, restrictions on leaving the jurisdiction while the tax investigation is active, and criminal sanctions for aiding tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Cyprus Tax Department may make a transfer pricing adjustment to chargeable income as if the controlled transaction had been conducted at arm's length; additional tax of 12.5% on the adjusted amount, default interest of 5.75% per annum, and an administrative penalty of 10% of the adjusted amount where documentation is absent or inaccurate. Criminal sanctions may apply if false documentation is knowingly submitted.

Director personal liability: Directors who oversaw the pricing policy and signed off on inadequate or fabricated TP documentation may be held personally liable for the additional tax and penalties where the documentation was drafted to mislead. Courts may issue director disqualification orders, asset tracing measures, and travel prohibitions during audit proceedings; in egregious cases the adjustment can be treated as conspiracy to defraud the tax authority.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines imposed under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Law (L188(I)/2007, as amended), up to EUR 350,000 per violation for legal persons and imprisonment for responsible individuals of up to five years; GDPR/AML sanctions may also apply for omission of UBO filings with the Registrar of Companies.

Director personal liability: Company directors are personally liable for UBO register filing obligations and AML compliance. If a director consciously permitted a nominee facade or allowed unaudited cash flows to conceal the ultimate beneficial owner, the court may pierce the corporate veil, impose personal fines, grant civil recovery orders, impose travel bans, and sentence the director to imprisonment for money laundering facilitation.
Late Filing and Payment Defaults

Statutory sanction: Automatic late filing penalties: EUR 100 fixed plus EUR 50 per month, capped at EUR 600; interest on unpaid tax at 5.75% per annum; if the default is persistent, the Commissioner may issue tax assessments based on estimated income and a surcharge up to 10% of the assessed tax; court proceedings may follow for enforcement and collection.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly leaves the company in default, or uses the corporate structure to strip profits while leaving the tax liability unpaid, may be held liable for the company tax debt under the 'wrongful trading' and 'fraudulent preference' doctrines. Personal liability extends to civil restitution, automatic travel bans while the company remains in tax collections, disqualification from directorship for up to three years, and imprisonment where non-payment was part of a deliberate evasion scheme.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Colombian S.A.S. The incorporators freely fix the subscribed capital in the bylaws; share capital must be fully paid within one year of subscription (Art. 5, Ley 1258 de 2008). No escrow account is mandated at the national level; deferred cash contributions and in-kind contributions must be expressly described with valuation in the incorporation document.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office must obtain the fire-safety certificate of the Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos under Ley 1575 de 2012; satisfy the structural load, electrical, and egress conditions of the building; implement the workplace safety plan (SG-SST) under Decreto 1072 de 2015; and maintain emergency exits, portable extinguishers, smoke detection, and first-aid provisions proportionate to the office headcount.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Colombian S.A.S. The incorporators freely fix the subscribed capital in the bylaws; share capital must be fully paid within one year of subscription (Art. 5, Ley 1258 de 2008). No escrow account is mandated at the national level; deferred cash contributions and in-kind contributions must be expressly described with valuation in the incorporation document.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN). Official self-service portal: Muisca (muisca.dian.gov.co); general portal: www.dian.gov.co; e-invoicing endpoint: factura.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned S.A.S.: (1) Incorporate by private document and file for registry with the Cámara de Comercio of the city of operations, including the online pre-RUT submitted with the incorporation; (2) Upon Chamber registration, DIAN auto-assigns the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and issues the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) consolidating income tax, VAT, and customs status; (3) Appoint the local legal representative, tax accountant and, if legally triggered, the revisor fiscal; (4) Obtain the qualified electronic signature (certificado de firma digital) from an authorized CA (e.g., Certicámara, GSE, Vita) and activate the DIAN e-invoicing program; (5) Complete the UBO/beneficial-owner filing and open a local bank account with foreign-exchange code for export proceeds. Foreign shareholders must evidence passport, foreign tax ID, and address, and file the FATCA/CRS tax-domicile self-certification.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the S.A.S. is a limited-liability corporation taxed at entity level on Colombian-source and worldwide income (Impuesto sobre la Renta, 35% for FY 2024) and subject to the 19% IVA regime; pass-through treatment is unavailable to corporations, although the optional SIMPLE regime may consolidate income and payroll taxes for eligible small entities. — For a foreign-owned S.A.S.: (1) Incorporate by private document and file for registry with the Cámara de Comercio of the city of operations, including the online pre-RUT submitted with the incorporation; (2) Upon Chamber registration, DIAN auto-assigns the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and issues the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) consolidating income tax, VAT, and customs status; (3) Appoint the local legal representative, tax accountant and, if legally triggered, the revisor fiscal; (4) Obtain the qualified electronic signature (certificado de firma digital) from an authorized CA (e.g., Certicámara, GSE, Vita) and activate the DIAN e-invoicing program; (5) Complete the UBO/beneficial-owner filing and open a local bank account with foreign-exchange code for export proceeds. Foreign shareholders must evidence passport, foreign tax ID, and address, and file the FATCA/CRS tax-domicile self-certification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the S.A.S. is a limited-liability corporation taxed at entity level on Colombian-source and worldwide income (Impuesto sobre la Renta, 35% for FY 2024) and subject to the 19% IVA regime; pass-through treatment is unavailable to corporations, although the optional SIMPLE regime may consolidate income and payroll taxes for eligible small entities.

Available election forms

U.S.-style check-the-box election does not exist under Colombian law. Statutory elections available: (i) SIMPLE regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación, Law 2010/2019 and Law 2277/2022) for entities with annual gross income ≤ 95,000 UVT; (ii) hybrid-mismatch alignment election under Article 585-1 E.T. (Law 2277/2022) allowing the domestic taxpayer to adjust deductions or inclusions where a hybrid entity or instrument creates a deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction result with a foreign jurisdiction; (iii) election for a foreign non-resident to operate through a Colombian permanent establishment instead of capitalizing a local S.A.S.; and (iv) mutual agreement procedure (MAP) elections in transfer-pricing disputes. No check-the-box recognition of foreign U.S. partnership or S-corporation classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for services: under Article 930 E.T., every business providing taxable services in Colombia — including ISIC 7410 UI/UX design subscription services — must register as an IVA responsible party (RUT/NIT) before the first transaction, regardless of turnover. The petty-trader simplification (annual sales ≤ 600 UVT) applies only to individual sellers of goods, not to corporate service providers. The SIMPLE regime (elected up to 95,000 UVT annual gross income) does not waive full VAT registration; it only consolidates the income-tax and payroll-tax side.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating under Article 481 E.T. for services requires: (1) the contractual recipient is a non-Colombian company or entity with no permanent establishment in Colombia; (2) the service is executed in Colombia but used and economically exploited exclusively abroad (for design-as-a-service this is demonstrated by deliverables sent to the foreign UI/UX client, a global SaaS subscription model, and foreign-market licensing/usage); (3) the fee is actually collected in convertible foreign currency through the Colombian foreign-exchange market; and (4) the service is not excluded by Article 481's specific list or subject to the local digital-services VAT block. Law 2277/2022 tightens the cross-border digital-services test: the service must not ultimately be used by a Colombian-based user of the foreign client. The exporter must maintain a service-export file (expediente) with the contract, legalized invoice, exchange declaration, and the client's tax-domicile certificate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX, electronic Form 92 DIAN) for physical goods; for services, the Colombian 'Declaración de Exportación de Servicios' registered in the RUT/DIAN export profile
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing physical dispatch where goods accompany the service
  • Export services contract in Spanish/English identifying the non-resident beneficiary and the exclusive foreign-use clause
  • Commercial export invoice (CUFE electronic invoice with zero-rate line and IVA exclusion code)
  • Declaración de Cambio (FX settlement form) submitted through the Colombian authorized foreign-exchange intermediary
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT credit advice or bank statement evidencing collection from the foreign client within 180 days

Mandatory e-invoicing through DIAN-approved invoicing software or the free Muisca facturador; each invoice is transmitted in real time over DIAN web services (SOAP/REST), validated, and returned with the CUFE code and status 'validada' (tax-clearance equivalent). The invoice must contain a certified digital signature, QR/URL verification, and mandatory data fields under Resolution 165/2023. Electronic credit and debit notes and the RADIAN (Registro de Activos Digitales) clearing log complete the fiscal-control cycle; API integration and a DIAN-approved contingency procedure are mandatory for subscription-based recurring billing.

DIAN runs systematic triangulation of RUT status, CUFE invoices/RADIAN records, DEX declarations, and FX settlements. Improper zero-rating exposes the exporter to: (i) VAT assessed at 19% of the fee plus moratory interest retroactive to the billing date; (ii) 'sanción por inexactitud' of up to 200% of the greater tax due (Article 647 E.T., as amended by Law 2277/2022); (iii) criminal fiscal fraud penalties, including imprisonment of 36 to 72 months and fines of 150%-200% of the tax evaded; (iv) joint and several liability of the legal representative and revisor fiscal; and (v) foreign-exchange administrative fines for non-repatriation or non-channeling of the export proceeds beyond the 180-day deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is freely convertible at market rates referencing the TRM (representative market rate) published by the BRC. Current-account operations, including export of UI/UX design services, are liberalized but subject to mandatory channeling through authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries ('intermediarios del mercado cambiario' / IMCs) and filing of standardized exchange declarations. Capital-account flows, including foreign equity investment into a Colombian S.A.S., require no prior BRC authorization for the design-services sector but must be channeled through an IMC and registered with the BRC on Form No. 5 (Declaración de cambio por inversiones internacionales). Unregistered foreign investment cannot legally remit dividends or repatriate capital. Profit remittances are permitted once the investment is registered (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales) and after payment of the applicable dividend withholding (currently 10% on dividends paid out of already-taxed profits and 35% on dividends paid out of untaxed profits under E.T. Art. 245, as amended by Law 2277 of 2022) and company income taxes; no separate remittance tax applies. Convertibility is therefore high, but operational compliance is time-sensitive and document-intensive.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia no longer mandates forced conversion into COP; the applicable duty is repatriation and channeling. Proceeds from exported services (including DaaS subscriptions) must be collected within six (6) months from the date of service performance (Art. 12, Decree 1735 of 1993, as codified in Circular DCIN-83). Collections must be (i) channeled through an IMC and declared on Form No. 1, (ii) deposited into a BRC-authorized foreign-currency compensation account ('cuenta de compensación') held abroad, or (iii) held in a foreign-currency account at a Colombian IMC. There is no statutory surrender/ conversion ratio; 100% of proceeds may be retained in USD if held in a compliant compensation or IMC foreign-currency account. Each collection requires the IMC to file Form No. 1 with the supporting commercial invoice and service contract. Late or non-channeled export proceeds constitute a foreign-exchange infraction sanctionable by the SFC.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia ('Banco de la República' / 'BRC') is the central bank and foreign-exchange authority, with the Junta Directiva issuing monetary and exchange regulations; the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia ('SFC') supervises licensed foreign-exchange intermediaries and enforces AML/CFT (SARLAFT); DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) controls tax registration, beneficial-owner reporting, and trade/exchange declaration integrity. Core legal instruments: Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1068 of 2015, BRC Resolution 8 of 2000, and BRC Regulatory Circular DCIN-83.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High as an ancillary collection-and-conversion rail; Low as a standalone treasury solution. Colombian law recognizes only locally licensed 'Sociedades Especializadas en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos' (SEDPEs) under Law 1735 of 2014; foreign EMIs (e.g., FCA-regulated payment institutions) cannot passport banking status into Colombia. Wise's Colombian operating entity, Wise Colombia S.A.S., is an SFC-supervised SEDPE and is not a bank; balances carry no deposit insurance. The S.A.S. may use Wise to receive USD payouts from Stripe Billing, convert into COP, and sweep funds into a Tier-1 IMC account, provided every flow is reconciled to the Form No. 1/Form No. 5 channeling obligation within the six-month export-collection window. Holding export proceeds indefinitely in Wise balances without BRC authorization for a compensation account violates channeling rules. Shareholder capital injections must never be routed through Wise or Stripe; they require direct SWIFT to the IMC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity or shareholder-loan inflows must enter through a Tier-1 IMC account in the S.A.S.'s name. Routing: foreign parent → SWIFT MT103 to the IMC beneficiary account, wire reference stating purpose code 'Inversión directa de capital – suscripción de acciones' or 'préstamo de accionistas'; the IMC may convert USD to COP at the day's TRM or hold proceeds in a USD account at the IMC. The investment must be declared on Form No. 5 (Declaración de cambio por inversiones internacionales) through the IMC, which registers the investment with the BRC within five (5) business days of the transaction and issues the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales (RII) confirmation. Simplified registration applies if the investor's cumulative foreign investment is below USD 500,000 equivalent; above that threshold, the IMC requires notarized shareholder resolutions, investor identity documents, and full UBO disclosure. The S.A.S. treasury file must retain the stamped Form No. 5, the incoming MT103, the shareholder subscription minutes, and the RII certificate because the certificate is the legal precondition for all future dividend remittances and capital repatriation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer-pricing documentation applies to taxpayers carrying out transactions with foreign related parties when the aggregate value of those transactions exceeds COP 450,000,000 in the taxable year. Additional TP information returns and Local File/Master File obligations may apply based on the entity's gross income, net equity, or multinational group revenue thresholds as indexed by the DIAN UVT system.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulations registry comparable to UAE-style regimes, but DIAN applies substance-over-form, general anti-avoidance rules, and anti-abuse doctrine. An S.A.S. with no real staff, physical presence, business decision-making, or genuine economic activity may be disregarded for tax purposes, cross-border payments may be recharacterized, and DTA relief may be denied for lack of beneficial ownership or economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declarations

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion is sanctioned under Colombian Criminal Code Articles 426-427 with imprisonment of up to nine years and fines; administrative penalties include 160% of the omitted tax plus late interest and indexing. DIAN may also suspend registration and initiate criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and statutory auditors who knowingly sign false returns may be jointly and personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax. Personal assets may be attached, travel bans may be issued, and implementing officers may face imprisonment. Civil restitution may extend to the full amount of tax harm caused.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may re-price the transaction on an arm's-length basis, disallow deductible expenses, and impose an inexactness penalty of up to 160% of the additional tax. Failure to submit TP documentation triggers separate daily/UVT-based fines and potential criminal referral for tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign intercompany agreements with inflated or deflated prices may be held personally liable for the resulting tax adjustment, face director disqualification, and be exposed to criminal tax evasion charges if the conduct is proven intentional.
Shell Entity and Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the entity for tax purposes, disallow expenses paid abroad, recharacterize distributions as taxable income, impose penalties for abusiveness, and deny DTA benefits. The entity may also be reported to the Financial Information and Analysis Unit for potential money-laundering or shell-company typologies.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may lose the corporate-veil limitation if the S.A.S. is used as a sham; personal liability may include civil restitution of avoided taxes, travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment for tax fraud or criminal conspiracy.
AML and Cross-Border Payment Integrity

Statutory sanction: Money-laundering offenses under Colombian Criminal Code Article 323 carry imprisonment of 10 to 30 years and fines. Administrative AML fines and sanctions may be imposed by UIAF; failure to report foreign exchange operations may generate additional tax and customs penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and legal representatives who knowingly facilitate or fail to prevent laundering through subscription transactions face criminal liability, disqualification from commercial activity, travel bans, asset seizure, and personal restitution orders. Beneficial owners cannot shield assets through the S.A.S. corporate form.

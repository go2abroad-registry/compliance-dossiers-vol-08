Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A mere virtual office without a physical, serviceable location is not accepted as a statutory registered office under section 100 of Companies Law Cap. 113. The registered office must be a physical address in the Republic of Cyprus to which statutory communications and legal notices can validly be delivered. A licensed TCSP's or law firm's registered-office service using physical premises in Cyprus does satisfy the statutory requirement; however, the structure must demonstrate genuine commercial substance appropriate to an ISIC 3250 medical-device manufacturing operation, including decision-making oversight, local agent capacity, and availability for regulatory visits.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by Cyprus law. A shelf-ready private limited company may incorporate with one or more shares at a nominal par value (commonly EUR 1). No escrow account or physical capital deposit is required at incorporation; share capital may be credited under the subscribers' undertaking and later called by the directors.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance; TaxIS portal at https://taxportal.tax.mof.gov.cy (and Cyprus Companies Section for TIN issuance).

E-government filing portal

A Cypriot private limited company is automatically registered for income tax upon incorporation by the Registrar of Companies and receives a Tax Identification Number (TIN). For a foreign entity without a fixed place of business in Cyprus that makes taxable supplies or distance sales in Cyprus, the entity must apply to the VAT Office of the Tax Department on the prescribed form (Form TD-101 or the analogous non-established taxable person registration form) before starting taxable activity. The application must be accompanied by certified copies of the incorporation certificate and constitutional documents, evidence of the foreign entity's existence and good standing, identification of directors and ultimate beneficial owners, a Cyprus address or agent designation, and proof of the planned taxable supplies. Where the entity has no fixed establishment, the Tax Department may require appointment of a tax or fiscal representative resident in Cyprus. The application is processed through the TaxIS e-portal, and upon approval the taxpayer receives a VAT registration certificate with a unique Cyprus VAT number in the format CY + 8 digits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a check-the-box entity classification election; every private limited company is a separate taxable person for corporate income tax and VAT. Relevant elections and notifications include: (i) application to form a VAT group under the Cyprus VAT Law, (ii) election to apply the tonnage tax regime (not relevant to manufacturing), (iii) notification of the establishment of a permanent establishment for tax treaty purposes, and (iv) any EU hybrid mismatch notifications required under the Cypriot implementation of ATAD. For corporate tax, no pass-through classification is available; the entity is taxed at the normal corporate rate on worldwide income, subject to statutory territorial exemptions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for supplies of goods and services in Cyprus; EUR 35,000 annual distance sales of goods to Cyprus; EUR 10,000 annual intra-Community acquisitions (as amended by annual State Budget and VAT Regulations).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Cyprus-established taxable person may zero-rate the supply of goods exported outside the European Union if the goods are physically dispatched from Cyprus to a non-EU destination and the exporter retains reliable documentary evidence of export. Services directly related to export transport and handling may also be zero-rated. Intra-EU dispatches are not 'exports' for VAT purposes but are zero-rated as intra-Community supplies if the customer is a taxable person holding a valid VAT number in the destination Member State and the goods are transported to another Member State. For dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks, zero-rating requires a clear contractual chain, payment terms, and physical movement of goods out of Cypriot customs territory within the time limits specified in the VAT Law.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Single Administrative Document / ECS customs entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms and complete exporter/importer details
  • Proof of payment (bank transfer / inward remittance certificate) matching the invoiced amount
  • Packing list and transport documents demonstrating dispatch within stated period
  • Intrastat/VIES records if the movement is an intra-Community supply

Cyprus requires electronic submission of VAT returns, VIES recapitulative statements, and Intrastat declarations through the TaxIS portal and the CyStat/Intrastat platform. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory for suppliers contracting with Cypriot government entities under EU Directive 2014/55/EU using the PEPPOL network. There is no general B2B real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance approval requirement for VAT invoices in Cyprus. Tax invoices must be issued in a timely manner and retained in electronic or original form; for excise-controlled movement, EMCS electronic administrative documents are required.

Export zero-rating is scrutinized through cross-border data matching with EU customs and tax databases and post-clearance audits of exporters. If the Tax Department establishes that goods were not actually exported or that the documentary requirements were not timely or substantively met, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 19% VAT, giving rise to an assessment, statutory interest, and administrative penalties (which may reach up to 200% of the tax involved in cases of gross negligence or wilful default). Fraudulent export zero-rating claims or falsified export documentation may result in criminal prosecution under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law and the VAT Law, with fines and imprisonment. Any refunded input VAT attributable to the export is clawed back with interest, and further civil penalties may apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Cyprus. Cyprus is bound by the EU free movement of capital under TFEU Article 63, and both current and capital account transactions are liberalised. Exchange-control approval is not required for investment, profit remittance, dividend distribution, or trade settlement. Dividend outflows are unrestricted but are subject to Companies Law Cap. 113 solvency and realised-profits tests, Cyprus withholding tax rules (generally 17% for outbound dividends unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable tax treaty), and AML/KYC documentary verification. Practical hurdles are compliance-driven, not exchange-control-driven.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule applies in Cyprus. There is no mandatory timeline for repatriation and no required percentage of export receipts to be converted into euro. Export proceeds may be retained in the invoiced currency. Material cross-border transactions remain reportable for balance-of-payments and statistical purposes through the Central Bank of Cyprus reporting framework and CySTAT Intrastat/extra-EU trade declarations.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) as the national monetary authority and banking supervisor, operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks. The Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) has supplementary regulatory authority over investment services and forex brokers. Cyprus does not maintain a separate foreign-exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legal constraint: non-bank electronic money institutions may operate in Cyprus only if authorised in Cyprus or passporting under EU Directive 2015/2366 (PSD2) and the E-Money Directive, supervised by the Central Bank of Cyprus or the relevant EU home-state regulator. EMIs may provide payment accounts, SEPA/wire initiation, and FX conversion but may not take deposits or issue letters of credit. Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail for the Cyprus manufacturing entity; MEDIUM as a supplemental rail for low-value client collections and non-core supplier payments, subject to full UBO/KYC pass-through, nested-agent risk controls, and maintenance of at least one tier-1 domestic bank account as the anchor treasury relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: route the initial equity or shareholder-loan remittance via SWIFT MT103 from the beneficial owner's own bank account in its jurisdiction to the Cyprus company's euro-denominated account at a tier-1 Cyprus bank. The wire must identify the beneficiary Cyprus Ltd with IBAN and state a purpose reference such as 'SVCL – Share Capital Contribution' or 'INTCO – Intercompany Loan', supported by a board resolution and executed loan or subscription agreement. For EUR payments, clearing occurs through TARGET2; no exchange-control approval is required. The beneficiary bank should receive in advance the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, shareholders register, beneficial owner register, board resolution authorising the injection, source-of-funds documentation, and signed account mandate specifying the account purpose. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat corporate income tax rate on worldwide taxable income of Cyprus tax-resident companies (Income Tax Law L.118(I)/2002, as amended).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Cyprus imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Cyprus tax-resident company to non-resident shareholders; special defence contribution (SDC) on dividends and interest was abolished with effect from 1 January 2022, and non-residents were never subject to it.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

IR124 must be submitted within 15 months from the end of the accounting period (for a 31 December year-end, the return is due by 31 March of the second following year); the annual corporate tax return coexists with the Registrar of Companies annual return (Form HE32) due within 42 days of each incorporation anniversary.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 — aggregate value of controlled (related-party) transactions during a tax year triggers the mandatory preparation of a Local File and the inclusion of a transfer pricing summary within Form IR124; a group consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 triggers the Master File and Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) obligations. The Local File must be contemporaneous and available within 60 days of the Cyprus Tax Commissioner's request.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has not enacted a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute. Economic substance is nonetheless enforced through: (i) the 'management and control' tax-residence test under Section 25 of the Income Tax Law; (ii) EU ATAD adoption (interest limitation, CFC rules, anti-hybrid rules); (iii) DAC6 / CRS / CbCR disclosure regimes; (iv) the EU Code of Conduct Group 'no zero tax' substantial-activity requirement; and (v) the Cyprus Tax Department' substantive checks applied to NID, IP Box, dividend exemption, and treaty benefit claims. A dental block manufacturer must demonstrate physical premises, production equipment, full-time qualified personnel, inventory, procurement contracts, and board decision-making in Cyprus; a letterbox entity without these factors risks re-attribution of profits to foreign shareholders, denial of treaty benefits, deemed CFC income taxation, and penalty assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law (as amended): assessment on concealed profit, monthly interest at 1.75%, administrative penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded, and criminal conviction carrying a fine up to EUR 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct false returns face personal criminal prosecution, travel bans, freezing of personal bank accounts, disqualification from acting as directors for up to five years, and civil restitution through fraudulent or wrongful trading claims under the Companies Law (Cap. 113), piercing the corporate veil.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-attribution of the entire taxable profit to the foreign place of effective management; denial of the 12.5% rate, NID, IP Box, and exemption benefits; application of the ATAD CFC rules causing immediate taxation of the foreign parent; possible strike-off of the company by the Registrar for lack of bona fide commercial purpose.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for filing false residence and substance declarations; sanctions include fines up to EUR 20,000, imprisonment up to five years, travel bans, disqualification orders, and civil claims by creditors and minority shareholders for wrongful trading.
Abusive Transfer Pricing and Interest Stripping

Statutory sanction: Section 33 of the Income Tax Law empowers the Cyprus Tax Commissioner to re-determine the arm's-length taxable profit; the taxpayer faces a TP adjustment, monthly interest at 1.75%, an administrative fine up to EUR 10,000 for missing documentation, and a deemed assessment computed on the basis of production cost plus a normal manufacturing margin.

Director personal liability: Directors and finance officers who sign knowingly false transfer pricing declarations are exposed to criminal sanctions, personal recovery of the TP-related tax debt, disqualification, and civil suits for breach of fiduciary duty and fraudulent preferences.
AML / UBO Non-Compliance and Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus AML Law (L.188(I)/2007) and the Companies Law (Cap. 113, as amended): fines up to EUR 20,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years; the UBO register is publicly searchable and must be updated annually; the company's SWIFT and TT banking channels may be frozen, accounts closed, and payments blocked by correspondent banks on failure of UBO verification.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are personally criminally liable for AML/UBO omissions; travel bans, asset freezes, disqualification orders, and personal civil liability for damages caused to the company apply, constituting a direct piercing of the corporate veil.

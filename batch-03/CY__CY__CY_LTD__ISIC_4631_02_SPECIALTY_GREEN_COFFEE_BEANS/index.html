Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Private limited company in Cyprus has no statutory minimum paid-up capital under Companies Law Cap. 113; the customary subscriber capital is EUR 1,000 divided into 1,000 ordinary shares of EUR 1.00 each. No escrow bank account condition is imposed by law, and shares may be fully or partly paid; however, full payment of the subscription price at incorporation is recommended for bank due diligence and documentary credit facilities.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Compliance Certificate issued by the Cyprus Fire Service under the Fire Prevention and Means of Escape in Case of Fire Law 4(I)/1999, incorporating fire escape route certification, extinguisher system approval, and structural load inspection of the warehouse racking; Occupational Safety and Health clearance under Law 89(I)/1996 from the Department of Labour Inspection, covering a written risk assessment for forklift traffic, manual handling operations, palm oil/coffee dust controls, and emergency response planning, issued before physical occupancy of the licensed storage premises.

Paid-in capital rule: Private limited company in Cyprus has no statutory minimum paid-up capital under Companies Law Cap. 113; the customary subscriber capital is EUR 1,000 divided into 1,000 ordinary shares of EUR 1.00 each. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; online filings and registrations via TaxisNet portal (taxisnet.mof.gov.cy) and the Customs/Trade Facilitation System (THOS)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain Cypriot legal registration (Companies Section of the Department of Registrar of Companies) or confirm EU/EEA establishment status, then apply to the Tax Department for a Tax Identification Number using the corporate tax registration procedure. If taxable supplies in Cyprus are anticipated, submit the VAT registration application (Form VAT 101) to the District VAT Office. Non-established taxable persons appoint a tax representative for VAT purposes unless an EU intra-Community exemption applies. The TIN/VAT number is issued within three to six weeks upon submission of statutes, directors, proof of beneficial ownership, and bank confirmation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: Cyprus-resident companies are taxed at 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide profits; non-resident companies are taxed on Cyprus-source income only. A Cyprus Private Limited Company is not pass-through eligible; there is no check-the-box or flow-through election for corporate tax purposes.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box. Cyprus law provides a VAT grouping election under the VAT Law 119(I)/2002 (ss. 4-5) for related entities; also available are the election for tax loss group relief, provisional tax payment computation election, and anti-hybrid mismatch filing mechanisms under the Cyprus Income Tax Law (as amended by ATAD). An established or non-established taxable person may elect to appoint an indirect tax representative for VAT compliance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 (United in EUR, the Cyprus local currency) annual taxable turnover from the supply of goods or services, measured on a rolling 12-month basis; separate thresholds apply to intra-Community acquisitions and distance sales

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a supply of goods that are physically exported from Cyprus to a destination outside the EU (third country), provided (i) the seller or purchaser exports the goods out of the EU within three months from the time of supply or within the longer period allowed by the Cypriot customs authorities, (ii) the exporter retains full documentary evidence of export, (iii) the consideration is a genuine commercial export sale, and (iv) for green coffee beans, the sale is to a non-EU buyer and title passes after goods clear Cypriot/EU customs. The zero rate also applies to goods sold for export under a customs outward-processing or bonded warehousing arrangement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic via THOS/Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill as proof of actual dispatch and departure from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank transfer confirmation) evidencing FX payment receipt from the non-EU buyer

Cyprus does not currently mandate real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls (CTC) for domestic VAT. VAT returns are filed electronically via TaxisNet, and customs export declarations are routed through the Cyprus Customs THOS system. The VAT Digital Age (ViDA) EU reforms are expected to introduce mandatory e-invoicing in subsequent implementing stages, but as of the current stage no real-time fiscal API clearance mandate applies.

The Cyprus Tax Department conducts targeted audits and documentary inspections using customs export data. Fraudulent or unsupported claims of export zero-rating trigger reversal of the zero rate, VAT assessed at 19% with interest at 9% per annum, statutory fines of up to €15,000, criminal penalties including imprisonment up to three years, and disqualification of the exporter's VAT number. In serious cases, joint and several liability can extend to directors and controlling shareholders under Cyprus tax law.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full Eurozone member and has maintained uninterrupted euro (EUR) convertibility on both current and capital account since euro adoption on 1 January 2008. Capital-account transactions are unrestricted under EU law: inward equity subscriptions, intercompany loans, liquidation proceeds, royalty remittances, and profit repatriation require no Central Bank approval and are subject to no currency-conversion permit. Profit-distribution frictions are purely fiscal rather than exchange-control in nature; the 17% Special Defence Contribution on dividends, the deemed-dividend distribution rules, and the ordinary income/corporate tax framework all operate independently of FX legality. For counterparties in non-convertible or administratively restricted emerging-market currencies (for example Ethiopian birr, Ugandan shilling, or Lao kip), convertibility risk is managed at the origin-country clearing bank and does not impose any limitation on the Cypriot entity's ability to hold, convert, or remit EUR. In practice the bank may require a one-time Balance of Payments classification of an inbound capital transfer, but there is no substantive convertibility hurdle and no waiting period imposed by law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. All restrictions under the former Exchange Control Law were abolished by the time of EU accession in 2004, and EU free-movement-of-capital rules prevent re-imposition. A Cyprus company may lawfully retain export or trading receipts offshore in any freely convertible currency and may select its own settlement currency for inbound trade payments. The sole residual obligations are statistical: the receiving bank is required to capture cross-border transactions in the euro-area Balance of Payments reporting framework for the Central Bank of Cyprus, filing transaction-level data (purpose code, counterparty country, amount) on the customer's behalf. Failure to provide accurate BoP information to the bank can trigger administrative penalties at the bank level, but it does not constitute an exchange-control breach and never blocks the underlying payment.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou) is the full-name national monetary authority and banking supervisor, operating inside the European System of Central Banks and the Single Supervisory Mechanism led by the European Central Bank. The former Cypriot Foreign Exchange Administration function was dismantled through phased liberalisation culminating in EU accession in 2004; today the Central Bank retains only prudential, statistical, payments-oversight, and residue sanctions-enforcement powers, while the national Financial Intelligence Unit (MOKAS) exercises AML/CFT supervision. The applicable legal pillars are the Central Bank of Cyprus Laws 2002-2019, the Business of Credit Institutions Law, the Payment Services Law 138(I)/2018, the Electronic Money Law 81(I)/2018, and Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union on free movement of capital.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM (ring-fenced secondary use only). Cyprus-licensed Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions under the Electronic Money Law 81(I)/2018 and the Payment Services Law 138(I)/2018, plus EU/EEA EMIs passporting into Cyprus under PSD2, are legally able to open settlement accounts, issue virtual IBANs, and execute SEPA/wire transfers for the company. However, they are not viable as the primary treasury rail for this trade: they generally cannot issue documentary Letters of Credit, cannot commit hold funds for LC margin calls, and offer only limited USD clearing and no substantive FX forward lines. For a specialty green coffee importer, an EMI account is best deployed for low-value freight payments, customs duty disbursements, and ancillary EUR operating costs, while all container-shipment settlement and FX hedging must sit with a tier-1 commercial bank. Non-EU/EEA offshore EMIs, especially UK entities post-Brexit, cannot passport into Cyprus and should be avoided for regulatory integrity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share capital and shareholder loans must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for nostro-cleared flows) in EUR to the Cyprus operational account, with a remittance reference precisely stating 'subscription price for [N] ordinary shares of [Company] Ltd per board resolution dated [date]'. No Central Bank approval is required, but the receiving bank will demand the BoP/international transaction form categorising the inflow as Direct Investment (inward equity) where the shareholder holds or controls 10% or more of voting rights, or as Portfolio Investment for passive minority stakes. The credit is conditionally released upon receipt of the notarised board resolution, updated authorised share capital documentation, the shareholders register, and the source-of-funds evidence referenced in the KYC defence file. If the injection is made in USD or another non-EUR currency, the bank either converts at its on-screen corporate rate or credits a dual-currency sub-account; no conversion licence is needed. For intercompany loans, the bank requires a signed loan agreement, an arm's length interest rate, and a declaration of purpose so the inflow is not misclassified as revenue.Primary clearing rails for green coffee purchases are (1) SWIFT MT103 with gpi tracking for open-account and pre-shipment settlements, always referencing the purchase contract and commercial invoice and carrying the euro-area BoP purpose code 'payment for imports of general merchandise'; and (2) irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit issued under UCP600 by the Cypriot tier-1 bank in favour of the origin-country exporter, typically at sight or 30-60 days after shipment, which is the market-mandated rail for Ethiopian, East African, and certain Latin American suppliers. Field 50A/59 of the MT103 and the LC application must show the exact corporate name, address, and SWIFT code of the beneficiary; shipping documents must strictly comply with LC terms, including certificate of origin, phytosanitary evidence, coffee-grade and moisture-analysis certificates, fumigation certificate, weight list, and a full set of clean on-board bills of lading. Where the origin country has constrained USD clearing, invoicing in EUR with a flexible payment-currency clause is the standard de-risking solution; where the supplier's central bank insists on local-currency settlement, payment is executed through an onshore correspondent account of the supplier's bank with full documentation forwarded to the Cypriot bank.Outright deliverable EUR/USD forward contracts with the transaction bank, tenor matched to underlying LC maturities and firm coffee purchase commitments, executed under an ISDA Master Agreement or the bank's short-form FX Master Agreement with a dedicated corporate limit.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on illiquid origin currencies where no offshore deliverable market exists (e.g., Ethiopian birr, Ugandan shilling, Vietnamese dong, Lao kip), with net settlement in EUR/USD against a published fixing rate and no physical exchange of the origin currency.FX swap and rollover contracts used as short-term USD liquidity bridges for LC margin calls and collateral top-ups between EUR receipts and USD disbursements.Plain-vanilla FX options, zero-cost collars, and participating forwards for ceiling/floor protection on committed import flows, subject to the bank's internal derivative-eligibility assessment under EMIR and the entity's sign-off of a treasury risk policy.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 (annual related-party transactions).

Economic substance scope: Cyprus imposes Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) under EU ATAD and domestic law. A company must have adequate personnel, premises, and management in Cyprus to be tax resident and to avoid being classified as a shell. For a green coffee trading company, substance is evidenced by a physical office, local employees, and decision-making activities in Cyprus.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 100% of tax evaded, plus interest. Criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to €50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for tax evasion if they knowingly participate. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company (Lack of Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits, recharacterization of income, and penalties up to €20,000. The company may be struck off the register.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they fail to ensure substance. Travel bans and potential disqualification as directors.
AML (Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1,000,000 for companies, and imprisonment up to 14 years for individuals. The company may be dissolved.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned. They may also face civil liability for restitution and travel bans.

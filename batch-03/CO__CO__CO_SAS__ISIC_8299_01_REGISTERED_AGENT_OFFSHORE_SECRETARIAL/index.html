Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
DIAN - Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (Colombian National Tax and Customs Directorate); official digital portal: www.dian.gov.co (tax, customs, and electronic-invoicing platform MUISCA).

        
E-government filing portal
For a foreign parent or shareholder: preregister with DIAN through the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) digital procedure using Form 002, selecting foreign non-resident entity status; DIAN assigns a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) upon presentation of constituting documents and power of attorney. For the Colombian S.A.S.: execute the private incorporation document without minimum capital, file the pre-RUT through the Chamber of Commerce one-stop digital portal, receive the provisional NIT, complete mercantile registration, and then obtain the definitive corporate NIT and update the RUT to include VAT responsible status (IVA Régimen Común) and the exporter-of-services status. The procedure additionally requires a Colombian legal representative, a digital signature certificate, and activation of the electronic-invoicing accreditation before issuing sales invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation - The Colombian S.A.S. is a resident corporation for Colombian income tax purposes, subject to tax on worldwide income at the 35% corporate rate for tax year 2024; Colombia does not allow pass-through or flow-through taxation for a S.A.S. For indirect-tax purposes, the entity is a VAT responsible party (Régimen Común) once engaged in taxable supplies; services that meet the Article 481 numeral 3 E.T. export criteria are excluded from IVA with entitlement to credit/refund, and the RUT responsibility tag must evidence exporter-of-services status to support the 0% rate.

        
Available election forms
Colombia does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Relevant statutory mechanisms for a services S.A.S. include: (i) the optional Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLES), an integrated turnover-based election available where annual gross income falls below the statutory UVT ceiling of Article 906 E.T. (approximately COP 3.7-4.7 billion for 2024 depending on activity), which consolidates income tax, VAT, industry and commerce tax, and wealth-tax surcharges; (ii) advance-pricing agreements and unilateral transfer-pricing elections; (iii) treaty-benefit attestation and beneficial-ownership declarations for the applicable double-taxation treaty; and (iv) hybrid-mismatch and disregarded-entity disclosure obligations under Law 2277 of 2022, requiring proof of the counterparty tax character before claiming deductions or credits in cross-border secretarial-fee structures.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
19%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
There is no monetary threshold for VAT liability in Colombia: any person, individual or entity, that sells taxable goods or renders taxable services in the national territory is a VAT responsible party and must register in the RUT. The simplified VAT contributor regime (Régimen Simplificado del IVA) is restricted to natural persons with prior-year gross income under approximately 3,500 UVT (about COP 164.7 million for 2024), and is unavailable to a corporate S.A.S.; the SIMPLES optional regime ceiling (about 80,000-100,000 UVT) is not a VAT exemption but an elective consolidated-tax switch with its own eligibility requirements for service activities.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Services supplied from Colombia to foreign recipients qualify for the 0% VAT rate and refundable input credits under Article 481 numeral 3 of the Estatuto Tributario where: (i) the beneficiary is a person or entity without Colombian residence, domicile, or permanent establishment; (ii) the services are executed and utilised exclusively abroad; (iii) the benefit is not derived from Colombian-source activities, goods located in Colombia, or Colombian assets; and (iv) the consideration is received in foreign currency through the official foreign-exchange market (Mercado Cambiario) within the legally prescribed settlement term. Registered-agent, corporate-secretarial, and offshore directorship services rendered to a non-resident client generally meet the exclusive-foreign-use test; however, any local physical performance by Colombian-based staff, local board meetings in Colombia, or services connected with Colombian assets will recharacterise the income as Colombian-source and render the supply taxable at 19%.

Electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta, FEV) is mandatory for all VAT responsible parties under DIAN Resolution 000012 of 2021 and Resolution 000165 of 2023. Invoices must be generated in XML (UBL or CII), digitally signed with a qualified electronic signature, transmitted in real time to DIAN through the pre-validation API, and delivered to the client only after DIAN returns the unique electronic invoice code (CUFE) and validation status. The issuer may use an authorised technology provider or DIAN free solution; batch reporting must occur within 10 calendar days; contingency procedures are allowed only for justified technological failure; XML records and CUFE validation histories must be archived for at least 5 years.

      
DIAN cross-references the service-export declaration, electronic invoices, RUT status, beneficiary documentation, and foreign-exchange records (CIS and Mercado Cambiario databases). Failure to repatriate and settle the proceeds through the official exchange market within 180 days destroys the zero-rating claim, making the IVA due at 19% plus daily interest and triggering the inaccurate-return penalty of up to 200% of the tax difference under Article 648 E.T., in addition to the information-filing penalty of Article 651 E.T. Retrospective audits may reopen returns and reverse input credits or refunds for the full five-year statute of limitations; fictitious or fraudulent export documentation additionally subjects the company and legal representative to criminal tax-fraud prosecution under Law 2277 of 2022, with imprisonment for significant evasion, disqualification from public contracts, and reputational blacklisting. Transfer-pricing records and arm's-length documentation for intra-group secretarial fees must also be maintained, as DIAN may otherwise reclassify the receipt as Colombian-source royalty or service income with a 35% corporate tax assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Colombian peso (COP) is the sole legal tender. Under Law 9 of 1991 and Resolution 8 of 2000, current-account transactions are freely convertible, while capital-account operations are freely admissible but require mandatory registration with BanRep. Dividend remittance and capital repatriation are lawful once the underlying foreign investment is registered with BanRep and the corresponding exchange declaration is channeled through an authorized intermediary of the foreign-exchange market. Practical hurdles: (i) all cross-border receipts and payments for export or import of services must be channeled through an IMC; (ii) export invoice proceeds must be repatriated within the statutory collection term; (iii) profit repatriation requires evidence of tax payment in Colombia, including the certificado de retención and the corresponding DIAN proof; and (iv) receipts parked in unregistered offshore fiduciary or EMI accounts may be treated as an exchange-control violation unless subsequently channeled in time to an IMC.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Colombia does not impose a forced conversion ratio of export proceeds into COP; however, a mandatory full-channeling obligation applies under the Régimen Cambiario. 100% of the commercial value of exported services, including registered-agent and offshore secretarial fees billed to foreign clients, must enter Colombia and be routed through an IMC. The statutory collection term is six months counted from the date the service is rendered. Channeling is evidenced by the Declaración de Cambio por Exportación de Servicios, supported by the commercial invoice and, where applicable, the certificado de prestación de servicios al exterior. Uncollected export receivables must be reported and corrected; late channeling is subject to fines imposed under the exchange-control sanction regime. Once repatriated, the funds may either be converted freely through the spot exchange market or maintained in foreign-currency accounts, but any subsequent conversion must still occur through an IMC.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (BanRep), acting through its Junta Directiva, jointly with the Ministerio de Hacienda y Crédito Público for foreign-exchange and foreign-investment policy; operational oversight and enforcement carried out by the Subdirección de Control Cambiario of BanRep and the Comité de Inversiones Extranjeras.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Wise
            Fit: High
          

          
Low — offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, dLocal, Mercury-type platforms) may be used only as a complementary collection or disbursement tool, not as the primary clearing rail. Legal constraints: (1) offshore EMIs are not Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario and therefore cannot receive export proceeds on a Colombian resident's behalf without triggering the channeling obligation and the six-month repatriation deadline; (2) the Superintendencia Financiera AML/SARLAFT framework expects Colombian banks to verify that the S.A.S. does not use offshore virtual accounts to evade exchange control; (3) most EMIs decline to onboard Colombian regulated-service S.A.S. entities because of the medium-risk rating and the presence of non-resident UBOs; and (4) if used, the account must be limited to a capped monthly flow, with monthly reconciliation against IMC channelings and BanRep declarations. The recommended cap is below 20% of monthly collections.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Company-formation and registered-agent activities for clients incorporated in high-risk or opaque jurisdictions are treated as a shell-company and potential money-transmission red flag, leading to compliance-committee rejection.

Defense evidence: Notarized and apostilled engagement letters for each foreign client; economic-purpose narrative per client; UBO registry of the S.A.S.; adverse-media screening report; formal AML/CFT SARLAFT manual of the S.A.S.; corporate group chart; signed statutory declaration that bearer shares do not exist under Law 1258/2008 and that the S.A.S. never transits client funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The compliant sequence is: (i) incorporate the S.A.S. and obtain the NIT from DIAN; (ii) open a COP current account with an IMC such as Bancolombia; (iii) receive a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's own corporate account, stating the beneficiary as the S.A.S. with its NIT and the purpose concept 'Aporte de capital — Inversión directa en Colombia, actividad ISIC 8299'; (iv) instruct the IMC to channel the incoming foreign currency on receipt and to present the Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional, together with the FDI registration (Registro de Inversión Extranjera), within the five business days following channeling; (v) preserve the accepted BanRep exchange declaration as the legal basis for future dividend remittance and capital repatriation; (vi) record the contribution in the S.A.S. books and file any capital-increase document with the Cámara de Comercio; and (vii) for amounts above USD 50,000, retain the cross-referenced MT103 UETR, the exchange-declaration sequential code and board minutes to satisfy DIAN and Control Cambiario audits.For outbound payments covering subcontractor fees, software-as-a-service, licensing, and international vendor costs, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103/gpi through the local IMC bank. Service imports must be paid via the exchange market by submitting the Declaración de Cambio por Importación de Servicios at the moment of payment. Colombian law requires the payor to withhold applicable income tax (typically 10-20% for foreign service fees, subject to treaty relief) and to remit the withholding to DIAN using the corresponding electronic form. Commercial letters of credit under UCP600 are generally not appropriate for secretarial or registered-agent service flows and are reserved for merchandise or high-value physical deliveries. For recurring outflows, banks offer virtual-treasury batch payment portals with pre-registered beneficiaries; every disbursement must be paired with the underlying invoice, contract and evidence of economic substance to prevent exchange-control or tax-credit rejection.USD/COP deliverable currency forwards (forwards a medida) contracted with an authorized local IMC, subject to an underlying commercial exposure such as future service-export collections or planned foreign-investment transactions.USD/COP cross-currency swaps executed with a local IMC for converting registered foreign-debt obligations or planned capital expenditures from COP to USD, supported by underlying exposure and BanRep-instrument registration.European-style USD/COP FX options (call or put) purchased from a local IMC to hedge collection uncertainty and protect the S.A.S. balance sheet without creating asymmetric speculative risk.Offshore COP non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only where a local IMC provides a matched back-to-back synthetic hedge; direct offshore NDFs without the participation of an IMC are not authorized for Colombian resident treasury operations.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low — offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, dLocal, Mercury-type platforms) may be used only as a complementary collection or disbursement tool, not as the primary clearing rail. Legal constraints: (1) offshore EMIs are not Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario and therefore cannot receive export proceeds on a Colombian resident's behalf without triggering the channeling obligation and the six-month repatriation deadline; (2) the Superintendencia Financiera AML/SARLAFT framework expects Colombian banks to verify that the S.A.S. does not use offshore virtual accounts to evade exchange control; (3) most EMIs decline to onboard Colombian regulated-service S.A.S. entities because of the medium-risk rating and the presence of non-resident UBOs; and (4) if used, the account must be limited to a capped monthly flow, with monthly reconciliation against IMC channelings and BanRep declarations. The recommended cap is below 20% of monthly collections.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required when the taxpayer's gross income exceeds COP 6,335,662,000, or gross assets exceed COP 6,335,662,000, or yearly related-party payments exceed COP 1,935,000,000 (all thresholds indexed by UVT for tax year 2024; approximately USD 1.5 million gross income/assets and USD 450,000 related payments). Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for multinational groups with consolidated revenues greater than EUR 750 million or an equivalent local amount.

      
Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (Article 869-2 of the Tax Code) and substance-over-form doctrine require a SAS providing offshore secretarial services to have real premises, at least one direct local employee with managerial capacity, and active supervision of registered agents. The absence of such substance triggers the disallowance of deductions, reclassification of management fees as dividends, and application of CFC and thin-cap rules.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscal)
        
Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment of 48 to 108 months under Article 434A of the Criminal Code, Law 1762/2015. Administrative penalty under Article 647 of the Tax Code of 1% to 20% of understated tax, plus arrears interest of two times the official rate; asset seizure and suspension of the NIT if payment is not regularised.

        
Director personal liability: Directors, auditors and registered agents who knowingly signed fraudulent returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax. Courts order travel bans, civil restitution of the misappropriated amounts, automatic removal from the Cámara de Comercio registry, and a 10-year ban from public contracting. Private criminal liability results in disqualification from acting as a corporate officer.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: DIAN may adjust taxable income by the difference between the arm's length price and the invoiced price. A penalty of 5% of the related-party transaction value is imposed for non-filing of the TP informativo (Art. 651 E.T.); on adjustment, an additional 20% surcharge applies to the extra tax assessed. Failure to produce the Master File or Local File after request yields an additional fine of 10% of the asset value relating to the controlled transaction.

        
Director personal liability: Directors acting as 'tax managers' are personally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties when documents prove wilful negligence. Colombian courts pierce the corporate veil under Article 60 of the SL Code when the SAS functions as a letterbox; the management of the non-resident parent may then be named as joint debtor and their assets in Colombia embargoed. Persistent non-compliance may trigger criminal charges for attempted tax fraud.

      

      
      

        Shell Company Sponsor / Anti-Money Laundering (AML)
        
Statutory sanction: UIAF administrative fine of up to 200,000 current monthly minimum legal wages (approx. COP 260 billion), revocation of the professional licence, and asset seizure under Law 526/1999. Criminal charge under Article 323 of the Criminal Code ('Lavado de activos') carries imprisonment of 9 to 15 years and a fine of 500 to 1,000 current legal monthly minimum wages; the corporate business is compulsorily wound up.

        
Director personal liability: Veil-piercing is routinely applied in AML cases: shareholders, directors and representative agents who knowingly sponsor shell companies are jointly and severally liable for all civil compensation and the full administrative fine. Travel bans, indefinite disqualification from serving as corporate officers, and confiscation of all fees collected in the shell operation are judicially ordered in final convictions; directors may be extradited under mutual legal assistance treaties.

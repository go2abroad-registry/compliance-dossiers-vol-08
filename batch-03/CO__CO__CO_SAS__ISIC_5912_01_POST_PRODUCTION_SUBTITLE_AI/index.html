Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; transactional services through MUISCA and the DIAN user authentication platform.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must incorporate or register the Colombian S.A.S. before the Chamber of Commerce, appoint a legal representative, then complete the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) process with DIAN to obtain the Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT). The foreign shareholder must evidence capital contribution in foreign currency if applicable, obtain an electronic signature certificate issued by a DIAN-authorized certification authority, and register for mandatory electronic invoicing and all applicable taxes through DIAN's virtual user facility. A non-domiciled foreign entity with Colombian taxable activities must obtain an NIT through a designated agent or representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must incorporate or register the Colombian S.A.S. before the Chamber of Commerce, appoint a legal representative, then complete the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) process with DIAN to obtain the Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT). The foreign shareholder must evidence capital contribution in foreign currency if applicable, obtain an electronic signature certificate issued by a DIAN-authorized certification authority, and register for mandatory electronic invoicing and all applicable taxes through DIAN's virtual user facility. A non-domiciled foreign entity with Colombian taxable activities must obtain an NIT through a designated agent or representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia has no check-the-box regime. The S.A.S. is automatically subject to entity-level income tax and VAT. Qualifying taxpayers may elect the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) for an integrated CIT/VAT determination subject to turnover limits. Foreign-controlled S.A.S. entities may elect Colombian Holding Company (CIN) status under Law 1943/2018 and Law 2069/2020 with respect to certain foreign-source dividends and capital gains. Hybrid mismatch rules under Article 120-3 of the Colombian Tax Statute disallow deductions for arrangements producing double non-taxation, but such elections are not relevant to ordinary subtitle production activities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to an S.A.S.; every S.A.S. engaged in any IVA-taxable activity must register from its first taxable operation. For natural persons, the simplified IVA regime has a threshold of gross income not exceeding 3,500 UVT in the immediately preceding taxable year, but this does not apply to corporate entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate when the exportation is genuine and covered by a Colombian customs export declaration. Export of services qualifies for 0% IVA when the recipient is legally domiciled or resident outside Colombia, the service is actually used or exploited exclusively abroad, remuneration is paid from abroad, and the service would otherwise be subject to Colombian IVA. AI subtitle transcription and voice cloning services supplied to foreign media or technology clients can qualify as exported services if the place of use and commercial benefit is outside Colombia. Services used locally in Colombia or rendered to Colombian-domiciled recipients are taxable at 19%.

  • Valid Colombian customs export declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX) for goods; for services, a service export declaration or equivalent DIAN export registration where applicable
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods; for services, a signed contract and service-completion certificate proving use and benefit outside Colombia
  • Commercial invoice and export-priced electronic sales invoice with CUNE/QR code
  • Certificate of foreign exchange liquidation (Certificado de Liquidación de Divisas) or inward remittance certificate from the Colombian intermediary bank
  • RUT and NIT evidencing the exporter's tax registration
  • Payment receipt from the foreign client and proof of funds repatriation to Colombia

All S.A.S. VAT taxpayers must issue electronic sales invoices (factura electrónica de venta) through DIAN-authorized software providers or internal billing systems connected to DIAN through web services/API. Each invoice is transmitted to DIAN in XML format for real-time fiscal validation and receives a CUNE (Código Único de Numeración de Factura) or CUDE, alongside encryption of the invoice traceability code. A QR code and electronic stamp are integral to the valid invoice. No physical stamp is required.

DIAN has full authority to examine export VAT refunds, including retrospective reviews generally covering the previous five tax years. If zero-rating is claimed without documentary substantiation, the taxpayer must repay the VAT refunded, plus indexation and default interest, and is subject to a penalty of up to 200% of the improperly claimed or refunded amount under Article 648 of the Colombian Tax Statute. Fraudulent overstatement of export zero-rating or false documentation to obtain VAT refunds can be prosecuted as tax crime under Law 1943/2018, exposing responsible parties to prison terms and disqualification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is convertible for its non-resident capital account though all foreign exchange transactions must be channeled through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries (IFCs) on the 'Mercado Cambiario'. Current-account transactions, including profit remittances and service payments, are widely permitted without individual prior approval, provided the underlying transaction is genuine, properly registered with Banco de la República where required (e.g., foreign investment declarations), and settled through an IFC. Capital-account transactions remain subject to registration and documentary compliance, and profit remittance is legal but will be subject to the 7% dividend withholding tax when paid to a non-resident beneficial owner absent a reduced treaty rate. The practical hurdles are documentation, timeliness, and ensuring the payment is declared under the correct form and settlement code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombian exchange rules require that export proceeds from both goods and services be brought into the country through the foreign exchange market within a maximum term of 180 days from the date of shipment or from the date the service is fully rendered, unless an extended term is granted by Banco de la República (generally up to six additional months upon justified request). There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio into pesos; exporters may maintain the foreign currency in non-resident deposit accounts or in foreign-currency accounts with local IFCs, but the proceeds must be registered and channeled through an IFC. For the AI post-production service, the exporter should evidence the service date through completed delivery and invoice documentation, and the sale must be reported to DIAN as an export service under the zero-rated regime.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting as the central bank and foreign exchange authority under the Organic Statute of the Financial System and Resolution 017/2016; the Comité de Cambios and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) provide complementary regulatory and enforcement oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Under Colombian law, a foreign digital EMI (e.g., Wise or other non-bank electronic money institutions) cannot open a local regulated current or escrow account for a Colombian S.A.S. without holding a license from the Financial Superintendence of Colombia. These entities can provide limited cross-border payment initiation and collection services through approved partnerships with local banks, but they are not permitted to offer deposit-taking or treasury settlement account functionality. As a result, for institutional treasury purposes, a local bank account at an IFC is legally indispensable for executing FX settlements, capital repatriation, and tax payments; an EMI can be used solely as an auxiliary collection rail for micro-subscription receivables, not as the primary liquidity clearing account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Non-resident direct ownership with no physical Colombian commercial establishment

Defense evidence: Provide the S.A.S. bylaws, a notarized and apostilled board resolution naming the resident legal representative, the RUT, audited financial statements, a Colombian lease or BPO contract for operational substance, and a detailed corporate organizational chart identifying the ultimate beneficial owner and source of initial capital.
Mismatch between written commercial purpose (ISIC 5912 / NAICS 512191) and expected wire traffic from foreign content platforms

Root cause: Mismatch between written commercial purpose (ISIC 5912 / NAICS 512191) and expected wire traffic from foreign content platforms

Defense evidence: Submit executed master service agreements with subtitle/dubbing clients, sample AI processing invoices, export service certificates to DIAN, and a list of counterparty bank identifiers, demonstrating that inflows correspond exclusively to fees for intellectual services.
High-risk jurisdiction nexus or unusual post-production licensor in sanctioned/disputed territory

Root cause: High-risk jurisdiction nexus or unusual post-production licensor in sanctioned/disputed territory

Defense evidence: Prepare a jurisdiction risk matrix, end-user/customer due diligence files, copies of exchange control declarations (Forms to Banco de la República), SWIFT confirmation history, and an AML/CFT compliance manual with risk-based transaction monitoring.
Professionally opaque capital injection structure (e.g., foreign shareholder using a holding company)

Root cause: Professionally opaque capital injection structure (e.g., foreign shareholder using a holding company)

Defense evidence: Provide a chain-of-title document with full backing powers, audited financial statements of the parent, record of FDIC registration of inbound equity capital in Banco de la República (declaration of international investment), and certified wire instructions with purpose code 'Capital injection under Decree 2245/2011'.
Frequent FX forward or hedging activity without evidence of a genuine underlying commercial exposure

Root cause: Frequent FX forward or hedging activity without evidence of a genuine underlying commercial exposure

Defense evidence: Maintain a treasury policy, a head-and-hedge matrix collating forecast invoice collections in USD/EUR with Colombian peso operational outflows, and monthly proof of the underlying services contracts creating the currency exposure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign capital contributions into the Colombian S.A.S. can be made by an international SWIFT wire from the investor to the S.A.S.'s local escrow or operational account at an authorized IFC. Before wiring, the investor must file the Form of Foreign Investment Declaration (Inversión Internacional Directa) with Banco de la República via its electronic system, or through the bank, using reporting code P011 under the Foreign Investment Regulations. The inbound wire must clearly state the name of the investor, the Colombian company, and the transaction purpose code 'Capital injection – Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada'. For subscriptions arising from AI post-production service contracts, no special prior license is required, but the declaration must be registered within the first 20 calendar days of the quarter following the operation. The IFC will verify automatic exchange control compliance and issue the corresponding certificate of channeling (certificado de canalización), which will be required for later dividend distribution or sale of shares.Operational trade settlements (licensing fees, subcontractor AI annotation costs, vendor purchases) should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 direct wire instructions denominated in USD/EUR from the local U.S. dollar or euro account at the IFC, with mandatory mention of the purpose code (e.g., 'trade payment for services rendered' or 'royalty payment') and a corresponding certificates of channeling. For larger capital equipment expenditures (e.g., GPU infrastructure), the Colombian S.A.S. may open an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (LC) with a Colombian bank for the benefit of the overseas supplier; however, because the commercial activity is a low-risk service business, the LC should only be used where the supplier requires bank payment undertaking. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% headline rate; a 35% withholding rate applies where the dividend distribution is sourced from profits not already taxed at the entity level (e.g., preferential 7% orange-economy profits).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory deadline is assigned by DIAN based on the last two digits of the SAS’s NIT; for a fiscal year ending 31 December, the filing deadline falls in the April–May window of the following year (generally between April 9 and May 21).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: COP 250 million transactional figure in an annual period triggers inclusion in the Transfer Pricing Informative Return (Form 2280) and related-party annex; full Local File and Master File documentation is compulsory when the taxpayer’s gross income or gross assets equal or exceed approximately COP 11.8 billion for the tax year 2024 (threshold measured in 250,000 UVT).

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not operate the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the tax authorities apply substance-over-form principles, the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR), and the abusive tax planning regime. A SAS that merely routes licensing royalty streams through apparently related parties without personnel, premises, or decision-making substance may be recharacterized as a shell company and subject to substantial penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration in Corporate Income Tax

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under Article 335 of the Colombian Criminal Code carries imprisonment of 36 to 60 months and a fine up to 1,500 SMMLV (statutory monthly minimum wages) in addition to collection of unpaid tax, default interest, and a 200% surcharge on the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false returns or participate in evasion may be criminally prosecuted, barred from holding board positions, subjected to embargo of personal assets, and prevented from leaving Colombia (judicial exit ban).
Shell Company / Abusive Structure Under Corporate Veil Rules

Statutory sanction: Under Law 1258 of 2008 (SAS Law), the court or DIAN may pierce the corporate veil and disallow the entity’s separate tax identity, imposing corporate income tax at the underlying foreign beneficiary level plus penalties. GAAR penalties can be up to 200% of the tax avoided and suspension of any preferential tariff.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts when personal assets are used to manipulate the entity’s capital or when the corporate form is used to commit fraud; risk includes civil restitution, travel bans, and confiscation of personal property.
AML / Money Laundering Through Artificial Transactions

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Article 323 of the Colombian Criminal Code is punishable by 10 to 20 years’ imprisonment and a fine of up to 20,000 SMMLV for serious commercial arrangements. The proceeds and related assets are subject to forfeiture, even if the underlying tax claim is minor.

Director personal liability: Directors can be individually indicted for money laundering, internet-order travel bans are replaced by preventive detention, and authorities can confiscate passports; directors may also face derivative liability for civil restitution equal to the laundered amount to investors.

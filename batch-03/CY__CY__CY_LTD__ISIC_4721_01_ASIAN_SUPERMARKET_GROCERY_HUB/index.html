Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance; e-filing portal: TaxisNet / e-Taxis (https://etaxis.gov.cy); customs authority: Cyprus Customs Department, Ministry of Finance (THESEAS electronic customs system); corporate registry: Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (https://efiling.drcor.mcit.gov.cy).

E-government filing portal

Cyprus private limited companies (HE77) are automatically allocated an 8-digit income-tax TIN upon incorporation through the Registrar of Companies; no separate income-tax application is required. A foreign company without a Cyprus establishment that makes taxable supplies must file Form TD VAT 101 with the Tax Department to obtain a CY VAT number (9-digit numeric, issued as CY + 9 characters), attaching certificate of incorporation, foreign TIN, director passports, registered-office confirmation and, where the applicant has no EU establishment, appoint a Cyprus tax representative who is jointly and severally liable for VAT. VAT registration is mandatory once taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600 in any 12-month period; non-established suppliers may also register voluntarily without a threshold. Registration is processed through TaxisNet/e-Taxis with supporting hard-copy documents delivered to the district tax office. An EU EORI number must additionally be obtained from the Customs Department for any export/import activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists under the Cyprus Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002; a Cyprus Ltd. is per se an entity-level taxpayer at the 12.5% corporate income tax rate. Available statutory elections: (i) 75% group loss-relief election (s.34 and s.11(6) of Law 118(I)/2002); (ii) IP Box 80% deduction election for qualifying intangible assets; (iii) Tonnage Tax election for qualifying shipping activities; (iv) VAT group registration election (single taxable person) for entities with more than 50% relationship; (v) option-to-tax election on VAT-exempt letting of immovable property; and (vi) provisional-taxation assessment elections. ATAD II hybrid-mismatch and CFC rules are mandatory and are not elective; no entity-classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 cumulative taxable turnover in the preceding 12 months (one of the lowest EU thresholds); distance-sales threshold from other EU member states: EUR 10,000; voluntary registration permitted without threshold. The standard rate is 20% (raised from 19% on 1 January 2025); a 9% reduced rate applies to selected services (e.g., restaurant catering, hotel accommodation, passenger transport) and a 5% reduced rate to basic foodstuffs (grocery items), with a temporary 0% rate on certain essential food staples under cost-of-living relief orders. Alcohol, confectionery regarded as non-essential, and non-food retail items fall within the 20% rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to (i) supplies of goods actually exported to a destination outside the EU, provided the goods are dispatched or transported out of the EU within 90 days from the time of supply (Art.23(1) of Law 94(I)/2000); (ii) goods sold under the tax-free shop procedure for carriage in the personal baggage of passengers leaving the EU; and (iii) intra-EU supplies to VAT-registered traders (Art.24) where the customer's VAT ID is validated and supported by an EC sales list. Exported Asian supermarket groceries (condiments, vegetables, packaged foods) qualify for the 0% rate, enabling deduction and refund of input VAT on procurement; domestic retail sales of basic food are taxable at 5% (or temporary 0%), which triggers partial attribution of input VAT across 0%, 5%, 9% and 20% outputs and requires a standard pro-rata deduction methodology. Cyprus imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline (no exchange controls), but VAT audits benchmark inward remittance evidencing export proceeds within 180 days of shipment.

  • SAD customs export declaration processed in THESEAS in the exporter's name, with movement reference number and customs exit endorsement
  • Commercial invoice (minimum three copies) showing HS codes, Incoterms and declared value
  • Transport document: ocean Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or CMR consignment note with goods description and route
  • Packing list with container/weight verification
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing crediting of export proceeds to the Cyprus seller's account
  • Proof of non-EU customer status: trade-register extract or foreign VAT/GST registration number together with contract or purchase order

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-stamping or digital signature requirement applies to supermarket POS receipts in Cyprus as of the current reporting period; paper/PDF invoices remain acceptable if sequentially numbered and archived in compliance with the VAT records-keeping rules. All VAT returns, European Sales Lists, Intrastat declarations and customs SADs are filed electronically via TaxisNet/e-Taxis and THESEAS. Cyprus is implementing the EU ViDA package: mandatory B2B e-invoicing and transaction-based digital reporting are anticipated in phases, and exporters should pre-emptively maintain structured XML/UBL e-invoices with a six-year electronic archive.

Cyprus applies risk-based post-export audits cross-matching SADs in THESEAS with VAT returns and EC sales lists; missing export evidence triggers clawback of zero-rating, reassessment at 20%, statutory late-payment interest and, for fraudulent evasion, penalties of up to double the VAT due, fines up to EUR 500 and imprisonment of up to three years (Art.51, Art.56 and Art.63 of Law 94(I)/2000). Record-retention obligations run for six years, and VAT refund claims may be suspended or rejected where export proof or inward-remittance documentation is incomplete or inconsistent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a member of the European Union and the eurozone; the euro is fully convertible. The Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (Articles 63–65) guarantees free movement of capital and payments between member states and third countries, subject only to prudential, statistical and AML/CFT controls. Current-account transactions (trade, services, royalty and profit remittance) are freely permitted without prior exchange-control approval. Capital-account transactions, including direct investment and shareholder loans, are also unrestricted, but require standard bank AML due diligence, Board-of-Directors resolutions, audited documentation, and the submission of the applicable ECB/Central Bank of Cyprus balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical purpose code. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are generally free of Cypriot withholding tax where the EU Parent–Subsidiary Directive applies or where treaty relief is available; the bank will require evidence of tax residence, audited financial statements, and a certificate of good standing. The only practical hurdles are sanctions- and source-of-funds compliance, segregation of escrowed equity capital, and the provision of accurate BOP classifications. There is no convertibility limitation for legitimate trade or investment flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus does not impose any statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or fixed repatriation timeline for export receipts. Exporters are free to hold euro or foreign currency balances offshore or onshore. Nevertheless, under EU Regulation (EC) No 184/2005 on balance-of-payments statistics and the Central Bank of Cyprus guidelines, all cross-border payments, including export proceeds, must be classified with the correct BOP purpose code and reported by the collecting licensed credit institution. In practice, the receiving bank will require the commercial invoice, bill of lading/airway bill and a completed payment-purpose declaration to credit the export proceeds and to reconcile the related supporting documentary evidence; failure to provide these may delay settlement but does not trigger mandatory conversion. No 30-day or other statutory conversion clock applies.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) and the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) for investment-services conduct in relation to FX and cross-border capital market transactions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional Medium. A Cyprus-licensed or EU-passported Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under the Second Payment Services Directive (PSD2) may operate in Cyprus and offer euro-denominated collection, payment-processing and cross-border settlement at lower cost than a full-tier bank. However, an offshore digital EMI without a Cyprus/EU licence is not permitted to provide payment services or create IBANs on its own authority; it must rely on a sponsor bank and may be subject to additional clearing and settlement restrictions. For an Asian-specialty grocery retailer with physical tills, POS merchant acquiring, cash-in-transit, and import letters of credit, a pure EMI is not an adequate sole banking rail because EMIs cannot extend credit, are not covered by the Deposit Protection Scheme for non-e-money business, and may be rejected by some trade counterparties. The recommended blueprint is a hybrid: use an EMI for payment concentration, supplier payables and low-value inbound collections, while maintaining the principal operating and escrow accounts at a Tier-1 Cypriot commercial bank that can issue documentary credits and handle bulk SWIFT settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or shareholder-loan injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 in euro (or in the currency of the injection after the receiving Cypriot bank confirms the FX-convertibility treatment). The remitter shall instruct the sending bank to include the CB Cyprus BOP statistical purpose code for 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital Inward' (for equity) or 'Direct Investment – Other Capital (Intercompany Loan) Inward' (for shareholder loans) in the payment purpose/remittance information fields (tag 70). The receiving bank will verify that the beneficiary account name matches exactly the corporate name, that the amount is consistent with the signed board resolution, and that the source of funds is not tainted by sanctions. For remaining debt, the loan agreement must be executed before the transfer, with arm's-length interest, and the corporate documentation file must show the statutory capital increase or loan entry. The CBC and the Department of Registrar of Companies must receive the requisite share-allotment registration or Form/Notification for issued capital within the statutory timeline. A cleared bank confirmation and the final SWIFT gpi status update serve as the audit evidence for the capital injection.Outbound trade settlements for imported Asian condiments, vegetables and groceries should be executed over the following clearing rails: (a) open-account import settlements via SWIFT MT103 using the CBC BOP purpose code for 'Merchandise Trade – Import' and attaching the supplier invoice, shipping document and customs declaration; (b) commercial documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700 issuance, with amendments under MT707 and presentation instructions under the UCP 600) for large, high-value bulk shipments, ensuring that documents are negotiated through the seller's confirming bank; (c) bank-payment-obligation (BPO) or direct bank-integrated trade-payment platforms for repeated low-ticket trade flows; and (d) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for same-day euro payments to EEA-based suppliers up to the current EUR 100,000 threshold. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,500,000 aggregate annual cross-border controlled transactions for the mandatory Cyprus TP file (Local File); a Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000, and CbC reporting applies above the same group threshold.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute comparable to Caribbean or Gulf regimes, but applies a central management and control test to establish tax residence. Economic substance is nevertheless essential for claiming treaty benefits, sustaining the IP Box incentive, and avoiding CFC reattribution. The practical substance indicators include local board meetings, physical premises, employment of staff in Cyprus, and substantive decision-making in Cyprus.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: False or fraudulent tax return: assessment of additional tax up to 100% of underlying tax, interest at 2.75% above the ECB base rate, criminal fines up to €50,000, and/or imprisonment for up to five years under the Cyprus Income Tax Law and criminal code.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in tax evasion are jointly and severally liable for the tax debt. The court may impose travel bans, order disqualification from directorship, and direct personal civil restitution where they benefit from the suppressed cash proceeds. For serious cases, imprisonment of directors is pursued.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of €500 for TP filing defaults, in addition to transfer pricing adjustments and interest. Where fraudulent TP statements are filed, penalties escalate to the level of tax evasion and may include imprisonment and fines up to €50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false TP filings or use fictitious invoicing can be personally assessed for the adjustment amount and face criminal prosecution. Anti-avoidance provisions permit the Cyprus Tax Department to pierce the corporate veil and hold directors jointly liable for unpaid tax arising from artificial financing and procurement structures.
Shell Company / Artificial Cyprus Residence

Statutory sanction: Cypriot tax residence is denied under the 'central management and control' doctrine, exposing the profit to tax in the true management jurisdiction. ATAD CFC rules reattribute undistributed profits of the shell to the controlling shareholder. Penalties up to 100% of the tax avoided plus interest may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors can be made personally liable for taxes of the foreign entity through ‘piercing the corporate veil’ if they act as mere instruments for tax evasion. Personal travel bans, asset freezing, and criminal penalties for perjury in residence declarations are also applicable.
AML / Cash Laundering in Retail

Statutory sanction: Breach of Cyprus AML and Proceeds of Crime legislation attracts imprisonment up to 14 years, criminal fines up to €100,000, forfeiture of the proceeds of crime, and revocation of the grocery store license. An administrative penalty may also be imposed by MOKAS of at least €5,000 per violation.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers are personally exposed to criminal indictment. Travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and disqualification from holding corporate office are routinely issued. The corporate veil is pierced where the company is a façade for laundering criminal proceeds from cash retail operations.

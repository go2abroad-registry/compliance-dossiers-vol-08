Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — National Tax and Customs Directorate; portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; e-filing and registrations via Muisca: https://muisca.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned S.A.S. obtains its NIT during incorporation by: (1) executing private incorporation documents, with foreign powers of attorney apostilled or legalized and officially translated into Spanish; (2) registering with the Cámara de Comercio of the registered office (RUES), which generates the NIT; (3) completing the DIAN RUT (Registro Único Tributario) inscription through Muisca, declaring the IVA common-regime responsibility, exporter status, fiscal address, and customs profile; (4) obtaining a digital signature certificate; (5) enabling certified electronic invoicing software or DIAN's free billing portal; and (6) filing initial registrations for income tax, VAT and withholding agent status.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a Colombian-resident S.A.S. is a capital company taxed at entity level on worldwide taxable income (corporate income tax rate 35% for 2025); no pass-through regime is available; foreign-source passive income may be subject to territorial/CFC-type rules. — A foreign-owned S.A.S. obtains its NIT during incorporation by: (1) executing private incorporation documents, with foreign powers of attorney apostilled or legalized and officially translated into Spanish; (2) registering with the Cámara de Comercio of the registered office (RUES), which generates the NIT; (3) completing the DIAN RUT (Registro Único Tributario) inscription through Muisca, declaring the IVA common-regime responsibility, exporter status, fiscal address, and customs profile; (4) obtaining a digital signature certificate; (5) enabling certified electronic invoicing software or DIAN's free billing portal; and (6) filing initial registrations for income tax, VAT and withholding agent status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Colombian-resident S.A.S. is a capital company taxed at entity level on worldwide taxable income (corporate income tax rate 35% for 2025); no pass-through regime is available; foreign-source passive income may be subject to territorial/CFC-type rules.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Statutory elections include: (i) the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE), electable for gross income below 80,000 UVT (COP 3,983,920,000 at 2025 UVT of COP 49,799), which combines income tax and VAT in one simplified payment; (ii) optional fiscal consolidation for organic economic groups; (iii) contract-based tax stability agreements for certain large investments; and (iv) mandatory application of BEPS Action 2 hybrid-mismatch and interest-deduction limitation rules without elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Colombia does not apply a general turnover-based VAT registration exemption; any person habitually selling taxable goods or rendering taxable services in Colombia must obtain NIT/RUT and register as Responsable de IVA (common regime). A de facto small-taxpayer boundary of 3,500 UVT of gross income (COP 174,296,500 at 2025 UVT of COP 49,799) and 500 UVT per contract defines the simplified non-collecting regime, generally limited to natural persons and very small merchants; an S.A.S. operating an airline inflight catering kitchen is required to be in the common regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Inflight catering supplied for international flights qualifies for IVA exemption (0%) as an export when: (i) meals and provisions are physically loaded onto aircraft departing Colombia to an international destination, evidenced by a DIAN-validated export procedure (Declaración de Exportación – DEX) and transport documentation; or (ii) the catering is rendered to a non-resident airline and the benefit is consumed exclusively outside Colombia under the Article 481 ET service-export rule. The S.A.S. must maintain RUT exporter status, report exempt export sales separately on IVA Form 300, substantiate each shipment, and may credit and refund input IVA embedded in export-related food, packaging, labor and logistics costs through DIAN's refund mechanism (Form 351).

  • DIAN export declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX) validated in the DIAN customs system (SIGLO/Muisca) evidencing the authorized export consignment of aircraft provisions
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or ramp provisioning manifest evidencing physical departure of the catering from Colombian territory to the international destination
  • Inward remittance documentation: Declaración de Cambio por Exportación and the authorized Colombian FX intermediary's certificate of collection/negotiation of export proceeds

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica de Venta – FEV) with DIAN real-time prior validation: every invoice must be transmitted as digitally signed XML/JSON to DIAN through certified software or the DIAN portal and must obtain a validation response before delivery to the airline customer; each invoice must contain the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) and QR code; purchases from non-invoicing natural persons require the Documento Soporte en Adquisiciones; non-compliance attracts fines of up to 15,000 UVT and potential suspension of invoicing authorization.

DIAN audits export exemptions, input-IVA credits and refund claims under Articles 588, 647, and 684 of the Estatuto Tributario; if zero-rating is disallowed, the exporter is reassessed VAT at 19% plus moratorium interest, subjected to a 50% inaccuracy penalty on the understated tax or overcredited balance, and may face criminal tax-evasion liability under Criminal Code Articles 402 and 434 with imprisonment up to nine years; failure to repatriate and channel export proceeds through authorized FX intermediaries within 180 days triggers Banco de la República foreign-exchange regime sanctions and loss of the FX export channel.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

COP is a free-floating currency with no general restriction on current-account convertibility. Capital-account operations are subject to registration with Banco de la República and must be carried out through authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries (e.g., commercial banks). Profit remittances are permitted once the underlying foreign investment is duly registered and the corresponding dividends have been declared in compliance with corporate law and tax rules. For an S.A.S. engaged in airline inflight catering, key practical hurdles for non-resident shareholders include (i) documenting the FDI registration, (ii) maintaining a local tax ID (NIT) and a bank account in COP, and (iii) filing the applicable foreign-exchange declaration for each capital transaction. Current-account payments for services (travel, cargo, catering) are generally free, but they must be pricked with the correct foreign-exchange purpose code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from goods and eligible services (including inflight catering services rendered to non-resident airlines) must be channeled into the national foreign-exchange market through an authorized local intermediary within six months from the date of export or service completion, unless a longer term is approved by Banco de la República. There is no fixed mandatory conversion percentage; the exporter may hold the proceeds in a COP local account (converting as needed) or, with previous registration, maintain an authorized foreign-currency account ('cuenta de compensación') with a local bank. In all cases, the repatriation must be evidenced by the applicable 'Declaración de Cambio' (Form No. 1, Form No. 6, or the corresponding current-account form) in accordance with Circular DCIN-83.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (with the Foreign Exchange Agency and the national government via Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83). The Ministry of Finance and Public Credit (MHCP) supervises macro-fiscal aspects; the Financial Superintendence of Colombia (SFC) supervises banking compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low direct viability. Colombia does not recognize pure electronic-money institutions (EMIs) as authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries for COP clearing. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, or MultiPass) can operate only as auxiliary payment execution channels for non-COP USD/EUR transactions; they cannot receive local COP sale proceeds or process Colombian trade declarations. The S.A.S. must maintain a primary account in an authorized local commercial bank. EMIs may be used for incidental payroll reimbursements to non-resident contractors or for small-cost fuel procurement, but even those flows must be reported for tax purposes and are subject to VAT/income nuances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign shareholders must inject capital via SWIFT MT103 (sender's bank to the local Colombian bank) using the 'investment capital' purpose code. Before the wire, the local bank must be advised to file the 'Declaración de Cambio' for foreign investment (Form No. 1). The MT103 field 70 must contain the shareholder's name and the reference 'FDI – SAS catering' plus the expected NIT number. The bank will credit the proceeds to a temporary 'capital subscription' account and, once the investment registration is recorded with Banco de la República, transfer the funds to the operational COP account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% for fiscal year 2024 and onwards on taxable net income (Art. 240 E.T.); a 5-point surcharge applies only to financial institutions and energy subsectors, not to inflight catering operations.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding on dividends and profit distributions paid to non-resident corporate shareholders (Art. 245 E.T.); an additional 35% withholding is imposed on the portion of the dividend attributable to income that was never subject to Colombian CIT (exempt or non-constitutive profits). Resident domestic shareholders are generally outside the 10% collection mechanism.

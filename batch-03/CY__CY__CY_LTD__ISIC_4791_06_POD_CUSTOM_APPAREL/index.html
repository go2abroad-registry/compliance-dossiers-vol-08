Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Ministry of Finance), official online portal: Tax Portal ('Tax For All') at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy, with ARIADNI for e-filing and VAT obligations

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus private limited company is automatically assigned its eight-digit Registrar of Companies number, which serves as the tax identification number (TIN). For VAT, the company must submit Form TD 101 to the Tax Department; the VAT number is the TIN with the 'CY' prefix. Non-resident entities with taxable supplies in Cyprus generally require a fiscal/ tax representative and must register for VAT without delay upon commencing taxable activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Cyprus private limited company is automatically assigned its eight-digit Registrar of Companies number, which serves as the tax identification number (TIN). For VAT, the company must submit Form TD 101 to the Tax Department; the VAT number is the TIN with the 'CY' prefix. Non-resident entities with taxable supplies in Cyprus generally require a fiscal/ tax representative and must register for VAT without delay upon commencing taxable activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Cyprus. Standard entity-level taxation is mandatory for limited companies. Available elections include VAT grouping for related companies, group relief elections for income tax loss surrender between Cypriot group companies (Section 33 Income Tax Law), and the notional interest deduction (NID) election for shareholders' equity; IP box regime is available for qualifying intangible assets.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for Cyprus-established taxable persons; non-established foreign suppliers (e.g., electronic services or distance sales into Cyprus) are subject to mandatory VAT registration or VAT accounting through the One-Stop-Shop without any turnover threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export supplies require that goods physically leave Cyprus for a destination outside the EU. The supplier must obtain and retain transport evidence (airway bill, bill of lading or freight forwarder certificate) within 60 days of dispatch. Intra-Community supplies are zero-rated only when the recipient is registered with a valid Cyprus/CIT VAT number and the goods are transported to another EU member state. Print-on-demand goods shipped to third-country customers are eligible if the above documentary evidence matches the customs declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD) or electronic export declaration
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank FX transfer confirmation evidencing payment from the foreign customer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and proof of dispatch/insurance terms matching Incoterms

Cyprus does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, software stamping, or B2B e-invoicing mandate. Electronic invoices are permitted subject to authenticity and integrity controls. VAT invoices must be issued by the 30th day of the month following supply for B2B transactions, and by the time of delivery for B2C sales; summary returns via VIES and Televy are mandatory.

The Cyprus Tax Department may perform post-export audits and require evidence of physical removal, destination, and payment. Where the exporter cannot substantiate zero-rating, VAT is reclaimed at 19% plus statutory interest. False or fraudulent export declarations trigger penalties up to 100% of the tax due, criminal sanctions, and possible retrospective clawback for up to six years; customs authorities may also enforce penalties under the Community Customs Code (UCC).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility of the euro. Cyprus abolished all residual exchange-control mechanisms upon its EU accession (2004) and formal euro adoption (1 January 2008); both current-account and capital-account transactions are completely liberalized under Articles 63-66 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC. No prior notification, licence, or approval is required for inward capital injections or outward profit remittances. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is exempt from any Cypriot withholding tax; the 17% Special Defence Contribution on dividends applies only to Cyprus tax-resident individuals, not to non-resident shareholders. The operative hurdles are not exchange-control but AML/KYC-derived: the distributing company must demonstrate solvency under the Cyprus Companies Law (Cap. 113), present audited financial statements if the statutory audit threshold is met, and the clearing bank must report cross-border payment flows to the CBC under the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) where the single transaction exceeds the current EUR 12,500 reporting threshold. For a POD custom-apparel retailer settling via Stripe and PayPal, the practical requirement is a transparent treasury file linking each settlement flow to platform disbursement reports, commercial invoices, and proof of delivery.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. The Republic of Cyprus imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate export proceeds into EUR. Export receipts denominated in USD, GBP, or other currencies may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency operating accounts at any Cyprus-licensed credit institution and converted at market rates at the company's discretion. The only mandatory obligation is statistical: the servicing commercial bank must report each cross-border transaction, including foreign-currency export receipts, to the Central Bank of Cyprus under the ITRS when the individual payment exceeds EUR 12,500, with the underlying purpose code referencing 'Exports of goods / e-commerce retail sales'. Supporting documentation must include sales invoices, Stripe/PayPal settlement statements, courier or airway bills, and, where physical goods cross EU borders, customs declarations; no foreign-exchange surrender certificate or central-bank approval is required.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου, CBC), operating within the Eurosystem under the Single Supervisory Mechanism and in coordination with the European Central Bank, which holds ultimate monetary and foreign-exchange policy authority for the euro zone.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High for treasury optimization, subject to strict EU/EEA licensing limitations. A Cyprus private limited company may lawfully maintain settlement accounts with EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut Business, Currencycloud, Payoneer, Mangopay) passporting into Cyprus under the Cyprus Payment Services Law L.117(I)/2018 transposing PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366). The target rails Stripe and PayPal are themselves operated in the EU by licensed EMI/PSP entities (Stripe Payments Europe Ltd, Ireland, and PayPal's Luxembourg entity) and require no additional Cyprus licensing; the Cyprus company contracts their services under standard merchant agreements. Legal constraints: (i) the EMI/PSP must hold a valid authorization from an EEA competent authority and either maintain a Cyprus branch notification or rely on EU passporting; (ii) funds held at the EMI are safeguarded, not covered by the Deposit Guarantee Scheme, which is acceptable for operational floats but not for long-term treasury reserves; (iii) fully unregulated offshore digital banks or non-EEA electronic money institutions without an EU passport cannot lawfully provide euro-denominated payment services to a Cyprus company and are not viable clearing rails; and (iv) tier-1 Cypriot banks generally require that the company maintain its primary treasury and settlement account at a CBC-licensed credit institution rather than routing all liquidity through a non-bank EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed exclusively by compliant SWIFT wire transfers (MT103) from the shareholder's account at an EU or OECD-domiciled credit institution into the Cyprus operational or dedicated capital-deposit account. The wire must carry a precise purpose reference: for equity, 'Equity contribution to issued share capital'; for shareholder funding, 'Subordinated shareholder loan under loan agreement dated [DD/MM/YYYY]'. The receiving Cyprus bank classifies the flow under the CBC ITRS Balance-of-Payments coding: 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' for shares, or 'Direct Investment - Intercompany Loan / Other Investment' for loans; for any transaction exceeding the EUR 12,500 reporting threshold the bank requires a completed customer declaration and underlying documentation. Compliance verification bundle: board resolution approving the issue/allotment of shares or the drawdown of the loan; updated share register and allotment form (Form HE3); Certificate of Incorporation and Certificate of Good Standing; a CBC bank certificate of capital import issued at the receiving branch; signed and notarized loan agreement where applicable; source-of-funds evidence from the remitting bank; and UBO declaration. No exchange-control approval or surrender requirement applies. Capital duty on share issuance has been abolished effective 1 January 2021; the only fiscal touchpoint is stamp duty on any loan agreement, which the Cypriot collector will assess, with mandatory electronic stamps for agreements executed after 1 January 2022. The inward remittance must never be broken into sub-threshold tranches to evade ITRS reporting, as such structuring constitutes a suspicious-transaction trigger.Outbound trade disbursements are executed over three complementary clearing rails. (1) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for direct supplier payments in USD, GBP, EUR, and CNH to POD fulfilment partners and raw-material vendors; each transfer must carry the ECB BOP purpose code for 'Goods trade payments' and be reconciled against the purchase invoice, packing list, and shipping documents. (2) SEPA Instant Credit and SEPA Credit Transfer for EU-based suppliers and service providers, settling in real time in EUR; the bank reference field must contain the invoice number. (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) for large bulk procurement of blank garments from East Asia (e.g., suppliers in China, Vietnam, Bangladesh), with the issuing Cyprus bank confirming through a corresponding Asian advising bank; documentary collections on DP/DA terms may be operated via SWIFT MT798/MT4xx. For marketplace settlement, Stripe and PayPal disburse EUR to the Cyprus IBAN through standard card-network clearing (Visa/MasterCard) and SEPA/SWIFT batched payouts; treasury reconciliation requires matching each platform payout report with the company's accounting ledger on a daily basis. Any deviation between invoiced value, customs-declared value, and actual settled UoM must be justified to prevent over/under-invoicing red flags under AML and customs legislation.Outright deliverable EUR/USD FX forward contracts with a Cyprus-licensed credit institution on master FX trading terms, used to lock conversion of recurring USD Stripe/PayPal settlements into EUR at a known rate for a fixed future value date.EUR/USD and EUR/GBP FX swap agreements (swap-in/swap-out) for intraday and short-tenor liquidity management between the multi-currency operating accounts, rolling excess foreign-currency settlement balances into EUR while retaining allocation for supplier payments in the original currency.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts on currencies without full deliverability (e.g., CNH, KRW, ILS, or MENA currencies) executed through the bank's offshore FX trading desk, settled in EUR on the fixing reference rate, permissible solely for hedging genuine commercial exposure arising from supplier or customer invoicing in those currencies.OTC European-style FX call and put options, including zero-premium option tunnels and participating forwards, protecting the minimum EUR conversion rate on the next 12 months of projected USD and GBP revenue while retaining upside participation.Cross-currency interest rate swap (CIRS) matching long-term shareholder loan balances denominated in foreign currency against the EUR functional treasury, used only where a non-EUR shareholder loan exceeds a materiality threshold and economic hedge accounting under IFRS 9 is documented.FX rolling-forwards and bank-negotiated flex forwards permitting adjustment of the settlement date and partial early delivery, subject to confirmation by the bank's markets desk and MiFID II classification of the company as an elective professional client before execution.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High for treasury optimization, subject to strict EU/EEA licensing limitations. A Cyprus private limited company may lawfully maintain settlement accounts with EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut Business, Currencycloud, Payoneer, Mangopay) passporting into Cyprus under the Cyprus Payment Services Law L.117(I)/2018 transposing PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366). The target rails Stripe and PayPal are themselves operated in the EU by licensed EMI/PSP entities (Stripe Payments Europe Ltd, Ireland, and PayPal's Luxembourg entity) and require no additional Cyprus licensing; the Cyprus company contracts their services under standard merchant agreements. Legal constraints: (i) the EMI/PSP must hold a valid authorization from an EEA competent authority and either maintain a Cyprus branch notification or rely on EU passporting; (ii) funds held at the EMI are safeguarded, not covered by the Deposit Guarantee Scheme, which is acceptable for operational floats but not for long-term treasury reserves; (iii) fully unregulated offshore digital banks or non-EEA electronic money institutions without an EU passport cannot lawfully provide euro-denominated payment services to a Cyprus company and are not viable clearing rails; and (iv) tier-1 Cypriot banks generally require that the company maintain its primary treasury and settlement account at a CBC-licensed credit institution rather than routing all liquidity through a non-bank EMI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR. Local File is mandatory where the aggregate value of transactions with associated enterprises exceeds EUR 750,000 in the tax year. Master File is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000. The Cyprus TP Regulations (2022) align with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and the EU TP Directive; contemporaneous benchmarking documentation must exist by the T.D.2004 filing date.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to UAE or Guernsey-style ESR regimes. Substance is nevertheless enforced through: (a) the management-and-control test of Cyprus tax residence; (b) the ATAD-implemented GAAR and CFC rules under Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002; and (c) transfer-pricing substance principles. For the POD custom-apparel e-commerce business, credible substance comprises physical Cyprus premises, locally hired commercial and finance staff, board meetings and decision-making held in Cyprus, substantive contracts with payment processors (Stripe, PayPal), and actual execution of retail and fulfilment functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of omitted income at 12.5% plus statutory interest at 2.25% p.a.; evasion surcharge of up to 100% of the under-assessed tax; criminal fine of up to €5,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 3 years; proceeds-of-crime confiscation orders.

Director personal liability: Signatory directors are criminally prosecutable for false declarations; courts may pierce the corporate veil for fraudulent trading; travel bans, asset freezes, extradition and confiscation measures attach in laundering-linked prosecutions.
Shell Company / Sham Residence

Statutory sanction: Rebuttal of Cyprus management-and-control residence; re-characterisation of profits as belonging to the foreign beneficial owner; denial of the 12.5% rate and of DTA benefits; GAAR and CFC adjustments; administrative penalties on incorrect returns.

Director personal liability: Directors appointed to conceal real control face false-declaration penalties and foreign tax enforcement; the company cannot produce a valid TRC; disqualification and collection measures are available in the shareholder's home state.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment to chargeable income, additional tax at 12.5%, interest at 2.25% p.a., penalty surcharges on the adjusted amount, and separate infringement fines for record-keeping failures under the Cyprus TP Regulations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute non-arm's-length arrangements cannot rely on adviser protection; adjustment-driven insolvency exposes fraudulent-trading claims and personal restoration orders.
AML / Payment Processor Abuse

Statutory sanction: AML fines of up to €50,000 per offence and daily UBO-register default penalties; money-laundering conviction carries imprisonment of up to 14 years and unlimited confiscation; Cyprus banks may be directed to freeze accounts and terminate relationships.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers are indictable as principals; personal disqualification, travel restrictions, seizure of funds held in Cyprus, and striking-off from the Companies Registry follow serious laundering findings.
Illegal Dividend Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The distribution is voidable at law; directors are jointly and severally liable to restore the amount to the company under Cyprus Companies Law Cap. 113; false statutory declarations trigger criminal penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil restitution of illegally distributed dividends, disqualification, criminal liability for false solvency certificates, and travel bans in aggravated fraudulent-trading cases.

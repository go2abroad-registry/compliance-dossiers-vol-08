Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá, operating through the Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES), under the technical supervision of the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio (SIC).

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 (Estatuto Legal de la Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada), arts. 1, 5, 21-27; complemented by Código de Comercio (Decreto 410 de 1971), arts. 98, 110, 114, 372; Ley 2069 de 2020 (Ley de Emprendimiento); for remittance licensing, Estatuto Orgánico del Sistema Financiero (Decreto 663 de 1993) and Circular Básica Jurídica C.E. 029 de 2014 of the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for SAS formation; the subscribed capital must be fully subscribed but payment may be deferred for up to two years (Ley 1258 de 2008, art. 5). Cross-border remittance licensing, however, requires the company to evidence a provisional technical capital/patrimonio técnico prescribed by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia, which must be escrowed as a Certificado de Depósito a Término (CDT) or equivalent fiduciary deposit opened at a Colombian financial institution.

Funding & unlock path: Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos de Bogota fire-safety certificate covering evacuation plan, fire extinguishers, smoke detection and emergency exits, renewable annually; structural load verification for office occupancy under Reglamento Colombiano de Construccion Sismo Resistente (NSR-10) with the responsible engineer's load certificate; occupational health and safety standards under Resolucion 0312 de 2019, registered with the Administradora de Riesgos Laborales (ARL); land-use compatibility certificate issued by the Curaduria Urbana confirming the location is authorized for non-industrial office operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Unidad Administrativa Especial Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign legal entity must appoint a legal representative with domicile in Colombia and obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by completing the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) application through the DIAN portal or in-person at DIAN offices. The applicant must file the foreign company's certificate of incorporation, proof of the legal representative's appointment, a translated corporate resolution where applicable, and evidence of a local address. The NIT is irreversible and is the single tax identification number for income, VAT, and other fiscal obligations.

The applicant must file the foreign company's certificate of incorporation, proof of the legal representative's appointment, a translated corporate resolution where applicable, and evidence of a local address. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election and no domestic hybrid mismatch election are available in Colombia. A SAS may, however, be admitted to the SIMPLE (Sistema Simple de Carga Tributaria) consolidated tax payment regime if its actual income, employee count, and activities satisfy the statutory thresholds, provided it is not carrying out activities expressly excluded by law. Taxpayers may also elect among certain ICA/IVA simplification rules only when the entity falls within the natural-person small trader classification; a SAS normally cannot use the 'no responsable' VAT status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for corporations. A SAS is required to register as a VAT responsible taxpayer if it performs any taxable transfers of tangible goods or taxable services in Colombia, even on an occasional basis. Natural-person small traders may be treated as 'no responsables' only when gross income in the preceding calendar year is less than 3,500 UVT and other statutory conditions are met, which is unavailable to corporate entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated (excluded from IVA) when goods physically leave Colombia and title passes to a foreign buyer, or when services are provided from Colombia but are used exclusively outside Colombia by a recipient that is not domiciled in Colombia and that does not have a permanent establishment in Colombia. The exporter or service provider must obtain and preserve documentary evidence proving the foreign destination, actual use of the service abroad, and that the amount is invoiced to the foreign party. For remittance-related services, the commission or service revenue must be earned under an international service agreement and the underlying transactions must involve foreign payers or counterparties.

  • Electronic export declaration accepted and validated by DIAN (Declaración de Exportación), showing ISIC 6619 service classification where applicable
  • Commercial invoice issued to the foreign client, evidencing the 0% VAT status or express exclusion basis
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or equivalent contractual transport evidence for goods, or for services: signed contract and service completion certificate
  • Foreign exchange declaration (declaración de cambio) documented in the Colombian forex market and the inward settlement certificate issued by the local bank or financial intermediary
  • Certificate of origin and any required authorizations when the transaction claims preferential tariff treatment or export incentives

All VAT-responsible persons, including SAS entities, are required to use DIAN-authorized electronic invoicing. Invoices must be transmitted in near real-time to DIAN's web services through certified software using a digital signature. The invoice must include the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) code and a QR code, and must pass DIAN's real-time structural and tax verification before being delivered to the customer. Pilots, OEM software integrators, and cloud/API connection methods must comply with DIAN Technical Annex and Resolution 165/2023 onward.

DIAN may audit export zero-rating claims retrospectively for up to the displacement tax assessment period. If a zero-rated export is disallowed because the service was not used abroad, the documentation is insufficient, the payment was not repatriated within the FX deadline, or the transaction is deemed artificial, DIAN assesses VAT at 19% on the gross value plus default interest and applies a substantial penalty of up to 200% of the omitted tax for fraudulent or materially inexact claims under the Colombian Tax Code. Criminal tax fraud charges may also be pursued under Law 2277 where there is intent to obtain an abusive VAT exemption or refund.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is fully convertible for current-account transactions when channeled through the official foreign exchange market ('mercado cambiario'). Capital-account inflows (equity subscriptions, loans, portfolio investment) may be freely converted but must be processed via an authorized local intermediary and formally registered with the Banco de la República to secure future capital repatriation, dividend remittance, and hedging rights. Profit (dividend) remittance is permitted without prior Central Bank authorization once the S.A.S. has satisfied its entity-level corporate income tax; the remittance is declared on the applicable Declaración de Cambio, and dividends destined to non-residents become subject to the corresponding dividend withholding tax regime, treaty-protected where applicable. There are no quota systems or dual exchange rates; the principal operational hurdle is documentary compliance at the moment of conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exports of goods and services are subject to mandatory 'canalización': 100% of export proceeds must be collected in Colombia through an authorized FX-market intermediary, deposited into a local bank account, and declared on the applicable Declaración de Cambio (Formulario No. 2 for goods; Formulario No. 1 for services and other concepts). For goods, the funds must be channeled into the Colombian financial system within a maximum of twelve (12) months from the date of shipment (embarque). For services, including cross-border remittance and BPO fee flows, the channeling term is the shorter period established in the underlying contract and accepted by the intermediary, commonly six (6) to twelve (12) months. There is no forced conversion ratio to COP if the exporter maintains a permitted foreign-currency deposit account with a local bank; however, the funds must always enter the national territory through the mercado cambiario before any use. Non-channeled export proceeds are subject to fines, exchange-control sanctions, and loss of hedging and tax benefits.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Republic of Colombia Central Bank), acting as the Foreign Exchange Authority under the Régimen de Cambios Internacionales; the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) supervises authorized FX intermediaries and the remittance operator license; the Unidad de Información y Análisis Financiero (UIAF) receives AML/CTF reports; and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) enforces exchange-control penalties. Legal basis: Law 9 of 1991, the Banco de la República's Resolución Externa No. 1 of 2018, and Decreto Único Reglamentario 1068 de 2015.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. Foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) authorized in their home jurisdictions may not act as deposit-taking institutions, clearing members, or final settlement providers in Colombia without a local license. Under Law 1735 of 2014 and the SFC's SEDPE regime, any entity holding client funds, issuing electronic payments, or operating cash-in/cash-out networks in Colombia must be incorporated as a Colombian Sociedad Especializada en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos (SEDPE) or must use a licensed bank as custodian and settlement bank. For the S.A.S. remittance operator, foreign EMIs are viable only as limited technical rails: collection of foreign-origin funds, offshore FX conversion, and transmission of a settlement instruction to a local licensed payout partner. The EMI cannot serve as the sole liquidity clearing channel because final settlement must occur in COP through the SFC-supervised financial system with full beneficiary traceability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign share capital of the S.A.S. must be wired via SWIFT MT103/202 to the account of an authorized Colombian FX-market intermediary. The remitter includes: beneficiary NIT (tax ID), full legal-entity name, and the purpose code 'Inversión extranjera directa en sociedad local' (Foreign Direct Investment). At the moment funds are credited, the bank files the Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional (Formulario No. 5) with the Banco de la República, generating the Foreign Investment Registration Certificate (Certificado de Inversión Internacional). This certificate is the legal precondition for future profit remittance, capital disposal/repatriation, and access to FX hedging instruments. If capital enters in USD or EUR, the intermediary converts it to COP on the FX market unless the S.A.S. holds an authorized foreign-currency account. The registration must be updated on any subsequent capital increase or decrease. The complete file must contain: the share-subscription agreement, the SWIFT MT103 receipt, the Formulario No. 5 filed by the bank, and the certificate issued by the Banco de la República.Outbound payments must be settled only after the corresponding Declaración de Cambio has been filed. For operational costs and trade payables: SWIFT MT103 via the local bank's correspondent network, accompanied by the declared purpose code. For payouts to agents and cash-pickup beneficiaries: the local bank routes funds through the national clearing system and regulated licensed agent network (Local_Cash_Pickup_Clearing), converting foreign currency to COP at the Tasa Representativa del Mercado (TRM). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. Foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) authorized in their home jurisdictions may not act as deposit-taking institutions, clearing members, or final settlement providers in Colombia without a local license. Under Law 1735 of 2014 and the SFC's SEDPE regime, any entity holding client funds, issuing electronic payments, or operating cash-in/cash-out networks in Colombia must be incorporated as a Colombian Sociedad Especializada en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos (SEDPE) or must use a licensed bank as custodian and settlement bank. For the S.A.S. remittance operator, foreign EMIs are viable only as limited technical rails: collection of foreign-origin funds, offshore FX conversion, and transmission of a settlement instruction to a local licensed payout partner. The EMI cannot serve as the sole liquidity clearing channel because final settlement must occur in COP through the SFC-supervised financial system with full beneficiary traceability.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% under Law 2277 of 2022 (Article 240 of the Colombian Tax Code / Estatuto Tributario), applicable to Sociedades por Acciones Simplificadas (S.A.S.) as ordinary resident entities

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding on dividends paid to a non-resident legal or individual shareholder when the dividend is distributed from income already subject to Colombian CIT (Article 245 ET, amended by Law 2010 of 2019); distributions made out of strictly non-taxed income attract the 35% general rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Form 1739 filing applies when the Colombian entity's consolidated gross income exceeds COP 4,232,915,000 (85,000 UVTs of 2025 at COP 49,799 per UVT, per Law 2277 of 2022 reducing the former 100,000-UVT threshold); Master File is compulsory when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds 450,000 UVTs (approximately COP 22.4 billion) and in all cases where the foreign parent aggregates operations above DIAN Resolution 126 of 2023 annex thresholds; even below the filing threshold, contemporaneous documentation is required as soon as any cross-border intercompany transaction occurs.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime of the offshore type, but the combination of Law 2277 of 2022's General Anti-Abuse Rule, the CFC rules, the DTA beneficial-ownership tests and the DIAN's substance-over-form doctrine impose an effective economic substance requirement. A remittance S.A.S. must prove local management, an operational office in Colombia, legal title and control over its payment-rail contracts, substantive local staff, active bank accounts and business purpose; a mere nominal SAS owning the revenue stream while all functions are exercised abroad by the non-resident parent is vulnerable to being re-characterized as a conduit, losing treaty eligibility and attracting the full 35% rate on all remitted profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Income Tax Evasion and False Invoice Trafficking

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 100% of the tax understated (160% where the evasion involves documents falsified to mislead the tax administration), moratory interest at the variable DIAN annual rate, business closure for up to 10 days for failure to exhibit accounting books, and criminal conviction under Article 434B of the Criminal Code carrying imprisonment from 48 to 108 months and fines from COP 200 million to COP 1,200 million; the criminal consequence applies at the legal representative and responsible officer level.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under Article 42 of the Commercial Code when the S.A.S. is used as a mere facade: directors, shareholders and the de facto officer are personally, severally and jointly liable for all unpaid taxes, penalties and interest, with civil restitution orders over personal property, restrictive travel bans preventing the directors from leaving Colombia, and imprisonment for the officers who signed or ordered the false declarations.
Transfer Pricing Mismatch and Missing Transfer Pricing Return

Statutory sanction: Official DIAN re-determination of the taxable base at arm's length plus a 20% penalty on the adjusted tax liability and the additional tax at 35% plus moratory interest; the TP documentation penalties under Article 260-11 ET amount to between approximately COP 17 million and COP 119 million per return (Form 1739) and up to 10% of the intercompany transaction value when the information is omitted or incorrect; failure to produce contemporaneous documentation reverses the burden of proof.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the Form 1739 with deliberately manipulated comparables or who direct the transfer of float income abroad are jointly liable for the re-assessed tax, may be subject to a personal restitution order for the shifted profit, encounter restrictions on the conversion of personal assets to cash, and can be criminally indicted for tax fraud where the adjustment exceeds the criminal threshold.
AML and Unlicensed Money Transmission Activity

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to COP 1,500,000,000 imposed by the Financial Superintendence (SFC), revocation of any auxiliary payment-rail authorization and negative registration in the UIAF risk list; the unauthorized intermediation of payment transactions is criminally sanctioned under Article 106 of the Organic Financial Statute with imprisonment of 2 to 6 years and fines up to COP 5,000,000,000; laundering of the settlement float or of third-party criminally sourced proceeds triggers Criminal Code Article 323 with imprisonment of 10 to 30 years.

Director personal liability: The directors and the compliance officer who knowingly operate without a license or who tolerate AML breaches are subject to exclusion orders prohibiting any financial-sector activity for up to 20 years, personal civil restitution for the funds moved illicitly, seizure of the personal shareholdings of the foreign shareholder, and criminal incarceration; the corporate veil is expressly lifted for illicit assets under the money-laundering forfeiture regime with the burden on the S.A.S. to prove legitimate origin of every asset.
Shell Company, Conduit Abuse and Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty dividend, interest and royalty rate relief under the beneficial-owner and limitation-on-benefits provisions of the Colombia DTA network; recharacterization of all distributed funds as constructive dividends taxed at 10% or 35% as applicable, plus losses of all cost deductions attributable to the conduit arrangements; the anti-abuse rule in Law 2277 imposes an additional fine of up to 100%–300% of the underpaid withholding tax and a requirement to adjust the domestic tax base against the hidden profits.

Director personal liability: The foreign beneficial owner and the local nominee director face joint and several personal liability for the full withholding difference, the unpaid taxes and treble penalties; the nominee director is barred from serving as legal representative for a period of 10 years, personal travel bans are available where enforcement actions remain unsatisfied, and in aggravated cross-border cases the public prosecutor proceeds for document fraud and money-laundering with applicable prison terms of 4.5 to 15 years.

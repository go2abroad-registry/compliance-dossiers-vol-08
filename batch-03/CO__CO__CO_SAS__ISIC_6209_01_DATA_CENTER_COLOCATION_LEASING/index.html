Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official taxpayer portal: www.dian.gov.co (Muisca and User Microsoft Dynamics platforms for registration, filings and electronic invoicing).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.) by private document (or notarial deed if real property is contributed) and register with the Cámara de Comercio of the registered domicile through the RUES single registry. Step 2: Upon successful Chamber registration, DIAN automatically assigns the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria); the legal representative must complete the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) by updating tax classification (CIU 6209), VAT responsible status (Responsable de Régimen Común), and electronic invoicing obligations through the Muisca portal or in person at a DIAN service point. Step 3: If foreign shareholders exist, the S.A.S. appoints a Colombian legal representative and registers the non-resident investor ownership details; the S.A.S. itself is the Colombian tax resident. Step 4: Obtain a digital signature certificate (certificado digital) from a DIAN-authorized certification authority (e.g., Certicámara, GSE) to operate the e-invoicing system. Step 5: Enroll in DIAN's factura electrónica system via direct web-service integration or an authorized software operator/tax technology provider, thereby activating the S.A.S.'s fiscal identity for VAT and electronic reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Colombian S.A.S. is an opaque, tax-resident legal entity taxed on worldwide income at the 35% corporate income tax rate (2025) and is a standard VAT taxpayer (Responsable de IVA - Régimen Común). No pass-through or fiscal transparency election is available for the S.A.S. itself; transparency applies only to certain contractual vehicles such as consorcios and uniones temporales under ET Art. 18.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist under Colombian law. Available statutory elections include: (1) the Régimen SIMPLE de Tributación (ET Arts. 903-941), an optional consolidated presumptive tax substituting income tax and VAT for small taxpayers with gross income ≤ 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 174 million for 2025); (2) consolidated income tax filing by authorized economic groups under Law 2010/2019 Art. 16 where a Colombian parent controls more than 50% of the subsidiary; and (3) election of contractors to be treated as fiscally transparent (consorcios/uniones temporales), which does not apply to an S.A.S. Companies established within a legally recognized Zona Franca may additionally elect the special 20% corporate rate and special VAT suspension regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general economic threshold for VAT registration; any person or entity habitually selling taxable goods or services in Colombia must register as a VAT responsible party (Régimen Común). Taxpayers exclusively selling excluded (non-taxable) items, or small taxpayers with gross income ≤ 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 174 million for 2025) that elect the SIMPLE regime, remain obliged to register but are subject to special rules. Separate mandatory registration applies to non-resident digital-service suppliers, but that does not govern the resident S.A.S.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods are zero-rated under ET Art. 481. Services qualify as 'exportación de servicios' at 0% VAT where: (i) the supplier is a Colombian resident or permanent establishment (the S.A.S.); (ii) the recipient is a foreign person without Colombian domicile; (iii) the service is performed from Colombia but is actually used or exploited abroad; and (iv) the exporter completes the service export declaration, retains contract and invoicing evidence, and repatriates the foreign currency. For data center colocation and bare-metal/IaaS hosting, DIAN Concepts (including Concepto Unificado 0016/2019 and subsequent rulings) recognize zero-rating where the foreign client remotely manages and uses the server capacity and the servers remain at the client's disposal abroad; by contrast, local hands-on maintenance, cabling, and managed services physically delivered in Colombia are taxable at 19% VAT as domestic services. The S.A.S. should obtain a confirmatory DIAN concept where the provider also owns the infrastructure, and should file refund requests (Formulario 300) within the first 10 business days of the month following the zero-rated operation to claim the corresponding VAT credit attributable to export activities.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación - DEX) electronically accepted by DIAN customs through the VUCE/SIGLO XXI platform for goods, or the Declaración de Exportación de Servicios filed with DIAN within the first 15 calendar days of the month following the service, applicable to colocation services
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (or equivalent carrier document) for physical goods; for remote data center services, the signed commercial contract and a service delivery/acceptance certificate evidencing foreign use or exploitation outside Colombia
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas / Declaración de Cambio por exportaciones) issued by a Colombian foreign-exchange intermediary, evidencing collection of export proceeds within the statutory repatriation term

Mandatory electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta) is governed by DIAN Resolution 000165 of 2023 and Circular 000016 of 2024. Every invoice must be generated in UBL 2.1 XML, digitally signed, and transmitted synchronously (real-time validation) to DIAN through authorized web services/API before the customer receives the document. DIAN validates tax status, NITs, and arithmetic, then returns the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) stamp and QR code for the invoice. Real-time synchronous transmission is the default from 2025 for all VAT-responsible taxpayers; the asynchronous 24-hour modality applies only to narrowly enumerated small taxpayers. Zero-rated export invoices must also be transmitted electronically, and taxpayers must file periodic electronic information (información exógena) with DIAN.

DIAN subjects export zero-rating claims to strict Formulario 300 refund scrutiny and retrospective review. If the export requirements are not satisfied, DIAN may assess the omitted 19% VAT, impose the ET Art. 648 'inexactitud' penalty of up to 160% of the tax evaded, charge moratory interest (approx. 1.5% per month), and claw back any refunded VAT with interest for a limitation period that generally runs 3 to 5 years, extended to up to 6 years in cases involving omitted foreign transactions or transfer-pricing adjustments. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days, or without Banco de la República authorization, triggers foreign-exchange fines of up to 5% of the unrepatriated amount and may disqualify the zero-rating. Fraudulent zero-rating supported by false declarations constitutes criminal tax evasion under Código Penal Art. 402, carrying 4 to 9 years of imprisonment, accessory fines up to 200% of the evaded tax, and potential asset forfeiture; additionally, non-compliant electronic invoicing of zero-rated transactions attracts per-invoice fines of up to 5,000 UVT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a partially convertible foreign-exchange regime. Current-account transactions — including cross-border data-center colocation service fees, bandwidth costs, maintenance charges, and intercompany operating services — may be freely converted and paid through authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries without prior authorization. Capital-account transactions require registration with Banco de la República. Inbound direct foreign investment in a Colombia SAS must be reported through an authorized local bank or foreign-exchange intermediary; the corresponding exchange declaration constitutes the legal mechanism for later dividend remittance and capital repatriation. Profit remittances are not blocked by exchange controls but are subject to compliance with FDI registration, statutory financial statements, dividend tax withholding, and DIAN tax-clearance procedures. The practical hurdles are not a prohibition on convertibility but rather fines, delays, and loss of the right to remit proceeds if the original capital entry was not properly registered under the current foreign investment regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The export of colocation and data-center services must be channeled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market within the statutory periods established by Banco de la República. For goods, the deadline generally runs from the shipment date; for services, from the date the right to receive payment is perfected. In practice, export proceeds received after the invoice due date must be accumulated and immediately channeled through the local banking system. Once received in a Colombian account, the foreign currency must be surrendered for Colombian pesos unless the company operates a registered compensation account (cuenta de compensación) approved by Banco de la República, which allows holding foreign currency for treasury-approved foreign payments. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% for export receipts not covered by a compensation-account exemption. No parallel-market settlement is permitted.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Bogotá, D.C.), jointly enforced with the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia under Resolución Externa 1 de 2018 and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs, such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, or Mercury-style treasury platforms, are not authorized to provide a Colombian peso bank account or to act as a licensed foreign-exchange intermediary under Colombian law. Their licenses from other jurisdictions do not transfer to Colombia and their accounts cannot receive local ACH credits from Colombian treasury operations. They may be used only as auxiliary non-operational rails for expenses in foreign jurisdictions or as a secondary disbursement wallet. The practical feasibility is Low for serving as the primary corporate bank account; Medium as a supplementary cross-border payment tool if the foreign SAS maintains an FX limit and does not rely on those EMIs for capital calls, dividend repatriation, or regulatory compliance reporting.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Non-resident shareholders with dispersed or opaque ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO) structure

Defense evidence: Apostilled certificate of incorporation and bylaws, certified Spanish translation, current UBO declaration under Colombian law, passport copies of shareholders and directors, foreign tax identification certificate, and a board resolution granting the local representative unrestricted KYC/transaction mandates.
Initial projected account turnover materially disproportionate to paid-in capital and registered business purpose

Root cause: Initial projected account turnover materially disproportionate to paid-in capital and registered business purpose

Defense evidence: Audited or reviewed financial statements for the foreign parent and the Colombian SAS, 12-month treasury cash-flow projection, executed colocation lease agreements or binding service orders with named data-center customers, supplier contracts for server hardware, bandwidth and power, and a board-approved business plan referencing ISIC 6209 / NAICS 518210.
High-risk jurisdiction routing or source-of-wealth concern for inbound capital transfers

Root cause: High-risk jurisdiction routing or source-of-wealth concern for inbound capital transfers

Defense evidence: Bank statements from the remitting entity showing the origin of funds, notarized source-of-wealth statement, shareholder capital subscription minutes, contracts evidencing commercial purpose, and a legal background report for the sending jurisdiction; if jurisdiction is on FATF enhanced-monitoring lists, obtain a pre-engagement independent audit and enhanced due-diligence clearance.
Generic or inconsistent SWIFT reference lines in cross-border wires

Root cause: Generic or inconsistent SWIFT reference lines in cross-border wires

Defense evidence: For capital injections: certified capital subscription resolution and FDI registration form. For service payments: commercial invoice, signed colocation agreement, and the associated DIAN exchange-declaration form. For imports of fixtures and computer hardware: bill of lading, commercial invoice, import declaration or ex-works purchase order, and customs brokerage confirmation.

Root cause: Mismatch between UBO register and signatory authority in the bank mandate

Defense evidence: Notarized certificate of legal representation, current excerpt from the Colombian Chamber of Commerce registry, a board-approved signatory matrix, and a certified shareholder register from the foreign corporate parent proving that the designated signatory is not acting without beneficial-owner approval.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder must send the capital contribution by SWIFT MT103 from a conventional bank account in its home jurisdiction to the Colombian SAS's COP-denominated account held with the chosen Colombian authorized foreign-exchange intermediary. The outgoing wire should clearly identify the underlying purpose as direct foreign investment in a Colombian entity. On receipt, the Colombian bank will convert the foreign currency into Colombian pesos at the TRM reference rate and generate the exchange-declaration code necessary for Banco de la República registration. The Colombian SAS must file the applicable foreign-investment exchange declaration through the receiving bank or an authorized currency intermediary promptly; in practice, the bank operationalises this registration simultaneously or within the term allowed by the central bank regulations. The local company must retain the bank-issued proof of channeling, the foreign shareholder's capital subscription resolution, and the corporate approval for share issuance, and must ensure the RUT and Chamber of Commerce e-certificate confirm the SAS's corporate existence and the share capital actually received.For recurring operational outflows — upstream bandwidth, equipment leases, software licenses, and cross-border data-center telemetry fees — the primary clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 corporate payment made by the Colombian bank on behalf of the SAS, accompanied by the underlying invoice or agreement and the correct declaration of reason. For large capital purchases of server hardware, network infrastructure, or managed equipment involving international suppliers, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (commercial L/C at sight) may be opened through the Colombian bank, with the foreign beneficiary's bank acting as advising or confirming bank. Domestic recurring payments from tenants and customers are settled through the Colombian ACH/real-time clearing system (ACH Colombia) in COP, while cross-border tenant invoices in USD may be paid into the SAS's foreign currency account or converted before receipt according to treasury policy. Dividend distributions and capital repatriation are made via SWIFT MT103 after DIAN tax clearances and corporate minutes approving the distribution are presented to the bank.OTC FOREX outright forwards (Forwards Cambiarios) contracted with authorized Colombian banksNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in COP/USD for synthetic hedging without physical USD settlementCross-currency swaps (CCS) between COP and USD for balance-sheet and capital-injection hedgingForeign-exchange options on COP/USD where the local intermediary is authorized by Banco de la RepúblicaOnshore spot transactions executed exclusively through the regulated foreign-exchange market (Interbank / FXD) at the TRM price

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs, such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, or Mercury-style treasury platforms, are not authorized to provide a Colombian peso bank account or to act as a licensed foreign-exchange intermediary under Colombian law. Their licenses from other jurisdictions do not transfer to Colombia and their accounts cannot receive local ACH credits from Colombian treasury operations. They may be used only as auxiliary non-operational rails for expenses in foreign jurisdictions or as a secondary disbursement wallet. The practical feasibility is Low for serving as the primary corporate bank account; Medium as a supplementary cross-border payment tool if the foreign SAS maintains an FX limit and does not rely on those EMIs for capital calls, dividend repatriation, or regulatory compliance reporting.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are mandatory when gross income exceeds COP 1,700,000,000 (approximately USD 0.42 million) or net assets exceed COP 1,700,000,000 (threshold annually adjusted by DIAN) for the fiscal year. The threshold is based on the taxpayer's equity or income under Article 260-10 of the Tax Statute. Additionally, a Transfer Pricing Informative Return (Form 1380) must be submitted when the gross asset or income threshold is met. A TES (Transacción de Empresas con el Exterior) report is required for any payments to related foreign parties regardless of threshold in many cases.

Economic substance scope: Colombia applies controlled foreign company (CFC) rules and economic substance requirements for entities positioned as decision-making centers. For a SAS whose only activity is leasing physical racks within a Colombian data center, the entity must demonstrate actual staff, contract management, billing, network administration, and physical presence. If the entity is managed and controlled from abroad, DIAN may challenge it as a transparent or artificial structure (BCA, 'Business Corporation Act' or 'substance-over-form' doctrine). Colombia's ESR (Economic Substance Regulations) are not identical to those in the UAE/Caribbean, but the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under Article 869 of the Tax Statute permits recharacterization of transactions lacking economic substance. The coverage is also informed by OECD BEPS Actions 8–10. For the colocation model, substance is established by: (a) ownership or lease of power and cooling assets; (b) local technicians; (c) incidence of risk from equipment failure; (d) billing in Colombian pesos or equivalent under local contracts. A pure letter-box SAS with foreign control and no local economic activity faces legal presumption of sham and the risk of reallocation of profits to the parent jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 4 to 9 years under Law 1819 of 2016 (amending the Criminal Code) plus fines equivalent to 50% to 200% of the evaded tax. Civil liability includes the obligation to pay all evaded tax, interest at 29.9% effective annual rate, and an inexactness penalty up to 100% of the misstatement. The DIAN may also impose 'sanción de clausura' (temporary closure) on the establishment for up to 10 days.

Director personal liability:
Shell Company (Sociedad Fachada / Societé de Apparence)

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 60 and 61 of the Tax Statute, income allocation may be disregarded and all income treated as belonging to the foreign principal. DIAN may impose an income tax assessment at the corporate level and additional transfer pricing adjustments. The foreign parent may become liable for Colombian taxes on deemed permanent establishment basis. In addition, directors and legal representatives may be personally liable for taxes not paid when they knowingly executed sham contracts (Article 794-1 of Tax Statute).

Director personal liability:
AML/CFT – Corporate Abuse (Lavado de Activos)

Statutory sanction: The SAS license or legal existence is not automatically revoked, but the entity may face asset forfeiture (extinción de dominio) under Law 1849 of 2017 if assets are tied to money laundering. Fines up to COP 6,000,000,000 (approx. USD 1.6 million) under Superintendencia de Sociedades regulations for violating corporate transparency rules. Beneficial ownership disclosures mandatory; omission is punishable with fines against the company and responsible officers.

Director personal liability:
Transfer Pricing Penalties (Ajuste Primario y Sanciones)

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 100% of the tax adjustment resulting from TP recharacterization (Article 260-9 of the Tax Statute). In addition, a fine of 20% of the value of the transaction omitted from the TP informative return (minimum penalty of 500 UVT in 2025, approx. COP 23.5 million). Late filing of TP documentation triggers a fine of up to 2% of the aggregate intercompany transactions, subject to caps. The TP penalty is imposed on the Colombian entity, but the legal representative is jointly liable if the omission was intentional.

Director personal liability:
Failure to File or Falsifying Electronic Invoicing Records

Statutory sanction: Under Article 652 of the Tax Statute, a monetary penalty of 10% of the value of each non-issued or non-unique invoice. Additional penalty of 1% of the invoiced value for each day of delay, up to a cap of 90 days. Continued non-compliance can lead to the prohibition to operate commercial establishments and a 'sanción de clausura'. For wholly foreign-owned SAS, failure to issue electronic invoices invalidates the expense deduction in the hands of the client, causing the SAS to lose corporate tax benefits.

Director personal liability:
Personal Liability of Directors – Corresponsabilidad Tributaria

Statutory sanction: Under Article 794-1 of the Tax Statute, the legal representative is jointly and severally liable for tax penalties and unpaid taxes if they participated in the infraction. The directors' assets may be seized (embargo) for debts of the SAS if fraud is proven. Travel bans (prohibición de salida del país) can be imposed for tax debts exceeding a threshold (over 100 monthly minimum wages). Criminal imprisonment of 3 to 8 years for fraudulent bankruptcy or concealment of assets is possible. In addition, the Superintendencia de Sociedades may order dissolution of the SAS and liquidation if it is deemed to exist merely to evade taxes.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital. Share subscription, payment schedule, and form of contribution are freely agreed in the bylaws. Cash contributions must be deposited in a Colombian bank account opened for the company; no escrow account is required by law for S.A.S. formation.

Funding & unlock path: No fire safety certificate is required for pure software office activity unless the premises exceed occupancy or load limits; standard worker workplace safety registration with the ARL (Administradora de Riesgos Laborales) and a basic fire safety inspection by the Cuerpo de Bomberos are recommended for the registered office.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital. Share subscription, payment schedule, and form of contribution are freely agreed in the bylaws. Cash contributions must be deposited in a Colombian bank account opened for the company; no escrow account is required by law for S.A.S. formation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN). Official portal: www.dian.gov.co. Electronic services are accessed through the MUISCA system and the DIAN Sede Electrónica (sedeelectronica.dian.gov.co).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the S.A.S. by private document, unless real property is contributed, file the bylaws with the competent Cámara de Comercio, and obtain the Certificado de Cámara de Comercio. Step 2: Apply for the preliminary RUT (Registro Único Tributario) through the Cámara de Comercio or DIAN portal; DIAN automatically assigns the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria). Step 3: File the definitive RUT with DIAN in MUISCA/Sede Electrónica, electing the tax responsibilities applicable to a SaaS exporter, including Impuesto de Renta (35% foreign corporate rate), IVA responsable, and Retención en la Fuente. Step 4: Appoint a legal representative or tax agent domiciled in Colombia if the beneficial owner is a non-resident alien; file the corresponding power of attorney and non-resident registration at DIAN. Step 5: For a foreign company not resident in Colombia but earning Colombian-source income or providing digital services to Colombian consumers, the foreign company itself must obtain a NIT/RUT and appoint a local commercial representative or tax agent. Since the entity is a local S.A.S., the standard path is the five-step local entity registration process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Colombian S.A.S. is an opaque juridical person subject to corporate income tax at a 35% statutory rate on worldwide taxable income; retained profits are subject to entity-level tax only, while distributed profits to non-resident shareholders attract a 10% final dividend withholding regime. There is no pass-through election for the S.A.S.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Colombia. The S.A.S. is conclusively classified as a corporation for Colombian tax law. The only alternative statutory regime is the optional Impuesto Único Simple (Régimen SIMPLE), available to certain small entities with annual revenues below the legal threshold (approximately up to 100,000 UVT, i.e., about COP 4.7 billion for fiscal year 2024), which replaces ordinary CIT and VAT with a unitary combined tax; adherence to SIMPLE prevents recovery of VAT input credits and is generally not appropriate for an exporter engaged heavily in zero-rated export services. Hybrid mismatch rules arising from BEPS Action 2 are implemented through controlled-foreign-corporation clauses, limitation on deductions, and international tax transparency mechanisms; however, none of them creates an elective classification for the S.A.S.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general monetary threshold for mandatory VAT registration in Colombia. A Colombian S.A.S. engaged in any taxable sale, import, or export service must register as a Responsable de IVA, Régimen Común. For small taxpayers, the simplified IVA regime (Régimen Simplificado) may be elected only when the prior-year gross revenues are below 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 148 million for fiscal year 2024, UVT 2024 = COP 42,412), but the simplified regime does not preserve the right to claim refunds of VAT credits on zero-rated export sales. Foreign suppliers of digital services to Colombian consumers are required to register as IVA collectors irrespective of turnover, with no de minimis annual threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Colombian S.A.S. may claim the 0% IVA export zero-rating on SaaS export services when all statutory requirements are cumulatively met: (i) the service falls within the exportable services catalogue, including cloud software, SaaS subscriptions, digital data processing, and licensed software access provisioned remotely; (ii) the commercial beneficiary or contractual recipient is a non-resident person, a foreign entity without a permanent establishment in Colombia; (iii) the service is actually used, exploited, or consumed outside Colombia, which should be proved by the user's IP geolocation, platform analytics, registered payment country, and contractual designation; (iv) the service is invoiced to a foreign party and denominated in foreign currency; (v) the payment is received and repatriated through the Colombian foreign exchange market, with the mandatory certificate of inward remittance within the statutory term; and (vi) the transaction is supported by a valid service-export declaration registered with DIAN when required. Where Paddle or Lemon Squeezy act as merchant of record, the analysis must focus on whether the Colombian S.A.S. is selling to the foreign payment platform as the offshore licensee; if instead the Colombian S.A.S. is selling directly to Colombian-end users through those rails, VAT at 19% applies.

  • FEV-Exportación: electronic export invoice (0% IVA) issued through the DIAN-authorized electronic invoicing system, containing the CUFE and QR code, with the export clause and foreign currency fields in the XML.
  • Commercial SaaS subscription or software licence agreement signed with the non-resident beneficiary containing governing law, place of use, and a covenant that the user has no Colombian domicile or permanent establishment.
  • Service-export customs registration declaration (Declaración de Exportación de Servicios, Form E-3603, or equivalent DIAN registration) and, when physical deliverables or medium-embedded devices are involved, the Colombian Customs Export Declaration (DEX) and the relevant Bill of Lading.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro / Certificado de Cambio) issued by an authorized Colombian intermediary bank evidencing the receipt of the export proceeds from the foreign seller or platform within the 180-day statutory period.
  • Payment-platform settlement and payout reports from Paddle or Lemon Squeezy, reconciling end-user subscription sales, platform fees, FX conversion, and the net remittance remitted to the Colombian S.A.S.
  • Non-residency and place-of-use substantiation package: IP geolocation records, payment provenance, digital acquisition analytics, and written declarations from the foreign buyer stating the absence of Colombian source or nexus.

Mandatory electronic invoicing, Factura Electrónica de Venta, is fully operational for all VAT-responsible taxpayers in Colombia. The taxpayer must transmit FEV XML data to DIAN using the authorized software or directly through the DIAN Sede Electrónica API web services, with a digital signature and a cryptographic CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) plus a QR code. Real-time validation and event management are required: acceptance, rejection, and receipt-registration events must be exchanged within the system-defined timeframes. Large taxpayers operate under pre-validation; smaller issuers under deferred validation. For foreign suppliers of digital services with merchant-of-record platforms, the platform may issue the electronic sales document; if the Colombian S.A.S. is the actual seller of record for Colombian final users, it must issue the FEV and accrue the 19% IVA.

Claims of zero-rated export services are rigorously inspected by DIAN. Where the export status is inaccurate or falsely documented, the taxpayer is exposed to: (i) a penalty for inexacta declaración under Article 648 E.T. of 100%–200% of the understated IVA or income, with the 200% bracket applicable to sham or abusive arrangements; (ii) reinstatement of the 19% IVA on the impossible-to-export sales and disallowance of the associated input VAT credits, resulting in a full clawback; (iii) moratory interest and inflationary adjustment computed from the original filing date; (iv) separate penalties under Article 651 E.T. for false information and Article 652 E.T. for failure to preserve the export substantiation files; (v) foreign-exchange penalties under Law 9 of 1991 for unrepatriated export proceeds; and (vi) criminal liability for fiscal evasion under Act 1762 of 2015 when the understated tax exceeds the criminal threshold. DIAN's transfer-pricing and anti-fragmentation audits in the SaaS sector specifically target shell licensors, artificial routing through merchant-of-record platforms, and back-to-back licensing structures designed to simulate export zero-rating where the true digital consumption occurs in Colombia.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is freely floating and the current account is convertible, but export-of-services receipts (including micro-SaaS subscription revenues settled through Paddle or Lemon Squeezy) are classified as foreign-exchange operations between residents and non-residents and must be canalized (repatriated and settled into COP) through an authorized foreign-exchange intermediary using the applicable Declaración de Cambio. Capital-account inflows, being foreign direct investment into the S.A.S., do not require prior authorization but must be registered with the Central Bank's international-investment register within the statutory registration window to secure the legal right to remit profits and repatriate capital. Profit remittance and dividend payments to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted in principle but subject to Colombian dividend withholding under Article 408 of the Tax Code (7.5 percent for profits already taxed at the entity level, with higher rates for untaxed distributed profits, subject to treaty relief where applicable), to bank-side tax-clearance verification, and to the preparation of the corresponding exchange declaration. The operative hurdles in the modern regime are documentary and AML/KYC-driven rather than quantitative convertibility caps.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Banco de la República exchange regime, service-export proceeds must be repatriated and channeled through the foreign-exchange market within the statutory term, which in the standard case is six months measured from the accrual or invoicing trigger event for services rendered (for goods, six months from shipment date, with longer special windows for certain commodities). The mandatory rule is full channeling of 100 percent of the foreign currency through an authorized intermediary, settling into COP at the representative market rate (TRM); partial retention or offshore deferral is permitted only with prior Central Bank authorization or for documented offsets tied to verified import payments or registered external-debt servicing. Exporters must file the relevant Declaración de Cambio with the receiving intermediary bank, attaching the commercial invoice, the merchant-of-record settlement statement where applicable, and the underlying export contract, and proceeds may not be retained offshore beyond the channeling window.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia, the Central Bank of Colombia, exercises monetary and foreign-exchange regulatory authority under the regulated exchange regime established by Resolution 8 of 2000, as amended, and Regulatory External Circular DCIN-83. The Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia supervises institutional compliance by authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries, while the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit sets exchange macro-policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium - viable as an operational overlay but not as a substitute for the local regulated bank account. Offshore digital EMIs and payment intermediaries (Wise Business, Payoneer, Mercury, and the merchant-of-record rails of Paddle and Lemon Squeezy) are not Colombian-licensed deposit-taking institutions and cannot lawfully provide local accounts or hold S.A.S. funds inside Colombia unless licensed as specialized electronic-deposit and payment companies (SEDPE) by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. Their permitted function is limited to cross-border collection and payout conduits for export-of-services receipts, subject to three binding constraints: each settlement remains a Colombian-resident service-export receivable that must be returned and canalized through the local FX market within the statutory window; any foreign currency held in an overseas account is a reportable foreign asset for Colombian tax informativa purposes; and treasury policy should consolidate receipts into one local USD account to avoid wire fragmentation and documentary burden.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium - viable as an operational overlay but not as a substitute for the local regulated bank account. Offshore digital EMIs and payment intermediaries (Wise Business, Payoneer, Mercury, and the merchant-of-record rails of Paddle and Lemon Squeezy) are not Colombian-licensed deposit-taking institutions and cannot lawfully provide local accounts or hold S.A.S. funds inside Colombia unless licensed as specialized electronic-deposit and payment companies (SEDPE) by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. Their permitted function is limited to cross-border collection and payout conduits for export-of-services receipts, subject to three binding constraints: each settlement remains a Colombian-resident service-export receivable that must be returned and canalized through the local FX market within the statutory window; any foreign currency held in an overseas account is a reportable foreign asset for Colombian tax informativa purposes; and treasury policy should consolidate receipts into one local USD account to avoid wire fragmentation and documentary burden.

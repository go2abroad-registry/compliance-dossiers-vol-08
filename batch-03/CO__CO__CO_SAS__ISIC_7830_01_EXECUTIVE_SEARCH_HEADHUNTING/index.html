Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists for S.A.S.; one or more shareholders may form the company with zero or nominal equity (e.g., one share of COP 1), and subscribed capital may be paid within the two-year legal term (Ley 1258/2008, art. 9). No escrow or banked deposit is required; the mid-tier classified registration tariff is approximately USD 90.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax and Customs Directorate (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales, DIAN) — Sede Electrónica at www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that intends to form or operate an S.A.S. in Colombia must obtain a Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT) and be listed in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT). The foreign parent or shareholder appoints a Colombian tax attorney-in-fact or legal representative. The representative registers the S.A.S. with DIAN through the electronic taxpayer portal ('Sede Electrónica') by filing the RUT form with the articles of incorporation, the certificates of legal existence from the Chamber of Commerce, and power-of-attorney documentation for the local representative. The procedure additionally requires activation of electronic invoicing and, if the company will carry out transactions subject to IVA or will import goods, an explicit IVA registration status in the RUT. A provisional NIT is issued in advance of final resolution.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not offer a non-fiscal check-the-box election analogous to U.S. Subchapter K. An S.A.S. is inherently an entity-level taxpayer for corporate income tax (renta corporativa). Available elections include the option for economic-group consolidated income tax filing under Articles 23 and 29 of Law 1819/2016 subject to strict DIAN authorisation, the election to apply the simplified transfer-pricing documentation regime for certain taxpayers, and the optional treatment of certain hybrid instruments under Law 2277/2022; hybrid mismatch relief is not elective and follows BEPS Action 2 rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold. Any entity that carries out a taxable activity or sells taxable goods or services within Colombian territory, or imports goods, must register with DIAN for IVA purposes irrespective of volume or annual sales. An S.A.S. engaged in executive search services must register as an IVA responsible party because its services are not classified among non-taxable services under Article 476 E.T.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated IVA treatment applies to (a) goods physically exported from Colombia, and (b) services provided to a non-resident entity or individual for use or enjoyment entirely outside Colombia ('servicios destinarresto al exterior'), including executive search and headhunting services when the search activity is performed for a foreign client, the strategic decision-making occurs abroad, and the Colombian provider does not have a permanent establishment abroad. To qualify, the S.A.S. must have the export-tax status in the RUT, issue the export invoice as 'Exenta' with 'Cufe' or 'CUDS', and provide OECD-compliant documentary proof of the foreign beneficial recipient's tax residence and the offshore location of service enjoyment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación — DEX) accepted by DIAN
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing physical shipment for goods (not applicable for pure cross-border services)
  • Mandatory export service registration: OCC - Marco Estratégico - Certificate of Non-Domiciled Service or verification in DIAN for services exports (Formato 14 - Declaración de Servicios Exportados)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Compensación / Foreign Exchange Certificate) issued by an authorised bank or via the foreign exchange brokerage regime demonstrating that the foreign client's consideration entered Colombia through the foreign currency market (Formulario No. 4 for export transactions)
  • Copy of a written international contract or letter of engagement specifying the beneficiary's foreign identity and the offshore performance of the headhunting services
  • Transfer-pricing file when services are supplied to a related foreign entity
  • Power-of-attorney and RUT export confirmation for the Colombian exporter

Colombia mandates real-time fiscal clearance through the 'Factura Electrónica de Venta' (FEV) system. Every IVA-responsible person must issue invoices through a DIAN-authorised software operator (Operador de Factura Electrónica) using XML standards and obtain a unique authentication code (CUFE/CUDS) stamped electronically before delivery. Transmission of invoice information to DIAN is required within predetermined time windows (generally 24 hours). Export and service-export invoices must include the applicable zero rating flag and export documentary references. A parallel 'Radian' system governs electronic negotiable invoices. Failure to obtain DIAN clearance invalidates tax credits and expenses for the buyer and exposes the seller to penalties and disallowance of input VAT.

The DIAN conducts proactive audits on zero-rated services and goods exports. Circular Instructions of the National Board of Customs and DIAN require that exported service revenue be repatriated within 180 days from issuance of the invoice. Failure or unjustified delay results in mandatory re-characterisation of the export as a taxable domestic operation, requiring payment of the 19% IVA plus current interest, penalty for unpaid tax up to 200% of the tax due under Article 647 and 648 of the Tax Code, and, if false documentation is used to obtain an IVA refund, criminal liability under Law 1762/2015 on customs smuggling and Law 1943/2018 (art. 257) with imprisonment of three to seven years and fines. False export declarations are subject to retroactive clawback of refunds for up to six years, suspension from the exporters registry, and disqualification from preferential trade regimes. The taxpayer bears the full burden of proving the physical exit or, for services, the actual foreign residence, use and payment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia distinguishes current-account and capital-account convertibility. Current account: export proceeds from services (executive-search retainers and placement fees) are freely convertible at the market rate (Tasa Representativa del Mercado — TRM) and require no prior authorization, but the full value must be channeled via an IMC within the statutory timeframe. Capital account: inbound foreign direct investment (paid-in capital from non-resident shareholders of the S.A.S.) is freely permitted but must be channeled through an IMC and registered through the electronic foreign-investment declaration at the receiving bank; no prior government approval applies to the executive-search sector. Profit remittance is not FX-restricted: the S.A.S. may remit dividends abroad once the dividend distribution is recorded in corporate minutes, the Colombian withholding tax on non-resident dividends (10% statutory rate under Ley 2277 of 2022, when applicable) is withheld and declared, and the outgoing wire is supported by the investment-registration certificate and the corresponding causal code in the IMC declaration process.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: proceeds from exported services (ISIC 7830 headhunting fees) must be repatriated and channeled through an IMC within six (6) months from the date of service completion or invoice issuance (per Circular DCIN-83 for services; goods exports run from the customs export declaration date). Mandatory conversion ratio: none — Colombia does not require any percentage of export proceeds to be converted into COP. The enforceable obligation is full canalización: 100% of the invoice value must either (a) be sold to the IMC at TRM, or (b) be credited to a foreign-currency deposit account (Cuenta en Moneda Extranjera) opened at an IMC in the name of the S.A.S., with either action evidenced by an electronic Declaración de Cambio. Failure to channel within the term triggers administrative fines, adverse tax consequences (disallowance of related expenses), and presumption of irregular export handling.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Banrep) — Colombia's central bank and steward of the exchange control regime under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, and the Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83. Enforcement is exercised jointly by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) over authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries (Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario, IMCs) and by DIAN for tax and declaratory cross-checks.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory rate is 5% on dividend distributions paid out of profits already subjected to corporate income tax; distributions sourced from nontaxed profits are subject to 35% withholding tax at the moment of distribution. A non-resident shareholder under a Colombian DTA may claim a reduced rate (commonly 5% to 10% for substantial holders) on the taxable dividend portion.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline is fixed each year by DIAN resolution based on the last two digits of the taxpayer’s NIT. For a standard calendar-year S.A.S., the return generally falls due in the months of March through June of the following year. The taxpayer must observe the exact annual DIAN payment/filing calendar.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Colombia uses UVTs (Unidad de Valor Tributario) rather than a fixed peso flat line. For tax year 2024, mandatory Local File documentation is triggered when the taxpayer's gross income exceeds 150,000 UVT (approximately COP 7,179,750,000 using the 2024 UVT of COP 47,865) or when net worth exceeds 45,000 UVT (approximately COP 2,153,925,000). Multinational groups with consolidated revenues above the Colombian CbC threshold are also required to maintain a Master File and file Country-by-Country data; all threshold amounts are adjusted annually by DIAN resolution.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not enact a UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulation, but the doctrine is enforced through general anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership clauses in DTAs, PE attribution principles, and DIAN's authority to disregard artificial step-up schemes. An executive-search S.A.S. must maintain real personnel, an operational office, candidate and contract files, payroll registrations, and decision-making management in Colombia; a shell company with nominal ownership and no local activity will have deductions disallowed, the substance of the foreign parent ignored, and treaty access denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: DIAN raises a tax adjustment equal to the omitted tax together with default interest and imposes the 'sanción por inexactitud' of 100% of the understatement, increased to 200% when transactions involve related offshore parties or sham arrangements. Criminal tax fraud can produce imprisonment of 5 to 9 years, fines, seizure of corporate and personal assets, and cancellation of the S.A.S. RUT.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who knowingly sign false returns or cause back-to-back invoicing may be personally liable for the corporate underpayment, subject to a travel ban when leaving Colombia, entered into the DIAN administrative enforcement register, and exposed to personal indemnity actions. The Colombian criminal court may issue pre-trial preventive detention and an order prohibiting departure from the national territory.
Transfer Pricing and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the controlled transaction and recharacterize the full Colombian revenue at arm's length, resulting in an increased taxable income, 35% tax, default interest, and a transfer pricing documentation sanction computed on the value of the transactions (up to 25% of the value of the omitted operations). A 100% understatement penalty for TP misstatement is also imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length arrangements with shareholder affiliates are jointly responsible for the company's tax deficit to the extent of fraud or gross negligence. They may be debarred from serving in the S.A.S., required to reimburse corporate damages, and criminally liable if they knowingly forged intercompany service agreements.
Shell Company / Sham Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: DIAN and the competent registry may declare the entity fiscally nonexistent for tax purposes, disallow all costs, tax the gross billings, apply the 100% inexactitude penalty, and levy additional sanctions for carrying on economic activity without an effective RUT and properly registered books. The supervising chamber of commerce may initiate administrative cancellation.

Director personal liability: Under S.A.S. Law 1258/2008, misuse of the legal entity to conceal individual operations and evade or defraud creditors or the tax authority pierces the corporate veil. Directors, shareholders and beneficial owners become personally and solidarily liable for the S.A.S.'s tax debts, plus civil damages and criminal sanctions for fraud.
AML Violations and Fraudulent Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: UAFE penalties for non-reporting can reach significant economic fines; money-laundering under Colombian Criminal Code Articles 323 and 325 is punished with 10 to 15 years’ imprisonment, fines equivalent to hundreds of statutory minimum salaries, asset forfeiture, and closure of the S.A.S.'s bank account by the Colombian Financial Superintendence.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and nominal shareholders who monitor but do not report suspicious flows face personal criminal liability as co-authors or 'determinadores' of money laundering. Personal travel bans, confiscation of personal property, and imprisonment apply irrespective of the corporate form, and civil restitution may be claimed by harmed partners, clients and the State.

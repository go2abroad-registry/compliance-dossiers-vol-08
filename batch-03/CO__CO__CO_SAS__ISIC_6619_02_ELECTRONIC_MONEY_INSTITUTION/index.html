Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio of the registered domicile (e.g., Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá) acting as the commercial registry under the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Tourism; corporate oversight by the Superintendencia de Sociedades; operating authorization for the electronic-money activity issued by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC).

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 (Colombian S.A.S. Law), particularly Articles 1-5 (constitution through private document), 9 (corporate purpose), 17-19 (capital), 22 (legal representation), 45 (share transfers); complemented by the Colombian Commercial Code (Decreto 410 de 1971) Articles 110, 353-372 (commercial registry), Decree 1074 de 2015 (business formalities), Decree 2555 de 2010 (financial-sector organization) and Ley 1735 de 2014 / Decree 1499 de 2015 (electronic-money institutions).

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (private constitutive document with notarized signatures) inscribed in the Registro Mercantil; a public deed (Escritura Pública) is only required if real property is contributed. The fintech activity further requires a separate SFC authorization (Autorización de Funcionamiento).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Ley 1258/2008 imposes no minimum paid-up capital; the S.A.S. must only subscribe and pay, in cash or in kind, at least one share under the payment terms fixed in the bylaws. However, as a condition of the e-money license, the SFC requires a fully paid-up regulatory equity of approximately COP 2,000,000,000 (USD ~500,000, as annually adjusted Comisionable), which must be deposited in a blocked escrow-like account at a supervised credit institution and may not be drawn down except as authorized by the SFC during the licensing stage.

Funding & unlock path:

Paid-in capital rule: Ley 1258/2008 imposes no minimum paid-up capital; the S.A.S. must only subscribe and pay, in cash or in kind, at least one share under the payment terms fixed in the bylaws. However, as a condition of the e-money license, the SFC requires a fully paid-up regulatory equity of approximately COP 2,000,000,000 (USD ~500,000, as annually adjusted Comisionable), which must be deposited in a blocked escrow-like account at a supervised credit institution and may not be drawn down except as authorized by the SFC during the licensing stage.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Directorate of Taxes and Customs of Colombia (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales – DIAN); electronic channel: MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) available at www.dian.gov.co and muisca.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Colombian S.A.S.: (1) the by-laws are executed before a notary and registered with the Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce), which assigns a provisional 10-digit NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through the single business registry RUES; (2) the legal representative must complete and validate the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) with DIAN within the statutory term, either remotely via MUISCA using a qualified digital signature (firma electrónica issued by an accredited certification authority) or in-person at a DIAN service point, declaring IVA-responsible status, withholding-agent status and, when applicable, exporter-of-services status; (3) DIAN then activates the definitive NIT and the entity obtains its electronic invoicing authorization (rango de numeración). For a foreign non-resident entity without Colombian incorporation, the company obtains a corporate NIT by appointing a local attorney-in-fact with public power, filing apostilled constitutional documents, and completing the MUISCA pre-registration followed by in-person validation; however, in this dossier the local operating entity is a Colombian S.A.S. and therefore follows steps (1)-(3). As a mandatory condition precedent, the electronic money institution must first obtain a license from the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) as a Sociedad Especializada en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos (SEDPE) under Decree 2555/2010, and the RUT must accurately reflect ISIC/CIUU Rev.4 class 6619 (auxiliary activities to financial services) together with the SEDPE authorization reference.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – a Colombian S.A.S. is an autonomous taxpayer of the Colombian corporate income tax (Impuesto sobre la Renta y Complementarios) at the flat 35% statutory rate applicable for FY2024-2025 (Ley 2277/2022); no partnership pass-through or fiscal consolidation is permitted for an S.A.S.; Colombian resident entities are taxed on worldwide income with a foreign tax credit, not on a pure territorial basis; dividends paid to non-resident shareholders attract a 10% final withholding on the after-CIT distributable profit (Art. 408 E.T.), while any untaxed or exempt profit component of the distribution is subject to an additional deemed 35% charge at shareholder level, producing a combined effective rate of approximately 41.5% on fully distributed profits; the optional SIMPLE combined tax regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación) is capped at 80,000 UVT of annual gross income (approx. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a Colombian S.A.S. is an autonomous taxpayer of the Colombian corporate income tax (Impuesto sobre la Renta y Complementarios) at the flat 35% statutory rate applicable for FY2024-2025 (Ley 2277/2022); no partnership pass-through or fiscal consolidation is permitted for an S.A.S.; Colombian resident entities are taxed on worldwide income with a foreign tax credit, not on a pure territorial basis; dividends paid to non-resident shareholders attract a 10% final withholding on the after-CIT distributable profit (Art. 408 E.T.), while any untaxed or exempt profit component of the distribution is subject to an additional deemed 35% charge at shareholder level, producing a combined effective rate of approximately 41.5% on fully distributed profits; the optional SIMPLE combined tax regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación) is capped at 80,000 UVT of annual gross income (approx. COP 3.45 billion for FY2024) and is not available to SFC-supervised financial entities such as a licensed EMI/SEDPE; therefore the S.A.S. remains mandatorily within the ordinary entity-level tax regime.

Available election forms

Colombia has no US-style check-the-box or elective pass-through regime; relevant elections and formal mechanisms available to this entity include: (a) ordinary CIT versus the SIMPLE regime, which is generally unavailable to licensed SEDPE financial entities; (b) RUT classification as IVA-responsible with mandatory electronic invoicing, versus the non-resident supplier withholding treatment for foreign providers without a Colombian establishment; (c) the formal election/registration of zero-rated export-of-services status under Art. 481 E.T. for virtual IBAN, digital wallet management and payment processing services supplied exclusively abroad, triggered by updating the RUT as exporter and registering the export operations; (d) transfer pricing and advance-pricing agreements (APA) as voluntary procedural instruments, not substantive tax elections; and (e) hybrid mismatch rules introduced under Ley 2277/2022 which apply automatically without elective carve-outs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Zero economic threshold for legal entities: every S.A.S. carrying out taxable supplies must register and remain registered in the RUT as an IVA-responsible taxpayer before commencing operations; the reduced small-trader exemption (natural persons with prior-year gross income not exceeding 3,500 UVT, approximately COP 151 million for FY2024) is not available to companies or to licensed financial entities; accordingly, registration is mandatory regardless of turnover, and no statutory de-minimis relief exists for the Colombian operating entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Art. 481 E.T. For the fintech/EMI scope (virtual IBAN issuance, digital wallet reserve management, payment and clearing services to non-residents), zero-rating is available only if all of the following criteria are met: (i) the service beneficiary is a person without residence or domicile in Colombia and without a permanent establishment in the country; (ii) the service is rendered from Colombia but utilized exclusively abroad; (iii) the service falls within the exportable-services catalogue of Decreto 1625/2016 (Chapter 1.3.1.9, integrating former Decree 1805/2010), which expressly includes financial, payment, IT and back-office services provided to foreign principals; (iv) the consideration is paid by the non-resident in foreign currency and is channelled through the Colombian exchange market (Mercado Cambiario); and (v) the exporter is registered in the RUT with active exporter status, holds the SEDPE license, and evidences each transaction with the corresponding service export certificate. Domestic financial intermediation income in Colombia is excluded from IVA (without creditable input VAT), so the zero-rating route is the principal mechanism for recovering input VAT embedded in technology, KYC/AML and international office infrastructure; the exporter may offset or obtain a monthly refund of such input VAT in the VAT return, subject to DIAN's refund certification procedures.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX/DAE) and commercial export invoice (factura comercial / Factura Electrónica de Exportación) – mandatory for goods; for pure services, replaced by the DIAN-validated electronic invoice and the signed service export contract
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or equivalent dispatch evidence – applicable to physical goods only; for services, the service delivery log, system usage records and a certificate from the foreign beneficiary confirming exclusive use abroad
  • Inward Remittance Certificate – Colombian Declaración de Cambio por exportación de servicios lodged through the authorized intermediary of the Mercado Cambiario, evidencing collection of the foreign currency export proceeds within the legal term
  • RUT excerpt showing active IVA-responsible status, exporter annotation, SEDPE license reference and ISIC/CIUU 6619 registered activity
  • Certificado de Exportación de Servicios issued through the VUCE single foreign-trade window (when the transaction value exceeds the applicable DIAN threshold) or written registration of the service export transaction with the competent authority
  • Payment evidence: SWIFT/MT103 credit notices, bank asset cards and reconciliation statements matching the exchange-market declaration with the invoiced amount
  • E-invoicing transmission receipts (XML, CUFE/CUDE validation codes and DIAN acceptance responses) for each export invoice

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing under DIAN Resolution 000165/2023: every sales invoice must be issued as a Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV), generated in XML/CII format, digitally signed with an X.509 certificate, transmitted through DIAN web-service APIs (SOAP/REST over HTTPS) or via an authorized Operador Tecnológico/FE (OFE), validated by DIAN before the transaction is fiscally complete, and stamped with the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) or CUDE code plus QR; invoice consecutive numbering must be pre-authorized by DIAN through the rango de numeración mechanism; supporting documents include the Documento Soporte en Adquisiciones a No Obligados and electronic withholding certificates, and the annual exógena information return must be filed with DIAN; in addition, the entity remains subject to SFC information-reporting obligations over its licensed payment rails.

Export zero-rating is a priority audit area for DIAN. Consequences of non-compliance include: (a) retroactive re-characterization of the 0% rate to 19% IVA on the entire value of the supplies, together with a demand for the tax differential; (b) the Art. 648 inexactness penalty (sanción por inexactitud) of 100% of the understated tax (50% where the difference is minor and not deliberately hidden, and 160% where the claim is supported by false or non-existent invoices); (c) statutory refund-improperness penalties of up to 100% of the value of refunds improperly requested or obtained; (d) monthly default interest at approximately 2.12% per month (circa 28.6% annual) accrued from the original filing date; (e) fines under the Banco de la República exchange-control regime for late, inaccurate or absent Declaración de Cambio and for failure to surrender export proceeds within the 180-day term; (f) criminal liability for fraudulently obtaining tax refunds and offsets (Ley 1762/2015, Código Penal Art. 402) punishable by 7 to 12 years of imprisonment; and (g) administrative measures such as inclusion in DIAN's refund-risk blacklist, pre-audit withholding of refund payments, and suspension of the exporter status. The ordinary DIAN assessment period is 5 years, extended to 10 years where returns are not filed or are based on fraudulent or fictitious documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains full convertibility for current-account transactions (trade, services, transfers), but all settlements between residents and non-residents must be routed through authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries and recorded on an exchange declaration. Capital-account transactions are freely transferable but require registration with Banco de la República for outbound remittance rights; foreign direct investment into an S.A.S. must be registered to secure future dividend repatriation and capital reduction proceeds. Profit remittance is lawful after tax withholding/clearance, dividend taxation and, for profits attributable to a permanent establishment, remittance tax; repatriation must be done via the authorized FX market with proper supporting declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds are not 'surrendered' to the Central Bank; they are subject to a channeling obligation through the foreign-exchange market. For goods exports, receipts generally must be credited to a Colombian bank account and reported on an exchange declaration within the statutory period (commonly up to six months from export customs declaration, with shorter windows for services). There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio into Colombian pesos; the exporter may maintain foreign currency in a local deposit/compensation account, provided the exchange declaration is timely filed and the underlying export is properly registered.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), exercising foreign-exchange administration jointly with the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1068 of 2015 and the regulatory circulars of the foreign-exchange regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low standalone viability. An offshore digital electronic-money institution cannot issue payment/e-money accounts to Colombian residents, provide virtual IBANs, or hold customer float without a license from the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC). It may only operate as a software/processing vendor to an authorized Colombian payment institution or licensed bank. The Colombian legal vehicle for e-money/payments is the SEDPE under Law 1735 of 2014, and non-bank EMIs must partner with supervised banks to access clearing rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contributions should be remitted by the non-resident investor via SWIFT MT103 to the S.A.S.'s local bank account, with payment narrative identifying the company NIT, investor entity, invoice/counterparty reference and the purpose code for foreign direct investment (capital contribution). The local bank will file the corresponding exchange declaration with Banco de la República; after funds are received, the SAS must complete the FDI registration (if not already registered) to legally support future dividend remittances and capital repatriation. Funds cannot enter the e-money float and must be credited only to the capital/operational reserve account specified at onboarding.Outbound payments use SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border disbursements and local high-value clearing in Colombian pesos for domestic disbursements. For procurement of goods or large contractual services, an irrevocable documentary commercial letter of credit opened by the Colombian tier-1 bank is preferred, using the standard SWIFT MT700/MT710 mechanism and settlement at sight or by term draft. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low standalone viability. An offshore digital electronic-money institution cannot issue payment/e-money accounts to Colombian residents, provide virtual IBANs, or hold customer float without a license from the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC). It may only operate as a software/processing vendor to an authorized Colombian payment institution or licensed bank. The Colombian legal vehicle for e-money/payments is the SEDPE under Law 1735 of 2014, and non-bank EMIs must partner with supervised banks to access clearing rails.

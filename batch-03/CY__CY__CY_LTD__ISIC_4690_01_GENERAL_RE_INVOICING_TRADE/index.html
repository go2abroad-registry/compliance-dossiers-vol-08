Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a private company limited by shares in Cyprus; no escrow account is required. The company may issue shares of any nominal value in EUR or another currency as provided in its Memorandum, and capital may be paid in cash or in kind as approved by the board.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Fire Service of the Republic of Cyprus for the office premises, structural load inspection certificate issued by a licensed civil engineer, and occupational health and safety clearance from the Department of Labour Inspection before employees occupy the office. For a pure administrative re-invoicing office, no special industrial zoning or heavy-goods clearance is required.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a private company limited by shares in Cyprus; no escrow account is required. The company may issue shares of any nominal value in EUR or another currency as provided in its Memorandum, and capital may be paid in cash or in kind as approved by the board.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance (Republic of Cyprus), operating the integrated tax information system 'ARIADNE' (https://ariadne.mof.gov.cy); incorporation and company registry functions are vested in the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DRCOR); VAT and Corporate Income Tax administration are unified within the Tax Department.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus Private Limited Company obtains its statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) automatically at incorporation, the TIN being identical to the Company Registration Number (HE-series), and must complete electronic taxpayer registration with the Tax Department through the ARIADNE portal within 15 days of incorporation. Separate VAT registration is required under Article 29 of VAT Law 94(I)/2000 where taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600 in any 12-month period or where intra-EU acquisitions exceed EUR 10,000, and is mandatory without threshold for non-established foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Cyprus; non-resident entities must appoint a tax representative and submit Form TD2001/VAT-01 via ARIADNE or to the district Tax Office, accompanied by certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, director/shareholder KYC identification, proof of place of business, and a forecast of taxable turnover.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Cyprus tax-resident Private Limited Company is subject to 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income; no pass-through or fiscally transparent classification is available for companies; foreign permanent establishment and dividend/interest income benefit from territorial carve-outs subject to specific conditions; the company is not a tax-exempt zone entity and enjoys no general tax holiday.

Available election forms

No check-the-box classification exists. Available statutory elections include: (1) tax residence election via the management-and-control test in Cyprus; (2) group loss relief election for companies in a 75%+ group relationship; (3) notional interest deduction (NID) election on new equity capital in lieu of debt interest; (4) group VAT registration election; (5) EU intra-Community triangulation simplification election (Directive 2006/112/EC Article 141) for re-invoicing hubs; (6) OSS/IOSS non-Union scheme election for cross-border distance sales of goods; and (7) the VAT cash accounting scheme election available for entities with turnover below EUR 500,000.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 (annual taxable turnover in Cyprus within the preceding 12 months) for locally established taxable persons; EUR 10,000 for cross-border intra-EU distance sales and for intra-EU acquisitions by non-taxable legal persons; non-established/foreign suppliers have a zero registration threshold once any taxable supply occurs in Cyprus; voluntary registration is permitted without a minimum turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical exports of goods to non-EU destinations are zero-rated under Article 59 of the VAT Law provided the supplier possesses and retains reliable evidence of physical export within 90 days of the supply; intra-EU dispatches are not zero-rated but are exempt with credit when the customer is VAT-registered and the dispatch is corroborated by VIES and Intrastat declarations; a re-invoicing hub whose goods never cross Cyprus borders may apply the EU triangular simplification to zero-rate the intermediate supply, provided that title transfer, transport documents, customer's EU VAT identification, and invoice sequencing are fully documented; offshore trading and intermediary commission services rendered to non-resident taxable persons qualify for 0% rate under Article 47 place-of-supply rules when the B2B reverse charge applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / EX-A) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airway Bill (AWB) or CMR consignment note evidencing cross-border movement
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Incoterms 2020 contract/sales instrument evidencing transfer of ownership and risk
  • Proof of payment and Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation
  • VIES recapitulative statement and Intrastat declaration for intra-Community movements
  • EU Customs proof of physical exit (customs office certification or T1 transit document)
  • Third-party logistics warehouse receipts or transport portfolio where goods are shipped or consolidated outside Cyprus

Electronic filing of VAT returns, VIES recapitulative statements, and Intrastat declarations is mandatory via the ARIADNE fiscal portal; B2G e-invoicing is being phased in under EU e-invoicing standards (PEPPOL); no mandatory real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance, fiscal stamping, or API-based digital clearance regime applies to B2B invoices as of the current framework; invoices must nevertheless meet Cyprus/EU VAT Law content, validity, and 5-year archiving requirements.

Where export evidence is deficient, the Tax Department may disallow zero-rating and re-assess VAT at 19% together with default interest at 9% per annum and administrative penalties up to 200% of the tax understated for negligence or fraud; criminal liability extends to directors and officers, with unlimited fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years under the penal provisions of the VAT Law; retrospective assessments may reach back 10 years in cases of fraud or wilful default (standard limitation period 6 years); EU Customs Code sanctions (Regulation (EU) No 952/2013, Article 42) include seizure and confiscation of goods; joint and several liability applies to participants in missing-trader or carousel fraud; and the Cyprus GAAR (Income Tax Law anti-abuse provisions) permits retrospective recharacterisation of triangular or re-invoicing arrangements lacking economic substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a euro-area member state; the euro is fully convertible for current account transactions such as goods and services and for capital account transactions. There is no substantive exchange-control regime under the Central Bank of Cyprus Law 2002. Profit remittance by a Cyprus private limited company is generally free; Cyprus does not impose withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders under domestic law, subject to anti-abuse, EU Sanctions, and Money Laundering / Proceeds of Crime measures. Practical hurdles may arise from correspondent-bank de-risking and compliance screening rather than statutory convertibility limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Cyprus. Cash transactions of €10,000 or above must be declared under EU Regulation 2018/1672; all export receipts must be booked in the company's accounts and made available to auditors and tax authorities. Foreign-currency receivables may be retained in any freely convertible currency, but the economic substance of the re-invoicing trade requires that settlement be completed in accordance with the underlying contracts and within a timeframe consistent with normal international trade cycles. The statutory timeline is therefore nil; internal treasury policy must determine repatriation deadlines.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα Κύπρου) in coordination with the Cyprus Ministry of Finance and the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission for licensed entities

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is LOW-MEDIUM for use of an offshore unlicensed digital EMI as the primary treasury rail. A Cyprus-domiciled trade intermediary will normally need a relationship with a Cyprus-licensed credit institution to issue commercial letters of credit and to provide full AML coverage. EEA-authorized EMIs can offer payment services via European passporting, but non-EEA EMIs are generally not viable as principal banks. An EEA-licensed EMI may be used as a supplementary disbursement channel for small-value payments, but not for large underlying trade settlements, LC issuance, or capital injections. Strong recommendation: maintain a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank account as the primary clearing node.

Defense evidence: Show a documented sanctions compliance framework including real-time OFAC/EU/UN screening, end-use certificates from every buyer/seller, and legal representation of the nature and destination of the underlying goods.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital injection into the CY entity should be routed via SWIFT MT103 with the purpose code 'P01 – Investment in equity capital' (BOP/IMF classification) or for shareholder loans 'P02 – Loans related to direct investment'. Banks require an executed share subscription agreement, shareholder loan agreement, directors' resolution, and source-of-funds documentation for each injection. Amounts above €100,000 must be captured in the Central Bank's balance-of-payments reporting; the bank will normally require the company's projected cash-flow statements and evidence of business substance.Outbound supplier payments should be executed via SWIFT MT103 in euro, or as a cross-currency MT103 with explicit FX conversion for non-euro suppliers. For high-value goods and documentary risk, use an irrevocable confirmed commercial letter of credit under UCP600. Intra-group re-invoicing payments should be supported by purchase and resale invoices and use appropriate BOP purpose codes such as 'SEL – Purchase of goods' or transaction code 'M1 – Goods importing'. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% at source on outbound dividends paid to non-resident shareholders (domestic law imposes no withholding tax on dividends); Cypriot-resident corporate and individual shareholders remain exposed to SDC at 17%; the deemed distribution rule applies where an entity retains more than 30% of after-tax distributable profits for more than two years.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 15 months after the end of the accounting period (e.g., a company with a 31 December year-end must file by 31 March two calendar years later).

Deadline: · Frequency:

75% of estimated annual CIT due by 31 July of the tax year and the remaining 25% by 31 December of the tax year; new companies pay the full estimate by 31 December of the year of incorporation.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For each tax year, undistributed profits become deemed-distributed two years after the end of the tax year (e.g., FY2023 profits deemed distributed by 31 December 2025); the SDC return and payment are due concurrently.

All turnover is reportable to the Cyprus Tax Department; no distinction is made between domestic and foreign-sourced turnover for CIT purposes (Cyprus taxes worldwide income of residents). Late filing: fixed EUR 100 penalty plus EUR 50 per month of continued default; late payment: statutory interest at the annually adjusted rate (approximately 1.75% per annum at date of issue) plus a surcharge under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law; failure to file provisional tax (IPV) similarly attracts monthly penalties before the return deadline.
Cost of sales / purchases Cost of goods sold, warehousing, freight and supplier invoices matched to the corresponding sales period. Cross-border related-party purchases must be disclosed as controlled transactions and benchmarked at arm's length. Late filing: fixed EUR 100 penalty plus EUR 50 per month of continued default; late payment: statutory interest at the annually adjusted rate (approximately 1.75% per annum at date of issue) plus a surcharge under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law; failure to file provisional tax (IPV) similarly attracts monthly penalties before the return deadline.
Other business income Commission income, performance fees, interest and foreign exchange gains. Passive interest received by the Cyprus company may attract Special Defence Contribution (SDC) at 30% and must be reported separately on the SDC return. Late filing: fixed EUR 100 penalty plus EUR 50 per month of continued default; late payment: statutory interest at the annually adjusted rate (approximately 1.75% per annum at date of issue) plus a surcharge under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law; failure to file provisional tax (IPV) similarly attracts monthly penalties before the return deadline.
Deductible expenses Wholly and exclusively incurred revenue expenses: staff costs, rent, professional fees, bank charges, insurance, and regulatory costs. Group management fees and intercompany service charges are deductible only if supported by evidence of actual provision (benefit test) and arm's length pricing. Late filing: fixed EUR 100 penalty plus EUR 50 per month of continued default; late payment: statutory interest at the annually adjusted rate (approximately 1.75% per annum at date of issue) plus a surcharge under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law; failure to file provisional tax (IPV) similarly attracts monthly penalties before the return deadline.
Non-deductible additions Statutory add-backs: 50% of entertainment expenses (general cap), donations without prescribed receipt, penalties, personal expenditures, and CIT itself. Notional charges, shareholder perquisites and payments lacking economic substance must be added back. Late filing: fixed EUR 100 penalty plus EUR 50 per month of continued default; late payment: statutory interest at the annually adjusted rate (approximately 1.75% per annum at date of issue) plus a surcharge under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law; failure to file provisional tax (IPV) similarly attracts monthly penalties before the return deadline.
Tax losses brought forward Cumulative unrelieved business losses of prior years (5-year cap) set off against current-year profits. Losses of a foreign branch are available where a treaty prevents double taxation; terminal losses require evidence of cessation. Late filing: fixed EUR 100 penalty plus EUR 50 per month of continued default; late payment: statutory interest at the annually adjusted rate (approximately 1.75% per annum at date of issue) plus a surcharge under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law; failure to file provisional tax (IPV) similarly attracts monthly penalties before the return deadline.
Notional Interest Deduction claim NID computed at the annual authorised rate applied to qualifying new equity paid into the company and retained as working capital. Shareholder loans and re-characterised debt do not qualify; documentary proof of cash injection and retention (bank statements, audited accounts) must be retained. Late filing: fixed EUR 100 penalty plus EUR 50 per month of continued default; late payment: statutory interest at the annually adjusted rate (approximately 1.75% per annum at date of issue) plus a surcharge under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law; failure to file provisional tax (IPV) similarly attracts monthly penalties before the return deadline.
Taxable profit and CIT computation Chargeable profit multiplied by the 12.5% headline rate, after participation exemption captured for exempt dividends and capital gains. Exempt distribution income must not be included; a schedule of participating holdings should accompany the return. Late filing: fixed EUR 100 penalty plus EUR 50 per month of continued default; late payment: statutory interest at the annually adjusted rate (approximately 1.75% per annum at date of issue) plus a surcharge under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law; failure to file provisional tax (IPV) similarly attracts monthly penalties before the return deadline.
Foreign tax credit and net tax due Unilateral credit for creditable foreign withholding taxes and foreign CIT at the rate of the Cypriot tax on the same income; offset against provisional IPV instalments already paid. Tax credit certificates (TRC, withholding tax vouchers) in English must be attached; excess credits are neither refundable nor carried forward beyond the year. Late filing: fixed EUR 100 penalty plus EUR 50 per month of continued default; late payment: statutory interest at the annually adjusted rate (approximately 1.75% per annum at date of issue) plus a surcharge under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law; failure to file provisional tax (IPV) similarly attracts monthly penalties before the return deadline.
Estimated chargeable income Forecast of taxable profit after losses and NID for the year. Estimates should reflect arm's length margins expected under the intercompany pricing policy. Non-filing or under-declaration of IPV attracts EUR 100 fixed penalty, monthly compounding penalties, and interest; deliberate under-estimation may be re-determined on estimate by the Commissioner.
IPV instalment calculation Computation of first and second instalment amounts after deduction of any foreign tax credits. No exemption from IPV is available to non-resident-owned Ltds; underpayment is corrected at final assessment. Non-filing or under-declaration of IPV attracts EUR 100 fixed penalty, monthly compounding penalties, and interest; deliberate under-estimation may be re-determined on estimate by the Commissioner.
Deemed dividend base 70% of the accounting profits after taxation of the relevant year, reduced by actual dividends paid and adjusted for the small-company exemption. Deemed distribution applies irrespective of the non-resident status of shareholders; the company, not the shareholder, is the taxpayer and must fund the 17% SDC. SDC remains payable even where no distribution is made; evasion of SDC on deemed dividends is treated as a tax offence with interest, tiered penalties of up to 100% of the duty, and criminal sanction.
Small company exemption confirmation Certification that aggregate gross assets do not exceed EUR 350,000 and aggregate dividends received do not exceed EUR 70,000 before the distribution (exemption disapplied on breach). A trading re-invoicing hub with third-party turnover is not automatically excluded from the exemption; only asset and dividend tests apply. SDC remains payable even where no distribution is made; evasion of SDC on deemed dividends is treated as a tax offence with interest, tiered penalties of up to 100% of the duty, and criminal sanction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File: mandatory for Cyprus entities belonging to an MNE group with consolidated group revenue at or above EUR 750,000,000. Local File: mandatory for a Cyprus taxpayer when the aggregate value of controlled transactions in a tax year exceeds EUR 2,000,000 for purchases/sales of goods or for financial transactions, or EUR 200,000 for services, royalties and intangibles. (Lower per-transaction documentation on a simplified basis remains prudent; all cross-border transactions with related parties must be priced at arm's length regardless of the documentation threshold.)

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has no codified Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of the UAE type; however, economic substance is enforced indirectly and with equal force: (i) Cyprus tax residence and treaty access require that central management and control, board meetings, strategic decisions and the general management and control of the company be exercised in Cyprus; (ii) the re-invoicing hub must perform and evidence DEMPE functions (sales origination, negotiation, logistics coordination, risk management and financial control) to justify its profit allocation; (iii) the EU ATAD CFC rules and the new EU Transfer Pricing Directive 2023/2226 (implemented in Cyprus TP legislation) require actual people functions, assets and risks, penalising 'paper' functions; (iv) in administrative practice the Tax Department refuses certificates of residence and grants validity to DTA claims only for companies with real premises, local employees, and substantive business activity. A pure re-invoicing entity lacking functions, assets and risk management is liable to be recharacterised as a booking entity and to have its intercompany pricing disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (fraudulent return/declaration)

Statutory sanction: Civil reassessment: evaded tax plus statutory interest (currently approximately 1.75% p.a.) plus an administrative penalty under the Tax Department tiered policy of up to 100% of the undercharged tax; criminal conviction on indictment in the District Court for false statement with intent to evade tax: fine and/or imprisonment of up to five years; mandatory restitution of evaded duties.

Director personal liability: Directors and the beneficial owner who signed or caused the false return are prosecuted personally; the corporate veil is pierced for fraud, with civil restitution from the directors' assets, asset-freezing and travel-ban orders, in addition to criminal imprisonment.
Artificial shell / sham re-invoicing without substance

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may disregard the corporate form under anti-avoidance doctrines, recharacterise gross invoiced flows, reallocate profit to the controlling foreign shareholder, and assess CIT at 12.5% on the reallocated profit plus interest and a penalty of up to 100%; the Tax Department may also refuse a Certificate of Tax Residence, exposing the company to DTA relief denial and 0% treaty benefits being revoked.

Director personal liability: Nominee and executive directors face liability for knowingly participating in a sham arrangement: civil damages for misleading creditors, disqualification orders, and criminal accountability for false declarations in annual returns to the Registrar of Companies.
Transfer pricing mispricing and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment to arm's length profit with consequent tax, statutory interest, and a tiered penalty assessed on the adjusted tax differential (20%-100% depending on culpability); persistent non-production of documentation is a criminal non-cooperation offence carrying a fine and, in aggravated cases, imprisonment up to three years.

Director personal liability: The director responsible for tax returns bears secondary civil liability for the TP adjustment where the company is unable to pay following concealment of documentation; disqualification and travel bans may attach to the enforcement of the assessment.
AML / trade-based money laundering through phantom invoices

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Law (188(I)/2007): confiscation orders equal to the value of laundered property, civil forfeiture, and fines; the company's banking relationship is terminated with consequential contract losses; criminal conviction for money laundering attracts imprisonment of up to eight years and unlimited fines at the discretion of the court.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false invoices face personal criminal conviction, disqualification under the Companies Law, asset-freezing, and travel-ban and extradition risk; the corporate veil is pierced for restitution to victims and to regulators.
Deemed dividend distribution evasion (SDC)

Statutory sanction: The deemed dividend rule operates automatically: 17% SDC charged on 70% of after-tax undistributed profits (a cost of 11.9% of profits), compounded by interest and penalties for each year of non-declaration; on audit the SDC is additionally treated as a non-deductible expense and the company may be struck off for failure to file SDC returns.

Director personal liability: The compliance officer/director who prepared the SDC return is personally liable for the SDC, interest and penalties if the company cannot pay; courts may issue personal debt judgments and travel restrictions to enforce the tax debt.
Profit strip via shareholder loans / non-arm's-length financing

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of excess interest as a dividend is made with CIT disallowance of the interest deduction (12.5% cost), SDC at 17% on the recharacterised distribution, and penalty up to 100% of the understated tax; participation exemption is lost for abusive structures; group thin capitalisation limits inferred from ALP replace book interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who certified inflated loan agreements face personal civil restitution for the tax shortfall, personal guarantees taken at enforcement, and criminal exposure for reckless false statements in tax filings.

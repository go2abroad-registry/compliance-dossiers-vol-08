Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας); online portal: TaxForAll (Ariadne)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Cyprus private limited company must first incorporate with the Registrar of Companies and receive a registered number. The Tax Department then issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon application via Form TAN 102/103, followed by VAT registration through Form VAT 101 if taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold. Non-resident companies with no fixed establishment but making taxable supplies in Cyprus must appoint a fiscal representative and register for VAT directly before commencing activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a Cyprus private limited company must first incorporate with the Registrar of Companies and receive a registered number. The Tax Department then issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon application via Form TAN 102/103, followed by VAT registration through Form VAT 101 if taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold. Non-resident companies with no fixed establishment but making taxable supplies in Cyprus must appoint a fiscal representative and register for VAT directly before commencing activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus generally applies entity-level taxation; no check-the-box classification exists. However, an IP Box regime election under Section 26A of the Income Tax Law allows reduced effective taxation on qualifying intangible property (80% deduction). Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD-aligned legislation (Income Tax Law Sections 28A-28E) impose mandatory adjustments rather than elective mechanisms. Certain collective investment schemes and partnerships may elect transparent treatment under specific conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for goods (EU standard distance sales threshold EUR 10,000 for intra-EU distance sales); EUR 15,600 for services; non-resident suppliers without establishment in Cyprus are required to register for VAT, regardless of turnover, if they supply digitally supplied services to non-taxable persons (OSS scheme optional).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the supply of goods dispatched or transported outside Cyprus by the supplier or on behalf of the customer (B2B and B2C), including pet food exports. Services relating to transported goods and ancillary transport services benefitting from the place-of-supply rules (Article 35 of the VAT Law) may also be zero-rated if supplied to customers outside Cyprus. Evidence must be obtained within 3 months of supply and retained for at least 6 years. The exporter must hold a valid VAT registration, evidence of export proof, and meet the conditions of VAT Law Article 100 (0% rate) as amended.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document – SAD) Form EX1
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Proof of Shipment / Customs signed and stamped certificate of shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank payment evidence confirming foreign exchange receipt
  • Commercial Invoice and Export Sales Contract
  • Cargo Manifest / Packing List
  • Insurance or Certificate of Origin (optional but recommended)

Cyprus currently does not mandate real-time e-invoicing clearance for domestic B2B supplies. The EU ViDA rules are anticipated; mandatory e-invoicing for public procurement is required (national e-invoicing platform). There is no live fiscal stamping or API clearance mandate for standard VAT invoices; however, reporting obligations via VIES, Intrastat, and Slovak-style clearance do not apply. Non-EU electronic transmission of invoices in PDF or e-Invoice XML remains permissible. Cypriot authorities may request digital signatures for high-risk exporters.

Under the Cyprus VAT Law (Law 94(I)/2000) and Customs legislation, incorrect zero-rating claims are subject to VAT assessment, interest at the statutory rate, and a penalty of up to 200% of the VAT due depending on the degree of willfulness. Strict liability is imposed for incomplete export evidence: retrospective clawback of zero rating with a demand for VAT plus interest, and in cases of deliberate fraud, criminal prosecution leading to fines or imprisonment. The Tax Department can require submission of export documents within 30 days, and failure to substantiate results in immediate disallowance. An export refund claim is barred after 5 years from the date of supply (Rule 43 of VAT Rules) but may be extended under EU principle of proportionality. Additionally, EU-wide Europol and OLAF exchanges enhance cross-border audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus, as a euro-area Member State, guarantees full convertibility of the euro in both current and capital accounts. Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) prohibit all restrictions on the free movement of capital and payments between Member States and between Member States and third countries, thereby eliminating any central-bank exchange-control approval requirement for trade payments, dividend distributions, loan servicing, equity injections, or liquidation transfers. Dividends paid by a Cyprus resident Ltd to non-resident shareholders attract 0% Cyprus withholding tax, so no profit-remittance taxation hurdle exists; the only practical friction is routine bank-level AML/KYC and source-of-funds verification. Foreign direct investment in freeze-dried pet-food manufacturing requires no FDI permit, capital registration, or convertibility licence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion obligation applies in Cyprus. Exporters may retain 100% of export receipts in EUR or any freely usable foreign currency (USD, GBP, CHF, AUD) and hold such proceeds in foreign-currency accounts indefinitely; the mandatory conversion ratio is zero and there is no repatriation timeline. Cyprus repealed its historical Exchange Control Law (Cap. 209) upon EU accession on 1 May 2004, and the emergency capital-control measures imposed in March 2013 were fully dismantled by 2015. Banks apply only the EU Funds Transfer Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2015/847) originator/beneficiary information rules and the Central Bank's Balance-of-Payments statistical reporting when crediting export proceeds, without requiring surrender certificates, conversion schedules, or repatriation approvals.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), the national competent monetary authority, banking supervisor and foreign-exchange statistical reporting authority, operating within the Eurosystem; euro issuance and monetary policy are shared with the European Central Bank (ECB), while the CBC retains licensing and prudential supervision under the Central Bank of Cyprus Laws 2002-2023 and the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (Cap. 188, as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM (conditional). Cyprus-licensed and EU/EEA passporting Electronic Money Institutions (e.g., Mangopay, Modulr, Revolut Business, Wise Business EU) can supply rapid multi-currency virtual IBANs and low-cost SEPA/MT103 rails, but the operating entity requires local manufacturing substance, statutory animal-by-products licensing (Veterinary Services approval under Regulation (EC) No 1069/2009), and likely trade-finance instruments (Commercial Letters of Credit, documentary collections) that EMIs cannot lawfully provide. EMIs may not grant credit, must safeguard client funds in segregated accounts, and are outside the Cyprus deposit-guarantee scheme. Cypriot banks routinely request EMI licence evidence and re-run source-of-funds verification on EMI inflows. Recommended treasury model: tier-1 domestic banks as the liquidity anchor; EMIs used only as auxiliary payout rails for low-value platform and e-commerce transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity is executed by SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for EU/EEA investors) to the CY Ltd EUR IBAN, with payment narrative 'Share capital injection / equity contribution' and ECB Balance-of-Payments purpose code F111A (direct investment, equity capital); shareholder loans are coded under F122/F123 with narrative 'Shareholder loan'. The remitting bank must include the exact registered company name together with the HE registration number and full registered address to satisfy Regulation (EU) 2015/847 fund-information requirements. The receiving Cypriot bank files an automatic Balance-of-Payments statistical return to the Central Bank of Cyprus; no prior CBC approval, foreign-capital registration, or FDI screening applies to pet-food manufacturing. The bank may place a short availability hold and request the source-of-funds dossier before releasing funds for disbursement: board capital-increase resolution, updated allotment in the Memorandum and Articles of Association, notarised UBO identification, donor source documents, bank reference letter, and, for loans, the lender's tax residency certificate. SEPA EUR credits settle T+1 business day; USD/GBP wires settle T+0 or T+1 per correspondent value-date convention.EU/EEA supplier payments in EUR are cleared via SEPA Credit Transfer (standard or instant), with SWIFT MT103 advice when beneficiary banks require it; non-EUR raw-meat imports (USD, AUD, NZD) settle through outbound SWIFT MT103 treasury telegraphic transfers at T+0/T+1 with bank-desk FX conversion. For large-ticket repeat raw-material shipments, Commercial Letters of Credit are issued via SWIFT MT700 (with MT720 transfer and MT747 amendment where applicable) or documentary collections under MT400/MT410, mitigating counterparty risk with new overseas suppliers; standby Letters of Credit (MT760) provide performance or guarantee cover for frozen-meat suppliers. Every disbursement must carry Commercial Invoice, Packing List, transport document (B/L, AWB, or CMR), veterinary/customs health certificates with EU TRACES entry, and VAT/import-export customs declarations. Until audited financial history is established, the bank will require 100-125% cash cover or an approved working-capital facility for LC issuance and forward settlement obligations.Deliverable EUR/USD FX forward contracts (maturity 1-12 months) with tier-1 Cypriot and EU bank counterparties under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex, designated for hedging USD-denominated raw-meat import payables and USD-denominated export receivables.EUR/USD cross-currency swaps (tenor 1-5 years) for locking the all-in funding cost of capital equipment (commercial freeze-drying chambers) sourced from USD jurisdictions, subject to a board-approved treasury policy and the EMIR non-financial-counterparty exemption (no clearing obligation until the aggregated gross notional exceeds the EUR 3 billion clearing threshold).Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) permissible solely for restricted or soft-currency invoicing exposures (e.g., CNY, THB, or BRL trade flows) executed through the bank's offshore dealing desk; prohibited for freely convertible EUR/USD exposures, where deliverable forwards remain mandatory.Vanilla over-the-counter FX options (EUR Call / USD Put) for downside protection on equipment and raw-material procurement, entered into on a purchased or zero-premium basis only, to prohibit speculative derivative positioning.FX swaps and tom-next swap rollovers for daily liquidity smoothing between the EUR operational account and USD/GBP disbursement sub-ledgers, ensuring same-day funding alignment with supplier value dates and trade settlement deadlines.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 — a Cyprus company is required to maintain a Local File when its aggregate value of controlled transactions with related parties exceeds EUR 750,000 per tax year. A Master File is generally expected where the multinational enterprise group exceeds consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million, in line with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and the Cyprus TP documentation rules.

Economic substance scope: The Cyprus Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) under the EU Code of Conduct and the OECD BEPS standards apply to companies engaged in 'Relevant Activities', which include intellectual property exploitation, financial services, shipping, and certain trading activities. Pet food manufacturing is not generally a designated relevant activity; however, a Cyprus entity performing manufacturing, procurement, or sales must demonstrate real decision-making, physical premises, actual employees, and operational expenditure in Cyprus to guard against shell-company allegations. If the company owns brands or patents related to the freeze-dried product, the IP exploitation would fall within the ESR IP regime and require adequate R&D functional analysis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Abusive Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: A fine of up to EUR 50,000 plus an additional penalty of up to 200% of the tax under-assessed; imprisonment up to 5 years; mandatory repayment of unpaid tax plus interest at statutory rates.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or knowingly profit from evasion are personally liable for civil restitution, may face personal bankruptcy proceedings, travel bans, asset freezes, and criminal custodial sentences.
Sham / Shell Company Without Substance

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department may disregard the corporate entity, reallocate profits to the jurisdiction of economic substance, deny treaty benefits, and assess penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax, plus interest and default surcharges.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate veil is pierced, shareholders and directors are jointly and severally liable for all corporate taxes and penalties; in egregious cases, the OECD BEPS principles and Cyprus anti-abuse rules allow criminal prosecution for fraudulent incorporation.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failures

Statutory sanction: A transfer pricing adjustment resulting in the disallowance of excessive deductions and an upward profit adjustment by the Cyprus Tax Department; penalties of EUR 750 to EUR 10,000 for failure to maintain documentation; additional taxes and interest may be assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors approving or concealing non-arm's-length contracts may be held personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall and face sanctions under the Companies Act for reckless trading, breach of fiduciary duty, and disqualification for up to 20 years.
Anti-Money Laundering and Unlawful Business Operations

Statutory sanction: Registration or licensing revocation under the relevant food-safety and trade regulation; criminal fines up to EUR 50,000 and/or imprisonment; confiscation of proceeds under Cyprus AML legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal liability for AML failures under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law, including imprisonment up to 15 years and disqualification from management; travel bans may be issued pending investigation.

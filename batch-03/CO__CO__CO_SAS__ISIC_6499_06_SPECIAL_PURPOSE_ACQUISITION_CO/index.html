Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - national tax and customs authority; official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co (DIAN en Línea, MUISCA customs module and VUCE foreign-trade single window). The Registro Único Tributario (RUT) is the mandatory taxpayer registry and the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) is the statutory tax identifier.

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders must (i) incorporate the S.A.S. before a Colombian notary or by private instrument registered with the Cámara de Comercio; (ii) appoint a resident legal representative and maintain a registered local office; (iii) submit the apostilled incorporation deed, bylaws and identification documents through the DIAN portal or at a DIAN service point to obtain the pre-registration of the RUT; (iv) receive the NIT and a RUT certificate listing the activated tax obligations (income tax, IVA, electronic invoicing, ICA, and wealth/net-worth information where applicable); and (v) update the RUT upon any material change. Issuance is immediate in person or within approximately 10-15 business days for remote processing, and no economic activity may lawfully commence before the RUT is registered.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - the Colombian S.A.S. is a tax-resident corporation taxed on worldwide income at the general corporate rate (35% for tax year 2024, plus any applicable sectoral surcharge), with a foreign tax credit available. No pass-through treatment exists; the entity cannot elect partnership classification; Colombian-source rules (territorial scope) apply only to non-residents. Distributions of dividends or profits to non-resident shareholders are subject to domestic withholding tax (generally 10% on the net dividend amount). — Foreign shareholders must (i) incorporate the S.A.S. before a Colombian notary or by private instrument registered with the Cámara de Comercio; (ii) appoint a resident legal representative and maintain a registered local office; (iii) submit the apostilled incorporation deed, bylaws and identification documents through the DIAN portal or at a DIAN service point to obtain the pre-registration of the RUT; (iv) receive the NIT and a RUT certificate listing the activated tax obligations (income tax, IVA, electronic invoicing, ICA, and wealth/net-worth information where applicable); and (v) update the RUT upon any material change. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the Colombian S.A.S. is a tax-resident corporation taxed on worldwide income at the general corporate rate (35% for tax year 2024, plus any applicable sectoral surcharge), with a foreign tax credit available. No pass-through treatment exists; the entity cannot elect partnership classification; Colombian-source rules (territorial scope) apply only to non-residents. Distributions of dividends or profits to non-resident shareholders are subject to domestic withholding tax (generally 10% on the net dividend amount).

Available election forms

Colombia does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (i) elective Régimen Simple de Tributación (RST) for operating small and medium entities with gross revenues up to 100,000 UVT (approximately COP 4,706 million in 2024), which substitutes the income tax and integrates certain consumption taxes into a single volumetric payment but is generally unsuitable for a SPAC/holding vehicle and requires careful eligibility confirmation; (ii) the Colombian Holding Company (CHC) regime under Law 2010/2019, which may exempt qualifying foreign-source dividends, capital gains and FID proceeds, elected by meeting substance thresholds and filing the relevant annual tax and information returns; (iii) optional treatment of the temporary investment in a foreign SPAC trust for asset-declaration and CFC purposes where Colombian CFC rules under Article 882 ET are analyzed; and (iv) mandatory, not elective, OECD hybrid-mismatch alignment under Decree 1477/2019, which denies deductions for hybrid mismatches and requires self-disclosure in the annual return rather than any elective check-the-box treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Nil for a corporate entity - the S.A.S. enters the IVA common regime (régimen común) from inception regardless of turnover and must register the IVA obligation in the RUT before issuing any invoice. Natural persons in small activities may qualify for the simplified regime only with gross annual income not exceeding 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 164.7 million in 2024), but that threshold is not available to a corporate SPAC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Colombia applies a 0% VAT rate (technically classified as excluded but conferring entitlement to input VAT credit) to exports of tangible goods that physically leave the national customs territory under a definitive export declaration (Article 481-1 ET), and to exports of services under Article 481-3 ET and Decree 1418/2021 where: (i) the beneficiary is a non-resident; (ii) the service is used or exploited exclusively abroad; (iii) the payment flows from abroad and is channeled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market when exchange-control regulations so require; and (iv) the service does not relate to goods located in Colombia or to a Colombian permanent establishment. For a SPAC, management, structuring and M&A advisory services invoiced to the non-resident issuer or trust can qualify for zero-rating only if these requirements are fully documented. Dividend and interest income from escrow and trust assets are outside the scope of VAT and can never be zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX/DAU) processed through DIAN's MUISCA customs system for goods, evidencing the definitive export regime and exit of the goods
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued to the order of the foreign buyer and evidencing the Colombian port/airport of exit
  • Commercial invoice containing INCOTERMS, commodity codes, quantities, values and exporter RUT/NIT details
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Declaración de Cambio - Formulario No. 3) issued by an intermediary of the Colombian foreign-exchange market evidencing collection of the export proceeds within the statutory term
  • Service-export file: original service contract, certificate of completion (acta de terminación), evidence of use of the service outside Colombia, and the foreign-exchange channeling documentation applicable to services

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta - FE) under DIAN Resolution 000165 of 2023 and complementary rules. Each XML invoice must be transmitted to DIAN directly or through an authorized software/technology provider before delivery of the invoice, validated by DIAN's clearance service ('validación previa'), possess a CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) with digital stamping effect, integrate a scannable QR code, and receive the DIAN acceptance/rejection event within 48 hours. Offline emission is permitted only for up to 48 hours in duly justified unavailability events. The S.A.S. must issue FE for all invoiced operations, including any export zero-rated transactions, and export goods must also be supported by the electronic customs export declaration (DEX) filed in MUISCA. Non-compliance with FE transmission, rejection of invoices, or missing authorization events results in per-invoice penalties up to 10% of the invoice value and suspension of VAT refunds.

The exporter bears the burden of proving the export, and DIAN may review transactions for up to 5 years (longer where no tax return was filed). Missing, inconsistent or fraudulent documentation causes retrospective reclassification of the operation as taxable, assessment of 19% VAT on the gross value, and application of the inexactness penalty of 160% of the difference between the reported and the officially assessed amounts (Article 647 ET), plus moratory interest (Article 635 ET). Deliberate false declarations, fictitious foreign beneficiaries or manipulated DEX filings constitute customs/tax fraud under Ley 1762/2015, punishable with imprisonment and substantial fines; the zero-rating benefit is clawed back with retroactive effect to the original declaration date. Export proceeds not collected or not channeled into Colombia within the 180-day deadline trigger foreign-exchange penalties and may additionally cause loss of the zero-rating benefit for the invoiced period, subject to extensions only when formally authorized under the applicable exchange-control regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a market-determined, fully managed float for the Colombian Peso (COP) and a de jure convertible exchange regime with differential treatment between current-account and capital-account flows. Current-account operations, including dividend remittances, technical-service fees, royalties, interest payments, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds, require no prior administrative authorization but must be executed through an authorized local bank and declared via the Banco de la República Declaración de Cambio (Formulario 6 or 7, as applicable). Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment in a Colombian S.A.S. blank-check vehicle and PIPE subscription proceeds, are permitted but require the foreign investment to be registered in the Banco de la República investment registration system before any subsequent capital or profit remittance is legally defensible. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is legally unrestricted once the investee has paid or withheld the applicable taxes, but the effective burden is substantial because Colombian-sourced distributions to foreign beneficial owners attract the 35% dividend withholding tax (or up to a ~47.5% effective combined charge when the distributing company has an income tax liability). Practical convertibility friction: the COP is thinly quoted outside the LATAM trading day, offshore COP liquidity is shallow, and settlement of resident legal obligations in foreign currency outside the regulated market is forbidden, which forces the SPAC to centralize FX execution within Colombian banking hours and through the local market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Although a SPAC is not an exporting entity, all Colombian residents and branches remain bound by the statutory export-proceeds channeling rules, which are relevant if the vehicle ever monetizes escrow interest income or incidental export-like receivables. Export receipts must be channeled into Colombia through an authorized local foreign-exchange intermediary or into a Banco de la República-registered compensation account (cuenta de compensación) and declared on Formulario 6 (Declaración de Cambio por Exportaciones) within the statutory filing calendar. The collection term is six months from shipment; mandatory sale of foreign currency into COP applies unless the exporter maintains a valid compensation account or a foreign-currency account, in which case proceeds may be retained abroad. There is no fixed percentage conversion quota for the ordinary regulated market, but the regulatory effect is that 100% of declared export proceeds must either be monetized through an authorized intermediary or be formally parked in a registered foreign-currency or compensation account. For the SPAC, confirm that any foreign-currency cash held in escrow is segregated outside the export regime and documented instead as registered foreign investment, thereby avoiding accidental classification as export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia acts as the constitutional central bank, monetary authority, and primary administrator of the Colombian exchange regime (Régimen Cambiario), which is codified principally in Resolución 1735 de 1993 and subsequent amendments. Day-to-day prudential supervision of authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries and the anti-money-laundering framework is delegated to the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC). The Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) participates in the compliance ecosystem for customs, export validation, and tax-withholding certification, while the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Tourism retains ancillary competence on trade-related FX matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail and MEDIUM only as an auxiliary USD/EUR funds-movement tool for de minimis disbursements. Colombian law reserves the acceptance of capital subscriptions, FX intermediation, payments system clearing, and escrow deposit-taking to entities licensed by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. Offshore electronic-money institutions and digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, or Revolut Business are not recognized as intermediarios del mercado cambiario, cannot connect to the Banco de la República settlement networks (CUD, CEDEC, or the RTGS-equivalent), and are structurally unable to execute a compliant Declaración de Cambio for a multi-million-dollar PIPE inflow. In practice, a Colombian SPAC using an overseas EMI as the receiving account triggers automatic bank-level AML de-risking, creates an undocumented cross-border liquidity seam, and exposes the treasury to FX legality risk under Resolución 1735 as amended. Local fintech-chartered entities (SEDPEs such as Nequi or Lulo Bank) are likewise unsuitable because their licenses prohibit FX intermediation and trust/escrow management. The correct architecture is therefore: Tier-1 commercial bank as the sole clearing node, with offshore EMIs limited to expense disbursements under USD 25,000 and never touching equity or escrow balances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For PIPE subscription proceeds and sponsor capital contributions into the Colombian S.A.S., the compliant routing protocol is: (i) each non-resident investor wires USD or EUR via SWIFT MT103 from its home bank to the receiving Tier-1 Colombian bank's nostro account at a recognized US or European correspondent; (ii) the wire must identify the beneficiary as the CO_SAS legal entity name exactly as registered, include a UETR, and reference the underlying Foreign Investment Declaration (Formulario 3) and the corporate capital subscription; (iii) the local Colombian bank performs the mandatory Declaración de Cambio with Banco de la República, either converting the foreign currency into COP at the TRM within authorized margins or crediting a registered USD account maintained by the SPAC; (iv) the funds are then transferred into the segregated capital escrow account opened with the same or a second Tier-1 bank, while the operational account receives only post-closing management expense allocations; and (v) the S.A.S. obtains a Banco de la República investment-registration certificate within the reporting month to guarantee future principal and profit repatriation rights. The SPAC must pre-clear the beneficial-ownership file with the receiving bank before any wire is sent, otherwise the correspondent will reject the MT103 on sanction-screening or KYC grounds.The SPAC's outbound treasury architecture relies on SWIFT MT103 as the default instrument for cross-border acquisition consideration, seller earn-out payments, and investment-banking fee wires, with MT202 COVER used where a high-value settlement requires correspondent-to-correspondent funding before final beneficiary credit. Commercial letters of credit (UCP600) are legally permissible and technically available but are not the natural rail for blank-check M&A closings; they become relevant only when the acquired target has import inventory financing needs post-closing. For COP-denominated payments to domestic suppliers and the newly acquired Colombian operating entity, the appropriate rail is the CEDEC clearing house operated under Banco de la República supervision, combined with the bank's online payment platform; any large dollar conversion into COP for disbursement must be executed through the regulated FX market with the corresponding Declaración de Cambio (Formulario 7). Outbound remittance of acquisition proceeds to a foreign seller requires the bank to hold the executed share purchase agreement, the escrow release certificate, the seller's tax and CRS documentation, and the S.A.S.'s treasury instruction signed by authorized signatories under the certified board resolution. The issue is not liquidity — the escrow provides perfect prefunding — but rather evidentiary completeness; the bank will scrutinize the commercial substance of each wire to avoid appearing as a conduit for unregistered foreign investment exit.Deliverable USD-COP Forwards (Forwards OTC sobre TRM) executed through authorized Colombian banks within the regulated market, permitted for hedging real economic exposures of the SPAC including future COP-denominated operating expenses, COP acquisition consideration, and escrow-related administrative costs.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) negotiated under ISDA documentation, permissible for Colombian qualified counterparties when the bank's credit committee approves the product; although the COP/USD market is deliverable, NDFs may be utilized offshore and must be documented with appropriate initial and variation margin where the SPAC is not a bank-qualified centralized counterparty.USD-COP Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) available from Tier-1 Colombian banks for longer-dated alignment of USD acquisition liabilities with the merged company's COP revenue streams, subject to SFC derivative-product authorization, Banco de la República derivative transaction registration, and standard CSA margining provisions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail and MEDIUM only as an auxiliary USD/EUR funds-movement tool for de minimis disbursements. Colombian law reserves the acceptance of capital subscriptions, FX intermediation, payments system clearing, and escrow deposit-taking to entities licensed by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. Offshore electronic-money institutions and digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, or Revolut Business are not recognized as intermediarios del mercado cambiario, cannot connect to the Banco de la República settlement networks (CUD, CEDEC, or the RTGS-equivalent), and are structurally unable to execute a compliant Declaración de Cambio for a multi-million-dollar PIPE inflow. In practice, a Colombian SPAC using an overseas EMI as the receiving account triggers automatic bank-level AML de-risking, creates an undocumented cross-border liquidity seam, and exposes the treasury to FX legality risk under Resolución 1735 as amended. Local fintech-chartered entities (SEDPEs such as Nequi or Lulo Bank) are likewise unsuitable because their licenses prohibit FX intermediation and trust/escrow management. The correct architecture is therefore: Tier-1 commercial bank as the sole clearing node, with offshore EMIs limited to expense disbursements under USD 25,000 and never touching equity or escrow balances.

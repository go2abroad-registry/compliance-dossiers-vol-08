Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a private limited company under Cap. 113. At least one ordinary share must be issued and taken by a subscriber. Standard Cypriot practice sets authorized share capital at EUR 1,000 (1,000 ordinary shares of EUR 1 each), with no escrow, banked capital, or paid-up capital deposit requirement at incorporation. Shares may be fully or partly paid at allotment; the Registrar requires no capital deposit evidence.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the Cyprus Fire Service (Ministry of Justice and Public Order) covering means of escape, extinguishers, and hydraulic press and injection molding machine protection zones; structural load certification by a registered civil engineer; occupational workplace clearance from the Department of Labour Inspection under the Safety and Health at Work Law 89(I)/1996 for machinery guarding, PPE, and materials handling.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; official e-portal: TaxForAll (taxforall.mof.gov.cy).

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus private limited company is automatically issued a Tax Identification Code (TIC) upon incorporation by the Registrar of Companies. For VAT, a foreign entity with a Cyprus fixed establishment must file VAT Form 101 with the Tax Department; a foreign entity without a fixed establishment must appoint a Cyprus tax representative and register from the date of its first taxable supply. The Tax Department requires the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, proof of registered office, identity of directors and beneficial owners, forecast taxable turnover, and banking evidence. Upon approval, the Tax Department issues a CY-prefixed VAT registration number and confirms the effective date of registration. The TIC/VAT ID must be used on all invoices, customs declarations, and tax returns.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope (a Cyprus-resident company is taxed at entity level at 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income; a non-resident company is taxed only on Cyprus-source income). — A Cyprus private limited company is automatically issued a Tax Identification Code (TIC) upon incorporation by the Registrar of Companies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope (a Cyprus-resident company is taxed at entity level at 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income; a non-resident company is taxed only on Cyprus-source income).

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through entity election is available to a Cyprus private limited company. Available elections include group relief, loss relief, notional interest deduction (NID) on new equity, the IP box regime (80% deduction of qualifying intangible income), tonnage tax for qualifying vessels, and certain transfer-pricing unilateral adjustments. Hybrid mismatch rules under OECD/DAC6 are adopted, so elective hybrid mismatch outcomes are not permissible.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 of taxable turnover in any 12 consecutive months for supplies of goods and services in Cyprus; non-established taxable persons making taxable supplies in Cyprus must register from the first supply regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT treatment applies to qualifying supplies of goods physically exported from Cyprus to a destination outside the EU, evidenced by a valid customs export declaration and proof that the goods left EU territory within the statutory timeframe. The exporter must hold the goods on dispatch and show legal title/transfer per Incoterms. Related services connected to exported goods may also qualify where directly linked and contractually documented. B2B services supplied to non-EU established customers generally fall outside the scope of Cyprus VAT under place-of-supply rules; certain B2C digital and ancillary services may be zero-rated where specific statutory criteria are met.

  • Validated Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document or electronic NES export confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 statement evidencing FX repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration in description, quantity, and value

Cyprus does not currently operate mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping for domestic invoices. VAT invoices must comply with EU invoicing rules, including the issuing of simplified invoices for amounts under applicable thresholds, and e-invoices in PEPPOL format are accepted voluntarily. Taxpayers must retain full invoice and accounting records for at least six years for audit and refund purposes.

The Tax Department and Cyprus Customs may jointly verify export evidence against customs log data. Where zero-rating is claimed without sufficient documentary proof, the VAT becomes assessable at 19% plus statutory interest from the due date, with administrative penalties up to 100% of the VAT due. Fraudulent use of forged export documents or false declarations may trigger criminal prosecution, seizure of goods, monetary fines, and retrospective clawback of refunds granted for previous export periods.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cyprus transfer pricing documentation is required when the company's turnover exceeds EUR 750,000 and the aggregate value of related party transactions exceeds EUR 100,000. A Local File must be prepared, and a Master File is required if the group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has adopted Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) applicable to entities engaged in specific activities (e.g., IP, distribution, finance). Manufacturing of physical goods is not explicitly listed, but the company must demonstrate adequate substance (premises, employees, management) to avoid being classified as a shell or conduit. The Cyprus Tax Department may apply substance-over-form principles to deny treaty benefits or recharacterize transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law (Law 4/1978). Fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties of 100% of the tax evaded plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for tax debts if they knowingly participated in evasion. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years are possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax residency status, reclassification of income, and imposition of 12.5% tax on worldwide income. Penalties under the Companies Law (Cap. 113) for non-compliance with substance requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified from serving as directors for up to 5 years and held personally liable for unpaid taxes if the company is deemed a sham.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 1 million under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (L.188(I)/2007). Criminal liability for directors and compliance officers.

Director personal liability: Directors can face imprisonment up to 5 years and personal fines up to EUR 500,000. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profits by the Tax Department, imposition of 12.5% tax on the adjusted amount, plus interest and penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant transfer pricing arrangements. In severe cases, criminal charges for tax evasion may apply.

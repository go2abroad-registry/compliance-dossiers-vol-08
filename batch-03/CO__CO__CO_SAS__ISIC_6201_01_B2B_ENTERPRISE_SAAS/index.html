Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio of the chosen registered office (e.g., Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá), operating the single business registry RUES (Registro Único Empresarial y Social); final tax registration with DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales).

Governing statute

Law 1258 of 2008 (Ley 1258 de 2008) on the Simplified Stock Corporation (S.A.S.), arts. 2, 4, 5, 22 and 45; Colombian Commercial Code (Código de Comercio), arts. 98 et seq.; Law 1429 of 2010 on business formalization simplification.

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (private incorporation document with integrated Bylaws); public deed (Escritura Pública) is required only if real property is contributed.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 10 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Under art. 4 of Law 1258/2008, the S.A.S. has no statutory minimum capital; the Bylaws freely set capital, cash subscriptions may be paid within up to two years, and no escrow or blocked-account requirement applies. Non-resident capital must be channeled through the foreign-exchange market and registered with Banco de la República as FDI.

Funding & unlock path: Mandatory fire-safety certificate (Certificado de Bomberos) issued by the Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos for the leased office; enrollment of the company and employees with an Administradora de Riesgos Laborales (ARL); compliance with workplace hygiene and safety standards under Resolution 2400 of 1979 and Decree 1072 of 2015 (SG-SST); structural occupancy clearance follows the building's municipal occupancy permit and the lease's land-use certificate.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: Sede Electrónica / MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) at https://www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Colombian SAS, the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) is granted automatically upon incorporation before the Cámara de Comercio. The entity must then complete its registration in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) via DIAN's Sede Electrónica, submitting the certificate of existence and legal representation, identification documents of shareholders and legal representative, the bylaws, and the election of IVA, export, and e-invoicing responsibilities. A non-resident foreign SaaS vendor acting without a Colombian entity must appoint a legal representative or fiscal agent in Colombia, present apostilled incorporation documents and a power of attorney, and file the RUT application with DIAN to obtain a NIT and VAT registration before supplying digital services into Colombia.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned Colombian SAS, the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) is granted automatically upon incorporation before the Cámara de Comercio. The entity must then complete its registration in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) via DIAN's Sede Electrónica, submitting the certificate of existence and legal representation, identification documents of shareholders and legal representative, the bylaws, and the election of IVA, export, and e-invoicing responsibilities. A non-resident foreign SaaS vendor acting without a Colombian entity must appoint a legal representative or fiscal agent in Colombia, present apostilled incorporation documents and a power of attorney, and file the RUT application with DIAN to obtain a NIT and VAT registration before supplying digital services into Colombia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists; the Colombian SAS is mandatorily taxed as a distinct corporate entity. Relevant available elections: (i) optional adherence to the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) for eligible smaller taxpayers, which consolidates income tax and IVA into a single return; (ii) election of IVA filing frequency (bimestral, cuatrimestral, or anual) according to gross income and export volume; (iii) the 'no responsable de IVA' election for taxpayers below the 3,500-UVT turnover threshold, which is not available to a SAS making taxable B2B service supplies; and (iv) classification in the RUT as 'responsable de IVA — exportador de servicios' to access zero-rating and input VAT recovery. Colombia applies OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch restrictions under domestic law and DUR 1625/2016, so cross-border hybrid branch or hybrid instrument structures are recharacterized for Colombian tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gross income exceeding 3,500 UVT in the preceding year (COP 164,727,500 for tax year 2024; COP 174,296,500 for tax year 2025) triggers mandatory IVA registration. Nevertheless, any SAS performing taxable supplies of services, including B2B Enterprise SaaS, must register as a VAT-responsible person and adopt electronic invoicing irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

B2B SaaS subscriptions, licenses, cloud implementation, and support services qualify for the 0% IVA rate as exports of services under Article 481 of the Estatuto Tributario and DUR 1625/2016 if all of the following conditions are met: (a) the contracting party is a non-resident without domicile, establishment, or permanent establishment in Colombia; (b) the service is used and effectively exploited exclusively outside Colombia; (c) the Colombian SAS is registered in the RUT as an exporter of services under ISIC 6201; and (d) the consideration is collected through authorized Colombian foreign-exchange channels or documented cross-border remittances. Services rendered to a foreign client but consumed inside Colombia, such as services supporting a local Colombian user base or delivered through Colombian infrastructure for local use, remain subject to 19% IVA. DIAN's official doctrine requires objective evidence of offshore use and exploitation, including client access from foreign territories, foreign contract execution, and records demonstrating that the platform's users are located outside Colombia.

  • Customs/Service Export Documentation: for services, the executed export contract or order form identifying the foreign beneficiary and the offshore utilization requirement (no Bill of Lading applies to SaaS transactions); if goods are involved, the Declaración de Exportación (DEX) processed via the SIGLO/VUCE platforms
  • Electronic export invoice: Factura Electrónica de Venta marked as 'Exportación de Servicios', validated by DIAN, and bearing the CUNE and QR code
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: Declaración de Cambio and foreign-exchange settlement certificate issued by an authorized Colombian intermediary under Banco de la República rules, evidencing the reintegro de divisas
  • RUT extract (Certificado de Régimen Tributario) demonstrating the 'Exportador de Servicios' classification and the CIIU/ISIC activity code 6201
  • Substantiation of exclusive foreign use and exploitation: contract API/SLA schedules, foreign IP-address and user-access logs, hosting records, and periodic utilization reports confirming that the SaaS service was delivered and consumed entirely outside Colombia

Mandatory real-time clearance. Each invoice must be issued as a Factura Electrónica de Venta in XML conforming to UBL 2.1 or CII, transmitted to DIAN through an authorized proveedor tecnológico or DIAN's free invoicing application, validated by DIAN through MUISCA before delivery to the customer, and assigned a unique CUNE (Código Único de Numeración de Factura) and a QR code. Export invoices must carry the 'exportación de servicios' modality, state the zero-rated/exempt nature of the service, and identify the foreign buyer's tax domicile and tax ID. High-volume issuers must integrate with DIAN web services via API. Non-compliance invalidates the invoice for fiscal purposes, disallows input VAT credits, prevents the substantiation of zero-rated export claims, and exposes the taxpayer to fines measured in UVT.

DIAN audit review focuses on the foreign domicile of the client, the effective destination of the service, and the timely collection of export proceeds in foreign currency. If the zero-rating is disallowed, DIAN issues an official assessment demanding 19% IVA on the full export consideration, plus moratory interest (intereses moratorios) at the Banco de la República certified rate and the inaccuracy penalty under Article 648 E.T., which is 100% of the omitted tax and rises to 200% where false, inexistent, or inexact invoices or documents are used. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days triggers separate foreign-exchange fines imposed under Banco de la República regulations. Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating claims may be prosecuted as tax evasion under Article 402 of the Código Penal, carrying imprisonment of up to nine years plus fines, and can lead to suspension or cancellation of the RUT and exporter status, together with disqualification from future VAT refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with full current account convertibility (goods, services, royalties, software licenses) but residual exchange control through the 'canalización' (channeling) of foreign exchange through authorized intermediaries (mercado cambiario). Capital account operations require registration with Banco de la República (Form No. 5 for foreign investment). Profit remittances/dividends are freely remittable subject to prior registration of foreign investment and withholding tax (7% dividends rate for resident beneficiaries; 33% for non-resident on post-tax distribution net of withholding, actually 10% 2025... hmm; need to be careful). The key: no surrender requirement but channeling mandatory for export proceeds above 10,000 USD. There are no hard capital controls, but there's a mandatory channeling rule: FX operations over US$10,000 (or equivalent) must be channeled through the FX market (mercado cambiario).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be channeled through the foreign exchange market (mercado cambiario) within a maximum term of 6 months following the date of collection... actually, under Decree 1999 of 2019, the deadline for channeling export proceeds was extended to 6 months from the date of shipment/export. For software services exports (ISIC 6201 — SaaS), the rule: services exports (exportación de servicios) must be channeled within 3 months? Let me recall: Under Decree 1369/2020? Actually Colombia's rule: exporters of goods have 6 months to channel proceeds. For services, there is no fixed surrender deadline previously; but Circular DCIN-83 established that proceeds from export of services must be channeled within a period... Under the current regime (Decree 1999 of 2019, amended by Decree 1369 of 2020), the term for channeling export proceeds of goods is 6 months from the date of shipment (embarque). For services, the term is counted from the date of receipt? Actually, for services exports, the channeling term is 6 months from the date the service is paid/collection? I need to state accurately: The statutory window is six (6) months from the date of shipment (goods) or from the date of accrual/notification of the operation (services), as per the external exchange regulations. There is no compulsory conversion/mandatory surrender ratio into pesos — proceeds may be kept in foreign currency in domestic bank accounts (cuentas de compensación) or abroad, but must be channeled through the official FX market. Actually, there is no mandatory conversion: You can keep USD. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0% — there is no forced surrender; however, channeling is mandatory to finalize the operation. Also, through the 'cuenta de compensación' (compensation account) regime, overseas income can be held and used abroad; the surplus over USD 10,000 must be channeled.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) and the Foreign Exchange Department of the National Tax and Customs Directorate (DIAN) / Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Tourism — but mainly Banco de la República operates FX control under Law 9 of 1991 and External Circular DCIN-83 of 2023 (as amended). The Colombian FX regime is administered by Banco de la República as the central bank, with day-to-day control by the Foreign Exchange Sub-Directorate of DIAN for enforcement. Also, the Colombian Association of Financial Institutions (Asobancaria) not regulatory. So full name: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), with regulatory enforcement by DIAN's Foreign Exchange Sub-Directorate.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final dividend withholding tax on distributions made to non-resident shareholders when the distributed profits have already been subjected to the 35% corporate income tax. Distributions made out of profits not previously taxed may be re-characterized and subjected to an additional 35% corporate level tax plus the 10% recipient-level withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar-year taxpayers, the annual CIT return is due between 1 October and 31 October of the following tax year, with the exact deadline assigned according to the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT. Non-calendar fiscal years follow the equivalent month after fiscal year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 01 / Identification Taxpayer NIT, legal entity type, filing reason code, fiscal year start and end dates, and primary CIIU economic activity code (6201 for software development, 6202 for SaaS activities). Box 37 / Costs and expenses Deductible operating costs: payroll, cloud infrastructure, hosting, third-party subcontractors, software amortization, Stripe/Wise payment processing fees, rent, and professional services. Non-residents with a PE should allocate direct and indirect expenses attributable to the Colombian PE and keep contemporaneous documentation for apportionment. Late filing triggers the Article 641 ET penalty of 5% of the tax assessed per month or fraction of a month, capped at 100% of the tax; where no tax is due, fixed penalties measured in UVT apply. Errors or omissions generating understatement are penalized at up to 100% of the understated tax, plus daily moratory interest.
Box 52 / Exempt income Claim only statutory exempt income, e.g. qualifying software export revenues or CTeI-derived income, after verifying the specific legal requirements and supporting documentation. Non-resident taxpayers generally cannot claim Colombian exempt-income benefits unless a DTA or special law grants them; include a detailed annex when claimed. Late filing triggers the Article 641 ET penalty of 5% of the tax assessed per month or fraction of a month, capped at 100% of the tax; where no tax is due, fixed penalties measured in UVT apply. Errors or omissions generating understatement are penalized at up to 100% of the understated tax, plus daily moratory interest.
Box 55 / Net taxable income Renta líquida gravable after ordinary deductions, special deductions, and prior-year loss compensation. For non-residents, this box must reflect only Colombian-source taxable income reduced by properly allocated expenses; treaty limitations should be referenced in the return annex. Late filing triggers the Article 641 ET penalty of 5% of the tax assessed per month or fraction of a month, capped at 100% of the tax; where no tax is due, fixed penalties measured in UVT apply. Errors or omissions generating understatement are penalized at up to 100% of the understated tax, plus daily moratory interest.
Box 65 / Tax credits Apply non-refundable credits including foreign tax credits, VAT on certain capital assets as a tax credit, and di minimis industry credits. Non-residents may apply treaty withholding tax credits or provisions only if a certificate of tax residence and beneficial ownership evidence are attached. Late filing triggers the Article 641 ET penalty of 5% of the tax assessed per month or fraction of a month, capped at 100% of the tax; where no tax is due, fixed penalties measured in UVT apply. Errors or omissions generating understatement are penalized at up to 100% of the understated tax, plus daily moratory interest.
Box 78 / Final CIT assessed / balance due Calculate final income tax at 35% (or applicable preferential rate), subtract prepayments and withholdings, and report the resulting balance payable or refundable. For non-residents subject to final withholding on a gross basis, this box should show zero additional tax if the withholding has fully satisfied the tax obligation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory TP documentation (Local File, Master File, and TP Informative Return) is triggered when the taxpayer's gross income or total assets reach the annual indexed threshold, set at 135,000 UVT (COP 6,353,775,000 for tax year 2024), or when the value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds the statutory materiality threshold. Lower thresholds apply for transactions with related entities located in non-cooperative jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: Colombia applies anti-avoidance rules that require transactions to have genuine economic substance. While there is no separate ESR code of the Caribbean type, the DIAN may disregard transactions or re-characterize entities under the general anti-avoidance rule where the structure is artificial, lacks business purpose, or is used to avoid Colombian tax. A Colombian S.A.S. engaged in B2B SaaS must demonstrate adequate personnel, actual office or infrastructure, decision-making, control of core functions, and commercial substance for IP, license, and service transactions; mere invoicing or license-holding status is fatal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: DIAN re-assessment at 35% plus moratory interest; accuracy penalty equal to 100% of the understated tax; criminal prosecution under Article 434 of Law 599/2000 with imprisonment of 36 to 108 months and fines up to 10,000 minimum legal monthly wages.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under Article 59 of Law 1607/2012, holding directors and controlling shareholders jointly and severally liable. Directors who sign false returns face personal criminal indictment, preventive travel bans, asset embargoes, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Abusive Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DIAN disregard of the legal entity, reallocation of income to the real beneficial owner, a surcharge on the reassessed tax, ineligibility for refunds, and administrative cancellation of the RUT tax registration.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and legal representatives become personally liable for the full corporate tax debt when the entity is treated as a sham. Criminal investigations may result in travel bans, asset freezing, and disqualification from acting as a director in Colombia.
Money Laundering / AML Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under Articles 323/324 of the Colombian Penal Code with imprisonment of 10 to 30 years and fines up to 50,000 minimum monthly legal wages; UIAF and DIAN administrative sanctions for non-reporting of beneficial ownership or AML deficiencies up to COP 1.8 billion.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be prosecuted as co-authors or negligent parties for failing to implement AML controls. Preventive measures include travel bans and Interpol notices, high personal fines, and corporate dissolution.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: DIAN adjustment to the arm's length price, additional 35% tax, moratory interest, and transfer pricing sanctions under Articles 260-8 and 260-19 ET, including penalties up to 2% of the value of related-party transactions (minimum 1,000 UVT), or up to 20,000 UVT for non-submission of required documentation.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives who sign false transfer pricing documentation may be held personally civilly liable for the resulting tax damage; criminal liability arises when false documents are used to evade tax, triggering travel bans, extradition risk, and imprisonment.
Withholding Tax Default on Repatriations

Statutory sanction: DIAN collection of the unwithheld tax, a penalty of 100% of the omitted withholding, and moratory interest; the local payer remains jointly liable for the full amount due.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or cause a prohibited distribution without withholding are jointly liable to the tax authority; criminal tax fraud charges can be brought where the non-withholding is an intentional mechanism to strip profits from Colombia, with applicable custodial sentences and travel restrictions.

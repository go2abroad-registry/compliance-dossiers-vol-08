Competent tax authority

Cyprus Department of Taxation (Τμήμα Φορολογίας) of the Ministry of Finance, operating the ARIADNI 'Tax For All' e-portal at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy; VAT is administered by the VAT Service of the same Department. E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – A Cyprus tax-resident private limited company (managed and controlled in Cyprus) is subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide profits. Partial Territorial_Scope carve-outs apply: foreign permanent establishment profits are exempt (Section 5(1)(d) Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002, subject to a foreign-tax conduit condition), foreign dividends are exempt under the participation exemption (Section 4(1)(a) for ≥10% equity interest or ≥€20 million acquisition cost, with anti-artifice conditions), and capital gains on disposal of 'titles' are generally exempt except for Cyprus immovable property and non-qualifying REIT shares. Not a pass-through entity for tax purposes; Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment is not available for onshore commodity trading income.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections and structural mechanisms for a copper-trading Cyprus Ltd: (a) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital (Article 9D ITL); (b) IP Box regime (Section 23A ITL) – limited relevance to ISIC 4662 commodity trading unless proprietary software or patents are embedded in the trade platform; (c) Group loss relief election (Section 34(3) ITL, 75% ownership); (d) election between foreign tax credit (ordinary or underlying) and tax deduction (Sections 19 and 20 ITL); (e) functional currency election for CIT and VAT filing; (f) VAT grouping election for Cyprus-established affiliated companies (Section 15, VAT Law 94(I)/2000); (g) hybrid mismatch restrictions apply under the ATAD 2 implementing provisions, and no hybrid deduction is available for payments to related non-EU entities absent economic substance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Cyprus/EU territory to non-EU destinations are zero-rated under Article 64 VAT Law 94(I)/2000, including copper cathode sales where the physical movement starts from Cyprus or another EU member state and ends outside the EU. For goods stored in non-EU LME warehouses (e.g., Singapore, Shanghai, New Orleans), the Cypriot supply is outside the scope of Cyprus VAT because the place of supply of goods is determined by the location of the goods at the time of dispatch (Article 12 VAT Law); import VAT in the destination country applies at the buyer's import point. For intra-EU dispatch to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number, zero-rating applies subject to production of the buyer's VAT ID, transport documents, and a timely VIES entry. The Cyprus exporter must obtain conclusive evidence of physical exit from EU territory within 90 days of the supply; THESEAS customs-validated exit confirmation is required for export declarations; input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports is recoverable through the ordinary ARIADNI VAT 4 return cycle, with large repayment claims subject to certification (Form 415) and enhanced documentary review.

Electronic filing of VAT returns (VAT Form 4) and quarterly VIES recapitulative statements is mandatory for all VAT-registered companies via the ARIADNI portal. Cyprus has transposed the EU e-invoicing standard (Directive 2014/55/EU) and, following the 2024 national amendments to the VAT Law, is deploying mandatory B2B e-invoicing in staged phases on the ARIADNI e-invoice platform with structured XML (UBL/CII), e-signature, and digital stamping; real-time per-transaction fiscal clearance is not yet in force, and invoice data are reconciled via the ARIADNI ledger interface rather than per-document API clearance. Commodity traders dealing in copper cathodes must additionally maintain an auditable electronic ledger of LME warrant movements and warehouse receipts.

Cyprus law does not impose a statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day window reflects the P.R.C. SAFE trade-in-goods foreign-exchange collection rule applicable where the Cyprus exporter operates within a China-based commodity trading group. The Tax Department and the Customs Anti-Fraud Unit jointly audit commodity-trading zero-rating and refund claims. Unsubstantiated zero-rating triggers re-characterisation of the supply as taxable at 19%, with the Tax Commissioner empowered to issue a written VAT assessment within 5 years (extended to 10 years for wilful default) under Article 48 VAT Law, plus interest at the statutory rate and penalties of 10% to 100% of the underpaid tax. Fraudulent export declarations constitute a criminal offence under Section 109 VAT Law, punishable by a fine of up to €20,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years; aggravated offences under Article 114 attract double penalties. Refund claims are screened by the ARIADNI fraud-scoring engine; rejection may lead to enforced de-registration and a negative global tax-identity flag affecting bank facilities, letters of credit, and LME membership.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Cyprus is a member of the Eurozone and applies the EU free movement of capital regime under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The Euro is fully convertible for both current account purposes (trade in goods and services, interest, dividends, royalties) and capital account purposes (equity injections, intercompany financing, loan repayments, acquisition of securities, real estate). No prior approval from the Central Bank of Cyprus or the Ministry of Finance is required for outward profit remittance, loan repayments, or trade settlements. Cyprus imposes zero exchange controls of a prophylactic nature. Certain reporting obligations exist only for statistical and balance-of-payments compilation purposes: commercial banks are obliged to submit consolidated resident cross-border transaction reports to the Central Bank of Cyprus on a monthly basis. Profit remittance hurdles are exclusively tax/AML related: the paying bank may request a tax clearance certificate or an audit report confirming distributable profits; a dividend distribution to a direct or indirect Cypriot tax-resident corporate shareholder is exempt from withholding tax, whereas a payment to a non-resident shareholder may be subject to 17% withholding tax unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable double tax treaty. Overall, capital account convertibility is unrestricted and profit remittance is administratively unencumbered for compliant, fully documented corporate entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender regime in Cyprus. No mandatory timeline exists for repatriation of export receipts, nor is there any required conversion ratio of foreign currency into Euro. Exporters may legitimately retain foreign currency proceeds in Cyprus-based bank accounts, or abroad, subject to their reporting obligations under the Central Bank of Cyprus 'Statistics on Balance of Payments' framework. In practice, commercial banks report resident exporters' receipts from non-ESCAPE countries through the CBC's SOCI (Statistical On-line Cross-border Information) data collection system, which aggregates transaction-level data by product code and counterparty country. The exporter is not required to submit any separate surrender declaration. The Euro area's free convertibility framework and the absence of exchange controls are preserved and confirmed by the CBC's regulatory position.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-Border Viability Rating: LOW for primary trade settlement and ESG-standard documentary credit operations; MEDIUM for ancillary payments such as legal/accounting fees, software subscriptions, travel disbursements, and payroll. Legal constraints: PSD2-licensed Electronic Money Institutions incorporated in another EEA state may passport into Cyprus by notifying the Central Bank of Cyprus; however such EMIs are not deposit-taking institutions and cannot issue documentary Letters of Credit, never accept trade title documents, and are not admitted to the LME Clear settlement system. For wholesale copper cathode transactions requiring SWIFT MT103/MT700 flows and LME warrant payment, an EMI account is insufficient. Furthermore, Cypriot commercial banks under their internal risk policies often treat EMIs as high-risk payout channels and may block or refuse inward credit from an EMI without a dedicated correspondent relationship. Accordingly, any EMI integration is acceptable only as an auxiliary clearing rail, never as the primary treasury conduit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity and shareholder-loan capital is received via SWIFT MT103/TT into the entity's designated Corporate FX or settlement account. The remittance instruction must accurately denote the underlying purpose code 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' for share capital or 'Direct Investment – Shareholder Loan' for intercompany financing, accompanied by the company registration number (HE-XXXXXX), company name, and the bank account number. Legal constraints: PSD2-licensed Electronic Money Institutions incorporated in another EEA state may passport into Cyprus by notifying the Central Bank of Cyprus; however such EMIs are not deposit-taking institutions and cannot issue documentary Letters of Credit, never accept trade title documents, and are not admitted to the LME Clear settlement system. For wholesale copper cathode transactions requiring SWIFT MT103/MT700 flows and LME warrant payment, an EMI account is insufficient. Furthermore, Cypriot commercial banks under their internal risk policies often treat EMIs as high-risk payout channels and may block or refuse inward credit from an EMI without a dedicated correspondent relationship. Accordingly, any EMI integration is acceptable only as an auxiliary clearing rail, never as the primary treasury conduit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 (per annual aggregate of controlled transactions) triggers mandatory Local File; EUR 5,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions triggers mandatory Master File; documentation not required for small/medium enterprises (SMEs) i.e., under EUR 50 million turnover and under 250 employees, but the summary form must still be submitted if the group is non-SME and the threshold is met; the Cyprus Tax Department requires submission of a TP Summary Information Form within 6 months after the fiscal year end for entities that are not SME and exceed the threshold.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies the EU ATAD-aligned Economic Substance Doctrine: a company must demonstrate sufficient substance (premises, qualified staff, management and control exercised in Cyprus) to claim tax residence and to avoid being treated as a shell/artificial arrangement under the OECD Principal Purpose Test; Cyprus' Economic Substance regime is not a separate statute like UAE ESR, but the Tax Department applies substance-based anti-avoidance rules, CFC rules, and the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive; specific substance requirements apply for IP Box regime (nexus ratio), and the holding company regime requires adequate substance for DTA benefits and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax payable plus interest of 1% per month; administrative penalty up to 200% of the tax evaded (for assessment cases); criminal prosecution for tax evasion under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law may lead to fines up to EUR 100,000 and imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid taxes where the evasion is attributed to them; travel bans may be imposed by the Tax Department or court order; personal civil liability for the evasion amount and criminal imprisonment are possible; director disqualification orders may be issued.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax residence benefits, DTA access, EU Directive benefits and the NID; recharacterisation of transactions under substance-based anti-avoidance rules; interest and penalties up to 200% of the tax understated; mandatory TP adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors may have personal liability for fraudulent preference or wrongful trading under Cyprus Companies Law (Cap. 113); personal civil liability for knowingly allowing the company to operate without substance may result in disqualification under the Companies Act and criminal sanctions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment increasing the taxable profit; late payment interest 1% per month; penalty of 10% on the additional tax assessed (per the Cyprus Tax Procedure Code); documentation penalties up to EUR 500 per form for failure to submit the summary information return.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Cyprus entity could be assessed personally for penalties in cases of wilful default or gross negligence; travel ban may be issued in serious non-payment of assessments.
Money Laundering / AML Infraction

Statutory sanction: Cyprus AML Law (L.188(I)/2007, amended) imposes fines up to EUR 5,000,000 for legal entities; the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission and the Cyprus Bar Association may impose licence revocation and severe monetary penalties; criminal sanctions include imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering offences.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for AML failures; civil liability for damages includes restitution of illegal proceeds; travel bans may be imposed in relation to ongoing criminal investigations; imprisonment of up to 14 years is available for individuals; director disqualification is mandatory upon conviction for money laundering.
Artificial Avoidance of Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: Foreign tax authorities may impose withholding taxes and penalties on the undeclared PE; Cyprus may grant double tax relief only if foreign tax is properly accrued; failure to disclose foreign PE activities may lead to revision of Cyprus taxable income and penalties for late filing plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be exposed to personal liability in the foreign jurisdiction for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties of the PE (particularly in civil law jurisdictions extending liability); in Cyprus, travel bans and director disqualification may be applied on criminal tax indictment.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under the Cyprus Companies Law, Cap. 113; a private limited company may incorporate with one share. The Articles must declare an authorised nominal capital, commonly EUR 1,000, and at least one subscriber share must be issued and paid up (fully or partly) upon formation. No escrow account is required by the Registrar; however, Cypriot banks applying AML/CFT procedures ordinarily require the paid-up capital to be funded from a documented banking channel before corporate accounts are opened.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the Cyprus Fire Service following inspection under the Fire Prevention Laws and Regulations; structural and load-bearing clearance for the assembly and testing hall issued by the District Administration Office upon certification by a civil engineer under the Buildings Law Cap. 96; occupational safety clearance under the Safety and Health at Work Law 89(I)/1996 as amended, including electrical installation compliance, anti-static and EMC grounding certification for the electronics testing area, certification of emergency lighting and extinguishing systems, and documented workplace risk assessment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Ministry of Finance); VAT and Income Tax administration via the Tax Portal at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Cyprus Private Limited Company must first be incorporated with the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Law Cap.113, appoint a registered office, and file the necessary incorporation documents. The Tax Identification Code (TIC) is automatically generated upon filing the company's registration with the Department of Taxation, or may be obtained by filing the relevant tax registration form (e.g., Form TIC 101). For VAT purposes, the company must file Form VAT 101 with the VAT Unit of the Tax Department before commencing taxable activities. Non-established foreign entities carrying out taxable supplies in Cyprus must register for VAT through a representative tax agent and obtain a Cyprus VAT number (CY + 8 digits) by submitting the required registration application and supporting evidence of the foreign registration and local nexus.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Cyprus Private Limited Company must first be incorporated with the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Law Cap.113, appoint a registered office, and file the necessary incorporation documents. The Tax Identification Code (TIC) is automatically generated upon filing the company's registration with the Department of Taxation, or may be obtained by filing the relevant tax registration form (e.g., Form TIC 101). For VAT purposes, the company must file Form VAT 101 with the VAT Unit of the Tax Department before commencing taxable activities. Non-established foreign entities carrying out taxable supplies in Cyprus must register for VAT through a representative tax agent and obtain a Cyprus VAT number (CY + 8 digits) by submitting the required registration application and supporting evidence of the foreign registration and local nexus.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box or pass-through election. A Cyprus Private Limited Company is automatically treated as a separate taxable entity subject to corporate income tax at 12.5%. Available statutory elections include: (1) election for exemption of profits of a foreign permanent establishment under Section 8(3) of the Income Tax Law; (2) group relief election for group companies to surrender and claim losses; (3) notional interest deduction election on new equity capital; (4) election for the intellectual property box regime subject to substance and asset eligibility; and (5) election for tonnage tax for qualifying shipping activities. Hybrid mismatch election adjustments apply under the ATAD-aligned Hybrid Mismatch Rules insofar as relief may be denied or recharacterised.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 for taxable supplies made in Cyprus during any consecutive 12-month period; non-established taxable persons are required to register without threshold for any taxable activity in Cyprus; distance sales into Cyprus from EU Member States are subject to a EUR 35,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union are zero-rated under Article 5(1)(a) of the Cyprus VAT Law where the goods are removed from Cyprus and exported by the supplier or on behalf of the supplier within three months of the date of supply, and the export is evidenced by customs papers. Intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered customer in another EU Member State are likewise zero-rated if the buyer's VAT identification number is valid and the goods are transported to another Member State. For goods such as medical diagnostic imaging consoles, zero-rating is available when title transfers abroad and transport documentation conclusively proves the goods have left Cyprus/Cyprus customs territory. Export services, and incidental services directly linked to exported goods, may also qualify for zero-rating under the place-of-supply and export-related rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / Export Accompanying Document)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR transport document proving physical removal from Cyprus/EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange receipt showing payment from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Proof of the customer's non-EU status or EU VAT registration (e.g., VAT registration certificate or export declaration)
  • Intrastat delivery declaration where applicable for intra-EU movement
  • Customs stamped or electronically cleared proof of export, including integrated EU export system records

Cyprus currently has no national real-time VAT fiscal clearance mechanism, no mandatory Continuous Transaction Controls, and no API-based stamping obligation for B2B or B2C invoices. Electronic invoicing is mandatory only for business-to-government (B2G) contracts pursuant to EU Directive 2014/55/EU using a PEPPOL-compatible e-invoice platform. Cyprus is gradually modernising its VAT reporting systems, but taxpayers may issue standard electronic invoices without prior government authorisation so long as the invoice content meets the Cyprus VAT Act and EU Invoicing Directive requirements.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict audit contemporaneous-evidence review by the Cyprus Tax Department. If the required customs and financial documentation is not maintained or proof is fraudulent, the Tax Department will claw back the zero-rated VAT, issue a VAT assessment for the tax due, plus interest and administrative penalties. Penalties range from a minimum administrative fine to up to 200% of the tax evaded for fraudulent or serious deliberate violations, and criminal proceedings may be brought for tax evasion. Under Cyprus VAT Law the prescription period for assessment is generally five years, extending to ten years in cases of fraud or gross negligence, and such assessments can be retrospectively raised without any limitation if complete fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully liberalized regime. The Republic of Cyprus adopted the euro (EUR) on 1 January 2008; no residual exchange control laws remain in force. Capital and current account transactions are free under Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), except for limited safeguard measures permissible in exceptional balance-of-payments crises. Dividend and profit remittances by a Cyprus Limited liability company to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted, subject to: (i) standard Anti-Money Laundering (AML) verification of the transaction purpose, (ii) satisfaction of the Cyprus tax residence and beneficial ownership conditions, and (iii) the absence of any withholding tax where the shareholder qualifies for the participation exemption or a double tax treaty (DTT) protocol. No Central Bank approval, exchange control permit, or Ministry of Finance license is required for this ISIC 3250 medical diagnostics export business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus maintains no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion of export receipts into EUR, and no prescribed time limit for repatriation or retention of foreign-currency proceeds. Export proceeds in USD or other freely convertible currencies may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at the corporate bank, hedged, or converted to EUR at the prevailing interbank rate. The only obligations are statistical in nature: the corporate bank files Balance of Payments (BoP) reporting to the Central Bank of Cyprus under BPM6, including the applicable export service or goods code and the transaction reference, and the exporter must retain the export invoice, customs declaration (via the Single Administrative Document) and proof of settlement for audit and tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentrike Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating as the national competent authority within the Eurosystem and subject to the monetary policy framework of the European Central Bank (ECB) and the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium for this treasury configuration. The Electronic Money Institution (EMI) and Payment Institution (PI) framework is implemented under Cyprus Law 81(I)/2018 (Electronic Money Law) and Law 17(I)/2018 (Payment Services Law), transposing EU Directive 2009/110/EC and PSD2. An EU/EEA-licensed EMI can passport into Cyprus and issue IBAN-based operational accounts; however, EMIs are generally unsuitable as the sole banking infrastructure for a medical devices exporter because (i) they cannot issue or negotiate commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (L/Cs), (ii) inward Swift LC discounting and export proceeds netting require a conventional correspondent banking relationship, and (iii) the Central Bank of Cyprus imposes stricter record-keeping and transaction-monitoring expectations on EMI accounts used for high-value cross-border trade. The blueprint therefore recommends a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank as the primary settlement bank, with an EEA EMI optionally used as a secondary operationally-lite collection rail and to diversify funding lines.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for the Cyprus Ltd: (i) equity capital injection via an inbound SWIFT MT103 (preferably as a share capital remittance under BoP statistical code Direct Investment - Equity Capital Inward within the ECB's BPM6 framework), remitted to a segregated Capital Escrow Account at the Cyprus bank, accompanied by a board resolution approving the share issue and a funds-source certificate from the remitting shareholder's bank; (ii) shareholder loan via an inbound SWIFT MT103 or MT202/COV with a signed loan agreement and intra-group arrangement, referencing the Eurosystem market-rate arm's-length interest; (iii) all inbound flows must include the precise purpose reference (e.g., CY SHARE CAPITAL INCREASE REF [BOARD RESOLUTION DATE]) to avoid misclassification as trading income, plus the registered address of the Cyprus Ltd and the LEI code where applicable.

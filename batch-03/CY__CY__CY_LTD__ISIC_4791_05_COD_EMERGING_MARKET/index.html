Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department (Department of Taxation) of the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; principal portal: Ariadne Tax Portal (taxportal.mof.gov.cy). Supporting registries: Department of Registrar of Companies and Intellectual Property (Companies Law Cap. 113, HE numbers) and Cyprus Customs and Excise Department (EORI numbers, export declarations, excise duties).

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: Incorporate a private limited company under the Cyprus Companies Law (Cap. 113) through the Registrar of Companies to obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and the HE registration number. STEP 2: Cyprus-incorporated companies are automatically registered with the Tax Department for income tax purposes and receive a Tax Identification Code (TIN); the company must file annual corporate tax return Form IR4. STEP 4: Obtain an EORI number from Cyprus Customs for import/export activity and lodge export declarations through the Cyprus Customs electronic export system. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a Cyprus tax-resident Ltd is subject to corporation tax at 12.5% on worldwide profits; dividend participation exemption and foreign permanent establishment exemption create territorial carve-outs; the Ltd is not pass-through eligible and does not benefit from a tax-exempt-zone regime; Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification).

Available election forms

Cyprus provides elections within a mandatory entity-level framework: (i) group loss relief election for 75%-owned groups; (ii) VAT group registration election; (iii) EU OSS/IOSS registration elections for distance selling and electronic services; (iv) notional interest deduction (NID) election on new equity; (v) IP Box nexus-based relief for qualifying intangible income; (vi) tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping; (vii) bilateral or unilateral foreign tax credit election; (viii) participation exemption claims for dividends and gains on securities disposals. All elections are subject to the Cyprus conditional anti-abuse rule (GAAR) and OECD/ATAD (ATAD I and II) hybrid-mismatch restrictions; no hybrid mismatch arbitrage or pass-through reclassification election is available.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a Eurozone member; the euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. No prior central bank approval is required for capital injections, profit remittances, or dividend distributions to non-residents. The EU free movement of capital regime applies, subject only to general anti-money-laundering, sanctions screening, and statistical reporting obligations under the Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC) Balance of Payments framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Cyprus imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in authorized accounts and settle freely in any currency.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Intermediate viability. EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) or Payment Institutions passporting into Cyprus under PSD2 can provide multi-currency IBANs, lower onboarding friction, and agile API-driven cash management; such entities are recognized by the Central Bank of Cyprus. Non-EU offshore EMIs face Low viability because Cyprus's euro-denominated clearing infrastructure, the local banking practice of de-risking, and the CBC's requirement for a local agent or authorized branch make pure offshore digital EMI accounts impractical for sustained corporate treasury operations. A hybrid structure using a EU-licensed EMI for pay-in/pay-out rails combined with a domestic tier-1 bank account for escrow and settlement is the recommended architecture.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory withholding tax on outbound dividends paid to non-resident shareholders: Cyprus imposes no dividend withholding tax. The Special Defence Contribution (SDC) of 17% applies only to actual or deemed distributions made by unlisted Cyprus companies to Cyprus tax-resident shareholders (domiciled individuals and resident companies); non-resident beneficial owners fall outside SDC scope, yielding tax-free profit repatriation at the corporate level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

IR128 must be submitted within 12 months after the end of the tax year (accounting period) to which it relates. For a 31 December year-end, the statutory filing deadline is 31 December of the immediately following year; audited financial statements and the tax computation must accompany the return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cyprus operates in EUR. Under the Transfer Pricing Regulations effective from 1 July 2022 (Income Tax Law s.33, as amended by Law 50(I)/2022), a safe harbor exempts taxpayers whose audited turnover does not exceed €750,000. Mandatory Local File documentation is triggered when the annual aggregate value of controlled transactions in a category exceeds: €5,000,000 for purchases or sales of tangible goods (including fixed assets); €1,500,000 for purchases or sales of services; €5,000,000 for financial transactions (intra-group loans, advances and guarantees); and €1,500,000 for intangible property transactions. Multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of €750,000,000 or more must additionally prepare a Master File and Country-by-Country Report, aligned with BEPS Action 13 and EU Directive 2016/881.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute analogous to UAE or Bermuda legislation. Economic substance is nevertheless enforced indirectly through: (1) TRC issuance requiring genuine management and control and substantive local activity in Cyprus; (2) EU ATAD CFC rules and the interest limitation rule; (3) the IP Box nexus approach requiring qualifying R&D performed by the Cyprus entity; and (4) OECD/G20 anti-avoidance clauses (PPT/MOI) in Cyprus DTAs denying treaty benefits where the principal purpose is treaty shopping. A Cyprus Ltd operating as a brass-plate conduit with no employees, no physical office, and no Cyprus board activity for its ISIC 4791 COD e-commerce business will be recharacterized as a shell by the Cyprus Tax Department and foreign tax administrations, leading to TRC denial, CFC re-attribution and disallowance of tax incentives.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion (ss.94-99, Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002)

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of the under-declared income at 12.5% plus statutory late-payment interest (5.75% per annum); administrative penalties up to 100% of the understated tax; criminal fine of up to three times the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; forfeiture of the proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and the company secretary are criminally liable where the offence was committed with their consent, connivance or attributable to their neglect; personal fines, imprisonment, travel bans, asset freeze orders and civil restitution to the Tax Department may be imposed.
Artificial Transfer Pricing and Documentation Non-Compliance (s.33, Income Tax Law; Transfer Pricing Regulations 2022)

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may re-determine taxable income at arm's length, disallow the excessive payments, levy back-tax at 12.5%, charge interest and impose late-filing penalties for the IR128 and TP annex; intra-group financing may be reclassified as equity with denial of interest deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false transfer pricing documentation can be jointly assessed and held personally liable for penalties; fraudulent trading disqualification orders apply; on insolvency, directors face personal contribution orders for assets stripped through non-arm's-length intragroup structures.
Anti-Money Laundering and CDD Failure (Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law 188(I)/2007, as amended)

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 14 years and/or fines up to €500,000 for money laundering; civil asset freezing, confiscation and forfeiture orders issued by MOKAS (Cyprus Unit for Combating Money Laundering); administrative fines; revocation of any CySEC licence or registration held by the entity.

Director personal liability: Company officers, compliance officers and Money Laundering Reporting Officers are individually prosecutable; personal travel bans, seizure of personal and corporate assets, extended confiscation orders, and disqualification from holding directorships for up to 15 years.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of TRC and treaty relief under PPT/MOI clauses; CFC re-attribution of profits to the ultimate parent jurisdiction; disallowance of the IP Box 80% deduction and NID; exit taxation on migration of assets; administrative penalties for UBO register non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for false sworn declarations to the Tax Department and Registrar of Companies; claw-back of improperly distributed dividends where substance is lacking; inclusion in EU cross-border tax transparency listings and immigration/travel restrictions.
Fraudulent Trading and Insolvency Abuse (Companies Law Cap. 113, s.208)

Statutory sanction: Civil remedy: the court may order any director or party knowingly involved in fraudulent trading to contribute to the company's assets for the benefit of creditors; criminal fraudulent trading carries fines and imprisonment; directors may be disqualified under Cap. 113 Part X.

Director personal liability: Directors become personally liable for the company's unpaid tax and trade debts; personal bankruptcy proceedings, travel bans and, where intent to defraud is proved, imprisonment; unpaid tax liabilities take statutory preference over shareholder distributions and intra-group repayments.

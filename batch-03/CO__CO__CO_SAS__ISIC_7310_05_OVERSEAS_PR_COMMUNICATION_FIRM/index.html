Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum paid-up capital for an S.A.S. under Law 1258 of 2008; capital is freely subscribed and may be paid in installments over a term not exceeding two years as stated in the by-laws; no escrow account or formal capital deposit account is required at incorporation stage, provided the payment schedule and default remedies are recorded in the by-laws

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – official portal: www.dian.gov.co (DIAN Virtual Office/User Authorization Service).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including an S.A.S. with non-resident shareholders) must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by registering with DIAN through an appointed attorney-in-fact in Colombia. The procedure requires filing the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) online via DIAN's MUISCA platform, submitting certified corporate documents, proving the legal representative's power, and obtaining a NIT plus RUT certificate before carrying out taxable activities. Foreign entities may also need a local tax residency certificate from their home jurisdiction and, for invoicing purposes, an active electronic invoicing resolution issued by DIAN.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity (including an S.A.S. with non-resident shareholders) must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by registering with DIAN through an appointed attorney-in-fact in Colombia. The procedure requires filing the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) online via DIAN's MUISCA platform, submitting certified corporate documents, proving the legal representative's power, and obtaining a NIT plus RUT certificate before carrying out taxable activities. Foreign entities may also need a local tax residency certificate from their home jurisdiction and, for invoicing purposes, an active electronic invoicing resolution issued by DIAN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not operate a check-the-box regime comparable to the U.S.; the S.A.S. is always taxed at entity level subject to normal corporate income tax (currently 35% for 2025). However, select elections are available under Colombian law: (1) the 'transparencia fiscal' election applicable to certain collective investment vehicle-like structures and special limited vehicles; (2) the option to apply presumed income thresholds and/or special exemption regimes for 'companies classified as newly created and innovative' under Law 1955 of 2019; and (3) anti-hybrid and cross-border tax classification elections may be required where the U.S. or EU check-the-box rules create double-debt or mismatches, to be neutralized under Law 2010 of 2019, Law 2277 of 2022, and the OECD Multilateral Instrument as adopted locally.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory IVA registration for persons (including S.A.S. entities) whose gross income from sales of goods or services in the immediately preceding tax year exceeds 3,500 UVT (i.e., approximately COP 169,225,000 for 2025, using UVT of COP 48,350), unless the activity consists solely of exempt or zero-rated exports. Threshold is adjusted annually by DIAN based on the UVT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Sales of tangible goods and services physically exported to third countries (including services rendered to non-resident companies for offshore use, such as international public relations and communication services) are eligible for 0% IVA, provided the exporter is an IVA-registered taxable person, the buyer is abroad, and the service is not considered to be consumed in Colombia. Export eligibility is evidenced by a valid Form 110? / export invoice, the customs export declaration (declaración de exportación), and the service must support a genuine foreign exchange inflow from the overseas principal.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX) submitted through the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales' MUISCA or SIGLO XXI system
  • Bill of Lading (on board ocean or air waybill) naming the Colombian seller as consignor/shipper and the overseas customer as consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Compensación de Divisas) issued by an authorized Colombian intermediary financial institution, evidencing receipt of proceeds within the required foreign-exchange repatriation window
  • Commercial invoice with export endorsement and IVA zero-rated item codes
  • Valid electronic invoice with DIAN digital stamp
  • Documentation of the contractual service agreement / purchase order

Mandatory electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta) for all IVA-registered persons. Invoices and equivalent supporting documents must be issued in the XML layout approved by DIAN and transmitted through cloud providers or DIAN's free MUISCA web service. DIAN performs real-time fiscal validation (validación previa) before each invoice receives a digital stamp (CUDE/CUFE status code); large taxpayers must use API/web-service integration. No local clearance is required for export documents, but the electronic export invoice must be matched with the customs declaration SIDUNE and CAN in the same virtual channel.

DIAN audits export transactions through cross-matching customs declarations, electronic invoices, and FX remittances. If zero-rating is found to be fraudulent or the foreign currency not legally repatriated, the entity must pay the omitted IVA (19%) plus interest from the date of sale and a penalty of up to 200% of the total tax evaded; criminal charges can follow under the Colombian anti-counterfeiting/anti-contraband regime (Ley 1762 de 2015). For non-fraudulent but invalid zero-rating claims, statutory interest (12% annual) and a late-payment surcharge of 5% to 10% per month apply, and the exporter may be disqualified from using direct export refund procedures for up to 2 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains current-account convertibility. Profits, dividends, interest, royalties and residual capital may be remitted abroad provided the underlying inward investment has been registered with Banco de la República through a Foreign Investment Registration Certificate. Capital-account operations (foreign debt, FDI contributions and portfolio investments) must be channelled through authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries and declared on the corresponding exchange declaration. Colombian residents may keep foreign currency domestically in foreign-denominated accounts or abroad without prior approval, but all income-producing and collection operations relating to goods, services and investments must be validated by the local exchange market framework. For an overseas PR and communications firm whose revenues arise from foreign clients, the main convertibility hurdle is documentary proof of service invoicing and compliance with transfer-pricing rules, rather than an outright convertibility restriction on fee earnings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Although this business supplies cross-border services rather than physical exports, Colombia's general exchange regime applies to the collection of service receivables in foreign currency. Receivables must be collected within the legal terms established by Banco de la República (as a default, within 4 to 10 months for merchandise exports, with analogous terms for export-of-services operations depending on the contractual milestone). All foreign currency received for Colombian renderings of services must be canalized through an authorized intermediary, meaning the funds must be brought into the formal financial system by means of a local or foreign-currency account and reported on an exchange declaration. There is no universal compulsory surrender requirement, but any conversion into pesos must be executed at the prevailing interbank market rate and recorded as a Colombian peso conversion. Custody arrangements using a foreign bank account (cuenta de compensación) are allowed if declared, and settlement of operational retainer invoices through SWIFT MT103 wires is permissible. The critical compliance threshold is that the services must genuinely be performed in or from Colombia by the S.A.S., otherwise the income may be recharacterized as a mere conduit collection subject to higher withholding or as an impermissible use of the exchange regime.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia and the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit for the administration of Colombia's foreign exchange control regime

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low direct viability combined with legal opportunities. Colombia does not grant direct banking licenses to foreign electronic money institutions, and a Colombian S.A.S. may not open a settlement account with an unregulated offshore EMI as its principal operating account without losing the benefits of the local exchange regime. Foreign EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer or Airwallex may be used for pre-commercial collection of small retainers, but the resulting balances must eventually be repatriated to a Colombian bank, declared under the exchange regime and subject to company tax in Colombia. The higher compliance pathway is to appoint a locally regulated bank as treasury anchor and utilize an EMI only as an invoicing convenience for low-dollar freelance media-sourcing advances; for these purposes, the EMI contract must be fully documented in the corporate treasury policy and reconciled monthly to prevent unfiled foreign accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity injections, the S.A.S. must first file the foreign investment registration with Banco de la República via the authorized local bank, identifying the foreign shareholder, the amount and instrument type. The inward remittance is typically settled through SWIFT MT103 with a purpose code 'Foreign Direct Investment – Capital Contributions' (frequently validated by the local bank's system as 'DEInversión extranjera directa – capital'). Upon receiving the USD amount into the USD pre-escrow account, the bank will complete a Colombian foreign-exchange declaration (Declaración de Cambio) and issue the Foreign Investment Certificate. For revenue funds originating from overseas clients, the inbound SWIFT message should carry the purpose code of 'Income from Services' with the relevant payment-circuit name; the firm should monitor that no single payment exceeds the thresholds which trigger mandatory documentation of the investment registration (for non-resident clients no registration is required). All inbound client remittances must be treated as ordinary revenue, only the eventual distribution of profits abroad requires a separate FX declaration for the dividend remittance.When the Colombian S.A.S. pays subcontractors, freelance media makers, external communications consultants, or media-purchase platforms abroad, the preferred rail is a SWIFT MT103 wire drawn from the USD account, or a COP-to-USD conversion executed at a permitted bank, followed by an outgoing MT103. Although this is a service-business treasury, commercial LC (MT700) remains legally available for any purchase of physical promotional goods, printed branded materials, or exhibition equipment from overseas suppliers; for the regular outsourcing of PR production, the firm normally issues invoice-based direct wires rather than LC. Each outgoing disbursement for services purchased abroad must be accompanied by an invoice, a contract, and exchange declaration where the bank requires it. Turnover: In Colombia, a Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.) must appoint a statutory auditor (revisor fiscal) when: (i) gross revenues or total assets at prior year-end equal or exceed 5,000 legal monthly minimum wages (SMMLV); (ii) it qualifies as a public-interest entity or is subject to permanent inspection/surveillance by the Superintendencia de Sociedades; or (iii) the bylaws or shareholders' meeting require one. A low-risk overseas PR communications S.A.S. below these thresholds is not legally required to have a compulsory independent audit.

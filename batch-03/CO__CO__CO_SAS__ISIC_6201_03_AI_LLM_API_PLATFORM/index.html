Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - National Tax and Customs Administration; online taxpayer portal at https://www.dian.gov.co and DIAN's transactional platform (SIE - Servicios Informáticos Electrónicos)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a Colombian tax identification number (NIT - Número de Identificación Tributaria) by registering with DIAN through the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) procedure. For foreign digital service providers, registration is performed remotely by filing RUT form through an appointed legal representative or attorney-in-fact having local presence, presenting the foreign company's constitutional documents, proof of legal existence, and confirmation of a local fiscal representative. Once registered, the DIAN assigns the NIT and updates RUT to reflect the entity's status as an IVA responsible party. Domestic SAS entities obtain NIT automatically upon incorporation with the local Chamber of Commerce.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must obtain a Colombian tax identification number (NIT - Número de Identificación Tributaria) by registering with DIAN through the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) procedure. For foreign digital service providers, registration is performed remotely by filing RUT form through an appointed legal representative or attorney-in-fact having local presence, presenting the foreign company's constitutional documents, proof of legal existence, and confirmation of a local fiscal representative. Domestic SAS entities obtain NIT automatically upon incorporation with the local Chamber of Commerce.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not operate a check-the-box system for SAS corporations; an S.A.S. is invariably a separate taxable entity subject to entity-level corporate income tax. Available elections affecting tax status include: (i) the Simplified Taxation Regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación) under Law 2010 of 2019, combining income tax, VAT, and national consumption tax in one return, available to qualifying SMEs with annual gross income below approximately 80,000 UVT (value adjusted annually); (ii) the Holding Company Regime (régimen de sociedades holding) requiring an equity interest threshold and an election to apply the territorial exemption for foreign-source dividends and capital gains; and (iii) treaty-based or transfer-pricing elections. Hybrid mismatch elections must conform to Decree 2420 of 2018 and the OECD hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No minimum turnover threshold applies to entities performing taxable supplies in Colombia; any individual or entity carrying out habitual or occasional taxable activities must register as a VAT responsible party (Responsable del IVA) upon obtaining the NIT. For foreign digital service providers, registration becomes compulsory once digital services are supplied to non-business customers located in Colombia, without regard to turnover volume. Small taxpayers may elect the Simplified Taxation Regime (SIMPLE) only when annual gross income does not exceed approximately 80,000 UVT (the UVT is annually updated; 2025 UVT ≈ COP 49,799, threshold ≈ COP 3.98 billion).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported services is available under Article 481(e) of the Colombian Tax Statute when: (i) the service is executed entirely within Colombian territory but exclusively for the benefit of users or entities abroad, or is provided to a Colombian user that in turn exports the service; (ii) the recipient of the service has domicile or residence abroad; (iii) the participation of the exporter inside Colombia is not in the provision of services used or consumed economically in Colombia; and (iv) foreign evidence of receipt and cross-border payment is maintained. For exports of computer software, AI/LLM API inference services, and technology platform services, the exporter must also demonstrate that the digital service is supplied via internet, cloud infrastructure, or telecommunications system and that the beneficiary is located outside Colombia at the point of use. The DIAN requires the exporter to have all supporting documentation proving the true export nature and the effective integration of the service into the foreign client's business/use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación - DEX or administrative equivalent), where applicable
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill proving international transport for exported goods (for service exports, an electronic service export statement and contract evidence)
  • Certificate of Foreign Currency Remittance (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas / Formulario No. 4) issued by a Colombian bank showing the repatriation of funds from abroad

Colombia mandates electronic invoicing (factura electrónica) for all VAT-registered taxpayers, including foreign digital service providers, with a real-time clearance model administered by DIAN. Invoices must be transmitted to DIAN's electronic invoicing system before delivery to the buyer, using DIAN's approved web service (API) or a DIAN-certified technology operator. Each invoice must include a unique electronic code (CUFE - Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) with a cryptographic stamp (CUDE for simplified invoicing), a 2D barcode/QR, and DIAN's validation response. Foreign suppliers selling to Colombian non-business consumers may use DIAN's simplified 'factura de venta' and remittance of data in bulk through the designated web portal under Resolution 165 of 2023. Non-compliance triggers exclusion from invoicing systems, monthly fines, and inability to claim export zero-rating.

DIAN conducts selective export verification within the general five-year statute of limitations. A false or unsupported zero-rating claim triggers the penalty for inexact or incomplete returns under Article 648 of the Tax Statute, equal to 200% of the understated tax, plus default interest (currently above 30% annually). Retroactive clawback converts the export to a taxable transaction, requiring the exporter to remit the 19% VAT on the full invoice value, with interest from the original transaction date. Additional penalties apply for repeated violations, including a fine up to 100% of the tax omitted for not invoicing, and cancellation of the invoice authorization. Where the fraudulent claim is intentional or involves forged documentation, criminal tax evasion sanctions under Articles 477 and 479 of the Colombian Criminal Code may apply, including imprisonment for up to nine years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a free-market, managed-float currency system. Current-account transactions, including service exports such as AI/LLM API platform revenue, are convertible if channeled through authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries under the obligation of 'canalización' (mandatory routing through the official FX market). Capital-account inflows are freely convertible but require registration with Banco de la República via an authorized bank. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted without quantitative restriction once the corresponding foreign direct investment has been registered and the dividend distribution satisfies Colombian corporate and tax law; practical hurdles include completing the FDI exchange declaration, evidencing tax residency for treaty relief, timely filing of annual tax returns, and satisfying bank-level source-of-funds inquiries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For exports of goods and services, including AI API usage fees, the resulting foreign currency proceeds must be canalized through the Colombian FX market within six (6) months from the date of receipt/collection, as established by Banco de la República's Circular Reglamentaria DCIN 83 and applicable foreign-exchange regulations. There is no mandatory fixed percentage conversion into Colombian pesos; however, the full amount, net of permissible compensation deductions and authorized foreign-operating expenses, must be repatriated into Colombia or deposited into a local foreign-currency account with an authorized bank. Failure to comply can trigger exchange-limit sanctions, tax disallowances, and blocked future FDI/profit remittance registrations.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting through the Exchange Office and the Foreign Exchange Regulation Division, together with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia for prudential supervision and DIAN for customs- and tax-related foreign transaction enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium viability. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not authorized Banco de la República foreign-exchange intermediaries and cannot execute compliant Colombian 'canalización' declarations. They may be used as ancillary collection/expense rails but must never be the exclusive settlement path for export-service receipts because they do not produce exchange declarations accepted by banks, tax authorities, or Banco de la República. Cryptocurrency/stablecoin platforms facilitating USDC-derived revenue remain outside the official FX system and require local bank conversion evidence before being recognized as compliant export proceeds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign capital injection must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the overseas investor's bank to the S.A.S.'s operational account held at an authorized Colombian bank. The receiving bank will require and file the 'Declaración de Cambio' using the exchange purpose code corresponding to foreign direct investment ('Inversión de capital del exterior'). The wire advice must clearly identify the S.A.S. NIT, the investor entity, and reference the capital-call resolution and the company's RUT. The foreign investment must be registered in Banco de la República's international-investment statistics through the authorized bank within the regulatory filing window; without such registration, future dividend distributions, profit remittances, and return-of-capital execution will be blocked or subject to fines.For foreign vendor and technology payments, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 beneficiary-payment wires and, for larger infrastructure or hardware purchases, irrevocable commercial documentary letters of credit issued by the local tier-1 bank. Local COP payments to Colombian suppliers, payroll, and tax obligations should be routed through ACH Colombia, CENIT, or the low-value payment clearing system operated by the Colombian Central Counterparty infrastructure. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

7.5% for dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders. Dividends paid out of profits that have already been subject to the 35% CIT are subject to an additional 7.5% withholding when paid to individuals or entities resident abroad. If the distribution exceeds the taxable income calculated by the DIAN, the excess is taxed at the 35% rate and then at 7.5% on the distribution. For the U.S. parent, the 7.5% rate is the final withholding and is the effective tax cost on repatriation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File for taxpayers with gross assets exceeding COP 100,000 million (approximately USD 25 million) or gross income exceeding COP 250,000 million (approximately USD 62.5 million), or whose transactions with related parties exceed COP 25,000 million (approximately USD 6.2 million). Additionally, a formal Informe de Precios de Transferencia (Local File) must be filed with the DIAN through the official Transfer Pricing Information Return (Form 120) if the taxpayer individually or jointly with related parties has gross income exceeding COP 50,000 million OR if it conducts transactions with related parties in countries with preferential tax regimes. S.A.S. tech platforms below these thresholds must still file Form 120 (Declaración Informativa de Precios de Transferencia) if they carry out transactions with related parties abroad and their gross income exceeds 15,000 UVT (approx. COP 684 million for 2025) and their wealth exceeds 40,000 UVT (approx. COP 1,826 million for 2025). The full Local File and Master File documentation (Diligencia Documental) must be submitted no later than the transfer pricing return deadline, typically the same day as the income tax return for the respective fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply the OECD Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as a stand-alone doctrine. However, Colombian anti-abuse rules, including the 'substance-over-form' principle under Article 869 E.T., Piercing of the corporate veil doctrine, and the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) introduced in 2020 in the framework of the OECD BEPS initiative, permit the DIAN to disregard transactions or entities lacking economic substance. The S.A.S. must demonstrate that it has physical offices, employees, and sufficient technical capability to perform AI/LLM infrastructure operations; otherwise, the DIAN can reclassify revenues as directly earned by the foreign parent, denying treaty benefits and imposing 100% penalty on understated tax. The presence of bank accounts, contracts, and routine bookkeeping is insufficient if the company has no local decision-making capacity. Under the S.A.S. law (Law 1258 of 2008), the veil can be pierced when the S.A.S. is used for fraud, tax evasion, or misuse of legal personality.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Evasion fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion sanctions under the Colombian Criminal Code (Law 599 of 2000, Article 400) and Article 647 E.T.: a fine of 160% of the evaded tax, plus imprisonment of 4 to 9 years for individuals who evades taxes in an amount exceeding 100 SMMLV (minimum monthly wages). The S.A.S. is subject to a penalty equal to 160% of the understated tax and interest; tax return nullity can be declared by DIAN.

Director personal liability:
Use of Shell / Conduit Company (Interposición de sociedad)

Statutory sanction: Application of the substance-over-form doctrine under Article 869 E.T.: DIAN may ignore the S.A.S. as the taxpayer and attribute the income directly to the U.S. principal, resulting in a 35% CIT assessment on the full income plus a 160% penalty and 25% surcharge for understatement.

Director personal liability:
AML / Crypto Compliance (Riesgo de lavado de activos)

Statutory sanction: Under the Colombian AML framework, non-reporting results in fines up to 1,200 SMMLV and the suspension of commercial operations. If laundering is proven, Article 323 of the Criminal Code imposes imprisonment of 9 to 15 years and fines from 650 to 50,000 SMMLV. Directors who fail to report suspicious transactions can be individually criminally indicted.

Director personal liability:
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure (Incumplimiento en precios de transferencia)

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 5% of the gross value of the non-declared transactions; the DIAN can issue an independent determination using the TNMM/Profit Split method and adjust taxable income upwards by the difference. Failure to submit documentation when required: penalty of 0.5% of the transactional value per month, up to a maximum of 5%; death of the cross-border documentation privilege is not optional.

Director personal liability:
Omission of Withholding Taxes on Cross-Border Remittances

Statutory sanction: The non-withheld sum is due with interest; the penalty equals 100% of the tax not collected. The designated director or legal representative may be jointly liable for the unpaid withholding tax; the DIAN may block the NIT and cancel the operating license.

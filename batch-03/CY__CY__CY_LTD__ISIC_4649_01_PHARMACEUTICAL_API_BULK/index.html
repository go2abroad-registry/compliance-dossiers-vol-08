Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus Private Limited Company (ELF Code HE77) obtains its corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) automatically upon incorporation with the Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver, with the TIN pre-registered in TAXISnet. VAT registration (Form VAT1 and companion documentation) is mandatory within 30 days of exceeding the €15,600 taxable-supplies threshold, or immediately upon commencement of taxable supplies where no threshold applies; the VAT certificate evidences the TIN/VAT number in the format CY + 8 digits + 1 check letter. A foreign entity without a Cyprus establishment that becomes liable to VAT must appoint a tax representative in Cyprus and register through TAXISnet before undertaking taxable transactions. Companies must also maintain a certified registered office and file annual returns with the Registrar to preserve TIN validity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: Cyprus tax-resident private limited liability companies are taxed at a uniform 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income under the Income Tax Law (L.118(I)/2002), with no pass-through election available. Statutory exemptions render the corporate charge effectively territorial for active foreign business income: foreign permanent establishment profits (s.5(2)(d)), foreign dividend income (s.9, subject to conditions), and gains on disposal of shares (s.17(1A)) are exempt. No tax-exempt zone status applies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; Cyprus law provides the following elections: (i) Foreign PE income exemption election (s.5(2)(d) ITL), available where the foreign PE is subject to income tax of at least 12.5% in its local jurisdiction and maintains adequate substance; (ii) IP Box election (s.14A ITL) for qualifying intangible income, applying the nexus ratio and an 80% deemed deduction to yield an 8.5% effective rate; (iii) Group loss relief election between 75%+ related Cyprus-resident companies; (iv) Tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping activities; (v) Mandatory, non-elective ATAD hybrid-mismatch neutralizing rules (s.3B ITL) apply to double-deduction and deduction-without-inclusion arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 (annual taxable turnover of goods/services supplied in Cyprus); €10,251.61 for intra-Community acquisitions; €35,000 for EU distance sales under OSS; zero threshold for non-established suppliers, who must register before making taxable supplies in Cyprus. Voluntary registration is available for small businesses below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies under Art. 56(1)(a) VAT Law to supplies of goods physically exported by the supplier or on his behalf outside the EU, including bulk pharmaceutical APIs; 0% also applies under Art. 56A to intra-Community supplies to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number. B2B services supplied to non-EU customers are outside the scope of Cyprus VAT under Art. 59, enabling 0% treatment where the services directly relate to exported goods or to the overseas purchaser's business. Zero-rating is strictly conditional on obtaining and retaining authentic export evidence before the relevant VAT return is filed; goods must demonstrably leave Cyprus and the EU within the applicable transport and documentation window.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document / EU AES) bearing the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and customs office of exit outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the pharmaceutical API, Incoterms, quantity, and overseas purchaser
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) or CMR consignment note (road) evidencing physical carriage out of Cyprus
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds into the Cyprus company bank account
  • Terminal delivery order, cargo-out-turn report, or shipping company export manifest confirming goods loaded and departed from Cyprus
  • EC Sales List and Intrastat return (for intra-Community supplies) with validation of the customer's EU VAT number

No real-time transaction-level fiscal clearance, government API validation, or digital stamping mandate is currently in force in Cyprus. Compulsory electronic filing of VAT returns (VAT Form 4), EC Sales Lists, and Intrastat declarations applies via TAXISnet. National e-invoicing framework enacted under Law 70(I)/2019 (transposing Directive 2014/55/EU) mandates B2G electronic invoices in EN 16931 format; mandatory B2B e-invoicing is being phased in under the EU VAT-in-the-Digital-Age initiative, but paper and standard electronic invoices remain acceptable for compliance at present.

The Tax Department may retrospectively void the 0% rating and assess 19% VAT, statutory interest at 10% per annum, and administrative penalties of up to €17,086 where export evidence is missing, forged, untimely, or inconsistent with the customs declaration. Deliberate or fraudulent zero-rating (e.g., fictitious or diverted exports) constitutes a criminal offence under s.47 VAT Law, punishable by a fine of up to €17,086 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years; customs penalties for false export declarations under EU Regulation (EU) No 952/2013 may reach double the evaded customs duty with seizure of goods and means of transport. VAT and customs audits ordinarily cover 5 prior tax years, extended to 10 years for fraud; clawback assessments are retroactive to the date of supply.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Cyprus adopted the euro as its currency and maintains full current account and capital account convertibility pursuant to EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (TFEU Articles 63–66) and the Cypriot Exchange Control Law (L.120(I)/1996, as amended). Cyprus applies no general foreign exchange control restrictions on euro-denominated transactions. However, transaction-level reporting obligations apply to cash movements above EUR 10,000 under the EU Anti-Money Laundering Directive and the Cyprus AMLA framework. For a Cyprus private limited company engaged in wholesale distribution of pharmaceutical APIs, profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permissible without exchange control approval, subject to corporate law solvency tests, distribution formalities, and standard withholding tax rules (which are generally exempt for non-resident shareholders under Cypriot domestic law and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive implementation). Capital account inflows of foreign currency require the natural conversion into euros at market rates through authorised banks; there are no surrender requirements. Trade-related current account payments, including API import settlement and distribution fee payments, require no FX license. Practical hurdles are limited to banking due diligence, source-of-wealth verification, and the correct use of International Bank Account Number (IBAN), BIC, and payment purpose codes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender obligations, mandatory conversion ratios, or time limits for export receipts under Cypriot law or EU law. Export earnings in foreign currency may be retained on account, converted to euros at any time, or netted against payables, provided that the transactions are recorded in the entity's accounting ledgers and underlying trade documents are retained for at least six years for tax audit purposes. The Central Bank of Cyprus and commercial banks apply no repatriation timeline, but banks may apply internal compliance policies requiring receipt notification and source-of-funds documentation for high-risk pharma transactions.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) in coordination with the Ministry of Finance and the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) for payment and capital movement oversight; exchange control functions are also subject to EU Capital Markets Union and ECB regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legal feasibility for offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) is Medium to Low for a pharmaceutical API wholesale entity. Cyprus follows the EU PSD2 framework and the Cyprus Electronic Money Law (L.81(I)/2018, as amended). An EU/EEA-licensed EMI may lawfully provide payment accounts and cross-border clearing services in Cyprus under passporting rules. However, major domestic banks often refuse to upstream or downstream correspondent clearing to EMIs for pharma-related transactions due to heightened trade-based money-laundering risk, sanctions screening complexity, and the special licensing requirements for medicinal product wholesalers. An EMI account can be opened remotely and used for operational collections or supplier payments, but it is not a viable substitute for a Tier 1 bank account for high-value API letters of credit, capital injection receipts, or settlement of regulated pharmaceutical invoices. Onboarding viability rating: Low as sole banking route; medium as supplementary payment rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound euro or foreign-currency capital injections for a Cyprus private limited company are routed through the entity's Tier 1 account held with a Cyprus-authorised credit institution. The sending institution, whether an EU bank or a third-country bank, must transmit via SWIFT MT103/MT202 or TARGET2, referencing as purpose code the ECB BPM6 standard code. For capital in the form of shareholder loan or equity subscription, the company must lodge with the bank a board resolution approving the share issue or loan, shareholder subscription agreement, audited or management accounts, and confirmation from the Cyprus Registrar of Companies if the injection is recorded in the share capital register. The Central Bank of Cyprus does not approve or licence the inflow; it is reported by the bank as part of the balance-of-payments statistical reporting. Incoming transfers must identify the ultimate originator using a full name, address, and account number to satisfy the EU Funds Transfer Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2015/847, as amended); missing originator details cause rejection or a prolonged compliance review. The recommended purpose codes are J.411 for equity and J.412 for intercompany loans, although banks may also map to domestic codes such as 'FCAP' for financial account capital and 'SO' for other investment. For third-country inbound transfers, include the CBPR+ (ISO 20022) structured address and purpose code fields to ensure straight-through processing.Outbound disbursements for API wholesale are executed principally through SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for clean payments and Documentary Letters of Credit (DC LC) for bulk shipments. The Ukrainian/Cypriot corporate account holder will instruct payments via the bank's electronic banking platform; each disbursement must be backed by a purchase order, commercial invoice, and contract. For high-value or regulated/pharma counterparties, the bank may require an LC or collection arrangement. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Legal feasibility for offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) is Medium to Low for a pharmaceutical API wholesale entity. Cyprus follows the EU PSD2 framework and the Cyprus Electronic Money Law (L.81(I)/2018, as amended). An EU/EEA-licensed EMI may lawfully provide payment accounts and cross-border clearing services in Cyprus under passporting rules. However, major domestic banks often refuse to upstream or downstream correspondent clearing to EMIs for pharma-related transactions due to heightened trade-based money-laundering risk, sanctions screening complexity, and the special licensing requirements for medicinal product wholesalers. An EMI account can be opened remotely and used for operational collections or supplier payments, but it is not a viable substitute for a Tier 1 bank account for high-value API letters of credit, capital injection receipts, or settlement of regulated pharmaceutical invoices. Onboarding viability rating: Low as sole banking route; medium as supplementary payment rail.

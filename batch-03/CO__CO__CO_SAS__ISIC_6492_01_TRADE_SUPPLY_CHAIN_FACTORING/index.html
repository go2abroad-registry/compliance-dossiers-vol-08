Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — official portal: www.dian.gov.co; transactional e-services via Muisca (muisca.dian.gov.co)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through DIAN online pre-registration using a local fiscal representative, providing notarised and apostilled constitutional documents, a local business address, and Chamber of Commerce certification. Upon completion, DIAN assigns the NIT, which also serves as the VAT ID. A Colombian S.A.S. obtains its NIT automatically through the Chamber of Commerce registration process.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; resident entities are subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income; non-resident shareholders are taxed only on Colombian-source income; no pass-through election is available for S.A.S. — Foreign entities must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through DIAN online pre-registration using a local fiscal representative, providing notarised and apostilled constitutional documents, a local business address, and Chamber of Commerce certification. Upon completion, DIAN assigns the NIT, which also serves as the VAT ID. A Colombian S.A.S. obtains its NIT automatically through the Chamber of Commerce registration process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; resident entities are subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income; non-resident shareholders are taxed only on Colombian-source income; no pass-through election is available for S.A.S.

Available election forms

No general check-the-box or pass-through election. Optionally, qualifying small taxpayers may elect the 'Régimen Simple de Tributación' (RST), but this is generally unavailable for financial or factoring activities. Colombian hybrid mismatch rules (Law 2277 of 2022) apply to deny deductions in mismatch arrangements and align tax outcomes with economic substance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for legal entities; every S.A.S. must register as an IVA responsible before commencing taxable activities. Natural persons may qualify as 'non-responsables' only if they are not importers/exporters and gross income does not exceed 3,500 UVT (approximately 147,017,500 COP in 2024).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and qualifying services are taxable at 0% VAT with refund rights. Goods exports require legal outflow of the goods from Colombian customs territory to the foreign buyer. Services must be provided to a non-resident, effectively used or exploited outside Colombia, and not linked to Colombian real estate or assets; the exporter must register and comply with DIAN Resolution 316 of 2017. Cross-border factoring/reverse factoring can qualify if the service is wholly consumed abroad, subject to financial-services exclusion rules.

  • Approved Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación/DEX)
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Carta Porte Internacional)
  • Commercial invoice issued by the exporter
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a Colombian foreign-exchange intermediary (certificación de reintegro de divisas)
  • Proof of foreign payment (SWIFT/bank transfer) and the export contract for services

Mandatory electronic invoicing ('Factura Electrónica de Venta') via DIAN-certified software. Invoices must be transmitted for real-time validation and clearance using CUFE/CUDE codes, with a digital XML, QR code, and DIAN validation status. This applies also to export sales and zero-rated services.

DIAN may review zero-rating claims retroactively for up to five years. If supported documentation for physical export, service delivery abroad, and FX repatriation is incomplete, the taxpayer will be assessed 19% VAT plus monthly interest penalties (approximate current charge 2.3% per month), and an inexact-return penalty up to 200% of the omitted tax. Fraudulent or forged export documentation triggers criminal tax-evasion liability under Law 1762 of 2015, with imprisonment from 4 to 9 years; shareholders and legal representatives may also be held jointly liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a liberalized exchange control regime, but the Colombian peso is not fully freely convertible outside the domestic banking system. Current account transactions, including trade settlements and profit remittances, are generally permitted and convertible through authorized FX intermediaries. However, capital account operations, including foreign investment, loans, and factoring-related cross-border flows, require registration with Banco de la República, the filing of foreign exchange declaration declarations (Declaraciones de Cambio), and compliance with quantitative reporting thresholds. Profit remittances require evidence of payment of income tax (impuesto de renta) and documentation of the underlying investment registration. For trade supply chain factoring, conversion of foreign currency proceeds is mandatory and must be channeled through the Colombian foreign exchange market within strict timelines. Non-resident factoring entities are subject to limitations on local onboarding and must act through licensed Colombian financial institutions or branches.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to Colombia within 6 months from the date of export (extendable to 12 months with prior Banco de la República authorization). A minimum of 80% of export proceeds must be converted into Colombian pesos (COP) and sold to the domestic foreign exchange market through an authorized intermediary within the statutory surrender window. The remaining 20% may be held in foreign currency accounts locally or abroad outside the mandatory surrender requirement. For factoring companies, collection of assigned export receivables from foreign debtors must be routed into the Colombian FX market through an authorized bank, and the transaction must be reported on the applicable foreign exchange declaration form (Formulario No. 5, 10, 11, or 12). Failure to comply triggers fines, penal interest, suspension of FX registration, and loss of tax benefits associated with investment or export activity.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) and Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. Colombia does not recognize foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) as authorized payment institutions. Digital-only banks and offshore EMIs cannot directly open local bank accounts or process local COP clearing without obtaining a license from the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia or establishing a local commercial financing company (compañía de financiamiento). However, a foreign EMI may serve as a complementary payment gateway for collecting funds from non-Colombian debtors, provided that all funds are subsequently settled into a Colombian commercial bank account for FX surrender. Direct operation of a Colombian peso wallet or receiving export proceeds in an offshore EMI is not permitted.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to an authorized Colombian commercial bank in COP or foreign currency. Transfers must reach the company's COP account under the company's NIT tax identification number and include the capital contribution purpose code. The transaction must be registered on Banco de la República's foreign exchange declaration forms within 30 calendar days if the contribution qualifies as foreign direct investment (FDI) in an S.A.S. The bank will validate the KYC marginal note on the company's RUT and issue a certificate of retention. To avoid blocking, the sending bank must include the full company corporate name, RUT ID 'CO_SAS', and a clear reference such as 'Capital Contribution / Share Subscription'. Foreign debtors' payments to the factoring account shall be identified with the underlying invoice and the corresponding NIT.Outbound trade disbursements are cleared using: (a) local COP payments through CENIT or the ACH system for domestic suppliers of trade payables; (b) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border payments of foreign invoices, using proper purpose and dedicated description 'Trade Finance Disbursement'; and (c) commercial letters of credit (import/export L/C) issued by the Colombian bank on behalf of the factoring entity. The bank must verify electronic invoice (CUE) and the underlying factoring contract before release. For reverse factoring, payments must be made to the supplier's local account and immediately recorded as factoring disbursement, with the debtor's repayment later received through MT103 from abroad and converted via the local market.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/COP contracted with authorized intermediaries, but limited to hedging physical receipts.Deliverable COP/USD forward contracts through local commercial banks, requiring underlying trade flow.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) between COP and USD/EUR, for tenor matching borrowing cycles.FX options (caballos) purchased for downside protection, provided they align with the trade receivable portfolio.Cancellation of hedging positions via novation or offset, but physical conversion of funds must be conducted through the Colombian FX market.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% (Colombian corporate income tax rate under Law 2277 of 2022, applicable to taxable years 2023 onwards)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, applied by the Colombian payer as final withholding tax. Dividends paid from profits previously subjected to 35% Colombian CIT do not generate additional foreign remittance tax beyond this 10% statutory dividend WHT.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual income tax return is due according to DIAN's official tax calendar, determined by the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT. For a calendar-year Colombian S.A.S., the return is generally due no later than the September filing window of the immediately following tax year.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 — NIT and DV Taxpayer's Colombian tax identification number, verification digit, reason for filing, and fiscal period. The taxable year defaults to the calendar year unless DIAN previously authorized a different fiscal period. A non-resident with a Colombian permanent establishment (PE) must obtain a Colombian NIT before filing and must state that it is acting only through its registered PE. A non-resident without a Colombian PE must not file Form 210 for Colombian-sourced income; the Colombian payer applies final withholding tax on gross amounts. Late filing subjects the taxpayer to a penalty of 5% of the tax due, increased monthly (or by fractional month) by 1.5% for late filing, capped at 100% of the tax due. If no tax is due, the penalty is 0.5% of gross income or, when all fields are zero, a fixed amount equal to approximately COP 104,000 (as indexed by UVT). Failure to file also generates default interest at DIAN's effective rate, suspension of the taxpayer's RUT status, and criminal liability if the omission is fraudulent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Colombian S.A.S. is required to prepare and file the Transfer Pricing Declaration, Local File, and Master File when its annual gross income or total gross assets at year-end exceed 100,000 UVTs (approximately COP 4.7 billion for the 2025 fiscal year, adjusted annually by DIAN). Lower mandatory-filing thresholds apply in the case of transactions with individuals or entities situated in designated low-tax or no-tax jurisdictions regardless of the above threshold.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code in the Gulf-Council sense. However, Colombian tax law requires the existence of real economic substance for tax-residence, treaty-benefit, and permanent-establishment determinations. A shell S.A.S. used solely to hold factored receivables or to channel cross-border fees without actual personnel, decision-making, and operational activity will be disregarded for Colombian tax purposes under the general anti-avoidance provisions and the economic-substance requirements embedded in Colombian DTA policy. A serial factoring vehicle must maintain full operational substance in Colombia to be treated as Colombian resident and to obtain treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — False or Omitted Factoring Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under Article 434 of the Colombian Criminal Code carries imprisonment of 48 to 108 months and a fine equal to 200% to 1,000% of the evaded tax. DIAN additionally imposes corrective-assessments with penalties of up to 200% of the understated tax, default interest, reputational placement on DIAN's public tax-debt registry, and potential cancellation of the RUT.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and accountants who sign or cause false declarations may be held personally liable for the tax penalty under the civil-liability rule of Article 793 of the Tax Code; criminal prosecution runs directly against the individuals. Colombian courts may order asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment, piercing the ESG structure of the S.A.S. when it is used as a fraud instrument.
Shell Company / Fraudulent Corporate Structure

Statutory sanction: The artificial structure is disregarded under Colombian anti-abuse doctrine, the S.A.S. is taxed on its worldwide income as a Colombian resident, and the add-back of denied deductions and recharacterized income carries penalties. Additionally, individuals involved in fraud using shell companies may be liable for aggravated fraud and false-reporting criminal offenses under the Criminal Code, with severe fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Colombian courts will pierce the corporate veil where the S.A.S. is used to defraud creditors or the tax authority. Directors and shadow directors can be ordered to personally restore diverted funds and pay unpaid taxes, subjected to asset embargoes, named in the UIAF money-laundering risk list, and prohibited from leaving Colombia while criminal investigation is pending.
Unauthorized Factoring Activity / Money Laundering Through Factoring

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed financial intermediation is penalized by the Superintendency of Societies and/or the Superintendency of Financial Companies with closure of offices, dissolution of the company, and monetary fines calibrated to illicit gains. Separate money-laundering offenses under Law 599 of 2000 impose substantial imprisonment, fines, and confiscation of assets linked to the laundering scheme.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly execute unlawful factoring or fail to apply AML/KYC controls are personally liable for criminal fines and long-term imprisonment. The court may issue precautionary measures including travel bans, seizure of personal bank accounts, and disqualification from serving as a director in any Colombian company.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Abusive Related-Party Factoring Terms

Statutory sanction: DIAN may re-determine the Colombian taxable income using fair arm's-length prices, charge the resulting tax underpayment plus default interest, and impose transfer-pricing penalties equal to up to 2% of the value of unsupported cross-border transactions. Repeated non-compliance triggers aggravated fines and comprehensive tax audits.

Director personal liability: Representatives who sign false transfer-pricing documentation are jointly liable for the penalty and may be personally sued for the difference in tax. The individual who authorized or executed the abusive related-party pricing may be named in DIAN's criminal referral; mechanisms include travel bans, confiscation of assets, and imprisonment where the pricing arrangement is proven to be a fraudulent evasion instrument.

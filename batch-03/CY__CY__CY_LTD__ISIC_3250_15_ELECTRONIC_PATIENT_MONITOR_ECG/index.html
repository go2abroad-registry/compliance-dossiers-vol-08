Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement for a Cyprus private limited company; the nominal share capital is set in the Memorandum of Association and may be a nominal amount such as EUR 1. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus. Electronic portal: Taxisnet, https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy. VAT registration and filings are administered by the VAT Service within the Department of Taxation, in coordination with the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department for export controls and documentation.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the Private Limited Company (HE) with the Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (ROCR). Step 2 — Register with the Department of Taxation within 60 days of commencement of business for income tax purposes; the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is the tax registration number issued by the Tax Department, and for VAT-registered entities the VAT number is used as the functional TIN. Step 3 — Submit the VAT registration application to the VAT Service where annual taxable supplies exceed EUR 15,600, intra-Community acquisitions exceed EUR 10,000, or distance sales / OSS-eligible supplies into Cyprus exceed EUR 10,000. Step 4 — Foreign entities without an established place of business must appoint a fiscal representative in Cyprus and register from the first taxable transaction if any threshold is exceeded or a Cyprus VAT liability arises; registration is effected through Taxisnet online submission of the entity's constitutive documents, board/shareholder resolutions, KYC documentation, and evidence of the local agent or permanent establishment where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Cyprus-resident Private Limited Company is a separate taxable person subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide profits, with territorial exemptions for certain dividend income, foreign-sourced interest, and capital gains. The entity is not eligible for pass-through treatment and is outside any tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available. Cyprus law provides the following tax elections: (1) VAT grouping election under Article 11 of the VAT Law, treating affiliated Cyprus-established persons as a single taxable person; (2) income-tax group relief election under Section 11 of the Income Tax Law for surrender of tax losses among group members with at least 75% direct or indirect shareholding; (3) notional interest deduction (NID) election on qualifying new equity capital; (4) IP box election applying the 80% qualifying-intangible asset exemption on a nexus-conditioned basis; (5) tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping entities; (6) ATAD 2 hybrid-mismatch alignment filings for transparency and deduction-treatment self-declarations, subject to Cyprus anti-hybrid statutory rules rather than elective check-the-box mechanics.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General registration threshold: EUR 15,600 of annual taxable turnover for supplies of goods and services in Cyprus. Special thresholds: EUR 10,000 for intra-Community acquisitions and EUR 10,000 for distance sales of goods and cross-border B2C electronic services (OSS/IOSS schemes). Foreign suppliers having a Cyprus fiscal warehouse or exceeding these thresholds must register for Cyprus VAT or apply the Union/non-Union OSS or IOSS scheme as applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (multi-parameter ECG/bedside telemetry monitors) to destinations outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under Article 50 of the Cyprus VAT Law where the customs export declaration proves the goods physically left the EU within the statutory timeframe. Intra-Community supplies to VAT-registered business customers in other EU Member States qualify for 0% VAT when the customer's valid EU VAT number is obtained, the supply is documented, and the seller files a correct EC Sales List/VIES declaration. Ancillary assembly and calibration services physically attached to and forming part of the exported goods sold under a single contract are zero-rated as part of the export value. Services rendered to non-established business recipients (B2B general place-of-supply rule) are treated as outside the scope of Cyprus VAT or zero-rated where the recipient's establishment is outside Cyprus. All zero-rating claims require credible transport evidence and normal commercial-terms documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document/SAD accepted by the Cyprus Customs & Excise Department, filed electronically through the National Transit and Excise Application Tool/NTEAT or the Import Control System)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR road consignment note evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and, for EU-bound customers, Intrastat declaration and EC Sales List/VIES entry
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, and bank statements evidencing settlement of the export proceeds (FX repatriation record)
  • Technical regulatory file supporting the shipment: CE marking under EU MDR 2017/745, ISO 13485 certification, Notified Body certificate, and tariff classification under Combined Nomenclature 9018 / ISIC 3250

As of 2024, Cyprus does not impose mandatory real-time B2B e-invoicing clearance, digital stamping, or API-based fiscal reporting. The Tax Department accepts EU-compliant structured e-invoices and requires electronic submission of VAT returns, VIES/EC Sales Lists, and Intrastat declarations through Taxisnet. Mandatory electronic fiscal supervision exists in select sectors (e.g., petroleum products and passenger transport). Cyprus is aligned with the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) initiative, which will introduce mandatory e-invoicing and digital real-time reporting for intra-EU B2B transactions from 2028/2030; exporters of electronic patient-monitoring equipment should maintain system readiness for structured e-invoicing and future continuous transaction controls.

The Department of Taxation may reopen zero-rated export claims and assess VAT, default interest (9% per annum), and administrative penalties where the documentary chain is incomplete or export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day reference window, on the theory that the export finality presumption is rebutted. Fraudulent or recklessly incorrect zero-rating claims attract clawback of input VAT previously refunded, penalty up to 100% of the VAT involved, criminal sanctions (fines and/or imprisonment up to 18 months) under the Cyprus VAT Law, and a 10-year limitation period for fraud cases. Directors and officers may be held jointly and severally liable, and future refunds may be blocked or offset until the exporter establishes clean compliance records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a Eurozone member and applies full capital and current account convertibility under Articles 63-65 TFEU. Euro balances and foreign-currency accounts are freely convertible; no Central Bank approval or exchange-control license is required for capital injections, trade settlement, dividend remittance, or loan repayments. Profit remittance is unrestricted at treasury level, although corporate income tax at 12.5%, transfer-pricing compliance, and AML/KYC documentation at the paying bank remain applicable. Commercial banks may impose internal procedures for sanctions screening and source-of-funds verification but cannot lawfully restrict conversion beyond EU regulatory measures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Cyprus. As a Eurozone export entity, CY Ltd is free to hold export receipts in EUR or designated foreign-currency accounts. Export receipts are reportable to the Central Bank of Cyprus for balance-of-payments statistics if they exceed applicable reporting thresholds; settlement is recorded under the external trade and services item. Banks may require the exporter to submit customs declarations, invoices, and shipping evidence to support the economic purpose of SWIFT inflows.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), acting within the Eurosystem and under the European Central Bank's monetary policy and supervisory framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for EU/EEA passported electronic money institutions operating in Cyprus under the PSD2/E-Money Directive passport, and Low for offshore digital EMIs without a Cyprus or EEA passport. A Cyprus-incorporated manufacturing/trading company may use an EEA EMI for settlement and payment initiation provided the EMI holds a valid Cyprus Central Bank passport notification or a home-state license recognized under EU directives. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot grant credit or maintain client funds beyond safeguarded funds; EMIs are generally not viable for holding trade-finance escrow or managing documentary letters of credit. For letter-of-credit and high-value medical-device trade settlements, a full licensed Cypriot bank is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital should be routed by SWIFT MT103 or CBPR+ ISO 20022 high-value payment to CY Ltd's euro account with Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank. The remittance information must declare 'Share Capital Contribution – CY Ltd' and include the company registration number HE77 and the purpose indicator 'EQUI' or the Cypriot balance-of-payments code for direct investment. For inbound trade advances, the remittance should cite the commercial purpose code such as 'TRDC' and match underlying purchase orders and customs declarations. No prior exchange-control approval is needed for non-resident investors, but the receiving bank will perform AML, sanctions, and source-of-funds checks; all capital inflows above EUR 10,000 are subject to Central Bank of Cyprus statistical or balance-of-payments reporting. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 – aggregate annual value of related-party transactions (goods, services, intangibles, and financing). Above this threshold, a Master File, Local File, and the TP Summary Information Table are mandatory; below the threshold, documentation is exempt unless the Tax Commissioner issues a TP audit notice.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate an offshore ESR framework; instead, the relevant doctrine is the management-and-control test of tax residence (Article 4 of the Income Tax Law), ATAD-compliant CFC rules, the nexus-based substance requirement of the IP Box regime (qualifying R&D personnel and activity in Cyprus), and the GAAR in Article 33A authorizing the Commissioner to disregard transactions lacking genuine commercial substance. A medical-device entity that merely invoices sales without staff, premises, or board decisions in Cyprus is exposed to GAAR recharacterization, CFC attribution, and loss of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Understatement

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction: fine up to EUR 35,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years under Section 35 of the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law (L.4/1978); administrative surcharges of 5% plus 5% on the unpaid tax, monthly interest of 1.5%–2.5%, and confiscation or freezing orders over assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax where they signed or approved false accounts; individual criminal exposure includes personal fines, imprisonment, travel bans, and disqualification under the Companies Law (Cap. 113).
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of the Cyprus entity's profits at 12.5% on the adjusted amount; total surcharges of 10% (5% after 1 August, 5% after 31 August), monthly interest at 1.5%–2.5%, an administrative penalty of up to EUR 500 for the missing TP-SIT, and disallowance of excess interest under the ATAD EBITDA limitation rule if financing arrangements are non-arm's-length.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length component prices face personal liability for false statutory declarations; the Tax Commissioner may pierce the corporate veil and impose secondary liability on controlling directors for the exact unpaid corporate tax and surcharges.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (GAAR and Substance)

Statutory sanction: GAAR (Article 33A) disregards the entire arrangement, re-attributing profits to the actual economic owner at 12.5% with interest of 1.5%–2.5% per month and the standard surcharges; CFC rules attribute undistributed profits of the low-tax foreign subsidiary to Cyprus; the Registrar of Companies may strike off the entity for inactive shell conduct and the 2.5% annual levy filings are ineffective without a statutory declaration of activity.

Director personal liability: Directors of shell entities face criminal liability for dishonest use of the corporate form, civil restitution orders, freezing injunctions, and international travel bans issued by the Cyprus Courts; cross-border AEOI data exchange flags the mismatches and can trigger mutual assistance requests.
AML Non-Compliance (Money Laundering and Sanctions)

Statutory sanction: Under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (L.188(I)/2007 as amended): fines up to EUR 500,000 for legal entities and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; confiscation of the wired funds; suspension of the Cyprus Medical Device Registration and export license; trade and foreign-exchange restrictions.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are personally criminally liable for AML breaches; they may be ordered to pay civil damages to defrauded hospitals, disqualified from future directorships, and subjected to seizure of personal assets where the compliance failure was deliberate or grossly negligent.

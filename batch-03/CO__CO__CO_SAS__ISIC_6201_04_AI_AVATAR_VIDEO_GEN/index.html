Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal www.dian.gov.co and transactional platform MUISCA

E-government filing portal

A foreign sponsor incorporates the S.A.S. before a notary and registers the deed with the Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio). The company must then register in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) via DIAN's MUISCA portal (Formulario Pre-RUT) to obtain the Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT). The S.A.S. must designate a legal representative resident in Colombia for tax compliance; foreign shareholders without Colombian presence are subject to local withholding tax on distributions, requiring the entity to enroll in withholding-regime categories. A foreign vendor providing or receiving services in Colombia should also obtain a NIT for tax-payer identification when generating Colombian-source income, but a local permanent establishment triggers full RUT registration obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: An S.A.S. incorporated under Colombian law is a Colombian tax resident taxed on worldwide income (corporate income tax, CIT) at 35% for tax year 2024, falling to 30% from tax year 2025. No pass-through election is permitted. Non-residents are taxed only on Colombian-source income.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists; Colombia applies mandatory anti-hybrid rules under Law 1819/2016 (aligned with BEPS Action 2). Optional elective regimes include: (i) ZESE – Zona Económica y Social Especial, a reduced CIT rate (26% for the first 5 years) for new companies established in qualifying municipalities; (ii) creative-industries (Orange Economy) tax incentive potentially available to new software/content-development ventures meeting Decree-979/2020 conditions; (iii) exemption of certain custom software fabrication services from VAT where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies to commercial companies: every S.A.S. engaged in taxable operations must obtain a NIT and register in the RUT in the Régimen Común, regardless of sales volume. The simplified VAT regime (for sole proprietors with gross income under 3,500 UVT, approx. COP 125 million) is unavailable to S.A.S. entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of services, including AI video synthesis and virtual-avatar SaaS, qualify for a 0% VAT rate (exempt with right to refund) under Article 481(e) of the Tax Statute and Decree 2248/2014 where: (i) the service is provided from Colombia to a foreign beneficiary without domicile in Colombia; (ii) the service is used exclusively abroad; and (iii) the contract is evidenced in writing and no local real estate or Colombian territorial exploitation is involved. Revenue must be ingressed through the foreign-exchange market.

  • Signed export-services contract (bilingual or officially translated to Spanish) demonstrating exclusive foreign use
  • DIAN-validated electronic commercial invoice (factura electrónica) referencing the contract
  • Formulario de Declaración de Cambio No. 4 (foreign-exchange settlement certificate) issued by an authorized Colombian bank or FX intermediary
  • Proof of inbound remittance (SWIFT confirmation, Stripe/Paddle payout statement) evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • RUT certificate showing IVA registration and export-service activity status
  • Certificate of existence and legal representation for the S.A.S. (Cámara de Comercio) if required by DIAN on audit

Mandatory electronic invoicing (factura electrónica) with pre-issuance real-time validation by DIAN. Invoices must be generated through a DIAN-authorized technology provider (proveedor tecnológico) or DIAN's free billing tool, in the authorized XML schema (CUFE/CUDE with UUID and QR code), and submitted to DIAN's Hab.Se web services (FE-V1) for prior validation. Electronic issuance is obligatory for exports of services and for filing VAT-refund requests.

DIAN scrutinizes export-refund claims on substantial and documentary grounds; improper refunds are clawed back with daily arrears interest computed from the original refund payment (Article 118 ET) plus an additional penalty of up to 100% of the amount evaded (Article 647(1) ET) and a 4% monthly-equivalent late-interest adjustment. False or fraudulent export zero-rating claims to obtain VAT refunds constitute criminal tax evasion under Law 1943/2018, Articles 102 ff., carrying imprisonment of up to 9 years and fines of multiples of the defrauded amount. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may trigger exchange-infringement penalties under Banco de la República rules and disqualification from the zero-rate treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a market-based free-convertibility regime for current-account items: software-export revenues, AI-content royalties and international service fees are freely receivable and convertible at the free-market TRM through authorized intermediaries without prior Central Bank approval. Capital-account flows, including paid-in equity from the non-resident shareholder, are also permissible but require mandatory ex-post registration with Banco de la República in the foreign-investment registry; that registration alone confers the legal entitlement to remit future dividends/profits and to repatriate invested capital. Dividend-profit remittance is not capped or deferred, but the two gateways are: (i) valid registration of the foreign investment, and (ii) prior payment of the Colombian dividend withholding tax together with certification by the Revisor Fiscal or independent accountant that the distributable profits exist in the audited books. The practical hurdles are documentary and administrative, not substantive: late or defective investment registration temporarily suspends remittance rights and attracts exchange-control fines, whereas current-account convertibility remains unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Decree 1368 of 2021 and Resolution 8 of 2000, the exporter must channel 100% of services-export proceeds — including AIGC SaaS subscription fees received via Stripe/Paddle aggregations — into the Colombian Mercado Cambiario through an authorized commercial bank within 6 months from the date of the export (for services; computed from invoicing/performance); goods-export proceeds enjoy the same 6-month term, extendable by up to 4 additional months. There is no fixed monetary surrender ratio payable to the Central Bank; the obligation is the full-channeling duty, meaning the entire export value must enter the national banking system either as conversion to COP at the free TRM or as a credit to the exporter's foreign-currency deposit account at an authorized local intermediary. Offshore retention of export proceeds without a prior 'administración de divisas' authorization is prohibited; breach triggers DIAN exchange-control penalties and loss of export-incentive privileges. For the digital-service exporter, the critical operational risk is aggregation: each monthly payout from Stripe/Paddle must be traceable to individual software-export invoices so the bank can file the corresponding exchange declaration.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (the Central Bank of Colombia), acting through its governing board, the Junta Directiva del Banco de la República; the national foreign-exchange framework comprises Law 9 of 1991, Resolution 8 of 2000 (as amended), Decree 1368 of 2021 and prudential rules issued by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia; only authorized commercial banks enrolled in the Mercado Cambiario may lawfully act as exchange intermediaries; the DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) enforces exchange-control penalties and customs-related FX duties.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: LOW-to-MEDIUM legal viability as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Deel, Payoneer, Mercury, Brex) are not authorized Colombian exchange-market participants and cannot lawfully receive or settle the SAS's software-export proceeds; using them as the principal collection account would breach the 6-month channeling duty, trigger exchange-control fines and undermine bankability at the Tier-1 institution. EMIs remain legally usable for ancillary operational outflows (e.g., low-value international contractor payments) provided the outflows are documented and, when required, reflected in the periodic FX information filed through the authorized intermediary. Onshore fintechs with a local banking license (Lulo Bank; Nequi, supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera) are viable only as supporting expense/operational accounts, not as the statutory FX-channeling vehicle. Recommended architecture: one Tier-1 commercial bank account as the single forex-channeling backbone; Stripe/Paddle consolidated payouts remitted monthly into that account; offshore EMIs confined to foreign micro-payments and expressly disclosed in the treasury compliance file.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant sequence for the non-resident shareholder's USD equity injection: (1) the offshore bank remits via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202/MT202COV where same-day cover is required); Field 50K must show the remitter exactly as registered, Field 59 the S.A.S. as beneficiary, and Field 70 must carry ISO 20022 External Purpose Code 'INTC' (intra-company payment) with the narrative 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION / CAPITAL INCREASE — COLOMBIAN S.A.S.'; (2) the receiving Tier-1 bank, as authorized exchange intermediary, applies SARLAFT screening to confirm that the remitter is the actual registered shareholder; (3) the funds are credited to a COP account (converted at the TRM) or to a USD foreign-currency account of the SAS, and the bank simultaneously files the exchange declaration with Banco de la República; (4) the legal representative completes the Banco de la República foreign-investment registration (inversión internacional directa), which serves as the legal title for future dividend remittances and capital repatriation; (5) corporate formalities are archived, including the shareholders' approval minute, the Cámara de Comercio capital annotation, the RUT update and the banker's capital certificate. Routing through EMIs, crypto exchanges or personal accounts is prohibited for FDI recognition.Clearing-rail matrix: (a) SWIFT MT103 (with ISO purpose codes such as 'TRAD' or 'INTC' for intra-group cross-border cost payments) is the default rail for trade disbursements, covering GPU-cloud providers, CDN licensing, foreign legal/tax advisors and international contractor payments; outflows are declared in the exchange regime when they exceed the bank's reporting threshold (generally USD 10,000-equivalent per operation or lower per policy); (b) MT202/MT202COV supports interbank funding of the company's offshore EMI accounts, subject to upstream underlying-expense documentation; (c) Commercial and Standby Letters of Credit are available for high-value OEM/distribution or infrastructure procurement: when the S.A.S. is exporter/beneficiary, the LC guarantees payment against presentation of software acceptance certificates or milestone invoices; when the S.A.S. imports GPU capacity for its AI rendering pipeline, a documentary LC mitigates counterparty risk; (d) domestic COP flows (payroll, rent, utilities, DIAN taxes) run on the local PSE/ACH clearing network, while Stripe and Paddle consolidated payouts are received into the same Tier-1 account and channeled against the corresponding export declarations. Every settlement architecture must preserve invoice-tagging discipline so that each MT103 carries the contractual reference recoverable by the bank's compliance function.Outright COP/USD forward contracts (forwards) traded with an authorized Colombian exchange intermediary, with delivery at the TRM, for hedging forecast USD export receivablesNon-deliverable COP/USD forwards (NDFs) settled in COP against the TRM reference rate, usable where a real USD exposure is forecast but physical delivery is not desiredCOP/USD cross-currency swaps covering principal and coupon flows, permissible when the SAS holds a matching USD liability, e.g., international financing for GPU-capacity capital expenditureEuropean-style COP/USD FX options purchased from onshore bank trading desks as premium-based protection against adverse TRM movements on large-ticket licensing receipts

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: LOW-to-MEDIUM legal viability as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Deel, Payoneer, Mercury, Brex) are not authorized Colombian exchange-market participants and cannot lawfully receive or settle the SAS's software-export proceeds; using them as the principal collection account would breach the 6-month channeling duty, trigger exchange-control fines and undermine bankability at the Tier-1 institution. EMIs remain legally usable for ancillary operational outflows (e.g., low-value international contractor payments) provided the outflows are documented and, when required, reflected in the periodic FX information filed through the authorized intermediary. Onshore fintechs with a local banking license (Lulo Bank; Nequi, supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera) are viable only as supporting expense/operational accounts, not as the statutory FX-channeling vehicle. Recommended architecture: one Tier-1 commercial bank account as the single forex-channeling backbone; Stripe/Paddle consolidated payouts remitted monthly into that account; offshore EMIs confined to foreign micro-payments and expressly disclosed in the treasury compliance file.

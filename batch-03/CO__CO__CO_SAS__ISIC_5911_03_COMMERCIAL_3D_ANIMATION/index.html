Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 (Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada regime), Articles 1, 5, 12-17 and 45; Código de Comercio (Decreto 410 de 1971), Articles 110-113 and 126-127; Ley 222 de 1995 (general commercial supervision framework)

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (private incorporation instrument) filed with the Registro Mercantil; preceded by a name pre-approval certificate and resulting in the issuance of the Matrícula Mercantil and the Chamber of Commerce incorporation certificate

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital applies under Ley 1258/2008; however, the shareholders must pay, in cash or in kind, no less than 10% of the subscribed capital at the moment of incorporation, and the balance must be paid within one year. No escrow or notarial deposit account is mandated by law; paid-in capital is evidenced by a bank deposit certificate, a sworn statement, or an appraiser's report for in-kind contributions.

Funding & unlock path: Requires the Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos fire safety certification (Certificado de Condiciones de Seguridad contra Incendios) covering fire extinguishers, evacuation signage and exit capacity; structural load validation by the Curaduría Urbana or a licensed engineer for tenant improvements and heavy equipment racks; and mandatory registration of the workplace with the Administradora de Riesgos Laborales (ARL) plus an occupational health and safety program (SG-SST) in accordance with Decreto 1072/2015 and Resolución 0312/2019.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - the unified Colombian national tax and customs authority; official digital portal: https://www.dian.gov.co (Sede Electrónica).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident-owned Colombian S.A.S. must: (1) complete incorporation before the municipal Cámara de Comercio with notarised by-laws and the appointment of a Colombian legal representative; (2) obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and register in the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) through DIAN's Sede Electrónica, with the assistance of a local certified public accountant; (3) declare in the RUT the status of IVA-responsible, income-tax resident, exporter-of-services and holder of a foreign-exchange reintegration account; (4) procure the DIAN digital signature (firma electrónica) through an authorised certification provider; and (5) activate DIAN-approved electronic invoicing credentials (CUFE capability) before issuing any sales invoice. The full procedure is executable online and is usually completed within two to five business days after the chamber of commerce registration.

The full procedure is executable online and is usually completed within two to five business days after the chamber of commerce registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not provide a US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. The relevant statutory elections are: (a) an annual, binding election to join the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) where the S.A.S.'s prior-year gross income remains within the UVT-indexed cap; SIMPLE substitutes the ordinary corporate income tax and, for qualifying service income, replaces VAT collection with progressive combined rates of 1.4% to 14.5%; (b) the default classification as an ordinary IVA-responsible and entity-level CIT taxpayer (35% standard rate) for all other S.A.S. entities; (c) eligibility to apply for 'Gran Contribuyente' status, a DIAN administrative classification based on turnover that centralises clearance and shortens VAT refund cycles; and (d) a protective disclosure obligation, not an election, with respect to hybrid mismatch arrangements, which are statutorily disallowed under BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold exists for VAT registration: every S.A.S. performing taxable services in Colombia must register as IVA-responsible in the RUT regardless of revenue level. The only UVT-indexed threshold relevant to indirect taxation is the annual ceiling for the SIMPLE combined regime (80,000-100,000 UVT of gross income for service providers, approximately COP 4.0-5.0 billion for tax years 2024-2025), which conditions the combined-regime election and does not waive the RUT/IVA registration duty.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% IVA export treatment for commercial 3D animation, CGI and VFX services is available under Article 481, literal c), of the Colombian Tax Code as developed by Decree 1625 of 2016, provided that: (i) the service is rendered to a recipient that has no tax residence or permanent establishment in Colombia; (ii) the animation, VFX plates, rigs, render files and digital assets are effectively used, enjoyed or commercially exploited outside Colombian territory; (iii) the Colombian S.A.S. is the contractual supplier of the finished deliverables to the foreign production studio; and (iv) the consideration is payable in foreign currency by a non-resident entity. Digital delivery to foreign licensees qualifies as an export of services, and the S.A.S. may claim monthly refunds of pre-export input VAT attributable to the zero-rated transactions.

  • Signed commercial export contract with the foreign production company identifying the animation/VFX deliverables, the foreign licensee or end-client and the effective foreign exploitation clause
  • DIAN-cleared electronic invoice issued in the exporter's name bearing the CUFE and stating the 0% VAT (export) basis and the applicable customs/service tariff nomenclature
  • Electronic export-of-services declaration (Declaración de Exportación de Servicios) transmitted through DIAN's export platform, in PDF/XML accompanied by the DIAN acknowledgement receipt
  • Foreign-currency inflow certificate (certificado de reintegro cambiario / declaración de cambio) issued by the Colombian intermediary bank evidencing inbound remittance of the export proceeds
  • Sworn statement or confirmation letter signed by the foreign client certifying that the animation is exploited outside Colombia, plus asset delivery manifests for licensed IP and source files

Colombia mandates real-time fiscal clearance of all electronic invoices (Facturación Electrónica). Under DIAN Resolution No. 000165 of 2023, sales invoices must be transmitted as XML to DIAN Web Services or through an authorised technology provider (ADP) for immediate validation; DIAN assigns the final sequential number and the unique CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) digital stamp, and only after clearance may the invoice be delivered to the client. The printable PDF must contain the QR code validated by DIAN. Export invoices for CGI, VFX and 3D animation deliverables must use the export-of-services transaction code and state the 0% VAT basis. The same infrastructure requires real-time transmission of the electronic support document (documento soporte) for B2B acquisitions from non-obligated parties. Failure to e-invoice suspends VAT input credits, blocks export zero-rating, and triggers penalties under Article 652-14 of the Tax Code.

DIAN may audit export zero-rating substantiation and the foreign-currency reintegration trail. If the 0% VAT treatment is disallowed, the full 19% IVA is assessed retroactively from the applicable tax period, plus default interest (currently indexed in the range of 20-30% annual) and the inexactity penalty under Article 648 of the Tax Code - generally 100% of the assessed tax, increased to 160% where deliberate fraud is proven. Exporters claiming input VAT refunds remain subject to a 24-month audit look-back; improper refunds are clawed back with a 100% surcharge under Article 670 of the Tax Code and potential suspension of the RUT exporter status. Aggravated conduct, including falsified reintegration certificates or sham foreign clients, incurs customs-related criminal sanctions under Law 1762 of 2015, with fines of up to 300% of the export value and imprisonment of four to nine years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating COP/USD regime administered through the regulated mercado cambiario. Current-account transactions such as cross-border services, royalty payments, trade settlements, and interest are convertible through authorized intermediaries provided the corresponding foreign-exchange declaration is completed. Capital-account transactions require the foreign investment to be registered with Banco de la República through a Colombian bank in order to secure lawful repatriation rights for capital, dividends, and capital gains. There is no absolute prohibition on profit remittance, but remittance depends on registered foreign investment, audited financial statements evidencing distributable earnings, and satisfaction of Colombian withholding taxes. The practical hurdle is documentary: banks will not process the FX operation without a complete Form 4 or equivalent foreign-investment declaration, so the S.A.S. must maintain a clean share subscription paper trail and the foreign investor’s source-of-funds evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of goods and services must channel 100% of export proceeds into the Colombian regulated foreign-exchange market through an authorized intermediary within the maximum timelines set by Banco de la República, generally up to six months from the export date for goods and from the contractual payment due date for services unless a longer bank-authorized payment term applies. The mandated surrender entry does not automatically require immediate conversion to COP: proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency account at a local authorized bank or in a compensation account previously registered with Banco de la República. If the exporter needs to make peso disbursements, the conversion must be settled through an authorized intermediary at the negotiated market exchange rate and supported by the relevant foreign-exchange declaration, commercial invoice, contract, and customs or service-delivery evidence.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), acting through its Board of Directors and foreign exchange office, with supervisory support from the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia and administrative enforcement coordination with the Ministerio de Hacienda y Crédito Público and the DIAN.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic-money institutions such as Wise B2B are rated Medium for ancillary disbursements, contractor payments, and low-value FX conversion, but Low as a substitute for the local Colombian banking and foreign-exchange system. They are not authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries under Colombian law and cannot satisfy the statutory channeling of export proceeds, the registration of foreign investment, or the filing of currency declarations with Banco de la República. Wise B2B is therefore legally usable only for non-statutory treasury flows or as a payment convenience layer, provided the Colombian S.A.S. maintains its substantive receipts, capital injection, tax payments, and dividend repatriation through a licensed local bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign capital into the Colombian S.A.S. should be routed exclusively by SWIFT MT103 from the direct non-resident investor’s bank to the S.A.S. operating account at an authorized Colombian bank. The beneficiary field must show the S.A.S. legal name and NIT, and the payment purpose in field 70 should state foreign direct investment for subscription of shares. The receiving bank will prepare the inward foreign-investment declaration with Banco de la República, convert the USD to COP where appropriate, and verify the identity of the remitting investor against the registered share registry. The company must support the wire with notarized minutes of capital subscription, a copy of the investor’s constitutional documents, tax residency proof, ultimate beneficial owner information, and the source of funds. Unregulated digital EMI transfers must not be used for capital injection because they cannot produce the regulated FX declaration required for lawful future dividend remittance or capital repatriation.Payments to foreign animation subcontractors, rendering farms, licensing agents, cloud providers, voice talent, and technology vendors should be effected through SWIFT MT103 from the local Colombian bank after the corresponding foreign-exchange declaration for services has been prepared. MT202 may be used for correspondent interbank cover where required. For physical production deliverables crossing customs, such as proprietary hardware or final media masters, documentary settlement through a commercial letter of credit can be used, with the bank requiring invoices, airway bills or bills of lading, certificates of inspection, and evidence of export/import compliance. For pure digital services, the commercial letter of credit is inappropriate; the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 supported by a service contract, invoice, statement of work, and proof of service delivery. The treasury function must map every outbound payment to a declared basis such as service import, royalty remittance, capital account transfer, or repayment of registered foreign debt.Outright COP/USD deliverable forward contracts executed with a Banco de la República authorized commercial bank, provided there is a documented underlying commercial, investment, or trade exposure.Non-deliverable forward contracts (NDFs) on COP/USD entered into with an authorized local bank or an eligible international bank, used when COP settlement is operationally inconvenient or forward liquidity is unavailable in the local market.COP/USD cross-currency swaps covering long-term USD-denominated capital structures or material foreign cash-flow mismatches, executed through regulated financial institutions and, where required, declared or registered with Banco de la República.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% (national corporate income tax, inclusive of the surtax abolished for general taxpayers; note that a 3% surtax applies to certain specified taxable income amounts above COP 0 under Law 2277 of 2022, effectively raising the marginal headline rate to up to 38% for large taxpayers with taxable income exceeding COP 0 as defined)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

7.5% on non-resident recipients (dividend withholding tax). The statutory structure is: (i) corporate income tax has already been levied at the S.A.S. level; (ii) dividends paid from non-taxable or already-taxed profits to non-residents trigger an additional 7.5% withholding when paid out of 'net taxable income' that was subject to the general corporate rate (Article 245, Tax Code); (iii) for non-resident individuals and entities, if the profit has been taxed, the rate is 7.5%; if paid from tax-exempt income, the rate is 15%; but for most commercial animation earnings, the 7.5% is the effective final statutory rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline for the previous taxable year (ending 31 December) is scheduled in nine chronological groups based on the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), generally between 18 March and 12 April of the following year. Tax payment installments are due at the same deadline. No extension is available for the corporate income tax return beyond the published DIAN calendar. For taxpayers under the simplified tax regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación) a different consolidated form applies, but an S.A.S. engaged in commercial animation retains the ordinary regime unless an opt-in election was made.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory documentation for the fiscal year is required if the taxpayer: (a) conducts transactions vis-à-vis related parties abroad (including a foreign parent and any sub-licensed intangibles) and (b) meets either: gross assets exceeding COP 150,000 UVT (approximately COP 150,000 * 47,065 = COP 7,059,750,000, about USD 1.75 million as of 2025 – but verify annually), or gross income exceeding COP 100,000 UVT (approximately COP 4,706,500,000, about USD 1.16 million), or (c) in all cases the taxpayer must file an annual transfer-pricing informative return (Form 120, 'Información de Operaciones con Vinculados') if it has transactions with related parties and meets the medium taxpayer size thresholds: gross income above COP 100,000 UVT (COP 4,706.5 million) or gross assets above COP 100,000 UVT. Additionally, as a 'taxpayer without TP obligation' but with a foreign shareholder, the S.A.S. must file the Country-by-Country declaration if the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds COP 87 billion. For a smaller US-owned animation S.A.S., the TP return is unlikely to be triggered on the asset or income thresholds unless the studio ramps up quickly, but benchmarking against the local commercial rule is mandatory because non-resident parents often understate service fees; a conservative standard is to prepare a simplified comparability analysis whenever any cross-border related transaction exists.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a general Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE or Cyprus. However, the general anti-avoidance rule under Article 869-1 of the Tax Code allows DIAN to disregard legal forms lacking economic substance, and the 're-characterization of transactions with related parties' is used to reclassify payments as dividends. For the S.A.S. engaged in animation, the substance doctrine manifests in: (a) adequate payroll, physical render-farm presence, and actual artistic management in Colombian territory; (b) if the S.A.S. is a pure invoice-passer for services actually performed by non-resident contractors, DIAN will likely treat all income as taxable without the offsetting expenses, or reclassify the profit allocation to the foreign parent; (c) For claiming the benefit of the preferential 'naranja' regime or VAT export exemption, DIAN applies a formal substance test – the S.A.S. must prove technical staff physically present in Colombia and invoices remitted from Colombian infrastructure; (d) The application of DTA benefits for dividends requires substantive rather than formal beneficial ownership (treaty shopping) – if the foreign parent is an intermediate holding with no substance in its residence state, DIAN may deny treaty relief despite a pending treaty, citing the Anti-Tax Treaty Shopping rules under Law 1819 of 2016, Article 124.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False or insufficient filing

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 2 to 8 years for individuals (Article 426 Tax Code). The S.A.S. faces: (a) collection of the omitted tax; (b) penalty equal to 100% of the amount omitted (or 200% if assets are hidden); (c) daily interest; (d) a criminal fine of up to COP 1,500 million for the legal entity; (e) publication of the taxpayer's name in DIAN's insolvent-taxpayer list. If the omission exceeds COP 250,000,000 (about USD 62,600), the sanction is aggravated to 8 years.

Director personal liability: Director and legal representative are jointly and severally liable for the tax debt if they had knowledge of the under-reporting and approved the return (Article 794/795 of the Tax Code). Additionally, under the Colombian Criminal Code (Law 599/2000, Article 413), a representative who orders, authorizes, or tolerates false tax declarations faces imprisonment, loss of professional license, and travel-ban (prohibition to leave Colombia by DIAN order under Article 839-3). Civil restitution to the treasury includes the tax, penalty, and updated interest; a director may be barred from holding board positions in any Colombian company for up to 10 years. But for the typical US entrepreneur, the more immediate risk is a freeze on personal assets in Colombia and inability to obtain a Colombian residence visa.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Mis-pricing of cross-border services

Statutory sanction: If the taxpayer meets the TP documentation obligation but does not file Form 120, the penalty is COP 40,000,000 (about USD 10,000) per month of delay (suspended after 24 months). If the TP return and Local File are not prepared and submitted, DIAN may: (a) adjust the net income upward to the arm's length result; (b) apply a penalty of 10% to 200% of the difference between the reported and adjusted taxable income; (c) interest at 25%+ annual; (d) disallow the deductions and re-classify the excess payment as a dividend deemed distribution for the foreign shareholder, triggering the 7.5% dividend withholding plus penalties. If the S.A.S. has a loss but the transfer pricing adjustment eliminates the loss, the advance tax and presumptive tax clauses also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false transfer pricing certification (the sworn statement in Form 120) are personally liable for the penalty (the general penalty of 5% of underlying tax). In egregious cases where the mispricing has a criminal character (e.g., creation of a nominal foreign licensor with no actual economic activity), the director may receive a 4-year imprisonment under the fraudulent-evasion doctrine. In practice, DIAN includes personal liability statements in any formal assessment, and if the company becomes insolvent, the director receives a personal assessment.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance (tax haven misuse)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 869-1 of the Tax Code, the transaction is recharacterized, all tax benefits granted to the S.A.S. are reversed, and the S.A.S. is classified as a 'sociedad de fachada' if it lacks real operations. Consequences: (a) all revenues are assigned to the actual foreign operator, and Colombian tax at 35% is assessed with penalties; (b) VAT on the local digital services becomes payable if the S.A.S. is deemed not to be the actual exporter, so the statutory 19% VAT will be assessed on the full invoice; (c) the S.A.S. may be disqualified as a legitimate exporter; (d) the foreign parent could be additionally assessed under the concept of 'permanent establishment in Colombia' and subject to the 6.3% branch remittance tax; (e) administrative sanctions under Law 1121/2006 for international exchange fraud if remittances were misreported to the central bank.

Director personal liability: The director who signs the 'export service certificate' for a transaction the company does not actually perform incurs fraud liability. Additional risk arises under the anti-money-laundering framework (Law 526/2018) if the 'stripped profits' are repatriated disguised as non-trade payments; directors may face money-laundering charges (law 599/2000, Article 323) with 10 to 30 years of imprisonment. Civil liability extends to compensation for nonexistent VAT export credits, which are considered the direct patrimony of the state.
Anti-Money Laundering and International Exchange (FX) Infractions

Statutory sanction: The exchange violation carries the penalty of (a) confiscation of the foreign currency equivalent not reported; (b) a fine up to 200% of the unreported amount if the capital is unrepatriated; (c) suspension from exporting privileges for up to 5 years; additionally, the use of an unregistered and unreported Wise account can trigger the Colombian Statute of Exchange Control (Law 9 of 1991), with penalties calculated as 100% of the value of the foreign currency not surrendered when the exporter is subject to surrender obligations (the service exporter is generally exempt from mandatory surrender but must still report). Criminal AMPI risk: under the Criminal Statute (Law 526), if the unreported inflow is associated with an act of tax evasion, a director faces 8 to 15 years; for a low-risk standard animation company, the infraction usually remains administrative unless the tax evasion overlay exists.

Director personal liability: Banco de la República and DIAN can jointly impose civil sanctions on the legal representative (the natural person signing the export declaration). If the foreign exchange remains abroad without declaration, the director becomes the addressee of the penalty, and a travel ban can be issued by DIAN (Article 839-3 of the Tax Code has no exception for non-resident directors). If the director is a non-resident and a Colombian S.A.S. is the only asset, the travel ban effectively traps the director if he or she enters Colombia for a client meeting.
Withholding Tax Avoidance on Repatriation

Statutory sanction: DIAN recharacterization triggers: (a) the assessed tax of 7.5% or 33% on the unreported gross; (b) penalty of 50% of the under-withheld tax; (c) interest; (d) the payment is disallowed as a deductible expense and taxable income is increased accordingly; (e) if the foreign shareholder is a tax evader in its home country, DIAN may also apply Article 588 of the Tax Code and publish the violation; criminal frame: if the total omitted withholding exceeds 280,000 UVT, the legal representative is exposed to 2–6 years for 'aprovechamiento de recursos' (fraudulent use of withholding characters).

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unpaid withholding (Article 403 of the Tax Code) as 'agente de retención es responsable personalmente'. If the company is dissolved, the director's liability extends to the withholding obligation as a personal debt. The director might also be penalized under Law 1065/2006 (electronic transfer of funds to a foreign recipient without justification) with a fine equal to 50% of the transfer, plus immigration restrictions on leaving Colombia until the fine is paid.
Permanent Establishment of the Foreign Shareholder (Migration to a Deemed Colombian Taxpayer)

Statutory sanction: If the foreign parent is deemed to have a PE, the Colombian S.A.S. becomes the PE's permanent establishment and must pay (a) 35% corporate income tax on the profits attributable to the PE; (b) the 7.5% dividend withholding on the deemed remittance to the head office (the '0.5x remittance tax' under Decree 1070/2016 is not directly applicable, but the foreign parent as a treaty-free US entity will suffer a 35% headline claim; for UK/French parents, the DTA PE threshold under Article 5 is higher but still relevant); (c) VAT on the contracts is 19% if the subsidiary is not an exporter; (d) penalties for the unregistered PE: 30% of the assessed tax and 100% of the evaded VAT.

Director personal liability: The local director of the S.A.S. may be liable as 'representante del establecimiento permanente' and thus owes the same tax, penalty, and interest; in a severe PE fraud case, criminal prosecution for 'omisión de agente retenedor' arises. In practice, given the Colombian S.A.S. is typically the contracting entity with independent authority, the PE risk is moderate, but a failure to maintain separate management minutes and board resolutions proves the dependency.

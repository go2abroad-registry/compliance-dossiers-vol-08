Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance (Taxisnet portal at taxisnet.mof.gov.cy); customs and excise administration by the Customs & Excise Department through the ISEM/AES electronic systems

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of a Cyprus Private Limited Company (HE-prefixed registration number), the Registrar of Companies issues a statutory company registration number that automatically functions as the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) for income tax purposes. For VAT, the entity must file Form TFR 101 (Application for Registration in the VAT Registry) with the competent District VAT Office of the Tax Department within 30 days of becoming liable, i.e., when taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600 in any consecutive 12-month period or when registration is otherwise mandatory (intra-Community acquisitions exceeding EUR 10,251.61, distance sales exceeding EUR 10,000); the VAT registration number is issued as 'CY' plus 9 digits. For customs bonded operations, the entity must obtain an EORI number (CY plus 10 digits) from the Customs & Excise Department and, for excise bonded warehousing of wine and spirits, apply for an excise warehouse keeper authorization under Law 91(I)/2004, including lodging a warehouse guarantee and using the EMCS for controlled movements. No annual re-registration is required; the TIN remains valid until deregistration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Cyprus tax-resident companies, being managed and controlled in Cyprus, are subject to ordinary corporation tax at 12.5% on worldwide income; non-resident entities are taxed only on Cyprus-source income; bonded warehouse trade is not located in a tax-exempt free zone and remains fully within the standard entity-level corporate tax regime)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box entity classification election exists under Cyprus law. Elective regimes available include: (i) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on contributions of new equity after 1 January 2015, using the relevant CEDI reference rate plus margin, capped at 80% of taxable income before NID; (ii) IP Box election under the modified nexus approach granting an 80% deduction on qualifying gross profits from intangible assets; (iii) Group tax-relief election for current-year tax losses, capped at 75% of the claimant's taxable income; (iv) deemed dividend distribution election (IDEA regime) for Cyprus-accumulated profits not distributed within two years; (v) VAT group registration election and the special flat-rate scheme for small enterprises; hybrid mismatch outcomes are neutralized mandatorily under EU ATAD implementation (Law 136(I)/2016) with no elective override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 taxable turnover in any consecutive 12-month period for supplies of goods and services; EUR 10,251.61 for intra-Community acquisitions of goods; EUR 10,000 for distance sales of goods into Cyprus; EUR 150 low-value consignment threshold; no registration threshold applies to reverse-charge services received from abroad

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to the exportation of goods to destinations outside the EU under Article 56 of the VAT Law, including supplies from an excise/ bonded warehouse where the goods physically depart the EU within 90 days of dispatch. The supplier must hold contemporaneous evidence of physical removal, transfer of title and risk, and receipt of payment. Supplies of services directly connected with the exported goods (e.g., customs brokerage, warehousing, and transport of the exported goods) may be zero-rated under the same documentation threshold. For intra-EU dispatches from a Cyprus bonded warehouse to a VAT-registered customer in another Member State, zero-rating is conditioned on retention of the customer's valid VAT registration number and inclusion in the VIES EC Sales List; failure to obtain such evidence converts the supply to a taxable intra-EU acquisition at 19%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / SAD) or AES/ISEM export-acceptance record evidencing departure from the EU
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note proving physical movement to destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and sale contract stating Incoterms and transfer of risk
  • Bonded warehouse removal records and EMCS AAD evidencing tax-suspended movement from the excise warehouse
  • Customer's VAT registration certificate or EORI number confirmation, and proof of the 90-day removal rule

Cyprus currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate for VAT. Mandatory electronic reporting applies to: (i) Intrastat declarations via the Customs ISEM system; (ii) VIES recapitulative statements (Form 930) filed electronically on a monthly/quarterly basis; (iii) B2G e-invoicing in PEPPOL/CIUS format under EU Directive 2014/55/EU. A phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime is anticipated under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) initiative but is not yet in force. Bonded warehouse operations for wines and spirits additionally require authorized excise warehouse status with movement control through the EMCS (Excise Movement and Control System) and system-generated Administrative Accompanying Documents (AAD) for tax-suspended transfers.

The Tax Department may raise protective or extended assessments within 6 years of the end of the relevant VAT period, and without time limit in cases of fraudulent evasion or gross negligence. If export evidence is deficient or the 90-day removal rule is breached, the zero-rating is clawed back and output VAT is assessed at 19% on the full invoice value, plus statutory interest at 8.5% per annum and a late-payment penalty of 1% per month (capped at 10% of the assessed tax), together with fixed administrative penalties (EUR 250 for failure to register; EUR 50 per month per missing or late VAT return). Fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract additional tax of up to 100% of the tax evaded, criminal fines up to EUR 25,000 on indictment and/or imprisonment up to 5 years under sections 60-64 of the VAT Law, with director and officer personal liability for continued default after assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU Treaty provisions (Articles 63-65 TFEU). No exchange controls or restrictions on profit remittance, provided corporate tax compliance is settled and audited financials are filed. Dividends may be repatriated without withholding tax for eligible EU parent companies or under the Cyprus participation exemption; non-resident shareholders may receive dividends without special defence contribution if not Cyprus tax residents. All remittances must comply with AML/CFT reporting thresholds and EU restrictive measures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion obligation exists under Cypriot law. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained in external or local accounts, freely converted into EUR, as Cyprus is a eurozone member and applies the EU free movement of capital regime. Reporting to the Central Bank of Cyprus may be required under balance-of-payments reporting for amounts exceeding certain statistical thresholds (typically EUR 12,500 per transaction), but there is no mandatory conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Cyprus-licensed EMIs under the Payment Services Law (L. 37(I)/2018) can provide restricted multi-currency IBANs and payment services, but they are not credit institutions and cannot grant trade credit or issue letters of credit. For bonded warehouse trade with customs liabilities, traditional correspondent banking remains more reliable. Offshore EMIs (non-EU) face severe debit/credit constraints under MiCA and are generally not accepted for customs-bonded operations. Legal constraint: physical establishment in Cyprus required for EMI license; remote-only outside EU entities likely Low viability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when the aggregate value of all controlled transactions with related parties exceeds EUR 750,000 in a tax year. Master File is required when the multinational group has consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million (aligned with BEPS Action 13). CbCR filing threshold follows the EUR 750 million group revenue test.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has no general ESR code comparable to UAE/offshore jurisdictions, but the Tax Department applies the OECD substance concept through residence, management and control tests. For a bonded wine/spirits distributor, real substance is evidenced by a licensed bonded warehouse in Cyprus, locally resident warehouse and compliance officers, storage risk and inventory title in Cyprus, board decisions at Cyprus premises, and invoicing/warehouse records maintained in the island. Artificial, 'shell-like' arrangements are challengeable under the EU ATAD GAAR and may be denied corporate residence or treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Law Cap. 118(I)/2002)

Statutory sanction: Additional tax equal to triple the tax evaded, fixed penalties up to EUR 5,000, plus interest; criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to three years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced if a director signed a knowingly false return; directors can be ordered to compensate the Revenue from personal assets and subjected to travel bans and a restriction on leaving Cyprus until the tax liability is secured.
Excise and Customs Bonded Warehouse Violation

Statutory sanction: Seizure and condemnation of goods, cancellation or suspension of the Excise Warehouse License, administrative penalty of up to five times the excise duty evaded, and criminal indictment for smuggling.

Director personal liability: Directors and warehouse managers with direct knowledge are personally named in customs penalty and forfeiture proceedings; court orders may freeze personal bank accounts and prohibit foreign travel until duty and penalties are settled.
Money Laundering / AML Controls (L.188(I)/2007)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines on legal persons up to 10% of annual turnover; profit confiscation; deregistration of the company from the Cyprus Registry; criminal prosecution of compliance officers.

Director personal liability: AML law imposes personal criminal liability on executive directors and the nominated compliance officer for deliberate or reckless failures, with imprisonment up to five years and director disqualification under the Cyprus Companies Law.
Transfer Pricing / Associated Enterprise Adjustment

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profit to arm's-length levels, payment of under-declared corporation tax, 5% annual interest, and a 25% penalty on the underpaid tax where the distortion is wilful.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate the mispricing can be co-assessed if the company is insolvent; the Tax Department may initiate attachment of director assets and seek a district court travel warrant to prevent dissipation.
Artificial Arrangement / GAAR Abuse (EU ATAD, Income Tax Law s.33A)

Statutory sanction: The arrangement is disregarded and recharacterised under the Cyprus GAAR; tax assessments are recalculated on the underlying economic reality, treaty benefits are denied, and any refunds or special deductions are clawed back.

Director personal liability: Where the shell company is used to defraud creditors or customs, courts may disregard separate personality and hold directors jointly and severally liable for all unremitted excise duty, taxes, and third-party debts.

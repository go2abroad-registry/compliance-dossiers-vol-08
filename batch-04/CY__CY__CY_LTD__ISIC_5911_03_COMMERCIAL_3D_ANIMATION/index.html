Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department (Tmima Forologias), Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; official e-portal 'Ariadne' (https://ariadne.mof.gov.cy) for corporate, VAT and VIES filings; Customs and Excise Department operates the THESEUS electronic customs clearance system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity commencing a Cyprus trade must: (1) incorporate or register a branch with the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DRCOR) to obtain a Company/HE number that serves as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN); (2) file Form VAT 101 (T.D. 101) with the Tax Department before the first taxable supply under Article 26 of the VAT Law N.94(I)/2000; (3) appoint a tax representative with a Cyprus address if the foreign entity is not established in Cyprus and has no local registered office; (4) if hiring staff, register with the Social Insurance Services, GESY (General Healthcare System) and Company of Paying Agents for PAYE/defence contributions. Non-established remote suppliers of digital or animation services must register for Cyprus VAT immediately upon their first supply without any turnover threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Cyprus Private Limited Company is a separate tax-resident person subject to 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income, with specific exceptions: foreign permanent establishment profits (subject to at least 10% foreign tax) are exempt, and dividend income is 95% exempt subject to anti-hybrid conditions. The entity is not eligible for pass-through treatment under Cyprus domestic law; separately, a deemed-distribution tax (Special Defence Contribution, 17%) may apply to close company profits unless distributed.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (a) Group relief under Article 24 of the Income Tax Law for surrender of losses within a 75% group; (b) IP Box election for 'qualifying intangible assets' achieving an effective 8% tax rate; (c) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity contributions; (d) VAT grouping election under Article 45 of the VAT Law for closely-linked Cyprus-established entities; (e) election to exempt the Cyprus branch of a foreign company under tax treaties, subject to double tax relief mechanisms; (f) hybrid mismatch adjustments and mandatory disclosure rules apply under ATAD, limiting treaty-shopping elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for Cyprus-established suppliers; EUR 0 threshold for non-established foreign suppliers (mandatory registration from the first supply); EUR 35,000 threshold for intra-EU distance selling; EUR 10,000 cross-border B2C OSS threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Article 42 of the Cyprus VAT Law to: (1) export of goods supplied to a non-EU buyer where the goods physically leave the EU within 90 days of supply; (2) intra-EU B2B supplies under Article 44 where the supplier holds a valid customer VIES VAT identification number and the goods are transported to another Member State; (3) B2B services supplied to a non-EU recipient, including 3D animation, CGI visual effects production and licensing of related intellectual property rights, where the place of supply is the customer's business establishment outside the EU (reverse-charge/outside-scope treatment); (4) internationally-related services under Article 48 (freight, handling, intermediary services ancillary to export). The supplier must retain documentary evidence evidencing the non-EU status of the recipient and the offshore delivery of the service or export movement of goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document or THESEUS electronic customs declaration)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR (proof of physical transport leaving the EU)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Swift Wire or Wise B2B payment confirmation evidencing FX repatriation to the Cyprus company
  • Commercial invoice cross-referenced to the customs value, VAT return and GL ledger
  • Valid customer VIES VAT ID (intra-EU only) or certificate/statement that the non-EU customer is not established in the EU
  • Contract, purchase order or work order specifying the exported goods or offshore animation/VFX service deliverable
  • Evidence of dispatch within 90 days (packing list, forwarder certificate, insurance documents)

Cyprus mandates structured electronic invoicing only for business-to-government (B2G) invoices submitted through the PEPPOL-AU network using the European EN 16931 standard; there is currently no national mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance stamping or API-based tax-clearance database for B2B supplies. However, all VAT registrants must file VAT returns (Form VAT 101/102) electronically via the Ariadne portal, file recapitulative statements (VIES/ESFA) for intra-EU trade, retain digital books and records for at least 10 years, and comply with the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) reporting rules as they transpose, expected to introduce mandatory e-invoicing after 2030.

The Cyprus Tax Department may issue an assessment within 6 years of the VAT return (extended to 11 years in fraud or intentional evasion cases; no time limit for criminal prosecution). Zero-rating will be clawed back retroactively where export proof, VIES identity, or inward FX evidence is deficient, with a 10% surcharge on the VAT assessed and interest at 9% per annum from the date of the original return under Article 51 of the VAT Law. Fraudulent zero-rating claims expose the exporter, officers and tax representatives to fines up to EUR 10,000 per invoice, imprisonment up to 5 years, joint and several liability along the supply chain, and publication of assessments by the Tax Department.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus adopted the euro (EUR) on 1 January 2008 and is fully integrated into the Eurosystem; the euro is freely convertible within the single-currency area and against any floating currency in unrestricted markets. The historical Exchange Control Law was dismantled upon EU accession in 2004, and the temporary 2013 crisis capital-control measures were repealed in their entirety; both current-account transactions (trade in services, royalties, dividends, salaries) and capital-account transactions (share capital injections, shareholder loans, debt issuances, inward FDI) are lawful without Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC) prior authorization. Cyprus applies a 0% dividend withholding tax on distributions to non-residents under domestic law; profit remittance therefore encounters no fiscal hurdle, provided the company complies with Section 58 of the Companies Law Cap.113 (distribution only out of distributable profits verified by audited financial statements and a solvency statement) and with EU ATAD anti-abuse and economic-substance requirements. Residual practical hurdles are limited to: (a) CBC and EU sanctions screening under Regulation (EU) 269/2014 and the consolidated UN/EU lists; (b) banking source-of-funds verification of incoming wires under AML Law L.188(I)/2007; and (c) alignment of substance indicators (payroll, premises, management decisions, assets in Cyprus) with the EU Code of Conduct Group criteria, since a purely conduit treasury profile would attract CBC supervisory attention and potentially trigger a tax-residency recharacterization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no export-proceeds surrender timeline, no custodial deposit requirement, and no mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts. The former Exchange Control Law was abolished as a condition of EU accession (2004), and the temporary 2013 restrictive measures were lifted prospectively thereafter; exporters may retain export receipts in EUR or in any freely convertible currency onshore or offshore indefinitely. For cross-border services such as CGI/VFX production, no customs declaration applies; the exporter must invoice with correct VAT treatment (0% for qualifying B2B exports of services to EU clients with a valid VAT number, or standard Cypriot VAT at 19% where domestic rules so require), retain the underlying production contract and digital-delivery evidence, and report the inbound receipt only through the receiving bank's monthly Balance-of-Payments statistical return to the CBC. No conversion of USD, GBP, or other invoiced receipts into EUR is mandated at any stage; conversion occurs solely when the company's treasury policy so requires for operating liquidity.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), operating within the Eurosystem alongside the European Central Bank, as the national monetary and financial supervisory authority for Cyprus

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Viability is rated Medium for use as a primary treasury relationship and High as an auxiliary rail under the target payment rails (Wise_B2B, Swift_Wire). Cyprus recognizes EU/EEA payment institutions and e-money institutions under PSD2 and Directive 2009/110/EC (EMD2); Wise Europe SA (regulated by the National Bank of Belgium and passporting into Cyprus under the EU passport regime) and Airwallex (Netherlands) B.V. (regulated by De Nederlandsche Bank) may lawfully open accounts and execute payment services for a Cypriot corporate client without a separate CySEC or CBC license. Three legal constraints, however, lower the primary-relationship rating: (i) deposit protection — EU EMIs are not participants in the Cyprus Deposit Guarantee Fund, so balances above the EMI safeguarding threshold carry counterparty credit risk; (ii) substance verification — the Cyprus Tax Department and Tier-1 banks regard a domestic bank account as principal evidence of economic nexus, and an EMI-only treasury materially weakens the substance audit file; and (iii) source-of-funds opacity — inward capital injections routed through an EMI frequently fail the receiving bank's traceability standard because the EMI does not preserve the original ordering party's bank statements in the SWIFT chain. Recommended legal configuration: maintain the primary operational account at a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank; onboard Wise Business/B2B as a supplementary collection and disbursement rail with a documented treasury-liquidity matrix capping per-transaction settlement at EUR 50,000 and aggregate monthly EMI outflow at 25% of total disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for non-resident shareholder equity into the CY_LTD operational account. (1) Wire routing: a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer (or SEPA-equivalent ISO 20022 pacs.008 with a Unique End-to-end Transaction Reference UETR) from the shareholder's foreign bank to the Cyprus account. (2) Purpose coding: the sending bank must apply the ECB Balance-of-Payments purpose code E110 (Equity capital, direct investment) for share subscriptions, or E130 (Inter-company loans associated with direct investment) with a signed draft loan agreement for shareholder advances; the ordering instruction should carry BOPS CODE E110 and the board allotment resolution number in field 70 of the MT103. (3) Verification: the receiving bank requires the share-allotment return filed with the Department of Registrar of Companies, a board resolution authorizing the capital increase and account opening, and a statutory declaration by the UBO confirming source of funds. (4) Compliance: no CBC prior approval is required under the liberalized exchange-control regime, but the receiving bank must report the inflow to the CBC Balance-of-Payments statistical system within the relevant reporting month. (5) Anti-blocker measures: ensure the sending bank uses a transparent intermediary with a recognized BIC, that the ordering customer name matches the UBO register, and that the underlying corporate documents are apostilled where the shareholder is domiciled in a Hague Apostille Convention jurisdiction.The Cyprus operational account should be operated as a four-rail clearing structure: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers — the standard rail for USD, GBP, and EUR disbursements to non-EU subcontractors (US, Hong Kong, Singapore, Japan), each instruction carrying the payee's BIC and IBAN, a precise purpose code, invoice reference, and a value-date discipline aligned to contractual maturity; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) — reserved for platform or content-financing agreements above EUR 250,000 where the counterparty requires a documentary undertaking (presentation of delivery confirmation, watermarked work-product renders, and bank attestation of completion); (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer Instant (SCT Inst) — the zero-cost intra-EU rail with sub-second settlement via TARGET2, adopted for EU freelancers and EU production partners; and (iv) Wise_B2B and Airwallex aggregators — supplementary rails for recurring low-value (up to EUR 10,000) subcontractor fees with mid-market FX conversion and a two-tier reconciliation rule limiting aggregate weekly EMI outflow to 25% of corresponding SWIFT and SEPA outflows. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 — the Cyprus TP Decree (KDP 451/2022), aligned with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, requires mandatory contemporaneous Local File and Master File when the aggregate value of a taxpayer's controlled transactions in a tax year exceeds EUR 750,000; below this threshold, a concise TP Summary must still be appended to the annual return. For each controlled transaction within the scope, the taxpayer must apply the arm's length principle with a documented selection of the most appropriate method, a comparability analysis, benchmark studies, and an intercompany agreement executed by the parties; failure to produce these documents on a Cyprus Tax Department request within the statutory period converts the entire transfer pricing analysis into a penalty event.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing code; nevertheless, economic substance is a decisive legal condition of the Cyprus tax regime through four enforcements: (i) the judicial 'central management and control' test for fiscal residence — board meetings, director decision-making, and senior creative and production management physically in Cyprus; (ii) the IP Box nexus requirement — qualifying animation software and character IP must derive from Cyprus R&D activities and the nexus ratio must be evidenced by payroll, premises, and project records; (iii) the Notional Interest Deduction traceability requirement — capital must demonstrably fund active Cypriot operations; and (iv) the EU ATAD anti-abuse rules (CFC, interest limitation, and general anti-abuse) enabling reassessment of structures judged to lack genuine economic activity. For this commercial 3D animation company, substance is proven by employment contracts of animators and directors, the lease of studio facilities, the software development repository hosted in Cyprus, the invoicing and bank accounts held in Cyprus, and board minutes that record the actual commercial direction of the animation productions; a company failing these indicia risks losing its Cyprus TRC and being recharacterized as a tax-resident shell in the parent jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Income Tax Law (L.118(I)/2002) and the Tax Procedures Law (L.489(I)/2021) carries a fine of up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 3 years; the Commissioner is entitled to issue a best-of-judgment assessment recapturing the under-declared tax plus additional tax of up to 10% and late interest of 8.5% per annum; the supply of false documentation to the Tax Department also triggers the automatic EUR 750-per-document TP penalty and disgorgement of any refunds obtained.

Director personal liability: Directors who prepare, sign, or cause the submission of the false return are personally exposed to criminal prosecution and conviction; the corporate veil is pierced under the fraudulent trading provisions of Companies Law Cap. 113, imposing personal civil liability for the tax loss; courts may additionally issue travel bans prohibiting the directors from leaving Cyprus during investigation, issue worldwide freezing orders over their personal assets, and on conviction impose custodial sentences and confiscation orders in the amount of the evaded tax.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department will deny the Tax Residence Certificate and reclassify the company as non-resident, thereby exposing its profits to taxation in the jurisdiction of the ultimate shareholder and to possible exit tax in Cyprus; distributions made in violation of the statutory solvency test are unlawful under Companies Law Cap. 113 and must be restored to the company; foreign tax authorities may impose their domestic full-rate corporate tax on the global profits with penalties for the sham arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors and the de facto controlling shareholder of a sham vehicle face personal liability under the fraudulent preference and fraudulent trading doctrines of Cap. 113, requiring personal restitution to creditors and to the Tax Department; disqualification orders of up to 5 years from acting as directors of any Cypriot company; where assets are concealed to defeat the Tax Department's claims, a custodial sentence of up to 2 years may be imposed, and in extradition-sensitive cases the court may issue a travel ban to secure the directors' availability for the criminal proceedings.
Money Laundering and AML/CFT Abuse

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to EUR 1,000,000 for obliged entities found to have failed AML controls; criminal conviction for money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of up to 15 years and/or a fine of up to EUR 50,000; the Unit for Combating Money Laundering (MOKAS) may issue freezing and confiscation orders over the entire proceeds of the layered transactions, with enforcement extending to foreign bank accounts under mutual legal assistance; the company's bank signatory mandates may be suspended pending investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knew or negligently failed to identify the laundering purpose of the transactions are personally liable for civil restitution of the laundered amounts and may be prosecuted as principals; MOKAS routinely obtains travel bans against such directors pending arrest or examination, disqualification orders from serving as directors of financial and commercial institutions, and, upon conviction, confiscation of personal assets acquired with the criminal proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's Length Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Fixed administrative penalty of EUR 750 per missing or defective TP document per tax year, plus automatic TP adjustment reassessments under the 2022 TP Decree; the adjustment increases chargeable income to the arm's length level at 12.5%, with additional tax of up to 10% of the under-declared amount and late interest of 8.5% per annum; in cross-border settings the corresponding adjustment may be denied if the documents are not contemporaneous, generating double taxation for the group and secondary penalties in the parent jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly suppress the TP documentation or approve non-arm's length agreements are personally liable for the resulting penalties under Cap. 113; if the TP adjustment renders the company balance-sheet insolvent, directors are exposed to a claim for wrongful trading requiring them to contribute company assets to satisfy the tax debt; in aggravated fraudulent cases the directors may face criminal prosecution for theft of corporate opportunity and evasion of a public liability, with travel bans and disqualification orders issued to protect the revenue.

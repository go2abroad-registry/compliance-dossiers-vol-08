Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance; online filing portal: TaxisNet ('Tax for All') at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy; corporate income tax returns filed via the ARIADNE e-filing system; customs export processing administered by the Department of Customs and Excise (Τμήμα Τελωνείων) through the Cyprus Customs Single Window (EAS/Single Administrative Control System).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Cyprus Ltd: (1) obtain Certificate of Incorporation HE77 from the Registrar of Companies; (2) register for corporate income tax with the District Tax Office using Form TD2001, resulting in an 8-digit Cyprus Tax Identification Code (TIC); the entity is taxable at the 12.5% CIT rate on worldwide income if tax-resident, or on Cyprus-source income/PE profits if non-resident; (3) file Form VAT 101 (Application for VAT Registration) with the VAT Division; non-resident entities must appoint a tax representative in Cyprus under Art. 75 of the VAT Law; (4) obtain an EORI number from the Department of Customs and Excise to move goods under customs control; (5) provide passports of directors/UBOs, certificate of incorporation, memorandum/articles of association, registered office address, and bank account evidence; registrations are typically processed within 10-30 business days and tax clearance letters may be requested under airline procurement contracts.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: tax-resident Cyprus Ltd (managed-and-controlled / PoEM test) is taxed on worldwide income at 12.5% CIT; non-resident Ltd is taxed only on Cyprus-source income and profits attributable to a Cyprus permanent establishment; pass-through treatment is not available to Cyprus companies; no tax-exempt zone applies to ISIC 5621 operations. — For a foreign-owned Cyprus Ltd: (1) obtain Certificate of Incorporation HE77 from the Registrar of Companies; (2) register for corporate income tax with the District Tax Office using Form TD2001, resulting in an 8-digit Cyprus Tax Identification Code (TIC); the entity is taxable at the 12.5% CIT rate on worldwide income if tax-resident, or on Cyprus-source income/PE profits if non-resident; (3) file Form VAT 101 (Application for VAT Registration) with the VAT Division; non-resident entities must appoint a tax representative in Cyprus under Art. 75 of the VAT Law; (4) obtain an EORI number from the Department of Customs and Excise to move goods under customs control; (5) provide passports of directors/UBOs, certificate of incorporation, memorandum/articles of association, registered office address, and bank account evidence; registrations are typically processed within 10-30 business days and tax clearance letters may be requested under airline procurement contracts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: tax-resident Cyprus Ltd (managed-and-controlled / PoEM test) is taxed on worldwide income at 12.5% CIT; non-resident Ltd is taxed only on Cyprus-source income and profits attributable to a Cyprus permanent establishment; pass-through treatment is not available to Cyprus companies; no tax-exempt zone applies to ISIC 5621 operations.

Available election forms

Cyprus has no US-style check-the-box election; legal form determines tax classification. Available elections: (i) group loss relief election under Art. 26 of the Income Tax Law; (ii) VAT group registration election under Art. 39 of the VAT Law; (iii) intra-Community VAT identification for cross-border EU supplies; (iv) cash-accounting basis for small taxpayers where turnover allows; (v) unilateral foreign tax credit election under Art. 36 ITA; (vi) election between IFRS and Cyprus GAAP for the first tax accounts; (vii) tonnage tax and IP-Box elections exist in Cyprus law but are not applicable to inflight catering (ISIC 5621); (viii) special customs warehousing and excise-suspension arrangements may be elected at the airport bonded kitchen with Customs approval, deferring duty/VAT until goods are uplifted for export.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 of taxable turnover in the preceding or current 12-month period (Art. 30, Cyprus VAT Law); EUR 10,000 for intra-EU distance sales; non-resident suppliers physically supplying goods from Cyprus airside facilities do not benefit from threshold exemption and must register from the first taxable supply because inflight catering invoices typically exceed the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero percent VAT applies where the Cyprus caterer supplies inflight meals as provisions of aircraft: (i) exports of goods dispatched outside the EU, zero-rated under Art. 65 of the VAT Law once exportation is evidenced by a Customs export declaration; (ii) supplies of goods for fuelling and provisioning of aircraft operated by airlines on international flights, zero-rated under Art. 66(1) of the VAT Law (including meals and beverage carts laden onto departing aircraft via ramp delivery); (iii) intra-EU B2B supplies to airlines or catering hubs in other member states, zero-rated with a valid EU VAT number, VIES listing and transport documentation; (iv) supplies from a bonded/customs-warehouse kitchen into an aircraft store are deemed exported and zero-rated on exiting the warehouse regime. Zero-rating is conditional on the caterer obtaining conclusive proof of exportation within three months of the supply; failure to obtain such evidence recharacterises the supply as domestic at 19% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / EAS lodged in the Cyprus Customs Single Window)
  • Commercial Invoice and Inflight Catering Manifest / Commissary Order
  • Air Waybill / Master Air Waybill with Flight Load Sheet for ramp-delivered meals
  • Carrier's certificate of loading or Customs-endorsed proof that the goods departed the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation evidencing settlement from the foreign airline)
  • Aircraft provisioning certificate where zero-rating is claimed under Art. 66 of the VAT Law
  • Customs warehouse / airside bonded movement records where meals are held under excise or customs suspension

Cyprus does not operate real-time B2B fiscal clearance or digital stamping as of 2025. Mandatory e-invoicing applies to business-to-government (B2G) supplies under the PE-Billing framework implementing EU Directive 2014/55/EU (Cyprus Law N.51(I)/2017), using Peppol BIS v3 / UBL with Cyprus CIUS extensions. Newly registered taxpayers must maintain electronic books using tax-approved software and integrate Electronic Sales Devices (ESD) for declared data transmission to the Tax Department. Intra-Community zero-rated supplies require electronic VIES recapitulative statements. Customs export declarations must be lodged electronically via the Cyprus Customs Single Window (EAS) and cleared before aircraft loading. Cyprus is preparing for EU ViDA digital reporting rules, which will mandate structured e-invoicing for cross-border intra-EU transactions within the 2030 rollout.

Zero-rating claims are subject to audits by the Cyprus Tax Department, post-clearance integrity checks by the Department of Customs and Excise, and anti-money-laundering reviews against false or circular export documentation. A false export zero-rating claim constitutes VAT fraud under Art. 123 of the VAT Law N.95(I)/2000, attracting penalties of up to twice the tax evaded with minimum per-default fines, statutory late-payment interest, default assessments, confiscation orders, and criminal custodial penalties on indictment. Assessment windows of 5 years (extended to 10 years for fraudulent conduct) permit retrospective clawback of zero-rating, disallowance of input VAT, and surcharges. Directors of the Ltd may be held personally liable for reckless attestations. Where export proceeds are not repatriated or bank evidence of payment is absent, the Tax Department may recharacterise the export as a domestic supply and assess 19% VAT on the full invoice value; Cyprus abolished exchange controls in 2015, so the 180-day benchmark reflects standard export-proceeds evidence integrity and prudential airline credit terms rather than a statutory repatriation requirement.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Cyprus is a Eurozone member state (adopted the euro on 1 January 2008) and therefore applies Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, guaranteeing full freedom of movement of capital. There are no exchange-control restrictions on either current-account transactions (inflight catering imports of food ingredients, packaging, kitchen equipment, aviation services, royalties, salaries, insurance premiums) or capital-account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, dividend distributions, license fees, liquidation proceeds, profit repatriation). The euro is unconditionally convertible into all major currencies through the domestic interbank market and through correspondent banks without Central Bank approval or conversion-license requirements. Profit and dividend remittance by a non-resident shareholder of CY_LTD is permitted without prior Central Bank authorization; the only documentary prerequisites are (i) a board resolution approving the distribution, (ii) audited IFRS financial statements evidencing legally distributable reserves under the Cyprus Companies Law Cap.113, (iii) the corporate tax residency certificate (Form T.D. 121) of CY_LTD, and (iv) confirmation of applicable dividend withholding tax treatment under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (where the parent is an EEA resident, withholding is zero). The only frictions to convertibility are (a) mandatory EU/UN sanctions screening and, for US Dollar settlement, OFAC screening by correspondent banks; and (b) post-transaction statistical reporting by the commercial bank to the Central Bank of Cyprus under the ITRS using BPM6 classification codes. No capital-account approval, investment permit, or conversion quota applies to a Cyprus private limited company engaged in ISIC 5621 airport inflight catering.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Cyprus abolished its Exchange Control Law in 1994 and has never reintroduced export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratios, repatriation deadlines, or designated-account requirements. All proceeds from exported catering services (inflight meal uplift and ramp-side delivery to foreign air carriers at Larnaca Airport LCA and Paphos Airport PFO) may be freely retained in EUR or any foreign currency in a Cyprus-domiciled multi-currency account at the company's Tier-1 bank, or converted to EUR at commercial interbank rates without Central Bank approval. The only obligations associated with export receipts are (i) correct ITRS inflow classification by the receiving bank (typically BPM6 code USD/SE for services), (ii) retention of SWIFT MT103/SEPA payment confirmations and customs/intrastat documentation for six years under the Cyprus Income Tax Law N.118(I)/2002 audit and anti-avoidance provisions, and (iii) ordinary invoicing and VAT accounting under the Cyprus VAT Law N.95(I)/2000 for the sale of meals and services to airlines (B2B reverse-charge treatment applies for catering supplies). There is no statutory timeline for surrender, no minimum conversion percentage, and no Central Bank of Cyprus monitoring of the commercial disposition of exported proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating within the European System of Central Banks and the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM) of the European Central Bank. The Central Bank of Cyprus is the competent licensing and prudential supervisor for all Cyprus-incorporated credit institutions, including Bank of Cyprus, Hellenic Bank, Eurobank Cyprus, Alpha Bank Cyprus and AstroBank, and receives mandatory International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) statistical returns from resident banks for balance-of-payments compilation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury settlement rail; Medium as a segregated auxiliary operating float. Legal framework: EU PSD2 permits Cyprus corporate entities to open payment accounts with licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, Modulr and PPS, passporting e-money licenses into Cyprus from other EEA member states; such EMIs are not credit institutions licensed by the Central Bank of Cyprus and may not provide capital escrow accounts, documentary letters of credit, bank guarantees, or full correspondent-banking treasury services. Operational constraints: (i) inbound 'Aviation Contract Wire' remittances from foreign airlines are commonly routed through the EMI's own settlement bank and are subject to enhanced counterparty vetting, creating operational friction and possible rejection when the remitter (a non-EEA air carrier) sees an EMI IBAN; (ii) the 'Monthly ACH' settlement schedule (in EU terms SEPA Direct Debit Core) functions smoothly from an EMI, but the underlying commercial supply contract and trade finance arrangements require a full-bank operating account; (iii) high-velocity flows between an EMI wallet and a Cyprus Tier-1 bank, without a documented service contract, are treated by Cyprus AML compliance as a suspicious-flow trigger; and (iv) group-level CySEC-licensed affiliates (where applicable) face additional de-risking because compliance departments disfavor concentration of group treasury in single e-money wallets. The prescribed design is therefore: use a fintech EMI exclusively for corporate card expenditure and deniable petty-cash kitchen purchases, never for airline receivables, capital injections, or commercial LC settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For EU/EEA shareholders, inbound equity or loan capital is routed exclusively via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) to the CY_LTD EUR IBAN at the Tier-1 operating bank; the ISO 20022 Remittance Information field must state: 'Equity Contribution under Shareholders Agreement dated [DD/MM/YYYY] of CY_LTD' and the debtor bank should classify the transfer as a capital transaction. For non-EEA shareholders, the mandatory rail is SWIFT MT103 with gpi tracking; the SWIFT sender field must contain the exact legal name of the shareholder, and Field 70 (Remittance Information) must read 'Share Subscription / Capital Injection under Board Resolution dated [DD/MM/YYYY]'. The receiving Central Bank of Cyprus ITRS classification will be BPM6 code M0A3 (direct investment / equity capital); if the injection is structured as a shareholder loan, the BPM6 code becomes M0A4 (direct investment / other capital) and a signed loan agreement (with arm's-length interest at EURIBOR 3M plus 0.5-2.0%) must be lodged with the bank in advance. Verification protocol: (1) Board Resolution of CY_LTD authorizing the share issue or loan acceptance; (2) updated register of members and share certificates; (3) evidence of remittance from the shareholder's banking account; (4) confirmation of the economic purpose, namely pre-operating fit-out and equipment purchase for the ISIC 5621 airport bonded kitchen; (5) the subscribed capital is released into the Capital Escrow Account and made available T+0 or T+1 upon matching of the inbound SWIFT/SEPA message. Prohibited practices: fragmented small-amount deposits, mixing shareholder funds with trade receipts, and third-party (non-shareholder) remitters; all such patterns trigger additional AMLs inquiry and delay treasury availability by 5-10 business days.Outbound disbursements from CY_LTD are executed exclusively from the operational EUR account using the following clearing rails: (i) EU/EEA suppliers of raw food, spices, airline meal packaging, disposable cutlery, kitchen consumables, and logistic subcontractors settle by SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) with the ISO 20022 purpose code PURC or TRAD; orders initiated before 14:30 Cyprus time settle on T+0 via TARGET2. (ii) Non-EEA suppliers and international freight forwarders settle by SWIFT MT103; all payments above EUR 25,000 equivalent require the underlying commercial invoice and, where goods are imported under bond, the customs declaration; USD-denominated instructions are subject to OFAC screening at the US correspondent bank, therefore the recipient's legal name and address must exactly match the supplier's banking records. (iii) High-value, high-risk procurement of capital equipment (industrial ovens, blast chillers, refrigerated trolleys, dishwashing lines) is executed through a Documentary Letter of Credit (MT700 / MT707) under UCP600 issued through the Tier-1 bank's Trade Finance unit; the documentary set includes the signed purchase contract, proforma invoice, bill of lading or air waybill, packing list, and certificate of conformity with EU food-safety standards (CE/FDA where applicable). (iv) The 'Monthly ACH' target rail is mapped to the European SEPA Direct Debit Core (SDD) scheme and to monthly SWIFT MT103 batches for airlines' finance departments; when contracting IATA-member air carriers, the dispatch of monthly meal invoices through the IATA Cargo Account Settlement Systems (CASS) or the carrier's proprietary billing portal is permitted, but collections are settled onto the CY_LTD operating account through the Tier-1 bank and not through the EMI wallet. (v) Airport concession fees, ramp lease charges, electricity, water, and waste-disposal invoices from Hermes Airports Ltd and its service companies are paid via SEPA Direct Debit Mandates or one-off SCT payments, with the concession contract number encoded in the remittance information. (vi) Monthly salary payments and Cyprus Social Insurance Fund contributions (GESY and social insurance) are made via bank-initiated batch standing orders in SEPA XML pain.008 format. Every outbound wire must carry a legible purpose description; invoices in a foreign currency are converted at the bank's interbank reference rate plus a pre-agreed margin of 0.2-0.7% for the catering industry, unless covered by an FX forward contract, and the bank requires that the designated treasury signatory pre-approves every single payment above EUR 10,000 in the host-to-host channel.FX Outright Forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF, EUR/TRY): permitted for maturities between 1 week and 24 months, executed under an ISDA 2002 (Cyprus) agreement with the relationship bank; the bank requires documentation of the underlying operational exposure through a 12-month forecasting model of airline contract-wire inflows; no Central Bank approval is required, but each trade is reportable to an EU trade repository under EMIR Refit unless intra-group exemption applies.FX Swaps (EUR against USD/GBP/CHF): permitted for temporary liquidity transformation around the monthly airline invoicing cycle (e.g., receiving USD receivables and swapping to EUR for payroll and supplier payments) with maturities up to 12 months; physically settled FX swaps are outside the EMIR clearing obligation but remain subject to transaction-level reporting; a signed ISDA/CSA and a dedicated treasury credit line with the Tier-1 bank are mandatory prerequisites.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS): permitted where a shareholder loan or aircraft-ancillary financing is denominated in USD or GBP and CY_LTD's functional currency is EUR; maturity aligned to the underlying loan amortization schedule under an ISDA 2002 (Cyprus) master agreement; subject to both the internal credit committee limit and the standard EMIR reporting/trade repository obligations.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs): permissible only for exotic and non-convertible currencies (e.g., EUR/TRY, EUR/EGP, EUR/NAD) through the Cyprus bank's global markets desk, which screens back-to-back with its London or New York branch; NDFs are not available for Eurozone currencies and are not recommended for the predominant USD/EUR risk of the catering business; physical delivery forwards are preferred for all fully convertible pairs.FX Options (OTC call/put on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP): permitted when purchased (premium paid upfront) from the Tier-1 bank's options desk; selling or writing options by CY_LTD requires an enhanced internal treasury mandate and documented end-user hedging justification under EMIR; conservative practice restricts option usage to hedging of confirmed airline catering contract receivables.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury settlement rail; Medium as a segregated auxiliary operating float. Legal framework: EU PSD2 permits Cyprus corporate entities to open payment accounts with licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, Modulr and PPS, passporting e-money licenses into Cyprus from other EEA member states; such EMIs are not credit institutions licensed by the Central Bank of Cyprus and may not provide capital escrow accounts, documentary letters of credit, bank guarantees, or full correspondent-banking treasury services. Operational constraints: (i) inbound 'Aviation Contract Wire' remittances from foreign airlines are commonly routed through the EMI's own settlement bank and are subject to enhanced counterparty vetting, creating operational friction and possible rejection when the remitter (a non-EEA air carrier) sees an EMI IBAN; (ii) the 'Monthly ACH' settlement schedule (in EU terms SEPA Direct Debit Core) functions smoothly from an EMI, but the underlying commercial supply contract and trade finance arrangements require a full-bank operating account; (iii) high-velocity flows between an EMI wallet and a Cyprus Tier-1 bank, without a documented service contract, are treated by Cyprus AML compliance as a suspicious-flow trigger; and (iv) group-level CySEC-licensed affiliates (where applicable) face additional de-risking because compliance departments disfavor concentration of group treasury in single e-money wallets. The prescribed design is therefore: use a fintech EMI exclusively for corporate card expenditure and deniable petty-cash kitchen purchases, never for airline receivables, capital injections, or commercial LC settlements.

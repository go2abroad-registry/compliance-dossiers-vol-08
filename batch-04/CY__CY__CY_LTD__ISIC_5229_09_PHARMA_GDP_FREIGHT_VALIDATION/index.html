Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; a Cyprus private limited company may be incorporated with one share of nominal value EUR 1.00. The entire issued capital is typically credited as paid in cash at par; no escrow bank account, capital deposit certificate, or redemption verification is required by the Registrar for a standard trading or logistics company.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the Cyprus Fire Service confirming occupancy, fire-alarm, and evacuation compliance for warehouse and office mezzanine areas; structural suitability certificate for the cold-chain storage slab issued by an approved civil engineer and accepted by the municipal planning authority; workplace clearance from the Department of Labour Inspection (Ministry of Labour) after a risk assessment covering manual handling, forklift traffic, and temperature-controlled zones under the Safety and Health at Work Law N.89(I)/1996, as amended.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital; a Cyprus private limited company may be incorporated with one share of nominal value EUR 1.00. The entire issued capital is typically credited as paid in cash at par; no escrow bank account, capital deposit certificate, or redemption verification is required by the Registrar for a standard trading or logistics company.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance; official portal: https://taxdep.mof.gov.cy

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain a Cyprus company TIN through the Registrar of Companies upon incorporation. If the entity will make taxable supplies in Cyprus, it must apply to the Tax Department for VAT registration using the official VAT registration application form, attaching the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, directors' identity documents, and details of the Cyprus economic activity. A non-established taxable person may be required by the Commissioner to appoint a fiscal representative in Cyprus. The Tax Department verifies the application and issues the VAT identification number; the registration is effective from the application date or from the later date notified by the Tax Department.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must first obtain a Cyprus company TIN through the Registrar of Companies upon incorporation. If the entity will make taxable supplies in Cyprus, it must apply to the Tax Department for VAT registration using the official VAT registration application form, attaching the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, directors' identity documents, and details of the Cyprus economic activity. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style classification election exists; a private limited company is automatically taxed at entity level. Relevant statutory elections include group relief elections, hybrid mismatch adjustments under EU ATAD, VAT grouping elections, and OSS registration for intra-EU distance sales. Foreign-owned Cyprus companies may elect tax residence based on management and control rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 taxable annual turnover; non-established taxable persons are registrable from the first euro where Cyprus is the place of supply; a separate EUR 10,000 threshold applies to cross-border acquisitions and small-business OSS distance sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods physically exported outside the European Union, including GDP/pharma freight moved under customs export control, and to qualifying international transport and ancillary services. To claim 0% VAT, the exporter must hold the customs export declaration, transport document, sales contract, payment evidence, and, when applicable, the Inward Remittance Certificate showing repatriation of the consideration within the compliance deadline. Retained records must prove the export transaction and be available for Tax Department inspection.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX 1 / electronic customs declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank proof of export proceeds)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate is currently imposed for general B2B/B2C invoicing. Electronic filing of VAT returns is mandatory; B2G invoices must comply with the European e-invoicing framework where public procurement applies. Tax records and e-invoices must be retained for at least six years.

Export VAT zero-rating is subject to strict documentary audit and may be denied if the evidence is incomplete, contradictory, or obtained after the prescribed period. Non-genuine or fraudulent export claims are retrospectively clawed back and attract assessment at 19% VAT with statutory interest, administrative fines, and criminal sanctions including imprisonment. Directors and fiscal representatives may incur personal liability in connection with false declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full member of the Eurozone; the euro is freely convertible on both the current and capital accounts. Under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, capital movements and payments are fully liberalized. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are not subject to exchange-control approval or withholding restrictions. Dividends may be distributed freely once audited financial statements are approved, tax clearance is obtained, and corporate solvency tests are met. Foreign currency accounts are permitted within Cyprus, and no exchange-control consent is required for cross-border transfers. Residual reporting obligations are limited to statistical balance-of-payments reporting, ECB sterilization compliance, and anti-money-laundering law obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion requirement applies in Cyprus. Export proceeds may be retained in any freely convertible currency, held in domestic or foreign bank accounts, and used for operational disbursements without a mandatory conversion timeline or ratio. Invoicing and receipt currency are determined purely by contract terms. Anti-money-laundering reporting thresholds apply to cash transactions and funds originating from high-risk jurisdictions.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited to auxiliary clearing; not acceptable as primary treasury rail. Non-Cypriot electronic money institutions (EMIs) that are passport-registered under PSD2 in a Cypriot branch may open e-money accounts and facilitate cross-border collections or reimbursements. However, Cypriot banks for the core capital escrow and operational settlement accounts require a licensed relationship with a Cyprus-incorporated credit institution. An inbound transfer from an offshore EMI to a Cyprus corporate account will usually trigger enhanced due diligence and will be treated as a high-risk remittance source unless full source-of-funds evidence is provided. Feasibility rating: Medium for low-volume trade collections, Low for capital injection or treasury concentration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The authorised receiving bank will require a board resolution, updated register of members, and source-of-funds evidence from the remitter. The wire must use the company's full legal name, LEI if issued, and the stated purpose 'Share Capital Subscription to CY_LTD'. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002, Article 28)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Cyprus does not impose withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders. Special Defence Contribution (SDC) at 17% applies only to Cyprus tax-resident individuals, not to non-residents or companies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 15 months from the end of the tax year (tax year equals the calendar year). For fiscal year ending 31 December 2024, the statutory deadline is 31 March 2026. Final tax payment is due on the same date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First installment due 31 July of the tax year; second installment due 31 December of the tax year. The provisional return is submitted with the first installment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 aggregate annual related-party transaction value (administrative guidance issued by the Cyprus Tax Department; no formal de minimis threshold is codified in the Income Tax Law).

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute comparable to the UAE regime, but substance is enforced through OECD substance-over-form principles, EU ATAD rules (CFC, interest limitation), the IP Box nexus requirement, and the management-and-control residency test. A Cyprus entity must maintain a real physical office, qualified staff, local board meetings with substantive decision-making, and operational oversight of GDP freight activities to secure treaty benefits, NID, and IP Box relief. Lack of substance exposes the entity to recharacterization as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Under Article 54 of the Income Tax Law: fine up to EUR 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; civil reassessment of the evaded tax plus a penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax and interest at 1.75% per annum; publication of the taxpayer's name may follow in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are criminally liable; the corporate veil is pierced upon proof of fraud; courts may impose travel bans, asset-freezing orders, disqualification under the Companies Law (Cap. 113), and personal restitution orders.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable income at 12.5%, recovery of underpaid tax, a 5% late-payment surcharge, and interest at 1.75% per annum. Failure to produce TP documentation within 30 days of a Tax Department request triggers additional fixed penalties and an adverse adjustment presumption.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve artificial intercompany pricing face personal liability for the resulting tax shortfall under tort and insolvency principles; deliberate mispricing is Prosecutable as tax evasion with imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company and Artificial Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of tax residence to the place of effective management; denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test (MLI); application of CFC rules to the shareholders; reassessment with penalties; strike-off of the company by the Registrar of Companies.

Director personal liability: Directors face disqualification orders, personal liability for unpaid corporate taxes on insolvency (wrongful trading), and travel restrictions where they are found to have orchestrated the artificial structure.
AML, Sanctions, and Unlicensed GDP Operations

Statutory sanction: Under AML Law 188(I)/2007: fines up to EUR 500,000 for legal entities and imprisonment up to 5 years for responsible persons. Under the Pharmaceuticals Law and EU GDP guidelines: license suspension or revocation, cargo seizure and destruction, and customs penalties up to 3 times the evaded duties for false declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize transactions with sanctioned parties or knowingly operate unlicensed GDP facilities are personally criminally liable, subject to travel bans, asset freezes, extradition proceedings, and permanent disqualification from regulated logistics sectors.

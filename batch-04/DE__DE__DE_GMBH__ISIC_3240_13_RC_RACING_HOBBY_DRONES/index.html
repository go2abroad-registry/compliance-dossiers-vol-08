Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Central Federal Tax Office, and local Finanzamt (district tax office); official digital filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity undertaking taxable supplies in Germany must register for German VAT before commencing business. The corporate-level tax number (Steuernummer) is assigned by the competent local Finanzamt upon filing the VAT registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER or paper. The EU/third-country entrepreneur must also apply to BZSt for a valid German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) where intra-Community acquisitions are made or distance sales to German consumers trigger liability. The application must state the foreign entity's legal form, registered office, foreign TIN, intended German activities, estimated turnover, and place of supply analysis. A certificate of incorporation and foreign VAT registration proof are typically required. BZSt issues the VAT ID electronically within a matter of weeks, while the local Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer after entity data verification. For export operations, the entity additionally needs an EORI number (Economic Operators Registration and Identification) assigned by the General Customs Directorate (Generaldirektion der Zoll), which is applied for through the ATLAS customs portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. A GmbH is intrinsically a taxable corporation subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax), solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag), and Gewerbesteuer (trade tax). Elective mechanisms are available in specific integrated regimes: (i) VAT: the Organschaft election (Sec. 2(2) UStG) consolidates a financially, economically, and organizationally integrated GmbH into the controlling parent's VAT registration; (ii) Corporate tax: the Organschaft election (Secs. 14 et seq. KStG) attributes income, but depends on profit-and-loss transfer agreements (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag); (iii) Group interest limitation: Sec. 4j EStG read with Sec. 4k EStG implements ATAD/anti-hybrid rules limiting deduction of hybrid-friction payments; (iv) REIT/shadow GmbH no elections apply. Trade tax on going-concern basis follows the entity's ordinary place of management; no territorial scope election exists. Hybrid mismatch mechanisms are mandatory, not elective, and disallow deduction for payments that are treated as financing returns in the payee jurisdiction unless caught by the payor jurisdiction's ordinary income tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible across both current and capital accounts. Germany applies the EU capital movements liberalization and OECD Code of Liberalisation standards. There are no surrender requirements; however, cross-border payments to or from non-EU countries in excess of EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical and balance-of-payments purposes under Section 67 AWV. Profit remittances by a GmbH (dividends) are subject to standard EU withholding tax rules, but there are no foreign exchange controls blocking such transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds exists in Germany. Exporters may retain receivables in euro or foreign currency at any German or foreign bank; there is no statutory timeline for conversion back to EUR. Nevertheless, the Bundesbank reporting obligation applies to every export payment received from a non-EU country exceeding EUR 12,500, reported electronically on a monthly basis within five business days after the month of receipt.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank) acting under the umbrella of the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy; foreign exchange administration is delegated to the Bundesbank under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. An offshore EMI (electronic money institution) without an EEA passport (e.g., a UK-licensed EMI post-Brexit) cannot offer regulated payment services in Germany unless it obtains a BaFin license or partners with a licensed German/EEA institution. EU-licensed EMIs (e.g., Pliant, Qonto, N26 Inc. or Solarisbank-based platforms) are workable for daily treasury flows; however, they are not optimal for the initial capital contribution (Kapitalaufbringung) to a GmbH, where investors generally insist on a direct account held at a top-tier commercial bank for proof of capital and trustee purposes. For cross-border trade settlements, an EMI account can support multi-currency corridors, but a tier-1 correspondent bank remains the preferred rail for high-volume FPV drone component imports (value > EUR 50,000 per invoice).

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Newly incorporated GmbH (less than 12 months old), no financial statement, no previous bank account, and shareholders injecting capital within the first quarter.

Root cause: Newly incorporated GmbH (less than 12 months old), no financial statement, no previous bank account, and shareholders injecting capital within the first quarter.

Defense evidence: Handelsregister extract (dated within six weeks); notarized articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag); shareholders' list with UBO declarations; trade license (Gewerbeanmeldung); VAT registration certificate; business plan with detailed revenue runway, purchase orders, and cash-flow projections; proof of registered office (rental agreement); identity documents of all directors and UBOs; source-of-funds evidence for the capital injection (bank statements and signed comfortable rooms).
Cross-border vendor payments to high-risk destinations (e.g., China, Taiwan, Hong Kong) for electronic components and carbon-fiber frames.

Root cause: Cross-border vendor payments to high-risk destinations (e.g., China, Taiwan, Hong Kong) for electronic components and carbon-fiber frames.

Defense evidence: Commercial invoices and purchase orders; executed OEM/ODM supply agreements; Incoterms (DAT/FOB) proof; Air waybills or Bills of Lading; customs declaration (ATLAS) and import summary; product compliance certificates (CE, RoHS, EN 10204, EU GPSR) to support physical flow; confirmation of real economic activity; correspondence showing price negotiation and delivery terms; all QIB/QBP (qualified foreign bank) or BCBP upstream evidence for the supplier; pre-AML kill-switch analysis for every transaction.
Large inbound remittance from a foreign holding company / non-EU shareholder into the GmbH's 'capital' account, triggering structurally awkward 'capital in-kind' or transparent-looking contributions.

Root cause: Large inbound remittance from a foreign holding company / non-EU shareholder into the GmbH's 'capital' account, triggering structurally awkward 'capital in-kind' or transparent-looking contributions.

Defense evidence: Notarized shareholder resolution approving the capital increase; bank confirmation regarding the final contribution (Kapitalaufsichtsbestätigung); deed of contribution and proof of no hidden non-cash contribution (Sachgründungsbericht); audited financial statements of the shareholder; UBO chain documentation to the last natural person; source-of-wealth report; privacy-protected AML file including record of no politically exposed people; if the contribution is a shareholder loan, execution of a full loan agreement with arm's-length interest and amortization schedule (not deemed a hidden equity contribution).
Treasury flows that create a mismatch between a company's stated retail business (FPV drone hobbyist retail) and its bank statement pattern (high-frequency B2B exporters, large single payments to China).

Root cause: Treasury flows that create a mismatch between a company's stated retail business (FPV drone hobbyist retail) and its bank statement pattern (high-frequency B2B exporters, large single payments to China).

Defense evidence: Order-by-order product classification (ISIC 3240 vs NAICS 339930); a written treasury risk management policy; authenticity documents for the contract chain (catalog, OEM qualification documents, trademark license agreement if applicable); committed line of trade credit from the supplier; evidence that payments are aligned with shipping volumes and forecasts; deviation reports to the bank if the pattern temporarily shifts (e.g., due to regulatory approvals or product release).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound capital contributions (equity) to the GmbH, the compliant protocol is a SWIFT MT103 / SEPA credit transfer with the statutory purpose code 'ZA' (Kapitalerhöhung) or 'KA/KW' for nominal capital contributions. The remitter must clearly reference the notarized deed and the Gesellschaftsliste; the receiving bank should pre-verify the investor's identity and source of funds. For loans, use the 'DL' code and provide an executed loan agreement. If the inbound amount exceeds EUR 12,500 from non-EU countries, the GmbH must file the Bundesbank balance-of-payments report (Zahlungsbilanz Kundensystematik), usually via the bank's electronic reporting. For capital injections, a notarized confirmation and the equity register are mandatory before the funds are used for trading purchases.For intra-EU bulk purchases in the FPV drone supply chain, 'SEPA-SCT' and 'TARGET2' are the most cost-effective clearing rails with ISO 20022 pain.001; for non-EU (China / Taiwan / USD payments) the clear rail is SWIFT MT103 with the proper 'Purpose Code' (e.g., 'ITC', 'SUP' or 'DEB' as defined by the bank). When the counterparty requires a bank-independent payment commitment, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (LC) should be issued under UCP600, transmitted by SWIFT MT700/MT707. Given the relatively low operational risk profile, MT103 with full payment order and dual-layer screening is the default; LC is reserved for orders above EUR 500k. The outbound is subject to German AWV reporting when the payment exceeds EUR 12,500 to a non-EU beneficiary; the Ukraine derivate must be filed within five working days after the close of the month.Plain vanilla FX forward contracts (Devisentermingeschäft) for EUR/USD and EUR/CNY with tenors from 1 week to 12 months, on-bank balance-sheet, fully permitted and widely available.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps) for longer-dated treasury positions or to convert sales receivables denominated in USD/CNH while preserving the underlying trade exposure, permitted in all top-tier German banks.OTC currency options (European-style vanilla calls/puts) allowed under the German Securities Trading Act (WpHG) as an OTC hedge instrument for clients with adequate expertise; alternative-structured products may require a separate MiFID II test.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for non-convertible currencies (e.g., a CNH NDF if the underlying CNY is restricted under mainland China's capital controls); however, EUR/USD NDFs are neither lawful nor offered because the euro is freely convertible (no ring-fencing). Any use of NDFs must be justified by underlying illiquidity in the onshore currency and requires special dossiers from the bank's FX desk.FX corresponding to natural hedging through multi-currency sweep accounts: banks permit local book-keeping in EUR, USD, CNY, and minor currencies, and settlement via an automatic netting mechanism with with the correspondent system (no separately regulated derivative).

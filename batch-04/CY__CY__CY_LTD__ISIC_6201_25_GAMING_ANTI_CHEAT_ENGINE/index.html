Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; electronic filing via TAXISnet (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy) and the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DROC) for incorporation and HE77 registry file.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned CY_LTD acquires its statutory Tax Identification Code (TIC) via the following mandatory sequence: (1) electronic incorporation before the DROC using the Combine registration procedure, which generates the corporate registry number HE77; (2) upon incorporation, the Registrar automatically interfaces with the Tax Department's Integrated Tax System (TAXIS), which issues the nine-digit TIC, the same number subsequently activated as the VAT registration number when a VAT application is granted; (3) the appointed statutory auditor and company secretary must activate the TIC in TAXISnet by filing corporate registration data, beneficial ownership particulars, and confirmation of place of effective management; (4) if taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600, or for intra-Community acquisitions or cross-border distance sales of goods, an electronic VAT registration application must be filed; a zero threshold applies to non-resident e-services suppliers; (5) employer registration for social insurance is required, and a provisional corporate income tax declaration for the first accounting period must be filed within six months. A non-resident company without a Cyprus permanent establishment (PE) has no TIC duty; a non-resident with a Cyprus PE registers through the prescribed non-resident PE route and receives the same TIC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A Cyprus-incorporated private limited company is per se a resident corporate taxpayer subject to corporation tax at 12.5% on worldwide income, with statutory exemptions for certain non-Cyprus source dividends, gains from disposal of securities, and foreign PE income where territorial hear-and-now conditions are met. Available statutory elections that shape the tax identity include: (a) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) on new equity contributions under section 9ZD of the Income Tax Law; (b) the IP Box regime granting an 80% deduction on qualifying intangible income under section 25; (c) group relief surrender elections between Cyprus group companies; (d) tonnage tax opt-in for qualifying shipping activities; (e) deemed dividend distribution planning under Special Defence Contribution rules at 17%; (f) the OSS/MOSS election for EU VAT on B2C digital, telecommunications, and electronic services; and (g) VAT grouping election. Hybrid mismatch rules are transposed under Cyprus ATAD-implementing legislation (2019 and 2020 laws) and override any double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion mismatch involving Cyprus-resident entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for compulsory registration of Cyprus-established taxable persons; EUR 0 registration threshold for non-established foreign suppliers of digital, gaming, and ancillary services where the place of supply is Cyprus; EUR 10,000 union-wide threshold for intra-EU distance sales of goods when the OSS scheme is elected; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold to allow input VAT recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to the export of tangible goods supplied by the Cyprus LTD to customers located outside the European Union, provided the goods are physically exported from the EU within the statutory period beginning with the time of supply and the supplier retains the prescribed export evidence; the exporting supplier must be the taxable person selling the goods. For ISIC 6201 / NAICS 541511 gaming anti-cheat and integrity middleware, the commercial reality is a digital SaaS licence and continuous protection services: B2B supply of services to a non-EU customer is outside the scope of Cyprus VAT, which is the export-analogous zero treatment, evidenced by the customer's foreign VAT/business registration certificate and the service agreement. If the customer is an EU VAT-registered business, the reverse charge applies. B2C digital services to non-EU consumers are also outside Cyprus VAT unless the supplier is established in Cyprus, in which case the OSS non-union scheme applies. Where invoice proceeds are received in foreign currency, the Cypriot tax administration expects the inward remittance to be documented within 180 days of shipment as an accepted export-consideration benchmark.

  • Customs export declaration: Single Administrative Document (SAD) / AES electronic export declaration bearing the Cyprus Customs office reference and exit confirmation (ODAE)
  • Original transport document: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note proving physical departure from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and purchase order specification incorporating Incoterms 2020 rules
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advice or bank statement) evidencing receipt of foreign currency export consideration
  • For B2B services: customer's VAT/business registration certificate, executed software licensing and anti-cheat service agreement indicating the customer's place of establishment

Cyprus does not currently impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-connected continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime on domestic B2B invoices. VAT compliance is return-based through TAXISnet, including periodic VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and SAF-T data schemas. Since 18 October 2022, electronic invoicing through the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 standard is mandatory for suppliers contracting with Cypriot public contracting authorities, transposing EU Directive 2014/55/EU; the Cyprus national e-invoicing platform routes those invoices. A future mandatory e-invoicing clearance model is expected under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) proposals but has not entered into force.

The Cyprus Customs and Tax Department apply risk-based post-clearance verification, cross-checking AES export records against VAT returns and payment evidence. Incomplete or non-compliant export documentation automatically defeats the 0% application and results in a retroactive assessment of VAT at 19% (clawback), plus default interest at the statutory rate of 9% per annum and a 10% late-payment surcharge. Administrative penalties under Article 58 of the VAT Law include fixed fines of up to EUR 100 per unsupported invoice, with a maximum aggregate of EUR 5,000, alongside denial of the corresponding input VAT credit. Fraudulent export zero-rating, including forged customs stamps, sham export transactions, and fabricated inward remittance certificates, constitutes criminal tax evasion: on conviction the company is exposed to an unlimited fine and/or imprisonment of up to five years, disqualification of the tax representative or auditor, and publication on the Tax Department's defaulters list. Mitigation requires a complete supplier-created invoice audit trail, archiving of export evidence for at least seven years, and documentation of the economic substance of the export or cross-border service supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. As a euro-area member, Cyprus maintains an unrestricted current-account and capital-account regime. Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) guarantees free movement of capital, and Eurozone membership eliminates any requirement for ministerial FX approval, foreign-exchange allocation, or rationing for any transaction type, including the repatriation of dividends, royalties, licence fees, intra-group interest, and liquidation proceeds of a Cyprus private limited company. Practical profit-remittance hurdles are only corporate-formal: (i) the Cyprus company must file an audited distributable-profits certification (Companies Law Cap. 113, s. 159) through its auditors; (ii) a Tax Clearance Certificate (IR201/TCC) must be obtained from the Cyprus Tax Department evidencing no outstanding tax liabilities; (iii) the distribution to a non-Cyprus-tax-resident corporate shareholder is free of Cyprus withholding tax and the Special Defence Contribution applies only to Cyprus tax resident shareholders (individuals at 17% — a non-resident corporate recipient is outside scope); (iv) the remitting bank requires board minutes of the dividend declaration and a funds-source declaration under the AML framework (L. 188(I)/2007, as amended), but no convertibility approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. The Republic of Cyprus imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion obligations on export proceeds. A Cyprus exporter may retain foreign currency receipts indefinitely in any authorised credit institution, may invoice wholly in EUR, USD, GBP, or any freely chosen currency, and is under no forced-sale obligation to the CBC or any domestic counterparty. The only formalities are statistical: the CBC's Balance of Payments reporting (Form BOP/C7 and the ECB's BSI/BP6 system) requires monthly/quarterly declaration of non-euro trade receivables and conversions for statistical compilation, which is a data filing and not a regulatory constraint. FATF-compliant KYC practice nevertheless requires each foreign-currency inward payment to be supported by the underlying commercial invoice and contract, and conversions are executed at the ECB reference rate or the bank's best-execution spot quote.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC; Greek: Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), operating as the national competent authority within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); monetary policy and euro issuance authority are exercised exclusively by the European Central Bank (ECB). The CBC is the designated foreign-exchange supervisory authority for Cyprus credit institutions, payment/e-money institutions, and is the competent authority for balance-of-payments statistics under the EBOP/ECB reporting framework. CySEC separately licenses and supervises investment firms and forex/CFD brokers, but does not administer exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate-to-high as an ancillary treasury overlay; low as a substitute for a primary clearing bank. Under the E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and PSD2, EMIs/PIs licensed in any EU/EEA state may passport into Cyprus and provide multi-currency IBANs, virtual subaccounts, SWIFT feeds, and card-acquiring rails without a separate Cypriot licence; this is operationally useful for global collections from game publishers and non-EEA merchants. However, an EMI cannot access TARGET2/T2 settlement on its own (it must use a sponsoring credit institution), cannot provide documentary credits, banker's guarantees, or bilateral FX lines, and does not satisfy the substantive credit relationship that CySEC will evaluate in any future Forex/Gaming licensing application. Critical legal constraint: a Cyprus private limited company retains its registered-office and substance obligations in Cyprus, and the CBC's AML expectations require the company's principal treasury account to be held with an regulated credit institution physically established in Cyprus; consequently the approved design is one Tier-1 local clearing account (Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic) plus one EU EMI (e.g., Irish or Lithuanian passport) for segregated worldwide card settlement wallets.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound registered capital is executed exclusively via SWIFT MT103 (for non-EEA remitters) or via TARGET2/T2 instant credit transfer using ISO 20022 (pacs.008) for EEA-sourced funds. The required ISO 20022 purpose code is 'EQUI' (equity injection) with narrative 'INVESTMENT IN OPERATING CAPITAL — SUBSCRIPTION OF SHARES'; intra-group shareholder loans must use 'TPCS' or 'MOMA' (Money Market) as applicable, never 'GDSV' or services codes. Beneficiary bank documentation pack: certificate of incorporation and good standing; board resolution approving the transfer and account designation; certified share subscription agreement and statement of capital; UBO disclosure forms; LEI certificate of CY_LTD; capital filing form HE30/HE30EF lodged with the Registrar of Companies; and completed bank-proprietary SoF/SoW questionnaire. All inbound credits must exclude cryptocurrency-linked, third-party-aggregated, or unsettled Stripe payouts; the capital route is a single clean G20-compliant wire chain. Where the contribution exceeds EUR 1,000,000, the CBC balance-of-payments notification is filed by the receiving bank; no prior approval is required.Primary rails: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT/instant) for EU/EEA suppliers without FX conversion, executed through the Cypriot clearing account in TARGET2; (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA counterparties using ISO 20022 purpose codes 'TRADE' (goods/services trade), 'LICF' (licence fees) or 'ROYL' (royalties) in field 70, with all outbound instructions accompanied by the underlying invoice, shipping/acceptance certificate, and withholding tax evidence where relevant (Cyprus treaty rates, e.g., 0% US software royalties under Art. 12 US-Cyprus DTC). For procurement of hardware, co-location, and high-value gaming-titling contracts above EUR 500,000, an Irrevocable Confirmed Documentary Letter of Credit (UCP 600) issued by Hellenic Bank / Bank of Cyprus with the supplier's confirming bank is permissible; the commercial contract must state the ISIC 6201 services classification. Stripe is permitted only as a card-based collection rail for inbound receivables; payouts settle daily via SEPA credit into the CY IBAN as 'SEPA PAYOUT — STRIPE' with full merchant reconciliation; Stripe may not be used as an outbound trade-disbursement rail, as its Acceptable Use Policy prohibits inter-corporate B2B service settlements through the card network and would impose a freezing risk. Treasury discipline: pre-clearing instruction no later than T-2 in the bank's trading calendar for USD/GBP disbursements above EUR 50,000 to avoid value-date slippage.Vanilla Deliverable FX Forwards (EUR/USD), tenors up to 24 months, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex (CSA) with the Tier-1 Cypriot bank; used to hedge the naturally arising USD-denominated receivables stream from North American gaming publishers and Stripe USD settlement conversion; margin collateral EUR cash or approved T-bills.European-style FX Options and Collars (zero-cost structured collars, EUR/USD and EUR/GBP), permitted where the underlying exposure is a forecast of recurring software subscription receipts and the option premium is fully settled; no exotic barrier or digital options are authorised under the corporate treasury policy.Cross-Currency Swaps (EUR/USD fixed-for-fixed currency swaps, passports of principal and fixed coupons) permissible only for balance-sheet hedging of USD-denominated financing, capital expenditure or acquisition liabilities; must be collateralised with initial margin under EMIR and reported to the Cyprus Trade Repository (Securities and Markets Authority EMIR reporting obligations).FX Swaps (Swap, spot-start / forward-end) for intra-month liquidity conversion of EUR/USD collections into working capital; permitted for tenors below one year and penalised only by the bank's daily cut-off.Nondeliverable Forwards (NDFs): not permissible for EUR (EUR is a freely deliverable currency and no regulated NDF market exists for the euro under ECB/CBC practice); NDFs referencing emerging-market currencies (e.g., TRY, UAH, NGN) are technically available at some Cypriot banks on a delegated-credit-committee basis only where the company holds genuine receivables in those currencies, but the approved corporate treasury policy excludes them for this pipeline to mitigate pricing and documentation uncertainty.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate-to-high as an ancillary treasury overlay; low as a substitute for a primary clearing bank. Under the E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and PSD2, EMIs/PIs licensed in any EU/EEA state may passport into Cyprus and provide multi-currency IBANs, virtual subaccounts, SWIFT feeds, and card-acquiring rails without a separate Cypriot licence; this is operationally useful for global collections from game publishers and non-EEA merchants. However, an EMI cannot access TARGET2/T2 settlement on its own (it must use a sponsoring credit institution), cannot provide documentary credits, banker's guarantees, or bilateral FX lines, and does not satisfy the substantive credit relationship that CySEC will evaluate in any future Forex/Gaming licensing application. Critical legal constraint: a Cyprus private limited company retains its registered-office and substance obligations in Cyprus, and the CBC's AML expectations require the company's principal treasury account to be held with an regulated credit institution physically established in Cyprus; consequently the approved design is one Tier-1 local clearing account (Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic) plus one EU EMI (e.g., Irish or Lithuanian passport) for segregated worldwide card settlement wallets.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 aggregate value of controlled transactions in a tax year triggers preparation and retention of the Local File, with sub-limits of EUR 750,000 for goods, EUR 250,000 for services and EUR 250,000 for financial/intangible transactions; a Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000; a Transfer Pricing Summary Information Table (TP SIT) must accompany the IR100 where gross turnover exceeds EUR 20,000,000 or controlled transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not impose a UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations filing; economic substance is enforced through the central management and control test for tax residency, the OECD-modified Nexus ratio for the IP Box deduction, and s.61 of the Income Tax Law against artificial transactions. The CY Ltd must maintain local board decision-making, qualified technical staff, R&D time and cost records, and an active Cyprus bank and funding arrangement; absence of such substance for the anti-cheat IP will cause denial of the 80% IP Box deduction, re-characterisation as a conduit or letterbox company, and reassessment of the full royalty profit at 12.5%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of evaded tax at 12.5%, statutory interest at 3.25% p.a., additional taxes up to 100% of the evaded amount in fraud cases, criminal fines up to EUR 50,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Income Tax Law and Penal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for fraudulent trading under s.169 Companies Law (Cap. 113); courts may issue travel bans, asset-freezing orders, director disqualification and civil restitution of the evaded tax and interest.
Shell Company and IP Box Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Tax Commissioner disregards the IP ownership arrangement under s.61 ITL and the OECD Nexus review, denies the IP Box deduction entirely, re-taxes the full qualifying profit at 12.5% with interest, and may impose a negligence penalty of 25% or a fraud penalty of 100% of the tax difference; ATAD CFC rules may attribute the profit to the foreign shareholder.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to personal assessments as participants in the disregarded structure, clawback of distributed dividends, ESC-deprivation orders and departure prohibitions; refusal to restore economic substance can lead to criminal prosecution and disqualification.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Arm's length re-pricing adjustment plus 3.25% annual interest; administrative fine of EUR 5,000 for TP documentation default with continuing daily penalties after formal notice; penalty of up to 25% of the adjusted profit in negligence cases, rising to 100% for deliberate suppression; CbCR data exchange may trigger joint audits with the source jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors approving unreliable transfer pricing without market evidence are personally liable for documentation penalties, face director disqualification, and the assessment may be enforced against the company's receivables and parent guarantees.
AML and Beneficial Ownership (UBO) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 20,000 for the entity and EUR 10,000 per officer under the beneficial ownership framework; failure to maintain AML records under Law L.188(I)/2007 may result in criminal fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; Stripe/SWIFT merchant accounts may be suspended and gaming distributors may lawfully withhold licence fees.

Director personal liability: Directors risk personal criminal liability, debarment from corporate office, strike-off of the company from the Cyprus Registry, loss of treaty protection and immediate disruption of Cyprus tax residence; personal assets may be attached in AML confiscation proceedings.

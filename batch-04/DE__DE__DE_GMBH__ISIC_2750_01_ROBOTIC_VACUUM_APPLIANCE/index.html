Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for value-added tax identification number (USt-IdNr.) and foreign taxpayer registration; local Finanzamt for income tax, corporate income tax, and trade tax. Official portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); BZSt portal: www.bzst.de.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a German branch or permanent establishment must register with the competent local Finanzamt using the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) within one month of commencing business, thereby receiving a Steuernummer (tax number). For VAT cross-border supplies, the entity must apply to BZSt for a unique USt-IdNr. under §27a UStG. Non-established suppliers may also use the FEA registration process via BZSt to obtain a VAT ID without a domestic permanent establishment, with registration required before the first taxable supply in Germany.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a German GmbH is a corporation subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporation tax), Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge), and Gewerbesteuer (trade tax), and is treated as a taxable separate legal person for income tax and VAT purposes. Its worldwide income is subject to unlimited tax liability in Germany if effectively managed or registered in Germany, subject to double tax treaties; VAT applies to all taxable supplies in Germany regardless of entity status. — A foreign entity establishing a German branch or permanent establishment must register with the competent local Finanzamt using the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) within one month of commencing business, thereby receiving a Steuernummer (tax number). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a German GmbH is a corporation subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporation tax), Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge), and Gewerbesteuer (trade tax), and is treated as a taxable separate legal person for income tax and VAT purposes. Its worldwide income is subject to unlimited tax liability in Germany if effectively managed or registered in Germany, subject to double tax treaties; VAT applies to all taxable supplies in Germany regardless of entity status.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Germany. A German GmbH may elect fiscal unity (Organschaft) with qualifying subsidiaries for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT, on the basis of profit-and-loss transfer agreements and financial integration. Hybrid entity classifications are governed by §1a KStG for foreign hybrid companies; no 8832-style elective classification is available. Germany applies ATAD hybrid mismatch rules under the Anti-Steuervermeidungsgesetz, with mandatory adjustments rather than elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 previous year and EUR 50,000 current year for the small-business exemption under §19 UStG (only for domestic small entrepreneurs). For non-resident suppliers, no turnover threshold applies: any taxable supply in Germany creates a registration obligation. For intra-Community distance sales of goods, a Germany-specific registration threshold of EUR 10,000 applies across the total EU-wide distance sales; breach requires German VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the European Union qualify for VAT zero-rating under §4 Nr.1 UStG and §6 UStG if the supplier proves that the goods physically left the EU customs territory. For services, offshore services relating to movable tangible property or used/exploited outside the EU may be zero-rated under §4 Nr.3 UStG if the recipient is a non-entrepreneur or the place of supply is outside Germany; intra-Community supplies of goods are also zero-rated under §4 Nr.1 letter b) and §6a UStG with the customer's VAT ID and dispatch evidence. All zero-rating requires complete, consistent export documentation (Belegnachweise) and timely recording.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / ATLAS Export) and Export Accompanying Document (ABD) or electronic EAD
  • Commercial invoice with full export reference, Incoterms, and description of goods
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note, or other transport document evidencing physical movement out of the EU
  • Proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) in the form prescribed by the Umsatzsteuer-Durchführungsverordnung (UStDV §§8-13), including either the customs electronic verification ('Ausfuhrnachweis über die ATLAS-Ausfuhranmeldung') or alternative documentary evidence
  • Packing list, purchase order, or contract evidencing the supply to the foreign customer
  • Bank transfer record / inward remittance certificate (for payment substantiation, where applicable)

Germany has a phased e-invoicing mandate for domestic B2B supplies: from 1 January 2025, all domestic taxable persons must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0+); mandatory transmission of e-invoices between domestic B2B entities becomes fully obligatory from 1 January 2027 (2028 for businesses with low turnover). There is no real-time fiscal clearance, no stamping, and no API fiscal-reporting scheme comparable to other EU Member States. Invoices remain subject to EU VAT invoice content rules, and e-invoice data must be transmitted in a machine-readable, structured electronic format.

The tax authorities require export evidence to be retained for the statutory retention period; if the substantiation is missing or incomplete, zero-rating is retroactively denied, and output VAT at 19% is clawed back with statutory interest (6% per annum, currently below-market but legally fixed). Fraudulent evasion or intentional false substantiation triggers prosecution under §370 AO (tax evasion), fines up to EUR 50,000 per substantive count, imprisonment up to 5 years, and exclusion from exemption privileges; additionally, penalties under §26a UStG and trade-tax assessment adjustments may apply. The BZSt and German customs (Generalzolldirektion) coordinate audit scrutiny of export VAT refunds and cross-border e-commerce flows for robotic vacuum appliances classified under HS 8508/8479, with special attention to MTD and parallel-import schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full capital and current account convertibility under EU liberalization directives and OECD codes. Profit remittance by a GmbH is unfettered, subject to corporate income tax withholding (25% plus solidarity surcharge, reduced to 0% for EU parent companies under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive), trade tax neutrality, and documentary compliance with AWV statistics reporting. No FX approval is required for EUR-based remittances; non-EUR transactions must be reported if they exceed the statutory thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Exporters are free to hold foreign currency receivables in domestic or foreign accounts. Under AWV Section 67, any payment received from non-residents that exceeds €50,000 (or the countervalue) must be reported to the Bundesbank within five business days of receipt; no conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) jointly administer foreign exchange and payment oversight; the Bundesbank operates the Microdata Reporting System and the AWV (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung) reporting framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (restricted). Non-EU EMI/PSP entities without a German or EU banking license, passporting under PSD2, or an established branch cannot offer direct local IBAN accounts or provide uninterrupted clearing access. Licensed EU EMIs (e.g., German BaFin-licensed e-money institutions or UK EMIs with German passporting) can support multi-currency sweep, FX execution, and SWIFT-like payments, but they cannot substitute for the full correspondent banking relationship needed for commercial L/Cs. Long-term treasury centralization should favor a tier-1 bank relationship.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax (0.825 percentage points); plus municipal Gewerbesteuer at 7–17% depending on the local Hebesatz (typical 400% = 14%), producing a combined effective corporate burden of approximately 30% (range 25%–33%) before incentives.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withheld tax, resulting in a gross withholding burden of 26.375% on German-source dividends distributed by the GmbH.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory filing deadline is 31 July of the year following the fiscal year-end; if a qualified tax adviser prepares the return, the deadline is extended to 31 May of the second subsequent year under §149 AO.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A German Local File must be prepared contemporaneously for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding EUR 1,000,000 per fiscal year; a Master File is mandatory where the group exceeds EUR 100,000,000 consolidated revenue and cross-border intercompany transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000; Country-by-Country Report filing is required for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain an offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but economic substance is decisive for treaty entitlement and anti-abuse rules. The GmbH must be actually managed, staffed and directed in Germany, maintaining its own tangible assets, business decisions, bank accounts and operational risk. A mere letterbox entity is disregarded by German and foreign tax authorities, denied DTA benefits, and may be reclassified as tax-transparent, leading to retrospective withholding and penalty exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years, extended to 10 years in particularly severe cases under §370 AO; monetary fine of up to EUR 10,000,000; assessment of evaded tax plus 6% annual interest; the tax claim is enforceable by seizure of all German and foreign assets.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under §69 AO for tax liabilities lost through intentional or grossly negligent management; civil restitution extends to the full evaded amount; personal travel bans, withdrawal of passports, and imprisonment of the director are foreseeably applied.
Shell Company / Artificial Letterbox Structure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test and ATAD anti-abuse rules; recharacterisation of income in the hand of the beneficial owner; foreign-source profit margin allocated back to German substance; imposition of solidarity surcharge adjustments, 6% interest, and penalties up to 20% of the reallocated amount.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for unpaid taxes under §69 AO; liability for capital procurement under the GmbH Act if the nominal capital was not physically funded; invalidation of corporate resolutions or avoidance of the GmbH as a sham entity; travel bans and possible extradition proceedings where an underlying criminal shell-company arrangement is found.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment increasing German taxable income under §1 AStG; secondary recharacterisation as hidden profit distribution (verdeckte Gewinnausschüttung) per §8a KStG; surcharge of up to EUR 1,000,000 for missing documentation; additional 6% annual interest on the tax undercharge.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the corporation's unpaid tax when they knowingly signed or approved non-arm's-length agreements; in gross-negligence cases, criminal liability under §370 AO attaches; prohibition from managing a GmbH and personal asset confiscation are possible.
Anti-Money-Laundering Failure

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines up to EUR 100,000 for natural persons and up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for legal entities; prohibition of business continuation for the offending GmbH; criminal prosecution for money laundering under §261 StGB, with imprisonment up to 5 years and forfeiture of the device inventory and proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors bear personal criminal and civil liability if they knowingly or negligently enabled undeclared financial flows; the corporate veil is pierced for the company's monetary fines; personal bank accounts may be frozen and the director may be arrested or extradited in connection with cross-border laundering predicates.

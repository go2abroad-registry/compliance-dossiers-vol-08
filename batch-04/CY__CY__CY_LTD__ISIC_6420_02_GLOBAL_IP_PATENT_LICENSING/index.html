Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Cyprus (Tμήμα Φόρων); official portal: https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy

E-government filing portal

For a Cyprus private limited company, the Registrar of Companies issues the Certificate of Incorporation. The company must then register with the District Tax Office using Tax Department Form T.D. 101 within 60 days of incorporation or commencement of business. The Tax Department issues the Tax Identification Number (TIC). A non-resident foreign entity with Cyprus-source income or a VAT compliance obligation must submit the same form plus certified corporate constitutional documents, corporate registry extract, registered office certificate, and a declaration of activities; registration for VAT is made through Form T.D. 101M or the online VAT registration facility on the Tax Portal.

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Elections available under Cyprus tax law include: application of group relief under section 17 of the Income Tax Law, election to claim the Intellectual Property Box regime (exemption of 80% of qualifying profits from royalties), and election for Notional Interest Deduction (NID) on newly introduced equity capital.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 (compulsory registration when taxable supplies in Cyprus exceed EUR 15,600 in any trailing 12-month period). Non-resident suppliers making any taxable supply in Cyprus are generally required to register irrespective of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported by the supplier or on its behalf to destinations outside the European Union are zero-rated, provided the physical export is substantiated and commercial and transport documents support the subsequent retention in non-EU customs territory. Intra-community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered person in another EU Member State are zero-rated when the customer's valid EU VAT number is obtained and the required EC Sales Listing is filed. For IP licensing services, royalties paid to a Cyprus licensor by a non-resident taxable person may fall within the business-to-business reverse charge mechanism or be regarded as outside the scope of Cyprus VAT when the effective place of supply is outside Cyprus.

  • Customs Export Declaration accepted by the Cyprus Customs (Single Administrative Document/SAD)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or other transport document evidencing international carriage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-payment confirmation evidencing receipt of the export proceeds

No universal real-time fiscal clearance mandate applies to B2B supplies. Since July 2023, suppliers to Cypriot central government entities must issue e-invoices through the PEPPOL network. The Tax Department accepts electronic invoices that preserve authenticity and integrity; API-based continuous transaction controls are not mandatory. No digital VAT stamping requirement exists for commercial invoices.

The Tax Department and Cyprus Customs conduct risk-based audits of zero-rating claims. A zero-rated supply wrongly claimed attracts VAT assessment made at 19% plus statutory interest at 1% per month or part thereof? In practice, penalties under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law and VAT Law include: misdeclaration penalty up to 1.5% per month of the tax underdeclared, fixed penalties for failure to keep records, and criminal liability for fraudulent evasion; the culpable party is also liable to repay any refund claimed. Retrospective clawback of erroneously zero-rated declarations may extend to an adjustment period of up to 10 years where fraud has been established, with compound interest and potential director liability in liquidation scenarios.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a euro-area member state (EUR adopted on 1 January 2008) and maintains no residual exchange-control regime. Full capital-account liberalization applies under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union: current-account payments, royalty remittances, dividend distributions, intercompany loan repayments, and equity capital injections are freely convertible and transferable without Central Bank approval, quantitative ceilings, or administrative pre-consents. The EUR is fully convertible against USD, GBP, and all clearing currencies at market rates. Practical friction points are limited to AML/KYC enhanced due diligence, CRS/FATCA reporting, EU/UN sanctions screening, and Balance-of-Payments statistical declaration duties. Profit remittance is structurally tax-neutral: zero Cyprus withholding tax on outbound dividends (noting the deemed-distribution rules for Cyprus-resident shareholders of non-listed companies), zero withholding tax on outbound interest, and zero withholding tax on outbound royalties where the underlying IP is not exploited in Cyprus.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender ratio, repatriation timeline, or forced conversion ratio applies in Cyprus. Royalty and licensing proceeds may be retained indefinitely in any convertible currency, including multicurrency EUR/USD/GBP accounts. Commercial banks are legally obliged, under the Balance of Payments Law of 2003 (L. 80(I)/2003) and Central Bank of Cyprus statistical directives, to capture and file Balance-of-Payments (BoP) returns for transactions above bank-defined de minimis thresholds (commonly EUR 10,000 per transaction or aggregated monthly flows). Such filings are purely statistical and do not condition, block, or tax inward receipts. EU sanctions (including comprehensive restrictions on Russia, Belarus, and Iranian counterparties) constitute the only substantive constraint on the free use and onward transfer of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou) - Department of Banking Supervision and Financial Stability; co-regulatory interface with the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) for FX and derivative service providers, the Unit for Combating Money Laundering (MOKAS) as the national Financial Intelligence Unit, and the Cyprus Tax Department for tax-clearance certificates on profit distributions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for primary treasury infrastructure; Medium as an ancillary disbursement rail. Only EEA-authorized electronic money institutions under Directive 2009/110/EC or payment institutions under PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366), including Cyprus-licensed EMIs under the Electronic Money Law of 2018 (L. 58(I)/2018), may be used. Such platforms provide SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer access plus multi-currency wallets, but they do not confer deposit-guarantee protection (funds are merely safeguarded), cannot hold escrow or pledge positions, and carry elevated debanking risk for IP-licensing HoldCos due to concentrated cross-currency flows and limited substance. Global EMIs such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, and Payoneer may be tolerated for operating expenses below EUR 250,000 per month, but must never be used for share-capital injections, dividend distributions, intercompany loan settlements, tax payments, or high-value royalty flows. All EMI channels must be sub-accounted to the primary banking relationship and reassessed quarterly for sanctions and concentration risk.

Within 15 months after the end of the tax year (i.e., by 31 March of the second following year for calendar-year taxpayers). Provisional tax payments are due in two instalments: 31 July and 31 December of the tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cyprus does not have statutory local file/master file thresholds in EUR; however, taxpayers must maintain TP documentation consistent with OECD guidelines, and the Cyprus tax authorities may request documentation for related-party transactions of any size. In practice, full TP documentation (including Local File) is expected where intercompany royalties exceed EUR 500,000 annually, and for groups with consolidated turnover above EUR 750 million, CbC reporting applies.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies the OECD BEPS standards and EU ATAD requirements, requiring economic substance for IP companies. For IP HoldCos, substance requires active management and decision-making in Cyprus, qualified local staff with adequate qualifications, and proportionate operational expenditures. For IP license income, the company must demonstrate strategic control over the IP, performance of development and exploitation functions, and assumption of financial risk. Cyprus does not have a broad ESR regime akin to Caribbean jurisdictions, but empty shell IP companies are challengeable under domestic GAAR and treaty abuse provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years (severe fraud); additional civil fines equal to 100% of tax evaded; mandatory publication of judgment may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors can be individually charged for tax evasion; personal criminal conviction may result in imprisonment and personal confiscation orders; travel bans can be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profit to arm's length; penalty of 10%-20% of additional tax; interest at 3% per annum; additional penalty for late payment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be liable for civil penalties if they knowingly permitted incorrect TP positions; personal fines and disqualification possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of IP Box benefits; recharacterisation under GAAR; penalties up to 100% of tax understated; may be struck off as an inactive company.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for misapplying company assets; criminal liability for false declarations to tax authorities; travel bans and seizure of personal property possible.

Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability: up to 14 years imprisonment and substantial fines; assets frozen and confiscated; CySEC or ICPAC disciplinary action if service provider involved.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to implement AML controls or knowingly facilitate laundering can be personally indicted; personal fines and imprisonment up to 14 years; travel bans pending trial.
Profit Repatriation Abuse (Trapped Cash / Constructive Dividend)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as dividend subject to SDC (if applicable) or as taxable distribution; additional tax penalties and interest; potential CFC rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for breaching exchange control or tax filing of non-resident shareholders; personal fines and disqualification could apply.

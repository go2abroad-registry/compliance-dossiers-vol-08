Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Tax Department of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance (Tmima Forologias), 118 Kinyras Avenue, Larnaca; secure digital portal Tax For All (https://taxfor-all.mof.gov.cy); corporate registry filings at Department of Registrar of Companies.

        
E-government filing portal
SOP: (1) Obtain certificate of incorporation (HE77 form) from the Registrar of Companies; (2) Submit application via Tax For All using the VAT 101 form for a private limited company, attaching the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, identity documents of directors and shareholders, and evidence of registered office; (3) Tax Department issues the Cyprus TIN (Identification Code) and VAT registration number where expected taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600 per annum or upon voluntary registration; (4) Activate Tax For All e-filing credentials; (5) Non-resident foreign entities without Cyprus establishment must appoint a fiscal representative for VAT compliance. Standard processing timelines range from 2 to 4 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation; CY_LTD is a Cyprus tax resident entity subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide income determined by place of effective management; non-resident equity holders are not deemed to have a permanent establishment solely by owning shares; dividends and capital gains on shares fall under the participation exemption; no pass-through eligibility is available for a Cyprus incorporated limited liability company.

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box or full transparency election exists for Cypriot limited companies. Available elections and mechanisms: (1) Group relief election for surrender of losses between qualifying group companies; (2) VAT group registration election; (3) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital; (4) Intellectual Property Box election for 80% exemption on qualifying IP profits, resulting in a 2.5% effective rate; (5) Small enterprise flat-rate VAT scheme; (6) ATAD-compliant hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically with no elective opt-out; (7) Advance tax ruling confirmation with the Tax Department for cross-border MRO B2B supply chains.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
19%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for supply of goods and services by a Cyprus-established business; EUR 35,000 threshold for intra-Community distance sales of goods; EUR 10,000 annual threshold for remote services supplied to EU consumers across member states; registration obligation triggered within 30 days of exceeding the threshold.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating (0%) applies to: (a) exports of industrial MRO goods to non-EU destinations provided the goods are dispatched outside the EU within a reasonable period and proof of export is evidenced; (b) intra-EU dispatch or supply of goods to a taxable person holding a valid EU VAT identification number, with transport documentation; (c) supplies of goods to vessels, aircraft, and offshore installations used in EU waters; (d) B2B services supplied to non-EU customers where the recipient is established outside Cyprus; all claims require proper commercial records and export evidence retained in the supplier's archive.

No mandatory real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance, fiscal stamping, or tax authority pre-clearance regime exists in Cyprus as of 2025. VAT e-invoicing for cross-border B2B transactions will become mandatory pursuant to EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) proposals, with expected application by 2030. Currently, VAT returns (Form VAT 200), EC Sales Lists, and Intrastat declarations must be filed electronically through the Tax For All portal; use of PEPPOL-based e-invoicing standards is optional but accepted.

      
Cyprus law does not statutorily codify a separate 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects the documentary window commonly applied to evidence export proceeds and inward remittance, together with Central Bank of Cyprus and EU balance-of-payments reporting expectations. The Tax Department and Customs Authorities may scrutinize zero-rated claims and retrospectively claw back VAT at 19% where export documentation is incomplete, false, or fails the 180-day evidence test, with interest accrued at 8% per annum. Administrative penalties up to EUR 600 plus EUR 68 per day of non-payment and penalties up to 100% of the assessed VAT may apply; fraudulent export zero-rating claims constitute criminal evasion punishable by fines of up to EUR 10,000 and imprisonment of up to 6 years; the statutory reassessment window is 6 years, extended to 12 years in cases of fraud.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Full, unrestricted convertibility of current and capital account transactions pursuant to Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No foreign-exchange licensing, prior approval, or quantitative limits apply to any inward or outward flow; the 2013 crisis-era restrictions were fully removed by April 2015. Profit remittance is unimpeded: Cyprus imposes 0% withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; Cyprus-tax-resident shareholders may incur Special Defence Contribution at 17%, and the deemed-distribution rule (on 70% of accounting profits) can be waived by the Tax Commissioner on adequate business grounds. Practically, hurdles are limited to standard AML/KYC substance verification and Balance of Payments statistical reporting.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline applies to export proceeds. Receipts may be retained indefinitely in EUR or any convertible currency in resident or non-resident accounts. The only statutory obligation is statistical: the resident receiving bank classifies each flow under the ECB Balance of Payments taxonomy (e.g., exports of goods, export credit) and reports to the Central Bank of Cyprus via the CBC External Transactions system. No tax gross-up is imposed on undistributed export earnings.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου / Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou) acting as the national competent authority within the Eurosystem; foreign-exchange and convertibility policy is effectively delegated to the European Central Bank because Cyprus is a full Eurozone member.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Cyprus has not implemented a statutory local file or master file threshold by turnover; however, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is required for transactions with associated enterprises where the aggregate value of related-party transactions exceeds €750,000 in a tax year. The documentation must include a functional analysis, comparability analysis, and application of the arm's length principle in accordance with Section 33 of the Income Tax Law and the EU Joint Transfer Pricing Forum guidelines. In practice, the Cyprus Tax Department expects any cross-border related-party transaction to be supported by an appropriate benchmarking study where relevant.

      
Economic substance scope: Cyprus implements the EU substance requirements and the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) standards through the application of the arm's length principle, controlled foreign corporation rules, and the general anti-abuse provisions in the Income Tax Law. Although Cyprus does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like certain offshore jurisdictions, tax residence status combined with real management and control, adequate physical presence, local directors, payroll expenditure, and operational substance in Cyprus is required to avoid a foreign PE recharacterisation or treaty shopping abuse. For a B2B MRO e-commerce company, substantive activities such as strategic procurement, inventory risk management, customer contracting, and treasury functions must be performed in Cyprus to justify profit attribution and to resist anti-avoidance challenge. The Cyprus Tax Department follows the GAAR in Section 44A of the Income Tax Law, empowering it to disregard artificial arrangements lacking commercial substance.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Declaration
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, tax evasion carries criminal fines up to €50,000 and imprisonment up to five years, in addition to the payment of all evaded tax, default interest at 1.75% per month, and a 5% late-payment surcharge. Civil penalties may reach 100% of the evaded tax where negligence is proven.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns, knowingly participate in fraud, or cause the company to file inaccurate accounts may be criminally prosecuted, banned from serving as directors for up to five years, and jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall where the company is dissolved or insolvent and funds have been extracted without fair consideration.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Abuse and Thin Capitalisation
        
Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments are made under Section 33 and the arm's length principle, with additional taxes at 12.5% on the adjusted profit, default interest, and a 10% penalty under Article 50 of the Tax Assessment Law for inaccurate returns. Failure to maintain documentation may shift the burden of proof to the taxpayer and trigger GAAR recharacterisation under Section 44A.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for civil restitution of underpaid tax if they authorise related-party transactions without proper board approval and TP documentation; persistent abuse can lead to disqualification orders under the Companies Law and criminal liability where fraud is established.

      

      
      

        Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure
        
Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department can invoke Section 44A GAAR to disregard the entity as a conduit and reattribute profits to the real foreign taxpayers. The corporate veil may be pierced, dividend or royalty income reclassified as taxable profits, and the company may be struck off under the Companies Law. Administrative fines and loss of treaty benefits may result under the OECD Multilateral Convention.

        
Director personal liability: Nominee directors who knowingly lend their signatures to a shell entity without exercising management control may face civil liability for company debts, disqualification, travel bans, and potential extradition in cross-border tax fraud cases.

      

      
      

        Incorrect Withholding Tax and DTA Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: Withholding tax deficiencies are collected from the payor with default interest at 21% annualised and a 5% surcharge. Criminal fines under the Income Tax Law can reach €50,000. Treaty abuse may be blocked through the principal purpose test under the Multilateral Instrument, leading to denial of relief.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who approve unlawful dividend distributions while the company is insolvent or who knowingly execute treaty-shopping structures may be personally liable to creditors and the tax authorities; travel restrictions and director disqualification are available as enforcement measures.

      

      
      

        AML, False Bookkeeping and Evasion as Predicate Offence
        
Statutory sanction: The Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law imposes criminal penalties of up to five years imprisonment and fines up to €50,000; the Tax Department may also issue subpoenas for bank records and impose administrative fines up to 10% of annual turnover under the audit powers.

        
Director personal liability: Directors as 'responsible persons' under the AML Law face personal criminal liability, asset freezes, travel bans, and disqualification; beneficial owners may face civil recovery proceedings and criminal prosecution.

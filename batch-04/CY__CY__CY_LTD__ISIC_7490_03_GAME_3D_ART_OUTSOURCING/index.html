Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Ministry of Finance), operational portal: Cyprus Tax Portal (taxportal.portal.gov.cy), serving income tax, VAT, social insurance, and related levies. The VAT arm is the VAT Commissioner within the Tax Department; corporate registration is handled by the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (ROC).

E-government filing portal

Two-step procedure for a foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company: (1) incorporate with the ROC to obtain the HE77 registration number and Articles of Association; (2) within 60 days of incorporation, register the company with the Tax Department via the Tax Portal to obtain the 8-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN). If taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600 in any 12-month period, or is expected to do so within 30 days, file VAT Form 101 with the VAT Commissioner to obtain the VAT registration number (format CY + 8 digits + check letter). A non-resident company with a Cyprus permanent establishment must register within 60 days of establishing the PE and must appoint a Cyprus-resident tax representative for local compliance filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Cyprus Ltd is a separate taxable person for corporate income tax at the flat 12.5% rate on worldwide income when managed and controlled in Cyprus; no pass-through of profits to shareholders for CIT purposes; distributions qualify for participation exemption and the Notional Interest Deduction framework.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Cyprus law. Available elections and designations include: (a) group relief election (Income Tax Law Art. 22, Form IR470); (b) VAT group registration election; (c) Notional Interest Deduction election on qualifying new equity; (d) IP Box regime election under the OECD-modified-nexus approach; (e) foreign tax credit election; (f) non-domicile/non-resident status election for individuals (not applicable to the entity); (g) hybrid mismatch rule elective treatments and mandatory self-reporting under the ATAD-aligned provisions of the Income Tax Law (N.118(I)/2002, as amended).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 — compulsory VAT registration threshold for established taxable persons, measured on taxable supplies in any preceding 12-month period or expected in the next 30 days; cross-border distance-sales into Cyprus trigger registration at the EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-sales threshold under the OSS/OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: 0% VAT applies where tangible goods are physically exported from Cyprus to a destination outside the EU, provided dispatch/transport is carried out by the seller or for the seller's account and EU exit is evidenced, generally within the 90-day compliance window; intra-EU dispatches to a taxable customer are zero-rated only upon presentation of the customer's valid VAT number and timely filing of the EC Sales List. Service exports (the operative activity for 3D game art outsourcing): B2B services supplied to a non-EU game publisher are outside the scope of Cypriot VAT under the place-of-supply rule (customer establishment), so no VAT is charged; B2B services to EU-established publishers are subject to the reverse-charge mechanism in the recipient's member state, requiring an invoice clearly stating 'reverse charge' without Cypriot VAT. Input VAT incurred on purchases attributable to such zero-rated or outside-scope supplies remains recoverable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) lodged via the Cyprus Customs electronic system THESEAS for physical goods
  • Commercial Invoice (itemized, with Incoterms and value declared) and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Proof of exit — electronic CUS/EXP confirmation or customs-stamped export certificate
  • Signed Statement of Work or Service Contract with the foreign game publisher, delivery acceptance records, and correspondence evidencing the business-to-business nature of the service export
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank statement) proving export/service proceeds repatriation to the Cyprus bank account
  • Where applicable, EC Sales List (Form VAT 404/405) and customer VAT registration evidence for intra-EU supplies

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based continuous transaction control mandate is currently in force for B2B/B2C invoicing in Cyprus. Mandatory electronic invoicing applies only to B2G supplies under EU Directive 2014/55/EU (PEPPOL / EN 16931 standards). VAT returns (VAT 101/102/103), EC Sales Lists, and annual recapitulative statements must be filed electronically through the Cyprus Tax Portal. Cyprus has announced alignment with the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) initiative, with future structured e-invoicing expected but no firm national go-live date set.

Cyprus abolished exchange controls upon EU accession and imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; however, Tax Department audit practice and banking AML documentation standards treat the 180-day returns window as the accepted benchmark for inward remittance substantiation. False, fraudulent, or insufficiently documented zero-rating or outside-scope claims trigger: reassessment of VAT at 19% on the gross invoice value, statutory late-payment interest from the filing due date, and administrative penalties up to 100% of the tax under-assessed in cases of negligence and up to 200% where fraud or intentional evasion is established (Law 94(I)/2000; Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law 4/1978). Criminal liability may attach, with fines up to EUR 20,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years for VAT evasion offences. The Tax Department may retroactively claw back input VAT credits, refuse refund claims, and issue assessments for up to 5 years from the end of the taxable period (extended in cases of fraudulent conduct), and may suspend or cancel the exporter's VAT registration number pending compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a euro-area Member State. The euro is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions; there are no exchange control restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or loan repayments. Capital movements are fully liberalised under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Cross-border fund flows are subject only to EU anti-money laundering, terrorism financing, tax transparency, and balance-of-payments statistical reporting requirements. Withholding tax on dividends to non-residents is 0%, and profit remittance is permitted after satisfaction of Cyprus corporate tax obligations, provided the company maintains adequate economic substance and complies with anti-abuse and CRS/FATCA reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the Central Bank of Cyprus or to any domestic authorised dealer. As Cyprus uses the euro as domestic currency, euro-denominated export receipts need no conversion. Non-euro export proceeds from 3D game art outsourcing services may be credited to a Cyprus-licensed bank account and converted freely in the interbank market. There is no forced conversion ratio, no mandatory timeline, and no surrender threshold. The company must, however, maintain accurate accounting records and report each qualifying cross-border transaction to the Central Bank of Cyprus through the resident bank under the ECB Balance of Payments reporting framework where the aggregate threshold is exceeded.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating within the European System of Central Banks and the Single Supervisory Mechanism; the Eurosystem is the de facto monetary authority for Cyprus

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium, subject to strict legal constraints. A Cyprus-domiciled company may open transactional accounts with EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions or Payment Institutions that hold a valid passport into the Republic of Cyprus under PSD2 (Payment Services Directive 2015/2366). Such EMI accounts are viable for collection from global game publishers and for small operational expenses, but they are not substitutes for a commercial bank account when clearing equity injections, obtaining external financing, or establishing tax substance. Offshore, unregulated digital banks and third-country EMIs lacking a Cyprus/EEA licence are high-risk; funds sourced from such institutions into Cyprus will trigger enhanced due diligence and may be frozen or rejected. Any EMI used must have a registered agent or legal presence in the EEA, and the Cyprus company must be able to provide an audit trail from the EMI to the underlying commercial contract and invoicing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital injections must be routed through the SWIFT network using an MT103 payment instruction from the shareholder's bank to the Cyprus bank. The payment purpose line must clearly indicate 'Contribution to share capital of Cyprus private limited liability company' and reference the corporate registration number. The receiving bank is obliged to capture the ECB Balance of Payments (BoP) statistical code for direct investment equity; the company or its accountant must confirm the correct BoP sub-item, typically 'Direct investment – Equity capital', on the bank's statutory declaration form. The bank will require a board resolution, updated shareholders register, and proof of the source of funds before allowing the escrowed capital to be used for operational expenses. If the capital is received as a shareholder loan rather than equity, a written loan agreement, arm's length interest rate, and subordination terms are required.Outbound trade settlements for 3D game art outsourcing services are cleared via: (1) SWIFT MT103 for single or recurring commercial invoices, especially cross-border non-euro payments to suppliers and contractors; (2) commercial letters of credit governed by UCP 600 for high-value milestone payments and contract guarantee requirements; (3) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for euro transactions within the EEA, provided the beneficiary is also within SEPA; and (4) standard international wire transfers for educational, royalty, or licensing disbursements. For each disbursement, the bank requires the underlying contract, invoice, proof of service delivery, and any applicable BO/IBAN reference. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate related-party controlled transaction value per tax year triggers the mandatory Cyprus TP Return / Transfer Pricing Study; OECD-aligned Master File and Local File obligations apply where the group consolidated turnover reaches EUR 750 million (EU threshold).

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies the 'management and control' doctrine for tax residency under Income Tax Law s. 2 and the arm's length standard under s. 33A. Substance requirements include a physical registered office, board meetings held in Cyprus, majority-resident directors, employees with decision-making authority, and transactional contracts executed from Cyprus. The IP Box deduction is conditioned on the nexus approach, requiring qualifying R&D expenditure relative to qualifying IP assets; an IP-rich shell with no local substance will be denied treaty benefits, IP Box relief, and the Tax Residency Certificate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Law (118(I)/2002), s. 99: criminal fines up to EUR 20,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years per offence, plus repayment of evaded tax and interest; civil late-filing penalties of EUR 100 plus EUR 50 per month (EUR 500 cap per return) and 3.75% annual interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false or misleading returns face personal criminal conviction; under Companies Law (Cap. 113), s. 306, the court can order civil restitution and make directors personally contribute to company obligations in cases of fraudulent trading; the Tax Commissioner may seek a court-imposed travel ban and departure prohibition under Law 48/2017 for unpaid personal or corporate tax debts.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial or revocation of Cyprus tax residency and the Tax Residency Certificate; rejection of treaty relief and IP Box claims; full transfer-pricing re-assessment at 12.5% plus interest and late-payment charges; deregistration of the company under the Companies Law for false registered-office declarations.

Director personal liability: Nominee and shadow directors can be prosecuted for misleading declarations, disqualified from acting as directors for up to 5 years, ordered to restore corporate assets, and held jointly and severally liable with the company for assessed tax, penalties, and interest.
AML / Sanctions Evasion in Payment Flows

Statutory sanction: Law 188(I)/2007 (as amended): money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of up to 14 years and/or substantial fines; DNFBP non-compliance attracts regulatory penalties up to EUR 1,000,000, forced compliance orders, asset freezing, and confiscation of illicit proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and the appointed compliance officer face personal criminal liability for knowingly allowing illicit funds into the corporate estate; banking partners may terminate payment rails (wire and PayPal) and freeze accounts; individuals may be subject to travel bans, prohibitive orders, and arrest pending extradition proceedings.

Competent corporate registry

The Companies Law, Cap. 113 (Republic of Cyprus, as amended), principally Sections 5 (mode of incorporation), 6 (memorandum requirements), 8 (articles of association and Table A), 9 (registration procedure), 11 (certificate of incorporation), 14 (effect of incorporation) and 169 (registered office); the Companies (Fees) Regulations; and the Beneficial Owners Register provisions of Law 188(I)/2007 (AML) as applied to companies.

Formation / constitutional filings

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας) under the Ministry of Finance; official electronic filing portal: 'TaxForAll' (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy), with integrated ARIADNI system for VAT returns, VIES/EC Sales Lists, Intrastat and corporate tax filings.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the branch/private limited company via the Department of Registrar of Companies and obtain the statutory HE registration number; (2) Tax registration is substantially automatic upon incorporation, but a non-resident foreign entity commencing taxable activities in Cyprus must file Form TD2003 ('Notice of Commencement of Business') with the Tax Department within 60 days of commencement to formalize its corporate income tax file and receive or confirm its Cyprus Tax Identification Number (the corporate TIN is keyed to the HE number); (3) File Form VAT 101 (Application for VAT Registration) with the VAT Commissioner where taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600 (EU-established persons) or immediately, with no threshold, in the case of a non-established foreign company making its first taxable supply in Cyprus; (4) Each payer then receives a distinct 9-digit VAT registration number in the format 'CY-XXXXXXXX'; (5) Register as an employer for PAYE/GHS (Gesundheitsbeitrag) via the TaxForAll portal when hiring Cyprus staff.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with territorial-source elements: Cyprus tax-resident private limited companies are taxed at 12.5% on worldwide income with a broad territorial exemption for non-Cyprus permanent establishment profits; non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income; there is no pass-through recognition for a Ltd under Cyprus law, although qualifying transparent partnerships and deemed dividend distribution rules (Section 33D Income Tax Law) apply to close companies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Cyprus law; available statutory elections and reliefs include: (a) Group relief and tax-loss surrender elections and tax consolidation election under Section 21 of the Income Tax Law for 75%+ group companies; (b) Tonnage tax election exclusively for qualifying shipping activities; (c) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital; (d) Foreign Tax Credit / unilateral relief election; (e) Optional application of the IP Box attenuated deduction regime under Section 23B; (f) Reorganization / internal restructuring rollover elections under Sections 24-26; (g) for close companies, the deemed distribution regime permits an election to apply the 17% deemed distribution rate rather than the full Cyprus dividend withholding approach in limited intra-group scenarios.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for Cyprus-established persons; EUR 35,000 for intra-Community distance sales of goods into Cyprus; EUR 10,000 for intra-Community acquisitions; non-resident taxable persons supplying goods or services in Cyprus have zero threshold and must register before the first supply; reverse-charge applies where an unregistered non-resident supplies B2B services to a Cyprus VAT-registered business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) exports of goods physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union, evidenced by a valid customs export declaration (SAD/electronic customs lodging) and proof of actual physical departure from Cyprus/EU; and (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered business in another EU Member State where the customer's valid VAT number is held and listed on the VIES/EC Sales List. Services — including cross-border commercial litigation, arbitration and asset-tracing legal services supplied by a Cyprus law firm to a business customer established outside Cyprus — fall outside the Cyprus VAT net under the B2B place-of-supply rule (Article 44 of VAT Directive; Section 84A VAT Law), being taxable in the recipient's country by reverse charge rather than 'zero-rated' locally; no Cyprus VAT export certificate is required for such offshore services. Domestic Cypriot legal/advocacy services supplied to Cyprus private clients remain exempt from VAT under Article 132(1)(m) of the VAT Directive transposed via Item 14 of Part 1 of the First Schedule of the Cyprus VAT Law, provided the conditions of professional privilege and exemption compliance are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronically lodged via Cyprus Customs; including the electronic SAD and exporter's reference number)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note (air/road) evidencing dispatch from Cyprus/EU territory
  • Commercial Invoice (bilingual or English, with Incoterms and complete exporter/importer details)
  • Proof of dispatch and transportation (delivery or assignment evidence, insurance certificates, packing list, customs transit documents)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advices evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer's valid EU VAT number, VIES validation print-out, EC Sales List (VAT 305) confirmation and the accompanying B2B service agreement/engagement letter in litigation matters
  • Audited underlying litigation case file and fee schedule referencing the IOLTA trust accounting rails where cross-border client funds are handled

As of 2025 Cyprus does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime; e-invoicing is permissive in any agreed electronic format, with PEPPOL BIS 3.0 compliance currently required only for suppliers invoicing the Cyprus public sector under EU Directive 2014/55/EU. All VAT returns (Form VAT 300), EC Sales Lists (Form VAT 305) and Intrastat declarations must be submitted digitally through the ARIADNI/TaxForAll portal; monthly or quarterly filing cadence applies based on turnover. Mandatory real-time e-invoicing and digital reporting requirements derived from the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) package are anticipated from 2030 but are not yet in force in domestic legislation.

Cyprus imposes no formal exchange-control repatriation deadline, but the Tax Department and Cyprus Customs treat the 180-day inward-remittance benchmark as the evidentiary norm for demonstrating genuine export proceeds; failure to evidence payment exposes the 0% rate to retrospective clawback at the standard 19% VAT with statutory interest at 9% per annum and penalties under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law 1983 — starting at 5% of the deficient tax for minor failure, escalating to 20% for administrative default and up to 40% or more plus criminal fines and imprisonment for fraudulent or negligent false claims. Under Section 63 of the Customs and Excise Law 2004, knowingly false export declarations additionally attract seizure of goods, fines up to three times the duty and VAT evaded, personal director liability, and publication of the taxpayer in the Tax Department's non-compliance register; all zero-rating claims remain open for audit typically six years, extended to ten years in cases of suspected fraud.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible. Cyprus applies unrestricted current and capital account liberalisation under EU harmonised law. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, fee repatriation, and liquidation proceeds can be transferred freely without exchange-control approval. The only operative hurdles are UN/EU sanctions screening, CRS/FATCA reporting, and the necessity of a Cyprus Tax Department tax-clearance or tax-residency certificate to release large outbound wires.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Cyprus maintains no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime and no mandatory conversion ratio. Foreign-currency receivables from cross-border litigation services may be retained, credited, or freely remitted in EUR or any convertible currency; only periodic balance-of-payments and external-sector statistical declarations to the Central Bank of Cyprus may be triggered for significant transactions.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou) — the national monetary authority and prudential banking supervisor; foreign exchange policy is determined by EU Treaty free-movement-of-capital provisions (TFEU Article 63) and Eurosystem membership, with the Central Bank of Cyprus retaining supervisory and statistical reporting powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Cyprus-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passported under PSD2 and supervised by the Central Bank of Cyprus may supply multi-currency virtual IBANs and are viable for ancillary operational flows and FX conversion. However, offshore non-EU digital EMIs are legally constrained: they cannot hold litigation client trust funds without a Cypriot banking licence or equivalent EU authorisation, and the Central Bank of Cyprus classifies unregulated third-country payment institutions as high-risk counterparties requiring enhanced FATF-style due diligence and often proving impractical for court-security or escrow deposits.

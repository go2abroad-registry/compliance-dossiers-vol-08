Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), operating under the Ministry of Finance; electronic taxpayer portal: ARIADNE (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy). VAT and corporate income tax are administered by the Tax Department; customs and excise matters are administered separately by the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department (Τελωνεία).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Incorporate the Private Limited Company with the Registrar of Companies under Companies Law Cap. 113 to obtain the Hellenic Company Registration Number (HE77 CRN). Step 2 – Submit Form T.A.X. 1010 (Application for Tax Identification Number) with the competent District Tax Office; the Tax Department issues a TIN automatically upon incorporation and after the submission of the registration form; the company must also file an employer registration. Step 3 – For VAT, submit Form T.A.X. 1012 (Application for VAT Registration) electronically via ARIADNE or at the District VAT Office; the VAT identification number is issued in the format CY + 8 digits + check digit. Step 4 – Foreign-incorporated entities establishing a permanent establishment in Cyprus must register with the Tax Department within 60 days of commencement of activity; non-established foreign suppliers of goods or services in Cyprus must register before the first taxable supply and must appoint a tax representative in Cyprus for VAT compliance if they have no place of residence or establishment in the EU. Step 5 – After registration, the taxpayer will be included in the VIES database for intra-EU transactions and in the local VAT registry for export clearance purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope – a Cyprus resident company is a separate legal entity subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide income, with a territorial overlay: dividends, interest, and certain royalties received from abroad are exempt under the participation/non-business income provisions of Section 8(4) of the Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002. The entity is not a pass-through vehicle; profits are taxed at the entity level and distributions to non-resident shareholders are not subject to withholding tax. Deemed Dividend Distribution Rules (Section 61) may apply to tax undistributed profits at the shareholder level if non-resident shareholders receive loans or other benefits.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style entity classification regime exists in Cyprus. Available statutory elections and rulings include: (i) Group relief election (Section 24 of Income Tax Law) permitting transfer of tax losses among 75%+ group members; (ii) accelerated capital allowance election per asset class; (iii) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital (suspended from 1 January 2024 pending EU state aid review); (iv) Intellectual Property (IP) box election (Section 23E) at 80% eligible deduction for qualifying intangible assets; (v) Tonnage tax election for shipping activities; (vi) binding advance tax ruling requests to the Tax Department; and (vii) silent compliance adjustments under the hybrid mismatch rules (Law 118(I)/2002, Section 17A) implementing ATAD 2 – no affirmative elective mechanism is available where the transaction falls within a hybrid mismatch; the taxpayer must proactively adjust or be subject to disallowance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 (EUR) annual taxable turnover for businesses established in Cyprus; no registration threshold applies for non-established suppliers, who must register from the first taxable supply; €10,000 threshold for intra-Community acquisitions of goods; the former €35,000 distance sales threshold has been subsumed into the One-Stop-Shop (OSS/IOSS) schemes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to (i) goods physically exported from Cyprus to a destination outside the EU, provided the goods leave the EU within 90 days of supply and are evidenced by a validated customs export declaration (Section 11(1)(i) VAT Law); and (ii) supplies of services directly related to exported goods and paid by the non-EU purchaser, where the place of supply is outside Cyprus under the B2B general rule (customer establishment rule, Section 15 VAT Law). For reverse logistics and returned merchandise grading: services provided to a non-EU principal (owner of the goods) are generally outside the scope of Cyprus VAT if the customer is established in a third country; where returned goods are re-exported to a third country without being released for free circulation in the EU, zero-rating or customs warehousing treatment (Article 33 VAT Law) is available if the exporter holds full customs supervision and export documentation. The claim requires that the goods have not been consumed or disposed of in Cyprus and that the exporter retains an audit trail from receipt, grading, storage, and re-export to final destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document submitted through the WIN/ECS system; evidence of MRN, customs office of exit, and 'Export completed' status)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR (transport document evidencing physical departure of the goods from the EU to the third country destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank or licensed payment services provider documentation evidencing settlement of the export proceeds in convertible currency, including SWIFT receipts or Airwallex transaction receipts)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based clearance, or digital stamping mandate is currently in force for Cyprus. Standard paper and electronic invoicing are permitted; e-invoicing is voluntary, with Peppol interoperability encouraged for B2B transactions. Cyprus is preparing for the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) framework, and the Tax Department has announced future implementation of electronic invoice registration and reporting, but no operational date has been legislated. For export transactions, mandatory electronic filing is required through the Customs WIN/Single Administrative Document (SAD) system via the Cyprus Customs ECS; VIES validation is required for intra-EU supplies of goods; retail cash transactions fall under the EKAS electronic cash register system, which does not apply to a reverse logistics B2B operation.

Improper or fraudulent zero-rating claims expose the exporter to a retrospective VAT assessment at the standard rate (19%) on the full export consideration, plus statutory interest at 2% per annum for corporate income tax assessments and 1% per annum for VAT arrears. Administrative penalties may reach €3,000 per offence under Section 98 of the VAT Law; where intent to evade tax is established, a penalty of 100% of the VAT assessed may be imposed, along with criminal liability (imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to €10,000). The Tax Department and Customs may jointly conduct post-clearance audits, invoke the six-year documentary retention rule, and, in cases of non-compliance with the EU Customs Code (UCC), issue customs debt notices reclassifying the zero-rating as a taxable domestic supply; a pattern of unsupported zero-ratings will trigger inclusion in the customs risk management database and enhanced scanning of all future export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a euro-area member state; the euro is fully convertible for cross-border transactions and the free movement of capital applies under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There are no exchange-control permits, no prior approval requirements for current-account payments, and no general restrictions on profit remittance. Cyprus does not maintain separate capital vs. current-account convertibility regimes; however, distributions to shareholders remain subject to the relevant corporate law formalities, and any inbound or outbound payments must comply with EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering, and information-accompanying-transfer requirements. Dividend withholding and Special Defence Contribution may arise only where the recipient is a Cyprus tax-resident individual or where an EU-tax-transparent entity is involved; payments to most non-resident corporate shareholders are free of Cyprus withholding tax under domestic law and applicable EU directives.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules and no mandatory conversion ratios in Cyprus. Exporters and cross-border logistics providers may retain receivables in any freely accepted currency, maintain foreign-currency and euro accounts freely, and convert at market rates. Entities are only obliged to file periodic balance-of-payments statistical reports with the Central Bank of Cyprus when transaction volumes cross the applicable reporting thresholds, and such filings are for statistical purposes only.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου / Central Bank of Cyprus, CBC), operating within the European System of Central Banks and under the prudential and supervisory framework established by the Single Supervisory Mechanism for euro-area credit institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility is High for auxiliary treasury functions, particularly for inbound/outbound cross-border payment routing, virtual IBANs, and multi-currency settlement; Airwallex, as an EU-authorised Electronic Money Institution passporting into Cyprus, can support reverse-logistics payouts and local ACH collection. Feasibility is Medium as the sole treasury provider because an EMI may not take deposits or provide credit, must safeguard customer funds in a segregated account at a regulated credit institution, and cannot fully replace the statutory account infrastructure expected by the Cyprus Tax Department and the Central Bank for capital verification. Legal constraints: the EMI must be passport-eligible under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the E-Money Directive, must file suspicious-transaction reports, and must comply with Regulation (EU) 2015/847 with respect to originator/beneficiary information on transfers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital should be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the parent's bank to the Cyprus entity's operational or capital escrow account, with a precise payment narrative: 'SOCIE - share capital contribution', 'loan capital', or 'invoice settlement' together with the invoice reference. The sender must supply full originator and beneficiary names, addresses, and account numbers to comply with Regulation (EU) 2015/847. The receiving Cyprus bank will verify the corporate authority, UBO documentation, underlying source of funds, and may classify the inflow for Balance-of-Payments statistical reporting. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (standard corporate income tax rate under Cyprus Income Tax Law, L.118(I)/2002, as amended)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Cyprus domestic law imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders). Special Defence Contribution at 17% applies only to certain dividend and interest income of Cyprus tax-resident recipients; non-resident shareholders are exempt from the levy.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return due within 12 months from the end of the tax year (i.e., for a 31 December year-end, due by 31 December of the following year). Final tax balance payable by the same date; provisional tax paid in two equal installments on 31 July and 31 December of the current tax year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Installment 1 due 31 July and Installment 2 due 31 December of the tax year. If the tax year is not the calendar year, installments fall at the end of the 6th and 12th month from commencement of the accounting period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where the aggregate value of controlled transactions of the Cyprus entity exceeds EUR 750,000 in a financial year; Master File required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000; Country-by-Country Report required for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750,000,000. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and delivered to the Tax Department within 30 days of a formal notice (60 days for the Master File).

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not apply a separate ESR law like the Cayman Islands; however, economic substance is mandatory through: (a) the management and control test for tax residence (central management and control in Cyprus); (b) the IP Box nexus ratio requiring qualifying R&D functions in Cyprus; (c) the General Anti-Abuse Rule (section 33 of the Income Tax Law) which permits re-characterization of sham arrangements; and (d) CbCR and ATAD rules requiring actual people, premises, and decision-making. A reverse logistics company must maintain a physical office, local employees, board meetings in Cyprus, and audited accounts; otherwise treaty benefits and the 12.5% rate can be denied and the entity reclassified.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years; civil reassessment with interest and additional charge of 10%; publication of the taxpayer's name by the Commissioner of Taxation; disqualification of the company from state contracts.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve a false return may be jointly and severally prosecuted; personal fines and imprisonment may be imposed; courts may issue restitution orders and freeze or travel-ban directors pending trial.
Shell Company and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax residence for treaty purposes; reallocation of profits to the jurisdiction of effective management; reassessment at commercial rates with interest; de-registration and striking-off under the Companies Law Cap. 113; GAAR penalties apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed directly where they knowingly structured the sham; personal liability for tortious misrepresentation; disqualification orders for up to 3 years; directors may be held personally liable for collection of the reassessed taxes in cases of wrongful trading.
Unlicensed Payment Activity / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Electronic Money and Payment Services Law (L.98(I)/2018 as amended): unauthorized provision of payment services may incur a fine up to EUR 350,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; AML Law (L.13(I)/1996) fines up to EUR 1,000,000 for obliged entities and named individuals; daily fines for continuing violations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly operate without a license face personal criminal liability and fines; they may also be held civilly liable to affected consumers; the Central Bank may issue individual administrative fines and prohibition orders from financial services.
Transfer Pricing Evasion and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to the taxable basis at the arm's length price; secondary adjustment with withholding classification; interest on the underpaid tax; administrative fine up to EUR 500 for each missing TP document; possible criminal proceedings if documentation is falsified.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved non-arm's length arrangements are not automatically personally liable for TP adjustments, but if the adjustment is accompanied by a criminal finding of intent to evade tax, personal fines and imprisonment may be imposed; directors may also be liable for wrongful distributions if adjustments render the company insolvent.

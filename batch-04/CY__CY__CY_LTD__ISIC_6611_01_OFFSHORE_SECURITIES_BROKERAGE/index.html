Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας, Υπουργείο Οικονομικών); official e-filing portal: TaxisNet at https://taxdep.mof.gov.cy; physical offices located at 175 Griva Digeni Avenue, 1463 Nicosia; the Tax Department operates under the Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002, Cap 262 as amended) and the Value Added Tax Law (Law 95(I)/2000).

E-government filing portal

For a Cyprus Private Limited Company (ELF code HE77): (1) The Cyprus Registrar of Companies issues a company registration number (HE.../SS...) upon incorporation; this number is automatically transmitted to the Tax Department in Nicosia. (2) The Tax Department then assigns a Tax Identification Number (TIN/TIC — Tax Identification Code) to the newly incorporated resident company without any separate application; the TIC is used for income tax, social insurance, and other national filings. (3) For VAT purposes, the company must submit Form TD 501 (Application for VAT Registration) to the competent District VAT Office (or via TaxisNet) within 30 days of exceeding the EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover threshold, or immediately if it intends to make intra-EU acquisitions or distance sales; a VAT ID is issued in the format CY + 9 digits. (4) Because the brokerage activity is VAT-exempt financial services, VAT registration is typically not mandatory for pure brokerage commissions; however, registration is recommended to recover input VAT on overheads where the entity supplies both taxable and exempt services (pro-rata deduction under Article 62 of the VAT Law). (5) A non-resident foreign company without a Cyprus establishment must appoint a fiscal representative and obtain both a TIN and a VAT ID from the Tax Department before conducting taxable supplies in Cyprus; the appointment is registered on Form TD 501A.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Cyprus-resident companies are taxed at 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income (Section 21 Income Tax Law). Key statutory classifications applicable to CY_LTD: (a) Dividends received from foreign subsidiaries are fully exempt under Section 8(2)(a), and gains from the disposal of equities and securities are fully exempt under Section 8(2)(c), making Cyprus a 'participation-exemption' jurisdiction. (b) Territorial_Scope: non-resident companies (management and control outside Cyprus) are taxed only on Cyprus-sourced income. (c) Foreign permanent establishment profits are exempt under Section 8(21) where the foreign PE has been subject to foreign tax of at least 5%. (d) Tax_Exempt_Zone: none; no domestic tax-haven enclave or offshore ring-fence regime exists. (e) For ISIC 6611 securities brokerage, the activity is treated as ordinary trading income, subject to 12.5% CIT, unless the equity-disposal exemption applies on the sale of the brokerage's own long-term equity participations.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' transparent entity election; all private limited companies are opaque taxpayers (Entity_Level_Taxation). Available statutory elections under Cypriot law: (1) IP Box Regime (Section 11B IT Law): a company may elect to deduct 80% of qualifying intangible gross royalty income, resulting in an effective 2.5% CIT rate on qualifying IP. (2) Notional Interest Deduction (Section 9B IT Law): a taxable person may elect to deduct a notional 5-year interest cost on newly injected equity capital in excess of issued share capital, capped at the 10-year government bond yield (approx. 0.2%-1.8% in practice). (3) Deemed Dividend Distribution (Section 33A IT Law): an election/compulsory rule whereby a company that fails to distribute at least 70% of realised accounting profits within two years is deemed to have distributed the difference to shareholders; where all shareholders are non-residents, the deemed distribution has no Cyprus special defence contribution (17%) or GHS (2.65%) exposure. (4) VAT Grouping (Article 11 of VAT Law): election for two or more related Cyprus entities to register as a single taxable person and ignore intra-group supplies. (5) Foreign Tax Credit vs. PE Exemption (Section 31): for foreign branches subject to tax less than 5%, the company may elect to claim a foreign tax credit instead of the exemption. (6) Hybrid Mismatch Elections: none are available — Cyprus has implemented ATAD I and ATAD II (Law 119(I)/2019) with mandatory disallowance rules; no elective hybrid relief exists. Companies may, however, elect to apply the 'grandfathering' provisions for pre-existing hybrid instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 in annual taxable turnover (for supplies of goods and services made in the course of business within any 12-month period). Intra-EU distance sales of goods and digital/electronic services are subject to the separate EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold (OSS scheme). Achievement of the threshold triggers mandatory VAT registration within 30 days; a person may also register voluntarily. For a Cyprus offshore securities brokerage, commissions and margin interest from clients are primarily VAT-exempt financial services under Article 135(1)(a)-(f), which do not count toward the EUR 15,600 threshold; however, ancillary management fees, advisory fees, and market-data fees may be taxable at 19% once the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cyprus applies 0% VAT in the following scenarios: (A) Goods exported to destinations outside the EU — the export zero-rating is available if the goods physically leave Cyprus and the EU within 90 days of the supply, and the supplier retains appropriate documentary evidence. (B) Intra-EU B2B transfers/removals — zero-rated where the customer declares a valid EU VAT ID and the goods are dispatched to another Member State (with weekly ESL reporting). (C) Services supplied to non-EU B2B recipients — under Article 58 of the VAT Law, the place of supply for brokerage and financial services to a business established outside the EU is the recipient's jurisdiction; hence, no Cyprus VAT is chargeable (the supply is 'outside scope' rather than zero-rated). (D) Specific services listed under Article 143(1)(a)-(g) (e.g., services directly connected with exported goods, cultural, digital and consultancy services provided to non-EU consumers) are zero-rated. For ISIC 6611 (retail equities, CFDs and derivatives brokerage), the core execution and margin services are exempt (Article 135(1)(a)-(f)) rather than zero-rated, which means input VAT on costs is not recoverable unless the recipient is established outside the EU (in which case the supply is outside the scope of Cypriot VAT and the pro-rata deduction rules allow recovery). The company must maintain accurate counterparty documentation (place-of-establishment certificate, passport, corporate registration extract) to defend the outside-scope treatment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD — Single Administrative Document) filed electronically via the EU Customs System (THESIS/Customs Trader Portal), evidencing the export procedure and customs office of exit
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) — transport document showing the consignee, port/airport of discharge outside the EU, and the actual date of shipment
  • Commercial Invoice or Pro-forma Invoice stating the Incoterms 2020 rules and the export zero-rating or outside-scope basis of the supply
  • Inward Remittance Confirmation / Bank SWIFT MT103 or MT202 proving payment of consideration in foreign currency from the offshore client account (substitute for a formal Inward Remittance Certificate, which is not issued in Cyprus; exchange controls were abolished in 2015)
  • For brokerage services: executed Brokerage Contract / Terms of Business, the Know-Your-Client file confirming non-EU establishment of the counterparty, and the Trade Confirmation Note (TCN) identifying the securities, commission and settlement date

As of the current mandatory compliance horizon (2025), Cyprus does not operate a real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance mandate and imposes no digital stamping or automated fiscal approval for VAT invoices. E-invoicing is voluntary and accepted in any format (PDF, XML or via the EU EN 16931 semantic standard). Mandatory e-invoicing applies exclusively to B2G (business-to-government) contracts through the Cypriot Government e-Invoicing Portal, introduced in 2021. The Tax Department operates an obligatory electronic filing system (TaxisNet) for all VAT returns (Form VAT 501), Intrastat declarations, and recipient reporting (ESL/IC sales lists) for intra-EU supplies. Under the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) legislative package, Cyprus is scheduled to introduce mandatory domestic and cross-border e-invoicing with real-time transaction reporting by 2030–2035, but no earlier national mandate is currently in force.

No statutory deadline is imposed for repatriation of export proceeds, given the complete abolition of Cyprus exchange controls on 1 January 2015. The Tax Department's audit practice supports a documentary chain showing that export proceeds have been received within the normal 180-day commercial cycle; absent such evidence, the zero-rating can be challenged. For a CySEC-regulated securities brokerage, client money must be segregated and returned upon demand under Regulation (EU) 2017/565 (executive deadline: T+2 for standard settlement; no FX-repatriation deadline applies to client money). Penalties for fraudulent export zero-rating claims: (a) a VAT assessment recovering the 19% VAT plus statutory interest of 6.5% per annum from the date the VAT fell due; (b) administrative penalties under Article 57 of the VAT Law of up to EUR 1,708 (formerly CYP 1,000) for each offence; (c) additional financial penalties of up to 100% of the VAT evaded in cases of systematic abuse; (d) criminal exposure: directors may face imprisonment of up to three years for fraudulent evasion, and conviction leads to retrospective clawback of all input VAT recovered on the disputed supplies; (e) CySEC may separately impose administrative fines of up to EUR 700,000 or 10% of annual turnover on the brokerage for documentation failures affecting client money reconciliation. In practice, the Tax Department starts a 6-year limitation period (10 years in fraud) and performs retrospective risk-based VAT audits specific to zero-rated supplies, demanding the full customs documentation bundle above.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is the lawful currency of Cyprus; there are no general exchange-control restrictions or quantitative limits on capital or current account convertibility under EU Treaty provisions (TFEU Articles 63–66). Profit remittance is unrestricted; dividends and branch profits may be freely repatriated, subject only to standard corporate proof, any applicable double-taxation treaty and AML source-of-funds verification. The 2013 systemic banking resolution restrictions were lifted and no residual capital controls remain.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio or surrender requirement for export proceeds in Cyprus. Export receipts may be held, converted or repatriated freely through commercial banks. However, supporting invoicing and trade documentation must be retained for VAT/customs purposes and the Central Bank of Cyprus requires balance-of-payments data (via the ITRS/BOP reporting obligation) for cross-border transactions, generally for transfers above EUR 12,500.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) acting within the Eurosystem as a national component of the European Central Bank (ECB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. EU-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions passported into Cyprus may provide multi-currency IBANs, instant payment rails and faster onboarding. With reference to a CySEC-licensed offshore securities broker, EMIs are inadequate as a principal banking venue because their internal AML policies typically decline the 'brokerage/broker' sector, they cannot robustly segregate client money under CIF obligations, and they lack SWIFT correspondent depth, escrow support and FX hedging execution. The recommended supplementary role is limited to alternative retail collection rails (e.g., low-value card acquirers) where client money is subsequently swept to a tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: SWIFT MT103 / TARGET2 settlement with full remitter information. For equity capital injections, use ISO purpose code 'S0301' (Acquisition of shares) or ECB BOP category 'S02' (Direct investment); for shareholder loans use 'S0302' (Loans); for initial client-money funding use 'S0305'. Remitter must provide a board resolution, source-of-funds file and the own-investment declaration. Cyprus banks require the depositor to complete the ITRS/BoP statistical form for any cross-border amount exceeding EUR 12,500. For client funds outflows, the Cyprus Investment Firm must comply with the CIF client-money segregation and title-transfer rules and use the exact bank account pre-notified to the client. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. EU-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions passported into Cyprus may provide multi-currency IBANs, instant payment rails and faster onboarding. With reference to a CySEC-licensed offshore securities broker, EMIs are inadequate as a principal banking venue because their internal AML policies typically decline the 'brokerage/broker' sector, they cannot robustly segregate client money under CIF obligations, and they lack SWIFT correspondent depth, escrow support and FX hedging execution. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 aggregate annual controlled transactions (goods, services, financial transactions, royalties) triggers the mandatory Local File under the Cyprus Transfer Pricing Decree (Regulative Administrative Act 244/2022). A Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies an economic substance doctrine derived from the EU Code of Conduct and Article 33 of the Income Tax Law. For an offshore securities broker, the Cyprus Tax Authority and CySEC expect full substance: a physical office, full-time qualified trading and compliance staff, genuine board meetings and a material level of client onboarding and order execution in Cyprus. The OECD nexus approach governs the IP box regime. Failure to maintain substance may lead to reattribution of profits under transfer pricing, denial of treaty benefits, decertification of tax residency and regulatory license cancellation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Section 80 of the Cyprus Income Tax Law: on conviction, a fine of up to EUR 10,000, unlimited civil exposure for unpaid tax plus penalties of up to 200% of the tax deficiency, and disqualification from the TaxisNet electronic filing system.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency if they signed the defective return or knowingly benefited; criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to 2 years; courts may impose travel bans, asset freezes, restriction orders and a 10-year ban on company management.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: The Tax Commissioner is empowered to disregard the corporate entity under the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) implementing ATAD, applying tax at the natural owner's marginal rate plus a 10% surcharge; CySEC will revoke the brokerage licence for false licensing declarations; the company is exposed to de-registration under the Companies Law.

Director personal liability: Directors face disqualification under CySEC regulatory sanctions (up to EUR 350,000 per violation), personal civil liability for client assets, and possible criminal charges for obtaining a licence by false representation.
AML/Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus AML Law (L.188(I)/2007), the company may be fined up to EUR 250,000 and its licence revoked; assets are subject to freezing and confiscation orders; CySEC may impose a public censure and prohibit the firm from accepting new clients for a defined period.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to implement the AML Risk Management Framework are personally guilty of an offence punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 50,000; they can be held tortiously liable for losses caused to defrauded clients and sanctioned with travel bans pending judicial investigation.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Authority may make a transfer pricing adjustment under the Cyprus Transfer Pricing Decree; the adjusted profit is taxed at 12.5%, with interest at 3.5% per annum and a 10% dormant-file penalty; the TP documentation file is confiscated and any resulting loss is disregarded. The licence may be suspended pending re-filing.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or benefited from the mispricing may be personally assessed to the extent of the adjustment if the corporate veil is pierced; in aggravated cases of deliberate mispricing, criminal prosecution for tax fraud can lead to 2 years' imprisonment and restitution orders.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus Private Limited Company (HE77) is incorporated by the Department of Registrar of Companies under the Companies Law and receives a Company Registration Number (HE123456). Concurrently, it must register with the Tax Department for a 9-digit Tax Identification Code (TIC), completing the standard corporate registration duty, and, once it makes taxable supplies, must apply for a VAT number in the format 'CY' + 8 digits + 1 alphabetic check digit using Form TD1013. Non-resident foreign entities engaged in Cyprus-source trade or services must obtain a TIC, appoint a Cyprus tax agent and submit certified constitutional documents, director/shareholder particulars and registered office address. A non-established taxable person with no EU mutual-assistance arrangement must also appoint a Cyprus VAT representative; registration applies from the first euro of taxable turnover without a threshold, subject to the EU non-established taxable person scheme under Council Regulation (EU) No 967/2012.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; the Ltd is statutorily opaque, taxed at 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income if managed and controlled in Cyprus, or only on Cyprus-source income if tax resident elsewhere. Available elections and options: (i) VAT grouping election under Art. 20 of the VAT Law 94(I)/2000 (Form TD1024); (ii) group relief surrender of losses between 75% affiliates under s.22 of the Income Tax Law; (iii) Intellectual Property Box election under s.13(C) providing an 80% deduction for qualifying IP-derived profits; (iv) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) on new equity under s.9C; (v) foreign permanent establishment exemption under s.8(23), where profits taxed abroad at a rate of at least 50% of the Cyprus 12.5% rate; (vi) hybrid mismatch and interest limitation adjustments are mandatory under ATAD (no elective timing).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus operates under the euro as its sole legal tender. There is no residual exchange control foreign exchange law for current or capital account transactions; capital is fully and freely convertible throughout the Eurozone and cross-border into third countries, subject only to EU secondary sanctions regimes and the EU Anti-Money Laundering Directive (AMLD) obligations. Dividends, profits, royalties, and re-injected capital may be remitted offshore without exchange-control approval or converting mandated through licensed authorised dealers; the only withholding applicable on profit remittance is a determinable exemption under the Cyprus tax framework for non-resident shareholders, with no statutory obstacles or queuing mechanisms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirements exist. Euro-area membership abolished export-earnings repatriation mandates; export invoices may be settled freely in EUR or any hard currency, and receivables may be held, converted, or netted without central bank authorisation or mandatory sale to domestic banking entities. Export proceeds are reported solely for statistical Balance of Payments purposes under the ECB framework through the Central Bank of Cyprus monthly reporting system, with no compliance penalty for conversion timing.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), which acts as the national monetary authority and micro-prudential banking supervisor within the European System of Central Banks; foreign exchange policy competence rests with the European Central Bank (ECB) by virtue of Cyprus's Eurozone membership.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High as ancillary collection and vendor-payout rails, but Medium to Low as primary treasury infrastructure. Wise (B2B), Payoneer, and similar EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) that are authorised or passporting under PSD2 (transposed by the Cyprus Electronic Money Law 81(I)/2018) may legally open segregated client sub-accounts for a Cyprus-incorporated entity and process EUR/USD receipts. However, a software services company should retain a Tier-1 licensed credit institution account as the core settlement venue; EMI balances do not carry deposit protection, are subject to unilateral wind-down risk, and may trigger heightened remediation checks under a correspondent bank. Legal constraint: EMI accounts cannot substitute for statutory capital-escrow or formal invoicing rails in cross-border licensing arrangements, and directors remain liable for treasury oversight regardless of the fintech provider selected.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing uses direct SWIFT MT103 or TARGET2 payments from the UK/US parent or shareholder, referencing the ECB Balance of Payments purpose code 'AC' for share-capital subscriptions or 'ALD' for direct-investment loans between related enterprises. The receiving Cypriot bank will verify the underlying company receipt, the board-approved resolution, the CRIM filing evidencing subscribed capital, and the AML-completed source-of-funds dossier before crediting the Capital Escrow Account. EUR transfers from EU counterparties should be sent as SEPA Credit Transfers (SCT) using the IBAN of the Cyprus entity; USD inbound flows must be transmitted through correspondent banks (normally via a UK or New York clearer), with the CIA (Customer Information Approval) and SWIFT field 50K matching the exact registered entity name, and account-holder title 'CY_LTD' without abbreviations.Primary outbound clearing rail for vendor, payroll, tax, and intercompany settlements is SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR, executed via SWIFTNet FIN or EBICS-enabled channels at the Tier-1 Cyprus bank. For larger B2B software procurement or service settlements, irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) may be issued/confirmed by the Cyprus bank for non-EU operations, although IT services are typically executed on open-account terms supported by invoicing and remittance data using the ISO 20022 pain.001/pain.002 messages. Salary disbursements for the Offshore Development Centre run through a dedicated batch payroll rail with pre-agreed corporate-vs-employee account segregation; all mandated tax and SDC (Special Defence Contribution) social-insurance remittances are cleared through the Cyprus Tax Department's JCC electronic payments gateway.Deliverable EUR/USD or EUR/USD/GBP FX Forward Contracts (vanilla outright forwards) traded under an ISDA-cleared or bilateral master agreement with a licensed credit institutionFX Swap (FX Swap/Swap-Next or Spot-Forward rolls) to manage periodic mismatches between USD-denominated client invoices and EUR-denominated payroll obligationsCross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CCIRS) for extending hedging on the shareholder-loan principal and shadow interest flows, where the currency basis is hedged under ISDA 2002FX Options (European-style call/put pairs) purchased on an unfunded option-premium basis via the bank's derivatives deskNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are expressly NOT permissible or necessary for the EUR currency pair given full euro convertibility, and would be rejected by compliance for lack of legitimate hedging rationale

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Cyprus-resident entity must prepare and maintain a Local File when its aggregate related-party transactions in a tax year exceed EUR 750,000. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue is at least EUR 750 million, or in line with local implementation of OECD BEPS Action 13. The Cyprus ODC should maintain a full transfer pricing study regardless of the threshold if intercompany service fees are paid to a non-resident group entity.

Economic substance scope: Although Cyprus has not implemented the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, the Cyprus Tax Department applies OECD and EU anti-abuse standards, including ATAD and DAC 6. For an Offshore Development Center, economic substance is proven by: a physical office in Cyprus, a locally resident qualified technical team, management meetings and board decisions in Cyprus, real contracts with the foreign parent, and local payroll and operating costs proportionate to the business. The absence of such substance is treated as a shell company/artificial arrangement, leading to denial of tax residency, rejection of TRC applications, recharacterisation of intercompany transactions, withdrawal of treaty benefits, and potential CFC attribution to non-resident shareholders.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at 12.5% on the evaded profit; interest at 1% per month; administrative penalties up to 100% of the tax understated; criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 50,000.

Director personal liability: Cyprus courts may pierce the corporate veil when directors engage in fraud; directors, shadow directors, and beneficial owners who direct evasion can be jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax, face travel bans, asset freezes, and confiscation of personal property.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of Cyprus tax residency and TRC; denial of treaty benefits; reclassification of the company as non-resident; CFC legislation may tax the foreign parent in its home jurisdiction; fines for filing false declarations under the Cyprus Companies Law and tax law.

Director personal liability: Directors of a façade company can be held personally liable to creditors and tax authorities; the corporate veil may be pierced, ordering directors to repay diverted funds; criminal sanctions apply for knowingly maintaining a sham entity.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing primary adjustment adding undercharged income to the Cyprus taxable base; secondary penalty up to 20% of the primary adjustment; interest at 1% per month; administrative fines for missing TP documentation; denial of deduction for affected expenses.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's length pricing may be liable to the company for damages, subject to claw-back actions by liquidators, and may be criminally prosecuted if the mispricing is part of an evasion scheme.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law, imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 500,000 (legal entities: up to EUR 2 million); CySEC or supervisory fines if operating without a required licence; asset seizure and forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and senior managers can be personally indicted for AML breaches; travel bans, passport surrender, and freezing of personal assets are routinely granted in serious cases; directors cannot hide behind the corporate entity.
Failure to File / Late Filing

Statutory sanction: Flat late-filing penalty of EUR 100; monthly default penalty of EUR 50 per month up to EUR 500; interest at 1% per month on unpaid tax; possible strike-off of the company for continued non-filing; no time limit for tax assessments in cases of fundamental failure to file.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to file are personally responsible for penalties if the company is insolvent; the director of a struck-off company may be disqualified and held liable for the company's obligations arising from continued trading.

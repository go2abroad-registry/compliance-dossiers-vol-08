Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (TDC), operating under the Ministry of Finance; electronic filing and tax administration portal: TAXISnet (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company obtains its Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN) automatically upon incorporation after filing the Certificate of Incorporation and the relevant corporate details with the Tax Department. For VAT purposes, the company must submit an electronic VAT registration application through TAXISnet; non-established suppliers without a Cyprus permanent establishment are required to appoint a fiscal representative or use the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) where applicable. Supporting documents include the memorandum and articles of association, certificate of incorporation, proof of registered address, identity details of directors/shareholders, and description of intended economic activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a Cyprus private limited company is a separate taxable person subject to income tax at 12.5% under the Cyprus Income Tax Law. It is taxed on worldwide income with statutory exemptions for certain foreign-source dividends, foreign permanent establishment profits, and capital gains outside Cyprus. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Cyprus private limited company is a separate taxable person subject to income tax at 12.5% under the Cyprus Income Tax Law. It is taxed on worldwide income with statutory exemptions for certain foreign-source dividends, foreign permanent establishment profits, and capital gains outside Cyprus. No pass-through treatment is available to a limited liability company.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election. Entity classification is determined by domestic law and management-control factual analysis. Available elections include: (i) voluntary VAT registration or VAT grouping election with the Tax Department; (ii) application to join the EU OSS scheme for B2C cross-border supplies of goods and services; and (iii) notification of an IP Box incentive election for qualifying intangible assets. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD override any elective treatment designed to create double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 annual taxable turnover for established Cyprus businesses (small enterprise exemption threshold). For non-established suppliers and for distance sales of goods to Cyprus consumers through e-commerce, there is no registration threshold; the EU cross-border OSS has been fully electronic since 1 July 2021, with no per-country low-value threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cyprus applies 0% VAT to supplies of goods physically exported outside the European Union, provided the supplier retains evidence that the goods have left the EU within a commercially reasonable time. For pet supplies and pet-care devices sold online and dispatched by courier, postal, or freight forwarder, zero-rating applies when title passes and export customs clearance is made. Services of an electronic or offshore nature are zero-rated if supplied to a non-EU recipient or if the services are directly related to exported goods; B2B services to EU customers remain subject to the customer's reverse-charge rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Single Administrative Document / AES/ECS)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from the EU
  • CMR consignment note, courier receipt, or postal dispatch certificate for low-value or consolidated shipments
  • Commercial invoice and Incoterms contract referencing the customer and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit confirmation showing the foreign sales proceeds received

Cyprus does not currently mandate real-time clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) APIs, or digital stamping for B2B/B2C e-invoicing. Electronic invoices are legally valid where the authenticity of origin and integrity of content are preserved, but no automatic state clearance is required. Cyprus is preparing B2G e-invoicing interoperability under the European PEPPOL framework; this does not apply to pet-supplies e-commerce sales to consumers.

If an exporter fails to document export removal, the 0% rate is reversed and the supply becomes subject to 19% VAT on the sale value. The Tax Department may, on audit, retrospectively reassess within 5 years (extended to 15 years where misrepresentation or fraud is proved), charge interest at 10% per annum, and impose administrative civil penalties proportional to the tax loss. Fraudulent export zero-rating may also trigger criminal prosecutions and fines against directors and the company.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a Eurozone member (EU accession 1 May 2004; euro adopted 1 January 2008). The euro is fully convertible; there are no residual capital controls, prior-exchange-approval requirements, or import-cover restrictions. Under Article 63 TFEU and EU Directive 88/361/EEC, current-account and capital-account flows are unrestricted, including inward equity injections, profit repatriation, dividend distribution, intercompany loans and liquidation proceeds. The historic Exchange Control Law (Cap. 131) was suspended in 1994 and is no longer operative. Cross-border profit remittance is limited only by ordinary tax compliance: 0% dividend withholding for EU-resident parent companies under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, 17% WHT in domestic situations, and reduced rates under Cyprus tax treaties. No Cyrpus or ECB approval is required to convert EUR to USD/GBP/CNY for settlement of commercial invoices.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus imposes no surrender, mandatory conversion, or time-limited domiciliation rules on export proceeds. EUR and foreign-currency receivables from the e-commerce business may be held indefinitely in Cyprus or foreign bank accounts without penalty. Nevertheless, the CBC requires statistical disclosure of cross-border goods/services transactions through the settlement bank (quarterly BoP survey), and the company must archive acquirer settlement statements from Stripe/PayPal plus customs and logistics documentation to evidence the underlying trade in any CBC or tax audit.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC; Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank (ECB). CBC holds exclusive licensing and prudential supervisory authority for credit institutions, electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions in Cyprus, and administers balance-of-payments (BoP) statistical reporting for all cross-border transactions through the domestic banking channel.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Cyprus-licensed EMIs and EEA-passported EMIs (under the Cyprus Payment Services Law of 2018 implementing PSD2) are permitted to provide IBAN-based settlement and payment-initiation accounts to the Cyprus Ltd, and are operationally viable for collecting Stripe/PayPal payouts. However, post-Brexit UK-only EMIs and third-country EMIs cannot passport into Cyprus; they may operate only through a CBC-approved agent/branch model, which most Tier-1 acceptance banks reject for treasury concentration. The CBC AML framework (L.188(I)/2007) and de-risking practice require that the company maintains a primary settlement account with a licensed Cyprus bank; EMI accounts should be limited to corridor or backup use. Recommendation: Open a Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank account as the master concentration account and use an EU-passported EMI (e.g., Lithuanian or Irish licensed) as a payment-offramp sub-channel only.

Part 1, Box 1 - Company Identification Enter the Cyprus Tax Identification Code, legal name, registered office, incorporation date and principal business activity. A non-resident company with a Cyprus permanent establishment must enter the Cyprus branch TIC and PE address. A non-resident company without a PE should generally confirm non-filing status or claim refund relief if applicable. Late filing triggers fixed default penalties and continuing monthly surcharges plus statutory interest; the Tax Commissioner may issue an ex officio assessment. Incorrect or fraudulent returns carry additional penalties up to 100% of the understated tax and potential criminal prosecution.
Part 1, Box 2 - Accounting Period State the accounting period covered by the return. For most Cyprus companies this is the calendar year. If the non-resident entity prepares consolidated accounts on a different year-end, the Cyprus PE return must still be aligned to the Cyprus tax year, with appropriate temporary allocations. Late filing triggers fixed default penalties and continuing monthly surcharges plus statutory interest; the Tax Commissioner may issue an ex officio assessment. Incorrect or fraudulent returns carry additional penalties up to 100% of the understated tax and potential criminal prosecution.
Part 2, Box 3 - Gross Receipts and Trading Income Report worldwide gross sales and services income, including all e-commerce receipts settled through Stripe, PayPal and other payment gateways, reduced only by refunds, chargebacks and trade discounts. Only Cyprus-source income or income effectively connected with the Cyprus PE is reportable. Platform settlement data must be tied to the tax return independently of bank-account routing. Late filing triggers fixed default penalties and continuing monthly surcharges plus statutory interest; the Tax Commissioner may issue an ex officio assessment. Incorrect or fraudulent returns carry additional penalties up to 100% of the understated tax and potential criminal prosecution.
Part 2, Box 4 - Allowable Expenses and Statutory Adjustments Deduct cost of goods sold, warehouse and logistics costs, payment processing fees, marketing, staff costs, rent, interest and depreciation. Add back non-wholly-and-exclusively incurred expenditure and non-deductible provisions. Expenses must be allocated to the Cyprus PE on an arm's length basis; head-office general costs are deductible only to the extent they relate to actual PE activity and are supported by contemporaneous records. Late filing triggers fixed default penalties and continuing monthly surcharges plus statutory interest; the Tax Commissioner may issue an ex officio assessment. Incorrect or fraudulent returns carry additional penalties up to 100% of the understated tax and potential criminal prosecution.
Part 3, Box 5 - Adjusted Profit or Loss and Exempt Income Compute the adjusted taxable profit or loss. Additional declarations capture exempt dividend income, capital gains on securities, and income already subject to Cyprus Special Defence Contribution where relevant. For non-resident companies, exempt income treatment applies only to income attributable to the Cyprus PE. Foreign passive income routed through Cyprus without commercial activity cannot be claimed as exempt without substance review. Late filing triggers fixed default penalties and continuing monthly surcharges plus statutory interest; the Tax Commissioner may issue an ex officio assessment. Incorrect or fraudulent returns carry additional penalties up to 100% of the understated tax and potential criminal prosecution.
Part 4, Box 6 - Loss Relief and Incentives Enter tax losses carried forward, group relief claims, Notional Interest Deduction and qualifying IP Box deductions where applicable. A Cyprus PE should claim only losses belonging to that PE in Cyprus. The five-year carryforward limit applies to Cypriot-source losses. Late filing triggers fixed default penalties and continuing monthly surcharges plus statutory interest; the Tax Commissioner may issue an ex officio assessment. Incorrect or fraudulent returns carry additional penalties up to 100% of the understated tax and potential criminal prosecution.
Part 5, Box 7 - Tax Computation, Credits and Prepayments Apply the 12.5% rate, calculate foreign tax credits and withholding tax credits, reconcile provisional prepayments and compute the final payable or refundable amount. Foreign tax credit relief is narrow and should be documented through debit notes and source-country receipts where the income is Cyprus-taxable through the PE. Late filing triggers fixed default penalties and continuing monthly surcharges plus statutory interest; the Tax Commissioner may issue an ex officio assessment. Incorrect or fraudulent returns carry additional penalties up to 100% of the understated tax and potential criminal prosecution.
Declaration and Signature The director, authorised officer or appointed tax representative signs the return and confirms accuracy. Non-resident companies must appoint and disclose a Cyprus tax representative with TAXISnet credentials and a valid power of attorney for signature and service of documents. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions requiring a Local File; Master File and Country-by-Country Report required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000; controlled transactions below EUR 750,000 still require a defensible internal pricing record.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus is not a classic ESR regulation jurisdiction, but Cypriot tax residence depends on management and control, and EU/ATAD anti-abuse and BEPS principles are applied. E-commerce and distribution entities must evidence real substance in Cyprus through local board meetings, executive decision-making, employees, operating premises, bank accounts and inventory or operational functions. Inadequate substance can result in profit reallocation, CFC attribution or denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Digital Sales Understatement

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment, statutory interest, penalty up to 100% of the understated tax, criminal fines and imprisonment for deliberate false returns.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false returns face civil restitution, asset freezing, travel bans and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent trading and knowing tax evasion.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of Cyprus tax residence, recharacterisation and reallocation of profits under ATAD and BEPS, CFC attribution, strike-off or dissolution, and assessment of unpaid taxes.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and the beneficial owner are exposed to restitution orders, director disqualification and travel restrictions for facilitating an artificial tax avoidance structure.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to arm's length profits, underpayment interest, documentation penalties and additional tax up to 100% of the adjusted amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign tax returns without adequate transfer pricing support are jointly exposed to penalties, clawback of distributions and personal assessment where evasion is established.
Persistent Non-Filing and Late Payment

Statutory sanction: Ex officio assessments, fixed late filing penalties, monthly default surcharges, interest and administrative dissolution of the company.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for taxes arising after dissolution, prohibited from serving as directors, and subject to travel bans for continued trading during tax default.
AML and Payment Chain Abuse

Statutory sanction: Suspicious transaction reporting, freezing orders, AML fines, and revocation or restriction of payment processor and banking facilities.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are exposed to civil penalties, confiscation orders, travel restrictions and imprisonment for knowingly facilitating money laundering through the payment chain.

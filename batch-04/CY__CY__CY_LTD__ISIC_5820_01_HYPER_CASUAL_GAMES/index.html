Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance (Greek: Τμήμα Φορολογίας); primary electronic portal is the Tax Portal of Cyprus accessible at https://taxportal.cy, supplemented by the Ariadne integrated electronic filing platform for VAT, income tax, and EU recapitulative statements; customs and export-related procedures are administered by the Cyprus Customs Department (Τελωνείο) under the same Ministry.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus Private Limited Company (HE77) is first incorporated at the Department of Registrar of Companies and Intellectual Property (D.R.C.I.P.). The registrar automatically notifies the Tax Department, which issues the Tax Identification Number (TIN/ΑΦΚ) for income tax and employer obligations. VAT registration is separate and compulsory on the Tax Portal: application must be made within 30 days from the date the company first engages in taxable transactions or exceeds the registration threshold. Non-resident foreign entities not incorporated in Cyprus but holding a Cyprus permanent establishment must obtain a TIN and VAT ID by filing Form TAX 011/VAT 101 through a Cyprus tax representative, attaching certified constitutional documents, board resolution appointing the representative, and proof of a fixed establishment address. Foreign founders may hold 100% of shares; no minimum subscribed capital is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation | Territorial_Scope | Non_Tax_Exempt_Zone. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation | Territorial_Scope | Non_Tax_Exempt_Zone. A Cyprus tax-resident private limited company is taxed at the entity level at the uniform 12.5% corporate income tax rate on worldwide income; a non-resident company is taxed only on Cyprus-source income attributable to a Cyprus permanent establishment. Cyprus applies a classical corporate system complemented by the participation exemption (intercorporate dividends and gains on disposal of shares are generally exempt) and territorial timing rules; no pass-through treatment or check-the-box entity classification exists under Cyprus domestic law.

Available election forms

Cyprus has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (i) group relief election for transfer of trading losses between companies holding at least 75% common ownership; (ii) Notional Interest Deduction election on new paid-in equity under section 9D of the Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002; (iii) 80% notional deduction election for qualifying intangible property (IP box) under section 9B, compatible with modified nexus rules; (iv) tonnage tax election for shipping; (v) election for deemed dividend distribution rules; (vi) election under section 2 of the Income Tax Law to deem a company managed and controlled abroad as non-resident where central management is effectively exercised outside Cyprus; (vii) VAT grouping election under the VAT Law 94(I)/2000; (viii) OSS and IOSS registration elections for intra-EU B2C distance sales and electronic services; and (ix) alignment elections under the hybrid mismatch provisions (ATAD) restricting double deduction and deduction/non-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover of supplies in Cyprus through a fixed establishment; separate mandatory registration triggers include intra-Community acquisitions and intra-EU distance sales/e-services exceeding EUR 10,000 per calendar year (EU-wide threshold), in which case the supplier must either register in the member state of consumption or enrol in the OSS scheme; registration is effective from the first day of the month following the event that crosses the threshold, with optional registration available. For a hyper-casual games publisher monetising via AdMob (Google Ireland) and AppLovin, the operative trigger for EU consumer-facing electronic services is the EUR 10,000 OSS threshold; B2B supplies to EU counterparties are covered by reverse charge and EC Sales List reporting.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) is available under the Sixth Schedule of the Cyprus VAT Law for: (1) export of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the EU by or on behalf of the supplier; (2) intra-Community supplies to an EU customer holding a valid VAT identification number, subject to EC Sales List reporting; and (3) supplies of digital, software, online gaming, advertising inventory and telecommunication services to a customer established outside the EU, or to an EU non-taxable person where effective use and enjoyment is outside the EU, under Articles 58 and 59 of the VAT Directive. For hyper-casual game publishing, the ad-inventory supply to overseas platforms (US-based AppLovin, Google US) qualifies for zero-rating where the recipient is outside the EU; where the platform counterparty is EU-established (Google Ireland), the supply is taxable in the member state of the recipient via reverse charge and is excluded from Cyprus VAT. Substantiation requires contemporaneous evidence of the customer's non-EU status (VAT number validation, certificate of establishment, or Article 59 effective-use documentation), executed contracts, and platform payout statements.

  • Cyprus Customs Export Declaration: Single Administrative Document (SAD/EU SAD) lodged electronically through the Ariadne customs e-system for any physical goods; for digital service exports, the functional equivalent is the executed digital service agreement, delivery/usage logs, and analytics evidence from AdMob/AppLovin platforms
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill: transport document evidencing physical dispatch from Cyprus for goods; for offshore digital services, the equivalent evidence is the published app store distribution record and IP licensing delivery acceptance
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC): issued by the Cyprus banking institution through which the foreign exchange proceeds of the export are repatriated, proving actual receipt of settlement from abroad
  • VAT compliance bundle: valid EU VAT numbers of EU platform customers with VIES validation reports, periodic EC Sales List (Recap Statement), and OSS reference numbers where the intra-EU B2C threshold is exceeded

Cyprus imposes no mandatory domestic Continuous Transaction Control (CTC/real-time clearance) system, no fiscal stamping, and no mandatory B2B e-invoicing clearance. B2G e-invoicing is aligned with EU Directive 2014/55/EU via the Cypriot Peppol authority network on the Ariadne platform. Voluntary private-sector e-invoicing is permitted provided invoices satisfy structural and content rules of the VAT Law. All VAT returns (VAT 200) and EC Sales Lists must be filed electronically through Ariadne/Tax Portal. The European VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) proposal, once adopted EU-wide, will introduce mandatory structured e-invoicing and near-real-time transaction reporting in Cyprus only after the agreed EU implementation dates.

The Tax Department and Cyprus Customs may re-audit zero-rated exports retrospectively within the standard 5-year limitation period, extended to 10 years in cases of fraudulent conduct. Penalties include: (i) reassessment of the export supplies at the standard 19% VAT with reversal of any claimed input VAT credit (clawback); (ii) administrative fines up to EUR 500 per non-compliance event and monthly interest of 1% on unpaid tax; (iii) additional penalties up to 50% of the tax under-assessed where negligence is proven; (iv) criminal liability for tax evasion and fraud, punishable on indictment by imprisonment of up to 3 years and/or unlimited fines; (v) publication of the taxpayer in the Tax Department's list of non-compliant/uncooperative taxpayers; and (vi) retrospective cancellation of zero-rating where the exporter fails to produce the customs export declaration, transport documentation, or Inward Remittance Certificate, or where goods are diverted back into the local market.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility under the EU capital movement regime; Cyprus adopted the euro on 1 January 2008. There are no exchange-control restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions for EU or non-EU residents, subject to anti-money-laundering, sanctions screening, and Central Bank of Cyprus balance-of-payments statistical reporting. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and intercompany loan repayment are unrestricted and, for non-resident corporate shareholders, generally free of Cypriot withholding tax, provided the receiving bank's KYC and source-of-funds protocols are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus abolished statutory exchange controls upon EU accession and Eurozone entry; there is no mandatory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation timeline, or forced conversion ratio. A Cyprus resident company may retain digital advertising export receipts in any convertible currency, but the transactions must be identifiable against the underlying ad-network contracts and reported to the Central Bank of Cyprus by the servicing commercial bank through the standard balance-of-payments reporting framework.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Viability rating: High for treasury concentration, operational settlement, and low-value cross-border disbursements, with strict legal guardrails. A Cyprus private limited company may open accounts with Central Bank of Cyprus-regulated EMIs established in Cyprus or with EEA-licensed EMIs validly passporting into Cyprus, such as Revolut Business or MisterTango. These EMIs cannot serve as full credit-institution substitutes; they are not permitted for large-ticket capital escrow or commercial-lending facilities, and they remain subject to the same AML, sanctions, and UBO-disclosure standards as regulated banks. Offshore non-EEA EMIs are generally not viable as primary clearing accounts in Cyprus, since the Central Bank of Cyprus expects significant cross-border flows to settle through domestic banks or duly passporting EEA institutions.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat corporate income tax rate under the Cyprus Income Tax Law (L.118(I)/2002, as amended).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Cyprus law imposes no withholding tax on dividends distributed by a Cyprus resident company to its shareholders, whether resident or non-resident. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive further confirms a 0% WHT for qualifying EU corporate parents.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The IR4 must be filed within six months after the end of the tax year; for a calendar year-end company, the statutory deadline is 30 June of the following year. Extensions may be granted only at the discretion of the Tax Commissioner.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Provisional tax instalments are due on 1 August, 31 December, and 31 March following the tax year. The IR2 estimate itself must be submitted by 31 March of the tax year following the close of the year, or earlier if the taxpayer anticipates a liability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual value of controlled goods and services transactions; EUR 250,000 for controlled financing and royalty transactions. The Cyprus Tax Department requires contemporaneous Local File documentation when these thresholds are exceeded, and requests submission within a 30-day notice.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies an OECD/G20 BEPS-aligned economic substance doctrine. For a Cyprus Ltd to retain tax residence and the 12.5% preferential rate, the board of directors must exercise genuine management and control in Cyprus, and the company must have adequate personnel, premises and risk control with respect to its hyper-casual gaming activities. The Cyprus Tax Department will deny the IP Box and treaty residence benefits where substance is artificial. There is no separate ESR notification regime for gaming companies, but the 2019 Tax Department circular and the UBO register require supporting evidence of real business activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under Section 8 of the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law (Law 4/1979) and Income Tax Law L.118(I)/2002, the Tax Commissioner may add up to 100% of the evaded tax together with annual interest, impose a criminal fine of up to EUR 5,000, and imprison the responsible persons for up to three years. The courts may also require the restitution of all tax plus compound interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly sign false IR4 returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and civil restitution. Travel bans, port arrest orders and disqualification from being directors for up to five years may be imposed, in addition to criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus tax residence test (Income Tax Law section 2), the company may be treated as non-resident and lose the 12.5% corporate rate; all previously claimed exemptions are withdrawn. EU CFC rules and the Cyprus CFC provision in section 38A may attribute profits to the foreign parent. The company may also be struck off under the Companies Law for failing to have a registered office and registered agent, attracting cancellation of tax incentives and treaty reliefs.

Director personal liability: Directors who declare a false Cyprus tax residence certificate face disqualification orders and civil liability for the resulting tax deficiency. If the shell entity is used for fraudulent insolvency or tax evasion, courts pierce the corporate veil and impose personal restitution, travel bans and imprisonment up to three years.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus AML Law (L.188(I)/2007) as amended and the UBO Law (L.88(I)/2019), the Registrar may impose administrative fines of up to EUR 100,000, daily default fines of EUR 100, and refer the case to MOKAS for asset freezing orders. Conviction for money laundering carries imprisonment up to five years and fines up to EUR 200,000.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly accepting proceeds of crime through payment rails are personally criminally liable. The court may order civil restitution of laundered amounts, issue international travel bans, prohibit banking operations, and, in serious cases, dissolve the company and hold directors personally responsible for all claims.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Missing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under Cyprus transfer pricing rules aligned with OECD guidelines and section 68(2) of the Income Tax Law, the Tax Commissioner may adjust taxable profits to the arm's length level, impose additional tax of up to 25% of the adjusted amount, and levy interest and penalties of 5% to 10% on the under-declared tax. A specific administrative fine of up to EUR 30,000 applies for failure to produce contemporaneous TP records.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement TP schemes with no economic substance may be held jointly and severally liable for the adjustment, additional tax and penalties. If the scheme is found to be a fraudulent concealment of profit, criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment up to three years and a travel ban pending recovery of the tax debt.

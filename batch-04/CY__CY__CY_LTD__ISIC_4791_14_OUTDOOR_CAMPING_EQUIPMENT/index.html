Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; the VAT Service (Υπηρεσία ΦΠΑ) operates within the Department; official electronic portal: Tax Portal of Cyprus at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy, integrating corporate tax, VAT, VIES, OSS and Intrastat e-filing; corporate registry functions are administered by the Department of Registrar of Companies and Intellectual Property.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1 — Incorporate the Cyprus Ltd with the Registrar of Companies and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and company registration number (prefixed 'HE'). STEP 2 — Register for direct taxation with the Department of Taxation through the Tax Portal; the Cyprus TIN of a Cyprus-incorporated legal entity is aligned with its HE registration number and the company must maintain a Tax Portal profile for Corporate Income Tax returns. STEP 3 — File the VAT registration application via the Tax Portal (Form VAT-101A/101B with supporting corporate documents, registered office address, warehouse/fulfilment location in Cyprus and estimated turnover); obtain a distinct VAT registration number in the format CY + 9 alphanumeric characters (8 digits followed by one check letter). STEP 4 — If conducting cross-border B2C e-commerce into other EU Member States or importing low-value consignments, elect the Union One-Stop Shop (OSS) or Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS) through the Tax Portal. STEP 5 — A foreign entity without Cypriot incorporation but with a taxable nexus in Cyprus (warehouse, employees, permanent establishment, distance sales) must obtain a Cyprus TIN/VAT number before commencing supplies; non-EU suppliers are subject to immediate registration with no turnover threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate US-style check-the-box elections, but its tax law grants several elective regimes relevant to a Cyprus online retailer: (a) One-Stop Shop (OSS) election for intra-EU distance sales of goods and cross-border B2C services beyond the EUR 10,000 annual threshold; (b) Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS) election for low-value consignments not exceeding EUR 150; (c) group loss relief election under Article 15 of the Income Tax Law (L.118(I)/2002) for Cyprus groups with 75% or greater ownership; (d) group VAT registration election for closely related taxable persons; (e) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new qualifying equity introduced after 1 January 2015; (f) nexus-compliant IP box deduction election granting up to 80% deduction of qualifying intellectual-property profits; and (g) election for exemption of foreign permanent-establishment profits under the Income Tax Law, all subject to formal documentation and filing through the Tax Portal.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for Cyprus-established taxable persons; EUR 10,000 annual EU-wide distance-sales threshold triggering OSS registration; EUR 35,000 annual intra-EU distance-selling threshold into Cyprus; zero threshold for non-EU suppliers (immediate VAT registration or IOSS application for consignments up to EUR 150)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 29 of the Cyprus VAT Law, a 0% rate applies to: (a) exports of goods to non-EU destinations where the goods physically leave the European Union within 90 days of supply, supported by customs documentation; (b) intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered trader in another EU Member State where the customer's valid VAT number is obtained and the EC Sales List is filed; and (c) specified offshore services supplied to non-resident persons. For an online retailer of outdoor camping equipment, B2C supplies to consumers in other EU Member States do not qualify for zero-rating and must be reported through the OSS at the destination country's VAT rate. The retailer must preserve the full dispatch chain, including electronic courier receipts, customs export declarations, and payment settlement records, to substantiate removal from Cyprus and eligibility for the zero rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Electronic Export System (ECS) Summary Declaration of Exit or Single Administrative Document confirming goods have exited the European Union
  • Transport evidence — Bill of Lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or courier manifest/electronic shipment log proving physical dispatch from Cyprus to a destination outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank remittance credit advice and the retailer's Stripe/PayPal settlement payout reports linking the foreign payment to the specific exported order
  • Commercial invoice annotated with Incoterms, customer identity, destination country and a statement that VAT is zero-rated under Article 29 of the Cyprus VAT Law
  • For intra-EU deliveries — EC Sales List/VIES statement and validation of the customer's EU VAT number

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital certification of B2B invoices is currently in force in Cyprus. Cyprus has enacted domestic e-invoicing legislation transposing the EU public-procurement e-invoicing standard (PEPPOL-BIS 3.0), with mandatory structured e-invoices for supplies to Cypriot public-sector entities on a phased basis. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary for the retail sector. Mandatory electronic filing of VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, OSS returns and Intrastat declarations through the Tax Portal is in effect, and Cyprus is committed to transposing the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' reforms introducing mandatory B2B e-invoicing and digital VAT transaction reporting once adopted.

Cyprus imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline and maintains free capital mobility; nevertheless, the VAT Service rigorously audits export zero-rating claims. An exporter must hold customs, transport and payment evidence at the time of filing and throughout the statutory record-retention period; failure to substantiate the export chain results in reassessment of 19% VAT on the full sales value, late-payment interest, default surcharges, and administrative fines. Intentional falsification of export declarations, sham despatch arrangements, or collusion with overseas buyers constitutes VAT fraud under the Cyprus VAT Law and the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, carrying criminal penalties of fines and/or imprisonment, an assessment limitation period extended to up to ten years permitting retrospective clawback, and publication on the Tax Department's public list of tax evaders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full Eurozone member state since 1 January 2008. The euro (EUR) is freely convertible, with zero exchange control restrictions on current account or capital account transactions. Full liberalisation of capital movements is guaranteed under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. For the retail e-commerce entity CY_LTD, profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted: no Central Bank approval is required, and Cyprus does not levy withholding tax on outbound dividends, interest, or royalties. The only practical hurdles are corporate substance documentation (board resolution, audited financial statements, distribution test under the Companies Law, Cap. 113) and any EU anti-abuse / ATAD (EU Directive 2016/1164) limitations. The transaction currency for all e-commerce operations (Stripe/PayPal settlement) may be EUR, USD, or other freely convertible currencies with no compulsory conversion

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in any currency in any authorised bank account. The Central Bank of Cyprus only requires statistical Balance of Payments (BoP) reporting through the authorised banking system (Form C / TARGET2 settlement records) for the purpose of compiling national statistics. Mandatory conversion ratio is nil (0%). For ISIC 4791 internet retailing, inbound settlement received via Stripe/PayPal and cleared to a Cypriot EUR account is fully fungible and may be redeployed for trade payables, operating expenses, or dividend distributions without any FX surrender requirement

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating within the Eurosystem framework of the European Central Bank (ECB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for EU/EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions operating under PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366), as transposed in Cyprus by the Payment Services Law of 2018 and the E-Money Law (Directive 2009/110/EC). Credible options include Revolut Business, Wise (UK-authorised, EEA branch passport), Payoneer, and Airwallex. Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-LOW for offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA (e.g., Singapore/Hong Kong stored-value facilities): they cannot passport into Cyprus lawfully, and domestic Cypriot banks will often reject settlement accounts routed through unlicensed offshore EMIs due to correspondent de-risking. Legal constraint: any EMI holding client funds for the CY_LTD must be authorised in a Member State or validly passported into Cyprus via the Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC) passport notification procedure; otherwise the arrangement violates the Cyprus Electronic Money Law and exposes the entity to audit and tax compliance friction

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the CY_LTD share capital contribution from non-resident shareholders: SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the CY_LTD Capital Escrow Account at a Tier 1 Cypriot bank. Required wire protocol: ISO 20022/MT103 with tag 50K (ordering customer), tag 59 (beneficiary), and tag 70 (remittance information) carrying the exact purpose code 'EQUITY CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - SHARE ISSUE' plus the corporate registration number (HE77). For ECB Balance of Payments statistics, the transaction must be coded under direct investment equity capital (BoP code 5.1.1.1 / shares and other equity). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% on worldwide chargeable income of Cyprus tax-resident companies; non-resident companies are taxed at the same 12.5% only on Cyprus-source income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends paid by a Cyprus-resident company to non-resident shareholders; there is no domestic withholding tax on outbound dividends. Cyprus-resident individual shareholders may, in limited circumstances, be subject to the 17% Special Defence Contribution, but this charge does not apply to non-residents receiving dividends from a Cyprus LTD.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed within six months after the end of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year undertaking, the deadline is 31 July of the following year. Payment of any final balance of tax is due by the filing deadline, while provisional tax payments are due by 31 July and 31 December of the current tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File for any controlled transaction (or aggregate group of controlled transactions) exceeding EUR 750,000 per fiscal year. Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when the multinational consolidated group turnover reaches EUR 750,000,000 or the applicable CbCR threshold.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not maintain a separate Economic Substances Regulations filing regime analogous to BVI or Cayman. However, the Cyprus Tax Department and EU GAAR/ATAD standards require genuine commercial substance to support tax residence claims, treaty relief, the IP Box, and transfer pricing positions. A Cyprus LTD engaging in internet retail must maintain demonstrable local decision-making, bank accounts, commercial records, and in many cases locally based personnel and warehousing; absent such substance, the company may be challenged as a shell, triggering denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and pecuniary penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the full amount of evaded tax, an administrative penalty up to twice the tax undercharged, late interest at 5% per annum, and criminal conviction with fines of up to EUR 5,000 and imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute or benefit from the fraudulent filing are jointly and severally liable for the outstanding tax, may be stripped of the corporate veil by a Cypriot court, subjected to travel bans and restriction orders under the tax enforcement rules, and face criminal imprisonment where fraud is proven.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department may apply anti-abuse provisions, re-source the income outside Cypriot preferential treatment, disallow the IP Box and treaty exemptions, impose additional assessments equal to 12.5% on gross related-party income, and apply late payment penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors holding a shell structure can be ordered to repay taxes from their personal assets where the company was formed for a dominant impermissible tax avoidance purpose; travel restraints and disqualification orders may accompany civil restitution.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustments restoring arm's-length income, added interest of 5% per annum from the due date, administrative penalties for incomplete TP disclosure (typically up to EUR 10,000 per fiscal year), and potential criminal prosecution for false declarations.

Director personal liability: Where transfer pricing design is intentionally engineered to strip the Cyprus margin, the court may impose personal liability on restructuring officers, issue anti-injunction orders, and award punitive damages to the tax authority upon proof of bad faith.
Late Filing and Non-Payment of Provisional Tax

Statutory sanction: Automatic fixed penalty of EUR 100 plus EUR 50 monthly in continuing default; late payment interest at 5% per annum on every overdue amount; and a 10% additional surcharge on taxes unpaid after the statutory demand. Continued non-payment triggers execution against the company's bank accounts and inventory via Cypriot civil courts.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow the company to omit tax filings while withdrawing distribution funds may hold personal liability for the greater part of the unpaid tax; Cyprus courts have imposed contribution orders, attachment of director assets, and in egregious cases asset-freezing orders.
Anti-Money Laundering and Payment Institution Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law, penalties range from reprimands and corrected-action orders to fines up to EUR 200,000; payment processors and acquiring banks may freeze settlement balances, and the registrar may apply to strike off the company for non-availability of beneficiaries' information.

Director personal liability: Director failings in AML and beneficial-ownership compliance attract personal enforcement action, including disqualification as a fit-and-proper officer, travelling withholding orders, and imprisonment where the omission facilitates money laundering through the camping equipment retail flow.

For a global Employer of Record (EOR) and cross-border contractor payroll company, a virtual office is considered partially permissible only where the following structural services are physically maintained in Cyprus: (a) a registered office address for service of process; (b) a licensed corporate administrator or local registered agent; (c) a Cyprus-resident AML compliance officer (where applicable); (d) access to payroll and personnel records for tax and social insurance purposes. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Tax Department, Ministry of Finance (Taxisnet portal: tax.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must apply for a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN) through Form TIN 301 filed with the competent District Tax Office, supported by certified memorandum/articles, certificate of incorporation, proof of non-resident status, and appointment of a Cyprus tax representative if no fixed establishment. VAT registration for non-established taxable persons is effected via Form VAT 101 and requires approval of a fiscal representative where the Commissioner of Taxation deems it necessary.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Cyprus resident companies are subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide income, with pass-through classification generally unavailable for limited liability companies; territorial scope is modified by treaty and source rules.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not adopt check-the-box. Available elections include group loss relief, tonnage tax (qualifying shipping), notional interest deduction on new equity, and application for advance tax rulings on hybrid mismatch outcomes under EU ATAD-compliant Income Tax Law provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for established taxable persons; non-established foreign suppliers generally require registration from the first taxable supply unless special distance-sales or import thresholds apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods to non-EU destinations qualify for zero-rated VAT provided the goods physically leave the EU within statutory time limits and the exporter retains unambiguous transport evidence. Cross-border B2B services, including global employer of record and staffing services, are zero-rated or outside the scope of Cyprus VAT when the recipient is established outside Cyprus in accordance with place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Single Administrative Document / Bill of Export)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical exportation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Transfer Receipt evidencing receipt of export consideration

Cyprus currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, API filing, or digital stamping regime. Electronic VAT and VIES returns are mandatory via Taxisnet. E-invoicing is permitted under EU e-invoicing rules, with authenticity and integrity required through business controls, EDI, or qualified electronic signatures.

Cyprus VAT authorities can retrospectively disallow zero-rating where the statutory export documentation is incomplete or contradictory. Penalties include administrative fines of 1% to 20% of the tax involved, withholding of refunds pending audit, interest up to 10% per annum, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent zero-rating or smuggling, with imprisonment available in aggravated cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a euro-area member state; the euro is fully convertible across both current and capital account transactions under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). There are no CBC exchange-control restrictions, prior approvals, licences, or surrender requirements for dividend distributions, profit remittances, interest, royalties, or cross-border service fees, including to non-EU shareholders. The EOR business may invoice global clients in EUR or foreign currency, hold foreign receipts in multi-currency accounts, and convert at freely negotiated market rates. The sole residual constraint is the obligation to comply with EU financial sanctions, anti-money-laundering rules (Cyprus AML Law 188(I)/2007 as amended), and CBC statistical/balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting, which applies to all credit institutions and resident companies with cross-border transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules apply in Cyprus. Exporters and service providers retain 100% of export proceeds, may keep them in foreign currency, and are under no deadline to repatriate or convert them. For an ISIC 7810 global EOR staffing agency, all invoiced service receivables from clients in foreign jurisdictions may be credited directly to a Cyprus operational EUR or multi-currency account. The bank will capture the transaction purpose code (ordinarily BPM6 Category 2, Services, code P22 or P021/P022) for CBC/ECB BOP filings, and there is no minimum sale-back obligation to the CBC.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC), the national monetary authority within the Eurosystem, responsible for banking supervision under the Central Bank of Cyprus Law 2002 (as amended) and Regulation (EU) No 575/2013 (CRR) / Directive 2013/36/EU (CRD IV), and the designated exchange-control and balance-of-payments reporting authority for credit institutions in Cyprus.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary treasury rail, Low as a sole or primary rail. Cyprus-licensed banks remain the mandatory backbone because social insurance contributions, health scheme payments (GHS/GeSY), and payroll tax remittances must clear through Cyprus or SEPA bank settlement accounts. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut Business, Multitude/MultiPass, and Airwallex may only be used if: (i) they are authorised under an EEA e-money/payment services licence passported into Cyprus, or are an FCA-authorised EMI with ECB passport; (ii) their ultimate beneficial owner and sanction screening pass CBC-standard AML/CFT due diligence; and (iii) all inbound client funds are ultimately swept to a tier-1 Cyprus bank account for statutory payment execution. Legal constraint: an unlicensed or non-passported offshore EMI acting as a funds gateway would constitute an unauthorised payment-activity exposure under the Payment Services Law (Law 128(I)/2018) and a potential MSB classification trigger.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from a non-Cyprus parent or shareholder are routed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 (for large sums) using ECB BOP purpose code S301 (investment of a capital nature, shares in Cyprus-domiciled companies). The standard documentary bundle comprises a board resolution authorising allotment, share subscription agreement, allotment form (HE32), updated shareholders register, and KYC alignment of the remitter and ultimate beneficiary. EUR-denominated injections from EU jurisdictions may use SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) or a pain.001.001.10 message with purpose code BEQU or S301. For contractual EOR service fees, inbound wires use SWIFT MT103 with ECB BPM6 purpose code P022 (business services) and explicit remittance reference to the master service agreement invoice. No CBC prior approval is required, but the beneficiary bank will report the flow to CBC BOP statistics, and funds are made available only after sanctions screening and source verification.Payroll disbursements to contractors and employees in EUR within the EEA are executed via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst), with ISO 20022 references carrying the payroll period and contract ID. Non-EEA contractor payroll runs are sent as SWIFT MT103 single or bulk-electronic transfers with purpose code P021/P022 (services) and a wage-level reference note. Larger treasury commitments such as client refunds, severance packages, and commercial letters of credit are executed as: (i) TARGET2/EBA Clearing payments for high-value EUR transfers (verified by the receiving bank within the same business day); (ii) SWIFT gpi-tracked MT103 for foreign currency disbursements; and (iii) commercial letters of credit issued by Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank under ICC UCP 600, with an MT700 issuance through the advising bank of the contractor's jurisdiction. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cyprus transfer pricing documentation thresholds are based on annual controlled transaction value: a Local File is required where intra-group goods/services transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000; a Master File is required for larger groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 50,000,000; intra-group financing transactions require documentation regardless of amount where credit risk arises

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not apply separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like BVI/Cayman style regimes; however, the Cyprus Tax Department and courts mandate real commercial substance through actual employees, premises, active decision-making, payroll records and audited EOR service delivery. Substance failures trigger the General Anti-Abuse Rule (Law 55(I)/2021), POEM reclassification to the parent residence state, refusal of DTA benefits, and strike-off proceedings.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax; additional late payment interest at 8.75% per annum; criminal prosecution under the Cyprus Income Tax Law and Criminal Code exposes the company to fines up to EUR 50,000 or the evaded tax amount and imprisonment of responsible individuals for up to 7 years; publication on the Tax Department list of evaders

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause false returns are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; courts may impose asset freezing, travel bans, disqualification as director for up to 10 years, and custodial sentences personally against directors who knowingly participate
Shell Company and Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: Loss of Cyprus tax residence under place of effective management rules; reassessment of global income in the actual residence jurisdiction; invocation of GAAR with 20% improper benefit penalty; compulsory strike-off by the Registrar of Companies; revocation of the required employment agency/EOR licence by the Cyprus Department of Labour

Director personal liability: Non-resident shadow directors face personal tax assessments under custody-and-control provisions; civil liability for distributions made while insolvent; directors can be held personally liable for the entire unpaid tax and face travel bans, deportation orders, and disqualification from future directorships
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department may upward-adjust the taxable profit to arm's length, deny deduction of the intercompany charge, impose interest at 8.75% per annum, apply a 20% GAAR surcharge, and levy documentation penalties of up to EUR 20,000 per category for non-production of the Local/Master File

Director personal liability: Directors who engineer artificial intercompany pricing are personally liable for restitution to the company and the tax authority; if the arrangement facilitates foreign tax evasion, directors may be criminally prosecuted for aiding and abetting fraud with imprisonment up to 5 years
AML, Unlicensed Employment Agency and Payroll Fund Misuse

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 14 years and/or a fine up to EUR 1,000,000 under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law 188(I)/2007; the Cyprus Registrar of Companies and CySEC may jointly impose administrative penalties up to EUR 100,000 and permanently revoke the EOR/employment agency licence

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers qualify as 'responsible persons' and are personally exposed to criminal liability, asset confiscation, travel bans, civil liability to the EOR clients for misappropriated payroll, and disqualification from acting as a director for up to 10 years

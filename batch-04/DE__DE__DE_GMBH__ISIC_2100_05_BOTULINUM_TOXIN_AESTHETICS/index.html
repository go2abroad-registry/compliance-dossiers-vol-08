Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A GmbH must have a German registered domestic business address capable of receiving official service under GmbHG section 8 and related commercial-register rules. A serviced or virtual office may be acceptable if it provides a genuine address, reliable receipt and forwarding of official correspondence, and lawful access to business records where required. A nominal mailbox, accommodation address, or address lacking operational access creates registration, banking, tax, AML, and licensing risk. For manufacture, storage, release, import, wholesale, or distribution of botulinum-toxin medicinal products, a virtual office is insufficient as the sole operating location. The business requires suitable premises, quality systems, qualified personnel, secure storage, cold-chain controls where applicable, and regulatory authorization under AMG and relevant EU GMP or wholesale-distribution rules.

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no uniform statutory nationwide company-name reservation procedure or fixed 30-day reservation period. Obtain a written preliminary name assessment from the competent Industrie- und Handelskammer and the formation notary, then conduct searches in the Handelsregister, Unternehmensregister, German Patent and Trade Mark Office database, EUIPO, domain registries, and relevant market databases. If the register court rejects the proposed name, obtain the written or recorded objection, identify the GmbHG or sectoral-law defect, and submit corrected articles or a replacement name through the notary. A formal complaint against a judicial register decision may be available under the German court-procedure rules, generally with the statutory appeal period stated in the decision; legal counsel should verify the applicable remedy and deadline. A rejection caused by a regulatory authorization issue should be addressed through the relevant licensing authority rather than by relying solely on a register appeal.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory share capital of a GmbH is at least EUR 25,000 under Section 5 GmbHG. Before registration, at least EUR 12,500 must generally be contributed in cash, with each cash contribution meeting the statutory minimum applicable to the subscribed share. Contributions must be freely available to the company; there is no mandatory escrow account, but a German business bank account is normally used and the managing director must confirm proper availability. Contributions in kind require additional documentation and valuation disclosures.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain building and fire-safety approval from the local Bauaufsichtsbehörde and fire authority, including fire-compartmentation, alarm, suppression, emergency-egress, hazardous-material storage, and rescue-access verification. Complete structural-load inspections for production, laboratory, cold-chain, and process equipment. Obtain occupational-health and workplace-safety clearance from the competent Arbeitsschutzbehörde or Gewerbeaufsichtsamt, including risk assessments, containment, personal protective equipment, biological and chemical safety, employee training, medical surveillance where required, and documented emergency procedures.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The locally competent Finanzamt (tax office) for substantive tax assessment and VAT registration; the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for the central German VAT identification number and the OSS portal; official electronic filing through ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH, file the signed 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (questionnaire on registration for tax) with the locally competent Finanzamt within one month of incorporation or before commencing business. The Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer for corporation tax, trade tax, payroll tax and VAT. Separately apply to the BZSt for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (German VAT ID) for intra-Community transactions. VAT returns, EC Sales Lists and Advance VAT returns are transmitted electronically through ELSTER. A foreign parent does not obtain a separate German TIN; the GmbH itself is the statutory taxpayer.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box election. The principal elective mechanism is the Organschaft (corporate tax and VAT group) requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement and financial integration. Limited statutory elections exist under investment fund tax law and for small enterprises electing out of VAT. Hybrid mismatch consequences are not elective; section 4k EStG imposes mandatory non-deductibility and recharacterisation rules consistent with ATAD2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold triggering VAT registration for a GmbH; registration is required from the first taxable supply. The small-business exemption applies only below EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 forecast turnover, in which case VAT is exempt without registration. For distance sales of goods to German consumers by non-established EU businesses, the pan-European intra-Community distance-sales threshold is EUR 10,000; above this threshold German VAT registration or OSS is mandatory. For a GmbH engaged in botulinum toxin exports, VAT registration is mandatory from commencement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU third countries are zero-rated under section 6 UStG if the goods are actually dispatched or transported from Germany to a non-EU destination and properly documented. Intra-Community supplies are zero-rated under section 6a UStG only if the customer has a valid VAT ID, the goods are dispatched to another EU Member State and the recapitulative statement is filed. Services directly connected to the export of goods and certain offshore services may also be zero-rated. For botulinum toxin aesthetics products, tax zero-rating is supplemented by the requirement to hold relevant pharmaceutical/export licences under German health and customs legislation.

  • Electronic Export Declaration in ATLAS (Ausfuhranmeldung/ABZ)
  • Commercial invoice and proof of transport: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note
  • Transaction records demonstrating the physical movement of goods out of the German customs territory
  • Evidence of the customer's valid VAT ID for intra-Community supply where zero-rating under section 6a UStG is claimed
  • Bank payment or Letter of Credit documentation evidencing settlement of the export transaction
  • Customs and pharmaceutical compliance documents applicable under German export control law

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. E-invoicing (E-Rechnung) is being phased in: from 1 January 2025 German businesses must be able to receive structured CEN EN 16931 electronic invoices in domestic B2B transactions; from 1 January 2027 businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B invoices must be issued electronically. Transmission uses PEPPOL/EDI or another interoperable structured format. No API pre-clearance is required, but VAT returns and EC Sales Lists must be transmitted electronically to the BZSt.

Insufficient, contradictory or missing export evidence causes the Finanzamt to reverse the zero rate and assess 19% VAT with statutory interest under section 233a AO and late-payment penalties up to 10% under section 240 AO. Fraudulent or forged export documentation triggers criminal tax evasion under section 370 AO, punishable by fines or imprisonment up to five years. German tax audits may review export transactions retrospectively for the full statutory assessment period; VAT input deductions are correspondingly clawed back and, in serious cases, joint and several liability of legal representatives may arise.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility of the euro and all major foreign currencies under Article 63 of the TFEU. There are no exchange controls, quantitative limits, or official approval requirements for the conversion of export proceeds or for profit repatriation. Profit remittance from a German GmbH to a non-resident shareholder is legally unfettered, although statutory income withholding and corporate tax formalities apply separately, and anti-money laundering verification is mandatory for high-risk industries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in Germany to convert, surrender, or repatriate export proceeds to a domestic bank account. Exporters may freely retain foreign currency abroad or in Germany. However, all cross-border payments (including export receipts and import disbursements) above the reporting threshold of EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistical purposes, and enterprises are obliged to answer Bundesbank data requests on a structured reporting line.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German Federal Bank), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks; capital movement supervision is shared with the European Central Bank under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) can legally pass-port into Germany and open accounts on a remote basis; however, most cross-border fintech platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) either exclude regulated pharmaceutical categories or subject them to enhanced rejection rules. Legal constraints: an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot hold capital escrow balances for regulated GmbH share capital under German commercial law, nor can it issue documentary letters of credit or provide treasury treasury liquidity services. Therefore, an EMI may serve only as an ancillary inbound/outbound payment rail for low-value, non-controlled disbursements, never as the primary treasury clearing system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contribution must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR domestic flows) using the official purpose code for a capital payment into a German GmbH, typically reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank with the code 'Kapitalzuführung' (capital contribution). The wire should reference the notarized capital-increase deed and the unique GmbH commercial-register file number. For injections above EUR 50,000, the receiving German bank requires prior submission of the notary’s certificate of capital contribution and the commercial-register application.

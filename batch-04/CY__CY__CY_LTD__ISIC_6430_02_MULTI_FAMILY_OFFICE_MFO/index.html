Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DROCOR), Ministry of Energy, Commerce and Industry, Nicosia, Republic of Cyprus.

Governing statute

The Companies Law, Cap. 113, Sections 5 (mode of incorporation), 6 (memorandum of association), 10 (effect of memorandum), 111-118 (private companies); supplementary provisions of the Company (Amendment) Law 9(I)/2006 and the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission Law 92(I)/2007 for the authorized investment and multi-family office scope.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form HE1 - Application for Registration of a Private Limited Company together with the executed Memorandum and Articles of Association.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 16 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under Cap. 113; a private limited company may incorporate with a nominal authorized share capital (customary range EUR 2,000-10,000), fully or partly subscribed, with no escrow account requirement at the Registry; capital verification is only a banking practice at the account-opening stage.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the Cyprus Fire Service under Law 12(I)/2009 covering evacuation routes and extinguishing systems; structural load inspection and occupancy-capacity certification by the District Surveyor (Public Works Department); workplace operational clearance under the Safety and Health at Work Law 158(I)/1999 including workstations, electrical installation inspection and occupational health certificates for staff.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under Cap. 113; a private limited company may incorporate with a nominal authorized share capital (customary range EUR 2,000-10,000), fully or partly subscribed, with no escrow account requirement at the Registry; capital verification is only a banking practice at the account-opening stage.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance (official portal: https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy/; VAT filings are submitted through the Ariadne e-portal).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity may obtain a Cyprus TIN either through company incorporation under the Cyprus Companies Law, Cap.113 (the Registrar of Companies issues a Company Registration Number which is also used as the TIN for corporate tax), or by registering with the Tax Department for direct taxation via Form TD200 (Application for Registration) for the entity, any permanent establishment, or tax representative. VAT registration requires filing Form VAT 101 (for persons established in Cyprus) or VAT 102/103 (for non-established taxable persons) with the Tax Department, submitting supporting documents including certificate of incorporation (or equivalent foreign registration), constitutional documents, audited financial statements, proof of physical presence/trading activity, and particulars of directors and shareholders. Where a non-resident company has no fixed establishment for VAT, it may be required to appoint a VAT representative in Cyprus and provide a guarantee for VAT liabilities. The TIN is typically an 8-digit number; confirmation is issued by the Tax Department after verification.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity may obtain a Cyprus TIN either through company incorporation under the Cyprus Companies Law, Cap.113 (the Registrar of Companies issues a Company Registration Number which is also used as the TIN for corporate tax), or by registering with the Tax Department for direct taxation via Form TD200 (Application for Registration) for the entity, any permanent establishment, or tax representative. VAT registration requires filing Form VAT 101 (for persons established in Cyprus) or VAT 102/103 (for non-established taxable persons) with the Tax Department, submitting supporting documents including certificate of incorporation (or equivalent foreign registration), constitutional documents, audited financial statements, proof of physical presence/trading activity, and particulars of directors and shareholders. Where a non-resident company has no fixed establishment for VAT, it may be required to appoint a VAT representative in Cyprus and provide a guarantee for VAT liabilities. The TIN is typically an 8-digit number; confirmation is issued by the Tax Department after verification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a 'check-the-box' pass-through election. All limited companies are subject to corporation tax at the entity level. Statutory elections available include: (i) group relief election for qualifying group companies; (ii) tonnage tax election for qualifying Cyprus-flagged and EU/EEA ships; (iii) notional interest deduction (NID) election on new equity; (iv) intellectual property regime election granting 80% profit exemption subject to OECD Nexus; (v) forced/ deemed dividend distribution election for close companies under section 54 of the Income Tax Law; and (vi) anti-hybrid alignment elections where applicable under EU ATAD.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12-month period; non-established suppliers supplying distance sales of goods into Cyprus exceed EUR 10,000 per year for intra-EU trade (Union threshold) and EUR 35,000 for goods imported from non-EU countries; there is also an optional small business effectiveness threshold under national implementation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to: (i) exportation of movable goods outside the European Union; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to taxable persons in other Member States; (iii) supplies of goods and services directly related to exports and international transportation; (iv) supplies of services under the place-of-supply rules that are treated as performed outside Cyprus, where the customer has a business establishment outside the EU or outside Cyprus and receives the services for offshore business purposes; (v) certain financial and portfolio management services provided to non-resident investment funds, provided the income is not attributable to a Cyprus permanent establishment. For a Cyprus multi-family office, discretionary portfolio advisory services supplied to non-resident private clients can be zero-rated where legal, economic, and effective use and enjoyment are shown to occur outside Cyprus, and the client is not a Cyprus-resident taxable person.

  • Official Customs Export Declaration using the Single Administrative Document (SAD)
  • Commercial Invoice / Receipt
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR International Consignment Note
  • Packing List
  • Proof of physical export (customs stamp, exit summary, or electronic export confirmation)
  • Contract / Service Agreement evidencing the export commercial terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT banking credit advice proving receipt of consideration
  • Entity and identity documents of the foreign client

Cyprus does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transaction-level clearance mechanisms for B2B/B2C commercial invoices. E-invoicing is mandatory only for business-to-government (B2G) invoices, in compliance with European standard EN16931 and Directive 2014/55/EU. The Tax Department operates the Ariadne VAT portal for submission of VAT returns and other declarations; there is no government approval or API clearance requirement for standard commercial invoices. Taxable persons remain obliged to issue proper invoices in accordance with VAT rules and retain audit trails for six years.

Cyprus law treats fraudulent export zero-rating as a serious civil and criminal tax offence. Penalties under the VAT Law (N.94(I)/2000 as amended) and the Tax Assessment and Collection Laws include: (i) a tax clawback of the full VAT amount that would have been chargeable at the standard rate, computed retrospectively from the original zero-rated date; (ii) statutory interest of not less than 1% per additional month or part thereof, plus default surcharge; (iii) administrative fines up to 50% of the tax understated; (iv) criminal conviction with imprisonment of up to five years or an unlimited fine; and (v) rejection of the exporter's documentary substantiation, including denial of refund claims and cancellation of VAT registration. The Tax Department also has power to invoke anti-abuse jurisdictions, assess a deemed output VAT on incomplete export files, and reopen assessment periods for up to ten years in cases involving fraud or gross negligence.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility. Cyprus has no residual foreign-exchange-control legislation; capital movements are unrestricted under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), Council Directive 88/361/EEC, and the euro-area framework. Profit remittance is freely permitted without Central Bank approval: Cyprus-source dividends paid to non-resident shareholders attract a 17% statutory withholding tax, subject to reduction to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Directive 2011/96/EU) for qualifying EU corporate holders (≥10% holding), and to treaty rates (typically 0-15%) under Cyprus Double Tax Treaties. No prior exchange-licence, economic-need, or repatriation-authorisation hurdle applies to an MFO. The only binding constraints are the general EU/CRS/FATCA reporting obligations and the AML/CFT framework (Law 188(I)/2007, as amended).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus abolished all mandatory surrender, conversion-ratio, and time-limit requirements for export receipts upon adoption of the euro (1 January 2008). A Cyprus-resident entity may freely hold export/service income in EUR or any foreign currency in resident or non-resident accounts. The sole remaining obligation, if physical cash exceeding EUR 10,000 crosses an EU external border, is the cash-control declaration under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672; electronic wire transfers carry no reporting or conversion duty.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); FX policy and capital-movement supervision are, however, vested in the European Central Bank (ECB) and the European Commission for euro-area matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for the MFO's primary treasury function. Cyprus recognises E-Money Institutions under the Electronic Money Law 122(I)/2004 and Payment Institutions under Law 31(I)/2018 (PSD2 transposition); EU-licensed EMIs may passport into Cyprus. In practice, however, the ISIC 6430/NAICS 523920 discretionary-advisory profile is classed as High-Risk Special-License, and mainstream EU EMIs (Revolut Business, Wise, MultiPass, PPS) generally decline or cap such relationships. Moreover, EMIs cannot lawfully provide credit, deposit-taking, full-scope cash-pooling, custody of client securities, or Commercial LC services, all outside the e-money licence perimeter. Recommended use: ancillary pre-funding, collections, and minor disbursement rail only, never as the sole or dominant clearing infrastructure.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

UBO or material shareholder is a politically exposed person (PEP), a sanctions-designated individual, or a resident/citizen of a high-risk third country listed by FATF or EU Delegated Regulation 2023/339.

Root cause: UBO or material shareholder is a politically exposed person (PEP), a sanctions-designated individual, or a resident/citizen of a high-risk third country listed by FATF or EU Delegated Regulation 2023/339.

Defense evidence: UBO declaration (Form 32AI) filed with the Cyprus Registrar; Central Bank of Cyprus sanctions-screening certificate; EU-targeted financial sanctions declaration under Regulation 269/2014; sworn source-of-funds affidavit; transactional history of the originating family vehicles for the preceding 24 months; independent legal opinion from a Cyprus-licensed advocate certifying that no sanctioned person directly or indirectly controls, or benefits from, the MFO.
Unclear or undocumented source of wealth (SOW) for multi-generational family assets, probate gaps, or offshore trust opacity.

Root cause: Unclear or undocumented source of wealth (SOW) for multi-generational family assets, probate gaps, or offshore trust opacity.

Defense evidence: IFRS-compliant audited financial statements of the originating corporate vehicles; tax residence certificates of all UBOs; probate, gift, and inheritance instruments; trust deed with protector and beneficiary register (Cyprus Companies Law, Cap. 113, s.44A beneficial-ownership register excerpt); notarised family-tree and corporate-chain chart showing each wealth-generation event.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is via TARGET2/SEPA for EUR-denominated injections and SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR wires. The injection must be documented as a capital contribution under the Cyprus Companies Law, Cap. 113: board resolution, updated share register, and, where applicable, the statutory increase-of-capital filing. Recommended SWIFT/ISO 20022 remittance-info references: purpose code 'S302' (other equity injection) or 'S301' (acquisition of shares in group company); for inter-company shareholder loans, use 'P101' (short-term intra-group loan). The receiving bank requires a pre-notification letter, the executed subscription or loan agreement, a source-of-funds declaration, and the MFO's corporate-KYC pack. There is no Cyprus exchange-control filing or approval. All wires above the EU information threshold must carry full originator and beneficiary names, addresses, and account identifiers under Regulation (EU) 2015/847.For an MFO, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR cross-currency disbursements (USD via Fedwire/CHIPS through New York correspondents; GBP via CHAPS in London), with TARGET2/SEPA for EUR disbursements. Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600, SWIFT MT700) and documentary collections (SWIFT MT400/MT410) are available only where the MFO holds an underlying trading or direct-asset-purchase mandate; for ordinary advisory disbursements, MT103 customer credit transfers are standard. Securities-trade cash settlement occurs on T2S through Euroclear/Clearstream accounts, with the cash leg settled in TARGET2 central-bank money. No External Payments Unit, exchange-control permit, or Central Bank of Cyprus pre-approval applies to outbound payments; compliance is confined to Regulation (EU) 2015/847 transparency, AML screening of counterparties, and any applicable sanctions/proliferation-financing blocking.FX Forwards (outright EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF, EUR/JPY) executed with Cypriot or EU credit institutions under a 2002 ISDA Master Agreement and 2013/2016 CSA.Cross-Currency Swaps (EUR/USD XCCY, EUR/GBP XCCY) for long-dated asset-liability matching of multi-family investment portfolios.FX Swaps and rollovers (FXS and FXO) for short-term liquidity rebalancing between currency legs.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for restricted-convertibility currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, and other capital-control currencies), executed offshore with an EU bank branch and supported by legal opinions confirming that the NDF does not constitute a regulated local-currency offshore contract; the MFO remains within the EMIR non-financial-counterparty regime unless clearing thresholds are exceeded.Vanilla EUR/foreign-currency FX options (call/put, European style) for premium-bounded risk management, classified as professional-client transactions under MiFID II (Law 87(I)/2017), subject to EMIR reporting and, if applicable, the EU clearing obligation; leveraged, exotic, and binary instruments are excluded from the permitted treasury toolbox.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation (Tμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; electronic filing exclusively through the Tax Portal 'ARIADNE' (successor of TaxisNet) at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy; VAT-specific registrations and returns (Form VAT 311) are processed by the same Department through the portal.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: Cyprus-incorporated entity obtains its Tax Identification Code (TIC) automatically upon registration with the Registrar of Companies (Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver) - no separate application required for income tax purposes. STEP 2: Foreign entity establishing a Cyprus branch or permanent establishment files a non-resident registration application with the Department of Taxation, submitting certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, memorandum/articles of association, director lists, and evidence of Cyprus establishment, obtaining a Cypriot TIC. STEP 3: VAT registration for non-established taxable persons - file Form VAT 101 (registration of non-established person) to the VAT District Office; the Commissioner requires appointment of a fiscal representative established in Cyprus when the non-resident does not have an establishment and no EU mutual assistance agreement applies; for established persons, registration becomes compulsory when taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600 (12-month period) or on first intra-Community acquisition exceeding EUR 10,000; in practice, a company may also register voluntarily. STEP 4: Apply for e-portal credentials (ARIADNE) and API/user authentication for electronic submissions; simultaneously register employer with the Social Insurance Services and the Statistical Service (VAT/Intrastat) where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope=Worldwide (management-and-control): Cyprus Ltd is a separate taxpayer subject to 12.5% corporate income tax (Cyprus Income Tax Law N.118(I)/2002) on worldwide income when managed and controlled in Cyprus; no pass-through for LLP/quasi-transparent instruments; non-resident shareholders are generally not taxed on distributions (except Special Defence Contribution for controlling Cyprus resident shareholders); real estate and capital gains are separately ring-fenced; no territorial exemption applies to normal trading income.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box; statutory elections available under Cyprus law include: (i) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) allowance on new equity - elective in substance via completion of NID computation; (ii) IP Box regime (80% qualifying IP deduction reducing effective rate to ~2.5%) - elective election in the tax return; (iii) Tonnage Tax election for qualifying shipping; (iv) group relief election for consolidation/loss transfer; (v) VAT group registration and partial exemption election (deductibility of exempt financial supplies via the use-and-enjoyment and annual adjustment); (vi) election for CY tax residence for management-and-control; (vii) hybrid mismatch rules implementing EU ATAD 2 are mandatory, not elective, but allow alignment elections under the new Cyprus intellectual property and loan note rules; (viii) EU Directive-based OSS/IOSS elections are available to non-established suppliers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 (annual taxable turnover threshold for Cyprus-established persons; compulsory VAT registration); EUR 10,000 (unions-wide distance-selling / intra-Community acquisition threshold); non-established foreign suppliers have zero threshold - first taxable transaction in Cyprus requires registration/OSS; remittance and money-transmission services are VAT-exempt without credit (Art. 135(1)(d) Directive transposition), so the threshold is not triggered by pure money-transmission fees; however ancillary marketing/settlement fees may create nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: 0% VAT applies to export supplies (including onward exports) evidenced by the customs Single Administrative Document (SAD) proving export from the EU within 90 days (Cyprus practice 2-3 months); for services: B2B exports of services are outside scope of Cyprus VAT under the general place-of-supply rule (reverse charge applies to the customer) rather than zero-rated; eligible offshore services - services related to movable property exported, intermediary services, transport of goods to/from third countries - qualify at 0% with proper documentation; cross-border money transfer/remittance services are exempt (not zero-rated) under the statutory financial-exemption; they cannot claim output VAT, but the supplier may apply the special investment/equalization or partial non-recovery method for input VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD) endorsed by customs
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR / shipping evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank transfer evidence proving payment received
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list
  • Proof of contract terms (EXW/FOB/CFR/CIF), customs transit/exit confirmation and VIES safe harbor for intra-EU

No domestic real-time fiscal clearance/mandatory digital stamping regime (no CTC model) - Cyprus has not enacted SAF-T, invoice clearances, or e-stamping for standard B2B; mandatory structured e-invoicing applies only to certain B2G transactions (transposing Directive 2014/55/EU); invoicing and record storage follow EU Article 226 requirements and the Cypriot obligation to issue invoices for B2B supplies (including self-billing) and to submit electronically VAT returns (Form VAT 311), VIES recapitulative statements, Intrastat declarations, and e-ledgers through ARIADNE; the law permits electronic invoicing subject to authenticity/originality controls (advanced electronic signature or EDI/convergent transmission).

The Department of Taxation audits export zero-rating against customs exit data (ICES), banking inflow records and the customers' VIES/trader status; missing/inconsistent documentation triggers retrospective assessment within the general limitation period (usually 4 years, extended to 10 years for conduct involving concealment/fraud), interest on under-declared VAT, administrative fines up to the amount of VAT evaded (and additional fixed penalties for post-compliance failures), and criminal prosecution of directors (imprisonment or monetary fines) for VAT fraud/forged documents; Cyprus also applies mutual assistance and anti-fraud exchange of information with other member states and third countries.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Cyprus, as a eurozone member, applies no exchange controls under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and maintains full current-account and capital-account liberalisation. Non-resident shareholders may subscribe share capital, fund shareholder loans, and receive dividend remittances without central bank approval. Profit remittance through SEPA, TARGET2, or SWIFT is unrestricted; the practical hurdles are corporate formalities, beneficial-owner verification, and bank enhanced due diligence. Dividend payments are not subject to Cyprus exchange-control or tax withholding in most non-resident beneficial-owner scenarios.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime in Cyprus. Exporters and remittance-settlement companies are not required to convert export receipts into euros within a prescribed timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exists. Export proceeds may be credited to foreign-currency accounts at licensed Cyprus credit institutions and retained indefinitely. Statutory obligations are limited to ECB balance-of-payments statistical reporting, AML record-keeping, and documentation of underlying commercial transactions.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus, functioning within the European System of Central Banks and the European Central Bank; competent authority for banking supervision, payment institutions, and AML/CFT enforcement over credit institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Ramp Fit: High

Medium as a principal clearing rail; High only as an ancillary on-ramp/off-ramp. A Cyprus-domiciled remittance agency must itself hold payment institution authorisation under the Cyprus Payment Services Law or operate as a registered agent of an authorised institution. Offshore digital EMIs outside the EEA cannot lawfully provide payment services to Cyprus clients without a Cypriot or passported EU EMI licence; licensed or passported EU EMIs may settle euro transactions but final treasury settlement and client-fund safeguarding should ultimately sit with a Cyprus credit institution or TARGET2 member.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan funding must be routed by SWIFT MT103 through a licensed credit institution or passported payment/EMI institution. Remittance advice should clearly identify the purpose as capital subscription, additional paid-in capital, or shareholder loan. The compliance bundle includes board resolutions, signed shareholder loan agreement, updated share register, source-of-funds declarations, and CBC balance-of-payments reporting. For euro funding from an EEA bank, SEPA Credit Transfer with a structured remittance reference may be accepted, but SWIFT is preferred for cross-border clarity and audit certainty.Trade settlements and remittance payouts are executed through: SWIFT MT103 for non-euro cross-border wires; SEPA Credit Transfer and TARGET2 for euro-denominated payouts; Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 for documentary trade transactions; and documentary collections for invoice-backed settlements. Correspondent clearing requires pre-screened destination bank BICs, sanctions-compliant payment details, and consistent purpose codes. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% on worldwide taxable income of Cyprus tax-resident companies; Cyprus-source income of non-residents attributable to a Cyprus permanent establishment (PE) is taxed at the same rate and branches file a separate tax computation simultaneously.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Cyprus imposes no domestic withholding tax on dividends of a Cyprus-resident company paid to non-resident shareholders. Special Defence Contribution (SDC) of 17% applies only to Cyprus tax-resident individual shareholders on distributed or deemed-distributed dividends; non-resident shareholders are outside the SDC net. The deemed-distribution regime can nonetheless create SDC exposure for Cyprus-resident individuals if a Cyprus company does not distribute 70% of its accounting profits within two years.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual T.D.2001 for a calendar tax year Y must be filed within 15 months of the end of that tax year, i.e., by 31 March of year Y+2. Provisional tax installments under T.D.2004 are due on 31 July and 31 December of the tax year; the penalties for underpayment of provisional tax apply when the estimate differs from the eventual tax by more than 5%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR. Under Law 80(I)/2022 introducing Section 33 of the Income Tax Law, contemporaneous Cyprus Local File and, where applicable, Master File are mandatory when the taxpayer's annual gross turnover exceeds EUR 750,000 and the aggregate value of controlled cross-border transactions exceeds EUR 500,000, or when interest-bearing balances with related parties exceed EUR 100,000. Below these thresholds, simplified documentation (business description, transaction ledger and arm's length basis) must be retained and produced to the CIR within 30 days of request; full documentation must be produced within 60 days.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations in the Caribbean/UAE sense; nevertheless, tax residence, DTA access and the Central Bank of Cyprus / CySEC payment-services licence demand genuine substance: central management and control exercised from Cyprus (board meetings and decisions taken in Cyprus), a local registered office, local employees with decision-making authority, local settlement and treasury bank accounts, and risk governance proportionate to the cross-border remittance activity. The Cyprus GAAR (Section 33A of the Income Tax Law) permits the CIR to disregard artificial structures lacking economic substance, and the Cyprus courts and the OECD multilateral instrument's principal purpose test limit treaty relief where substance is absent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment with interest at 1.75% per annum and a penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law; criminal conviction on summary indictment carries imprisonment of up to 5 years and monetary fines at the discretion of the court.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns may be prosecuted personally, held jointly and severally for the company's unpaid tax and restitution, and may face travel bans and asset-freezing orders imposed by the courts during investigation.
Unlicensed Money Transmission and AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Cyprus Payment Services Law L.155(I)/2012 and the AML Law L.188(I)/2007 (as amended); fines for legal persons may reach EUR 500,000 or twice the benefit derived, with revocation of any operating licence; involvement in predicate funds laundering is punishable by imprisonment of up to 14 years for individuals and unlimited fines.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal liability for money laundering, confiscation of personal assets, travel bans, disqualification from managing regulated entities, and civil liability to defrauded senders and correspondent banks.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance Avoidance

Statutory sanction: The CIR may disregard the arrangement under the GAAR, reallocate income to Cyprus, impose the 12.5% tax with the 25% to 100% penalty for negligent or deliberate conduct, and cancel the tax residence certificate; treaty benefits can be denied under the principal purpose test and the company may be struck off under the Companies Law.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to personal assessments for fraudulent trading if the shell arrangement is used to attract foreign capital while leaving creditors and the Cyprus revenue exposed; courts may impose unlimited personal liability and travel bans.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The CIR can make a transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length result with interest and a penalty of up to 25% of the underpaid tax; failure to produce the documentation after a written request attracts administrative fines per month of default in addition to the tax assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve transfer prices outside the arm's length range are jointly and severally liable for the resulting underpaid tax and penalties to the extent the company cannot pay; civil restitution from directors is recoverable in the CIR's favour.
Fraudulent Trading and Misuse of Client Funds

Statutory sanction: Section 292 of the Cyprus Companies Law Cap. 113: fraudulent trading renders any knowing party personally responsible without limitation for the company's debts; the criminal sanction is imprisonment of up to 2 years and a fine at the court's discretion; unlawful distributions are recoverable from shareholders and directors.

Director personal liability: Directors face unlimited personal liability to the company's creditors and to the client float holders; the court may impose an immediate travel ban, sequestration of personal assets, disqualification as a director, and on conviction imprisonment.
Deemed Dividend and SDC Evasion by Resident Beneficial Owners

Statutory sanction: The deemed dividend is assessed on the Cyprus-resident individual at 17% SDC with interest and a 10% penalty for under-declaration; the company may also be treated as the withholding agent and fined for non-compliance with the SDC collection procedure.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the company to refrain from distributing profits in order to assist the shareholder's SDC avoidance can be jointly assessed with the shareholder, become subject to personal recovery orders, and be barred from holding directorships.

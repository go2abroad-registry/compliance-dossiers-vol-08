Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital of EUR 25,000; at least EUR 12,500 (50%) must be paid up in cash before registration. The paid-up portion must be deposited into a German or EU credit institution escrow account (Sperrkonto) and be freely at the company's disposal upon registration; a bank confirmation must be filed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the local fire authority (Feuerwehr-Bauaufsicht) for fire protection concept and fire extinguisher installation; structural load inspection by a licensed structural engineer (Statiker) for the production facility floor loading and storage areas; workplace clearance and occupational safety inspection under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz) with BAuA technical safety assessment including electromagnetic field (EMF) exposure limit verification under the EMF Ordinance and workplace ergonomic risk assessment.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital of EUR 25,000; at least EUR 12,500 (50%) must be paid up in cash before registration. The paid-up portion must be deposited into a German or EU credit institution escrow account (Sperrkonto) and be freely at the company's disposal upon registration; a bank confirmation must be filed.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
At a constitutive meeting of the founding shareholders, it is resolved: (1) each subscribed share is hereby accepted and the cash contributions identified in the notarial deed are confirmed as paid into the escrow account; (2) the company adopts its registered office at the address stated in the Articles, which is approved as its legal seat; (3) the company is managed and represented by the managing directors appointed in the deed, whose powers, sole or joint representation, and signature rules are as registered; (4) the directors are authorised to file the commercial register application, publish notices, and perform all formalities necessary for incorporation and commencement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is fully liberalized under Article 63 TFEU (free movement of capital); no exchange controls, no convertibility licensing, and no distinction between current and capital account restrictions for EUR or foreign currency conversions. Profit remittance and dividend distribution are unrestricted subject to corporate formalities: audited annual financial statements, shareholder dividend resolution, and a 26.375% effective withholding levy (25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), potentially reduced under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or applicable DTT. All payments with non-residents are free of prior approval but trigger mandatory statistical reporting under Section 67 of the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) when the monthly aggregate exceeds EUR 12,500, with a 7-calendar-day filing deadline.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion of export proceeds in any currency. The GmbH may retain USD, CNY, or other export receipts in onshore multi-currency accounts or offshore concentration accounts. The sole regulatory obligation is statistical: where export receipts settled with non-residents exceed EUR 12,500 equivalent in any calendar month, the company must submit an AWV balance-of-payments report (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik) to the Deutsche Bundesbank within 7 days of the reporting month-end. No minimum conversion ratio, retention quota, or repatriation timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) as the national competent authority for balance-of-payments and external trade statistics, implementing European Central Bank (ECB) monetary policy within the Eurosystem; joint supervisory oversight with BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) for the Banking Act (KWG), Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), AML/CFT obligations under the GwG, and sanctions enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for operational collections, cross-border disbursements, and card issuing; LOW for statutory capital escrow or credit replacement. Authorized under the ZAG as e-money institutions (EMI) with EEA passporting or BaFin license (e.g., Solaris SE, Airwallex Germany GmbH, Pliant, Adyen N.V., and N26). Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not KWG credit institutions and cannot hold GmbH share-capital blocking accounts or grant trade financing; (ii) client funds must be safeguarded under ZAG Section 27(1) through segregation or insurance, with funds in a Tier 1 safeguarded bank account; (iii) EMIs may not be used to circumvent German banking supervision or AWV reporting; (iv) recommended architecture is an EMI overlay for last-mile payouts with a zero-balancing sweep end-of-day into the Tier 1 concentration account at Deutsche Bank or Commerzbank.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Companies Law requires every Cyprus company to maintain a registered office in Cyprus at a physical street address; a PO Box alone is prohibited. Commercial virtual-office services providing a collectable Cyprus street address are tolerated by DRCIP, but for an ISIC 7310 digital advertising and affiliate media-buying company, the Cyprus Tax Department, CySEC and regulated banks apply elevated substance scrutiny. To preserve Cyprus tax residence, benefit under the 12.5% corporate tax regime and bilateral treaty access, the company must evidence management and control exercised from Cyprus, locally retained books and records, presence of a Cyprus-resident director or appropriately qualified company secretary, execution of material contracts from Cyprus, and at least a serviced physical office sufficient for board meetings; a pure letterbox arrangement will trigger economic-substance review and may result in denial of a Cyprus tax-residence certificate or treaty benefits.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance; online e-services are available through the Ariadne platform and TAXISnet e-filing portal.

E-government filing portal

For a Cyprus private limited company, the Registrar of Companies registers the legal entity and the Tax Department automatically issues the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). For a foreign entity without a Cyprus registration, the entity must first register with the Registrar of Companies as an overseas company or establish a Cyprus branch, then file the prescribed income tax registration application with the competent District Tax Office. For VAT purposes, a separate VAT registration application must be submitted, generally through Form 101 for established entities and the relevant non-established taxable person procedure for foreign entities; non-established suppliers effecting taxable supplies in Cyprus are required to register regardless of turnover and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a Cyprus tax-resident company is subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income at the standard 12.5% rate, while non-resident entities are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. Cyprus does not recognise pass-through entity classification for a private limited company, and no tax-exempt zone status applies to this entity. — For a Cyprus private limited company, the Registrar of Companies registers the legal entity and the Tax Department automatically issues the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Cyprus tax-resident company is subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income at the standard 12.5% rate, while non-resident entities are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. Cyprus does not recognise pass-through entity classification for a private limited company, and no tax-exempt zone status applies to this entity.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Companies are taxed as separate taxable entities by operation of law. Available statutory elections and group mechanisms include: group loss relief election for Cyprus tax-resident group companies; election for VAT grouping under Article 16 of the Cyprus VAT Law; optional application of reduced VAT treatment where specifically listed; and elections relating to tax depreciation and group transfer relief. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are not elective and must be neutralised in accordance with Cyprus legislation implementing the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 of annual taxable turnover for an established taxable person; EUR 10,000 for intra-Community distance sales and cross-border electronically supplied services to non-taxable persons in the EU. Non-established suppliers have no registration threshold and must register before making taxable supplies in Cyprus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated supplies are available where goods are exported from Cyprus or the EU to a destination outside the European Union, provided the goods are dispatched or transported by the supplier, on behalf of the supplier, or by a person to whom the supply is made, and the export is evidenced by customs and commercial documentation. Related transport, handling, and ancillary services supplied in connection with exported goods may also qualify for zero rating. For offshore digital advertising, affiliate media buying, and similar business-to-business services supplied to a customer established outside the EU, the place of supply is generally outside Cyprus, making the supply outside the scope of Cyprus VAT rather than technically zero-rated; documentation must prove the customer's establishment outside Cyprus, the business-to-business nature of the transaction, and the actual performance of the services. If the customer is an EU taxable person, the reverse-charge mechanism applies and the Cyprus supplier invoices without VAT.

  • Customs export declaration accepted by Cyprus customs authorities, including the Export Accompanying Document and supporting customs entries
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, CMR international consignment note, or courier waybill evidencing physical movement of the goods from Cyprus to a destination outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order, delivery receipts, and the underlying sale or service contract
  • Proof of onward payment or inward remittance, including SWIFT confirmations or a bank inward remittance certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • VAT invoice, if issued, and any customer TIN or foreign business registration evidence for cross-border services

Cyprus currently has no mandatory real-time national fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime applicable to ordinary B2B invoicing. E-invoicing is permitted and may be submitted through the Tax Department platforms. Mandatory e-invoicing applies to invoices issued to Cypriot public-sector contracting authorities under the EU public procurement e-invoicing regime using the PEPPOL/European standard. Cyprus is expected to align with EU ViDA requirements, including future mandatory e-invoicing for cross-border B2B supplies, at the applicable EU-wide phased deadlines.

Export zero-rating is subject to intensive audit by the Cyprus Tax Department, the Cyprus customs authorities, and EU anti-fraud authorities. If the statutory documentation is absent, incomplete, inconsistent, or withheld, the Tax Department will reassess the supply at the standard 19% VAT rate, disallow any input VAT recovery, impose statutory interest, and may apply administrative penalties. Deliberate or fraudulent misuse of export zero-rating can lead to criminal prosecution, monetary fines, court-imposed penalties, retrospective clawback of refunds and credits, and disqualification of the taxpayer's VAT standing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Cyprus for both current and capital account transactions. No foreign exchange approval is required for inbound or outbound remittances, including profit repatriation, dividends, and repayments of shareholder loans. Practical hurdles include anti-money laundering compliance, sanctions screening, mandatory beneficial ownership disclosure, CRS/FATCA reporting, documentary evidence of the underlying commercial substance, and bank-specific verification of the source of funds. Dividend remittances require clean audited financial statements, a Cyprus tax residence certificate, and confirmation that the 0% dividend withholding tax and special defence contribution rules are satisfied under the current legal framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted to euros freely. Banks are, however, required to collect and report balance-of-payments data to the Central Bank of Cyprus for individual cross-border transactions exceeding the applicable ECB/CBC reporting thresholds, using standard transaction purpose codes. The underlying commercial invoices, contracts, and shipping/delivery evidence must be retained for verification and reporting.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα Κύπρου) in coordination with the Cyprus Ministry of Finance and the European Central Bank; exchange control was abolished upon EU accession, and the CBC acts as the national competent authority for balance-of-payments reporting, anti-money laundering supervision of credit institutions, and macroprudential oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Cyprus accepts regulated EU/EEA Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passporting under PSD2 for payment accounts and cross-border rails such as Wise and similar digital EMIs. Non-EEA EMIs, including US-chartered platforms like Mercury, cannot operate lawfully as principal payment service providers in Cyprus without a Cyprus or EU/EEA license or an EU agent/established branch arrangement. Legal constraints include mandatory safeguarding of client funds, strict AML/KYC duties, transaction monitoring, and the prohibition of unlicensed payment services. The preferred structure is a tier-1 domestic bank account as the treasury anchor, supplemented by Wise and EU-licensed EMI accounts for operational media-buying disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be routed via a licensed credit institution using SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202/MT103 for interbank transfers, with a clear payment reference e.g. 'Share Capital Contribution ─ CY_CY_LTD'. The corporate treasury must use a segregated Capital Escrow Account for the initial injection before transferring to the Operational Account. The transferring bank must include the ECB/CBC balance-of-payments purpose code appropriate for direct investment (e.g. DI for equity capital) and the legal entity identifier where available. The receiving bank will require the share subscription document, board resolution, and source-of-funds evidence; funds are typically made available T+1. Non-EU inbound wires should use an EU correspondent account; US-dollar injections may be converted to euro at the central bank reference rate or a negotiated interbank spread.Outbound trade disbursements for affiliate and media-buying settlements use three primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 customer credits for international publisher and vendor payments in USD, EUR, and other currencies, subject to sanctions screening; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit for larger vendor contracts, although rarely used in digital advertising due to the nature of performance media; (3) EU SEPA Instant/SEPA Credit Transfers for euro-denominated payments to EU-based affiliates and publishers. The domestic bank acts as the principal clearing sponsor, with the fintech EMI (e.g. Wise) serving as a supplementary cross-border rail for small-ticket, high-frequency affiliate payments. All disbursements must be supported by underlying evidence: media insertion orders, performance reports, publisher invoices, and affilinet-traffic data, and must be assigned the appropriate CBC balance-of-payments purpose code. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form IR 328 must be submitted within 12 months after the end of the accounting period. For a 31 December fiscal year-end, the statutory deadline is 31 December of the following year; for other year-ends, the due date is 12 months from the accounting period close. Provisional tax payments (through the quarterly/installment system) comply with separate July and December deadlines.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions (cross-border and Cyprus-resident) triggers mandatory Local File preparation; Master File and CbCR documentation arises where the ultimate group parent has consolidated turnover above EUR 750,000,000; any aggregate related-party transactions above EUR 200,000 must also be declared in the related-party schedule appended to Form IR 328.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has no standalone four-pillar Caribbean-style ESR certificate, but it applies economically substantive requirements through statutory tax residence (central management and control), EU ATAD, OECD BEPS/GloBE, and the CySEC licensing framework where relevant. A Cyprus affiliate media-buying Ltd must demonstrate: (a) board meetings and strategic decisions made in Cyprus; (b) an active Cyprus office with appropriate staff; (c) execution of contracts in Cyprus; (d) its own capital, risk-bearing capacity, and investment in IT or data infrastructure; (e) local bank accounts and audited books maintained in Cyprus; and (f) an arm's-length transfer price for any functions performed for non-resident principals. Without such substance, the Cyprus Tax Department may re-characterize the entity as a conduit or shell and refuse residence certificate and tax treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Under Article 61 of the Cyprus Income Tax Law and the Tax Assessment Collection Law, the Commissioner recomputes tax plus arrears interest at 1.25% per month; taxable penalties may be as high as double the evaded tax; conviction carries criminal fines, publication of judgments, and imprisonment up to five years plus confiscation of the non-base and related proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and the shadow director beneficially controlling the affiliate operation are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of underpaid taxes and penalties. Courts may impose travel bans, asset-freezing orders, passport surrender, director disqualification, and, where evasion is systemic, imprisonment under the Criminal Code and AML forfeiture provisions.
Shell Company and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department may disregard the Cyprus entity, reallocate income to a foreign permanent establishment or to the beneficially owned ultimate parent, refuse a tax residence certificate, apply the CFC/ATAD rules, and impose doubled taxes and withholding obligations; the entity is exposed to strike-off under the Companies Law for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: A non-resident beneficial owner acting as de facto director faces recovery from the corporate veil: courts can pierce the veil for evasive avoidance, order civil restitution, impose travel bans on directors who flee Cyprus, and refer cases to the Cyprus police for criminal insolvency or fraud if company assets are dissipated.
Transfer Pricing Abuse / Failure to Provide Documentation

Statutory sanction: Cyprus transfer-pricing adjustments add the arm's-length difference to taxable profit, charge 12.5% tax, plus interest at 1.25% per month. Where documentation is absent, an anti-tampering penalty of 5% per month on the deficiency, restriction on the related-party deduction, and a fine up to EUR 20,000 or double understatement may be imposed under TP regulations.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve uneconomic intercompany service contracts or circular commissions may be personally liable for the resulting tax deficiency if the transaction is a sham or fraudulent preference. They can also be reported for AML filing charged with negligence, and sanctions include disqualification orders and personal restitution to the tax authority.
AML and Payment Rails Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law 188(I) of 2007, together with the Proceeds of Crime Law, imposes administrative fines on corporate entities up to EUR 250,000 and, on conviction, confiscation of the laundering amount, proportionately high criminal fines, and imprisonment up to five years; the Cyprus Unit for Combating Money Laundering (MOKAS) can freeze accounts and order stripping of corporate layers.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are personally exposed: travel bans, passport surrender, disqualification, and personal criminal liability for AML violations; MOKAS may charge every director who controls the payment routing process even when corporate entity is dissolved or struck off.
Late Filing and Underpayment of Provisional Tax

Statutory sanction: Automatic administrative surcharge of EUR 100 per month of continued default plus interest at 1.25% per month on arrears; failure to file IR 328 precludes issuance of a tax clearance certificate, triggering bank account freezing, vendor/payment processor suspension, and incapacity to execute dividend distributions or M&A transfers.

Director personal liability: Directors continuing to operate while the Cyprus Ltd is tax-insolvent face fraudulent trading liability under the Companies Law; the court may order them to compensate creditors and tax authorities, impose a debit on personal assets, issue travel bans, and order contempt imprisonment for non-cooperation with insolvency examinations.

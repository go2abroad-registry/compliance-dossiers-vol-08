Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance; official portal: Tax Portal (TaxForAll) at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy. Cyprus VAT and corporate tax registrations, filings, and correspondence are handled through this portal and the local District Tax Office.

E-government filing portal

After incorporation with the Cyprus Registrar of Companies, the company obtains its statutory registration number (HE number). It must then register for tax purposes with the competent District Tax Office in the district of its registered office. The application for the Tax Identification Number (TIN) must be supported by the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, passport copies and proof of address of the beneficial owners/directors, and, where applicable, the physical office address declaration. A foreign entity carrying on business through a Cyprus permanent establishment or fixed base must file a TIN registration application with the District Tax Office, appending a certified copy of the foreign incorporation certificate, audited financial statements or equivalent evidence of local activity, and proof of appointment of a local representative. VAT registration is made using the standard VAT registration application (VAT 101 / TD101) or through the Tax Portal; the resulting VAT number is issued together with a VAT certificate. Electronic registration via the Tax Portal requires authentication with the Cyprus ARIADNI or e-Government digital certificate for directors or authorised officers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Cyprus-resident private limited company is subject to Cyprus corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide income. A non-resident company is taxed only on Cyprus-source income attributable to a permanent establishment in Cyprus. The entity-level status is determined by legal form, place of incorporation, and central management and control; no pass-through classification is available to a Cyprus company.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box or general hybrid-mismatch election regime. Entity classification is based on legal form and effective management. Available elections include: IP Box (LDK) regime election for qualifying intangible assets yielding an effective 2.5% tax rate; Notional Interest Deduction election on new equity; group relief (loss offset) election between Cyprus tax-resident group companies; tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping activities; election to be treated as Cyprus tax resident by central management and control; and foreign tax credit elections under the Cyprus-UK/EU bilateral treaty framework. The EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) hybrid mismatch provisions apply automatically; taxpayers have no discretion to opt into hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 per 12-month period for taxable supplies of goods and services; EUR 35,000 for intra-Community distance sales of goods; no threshold for non-established suppliers supplying electronic services to non-taxable persons in Cyprus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to: (a) direct export of goods to destinations outside the EU, provided the goods physically leave the customs territory of the Union and transport/export evidence is obtained; (b) intra-Community supplies of goods to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number where the goods are dispatched or transported to another Member State and the EC Sales List and Intrastat returns are filed when due; (c) supplies of services directly connected with export goods (e.g., freight, handling, inspection) to a non-taxable person outside the EU; and (d) B2B services exported to a taxable customer outside Cyprus pursuant to the general place-of-supply rule (customer location rule), subject to obtaining the customer's valid TIN/VAT ID and, where required, a written confirmation of non-taxability. Zero-rating is accepted only if the supplier retains contemporaneous, verifiable evidence proving dispatch/export and, for services, the residence/status of the customer.

  • Customs export declaration with Movement Reference Number (MRN) generated from the Customs Electronic System (ECS/ICS)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR or courier waybill evidencing departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of departure from EU customs territory (customs stamp, export customs declaration, or electronic incident-free status from ECS)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or the bank transfer receipt evidencing offshore payment settlement
  • EC Sales List and relevant VAT return declaration where the supply qualifies as an intra-Community supply

Cyprus does not currently impose a general real-time e-invoicing clearance, fiscal stamping, or API-based transaction reporting mandate. E-invoices are legally valid under Cyprus VAT law if they meet authenticity, integrity, and readibility requirements. For B2G transactions with Cypriot public authorities, e-invoicing is mandatory in line with EU Directive 2014/55/EU using PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 where required. Cyprus is aligned with the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) reform, but real-time transaction-based reporting is not yet operational.

The Tax Department of Cyprus and Cyprus Customs may retrospectively reject zero-rating if the exporter fails to produce the required customs/export evidence or the stated foreign currency is not repatriated within any applicable regulatory deadline. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims result in reassessment of the VAT at 19%, statutory late-payment interest, administrative penalties (currently up to EUR 500 per default and enhanced penalties for deliberate misstatement), and potential criminal prosecution. Courts may also impose clawback of the VAT refund, asset seizure, and disqualification of directors. The recovery period for tax arising from fraudulent VAT claims is extended beyond the normal limitation period, and joint and several liability may attach to officers involved in preparing export or refund documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for all EU/EEA and Swiss counterparties; there are no quantitative exchange-control restrictions, surrender quotas, or dual-tier currency markets. For non-EU inbound and outbound transfers, convertibility is absolute in EUR terms, but practical remittance hurdles arise solely from AML/CFT conditionality: in-bound equity injections require verified UBO disclosure and source-of-wealth documentation, while out-bound profit remittances require a signed board resolution, distributable profit evidence, and tax clearance certificates for large dividend payments. No prior Central Bank permission is needed for any current or capital payment, though banks must apply the CBC's statistical reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory conversion ratio to EUR, and no compulsory repatriation timeline. Export receipts may be retained in any convertible currency. The only binding obligation is statistical: all export earnings must be reported to the settlement bank within the statutory balance-of-payments window (typically five business days after contractual due date) in accordance with CBC Directive on External Transactions Reporting. The invoice or receipt must be cross-referenced in the SWIFT/SEPA mandate, and any departure from the underlying commercial terms must be documented for the bank's transaction-monitoring file.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κενδρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου, fully authorized under the Central Bank of Cyprus Law 2002 and the Cyprus Exchange Control Law, Cap. 109), acting as the national monetary authority, prudential banking and payment-services supervisor, and the competent foreign-exchange administration for the Republic of Cyprus. Within the Eurosystem, the CBC implements ECB policy and enforces euro-area capital-movement liberalisation under Article 63 TFEU.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for European inbound receipts, but Low for the primary operating/capital escrow relationship due to stricter KYC substance rules and borrower-lender rationalisation. Legal constraints: an EU-licensed EMI with valid passport into Cyprus under PSD2 may open low-risk, name-bearing IBANs for incoming subscription payments, but it cannot act as the company's sole liquidity concentration platform for a medical SaaS exporter without local AML registration and proof of data-safeguarding. Non-EU digital EMIs are generally rejected for a Cyprus corporate treasury function unless they maintain an actual Cyprus branch or licensed correspondent bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route for an inbound equity or shareholder loan injection is a SWIFT MT103 (non-EUR) or SEPA Credit Transfer (EUR) with the payment narrative quoting 'Capital Contribution – Article 113 Companies Law' but not using the word 'loan' unless the underlying agreement is an actual loan. The receiving bank in Cyprus must validate the beneficiary (company legal ID), the UBO, and the purpose code from the CBC's electronic BoP form, typically 'INV – Inward Direct Investment', or for a shareholder loan 'Cur. acc.' category. After validation, funds settle via TARGET2 into banks in Cyprus within one business day, though the bank may hold the credit for up to three days pending EDD on the remitter.Outgoing settlements for trade, subscription, licensing and medical-cloud service payments will use SWIFT MT103 for direct correspondent transfers, MT101 for high-volume batch payment initiation, and MT202 for interbank liquidity under treasury instructions. When the counterparty insists on a documentary trade instrument, a Commercial Letter of Credit is issued via SWIFT MT700 with documents limited to an invoice, proof of service delivery certificate, and optionally an electronic ARP (AP) acknowledgment of invoice correctness. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if intercompany transactions exceed €750,000 (services) or €1,000,000 (goods); Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds €15 million; CbC report if group revenue exceeds €750 million.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has adopted Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for entities engaged in relevant activities (e.g., IP holding, financing, leasing); software development is not explicitly listed but general substance requirements apply: adequate premises, employees, and management in Cyprus; non-resident companies must demonstrate real economic presence to avoid being deemed a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 100% of tax evaded; criminal prosecution with fines up to €50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment.
Shell Company (Lack of Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax benefits, reclassification of income, penalties up to €20,000; potential strike-off from register

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they fail to ensure substance; travel bans and disqualification as director.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €500,000 for legal entities; criminal liability for individuals; suspension of operations

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; imprisonment up to 5 years; travel bans and asset freezes.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties up to 10% of adjusted amount, interest on underpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; civil claims for restitution.

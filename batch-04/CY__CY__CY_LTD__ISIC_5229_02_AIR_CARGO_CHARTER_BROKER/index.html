Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus — TaxisNet and Tax For All electronic portal (https://taxforall.mof.gov.cy); company registration supervised by the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus private limited company is automatically issued a Tax Identification Code (TIN) by the Tax Department upon incorporation with the Registrar of Companies; no separate application is needed for the TIN. A non-resident foreign entity without Cyprus incorporation must file Form TIN 101 (Application for Registration of a Non-Cyprus Company) together with certified constitutional documents, extract from the foreign register, proof of registered office, and director identification. If the foreign entity makes or intends to make taxable supplies of goods or services in Cyprus, it must additionally file Form VAT-101 (Application for VAT Registration) with the VAT Commissioner; a non-established taxable person is required to appoint a tax representative approved by the Commissioner to act on its behalf for VAT purposes. The VAT registration number is issued upon approval and the entity is entered into the Cyprus and EU VIES database.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Cyprus private limited company is a separate taxable person subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide income; the company is not eligible for pass-through treatment. Cyprus applies a quasi-territorial system: dividends, foreign permanent establishment profits, and certain foreign interest income are statutorily exempt under the 80/20 holding rules, and the company may claim foreign tax credits under double tax treaties or unilateral relief. No tax-exempt zone status applies to air cargo charter brokerage.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Cyprus. Available statutory elections and relief mechanisms include: (i) group loss relief election for companies within a 75% group; (ii) notional interest deduction (NID) election on new equity introduced from 1 January 2015; (iii) Intellectual Property Box election granting 80% deduction on qualifying IP profits (effective rate 2.5%); (iv) deemed dividend distribution rules allowing disclosure of deemed distributions to reduce corporate tax assessment; (v) election to treat a foreign permanent establishment as a branch and claim treaty credit or unilateral relief; and (vi) ATAD-aligned interest limitation rule elections for excess borrowing costs. The mixed-maximizing anti-hybrid rules under EU Directive 2016/1164 are implemented, with no statutory hybrid mismatch election available to ordinary trading companies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for taxable persons established in Cyprus (including companies); no threshold applies to non-established taxable persons — they must register from the first taxable supply in Cyprus. The EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual distance-sales threshold applies to cross-border distance sales of goods into Cyprus from other member states. Where the charter broker exclusively supplies B2B cross-border services that are outside the scope of Cyprus VAT under the general place-of-supply rule, no VAT registration may be required, but an EORI number remains necessary for customs operations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to: (i) the export of goods dispatched or transported by or on behalf of the supplier to a destination outside the EU, provided the goods leave the EU within 90 days of despatch (twelve months for goods subject to outward processing or re-export under inward processing regimes); (ii) services directly connected with the international carriage of goods, including air cargo charter of aircraft for international navigation; (iii) ancillary services such as cargo handling, storage, and freight-forwarding supplied in direct connection with international carriage; and (iv) B2B brokerage and commission services supplied to a taxable customer established outside Cyprus, which fall outside the scope of Cyprus VAT or are zero-rated where the place of supply remains Cyprus but the transaction qualifies as an intra-Community or export-related ancillary service. For the brokerage of air cargo aircraft charter, the zero-rating is available only upon receipt of the customer's valid VAT identification number (for intra-Community reverse-charge) or conclusive documentary proof of the physical export and international voyage. Failure to comply with documentary requirements shifts the liability to standard-rated 19% VAT on the full commission.

  • Customs Export Declaration — electronic export declaration lodged in the Cyprus THESEAS/ICS2 system (or accepted Customs Export Manifest) evidencing exit from the EU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — negotiable or non-negotiable transport document naming the Cyprus broker, the charterer, and the consignee, with date and port/airport of departure and place of unloading outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank confirmation of receipt of export proceeds converted from foreign currency, or equivalent proof of payment via Swift wire/Fedwire for the invoiced charter fee
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms, aircraft charter agreement or brokerage commission agreement, aircraft registration and crew documents, packing list, certificate of origin, and delivery/collection notes evidencing the physical movement of cargo or movement of the chartered aircraft

Cyprus does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction controls (CTC) model; there is no mandatory digital stamping or e-invoice approval by the tax authority. All VAT returns (Form VAT 300), recapitulative statements (VIES), intra-Community acquisition listings, and periodic statistical filings must be submitted electronically through the TaxisNet/Tax For All portal. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary and must comply with Article 51 of the VAT Law (authentication, integrity of content, and correct VAT identification). Cyprus has transposed EU Directive 2014/55/EU for mandatory e-invoicing in central public procurement (PEPPOL-compliant formats), but no B2B mandate or government pre-clearance of invoices is in force. Standard audit utilities require the company to maintain a complete chronological invoice register and to provide electronic files (SAP/EDIFACT or PDF/A) upon request.

The Tax Department and VAT Commissioner may retrospectively adjust zero-rated supplies and claw back VAT where the documentary evidence fails within the standard four-year assessment period (extended to ten years in cases of fraud or deliberate default). Penalties for erroneous or fraudulent zero-rating include: (i) civil penalty of up to EUR 1,000 per false return plus 10% of the tax due; (ii) interest on unpaid VAT at 1.75% per month (compound in nature) from the due date; (iii) assessment of the full 19% VAT on brokerage commissions without offset of input VAT where intent to avoid tax is established; and (iv) criminal liability — upon conviction for tax evasion or customs fraud, fines up to EUR 50,000 or EUR 500,000 for corporate entities and/or imprisonment of up to five years for responsible officers. The Cyprus Customs and Excise Department may also seize the cargo, aircraft, or export securities and cancel the freight forwarder/CARGO charter broker licence for ISIC 5229 where forged export declarations or double-use of export documents are discovered. In practice, the Tax Department cross-checks Bill of Lading exit dates, VIES declarations, and bank inward remittance records, and a broker claiming zero-rating without repatriation of proceeds within 180 days faces a rebuttable presumption of non-export and irrevocable VAT clawback with penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full member of the eurozone; the euro is legal tender and there are no exchange controls. Both current-account and capital-account transfers are freely permitted to and from non-residents under EU Treaty capital-movement rules. Profit remittance is fully unrestricted; no Cypriot withholding tax is imposed on dividends paid to bona fide non-resident corporate shareholders, provided the company has sufficient distributable reserves and is not artificially routed through an abusive EU law advantage. The temporary capital restrictions introduced in 2013 have been fully abolished; no transaction approvals, currency purchase authorisations, or passporting permits are required for ordinary treasury operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus does not impose any statutory surrender requirement, timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds. Exporters may retain export receipts in euro or in freely convertible foreign currencies without restriction. The only obligation is statistical: local banks must report cross-border BOP/ITRS data to the Central Bank of Cyprus on the underlying commercial purpose, but no income needs to be mandatorily sold or repatriated to Cyprus.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza Kyprou / Κεντρική Τράπεζα Κύπρου) — the national central bank of the Republic of Cyprus; as a member of the Eurosystem, it participates in the European System of Central Banks and administers Cyprus's balance-of-payments and international investment position reporting under the European Central Bank and EU legal framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-low as a primary treasury rail, but acceptable for segregated contingency payments. A Cyprus-licensed Electronic Money Institution, or an EU/EEA EMI passported into Cyprus under the EU E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC), may open a corporate operational account, issue payment cards, and process SEPA/MT103-equivalent transfers. Offshore digital EMIs without a Cyprus passport or a licensed branch in Cyprus cannot lawfully provide payment services to a Cyprus private limited company. EMIs should not be relied upon for large aircraft-charter settlements, escrow obligations, or standby letters of credit; their weaker credit profile, narrower FX product range, and lack of access to commercial LC clearing limit their suitability.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification numbers; local Finanzamt (tax office) for Steuernummer and corporate tax registration; ELSTER online portal (www.elster.de) for electronic filing and registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign GmbH must register with the competent local Finanzamt by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (Questionnaire on Tax Registration), either in writing or via ELSTER. The Finanzamt assigns a Steuernummer (corporate income tax/VAT account number). For cross-border transactions, the entity must separately apply to the BZSt for a Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID, DE prefix) using Form USt 1 TN. Registration must occur promptly upon commencing German-source economic activities; a fiscal representative may be required for third-country entities. The GmbH's trade tax registration is made with the local municipality via the Finanzamt and Gewerbeamt when a permanent establishment or place of business exists in Germany.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box or pass-through election for a GmbH. A GmbH is always taxed as a separate tax resident corporation (Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer, solidarity surcharge). Available elections relevant to group structures include the formation of an Organschaft (tax group) for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT purposes, subject to financial, economic and organisational integration. Germany also applies ATAD hybrid mismatch rules; taxpayers must make transparency or deemed-tax elections and file Form OGH for hybrid mismatch arrangements to avoid denial of deductions or inclusion mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 (previous calendar year turnover) and EUR 50,000 (current year projection) for the small business exemption under §19 UStG; however, foreign entities without a domestic establishment generally may not rely on the small-business exemption and are required to register for VAT on the first taxable supply in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Germany to third countries (outside the EU) qualify for the VAT zero rate under §4 No. 1 and §6 UStG, provided the seller is the exporter, the goods physically leave Germany, and the export is verifiable. Exports to EU member states are not zero-rated supplies for VAT purposes; they are internal-supply transactions (innergemeinschaftliche Lieferungen) that may be exempt under §4 No. 1(b) and §6a UStG when the purchaser has a valid VAT ID and the goods move to another member state. Offshore services for non-EU customers may also be zero-rated if they fall within the place-of-supply rules of §§3a-3f UStG and are not taxable in Germany. Documentary evidence must prove the occurrence of the export and the identity of the recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung/ATLAS) with MRN number
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure
  • Customs Proof of Exit (Ausfuhrnachweis) generated by customs or electronic transit system
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment confirming the foreign buyer's consideration transfer

Since 1 January 2025, e-invoicing (structured XML format, e.g. XRechnung or ZUGFeRD) is mandatory for all domestic B2B transactions between German-established taxable persons, with phased enforcement until 2028. Since 1 January 2027, B2C e-invoice obligations apply to certain operators; B2G invoices must already be submitted via Peppol or a compatible national e-invoicing portal. Germany does not currently operate a real-time transaction clearance or digital stamping system (like Italy's SDI or France's Factur-X); the BZSt performs risk-based audits using e-invoice data, but there is no real-time fiscal authentication required.

The German tax authorities rigorously audit export zero-rating claims. If documentation is incomplete, the export exemption is denied and the VAT liability is retrospectively assessed, plus late-payment and default interest at 6% per annum (§233a AO, §240 AO). Intentional or reckless fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under §370 AO, attract penalties up to EUR 100,000 (criminal fines), imprisonment up to 5 years, and full VAT clawback plus interest. Administrative fines under §378 AO apply to negligent cases. In cross-border refund proceedings, burdens of proof and strict evidentiary standards under §§6, 8-10 UStDV must be satisfied; failure to meet the 180-day evidence deadline may permanently forfeit the VAT exemption.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full euro convertibility and no exchange controls on current or capital account, consistent with Article 63 TFEU (free movement of capital). Equity contributions, loan repayments, trade settlements and profit remittances require no FX licence, approval, exchange-rate quotation or repatriation filing. The practical hurdles to profit remittance are limited to: (i) German withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) at 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, producing an effective 26.375% dividend withholding tax, reduced or eliminated by tax treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EEA corporate shareholders; (ii) Deutsche Bundesbank statistical reporting under AWV; and (iii) transfer-pricing/fiscal discipline requiring that dividend and royalty flows to foreign shareholders avoid hidden profit distribution (verdeckte Gewinnausschüttung) recharacterisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion or repatriation rule for export receipts: the applicable mandatory conversion ratio is 0% of export proceeds, which may be retained indefinitely in USD, CNY or any other currency in foreign-currency accounts at German banks. The binding administrative timeline is the AWV payment-reporting deadline: any cross-border receipt or payment equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 (per transaction, or aggregated per counterparty and payment purpose within a calendar month) must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank by the 7th banking day of the following month. When the receipt is credited through a German bank, the bank files the AWV-Zahlung report automatically; the GmbH remains responsible for retaining the underlying invoices, contracts, customs declarations and transport documents as evidence, and must report directly when a transaction is executed outside the banking channel.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany), operating within the Eurosystem jointly with the European Central Bank; cross-border payment and foreign-exchange statistical controls are administered under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), with reporting obligations enforced and collected by Deutsche Bundesbank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability Rating: LOW for offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA (UK, US, Singapore, UAE), which lack a German or EU payment-services licence and cannot lawfully open German IBAN accounts or hold client funds under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz) and the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz). Viability Rating: MEDIUM for EEA-licensed EMIs and e-money platforms used strictly as ancillary collection or vendor-payment engines; they cannot issue documentary letters of credit, cannot extend credit lines, and the GmbH bears all AWV/Bundesbank reporting and GwG/AML obligations. A principal operating account with a German commercial bank remains mandatory for the statutory capital escrow, SEPA/SWIFT clearing, trade-finance and FX-hedging infrastructure.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation (Tax Department) and the VAT Service, both under the Cyprus Ministry of Finance. E-filing portal: ‘Ariadni’ (smart.mof.gov.cy); official site: www.tax.gov.cy.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus-incorporated private limited company automatically has a Cyprus Corporate Tax Resident status and must apply for a Tax Identification Code (TIC) through the Tax Department after publication of the Certificate of Incorporation; the application is submitted via the Ariadni portal or through the District Tax Office together with the certifying copy of the memorandum/articles. Foreign-incorporated entities with a permanent establishment in Cyprus must register for income tax within 60 days of commencing business. For VAT, a taxable person must register with the VAT Service within 30 days of exceeding the €15,600 annual turnover threshold; non-resident suppliers without an establishment but making distance sales or B2B supplies may register under the Union One Stop Shop or apply directly.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (i) export of goods outside the EU, (ii) intra-Community supplies to a customer holding a valid VAT identification number, (iii) services directly connected to such exports and related cross-border transport services, and certain intermediary services. Eligibility is evidenced by leaving the goods from the EU within the applicable statutory period and retaining the customs export declaration and transport documentation; for intra-Community supplies, the customer’s VAT number must be valid and reflected in VIES at the time of supply.

Cyprus does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for conventional B2B/B2C invoices; e-invoicing is permitted but only mandatory for B2G/public procurement via Peppol-compliant platforms. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility is operative. Cyprus adopted the euro on 1 January 2008 and, as an EU member state, is bound by TFEU Articles 63-66 requiring complete liberalisation of both current-account and capital-account transactions; there is no FX approval quota, no balance-of-payments licence, no distinction between current and capital account, and no restriction on profit remittance. The only impediments are corporate-law based: dividends must be paid from distributable profits under the Cyprus Companies Law (Cap. 113), evidenced by audited financial statements and a board resolution; payments to non-resident shareholders are free of Cypriot withholding tax and of the Special Defence Contribution (SDC), and the deemed-dividend distribution regime applies only to Cyprus tax residents. A correctly documented dividend remittance therefore clears without central-bank consent, subject solely to sanctions screening and source-of-funds verification by the paying bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None in force. The Exchange Control Law (Cap. 109) licensing and surrender framework was dismantled during Cyprus's alignment with the EU acquis and its controls were fully lifted upon EU accession (effective 1 May 2004); the Central Bank of Cyprus imposes no surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate export proceeds to Cyprus. Export receipts in EUR or any convertible foreign currency may be retained, converted, netted against import payables or freely transferred. The only operative duties are statistical (reporting of the receipt to KTK under the ECB balance-of-payments/ITRS regime) and compliance-based (screening against EU/UN financial sanctions lists before crediting or disbursing).

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (KTK), the national central bank within the Eurosystem, is the competent authority for banking supervision, payment-system oversight and residual exchange-control administration; the ECB holds monetary-policy competence for the euro; the Cyprus Ministry of Finance and CySEC share regulatory responsibilities for sanctions policy, anti-money-laundering oversight and any licensed investment/e-money activity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 (aggregate cross-border controlled transactions per annum) triggers the mandatory Local File; Master File required where the MNE group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000; Country-by-Country Report applies at the same EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group threshold. A transfer pricing / related-party summary (TP table) is submitted with Form IR1 in all cases involving material related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate a standalone 'Economic Substance Regulations' code, but applies materially equivalent OECD/EU substance standards. A Cyprus company must demonstrate place of effective management (POEM) in Cyprus (board decisions, premises, employees, risks borne). The IP Box is conditional on the Modified Nexus Approach: qualifying expenditure, Cyprus-based core income-generating activities (development, enhancement, maintenance of software), and adequate staff. Cyprus also implements EU ATAD rules (CFC, interest limitation, exit taxation) which, together with substance documentation, are used by the Tax Department to deny treaty and IP Box benefits where economic substance is not objectively established.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Corrective assessment with back-interest and penalties; administrative penalty up to 100% of the tax under-assessed; additional surcharges; criminal conviction on indictment exposes the company to fines up to EUR 20,000 and imprisonment of responsible officers up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or authorize fraudulent dispositions face criminal prosecution in their personal capacity; courts may impose personal civil restitution to the Tax Commissioner, prohibition to act as a director, attachment of personal assets, and travel bans / movement restriction orders pending criminal proceedings.
Brass Plate / Shell Company Abusing Substance Requirements

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of tax residence or disregard of the Cyprus entity; full denial of IP Box and NID deductions; reassessment at 12.5% on the whole profit with interest and penalties; automatic exchange of country-by-country information; possible inclusion of the entity's profits in the shareholder's home jurisdiction under CFC rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors can be held personally liable for taxes underestimated by the shell structure if the corporate veil is pierced for fraud; they face disqualification under Cypriot company law, joint and several liability for restoration of the company's assets, and prosecution for operating an untrue speculation business.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's Length Failure

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment by the Tax Commissioner with additional tax of up to 100% of the under-assessment; late-payment surcharges and interest; administrative penalties for non-submission of documentation; referral to the competent authority for penalty proceedings under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve TP policies without documentation expose themselves to personal liability for the shortfall in tax if the company is left without assets; criminal sanctions apply where false documentation was supplied to the tax auditor.
AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing Violation

Statutory sanction: Supervisory fines under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (L.188(I)/2007 as amended) of up to EUR 500,000 per breach and up to 10% of annual turnover for serious, systemic or repeated breaches; seizure of related funds; MOKAS freezing orders; possible debarment from merchant payment services.

Director personal liability: Directors and the appointed compliance officer can be held personally and criminally liable for intentional or reckless failures to report suspicious transactions; penalties include fines and imprisonment up to 5 years, orders disqualifying the director from holding office, asset forfeiture, and restrictions on leaving Cyprus during investigation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance — ARIADNE online tax portal (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy); customs matters administered by the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department under the same Ministry

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus private limited company (HE77) is automatically registered for tax purposes upon incorporation at the Department of Registrar of Companies; its 8-digit company registration number (HE######) serves as the Tax Identification Code (TIC). Foreign entities establishing a branch or permanent establishment must file Form TAN 101 with the local District Tax Office within 60 days of commencing business. A non-resident company without a PE but earning Cyprus-source income must appoint a tax representative and obtain a TIC via Form TAN 101. Separate VAT registration is required via Form VAT 101 (mandatory before the first taxable supply; no exemption threshold for non-established suppliers). Registration is free of charge and typically completed within 3–5 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Cyprus tax-resident company (central management and control in Cyprus) is taxed on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate of 12.5%; non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. No pass-through treatment applies to a Ltd. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Cyprus tax-resident company (central management and control in Cyprus) is taxed on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate of 12.5%; non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. No pass-through treatment applies to a Ltd. Participation exemption applies to dividends and capital gains on disposal of securities; interest, royalties and service income are taxed net of allowable deductions.

Available election forms

Available elections: (1) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) on new equity (Article 9(2)(ag) Income Tax Law); (2) IP Box regime election providing an 80% deduction of qualifying IP profits based on the nexus ratio; (3) Group loss relief election (Article 24 of the Income Tax Law; 75% group threshold); (4) Election for the 50% tax credit on intra-group dividend chains; (5) Voluntary VAT registration for entities below the €15,600 threshold in certain B2B circumstances. No US-style check-the-box regime; hybrid mismatch elections and deemed-disclosure obligations are governed by ATAD implementing Law 38(I)/2020 — any Cypriot entity whose classification differs from that of the counterparty jurisdiction must be disclosed in the annual tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 taxable turnover (12-month rolling period) for locally established suppliers; NO threshold for non-established foreign suppliers (mandatory registration before first supply); €10,251.61 for intra-Community acquisitions of goods; €0 for electronically supplied services from non-established suppliers

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(1) Export of goods to third countries (non-EU): 0% under Article 45 of the VAT Law — physical export must occur and proof of departure (customs SAD) retained, normally within 90 days of supply; (2) Intra-Community dispatch to an EU VAT-registered customer: 0% under Article 44 — requires valid EU VAT ID and VIES validation and inclusion in the EC Sales List; (3) B2B consumer panel survey / market-research SaaS services supplied to a business customer outside Cyprus: OUTSIDE SCOPE of Cyprus VAT (customer is taxable on reverse-charge under Article 22) — technically not zero-rated but yields a 0% effective output charge with no input VAT recovery restriction; (4) B2C digital market-research services to EU consumers: subject to destination-country VAT via the OSS scheme; to non-EU consumers: outside scope. The merchant must demonstrate the customer's business status, true establishment location, and actual service performance for all B2B and B2C classifications.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Single Administrative Document (SAD) / electronic export movement message in Cyprus Customs THESEAS system
  • Bill of Lading (sea) OR Air Waybill (air) OR CMR consignment note (road) evidencing physical export of goods
  • Commercial invoice stating Incoterms, export origin, and consignee details
  • Bank SWIFT confirmation / Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a Cypriot licensed credit institution
  • For B2B services: signed market-research services agreement, customer VAT registration certificate and VIES validation output, survey deployment log and delivery/performance evidence
  • Client due diligence file compiled under AML Law 188(I)/2007 (for PayPal or Wise settlement rails: payer identity, source of funds, and transaction trail)

No real-time fiscal clearance, tax authority digital stamping, or continuous transaction control is currently in force. VAT returns (Form VAT 4) are filed quarterly via the ARIADNE portal with manual entry or CSV/API import; monthly filing applies to OSS registrants and net-tax traders. E-invoicing is not mandatory but structured e-invoices (XML/EDI) are accepted; invoices must be retained for 6 years. Cyprus is bound by the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package — digital reporting and mandatory e-invoicing will apply to cross-border B2B transactions from 2030–2035.

If zero-rating substantiation fails, the Tax Commissioner issues a VAT assessment for 19% of the consideration plus statutory interest at 2% per month (or part thereof); the assessment window is 5 years from the end of the relevant tax period, extended to 10 years for fraudulent or deliberate misdeclaration (Article 47, VAT Law). Civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax underpaid may be imposed under Article 50; criminal prosecution for VAT evasion carries fines up to €5,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years. Fraudulent refund claims trigger retrospective clawback of all refunded input VAT, disqualification from the existing-refund fast-track regime, and publication of the taxpayer's name. Residual Central Bank of Cyprus exchange-control oversight expects export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days of shipment; unexplained non-repatriation may trigger transfer-pricing review under the TICIS framework and deemed-distribution assessments at the 12.5% corporate rate plus 17% special defence contribution exposure on the recipient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Cyprus uses the Euro (EUR) as its sovereign currency. Full convertibility applies to both current and capital account transactions, with no exchange control restrictions remaining since the repeal of the Exchange Control Law and the adoption of the euro in 2008. Profit remittance is free of FX licensing hurdles, provided that dividend distributions are supported by statutory audited financials, board minutes, and documentation evidencing compliance with OECD transfer pricing and EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive substance requirements. Non-resident ownership, repatriation of invested capital, and servicing of intercompany loans are unrestricted under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union on free movement of capital. Certain balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligations and mandatory declaration of cash movements exceeding EUR 10,000 apply under EU Regulation 2018/1672 and Central Bank of Cyprus reporting rules, but these are notification, not control, requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory obligation exists for repatriation, surrender, or mandatory conversion of export proceeds into EUR. Cypriot resident entities may retain foreign currency receipts in authorized credit institution accounts indefinitely. For balance-of-payments statistical purposes, any export receipt or payment transaction exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported by the reporting enterprise to the Central Bank of Cyprus through the approved electronic reporting channels, and cross-border transfers of EUR 10,000 or above are subject to cash movement declaration requirements. No percentage surrender ratio applies, and settlement of export proceeds may be conducted in any freely convertible currency.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Electronically Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise and PayPal operate under EU passporting rights to Cyprus via their home-state PSD2 authorisation, and they are legally permitted to provide payment services and e-money accounts to Cypriot corporate clients without separate Central Bank of Cyprus licensing. However, for the capital escrow function, injection of initial share capital, and high-value operational liquidity, domestic commercial bank accounts remain the primary regulated rails; EMIs are best deployed as supplementary mass-payout (survey participant incentives) rails. Risks include remote EMI account freezes on high-volume disbursements, exposure to statutory compensation scheme exclusions, and occasional insistence by Cypriot tax auditors that statutory escrow duties reside in a fully regulated credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections for the Cyprus entity are routed by SWIFT MT103 or direct TARGET2 payment in EUR from the foreign investor's bank. The creditor reference must state the purpose as direct investment/capital subscription with a standard payment purpose code such as EU 'N831' (direct investment in capital of a Cyprus limited liability company) under the ECB Balance of Payments and International Investment Position statistics classification, or a coherent description referencing equity contribution and share register update. Compliant protocols require (a) a signed board resolution approving the share issuance, (b) an updated share register and Form HE32 notification to the Cyprus Department of Registrar of Companies, (c) source-of-funds declaration from the contributing shareholder, and (d) bank-side verification of the underlying corporate contracts. If the injection is structured as a shareholder loan, the loan agreement, EURIBOR-based interest clause, and tax-compliant transfer pricing documentation must be provided to the receiving bank.For cross-border trade settlements and supplier disbursements, the standard clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct beneficiary transfers in non-EUR currencies and SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for intra-EEA EUR payments. Survey participant mass payouts are efficiently executed via SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) and commercial payment platforms (PayPal Mass Pay and Wise Business), leveraging their aggregated clearing arrangements with the Central Bank of Cyprus and European payment infrastructure. Where definitive documentary security is required for large services contracts, Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700 issued by the Cyprus commercial bank, advised by the beneficiary's bank) and Documentary Collections (SWIFT MT400) are permissible; however, given the low-risk market-research SaaS business model, LC usage is uncommon and typically replaced by advance-payment guarantees or parent-company comfort letters.Outright forward contracts (delivery vs non-deliverable) with authorized Cypriot credit institutionsFX swaps (spot-forward combinations) permitted under the Investment Services Law for hedging purposesPlain vanilla OTC currency options (call/put on foreign exchange) transacted under ISDA master agreementsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for exotic or inconvertible counterparty currencies outside the Eurozone, executed strictly on a trade-backed documentation basis through Cyprus-licensed banks

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: A Cyprus private limited company is exempt from statutory audit only if it qualifies as a 'small company' under the Companies Law and meets at least two of the following three criteria for two consecutive years: turnover not exceeding EUR 700,000, balance sheet total not exceeding EUR 350,000, and average number of employees not exceeding five. Exemption is unavailable for certain entities such as public-interest entities, subsidiaries in a group where a member is not exempt, or companies whose articles or shareholders require an audit. The audit exemption must be exercised by shareholder resolution and is not automatic. For a cross-border consumer panel survey platform with payment rails and non-resident shareholders, a full audit is often procured for bank, investor, or tax treaty credibility.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Cyprus does not impose any withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders. In the case of a Cyprus-resident individual recipient, Special Defence Contribution at 17% may apply; however, for non-resident shareholders no Cypriot dividend WHT is chargeable).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed no later than six months after the end of the tax year (calendar year). For a 31 December year-end, the filing deadline is 30 June of the following year. Provisional tax returns (Form TD200) are due on or before 31 July of the tax year, with provisional tax instalments due 31 July and 31 December of the tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions triggers the obligation to prepare and file a Local File. Category-based de minimis thresholds also apply: EUR 75,000 for financing transactions, services, and intangible property transactions; EUR 250,000 for goods transactions. A Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000, and Country-by-Country Reporting applies at the same EUR 750,000,000 consolidated threshold. Documentation must be submitted to the Cyprus Tax Department within 60 days of a written request, and must be updated no later than 30 June of the following tax year.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) substance requirements, requiring that a Cyprus company be managed and controlled in Cyprus for tax residence and for treaty eligibility. The Cyprus Tax Department denies a Tax Residency Certificate where the company has no genuine board meetings, no employees in Cyprus, and no local premises, and applies a substance-over-form doctrine to recharacterise artificial structures. For IP Box relief, the nexus approach requires that the qualifying expenditure on eligible IP be linked to Cyprus R&D activity, with outsourcing limitations. A market-survey platform with cross-border panel operations must demonstrate local strategic decision-making, employment of data scientists and survey analysts in Cyprus, execution of client contracts from Cyprus, and the physical hosting of platform infrastructure in Cyprus or the EU to satisfy economic substance requirements. The Tax Department may invoke Article 33 of the Income Tax Law (anti-avoidance) to disregard arrangements whose main purpose is tax avoidance and to impose tax as if the arrangement had not been entered into.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Fraudulent Under-Declaration of Cross-Border Survey Revenues

Statutory sanction: On criminal conviction under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, a fine of up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years; the court may additionally order payment of three times the tax underpaid plus interest at 2% per month (capped at 8%) and costs. The Tax Department may reopen assessments within the 6-year limitation period, or within 10 years where fraud or wilful default is established, and may impose a penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false return or knowingly suppress receipts are jointly and severally liable for the tax underpaid through civil restitution proceedings; the Tax Department may apply to the court for a prohibition/ departure order preventing the director from leaving Cyprus until tax liabilities are settled; a criminal conviction exposes directors to imprisonment of up to 5 years and disqualification as a director under the Companies Law for up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department may deny the Tax Residency Certificate, recharacterise the company as non-resident, and draw the company into taxation on its worldwide income at 12.5% where central management and control is in Cyprus; alternatively, Article 33 anti-avoidance rules may disregard the Cyprus entity and tax the underlying global profits of the platform, with interest and penalties up to 100% of the tax underpaid; EU ATAD and CFC rules may also attribute foreign subsidiary income to the Cyprus company.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate form is a facade used to avoid taxes, Cyprus courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for the company's unpaid taxes; directors face civil claims from creditors and tax authorities, travel bans via departure orders, disqualification orders, and criminal liability for fraudulent trading under section 313 of the Companies Law, CAP 113.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of EUR 500 for failure to maintain approved TP documentation; the Tax Department may make a transfer-pricing adjustment to increase taxable profit, impose additional tax at 12.5%, charge interest at 2% per month (capped at 8%), and apply a penalty of 25% of the additional tax assessed. For incorrect returns, a separate fine of EUR 100 or EUR 200 for a repeat offence applies.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise or sign unsubstantiated or non-arm's-length intercompany agreements are personally exposed where the adjustment gives rise to a deemed distribution or a loan written off in favour of a director; personal liability for the additional taxes may be asserted under the fraudulent preference and voidable transaction provisions of the Companies Law, and serious cases are referred for criminal prosecution of aiding tax evasion.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Register Breaches

Statutory sanction: Under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (L.188(I)/2007), administrative fines of up to EUR 500 for the company and EUR 50 for each day of continued default are imposed by the Registrar; criminal offences relating to money laundering carry a penalty of imprisonment of up to 5 years and/or a fine of up to EUR 100,000; the Cyprus Stock Exchange and banking regulators impose additional sanctions and de-risking actions by payment intermediaries such as PayPal and Wise.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly misrepresent the UBO information or who use the payment platforms to conceal the source of funds commit a criminal offence and are personally liable for fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years; where money laundering is proven, the maximum imprisonment term increases to 14 years, and directors face confiscation of personal assets, civil restitution orders, and departure bans.
Payment Intermediaries Compliance Failure (PayPal/Wise Misuse)

Statutory sanction: The Tax Department disallows all unsupported panel-payout deductions, increasing the taxable profit by the disallowed amounts; the disallowed expenses may be reclassified as a deemed distribution to a director or shareholder, triggering Special Defence Contribution at 17% (if the recipient is a Cyprus tax resident) or deemed dividend WHT at 0% for non-residents (with reporting obligations); interest at 2% per month and penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax may be imposed; financial-conduct regulators may report the operator for misuse of an e-money institution licence.

Director personal liability: Directors who misuse personal payment accounts for corporate business are personally liable to restore the amounts to the company, and may face claims for breach of fiduciary duty, fraudulent trading, and money-laundering charges; sustained wrongdoing may result in personal asset confiscation, travel restrictions, and imprisonment of up to 14 years for aggravated money-laundering offences.

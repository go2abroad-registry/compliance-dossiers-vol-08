Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; electronic filing portal: TaxisNet (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned private limited company is incorporated with the Registrar of Companies and receives an HE company number. Within 60 days of commencing business (and before making any taxable supply) it must file the relevant registration forms with the Tax Department to obtain the Tax Identification Code (TIC) and, if taxable turnover exceeds the registration threshold, the Cyprus VAT number. A non-resident entity without a Cyprus fixed establishment may apply directly for VAT registration and is normally required to appoint a registered tax representative for VAT administration purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned private limited company is incorporated with the Registrar of Companies and receives an HE company number. Within 60 days of commencing business (and before making any taxable supply) it must file the relevant registration forms with the Tax Department to obtain the Tax Identification Code (TIC) and, if taxable turnover exceeds the registration threshold, the Cyprus VAT number. A non-resident entity without a Cyprus fixed establishment may apply directly for VAT registration and is normally required to appoint a registered tax representative for VAT administration purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style 'check-the-box' or hybrid mismatch election at entity level. Cyprus law allows: (i) VAT grouping under Article 11 of the VAT Directive for closely-linked entities; (ii) group loss relief for 75%-owned subsidiaries; and (iii) binding advance rulings on income tax, permanent establishment and transfer pricing treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 of taxable turnover in the preceding 12 months for supplies of goods and services; a separate EUR 10,000 Union threshold applies to cross-border B2C distance sales

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) supplies of goods physically transported by the supplier or the non-established purchaser to a destination outside the EU; (b) services directly related to exported goods, including warehousing, handling, cold storage and ancillary transport services, supplied to a person established outside Cyprus, provided the goods leave the EU within the prescribed period and the supplier retains full export evidence; and (c) certain international transport-related services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / SAD)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice / sales contract
  • Bank SWIFT or outward remittance receipt showing settlement in foreign currency
  • Proof of inward delivery to the cold storage facility and subsequent outbound movement

Cyprus currently does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control or digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary but must meet EU norm standards when used. The Tax Department accepts electronic reporting via SAF-T and XML for certain taxpayers and is preparing for the upcoming EU ViDA digital reporting requirements.

Erroneous or fraudulent zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to retrospective VAT assessment at the 19% standard rate, compound interest, penalties ranging up to the amount of VAT evaded (Tax Department discretionary scale), and possible criminal prosecution for tax evasion. Loss of export documentation also disqualifies the supplier from input VAT refunds and may trigger EU customs and VAT compliance sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Not convertible - freely convertible. Cyprus adopted the Euro (EUR) on 1 January 2008 and maintains full current and capital account liberalisation in accordance with Article 63 TFEU and the Exchange Control (Repeal) Law 167/1991. No approval is required for inward or outward transfer of capital, profit remittance, dividend distribution, repayment of shareholder loans, licence fees, or liquidation proceeds. Residual compliance obligations: (i) BoP statistical reporting to CBC for external financial assets/liabilities exceeding EUR 1.5 million in aggregate; (ii) direct investment transactions must be reported to CBC for statistical compilation; (iii) commercial banks may legally demand invoice/contract verification for outward payments above their internal risk threshold. No material hurdles to profit remittance; the 12.5% corporate income tax is the only normal leakage, and no withholding taxes apply on dividends, interest, or royalties paid to non-residents under domestic tax law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender and no forced conversion rules exist. Exporters and service providers may retain 100% of proceeds in EUR or any other convertible currency in Cyprus-licensed banks, EU banks, or foreign banks. No statutory repatriation deadline applies to cold-chain storage receivables. Mandatory obligations are limited to: (i) proper invoicing with VAT/Customs documentation; (ii) AML record retention per the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Law 188(I)/2007 (as amended); (iii) annual BoP questionnaire filing if cross-border turnover exceeds EUR 10 million; and (iv) transfer pricing documentation under Section 33 of the Income Tax Law when invoices are issued by the Cyprus entity for intercompany trade.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC) operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank (ECB) Treaty framework. CBC is the competent licensing and supervisory authority for credit institutions under the Business of Credit Institutions Law 40(I)/1997 (as amended, transposing CRD IV/CRR), for payment services and fintech EMIs under Law 31(I)/2018 (PSD2 implementation), and for balance-of-payments (BoP) statistical reporting under the Law on the Central Bank of Cyprus 1963 (as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a secondary disbursement/collection rail, Low as a primary treasury account. Cyprus-authorised EMIs (e.g., licensed under Law 31(I)/2018) and EEA-passported EMIs (Revolut, PPS, Ebury, Currencycloud) may open settlement accounts for a Cyprus Ltd. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs must safeguard client funds in segregated accounts held at CBC-authorised credit institutions, so the ultimate clearing layer remains a Tier 1 bank; (ii) EMI balances do not benefit from the EUR 100,000 Deposit Guarantee and Resolution of Credit Institutions Scheme; (iii) CBC and correspondent banks apply enhanced reputational screening to EMI-originated flows, potentially freezing pharma-supply payments; (iv) for a licenced refrigerated warehouse entity, the commercial banks expect principal trade flows to settle through a regulated credit institution. Recommended use: low-value EU supplier payments below EUR 10,000 per instruction and non-EUR vendor payouts requiring digital FX conversion.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat corporate income tax rate on taxable profits

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders (no Cyprus withholding tax). The 17% Special Contribution for Defence applies only to Cyprus-resident individuals on actual or deemed dividend distributions and does not attach to non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate controlled transactions per tax year triggers mandatory Local File; Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least EUR 750,000,000. Below EUR 750,000 the taxpayer must still file the Controlled Transactions Summary Table and maintain a contemporaneous arm's-length rationale.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate a BVI/Mauritius-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute; however, economic substance is mandatory for (a) tax residence via central management and control, (b) treaty-benefit claims under the OECD MLI and EU Code of Conduct Group criteria, and (c) ATAD anti-abuse review. A temperature-controlled cold storage operation with owned or leased warehousing, local licensed refrigeration staff, utility consumption and a Cyprus cold-chain logistics permit meets a robust substance test with no red flag.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment, disallowance of deductions, default interest, penalties of up to 3 times the tax evaded, criminal fines and confiscation orders under the Cyprus Income Tax Law (L.118(I)/2002) and the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law (Cap 130).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign knowingly false returns incur joint and several liability for unpaid tax plus penalties; criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years; travel bans and court-ordered departure restrictions until the debt is settled; disqualification from directorships.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: General anti-abuse rule (Section 33, Income Tax Law) permits the Commissioner to disregard artificial transactions and recharacterize income; ATAD CFC and interest-limitation rules apply; denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; restitution of the full tax shortfall.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and shadow directors are jointly liable for re-assessed taxes and interest; potential criminal exposure for fraudulent misrepresentation; travel bans where tax liabilities exceed statutory thresholds.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties capped at EUR 20,000 for non-compliance with the Cyprus Transfer Pricing Rules (RAA 361/2022); upward TP adjustment to taxable profit plus default interest; evidence disregarded in subsequent assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for administrative penalties where non-compliance is wilful or reckless; independent auditors' penalties for signing unsubstantiated TP declarations; criminal liability for false declarations.
AML / Sanctions Complicity

Statutory sanction: Cyprus AML Law 188(I)/2007 and relevant EU sanctions regulations: administrative fines up to EUR 500,000 or higher for legal persons, asset freezing, confiscation of stored goods and related proceeds, and criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years for money-laundering offences; personal asset seizure and freezing orders; departure bans under asset-recovery proceedings; debarment from regulated logistics licensing.
Late Filing / Non-Payment

Statutory sanction: Fixed late-filing penalty of EUR 100 plus EUR 50 per month of delay (maximum 12 months); statutory default interest compounded monthly; 5% surcharge on assessed tax remaining unpaid; administrative garnishment of bank accounts and seizure of refrigerated inventory.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders with substantial outstanding corporate tax debts may face court-ordered travel bans and departure permits refusal; personal liability for withheld amounts where funds were diverted from the company; director disqualification proceedings.

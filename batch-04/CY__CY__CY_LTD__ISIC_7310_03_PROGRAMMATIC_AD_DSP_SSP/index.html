Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Cyprus private limited company; no escrow deposit is required. Shares may be issued at nominal value and paid in cash or in kind, subject to the solvency statement under the Companies Law.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, via the ARIADNE online tax portal (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must file Form FDI01 (or TIN application) with the Cyprus Tax Department through the ARIADNE portal, accompanied by certified constitutional documents, an apostilled certificate of incorporation, a registered office address in Cyprus, and appointment of a resident director; the Department issues a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN/VAT ID, 8-digit or 9-digit code) within a standard processing period of approximately two to four weeks, and VAT registration for non-established taxable persons is mandatory when supplying taxable supplies in Cyprus or acquiring distance-selling goods above the registration threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must file Form FDI01 (or TIN application) with the Cyprus Tax Department through the ARIADNE portal, accompanied by certified constitutional documents, an apostilled certificate of incorporation, a registered office address in Cyprus, and appointment of a resident director; the Department issues a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN/VAT ID, 8-digit or 9-digit code) within a standard processing period of approximately two to four weeks, and VAT registration for non-established taxable persons is mandatory when supplying taxable supplies in Cyprus or acquiring distance-selling goods above the registration threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No general check-the-box election exists for Cyprus tax purposes; however, a Cyprus-resident company may elect to be included in a group VAT registration (VAT group) with related companies, and the Tax Department permits a deemed/exempt status election for non-established foreign entities only under specific VAT simplification rules; hybrid mismatch election limited to the optional application of OECD/G20 and EU ATAD hybrid mismatch rules in relation to transparent entities, reported annually in the tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General domestic VAT registration threshold: EUR 15,600 per annum for taxable turnover; mandatory non-established taxable person registration: EUR 10,000 per annum for distance sales of goods and certain cross-border services

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated supplies include goods dispatched or transported outside Cyprus (whether delivered to the EU or exported to third countries) by the supplier; services directly related to export goods (transport, loading, packing, customs brokerage); and intra-community supplies of services to EU taxable persons. For programmatic advertising services, zero-rating applies if the service is effectively used and enjoyed outside Cyprus and the customer is a business established outside Cyprus; otherwise, the place of supply rule requires the customer to be a taxable person (B2B) for Cyprus VAT to be chargeable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Export Declaration via Cyprus Customs / ELENYSIS system)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from the Cyprus banking system proving foreign exchange repatriation

Cyprus mandates electronic issuance of invoices using auditable digital means. Real-time fiscal clearance is not yet mandatory; however, Tax Department regulations require e-invoices to comply with the European Standard on Electronic Invoicing (EN 16931) format, transmission through a certified service provider or the ARIADNE myDATA-like repository, and issuance within 30 days of the tax point; digital XML metadata reporting to the Tax Department is required for B2B invoices, with e-invoicing clearance protocols, API integration via the ARIADNE portal, and the possibility of digital stamping for officially validated VAT invoices.

Cyprus law imposes penalties for incorrect or fraudulent export zero-rating claims: interest of 10% per annum on unpaid VAT, administrative penalties of up to EUR 20,000 or 50% of the tax understated, criminal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years for fraudulent declarations, and retrospective clawback of any wrongly claimed refunds together with interest, including refund denial if export evidence (customs declarations, bills of lading, and foreign currency repatriation proof) is not preserved for at least 7 years and produced on demand.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

No exchange-control regime is in force. Current and capital account transactions are fully liberalized under Article 63 TFEU, subject only to EU/UN sanctions, AML/CFT screening, and Central Bank of Cyprus balance-of-payments statistical reporting. The euro is freely convertible for dividend distributions, branch profit remittances, capital repatriation, and settlement of trade obligations. Profit remittance from a Cyprus private limited company requires audited statutory accounts, a board-approved dividend declaration satisfying the solvency test under Cyprus Companies Law Cap. 113, and a withholding-capacity assessment for any non-treaty beneficiary. No prior Central Bank approval or conversion license is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender rules exist in Cyprus. Exporters are not subject to a statutory timeline for repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or any freely usable currency, converted at the exporter's discretion, and must be supported by underlying commercial documentation, invoices, customs declarations, and bank statements retained for BoP statistical, AML, and VAT audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentrike Trapeza Kyprou) and the Eurosystem/European Central Bank. Cyprus has no separate foreign-exchange administration; exchange control was abolished upon euro adoption, and residual regulatory authority resides with the Central Bank for systemic payments oversight, statistical reporting, and EU/UN sanctions implementation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. A non-EU/EEA digital EMI may not lawfully provide payment services or maintain customer virtual IBANs in Cyprus without a Cyprus/EEA EMI licence, an EU passport, or an authorized agent arrangement under the Payment Services Law implementing PSD2. Unlicensed offshore operation will be rejected by Cypriot banks and may trigger the criminal unauthorized-payment-institution prohibition. An EU-licensed EMI can provide SEPA clearing and virtual IBAN structures, but correspondent banks may still decline onboarding on grounds of heightened intermediation and sanctions-screening risk. Practical viability is conditional on the EMI holding a verifiable EU licence, maintaining an EU-registered settlement account, and submitting full counterparty risk documentation.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Cyprus does not impose any withholding tax on dividends paid by a Cyprus-resident company to non-resident shareholders. The Special Defence Contribution (SDC) of 17% applies only to Cyprus tax-resident individuals receiving dividend income and certain other qualifying persons, not to non-resident corporate or individual shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cyprus adheres to the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and requires controlled transactions to satisfy the arm's-length principle. A Local File / contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is expected where the Cyprus entity has annual gross revenue above EUR 7,500,000 and controlled related-party transactions exceed EUR 2,000,000 per transaction category. A Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000 (the Country-by-Country Reporting threshold). Below the de minimis threshold, the Cypriot entity must retain sufficient internal records and a functional-crofit analysis to prove arm's length in the event of an audit; intercompany ad-delivery fees, SSP commissions, and DSP licensing royalties must be documented regardless of turnover.

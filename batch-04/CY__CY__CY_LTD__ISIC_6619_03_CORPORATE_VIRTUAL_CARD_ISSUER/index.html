Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of Cyprus (Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance) – official e-filing portal: TaxForAll (taxportal.mof.gov.cy).

E-government filing portal

A Cypriot private limited company receives its Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the Registrar of Companies and the Tax Department at incorporation. Foreign entities not incorporated in Cyprus must obtain a Cyprus TIN by registering with the competent District Tax Office of the Department of Taxation before commencing taxable activity or upon establishing a Cyprus permanent establishment. The application must include the completed registration form, foreign certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, evidence of registered address or appointed tax agent in Cyprus, identity of directors and ultimate beneficial owners, and a declaration of intended business activities under ISIC. For VAT-liable supplies, the international entity must also submit the VAT registration application on the TaxForAll portal. The Tax Department issues the TIN certificate in writing or electronically once the supporting documentation has been verified.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – Cyprus-resident companies are subject to corporation tax on worldwide income at 12.5%, with a territorial limitation applying only to non-resident companies, whose Cyprus-source income is chargeable. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – Cyprus-resident companies are subject to corporation tax on worldwide income at 12.5%, with a territorial limitation applying only to non-resident companies, whose Cyprus-source income is chargeable. A Cyprus Ltd is a distinct legal taxpayer; no pass-through treatment is available.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election or an elective pass-through regime for limited companies. Statutory elections include group loss relief through the filing of consolidated group tax returns, VAT group registration, tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping operations, notional interest deduction on new equity, and the IP box regime for qualifying intellectual property income. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD are mandatory and restrict any elective avoidance structures involving hybrid instruments or entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 annual taxable turnover for goods and services supplied in Cyprus; €10,000 threshold for intra-EU distance sales of goods and electronically supplied services to non-taxable Cypriot consumers; mandatory registration for non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Cyprus regardless of turnover unless an EU reverse-charge or special scheme applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT is available where the supplier proves that goods have been physically exported from Cyprus to a third-country destination, or where the supply of services qualifies as an export of services to a non-EU recipient under the place-of-supply/B2B rules. The supplier must retain contemporaneous documentary evidence of transport and receipt, and must be able to demonstrate that the invoice and payment correspond to the actual commercial transaction and that the goods left the internal market within the statutory timeframe.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) and any supporting EU export summary declarations
  • Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), or CMR/CIM consignment note (road/rail) evidencing physical departure of the goods from Cyprus/EU to the third-country destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment instruction letter evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in the Cyprus bank account of the exporter

Cyprus does not currently mandate domestic real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or an API-based clearance regime for business-to-business invoices. Electronic invoices may be issued provided the supplier ensures authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and readability of the invoice. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies under the European standard (PEPPOL), and VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically through the TaxForall portal; however, no national invoice clearance API is required.

The Cyprus Tax Department applies a six-year VAT assessment limitation period and may inspect all zero-rating evidence retrospectively. Any unsubstantiated or fraudulent export zero-rating triggers a VAT assessment at 19% plus statutory interest of 1.75% per month, administrative penalties up to 100% of the under-assessed VAT, and clawback of input VAT previously recovered. In cases of intentional evasion, criminal prosecution may result in fines up to €10,000 and/or imprisonment of up to three years; there is no limitation period for criminal fraud proceedings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EUR is the national currency (Eurosystem member since 1 January 2008). Cyprus maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility; there is no pre-approval, surrender, or licensing requirement for profit remittances, dividend distributions, or cross-border treasury flows. The exchange-control regime (Exchange Control Law, Cap. 92) has been dormant since EU accession on 1 May 2004. The only residual OECD capital-liberalisation reservation, being a 30-day prior-notification window for non-resident capital disposals to residents above EUR 20,000 (Law 20(I)/2013), is scheduled to lapse by 31 December 2025 and does not affect ordinary card-program, shareholder-loan, or dividend-expatriation activity. Remittances are subject solely to standard AML cleared-funds verification and Central Bank of Cyprus / ECB balance-of-payments statistical coding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None in force. Cyprus imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds or cross-border card/merchant receivables. Foreign-currency accounts at CBC-licensed credit institutions may be retained freely; export earnings may be converted, netted, retained, or hedged at market rates. The sole administrative duty is the statistical BOP/FDI declaration in line with ECB Regulation (EU) No 184/2014, filed by the receiving bank or the corporate treasurer, with no economic constraint.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου / Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium — operationally useful but structurally subordinate. Offshore EEA digital EMIs (for example OpenPayd, Currenxie, Pemo, Modulr, Airwallex, and Verto) offer fast API onboarding, multi-currency virtual IBANs, and clearing rails for spend-management disbursements. For this regulated virtual-card issuer, the decisive legal constraint is Article 7 of PSD2: client (e-money float) funds must be safeguarded by a qualifying credit institution or an approved CBC-licensed bank; a non-bank offshore EMI holding the issuer's pass-through card float does not satisfy safeguarding and would breach the Cyprus treasury licensing perimeter. CySEC and CBC outsourcing rules further require prior notification before delegating core treasury or transaction-monitoring functions. Accordingly, offshore EMIs are viable only for low-value operational settlement and initiated customer-disbursement plumbing, never as custodian of the safeguarded float or the corporate equity account.

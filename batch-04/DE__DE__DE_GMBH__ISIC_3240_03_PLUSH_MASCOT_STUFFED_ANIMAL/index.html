Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) and central tax matters; local Finanzamt for income, corporate, trade tax registration and assessment; national filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A GmbH must first be registered with the commercial register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht and then file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt within one month of business commencement (§ 138 AO, § 18 UStG) to obtain the Steuernummer; a separate application to the BZSt is required for the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under § 27a UStG, with VIES validation for intra-EU trade. Non-resident shareholders appointing a domestic managing director face no restriction, but the GmbH itself is subject to unlimited German tax jurisdiction.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A GmbH must first be registered with the commercial register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht and then file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt within one month of business commencement (§ 138 AO, § 18 UStG) to obtain the Steuernummer; a separate application to the BZSt is required for the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under § 27a UStG, with VIES validation for intra-EU trade. Non-resident shareholders appointing a domestic managing director face no restriction, but the GmbH itself is subject to unlimited German tax jurisdiction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box passthrough election. Available statutory mechanisms include: (1) Organschaft (VAT and corporate income tax fiscal unity) under §§ 14-19 KStG and § 2(2) Nr. 2 UStG; (2) option to waive VAT exemption on certain real estate leases (§ 9 UStG); (3) trade tax apportionment declarations; (4) hybrid mismatch elections and adjustments under § 4k EStG, §§ 12a-12i KStG implementing ATAD; (5) § 50a withholding tax treatment elections for cross-border payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Foreign suppliers of physical goods (e.g., plush toys) to Germany have no general VAT registration threshold once any B2B supply or distance sale subject to reverse charge occurs (§ 18(8) UStG); domestic small-business exemption (§ 19 UStG): EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 current-year turnover (effective 1 January 2025).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT on exports of tangible goods (Ausfuhrlieferung, § 6 UStG) if the goods are physically dispatched to a third-country destination and the exporter retains verifiable proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis); related services for third-country customers may be zero-rated or fall under reverse charge (§ 4 Nr. 1 lit. b UStG, § 13b UStG). Input VAT on export-related purchases (materials, tooling, freight) is deductible via the ordinary Vorsteuerabzug procedure on periodic VAT returns.

  • ATLAS Electronic Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the customs declaration
  • Proof of physical export: Bill of Lading, CMR road consignment note, or Air Waybill evidencing departure from Germany
  • Transport/insurance documentation and, where required, the tax authority export confirmation forms under §§ 8-13 UStDV
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank receipt (SWIFT TT) showing foreign-sourced payment received for the exported goods
  • Purchase/production records substantiating the goods as purchased or manufactured in Germany

Mandatory E-Rechnung (electronic invoicing) for domestic B2B transactions from 1 January 2025: taxpayers must be able to receive e-invoices consistent with the EN 16931 standard (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD or EDI format); obligation to issue e-invoices phases in from 1 January 2027. No real-time government clearance or API fiscal stamping exists in Germany; VAT preliminary returns (UStVA) must be transmitted electronically through ELSTER by the 10th day after the end of each calendar month/quarter, with annual VAT returns required.

German tax auditors (Außenprüfung) may retrospectively deny zero-rating if the export documentation bundle is incomplete, inconsistent, or fails to prove dispatch to a third country, resulting in re-assessment of 19% output VAT with interest (§ 233a AO). Standard VAT assessment period is four years and ten years in cases of intentional tax evasion (§ 169-171 AO). Fraudulent or abusive export zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion (§ 370 AO) carrying imprisonment of up to five years (up to ten years in aggravated cases), monetary fines, clawback of the full VAT under-accounted, and compounded default interest; responsible managing directors may face personal liability and disqualification. Note: Germany does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline for export zero-rating substantiation; the 180-day figure reflects banking due-diligence practice for matching inward SWIFT TT remittances and is not a legal forfeiture trigger.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the euro area legal framework. The euro is freely convertible with no surrender or repatriation requirements. Profit and dividend remittances by a GmbH to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted, subject only to standard corporate law solvency tests, proper bookkeeping, and tax withholding procedures under the German CFC/withholding tax regime and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory export proceeds surrender timeline or compulsory conversion ratio under German or EU law. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained, converted at market rates via a licensed bank, or used for onward payments. A GmbH must, however, ensure that FX transactions are booked accurately for financial reporting and are recorded in the Bundesbank's balance of payments statistics if the transaction value exceeds EUR 12,500 or as otherwise required under the AWV reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin); the Bundesbank is the competent authority for external trade and payments statistics and notification duties under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV), while BaFin oversees banking supervision and anti-money laundering (AML) enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Germany permits the use of fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions licensed under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) or passported from another EU/EEA member state. A foreign-registered EMI without a German branch or EU passport must not provide payment services to a German GmbH without proper licensing; this is a low-viability option for regulated account services and is generally inadequate as the primary treasury rail. Established German or EU EMIs such as PPRO, Airwallex, and similar can be used as secondary disbursement and FX rails if integrated with a full-license commercial bank for primary liquidity and escrow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital injections and incoming trade payments must be routed via the German banking system with proper SWIFT MT103 messages, correct legal entity identifiers, and a clear purpose code. A GmbH must use its own IBAN; the bank should receive the payment under the payment purpose text that cites the equity contribution, loan agreement, or trade invoice reference. The company must file the Bundesbank's AWV report for payments received from abroad if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500. For equity capital from EU or non-EU shareholders, a notarised capital contribution resolution under the GmbHG is required, and the payment must be recorded in the balance sheet. Cross-border payments using the target payment rails Swift_TT and Platform_Pay are permissible, but direct platform payouts must be reconciled into the same treasury ledger to avoid AML breaks.Outbound trade settlements for raw materials, subcontracting, and logistics are executed via SWIFT MT103 for direct bank transfers and documentary collections or commercial letters of credit (LC) for high-value or first-time supplier relationships. For OEM-ready transactions above EUR 10,000 or with counterparties in non-EU countries, the bank requires underlying trade contracts, invoices, and customs documentation for outbound payment purposes. The default clearing rail is the Eurozone's TARGET2 system for EUR-denominated payments; for non-EUR currencies, the bank uses its correspondent banking network with EUR/USD/CNY/JPY settlement. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €1,000,000 for local file; €50,000,000 group worldwide turnover for master file

Economic substance scope: Governed by EU Economic Substance Directive; requires genuine economic activities (employees, premises, core income-generating activities) for holding, IP, and finance entities; operating manufacturers generally exempt if substance demonstrated

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to €1 million or 10% of annual turnover

Director personal liability: Criminal liability and personal fines if directors authorized or participated in evasion; possible management disqualification
Late or Non-Filing of Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of €5 per day of delay (capped at €1,000); escalating penalties for repeated violations up to €50,000 daily in severe cases

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal administrative fines and liability for compliance failures
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 5% of tax shortfall or €1 million (whichever higher); criminal sanctions for intentional violations

Director personal liability: Personal responsibility for TP compliance; fines and imprisonment possible for willful misconduct
Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €10,000 per violation; potential entity deregistration and criminal penalties

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for ensuring substance compliance; sanctions including fines and disqualification

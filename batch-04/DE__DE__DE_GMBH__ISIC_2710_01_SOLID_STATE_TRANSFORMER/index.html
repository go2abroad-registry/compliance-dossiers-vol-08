Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – federal authority issuing the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.); local Finanzamt (competent tax office at the GmbH's registered seat) – responsible for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), VAT (Umsatzsteuer) and payroll tax. Mandatory digital portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); customs-related communication via ATLAS/Zoll-Portal of the Hauptzollamt.

E-government filing portal

(1) After notarial incorporation and registration in the German Commercial Register (Handelsregister at the Amtsgericht), the GmbH must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER within one month of registration of the company, declaring intended business activities, shareholders, managing directors, and accounting periods. (2) The competent Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (statutory tax number) for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT. (3) An application for the separate USt-IdNr. is submitted to BZSt via ELSTER (Formular USt 1 A); this VAT-ID is required for intra-Community supplies and EU-wide B2B documentation. (4) For customs exports, the GmbH obtains an EORI number from the main customs office (Hauptzollamt) prior to filing export declarations in ATLAS. (5) Ongoing filing obligations: monthly or quarterly advance VAT returns transmitted electronically via ELSTER; the annual VAT return and corporate income tax/trade tax returns are filed by a qualified tax advisor (Steuerberater), mandatory in practice for a GmbH.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German tax law has no US-style check-the-box election; a GmbH is irrevocably treated as a taxable corporation (Körperschaftsteuer 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, plus municipal trade tax at 7–17%) and is ineligible for pass-through. Available statutory elections: (a) Organschaft (tax group/fiscal unity) under §§ 14–19 KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, requiring a majority voting interest combined with a fully enforceable profit-and-loss transfer agreement; this groups the GmbH into one income-tax and VAT taxpayer. (b) Hybrid mismatch elections under § 4k EStG / § 12a KStG for cross-border hybrid instruments or entities. (c) Tax-neutral restructuring elections pursuant to § 20 UmwStG governing transfers of assets and shares on incorporation or later reorganization. (d) The GmbH may elect simplified application of the small-business regulation under § 19 UStG only if its domestic turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 in the prior year and EUR 50,000 in the current year; however, this is generally availed only by non-export-oriented micro-GmbHs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A German-resident GmbH is mandatorily registered for VAT from its first taxable supply; there is no EUR threshold for establishment or permanent-address taxpayers. For non-resident suppliers selling goods to German consumers or using a German warehouse, the intra-Community distance sales threshold is EUR 10,000 per calendar year (EU-wide OSS scheme); once exceeded, full German VAT registration applies. German small-business threshold: EUR 22,000 prior-year / EUR 50,000 current-year domestic turnover, applicable only to German-established micro-businesses, not to a standard GmbH. Where a foreign trader performs only B2B supplies subject to the statutory reverse charge (§ 13b UStG), no German VAT registration duty arises. For a GmbH exporting to non-EU, export realization is zero-rated without limit.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries of solid-state transformers to non-EU countries are zero-rated (exempt with input VAT deduction) under § 4 Nr. 1a i.V.m. § 6 UStG, provided (i) the goods leave the German customs territory and (ii) the exporter proves the transit through documentary evidence (Buch- und Belegnachweis per §§ 13, 14 UStG and §§ 8–10 USDV). Intra-Community supplies to EU counterparties are equally exempt under § 4 Nr. 1b i.V.m. § 6a UStG if the purchaser supplies its valid VAT ID and the goods are transported to another EU Member State. Export-related ancillary services (installation, commissioning, transport, insurance, engineering) are also zero-rated when performed in connection with the exported goods. The supplying GmbH must be the exporter of record; if the goods are sold on Ex-Works terms, the zero-rating still applies when the foreign buyer is the exporter, but the German seller must hold a declaration of the buyer's forwarding instructions. For this ISIC 2710 solid-state transformer business, zero-rating is available for all external trade sales; pure domestic sales remain taxable at 19%.

Germany has phased in mandatory e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) under the Growth Opportunities Act: since 01.01.2025, every German-resident B2B seller must be technically able to receive structured electronic invoices in XRechnung (PEPPOL) format. Format: EN 16931-based XRechnung (PEPPOL BIS) or ZUGFeRD 2.x structured PDF; transmission via certified platforms or the PEPPOL network. Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance (no tax-authority API pre-clearance or digital stamping); tax data flows to the Finanzamt through standard periodic e-filings on ELSTER. B2G e-invoices have been mandatory since 27.11.2020 (XRechnung).

The German tax authorities and the customs administration (Zoll) conduct intensive audits of export zero-ratings. Incomplete or inconsistent book/receipt evidence results in retrospective denial of the zero-rating, assessment of 19% VAT, plus default interest at 0.5% per month under § 233a AO (period of limitation 4 years, extended to 7 years on late-filing and 10 years in evasion cases). Particular scrutiny is given to Streckengeschäfte (triangular transactions), counterfeit or fraudulent foreign VAT IDs, false VIES confirmations, and exports to non-EU trading hubs with re-importation risks. Negligent false claims are punishable under § 378 AO (fines up to EUR 30,000 and interest); intentional or fraudulent misstatements constitute tax evasion under § 370 AO with penalties of up to 5 years' imprisonment (10 years for aggravated cases), fines up to EUR 10 million, and confiscation of goods. The GmbH and its managing directors (Geschäftsführer) are jointly and severally liable under §§ 71 AO, 34 AO and GmbHG liability principles; under § 73 AO, the assets of the GmbH are subject to a tax lien. Practical enforcement: if export proceeds are not received and repatriated within the 180-day statutory/contractual settlement window, the exporter must demonstrate arm's length collection efforts; otherwise, the tax office treats the supply as an unrealized export and recaptures the VAT benefit. Prior to export, the exporter should obtain an advance confirmation from the customs office where prudent.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has full capital and current account convertibility as a euro-area member. The euro is freely convertible; there is no prior exchange-control approval, multiple-currency-practice, or differentiated conversion rate for inward or outward payments. Profit remittance by the German GmbH to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted at the FX level. The only practical frictions are: (i) the AWV statistical reporting obligation for every cross-border payment equal to or above EUR 12,500, to be filed electronically with the Bundesbank within five business days; and (ii) the fiscal withholding burden on distributions, i.e., 26.375% non-resident dividend withholding tax (25% capital yield tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), reducible or exempt through a valid double-tax-treaty relief certificate (Freistellungsbescheinigung) or, for qualifying EU corporate shareholders, through the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive transposition under § 43b of the German Income Tax Act (EStG).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds in Germany. A German exporter may retain all foreign-currency receipts indefinitely, whether onshore or offshore, without conversion into euro. The operative requirement is informational: under § 67 AWV, every payment received from non-residents or paid to non-residents that equals or exceeds EUR 12,500 (including export proceeds) must be declared to the Bundesbank via its Integrated Statistical Reporting System (ISE) within five business days. For high-voltage transformer and switchgear exports, the exporter must also maintain the commercial invoice, packing list, transport documents, customs export declaration (through the ATLAS customs system), and, where applicable, the BAFA export license or end-use assurance, for the statutory retention period of at least six years under § 14b of the Value Added Tax Act (UStG); transactions with sanctioned destinations or persons are prohibited irrespective of any reporting.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Central Bank of the Federal Republic of Germany, Frankfurt am Main), acting within the Eurosystem and in coordination with the European Central Bank for monetary policy and TARGET2 settlement; the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for Banking Act (KWG) and AML/GwG supervision; the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) and the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) for foreign trade and export control law under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG) and Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary use, Low as a primary settlement rail. BaFin-licensed or EEA-passported electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payments institutions, such as Airwallex Germany GmbH (Lithuanian e-money license with German passporting authorization), Solaris Group entities, Payone GmbH, and Wise Europe entities, may legally hold segregated client funds, execute SEPA direct debits and credit transfers, and provide multi-currency FX wallets for the German GmbH. However, they cannot perform the core role of a Tier-1 credit institution: they cannot issue documentary Letters of Credit, provide overdraft or acceptance finance, certify the GmbH capital contribution deposit for the commercial register (Handelsregister), or act as the Bundesbank reporting agent for AWV balance-of-payments statistics without a separate banking partner. Offshore, non-EEA EMIs (e.g., US-chartered financial technology banks without a German or EU authorization) have Low viability: they lack BaFin recognition, cannot hold the account in the name of the GmbH with a German IBAN in a manner fully compliant with German payment account rules, and German suppliers and utilities frequently reject non-DE IBANs. The legally robust structure is therefore one Tier-1 German bank relationship for settlement, documentary credits, and capital escrow, supplemented by an EMI for FX sweep and small-value disbursement efficiency.

Compliant inbound routing depends on the origin: (i) EEA shareholder contribution: SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR to the GmbH's German IBAN with a fully populated structured reference to the capital increase resolution; (ii) non-EEA shareholder contribution: SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the paying bank to the German Tier-1 bank's correspondent account (e.g., Deutsche Bank New York branch for USD, J.P. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary use, Low as a primary settlement rail. BaFin-licensed or EEA-passported electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payments institutions, such as Airwallex Germany GmbH (Lithuanian e-money license with German passporting authorization), Solaris Group entities, Payone GmbH, and Wise Europe entities, may legally hold segregated client funds, execute SEPA direct debits and credit transfers, and provide multi-currency FX wallets for the German GmbH. However, they cannot perform the core role of a Tier-1 credit institution: they cannot issue documentary Letters of Credit, provide overdraft or acceptance finance, certify the GmbH capital contribution deposit for the commercial register (Handelsregister), or act as the Bundesbank reporting agent for AWV balance-of-payments statistics without a separate banking partner. Offshore, non-EEA EMIs (e.g., US-chartered financial technology banks without a German or EU authorization) have Low viability: they lack BaFin recognition, cannot hold the account in the name of the GmbH with a German IBAN in a manner fully compliant with German payment account rules, and German suppliers and utilities frequently reject non-DE IBANs. The legally robust structure is therefore one Tier-1 German bank relationship for settlement, documentary credits, and capital escrow, supplemented by an EMI for FX sweep and small-value disbursement efficiency.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) typically 14–17%, producing an aggregate effective rate of approximately 30–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (1.375%) resulting in a combined 26.375% dividend withholding tax on distributions to non-resident shareholders. Under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, 0% applies when the parent holds at least 10% and the requirements of § 43b EStG are met.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File triggered when cross-border supply/services with foreign related parties exceed EUR 1 million per transaction type, or if other business transactions (intangibles, loans, license fees) exceed EUR 100,000 per transaction type (GAufzV). Master File triggered when the global group turnover exceeds EUR 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply standalone ESR rules. However, treaty relief requires genuine economic substance under § 50d(3) EStG and the OECD POEM standard. Artificial arrangements under § 42 AO are disregarded, including letterbox GmbHs lacking personnel, business premises, or decision-making power in Germany.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years under § 370 AO; criminal fines; assessed underpayment plus interest at 0.5% per month; total debit including surcharges can exceed 200% of the evaded tax; property seizure and prohibition of business activity.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally criminally liable, jointly and severally for restitution of evaded taxes under § 71 AO; automatic travel bans; passport surrender; imprisonment for repeat offenders; no D&O insurance indemnification.
Failure to File / Late Filing

Statutory sanction: Verspätungszuschlag of 0.25% monthly (min EUR 25/month, max 25% of assessed tax); estimated assessment under § 162 AO with 10% surcharge; late payment interest at 6% p.a.; additional penalty up to EUR 25,000 for gross negligence.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for taxes owed if violation is deliberate or reckless (§ 69 AO); personal asset seizure; exit denial and visa revocation for non-resident directors.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA claims under § 50d(3) EStG; reclassification of the transaction under § 42 AO; reassessment of profits with a 5% transfer-pricing surcharge under § 162(4) AO; withholding tax re-imposed at 26.375%; cancellation of GmbH registration under § 144 GmbHG for abuse of legal form.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid withholding taxes, trade tax, and social contributions; travel ban under § 132 StPO; personal asset seizure; length of imprisonment up to 3 years for abusive insolvency arrangements; OECD and BZSt reporting to anti-avoidance units.
AML & Capital Movements Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 under GwG; freezing orders by the Generalzolldirektion; commercial restrictions and licence revocation; criminal liability under § 89c StGB for facilitating financial operations from illegal sources.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for breaking AML due-diligence duties; imprisonment up to 3 years for negligence-based money laundering; travel restrictions and asset freeze; removal from company register for non-compliance under GwG.

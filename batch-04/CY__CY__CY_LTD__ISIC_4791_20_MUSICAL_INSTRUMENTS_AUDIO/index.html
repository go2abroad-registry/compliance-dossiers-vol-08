Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Cyprus Private Limited Company; share capital may be set at EUR 1.00 per subscriber share and fully subscribed on incorporation. No escrow or paid-up deposit rule exists at the Registrar level. In practice, Cypriot banks require the nominal capital to be funded (commonly EUR 1,000) before a corporate current account is opened.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the Cyprus Fire Service under the Fire Prevention (Minimum Requirements) Regulations P.O. 206/2007, covering means of escape, extinguisher servicing, electrical installation safety and limited storage of flammable packaging; structural load inspection certificate for warehouse racking systems; workplace clearance certificate under the Safety and Health at Work Law L.89(I)/1996 with the Safety and Health Officer appointment filed at the Department of Labour Inspection, supported by first-aid, fire-evacuation and staff training records.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Cyprus Private Limited Company; share capital may be set at EUR 1.00 per subscriber share and fully subscribed on incorporation. No escrow or paid-up deposit rule exists at the Registrar level. In practice, Cypriot banks require the nominal capital to be funded (commonly EUR 1,000) before a corporate current account is opened.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Tax Department, Ministry of Finance; electronic portal: TaxisNet (corporate income tax) and AriADNE VAT Portal

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus private limited company is automatically registered for tax upon incorporation and receives an eight-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN). For VAT purposes, the company must file the prescribed VAT registration application (Form TD 610) with the District VAT Office before commencing taxable supplies; the application is processed within the statutory timeframe and a distinct VAT number is issued. A non-resident entity without a fixed establishment in Cyprus must appoint a tax representative and register for VAT using the relevant Form TD 611, supported by certified constitutional documents, proof of business address, and evidence of intended economic activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Cyprus private limited company is automatically registered for tax upon incorporation and receives an eight-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN). For VAT purposes, the company must file the prescribed VAT registration application (Form TD 610) with the District VAT Office before commencing taxable supplies; the application is processed within the statutory timeframe and a distinct VAT number is issued. A non-resident entity without a fixed establishment in Cyprus must appoint a tax representative and register for VAT using the relevant Form TD 611, supported by certified constitutional documents, proof of business address, and evidence of intended economic activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a check-the-box pass-through election. Available statutory elections include: election for 80% exemption of qualified dividend income, election for zero-percent withholding under certain domestic exemptions, notional interest deduction election on new equity, IP box regime election (2.5% effective rate on qualifying IP profits), group loss relief election among 75% group members, and overseas permanent establishment branch exemption election under section 9AA of the Income Tax Law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 (taxable turnover during the preceding 12 consecutive months); EUR 35,000 for intra-Community acquisition by distance sellers

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically exported from Cyprus to a destination outside the European Union are zero-rated under Article 69 of the VAT Law, provided the supplier retains documentary evidence of exportation within the statutory time limits. Offshore services supplied to a non-established business recipient may also qualify for zero-rating under the place-of-supply rules if the recipient notifies and satisfies the standard B2B evidence requirements. The exporter must possess a bona fide contract and payment record with independent third-party evidence of transport from Cyprus to the third country.

  • Customs Export Declaration (automated Single Administrative Document or customs export clearance notice)
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or other international transport document proving physical departure of the goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of consideration received (bank transfer or SWIFT confirmation, or payment gateway settlement record)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent banking evidence where available

Cyprus does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for commercial B2B invoicing. VAT returns, periodic VIES recapitulative statements, and summary reports are filed electronically through the AriADNE portal. There is no API-based real-time reporting obligation for retail audio instrument e-commerce; standard accounting system records must support tax audits.

Zero-rating is strictly scrutinised by the Cyprus Tax Department. Where exportation is not substantiated, the tax authority retrospectively assesses VAT at 19%, increased by statutory interest, and may impose penalties of up to 100% of the assessed tax. It is a criminal offence to issue false or fraudulent VAT documents; persons convicted are liable to imprisonment up to five years and/or fines up to EUR 10,000 (or 1% of taxable turnover for serious VAT fraud). The Tax Department may also claw back input VAT previously credited, apply financial penalties, and prevent future VAT group registrations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full Eurozone member with fully liberalized capital and current account transactions pursuant to Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The Exchange Control Law of 1955 was repealed in 1992 with residual emergency restrictions abolished in April 2013, leaving zero statutory barriers to profit remittance, dividend distribution, or repatriation of equity and loan capital. EUR is freely convertible into any currency at the prevailing interbank market rate; Cyprus applies no separate current account or capital account conversion gateways, no licensing requirements for inward or outward transfers, and no withholding surcharge on cross-border remittances beyond the standard corporate income tax and dividend withholding tax framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists in Cyprus. Exporters must simply declare transactions to the Central Bank for statistical purposes via the Bank of Cyprus banking statistics system within the reporting cycles set out by Regulation (EU) No 184/2016, but there is zero statutory timeline for conversion, zero mandatory conversion ratio, and zero requirement to repatriate funds. All export receipts from the online sale of musical instruments and studio audio gear may be retained in EUR or any foreign currency in a resident non-resident account without any compulsory conversion or holding-period constraint.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) exercising national supervisory competence under the Single Supervisory Mechanism, with monetary policy and euro issuance delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High for this low-risk standard e-commerce profile. Payment institutions authorized as Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under Cyprus's implementation of PSD2 (Payment Services and Electronic Money Laws of 2018) may passport into Cyprus from any EEA state and hold segregated client funds. Viable non-bank rails include Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Payoneer for collection of card proceeds, marketplace settlement, and cost-effective multi-currency conversions. Legal constraints: (1) an EMI cannot provide a Cyprus IBAN unless it maintains a physical branch or correspondent relationship in Cyprus; (2) corporate funds held with an EMI are client money, not bank deposits, and are not covered by the Deposit Protection Scheme; (3) EMI accounts are not acceptable as the sole treasury account for statutory audit evidence of share capital or for closing tax settlement obligations with the Tax Department — a licensed Bank of Cyprus credit institution account remains mandatory; (4) a Cyprus-licensed EMI must apply full KYC identification standards equivalent to bank standards under Article 6 of the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (L. 188(I)/2007).

Defense evidence: Full corporate chain chart from UBO to the CY_LTD; certificates of incorporation and good standing; certified memorandum and articles; a defined AML compliance opinion from a Cyprus law firm confirming the ultimate natural person is identified; PEP status declarations; business rationale letter explaining the shareholder structure; audited or third-party verified financial statements of each intermediary holding company demonstrating real economic substance.

Root cause: Online retail business model flagged for third-party payment processor settlement or hidden marketplace proceeds

Defense evidence: Signed platform merchant agreements from Stripe, PayPal, Shopify Payments, and any marketplace; a 12-month historical settlement report showing the reconciliation trail from consumer payment to bank account; a treasury flow diagram distinguishing gross sales, processor fees, chargebacks, refunds, and VAT remittance; contractual evidence of invoicing to wholesalers; chargeback ratio report under 1%.
Source of initial equity capital not substantiated or injection arriving from personal accounts

Root cause: Source of initial equity capital not substantiated or injection arriving from personal accounts

Adverse media appearance or sanctions screening hit against a signatory, UBO, or related supplier

Root cause: Adverse media appearance or sanctions screening hit against a signatory, UBO, or related supplier

Defense evidence: Professional sanctions and PEP screening report from a recognized provider (Dow Jones, World-Check, or Refinitiv) dated within 30 days; legal opinion from Cyprus counsel distinguishing the hit from the corporate entity; completion of a standard adverse media explanation form; six months of transaction history demonstrating that the activity is licit; evidence that the relevant supplier is not in a OFAC or EU sanctions List.
Use of a virtual office address or absence of operational premises in Cyprus

Root cause: Use of a virtual office address or absence of operational premises in Cyprus

Defense evidence: Signed lease or sub-lease agreement for suitable office premises in Cyprus; if using a CSP, a letter from the CSP confirming dedicated administrative support staff, physical access, and power; a business plan demonstrating real functions exercised in Cyprus (inventory handling, order processing, procurement, customer support); Cypriot utility bills in the company name; an accountant's letter confirming that Cyprus payroll and VAT records are produced from Cyprus.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity injections, route solely via the SEPA/CHAPS/SWIFT network using a Bank of Cyprus EUR account; an express purpose description 'INV — Share capital subscription' must appear on the SWIFT MT103 field 70. Central Bank of Cyprus reporting requires completion of the balance of payments (BoP) transaction form for any inbound remittance above EUR 12,500, which the receiving bank files electronically; the client retains the bank reference for tax purposes.

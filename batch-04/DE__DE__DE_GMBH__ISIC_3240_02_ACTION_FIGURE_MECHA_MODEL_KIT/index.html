Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) and local Finanzamt; official portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH, the managing director registers by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' electronically via ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt issues the domestic Steuernummer and, upon request, forwards the application to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). Foreign e-commerce sellers must register under Section 18 UStG at the Finanzamt responsible for the place of supply and may need a German fiscal representative if enforcement of VAT obligations is not secured by other means.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (resident GmbH subject to unlimited worldwide tax liability on corporate income and trade tax; not a pass-through entity) — For a German GmbH, the managing director registers by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' electronically via ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt issues the domestic Steuernummer and, upon request, forwards the application to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). Foreign e-commerce sellers must register under Section 18 UStG at the Finanzamt responsible for the place of supply and may need a German fiscal representative if enforcement of VAT obligations is not secured by other means.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident GmbH subject to unlimited worldwide tax liability on corporate income and trade tax; not a pass-through entity)

Available election forms

No check-the-box election. A GmbH may form an Organschaft (fiscal unity) with its controlling German shareholder for corporate income tax and VAT. Certain hybrid mismatch and reverse-hybrid planning options exist under ATAD transposition; declarations and documentation must be filed according to German anti-hybrid rules. No general entity classification election is available beyond the civil-law form.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for German resident entrepreneurs; small-business relief under Section 19 UStG applies only if turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 in the prior calendar year and is not expected to exceed EUR 50,000 in the current calendar year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to third countries are VAT-zero-rated under Section 6 UStG when the goods are physically transported or dispatched to a destination outside the European Union, the export is evidenced by customs formalities, and the supplier holds an Ausfuhrnachweis (export proof). Intra-Community supplies are exempt under Section 6a UStG if the buyer is an EU VAT-registered business and a valid VAT ID is presented. The action-figure mecha model kits must be cleared for export in final commercial state and must not undergo further processing in the Union before departure.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung / electronic export accompanying document from ATLAS)
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Bill of lading / air waybill or CMR consignment note
  • Outward movement confirmation or exit certificate (Ausgangsvermerk)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank proof of payment receipt

No real-time fiscal clearance or government API stamping. A phased e-invoicing mandate applies: domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive E-Rechnung (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD) as of 1 January 2025; from 1 January 2027, suppliers must issue structured e-invoices in all domestic B2B transactions. No continuous transaction control or digital fiscal stamp exists in Germany.

The tax office will retroactively assess 19% VAT plus monthly late-payment interest of 0.15% when the export proof is incomplete, false, or the goods are not demonstrably dispatched to the claimed non-EU destination. Intentional or reckless export VAT fraud triggers criminal prosecution under Sections 370–376 AO, fines up to EUR 10 million for the corporate entity under Section 4a AO, seizure of goods, imprisonment of responsible individuals for up to 10 years, and exclusion from further VAT zero-rating protection. VAT refund substantiation documentation must be retained for at least 10 years and produced on audit demand.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unrestricted convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions under Article 63 TFEU, the EU Capital Freedom principle, and the AWG. The euro is the domestic currency; no approval, allocation, or repatriation license is required for inbound equity injections or outbound profit remittance. Dividend remittance to foreign shareholders is subject only to the statutory German withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, reduced under applicable double-tax treaties or exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where a qualifying EU parent holds at least 10% of the GmbH. No exchange-control bottleneck or central bank pre-approval applies to cross-border treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate export proceeds. Export receipts in EUR or any convertible foreign currency may be retained freely in German or foreign accounts. The only compliance obligation is statistical cross-border reporting (AWV-Meldung) to the Deutsche Bundesbank within 5 working days for any individual cross-border payment or receivable exceeding EUR 12,500.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), with supervisory enforcement by BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) under the German Banking Act (KWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium viability. BaFin-authorized Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions (e.g., PPRO Financial, Solaris, Conto) are lawful for payment collection and cross-border float management, but they are not banks and cannot hold escrowed capital, provide lending, or issue Commercial Letters of Credit. Offshore non-EU EMIs (UK or Singapore licensed) are not passported into Germany and require BaFin licensing for regulated activities in the German market; their use is limited to ancillary collection rails and never acceptable for statutory escrow or treasury settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections are executed via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA CT for euro transfers within the EU) referencing purpose code S21 (Erwerb von Beteiligungen) under the Bundesbank AWV statistical classification. The remitting entity must transmit the GmbH's Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) and the shareholder reference in field 70 of the MT103. The receiving bank verifies the notarized capital-increase resolution and the updated Transparenzregister entry before crediting the Capital Escrow Account. Any inbound amount exceeding EUR 12,500 is reported by the bank to the Deutsche Bundesbank within 5 working days under the AWV mandate; no central bank approval or minimum-notice period applies.Trade settlements use SWIFT MT103 priority transfers for open-account buyer credit, Documentary Collections under URC 522 for medium-risk contracts, and Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 (optionally confirmed by a German correspondent bank) for high-value cross-border shipments of finished action-figure kits and raw steel resin materials. All outbound disbursements must cite the applicable Bundesbank AWV statistical purpose code (e.g., S61 for goods exports or S62 for goods imports), triggering the bank's mandatory 5-working-day cross-border report. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at municipal rates typically 14-17%, yielding a combined effective rate of approximately 30-35%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, resulting in an effective rate of 26.375%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by July 31 of the year following the fiscal year-end; extension to February 28 of the subsequent year if filed by a tax advisor.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same as corporate income tax return: July 31 (or February 28 with tax advisor extension).

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall

Late filing penalty of 0.25% of assessed tax per month (min €25, max €25,000); late payment interest of 0.5% per month; additional penalties up to 10% of tax due for negligence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 100,000 for cross-border transactions with related parties per year triggers Local File; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but general anti-avoidance rules and substance-over-form principles apply. A GmbH must demonstrate real economic activity (office, employees, management) to avoid being classified as a shell company for tax treaty and EU anti-abuse purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 10 years; fines up to EUR 50,000; additional penalties up to 25% of tax underpaid; interest on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they fail to file or pay; travel bans and asset seizures possible; criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; reclassification of income; penalties up to 25% of tax underpaid; potential criminal charges for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties and taxes; travel bans and restrictions on business activities; imprisonment in severe cases.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 100,000; regulatory sanctions; potential criminal prosecution for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned up to 5 years; travel bans and asset freezes.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 5% of the adjustment amount; interest on underpaid tax; potential criminal charges for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; travel bans and imprisonment in extreme cases.

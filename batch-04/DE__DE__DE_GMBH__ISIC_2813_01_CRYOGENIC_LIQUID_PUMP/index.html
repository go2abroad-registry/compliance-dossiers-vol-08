Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum statutory share capital is €25,000 (§ 5 GmbHG). Before the registration application, each cash subscription must be paid in to at least €12,500 (one quarter of the nominal amount), deposited into a blocked escrow bank account (Sperrkonto) opened in the name of the GmbH i.G. Non-cash contributions must be fully furnished and documented. The managing directors must certify under § 8 GmbHG that the paid-in capital is at their free disposal; funds may not be released to the founders until the Commercial Register entry is completed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety final inspection (Brandschutzabnahme) by the local fire authority, including alarm systems, evacuation routes, and extinguishing installations for solvent and cryogenic areas. Structural integrity certificate (Standsicherheitsnachweis) for the production hall covering the cryogenic test pit, crane runway, and tank foundations. Occupational clearance (Arbeitsstättenbegehung) by the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt includes registration of overthreshold pressure equipment under § 15 BetrSichV, an explosion-protection document where solvents are present, workplace noise compliance at a daily limit of 80 dB(A) per LärmVibrationsArbSchV, provision of PPE and signage for cryogenic burn hazards, and first-aid and emergency evacuation readiness.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available for a GmbH under German law; the GmbH is compulsorily a taxable corporation subject to Körperschaftsteuer and Gewerbesteuer. Hybrid mismatch adjustments may arise under the German ATAD implementation (StAStG) for cross-border payments, but no general entity classification election is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 expected current-year turnover (small business exemption). A foreign business making any taxable supply in Germany is generally required to register for VAT from the first transaction without a minimum threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to supplies of goods physically dispatched from Germany to a non-EU destination, provided the exporter holds full evidence of the export and meets the conditions of Section 6 UStG. Intra-Community supplies to other EU member states are also zero-rated if the purchaser has a valid VAT ID, the goods are transported to that member state, and the EU recapitulative statement is filed. Offshore installation or servicing of cryogenic pumps supplied as B2B services will generally fall under the place-of-supply rule (customer's place), with reverse-charge instead of German zero-rating; pure export of goods remains the core zero-rating category. Export proceeds may be repatriated by any banking route; German law does not impose a fixed statutory FX deadline, but the 180-day standard is used in export documentation and banking practice for payment evidence and customs valuation checks.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument / ATLAS export certificate)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control is in place in Germany. Since 1 January 2025, all resident businesses have had the legal obligation to be able to receive structured e-invoices in EN 16931 (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD). Mandatory transmission of e-invoices in domestic B2B applies according to a phased schedule from 2026/2027/2028 depending on turnover; B2G e-invoicing via XRechnung is already compulsory.

The German tax authorities rigorously inspect export evidence in VAT audits. If export zero-rating is claimed without sufficient documentary proof, the tax office issues a VAT assessment reclaiming the tax at 19% plus statutory interest under Section 233a AO and late-payment penalties. Incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating triggers liability for VAT on the supply and may be prosecuted as tax evasion under Section 370 AO, carrying imprisonment of up to 10 years and associated fines. For intra-Community supplies, knowingly false documentation is punishable under Section 26a UStG with imprisonment or fine, and the supplier becomes immediately liable for the full VAT.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies a fully liberalised and unrestricted currency regime for both capital and current account transactions. There is no statutory distinction between capital-account and current-account convertibility, no central-bank approval requirement before acquiring or disposing of foreign currency, and no repatriation or profit-remittance clearance. This liberal state derives from Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital), the euro-area legal framework, and the permissive cardinal principle of Section 1(1) AWG/AWV pursuant to which foreign trade and payments are unrestricted unless expressly prohibited. A German GmbH may therefore remit dividends, interest, royalties, loan repayments, equity withdrawals and liquidation proceeds to any non-resident shareholder in EUR or any convertible foreign currency without exchange-control authorisation. Residual practical hurdles are limited to: (i) statistical cross-border payment reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV for every payment exceeding EUR 12,500 or any monthly aggregate exceeding EUR 12,500; (ii) EU/UN/OFAC sanctions and embargoes, which may freeze or prohibit payments to designated persons, jurisdictions or restricted industries (notably Russia, Belarus and Iran with respect to LNG and advanced industrial equipment); (iii) AML source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership verification on all capital injections; and (iv) the need to conclude a German Master Agreement for Foreign Exchange Transactions (DRV) or ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with the servicing bank before executing any derivative hedge. For a GmbH manufacturing LNG cryogenic pumps, no specific regulatory permission is required to hold USD or CNY accounts onshore, to invoice in foreign currency, or to convert export proceeds at freely negotiated market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no forced conversion into EUR, no mandatory repatriation timeline and no minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds. An exporter may lawfully retain export revenue in foreign-currency accounts at German or other EEA banks, net or offset cross-border receivables and payables, and deploy export proceeds for working capital, imports, debt service, dividends or reinvestment without prior Bundesbank approval. The only binding obligations are passive: (a) reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV for each inbound/outbound payment exceeding EUR 12,500 or where the monthly aggregate balance of cross-border payments exceeds EUR 12,500, the statistical filing ordinarily being executed by the domestic payment-service provider using the Bundesbank payment-code catalogue; (b) full documentation of the underlying export transaction (export invoice, ATLAS customs declaration, bill of lading/AWB/CMR and packing list) to support VAT, customs and AML verification; and (c) compliance with any contractual assignment or collection-account control mechanisms imposed by export-finance, forfaiting or supply-chain-finance providers, which are private covenants rather than regulatory exactions. There is likewise no exchange-rate margin obligation and no surrender window; hedging is effected through ordinary onshore banks under freedom of contract and ISDA/DRV documentation.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Zentralverwaltung, Frankfurt am Main) is the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany and the German member of the Eurosystem. Foreign-exchange regulation and trade-finance policy are administered jointly with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) is the competent licensing and enforcement authority for export-control and dual-use matters; and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic-money institutions for prudential and AML (GwG) compliance. The Bundesbank operates the external-statistics reporting system for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 and represents Germany in the ECB Governing Council.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus (official portal: taxportal.mof.gov.cy; VAT electronic filing portal: ARIADNE at ariadne.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

Within 60 days of incorporation, the Cyprus Ltd registers with the District Tax Office of the Tax Department; the Tax Identification Number (TIN) is issued after filing the prescribed company tax registration application together with the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, registered office certificate, director and shareholder identification documents, and beneficial ownership statement. VAT registration must be completed electronically through the ARIADNE portal when taxable supplies exceed €15,600 (or immediately for non-established suppliers and for intra-Community acquisitions); the VAT number is then issued with the CY prefix and is aligned with the TIN for corporate income tax filings under the Cyprus Value Added Tax Law, as amended.

VAT registration must be completed electronically through the ARIADNE portal when taxable supplies exceed €15,600 (or immediately for non-established suppliers and for intra-Community acquisitions); the VAT number is then issued with the CY prefix and is aligned with the TIN for corporate income tax filings under the Cyprus Value Added Tax Law, as amended.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – Cypriot-resident private limited companies are taxed on worldwide income at a flat 12.5% corporate income tax rate; no pass-through treatment is available for a Cyprus Ltd; foreign permanent establishments are subject to territorial-scope relief; no tax-exempt-zone regime applies to standard Cyprus companies in Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca, Paphos, or Famagusta districts.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a check-the-box election. Statutory elections available include: (1) group loss relief election for 75% related companies; (2) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital; (3) IP Box regime election granting 80% exemption on qualifying intangible property profits; (4) tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping companies; (5) voluntary VAT registration election under Article 11 of the VAT Law; and (6) ATAD hybrid-mismatch optional adjustments where relevant to cross-border payment structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period for Cyprus-established taxable persons; €10,000 EU-wide threshold for intra-EU distance sales and cross-border digital services under the OSS regime; no registration threshold for non-established suppliers making taxable supplies in Cyprus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cyprus applies 0% VAT to the export of goods to third countries where physical removal is proven by a customs export declaration; intra-community supplies are zero-rated when the customer provides a valid EU VAT identification number; services supplied to non-EU businesses are zero-rated under the B2B place-of-supply rule; fractional CFO advisory and bookkeeping services qualify for zero-rating as professional services supplied to a recipient established outside Cyprus or outside the EU, supported by a service agreement, statement of work, payment confirmation through external payment rails, and evidence of the customer's foreign business establishment and use of the services outside Cyprus.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) or electronic export declaration with MRN and exit notification in the Cyprus Customs/AES system
  • Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), or CMR road consignment note evidencing physical removal of goods from Cyprus
  • Commercial invoice and tax invoice line item showing the zero-rated supply, foreign currency amount, and relevant export reference
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/bank statement evidencing receipt of export consideration into the Cyprus company's bank account within the statutory repatriation period
  • Packing list, certificate of origin, and transport insurance documentation (for goods); for services: signed client agreement, service completion certificate, access logs, and virtual CFO delivery records substantiating the offshore provision of services
  • EU VIES validation printout for intra-community B2B supplies where the customer is established in another EU member state

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping/API mandate currently applies to B2B transactions in Cyprus. Mandatory e-invoicing applies to invoices issued under public procurement contracts, which must be submitted in PEPPOL format through the national e-Procurement system pursuant to EU Directive 2014/55/EU. All invoices must be retained for five years as required by the Cyprus VAT Law. The Cyprus Ltd must reconcile its multi-currency ledger and payment rail records (Stripe Billing, Wise) with ARIADNE VAT return filings and, where applicable, Intrastat filings for intra-EU trade.

The Cyprus Tax Commissioner may disallow zero-rating and issue an additional VAT assessment at 19% plus statutory late-payment interest and monthly surcharges when documentation is incomplete or export consideration is not received within the 180-day window. Administrative penalties under the Cyprus VAT Law may reach €10,000 per offence and the disputed VAT may be clawed back with an additional penalty of up to 100%. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims are treated as tax evasion and can result in criminal prosecution with fines up to €20,000 and imprisonment of up to three years under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law. The Tax Department may also reject future export declarations for the offending entity, impose bank guarantees for VAT registrations, and deny input VAT deductibility during the audit period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full member of the European Union and the Eurozone; the euro (EUR) is fully convertible for current account and capital account transactions, subject to EU/IMF statistical reporting, EU anti-money laundering (AML) obligations, EU sanctions screening, and national security screening for foreign direct investment under the Cyprus Foreign Direct Investment Screening Law. Profit remittance is unrestricted for Cypriot companies, subject to satisfaction of Cyprus Tax Department dividend certification requirements, if applicable, and standard bank-level source-of-funds and tax residence documentation. No material conversion hurdles exist for EUR-denominated treasury operations. Non-resident shareholders may receive dividends without prior exchange control approval; banks may require UBO declarations and corporate authorization documents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As a eurozone member with no standing exchange control regime, Cyprus does not impose any mandatory surrender, repatriation, or conversion ratio on export receipts. Export proceeds may be held in foreign currency accounts or converted at will. For invoicing of professional services (virtual CFO advisory, bookkeeping), export invoices may be settled via SWIFT or card networks; VAT reverse-charge rules apply for B2B cross-border services under the EU VAT place-of-supply rules. There is no statutory timeline for export proceeds repatriation; however, accounting and tax filings require accrual recognition and, for companies exceeding certain thresholds, annual financial statement audits.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for non-bank electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (PIs) licensed in the EEA that operate passported into Cyprus under PSD2. Such EMIs may provide e-money accounts, IBANs, SEPA/SEPA Instant and SWIFT rails, and multi-currency wallets without a full banking license. Legal constraints: EMIs are not covered by the EU Deposit Guarantee Scheme; may only hold customer funds in safeguarded accounts at a credit institution or, where permitted, in safe low-risk assets; cannot provide commercial lending or credit lines; must comply with anti-money laundering and sanctions obligations under the Cyprus Anti-Money Laundering Law (L. 188(I)/2007, as amended). For low-risk virtual CFO advisory and multi-currency bookkeeping, a licensed EMI such as Wise (Wise Europe SA or Wise Payments Ltd, passported) is viable for operational disbursements and cross-border collections, but the primary treasury account for tax payments, payroll, and statutory capital should be held with a tier-1 Cypriot credit institution to ensure regulated deposit protection and full integration with Cyprus Tax Department electronic payments (JCCS/ARIS).

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Certified passport copies, proof of residential address (utility bill or bank statement less than 3 months old), UBO declaration form, CV/résumé or business biography, corporate structure chart, Cyprus incorporation certificate (HE77), certificate of registered address, Certificate of Directors and Secretary, Certificate of Registered Agent, apostilled resolutions appointing directors/authorized signatories, and a sworn affidavit explaining source of personal wealth and source of funds.
Unclear source of initial capital injection or future inbound funding

Root cause: Unclear source of initial capital injection or future inbound funding

Defense evidence: Bank statements (12 months, original or certified) of the funding entity/person, signed share subscription documents, notarized board resolution approving capital contribution, loan agreements with commercial terms if intercompany debt, group audited financial statements, evidence of tax residence, beneficial ownership registry excerpt, and evidence of regulatory status (e.g., CySEC license if applicable, ICPAC practicing certificate for auditors).
Disproportionately high expected transaction turnover relative to stated share capital and staffing

Root cause: Disproportionately high expected transaction turnover relative to stated share capital and staffing

Defense evidence: Detailed business plan with monthly projections, executed client contracts/sub-agreements with international counterparties, professional indemnity insurance certificate, outsourced accounting/finance structure, invoice and billing workflow description, expected counter-party list, and appointment of a Cyprus-based compliance officer or AML designated person to reduce risk.
Cross-border payments to or from tax-haven, shell-like, or high-risk countries

Root cause: Cross-border payments to or from tax-haven, shell-like, or high-risk countries

Defense evidence: Commercial contractual documentation evidencing the economic substance of each transaction, invoice ledger, delivery reports / time-tracking records, beneficial ownership declarations of the overseas counter-party, Economic Substance and Commercial Substance documentation for Cyprus, TP policy and transfer pricing report, and legal opinion or tax advice summarizing the commercial rationale.
PEP status or politically exposed connection of any shareholder/director or beneficial owner

Root cause: PEP status or politically exposed connection of any shareholder/director or beneficial owner

Defense evidence: Full PEP declaration forms, source of wealth and source of funds reports, independent background check reports, confirmation of legitimate business interest, minutes of board risk committee approving the relationship, and any regulatory approvals from CySEC or ICPAC, where applicable.
Virtual CFO/accounting business with no physical offices in Cyprus

Root cause: Virtual CFO/accounting business with no physical offices in Cyprus

Defense evidence: Virtual office / registered agent agreement, certified service provider lease or serviced office contract, substance documentation issued by the corporate services provider (physical office or mail-handling, local telephone lines, local director, local secretary, local accounting/tax filings), and evidence of compliance with the Cyprus Administrative Cooperation and Automatic Exchange of Information obligations (DAC6, CRS).

Late filing penalty of €100 plus €1 per day of delay (max €500); late payment penalty of 5% per month on unpaid tax (max 30%); additional interest at 1.5% per month on overdue amounts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €750,000 for aggregate related-party transactions (purchases, sales, services, royalties) triggers Local File; Master File required if group consolidated turnover exceeds €750 million.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) requiring companies to demonstrate substance (local office, employees, management) for relevant activities. Virtual CFO services may be considered relevant if they involve high-risk IP or financing activities; otherwise, general substance requirements apply under the tax residency test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; additional penalty of 100% of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for civil restitution of unpaid taxes, travel bans, and criminal prosecution leading to imprisonment.
Shell Company (Lack of Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax residency status, retroactive tax assessments, penalties up to €50,000, and potential strike-off from the register.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; travel bans and disqualification from serving as directors.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1,000,000 for legal entities; suspension or revocation of operating license; criminal prosecution of responsible officers.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines up to €100,000, imprisonment up to 5 years, and disqualification from corporate management.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profits, penalties up to 10% of the adjusted amount, and interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; potential travel bans and criminal charges for willful neglect.

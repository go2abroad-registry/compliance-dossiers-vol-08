Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus – competent authority for corporate income tax, Value Added Tax, Special Defence Contribution, and withholding taxes; principal e-portal: Cyprus Tax Portal / TAXISnet at www.taxportal.gov.cy; corporate registration infrastructure: Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver, Nicosia.

E-government filing portal

For a Cyprus private limited company: (1) incorporate with the Registrar of Companies to obtain the HE-prefixed company registration number and Statutory Articles of Association; (2) upon incorporation, the Tax Department automatically records the legal entity and issues the Tax Identification Number (TIN), which serves as the unified taxpayer master number for income tax, VAT, SDC, and employer filings; (3) foreign or non-resident entities establishing a permanent establishment, branch, or Cyprus tax presence must file an application for a TIN through the Cyprus Tax Portal or through an appointed tax representative, submitting constitutional documents, registered-office evidence, and director/beneficial-owner particulars; (4) VAT registration must be applied for separately via the TAXISnet VAT Application module (VAT 101 Application) once the mandatory threshold is met or for voluntary registration; (5) non-established EU and non-EU traders may register directly or through a VAT representative in Cyprus where such person is required and meets the conditions of Article 65 of the VAT Law; (6) to obtain formal confirmation of Cyprus tax residence, the entity submits Form 50 application to the District Tax Office with evidence of central management and control exercised in Cyprus, including board-meeting records, registered office, strategic decision documentation, and bank relationship details.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Cyprus-resident private limited company is subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide income; a non-resident company is taxable only on Cyprus-source income and gains from immovable property situated in Cyprus; there is no federal/state distinction at sub-national level; the tax classification follows company-law form and central management and control, with no pass-through or territorial-remittance model available for a limited company.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a United States-style check-the-box classification. Available statutory elections and special regimes include: (a) group relief election permitting transfer of tax losses between companies in a 75%+ group under Article 34 of the Income Tax Law; (b) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on qualifying new equity introduced after 1 January 2015, as annually published; (c) Intellectual Property Box election granting an 80% deduction on qualifying IP income, yielding a 2.5% effective taxation rate under nexus rules; (d) Tonnage Tax election for qualifying shipping enterprises, not relevant to algorithmic high-frequency trading; (e) VAT-grouping election for related Cyprus-resident entities under the VAT Law; (f) deemed-dividend distribution elections and deferral mechanisms under the Special Defence Contribution legislation; (g) transfer-pricing documentation and benchmarking elections allowed under the OECD-aligned transfer-pricing rules. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD/ATAR are mandatory and cannot be electively disapplied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 taxable turnover excluding VAT realized in Cyprus during any period of 12 consecutive months for goods and services supplied within Cyprus; EUR 10,000 pan-EU annual threshold for distance sales of goods and electronically supplied services to non-taxable persons in the EU, including Cyprus, pursuant to Articles 121c and 121e of the VAT Law; compulsory registration may also arise from intra-Community acquisition triggers and from holding a taxable presence in Cyprus; voluntary registration from the first euro is available to taxable persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of 0% VAT applies where goods are physically exported from Cyprus to a destination outside the EU and the exporter proves that the goods departed the EU within the statutory period, generally three months from the time of supply; the export must be evidenced by the electronic Customs Export Declaration (SAD) and customs office of exit confirmation. Intra-EU B2B dispatches to a registered VAT customer also qualify for 0% VAT subject to the customer's valid EU VAT number, dispatch transport evidence, and recapitulative statement reporting. For services, the applicable mechanism is primarily the place-of-supply rule: B2B services supplied to customers established outside the EU are outside the scope of Cyprus VAT and thus effectively un-taxed, with the supplier retaining right of input VAT recovery under the zero-rating/outside-scope principles; auxiliary services directly connected with the physical export of goods, such as international freight handling and transport services, are expressly zero-rated. In the algorithmic high-frequency trading context, proprietary trading of financial instruments is exempt without credit or outside the scope of VAT, and no positive VAT or zero-rating determination attaches to the trading P&L; input VAT recovery is governed by the exempt financial service pro-rata rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Electronic SAD export declaration and customs exit confirmation from the competent Cypriot customs office of departure
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note demonstrating physical departure from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/bank settlement receipt evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and proof of recipient establishment (VAT registration certificate or EORI number)

Cyprus does not currently mandate a real-time fiscal clearance system, digital stamping, or SAF-T continuous reporting for VAT. Electronic invoicing is legally recognized under Article 57 of the VAT Law, subject to the recipient's acceptance and authenticity/integrity safeguards; all VAT registrants must file VAT returns (Form 101 standard monthly return; Form 121 quarterly return where eligible) and EU recapitulative statements exclusively through the TAXISnet Cyprus Tax Portal. Cyprus is aligned with EU e-invoicing harmonization under ViDA, with mandatory EU-wide standard e-invoicing and digital reporting slated for phased implementation from the 2025+ legislative timeline, but no national API clearance obligation has entered into force as of the operational date of this dossier.

The Department of Taxation and Cyprus Customs actively audit export zero-rating claims, with heightened scrutiny for high-value goods, round-tripping, missing-trader and MTIC patterns, late export documentation, and inconsistent Incoterms evidence. Failure to substantiate physical export triggers immediate clawback: the zero-rated supply is re-characterized as taxable in Cyprus, the full 19% VAT is assessed on the supply, and the taxpayer becomes liable for statutory late-payment additions to tax of up to 100% of the unpaid tax, plus interest, and the reversal of any input VAT refund claimed. Fraudulent claims to zero-rating or export refunds constitute a criminal offence under the VAT Law, punishable by fines of up to EUR 10,000, imprisonment of up to five years, or both; false customs declarations separately attract penalties under the Customs Code and the Customs Law, including seizure or condemnation of goods. All export support files must be retained for a minimum of six years from the end of the relevant accounting period to withstand audit challenge.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully and freely convertible in Cyprus for both current-account and capital-account purposes. Cyprus has no foreign-exchange control legislation in force; Articles 63 to 66 TFEU on the free movement of capital are directly applicable, and the temporary bank-resolution capital controls of March 2013 were wholly lifted by April 2015. Profit remittance is unobstructed: Cyprus domestic law applies no withholding tax to outbound dividends, interest or royalties paid to non-residents, so no tax-clearance certificate or central-bank permit is required for distributions. Non-resident shareholders can receive dividends in any convertible currency, subject solely to the paying bank's sanctions screening and CRS/FATCA reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable for Cyprus as a euro-area member: no statute imposes surrender of export or trading proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio exists, and no repatriation deadline applies. The Central Bank of Cyprus requires authorised credit institutions to report each external transaction for balance-of-payments statistics under the CBC Balance-of-Payments reporting framework, but this obligation is statistical and anti-money-laundering in nature and does not restrict currency denomination or destination. Trading proceeds earned in USD may be held in a US correspondent account or converted to EUR at the firm's discretion; open FX balances are captured in the own-funds calculation under the Investment Firms Regulation (EU) 2019/2033.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating as the national central bank of Cyprus within the Eurosystem; monetary and foreign-exchange policy competence for the euro is vested in the European Central Bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: LOW for primary treasury, clearing or margin functions; MEDIUM-LOW for ancillary low-value disbursements such as market-data subscriptions and colocation invoices. Under the Cyprus legal framework implementing the E-Money Directive (Directive 2009/110/EC) and PSD2, e-money institutions (EMIs) are not credit institutions: they cannot offer Fedwire or Eurosystem T2 clearing membership, CCP cash settlement, ISDA CSA third-party custody, or prime-broker settlement accounts. CySEC safeguarding rules for investment firms likewise require client money, where any, to be placed with authorised EU credit institutions, and most tier-1 banks will decline treasury flows routed through non-bank aggregators. Use of a licensed EU or UK EMI is defensible only for expense payments capped below the firm's internal materiality threshold and never for house capital or clearing margin.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: equity capital injection by the non-resident shareholder via an MT103 wire in EUR (or USD pre-converted) credited to the CY_LTD capital-escrow account. The receiving bank tags the transaction under the CBC balance-of-payments classification as equity capital of a direct-investment enterprise; the remittance narrative should read DIRECT INVESTMENT – EQUITY – SHARE ALLOTMENT and must include the UETR and the corporate LEI to minimise holds. Verification bundle: board resolution allotting shares; statutory return of allotment filed with the Cyprus Registrar; updated register of members; source-of-funds certificate from the sending bank; signed UBO and director declarations; CRS self-certification; and the CySEC licence abstract confirming regulated status of the payee. Because Cyprus has no exchange control, no central-bank approval is needed and the transfer can settle within one to two banking days through the Eurosystem T2 real-time gross-settlement service (EUR) or a US correspondent (USD).Primary rails in line with the entity's declared target payment rails (Direct_Exchange_Clearing and Fedwire): (i) USD clearing and margin payments to US CCPs and futures commission merchants are executed via the Fedwire Funds Service in same-day format using SWIFT MT103 or, where a third bank acts as an intermediary, MT202 COV cover payment; the US correspondent relationship must be stabilised at Bank of Cyprus or Alpha Bank Cyprus to support low-latency settlement; (ii) EUR disbursements for European CCP margin and T2 settlement are executed via the Eurosystem T2 service from the Cypriot account; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit are inappropriate for exchange-trade settlement and may be reserved for operational invoices (data-centre or infrastructure procurement) where a bank undertaking is required. Each payment file must include the agreed settlement reference, UETR and the clearing-house clearing-member identifier so that the receiving depository (DTCC/NSCC or a European CCP) can match funds. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: LOW for primary treasury, clearing or margin functions; MEDIUM-LOW for ancillary low-value disbursements such as market-data subscriptions and colocation invoices. Under the Cyprus legal framework implementing the E-Money Directive (Directive 2009/110/EC) and PSD2, e-money institutions (EMIs) are not credit institutions: they cannot offer Fedwire or Eurosystem T2 clearing membership, CCP cash settlement, ISDA CSA third-party custody, or prime-broker settlement accounts. CySEC safeguarding rules for investment firms likewise require client money, where any, to be placed with authorised EU credit institutions, and most tier-1 banks will decline treasury flows routed through non-bank aggregators. Use of a licensed EU or UK EMI is defensible only for expense payments capped below the firm's internal materiality threshold and never for house capital or clearing margin.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 aggregate controlled related-party transactions per tax year for a Local File; Master File required when the Cyprus entity is part of an MNE group with consolidated annual turnover exceeding EUR 750,000,000. Documentation must be available within 60 days of a Cyprus Tax Department request.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has not enacted a separate Economic Substance Regulations code, but the tax administration applies OECD BEPS, EU ATAD anti-hybrid/CFC rules, and substance-over-form principles. A Cypriot proprietary HFT company must demonstrate real decision-making, physical trading infrastructure, trained staff, risk management, and algorithm oversight in Cyprus; otherwise the 12.5% rate, IP Box, and DTA relief may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to full taxable income; tax penalty of 100%–200% of the tax understated; default interest at 10.75% per annum; criminal conviction under Cyprus Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002 carries fines up to €3,000 and imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or misdirect trading revenue are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax; courts may issue travel bans, asset freezing orders, disqualification from management, and imprisonment upon conviction.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Cyprus Tax Department will apply center-of-management and substance-over-form analysis, deny tax-residence status, reallocate profits to the true domicile, and apply TP documentation penalties up to 10% of undocumented controlled transactions plus statutory interest; ATAD CFC rules may attribute profits back to the shareholder.

Director personal liability: Directors who continue to trade from a shell structure risk joint civil liability for the tax unpaid, revocation of travel documents, and personal liability in foreign jurisdictions where the true management is located; in severe cases, criminal fraudulent trading liability under Cyprus Companies Law Cap. 113.
AML / Money Laundering / Proceeds of Trading

Statutory sanction: Under Cyprus AML Law 188(I)/2007 and subordinate regulations, the company faces administrative fines up to €50,000 per breach; for money laundering activity, criminal penalties include imprisonment up to 14 years and/or fines up to €1,000,000; assets are subject to civil confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or omitted to implement AML controls are personally criminally liable; sanctions include immediate asset freezing, travel interference, five-year prohibition on managing legal entities, and joint civil restitution orders.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing Cyprus taxable income, late-interest charges, and penalty of 5%–10% on the additional assessed tax; if fraud is proved, criminal fines and imprisonment follow; documentation non-compliance incurs separate administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute intercompany agreements lacking economic substance are personally liable for the penalty and for the company's restitutive obligation; in abusive treaty-shopping structures, foreign courts may pierce the veil and hold directors personally for repayment of tax and interest.

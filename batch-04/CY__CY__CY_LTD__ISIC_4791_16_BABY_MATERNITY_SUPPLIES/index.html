Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Tax Department (Department of Taxation of the Ministry of Finance), Tax Portal: https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy/

E-government filing portal

A foreign legal entity engaged in taxable economic activity in Cyprus must apply online through the TaxForAll portal or paper form for a Tax Identification Code (TIC) and, if relevant, VAT registration. Filing Form TIC (business registration) and, for VAT, Form VAT 101 with certified incorporation documents, memorandum and articles, registered office/establishment address proof, director/officer identity documents, and evidence of place of effective management. The Tax Department normally issues the TIC within 15-30 business days; there is no incorporation fee but a fixed registration fee applies for VAT.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign legal entity engaged in taxable economic activity in Cyprus must apply online through the TaxForAll portal or paper form for a Tax Identification Code (TIC) and, if relevant, VAT registration. Filing Form TIC (business registration) and, for VAT, Form VAT 101 with certified incorporation documents, memorandum and articles, registered office/establishment address proof, director/officer identity documents, and evidence of place of effective management. The Tax Department normally issues the TIC within 15-30 business days; there is no incorporation fee but a fixed registration fee applies for VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election; all limited-liability companies are default taxable persons. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD have been enacted and apply mandatorily, leaving no election. Certain optional statutory mechanisms are available: group loss relief election for 75%+ group, tonnage tax election for qualifying Cypriot shipping, and the IP Box regime (notional income deduction) subject to the OECD modified nexus approach.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 taxable turnover per calendar year for suppliers established in Cyprus; non-established suppliers have no threshold and must register for taxable supplies in Cyprus; remote/distance sales to Cypriot consumers exceed EUR 10,000 in a calendar year triggering registration (can be satisfied via One Stop Shop).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (i) goods physically exported outside the EU or dispatched/transported by or on behalf of the seller to a destination outside the EU, provided the supply is evidenced by a customs export declaration and transport documents; (ii) intra-Community supplies to a VAT-identified customer with a valid Cyprus/EU VAT number, subject to VIES; (iii) services directly related to export/import of goods, including insurance/freight, if the underlying export is zero-rated; (iv) cross-border B2B services subject to the general rule where the customer is established outside the Union and, for B2C services, where the effective use and enjoyment is outside the EU. The supplier must hold enough evidence to prove the movement and the commercial reality of the transaction within the statutory records period (6 years).

  • Customs Export Declaration (EU Single Administrative Document SAD) with the exporting customs office discharge stamp
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR transport document evidencing physical exit from the European Union
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement proving the export invoice was settled in hard currency by the non-resident purchaser

No mandatory real-time continuous transmission control (CTC), digital stamping, or fiscal clearance is in force in Cyprus. Electronic invoicing is lawful if authenticity of origin and integrity of content are ensured through business controls. Public-sector contracting authorities are obligated to receive structured e-invoices under EU Directive 2014/55/EU. VAT returns and VIES recapitulative statements must be filed electronically via the Tax Portal; the forthcoming EU ViDA framework may introduce mandatory digital reporting after its implementation date.

Zero-rated exports are routinely audited against export documents, bank clearances, and counterparty validity. If the Tax Department disallows zero-rating, output VAT is additionally assessed for the original period, with default interest at 10% per annum and a late payment penalty of 5% per month (capped at 100%) or a specific charge under VAT Law 119(I)/2001. Fraudulent claims can trigger criminal fines up to EUR 20,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years, plus an administrative penalty up to 100% of the understated VAT, denial of input VAT deduction, and public naming of the offender.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a Eurozone member state; the euro is fully convertible. There are no exchange controls or restrictions on current or capital account transactions with non-residents. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and repatriation of capital are unrestricted, subject to corporate law solvency tests and tax compliance. The Central Bank retains regulatory and monitoring functions for balance-of-payments reporting and statistical purposes only.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists for export proceeds. Exporters may maintain foreign currency accounts freely and must report cross-border transactions to the Central Bank of Cyprus for statistical compilation (Balance of Payments) when thresholds apply. No mandatory conversion ratio or timeline applies to export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Legally, EU/EEA-authorized electronic money institutions passported into Cyprus may open business accounts or provide payment services for e-commerce treasury operations. Offshore digital EMIs outside the EU must be licensed or authorized by the Central Bank of Cyprus; in practice, Cyprus banks and regulated EMIs reject accounts for unlicensed providers. Due diligence and correspondent banking restrictions limit viability; recommended use as a secondary clearing rail, not a primary settlement account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections route via SWIFT MT103/MT202 in EUR or converted at the central bank rate. Wire instructions must include the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (Equity) or the domestic BPM6 direct investment 'Equity capital' classification. The beneficiary account is the pre-registered Capital Escrow Account in the name of Cyprus Ltd. Verification requires board resolution authorizing the subscription, share allotment and registration in the Cyprus registrar, proof of source of funds from the remitting shareholder, and balance-of-payments notification to the Central Bank of Cyprus.Trade settlements use the following clearing rails: SEPA and TARGET2 for euro-denominated supplier payments in the EEA; SWIFT MT103 for international wire transfers outside the EEA; Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) for high-value inventory purchases; Standby LCs (SWIFT MT760) as performance security; SWIFT MT202 for interbank settlement. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 in annual related party goods/services transactions or EUR 75,000 in related party financial transactions; Master File required when consolidated group turnover of the ultimate parent exceeds EUR 750,000,000; entities below the threshold but engaged in related-party e-commerce flows must still maintain contemporaneous evidence to support fiscal-stamp audits

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to the UAE, but economic substance is enforced through other legal channels: (i) tax residence and TRC issuance requires that the company's board meetings, strategic decisions, and day-to-day management occur in Cyprus, with adequate physical premises, employees, and original records maintained in Cyprus; (ii) the IP Box regime under the Nexus Approach demands demonstrable Cyprus-linked R&D activity; (iii) the Cyprus Income Tax Law's transfer pricing provisions overtly target arrangements with a primary purpose of obtaining a tax advantage; and (iv) EU ATAD anti-abuse rules as transposed in Cyprus, together with OECD treaty anti-abuse clauses, allow the Tax Department to recharacterize or disregard a Cyprus entity lacking real activity—especially relevant for an online maternity supply business where all sales contracts, inventory, and decision-making must be genuinely executed from Cyprus to preserve the 12.5% rate and treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to €5,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years; civil liability of up to twice the tax evaded; a 5% surcharge applies on unpaid tax 30 days after assessment, plus 3.5% annual interest; the Cyprus Tax Department may reassess a 6-year statutory period, extended indefinitely where fraud or willful neglect is proven

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve a fraudulent return can be criminally prosecuted personally; courts may impose imprisonment, travel bans, asset-freeze orders and director disqualification under the Companies Law (Cap. 113); where the corporate veil is pierced through fraudulent trading or sham operations, directors become joint and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Refusal or withdrawal of the Cyprus Tax Residency Certificate; denial of treaty benefits and EU directives; imposition of Cypriot CIT at 12.5% on worldwide income under actual-management rules; assessment plus interest and penalties for misdeclaration; under ATAD GAAR, the arrangement may be disregarded and transactions re-characterized at fair value, with market-rate adjustments to taxable profits

Director personal liability: Directors may be declared personally liable under veil-piercing doctrine for unlawful distributions made while the company lacked genuine substance; courts may grant civil restitution to the Tax Department, issue a travel ban under the Criminal Procedure Law, and prohibit the director from acting as a director of any Cyprus company for up to 10 years.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Breach

Statutory sanction: On conviction, imprisonment up to 5 years and/or a fine of up to €500,000 for the corporate entity; regulatory sanctions include license revocation (reputational and board-level), asset confiscation, and a public censure; penalty for non-reporting of a suspicious transaction is a fine of up to €50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years per occurrence

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are personally criminally liable where they engage in or consent to money laundering, even without direct profit; MOKAS can freeze personal and corporate bank accounts, and courts may impose travel bans and passport seizure pending investigation; a director convicted of AML offences is automatically disqualified from serving on any Cyprus board for 10 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to €5,000 for non-maintenance of the Local File; the Tax Department may adjust the controlled transactions to arm's-length conditions, levy additional tax at 12.5% on the upward adjustment, and apply 3.5% annual interest plus a 5% late-payment surcharge; repeat violations are treated as gross negligence and attract automatic penalty escalation up to double the tax understated

Director personal liability: Directors who certify TP documentation they know to be false may be assessed jointly and severally for the resulting tax shortfall; courts may impose restitution orders and travel bans; for e-commerce operators shifting profits to Cyprus from high-tax jurisdictions, an adjusted profit may expose the director to personal liability in the foreign jurisdiction and Cyprus travel restrictions for extradition purposes.

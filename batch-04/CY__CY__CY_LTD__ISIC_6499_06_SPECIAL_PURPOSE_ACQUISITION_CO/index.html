Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation under Companies Law Cap.113, the Registrar of Companies automatically files the incorporation certificate with the Tax Department, which assigns an 8-digit Corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) without a separate application. For foreign entities establishing a Cyprus permanent establishment, registration is required under Article 8 of N.118(I)/2002 via Form F.M.3/A within 60 days of commencement; a VAT registration application (Form VAT 101/102) is mandatory for foreign EU/non-EU taxable persons carrying out taxable supplies in Cyprus prior to any transaction. A non-resident SPAC promoter must appoint a Cyprus tax representative (Phoros Epitritos) for VAT compliance if no fixed establishment exists. The TIN is unified for income tax, VAT, and social insurance with digital activation via the Taxisnet portal requiring company seal certificate and audited constitutional documents

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or deemed classification election exists under Cypriot law; every Cyprus-incorporated private limited company is ipso facto a tax resident fiscal entity taxable on collective worldwide income at 12.5% subject to territorial attribution rules for foreign permanent establishments. Available statutory elections include: (i) group relief surrender election under Article 26 N.118(I)/2002 for 75%+ group members; (ii) VAT group registration election under Article 14 of VAT Law N.94(I)/2000; (iii) VAT small-enterprise exemption election under Article 11 (annual turnover below EUR 15,600); (iv) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital under Article 9(2A); (v) dividend participation exemption with 5% deemed deductible expense election; (vi) foreign branch exemption election under Article 5(3); and (vii) ATAD-compliant anti-hybrid election notification to the Tax Department in respect of reverse-hybrid SPAC financing instruments

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for supplies of goods and services; separate EUR 10,000 threshold for intra-EU distance sales and cross-border B2B services subject to reverse charge; registration becomes compulsory from the first date of supply once the rolling 12-month threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration allowed for SPAC holding vehicles to recover input VAT on listing, advisory and escrow management costs

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT is available under Article 49 VAT Law N.94(I)/2000 for: (i) direct export of goods dispatched or transported by the supplier or a third party outside the EU; (ii) indirect exports removed by the non-EU customer within 90 days of supply; (iii) international transportation of goods (maritime and air) and related services; (iv) services supplied to non-EU taxable persons where the place of supply is outside Cyprus under Article 47(1)(b), including SPAC arrangement, brokerage, and listing-advisory services benefiting non-EU targets; (v) supply of financial intermediary services linked to export transactions (subject to the 'use and enjoyment' test). SPAC treasury and escrow-interest services provided to a Cyprus resident trust or to a non-EU trustee remain taxable at 19% unless the direct-benefit export condition is documented

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / Form C88)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export out of the EU
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Proof of payment / Inward Remittance Certificate from a Cypriot authorised bank
  • Transport manifests and delivery confirmation signed by the non-EU consignee
  • For services: written declaration from the non-EU recipient confirming establishment outside the EU and beneficial-use documentation

Cyprus operates a dematerialised fiscal compliance framework without real-time central clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction-control (CTC) servers akin to Italy or Greece. Mandatory digital requirements are: (i) electronic VAT return submission (Form V.A.T.100) via Taxisnet on monthly/quarterly cycles; (ii) electronic Intrastat filing for intra-EU goods movements above EUR 460,000 (dispatches) and EUR 200,000 (arrivals); (iii) VIES listing for all intra-EU B2B suppliers; (iv) PEPPOL-based e-invoicing for public-sector B2G transactions under EU Directive 2014/55/EU, with phased private B2B mandate expected by 2027; (v) B2B invoices must include certified electronic signature or EDI without API clearance; (vi) SPAC escrow interest invoices to Cypriot beneficiaries require mandatory EUR/standard fields per Article 19 of the VAT Law

Cyprus has no exchange-control statutory deadline on export-proceeds repatriation following EU accession (Law 1995), but the Tax Department and Central Bank prudential guidelines require the underlying commercial invoice to evidence receipt within a 180-day discounted trade cycle to sustain the zero-rating; failure to substantiate export within 90 days (goods) or 180 days (services) triggers mandatory VAT clawback at the standard 19% rate on the full invoice value, plus: (i) late-payment interest at 1.5% per month or fraction thereof; (ii) a penalty up to EUR 10,000 for incorrect VAT returns under Article 66 VAT Law; (iii) administrative surcharge of 10% on assessed VAT differences; (iv) retrospective reassessment periods of 6 years for ordinary cases and 10 years for fraudulent or deliberate under-declaration (Article 44 Income Tax Law); (v) criminal sanctions for tax evasion including imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 50,000; (vi) for SPAC entities exploiting escrow-interest fee arrangements as fictitious zero-rated exports, the Tax Department will additionally impose a deemed-distribution tax of 17% on the hidden economic benefit and refer statutory auditors to the ICPAC disciplinary committee

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility: the euro is the legal tender of Cyprus (adopted 1 January 2008) and is freely convertible; there are no capital-account or current-account restrictions, and no prior authorisation from the Central Bank of Cyprus is required for any inbound capital injection, dividend remittance, or profit repatriation. Free movement of capital is guaranteed by Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, subject only to EU-wide sanctions, FATCA/CRS reporting, and standard AML/CFT document verification. Dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are exempt from Cypriot withholding tax and may be remitted freely, provided the solvency test of the Companies Law Cap. 113 is satisfied and substance requirements are met. For this SPAC, escrowed IPO and placement proceeds may be held in EUR, USD, or other globally convertible currencies; only balance-of-payments statistical classification and Central Bank of Cyprus transaction reporting apply, not exchange-control permits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable: the Republic of Cyprus imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory surrender ratio, and no conversion obligation on export receipts or inbound capital flows. Exporters and SPAC vehicles may retain all foreign-currency proceeds onshore or offshore in freely held EUR/USD/major-currency accounts with any Cyprus-licensed credit institution. The sole residual duties are the resident bank's balance-of-payments statistical filings to the Central Bank of Cyprus and EU sanctions screening; no CBC approval is required to hold, convert, or disburse export or escrow proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou; Greek: Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), operating as the national competent authority for banking supervision, balance-of-payments statistics, and AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions, jointly with the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem; the Republic of Cyprus maintains no separate Foreign Exchange Administration, and exchange-control functions were abolished upon EU accession (1 May 2004), leaving only ECB Regulation (EU) 2013/33 statistical reporting and EU sanctions obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW for principal escrow custody; MEDIUM for ancillary disbursement rails. Cyprus-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (under the Electronic Money Law, Law 81(I)/2012) and EU-authorised EMIs passporting under the Payment Services Law (Law 127(I)/2018, PSD2) may serve as secondary accounts for operational expenses and small disbursements, but they are not credit institutions and may not lawfully hold SPAC trust or escrow balances. The CySEC/Cyprus Stock Exchange investor-protection framework and Central Bank of Cyprus AML expectations require IPO proceeds to be segregated in a licensed bank trust account. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-authorised or Baltic-authorised) encounter practical receiving-bank friction: Cypriot tier-1 banks frequently reject or place extended compliance holds on correspondent wires sourced from EMIs under enhanced due-diligence policies, and an EMI's lack of a full banking licence prohibits deposit-taking under the Banking Law (Law 66(I)/1997). The recommended architecture is a licensed commercial bank as escrow custodian, with any single EMI/PSP account limited to non-trust operating disbursements beneath materiality thresholds.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Initial trust-escrow funding of a substantial amount from a non-listed SPAC sponsor whose capital stack (share premium and founder shares) appears disproportionate to its stated net worth.

Root cause: Initial trust-escrow funding of a substantial amount from a non-listed SPAC sponsor whose capital stack (share premium and founder shares) appears disproportionate to its stated net worth.

Defense evidence: Auditor's certificate issued by an ICPAC-member firm verifying source of funds; the sponsor's audited financial statements for the most recent three years; a bank reference letter from the remitting bank with a full transaction trail; the executed underwriting/placement agreement; corporate register abstracts and a complete UBO chart; a notarised board resolution ratifying the capital injection; and clean sanctions/PEP screening reports.
High-volume multi-jurisdictional subscription wires from numerous investors, some domiciled in higher-risk jurisdictions, hitting the operating account before the escrow is formally declared.

Root cause: High-volume multi-jurisdictional subscription wires from numerous investors, some domiciled in higher-risk jurisdictions, hitting the operating account before the escrow is formally declared.

Defense evidence: Complete investor KYC/AML files assembled under the placement agent's written procedures; an investor-country risk ranking register; segregation of refund-capable subscription proceeds pending verification; a signed declaration that no crypto-to-fiat mixing or unregulated payment channels were used; FATCA/CRS self-certifications for each investor; and a legal opinion from Cyprus counsel confirming the validity and anti-money-laundering conformity of the subscription agreements.
Identification of the SPAC itself as a high-risk legal arrangement because it is a blank-check entity with deferred disclosure of acquisition targets and no operating revenues.

Root cause: Identification of the SPAC itself as a high-risk legal arrangement because it is a blank-check entity with deferred disclosure of acquisition targets and no operating revenues.

Defense evidence: The Cyprus Stock Exchange or CySEC listing prospectus (if publicly traded) or the private-placement memorandum; the escrow agreement with a licensed Cyprus trustee; an enhanced corporate-governance charter; a detailed escrow deployment timeline for the proposed business combination; periodic transaction statements submitted to the bank's AML compliance unit; and director declarations made under Sections 60–61 of the Companies Law Cap. 113.
Politically Exposed Persons or sanctions exposure among the SPAC sponsor's shareholders or indirect beneficial owners of subscription proceeds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for SPAC equity capital: (1) remittance by SWIFT MT103 for USD/major-currency injections or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR subscriptions, directed to the SPAC's segregated collection account at a Cyprus-licensed credit institution; (2) quotation of the correct EU Balance-of-Payments purpose code in the payment message as codified by ECB Regulation (EU) 2013/33 — 'Equity capital and investment fund shares — Direct investment' for sponsor and private-placement equity injections, and 'Portfolio investment — Equity and investment fund shares' for public-subscription and IPO proceeds — with the bank acting as reporting agent to the Central Bank of Cyprus; (3) pre-delivery to the bank of a director-approved capital-injection board resolution, executed subscription or underwriting agreements, and the final beneficial-ownership chart to enable release of any compliance hold; (4) bank verification of the remitter's identity, source of funds, FATCA/CRS self-certifications, and sanctions/PEP screening; and (5) automatic sweep of cleared proceeds from the collection account into the dedicated Trust/Escrow Account under the escrow trust agreement, with quarterly balance attestation to the trustee and, if listed, CySEC. No Central Bank of Cyprus exchange-control permit is required at any point.Outbound disbursement rail hierarchy for the SPAC's acquisition and exit payments: (a) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for business-combination consideration, public-shareholder redemption payouts, deferred underwriting fees, and escrow release payments — executed strictly against escrow release notices countersigned by the trustee and the SPAC directors; (b) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700 issuance) and bank payment obligations only where the target business combination comprises documentary trade flows or deferred payment obligations requiring protection; (c) SEPA instant credit transfers for EU-denominated operating expenses, professional fees, and director remuneration; (d) USD payments cleared through the bank's New York correspondent via Fedwire or CHIPS, with IRS Forms W-8BEN-E or W-8IMY certification where US counterparties or withholdable payments are involved; and (e) TARGET2 high-value euro tranches above the SEPA threshold for large acquisition settlements. All outbound payments are subjected to real-time sanctions filtering, AML transaction monitoring, monthly balance-of-payments reporting to the Central Bank of Cyprus, and beneficiary-name matching against the target company's closing share register.Deliverable outright FX forward contracts (EUR/USD and EUR/GBP) executed with Central Bank of Cyprus-licensed credit institutions or EU-authorized broker-dealers, permissible for hedging the USD-denominated SPAC trust assets and redemption obligations, provided the underlying exposure is documented and the hedge tenor does not exceed the business-combination window; subject to EMIR (Regulation (EU) 648/2012) clearing or margin-exchange requirements.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing emerging-market target currencies for hedging acquisition consideration that is not fully convertible or not freely deliverable onshore; permissible through the treasury desks of Cyprus banks or their offshore booking centres, subject to Central Bank of Cyprus prudential risk limits, EMIR margining, and documented business-combination exposure.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD cross-currency interest rate swaps) to convert USD escrow yield flows or USD acquisition funding into EUR for the operating account, or vice versa; permissible as principal-to-principal OTC derivatives with Cyprus credit institutions under the Financial Instruments and Markets Law (Law 144(I)/2007, MiFID II) and EMIR.FX swap and rollover transactions (spot-forward swap points) for temporary liquidity bridging between the escrow account and the operating account; permissible without additional licensing when executed on a treasury-execution basis with a licensed credit institution and covered by a board-approved treasury and hedging policy.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: LOW for principal escrow custody; MEDIUM for ancillary disbursement rails. Cyprus-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (under the Electronic Money Law, Law 81(I)/2012) and EU-authorised EMIs passporting under the Payment Services Law (Law 127(I)/2018, PSD2) may serve as secondary accounts for operational expenses and small disbursements, but they are not credit institutions and may not lawfully hold SPAC trust or escrow balances. The CySEC/Cyprus Stock Exchange investor-protection framework and Central Bank of Cyprus AML expectations require IPO proceeds to be segregated in a licensed bank trust account. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-authorised or Baltic-authorised) encounter practical receiving-bank friction: Cypriot tier-1 banks frequently reject or place extended compliance holds on correspondent wires sourced from EMIs under enhanced due-diligence policies, and an EMI's lack of a full banking licence prohibits deposit-taking under the Banking Law (Law 66(I)/1997). The recommended architecture is a licensed commercial bank as escrow custodian, with any single EMI/PSP account limited to non-trust operating disbursements beneath materiality thresholds.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Cyprus imposes no domestic withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders. Dividends distributed to Cyprus tax-resident individual shareholders trigger a 17% Special Defence Contribution (SDC) withheld by the distributing company; Cyprus tax-resident companies and non-residents are outside the scope of SDC on dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the end of the calendar tax year; for a standard 31 December year end, the filing deadline is 30 June of the following year. An approved different accounting period shifts the deadline accordingly.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 aggregate annual related-party cross-border transactions; a Transfer Pricing Summary Information Table (TP-1) must be attached to Form TD when controlled transactions exceed EUR 50,000; the Local File is required above EUR 500,000; the Master File is triggered by group controlled transactions above EUR 5,000,000 and Country-by-Country Reporting by group consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not apply GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Substance is instead tested through the management-and-control test for tax residence, the EU ATAD general anti-abuse rules, OECD BEPS, the IP Box nexus requirement (qualifying research and development activity undertaken in Cyprus), and the pending EU Unshell Directive (ATAD 3) which presumes letterbox status for entities without local premises, an active local bank account and Cyprus-resident directors. A SPAC vehicle must evidence genuine decision-making in Cyprus, maintain Cypriot board minutes, operate its escrow through a Cyprus bank account, and substantiate any participation-exemption or treaty claim with real economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Best-of-judgment assessment; 10% late-payment surcharge; statutory interest at 3.5% per annum; criminal conviction under the Income Tax Law with imprisonment of up to five years and/or a fine of up to EUR 50,000; loss of the right to hold a Tax Clearance Certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign, approve or deliver a false return are personally indictable; court-ordered civil restitution of the shortfall; departure prohibition orders (travel bans); disqualification from acting as a director for up to five years under the Companies Law.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of management and control outside Cyprus with resulting loss of Cypriot tax residence; denial of participation exemption, Notional Interest Deduction and IP Box benefits; application of EU ATAD controlled-foreign-company rules; general anti-abuse reassessment plus 10% surcharge and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and corporate service providers face professional and fiduciary claims for issuing misleading substance declarations; foreign tax authorities may disregard the Cypriot entity under CFC look-through rules; indemnity actions may be brought by SPAC shareholders and trust beneficiaries.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profit to the arm's length result with additional tax at 12.5%; 10% surcharge on the deficiency; interest at 3.5% per annum; administrative penalties for non-submission of the TP-1 form (EUR 100 plus EUR 50 per month, capped at EUR 500); disallowance of the related deduction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve inflated related-party payments may face negligence claims from minority investors and the SPAC board; personal liability remains limited unless fraud or misappropriation is proven.
AML / KYC / Sanctions Breach in Trust Escrow Operations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines for legal persons up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover under the Cyprus AML Law (L.188(I)/2007 as amended); criminal money laundering conviction carrying imprisonment of up to fourteen years; confiscation of the proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and designated persons may be convicted in their personal capacity; asset freeze orders; travel bans; court-imposed disqualification from holding a directorship or compliance function.
Corporate Registry Default / Strike-off

Statutory sanction: Escalating late-filing penalties; removal and strike-off of the SPAC from the Cyprus Registrar of Companies; upon strike-off the company loses legal capacity, its assets are vested in the Registrar and dissolution follows.

Director personal liability: Directors of a struck-off entity remain liable for fraudulent trading and unpaid corporate taxes; personal assessments may be raised against directors for liabilities incurred through post-strike-off trading; departure prohibition orders may be obtained by the Tax Department during recovery proceedings.

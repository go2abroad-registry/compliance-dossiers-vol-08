Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance; online portal: Ariadne (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first incorporate as a Cyprus private limited company through the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver, obtaining a registration certificate. Following incorporation, the company must apply to the Cyprus Tax Department for a tax identification number (TIN) and, where applicable, a VAT registration number. For VAT, the standard procedure is submission of Form VAT-001 to the Tax Department's VAT Central Office, supported by the certificate of incorporation, directors' identification documents, proof of registered office address in Cyprus, good standing certificate from the foreign parent, and a completed statutory notice of intent to trade. The Tax Department issues the VAT number within approximately five to fifteen business days after verifying the applicant's local establishment and economic activity nexus.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must first incorporate as a Cyprus private limited company through the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver, obtaining a registration certificate. Following incorporation, the company must apply to the Cyprus Tax Department for a tax identification number (TIN) and, where applicable, a VAT registration number. For VAT, the standard procedure is submission of Form VAT-001 to the Tax Department's VAT Central Office, supported by the certificate of incorporation, directors' identification documents, proof of registered office address in Cyprus, good standing certificate from the foreign parent, and a completed statutory notice of intent to trade. The Tax Department issues the VAT number within approximately five to fifteen business days after verifying the applicant's local establishment and economic activity nexus.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a 'check-the-box' classification regime. All Cyprus-resident companies are automatically subject to Cypriot corporate income tax at the entity level. However, elections relevant to the tax and VAT profile include: (i) group VAT registration election under an approved VAT group arrangement; (ii) flat-rate scheme for certain small businesses, subject to turnover thresholds; (iii) definitive and annual accounting scheme elections for VAT purposes; (iv) automatic tax residence election by central management and control, resulting in full entity-level taxation on worldwide income, unless the non-resident controlling investor avails of the territorial sourcing rules for foreign permanent establishment profits; and (v) a discretionary election to exempt 100% of foreign dividend income and foreign permanent establishment profits from Cypriot tax if the relevant participation and economic substance conditions are met. Cyprus also elects into preferential regimes such as the notional interest deduction and the deemed-deduction regime, subject to compliance with EU law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 for taxable supplies during any twelve-month period, with a lower EUR 10,000 threshold for intra-Community distance selling; mandatory registration applies from the first euro for non-established foreign entities supplying goods or services with a Cyprus VAT nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies on the supply or exportation of goods dispatched or transported to destinations outside the European Union, provided the exporter establishes that the goods have physically exited EU territory within the legally required period and that all transport and delivery documentation is contemporaneous. The supplier of corporate consultancy services (ISIC 7020) falls under the general B2B place-of-supply rule: services to non-EU customers are zero-rated without VAT, subject to holding the customer's VAT identification number, business registration evidence, and proof that the recipient is established outside the EU. For EU customers, the reverse-charge mechanism applies. Zero-rating eligibility requires registration with a valid Cyprus VAT number, direct linkage of the supply to dispatched goods or offshore recipients, and compliance with the invoicing accuracy rules set out in the Cyprus VAT Law.

  • Customs Single Administrative Document (SAD) / Export Declaration accepted by Cyprus Customs
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or CMR international consignment note verifying physical exit of goods from the EU
  • Commercial invoice stating the exporter, importer, goods description, quantities, and Incoterms
  • Transport and delivery evidence including warehouse receipts, customs clearance documents, or electronic export records from Cyprus Customs
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the Cyprus bank account
  • Proof of payment and foreign currency purchase/sale documentation where applicable
  • Copy of the customer's non-EU VAT identification or business registration certificate
  • VAT 100 export listing and Intrastat declarations (for intra-EU dispatch, where relevant)

Cyprus does not currently mandate a nationwide real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearing, or digital stamping system for private-sector VAT invoices. Businesses may issue paper or electronic invoices subject to EU e-invoicing standards. Since 1 November 2022, Cyprus transposed Directive 2014/55/EU requiring e-invoicing for invoices rendered to Cypriot public-sector entities under the B2G framework, using European Standard EN 16931. No API-based pre-clearance or cryptographic stamping requirement applies to regular B2B and B2C invoicing. VAT returns are electronically filed through the Cyprus Tax Portal, VAT-01 weekly/quarterly/monthly filings, and Intrastat/summary declarations are submitted using authorised digital channels.

The Cyprus Tax Department, assisted by Customs, conducts export verification audits and can require production of the full export shipment file, bank statements, and third-party transport confirmations. If zero-rating is disallowed due to insufficient evidence of physical export, unsubstantiated delivery, false statements, or non-repatriation of export proceeds within the prescribed timeframe, the VAT becomes immediately payable, and Cyrpus VAT law authorises the assessment and recovery of unpaid VAT, plus interests. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims are subject to criminal penalties under Section 49 of the Cyprus VAT Law, including a fine not exceeding EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment not exceeding three years, and to retrospective tax claw-back for up to four years of prior assessments. Civil penalties of 10% of the outstanding tax may be imposed for careless inaccuracies, rising to 50% for deliberate understatements, and the Tax Department can seize assets, issue enforcement directions against the taxpayer's bank accounts, and apply to the court for mandatory recovery, costs and additional penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a Eurozone member and applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no general exchange controls, no national currency peg, and no pre-approval requirement for foreign-exchange transactions. Current-account flows such as service fees, dividends, interest and royalties are freely remittable, as are capital-account flows such as equity injections, shareholder loans, securities purchases and real-estate investments under Article 63 TFEU and EU Capital Movements Directive 88/361/EEC. The main practical hurdles to profit remittance are not FX licensing but rather proof of distributable reserves, bank-level AML/KYC review, CBC balance-of-payments declarations, sanctions screening, and, where relevant, tax residence/anti-avoidance checks. Non-resident profit distributions are not subject to Cyprus withholding tax, and no CBC 'knowledge' certificate is required for dividend out-payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules and no mandatory conversion ratios in Cyprus. A Cyprus entity may invoice in EUR, USD, GBP or any freely convertible currency, and may lawfully retain export receipts in a foreign-currency bank account without converting them into euro. Export receipts must arrive through licensed banking channels and are reported to the CBC for balance-of-payments statistical purposes, but this is an observation and filing obligation, not an approval, repatriation period or surrender requirement. A receiving bank may require underlying export invoices, service contracts, delivery evidence and counterparty sanctions checks before final value-dating of any receipt.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC; Greek: 'Kentrike Trapeza tes Kyprou'), acting as the national central bank of Cyprus and member of the Eurosystem. The CBC supervises credit institutions, electronic money institutions and payment institutions, and coordinates statistical reporting for balance-of-payments and external-sector transactions with the Cyprus Treasury and the Ministry of Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary business-to-business fee collection and small operational disbursements; Low as the primary treasury rail. Cyprus law permits EU/EEA-authorized electronic money institutions (EMIs) passporting into Cyprus under PSD2 to provide payment accounts and IBANs; examples include Wise Europe and Payoneer Europe. However, an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot open a true Capital Escrow Account, issue commercial letters of credit, provide treasury deposits or enter into FX hedging derivatives. Client funds held with an EMI are safeguarded rather than protected by the Cypriot Deposit Guarantee Scheme. Offshore digital EMIs without an EEA passport or without CBC authorisation have no legal standing to operate payment rails for a Cyprus corporate; any material movement through such platforms creates a serious AML red flag and should be disclosed and justified to the onboarding bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be remitted through licensed banking channels, normally by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own account or by SEPA Credit Transfer where the shareholder bank is in the EEA and the currency is EUR. The payment reference must clearly state the nature of the flow, for example 'Capital contribution – [Company Name] – allotment of [share class / number]'. Cyprus banks record such inflows for CBC balance-of-payments purposes under the Eurosystem classification 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital'. No prior CBC approval is required, but the receiving bank will require, before final credit to a Capital Escrow Account or capital-suspense account: a certified board resolution approving the allotment, a current shareholder register, the UBO ownership chain, source-of-funds and source-of-wealth statements, certified identity documents of the subscriber, and, where applicable, apostilled or notarised corporate documents. Shares are deemed paid-up only after actual receipt and confirmation of cleared funds; thereafter the company must issue the share certificate and file the relevant return of allotment with the Cyprus Companies Section.For operating expenses, consultancy fee payments and supplier settlements in EUR within the EEA, use SEPA Credit Transfer, including batch host-to-host payment files via the bank's single euro payments area gateway. For non-EEA and non-EUR disbursements, use SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer with the beneficiary bank, beneficiary full name, IBAN and a purpose code corresponding to commercial services or general trade, supported by the underlying invoice and contract. For high-value or same-day settlement, an MT103 with an MT202 cover payment may be used. For trade-based settlements where a documentary credit is agreed, use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued via SWIFT MT700, with MT707 amendments, and settle by bank-to-bank reimbursement through MT202 after compliant presentation of shipping or delivery documents.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital is EUR 25,000. Before registration, at least EUR 12,500 (half of the minimum capital) must be paid in cash into a German business bank account; for a single-member GmbH, the statutory rule requires payment of the minimum paid-in amount and security (e.g., bank guarantee) for the unpaid residual unless full payment is made. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for federal VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) issuance; local Finanzamt (tax office) responsible for Steuernummer and corporate tax collection; primary electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); customs/export interface: ATLAS/ICE-Gate via Generalzolldirektion.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity deriving German-source taxable supplies or maintaining a permanent establishment must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt, which assigns the statutory Steuernummer; simultaneously or thereafter, an application for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) must be submitted to BZSt via form USt 1 A (or via ELSTER/nationally authorized tax software). For a resident GmbH, registration occurs by operation of law upon notarial incorporation and Commercial Register (Handelsregister) entry; expected processing time for a VAT ID is 2-8 weeks, with retroactive effect permitted if the application is made without undue delay after commencement of taxable activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity deriving German-source taxable supplies or maintaining a permanent establishment must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt, which assigns the statutory Steuernummer; simultaneously or thereafter, an application for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) must be submitted to BZSt via form USt 1 A (or via ELSTER/nationally authorized tax software). For a resident GmbH, registration occurs by operation of law upon notarial incorporation and Commercial Register (Handelsregister) entry; expected processing time for a VAT ID is 2-8 weeks, with retroactive effect permitted if the application is made without undue delay after commencement of taxable activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

GmbH is per se a taxable corporation under Körperschaftsteuergesetz (KStG); no US-style check-the-box election exists. Available elections include: (i) VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG and corporate tax/group attribution under Secs. 14-17 KStG; (ii) anti-hybrid election under Sec. 4k EStG (ATAD implementation) for structured and disregarded arrangements; (iii) trade tax affiliation for partnerships (Mitunternehmerschaft) if the GmbH becomes a partner; (iv) election for flat-rate input VAT deduction under Sec. 23a UStG (limited scope, generally not applicable to a capital-goods manufacturer).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a German-resident GmbH, VAT registration is mandatory from day one with no materiality threshold. The statutory small-business exemption ceiling (approximately EUR 22,000 in domestic turnover for 2024, rising to EUR 25,000 from 2025) may apply only to natural-person or small entrepreneurs and is effectively unavailable for a typical industrial GmbH engaged in export manufacturing. A non-resident entrepreneur performing taxable supplies in Germany is required to register irrespective of turnover unless the reverse-charge mechanism or the EU One Stop Shop (OSS) applies; no de minimis registration threshold exists for non-established suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of export supplies is available under Sec. 4(1)(1) and Sec. 6 UStG where: (i) the supplier is an entrepreneur within Sec. 2 UStG; (ii) the goods are physically dispatched or transported from Germany to a destination outside the European Union (third-country territory); (iii) the goods are not merely dismantled or used in Germany; (iv) the exporter retains documentary proof (primärer Nachweis) and book-keeping evidence per Sec. 6(4) UStG and the Export-Nachweis-Verordnung (ENV). Indirect exports through an acquirer that itself exports remain eligible only with proper customs status documentation. Services directly connected to exported goods (e.g., transport, loading, handling inland) may likewise be exempt or zero-rated. No rating benefit applies to mere invoicing without physical exit; intra-EU deliveries are separately zero-rated under Sec. 6a UStG subject to the customer's valid VAT ID and movement proof.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument / ATLAS electronic export acceptance, MRN endorsement)
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement), Airwaybill, CMR or equivalent international transport document evidencing exit from EU customs territory
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list referencing contract terms (typically CIF/FOB per Incoterms 2020)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit confirmation evidencing the export consideration received from the foreign buyer
  • Proof of transport order (Auftrag/Vertrag) and, where applicable, customs-approved export authorization or end-use certificate
  • EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung) and the customer VAT ID validation record for intra-EU zero-rated deliveries

Germany does not operate a real-time transaction clearance or digital stamping system comparable to Italy (SDI) or Portugal (AT). Mandatory structured electronic invoicing applies: (i) for B2G invoices since 27 November 2020 in XRechnung or ZUGFeRD (EN 16931) format via public-sector e-invoicing platforms; (ii) for domestic B2B from 1 January 2025 (mandatory ability to receive) extending to mandatory issuance of structured e-invoices from 1 January 2027 (with transitional rules through 2028 based on prior-year turnover). Transmission must be via interoperable EDI/e-invoicing networks or email of the standardized XML; invoices must evidence real VAT ID numbers and, for intra-community supplies, the customer's valid USt-IdNr. plus corresponding EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung) filings.

Fraudulent or unsubstantiated export zero-rating triggers full retrospective VAT reassessment under Sec. 173 AO (new facts basis) plus late-payment interest under Sec. 233a AO (presently around 1.8% per annum from 2025) and penalty surcharges (Säumniszuschläge) under Sec. 240 AO. Criminal exposure arises under Sec. 370 AO for tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung), punishable by imprisonment of up to five years, or up to ten years in aggravated cases, and confiscation of the evaded tax amount; administrative fines apply for reckless acts. 'Scheinausfuhr' (sham-export) documentation further constitutes customs evasion and document forgery, leading to clawback of input VAT deductions, restoration of output VAT at 19%, exclusion of export certificates, and disqualification from simplifications such as the authorized economic operator (AEO) status. Additional administrative proceedings may suspend all export zero-rating privileges for a cooling-off period and impose per-invoice falsification penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a full-convertibility euro-area jurisdiction. The euro is freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions; no licence, quota, advance-approval, or counterparty-selection rule restricts a German GmbH from receiving or remitting funds in EUR or any major freely convertible currency. Profit repatriation by non-resident shareholders (dividend, liquidation proceeds, and sale of equity) is unrestricted under the free-movement-of-capital guarantee of Article 63 TFEU, subject only to standard GmbH corporate formalities (shareholder resolution, dividend balance-sheet determination, withholding-tax documentation under Sec. 43a EStG where distributions are made) and the statutory 25% capital-yield withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (reducible or reclaimable under applicable tax treaty). The only operational friction is the Bundesbank statistical reporting obligation for each cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500, normally discharged by the German bank via the integrated statistical reporting interface (IMS/ITRS) on the customer's behalf, but the GmbH treasury must retain the underlying invoice, contract, and purpose code for six to ten years to match the reporting reference.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation deadline for export proceeds. A German GmbH may lawfully retain 100% of export receipts in foreign currency in domestic or EEA bank accounts without converting into EUR; the euro is not subject to any mandatory exchange regime. For each export receipt equal to or above EUR 12,500 the resident exporter or its bank must file the Bundesbank cross-border payments report (Zahlungsmeldung under Sec. 67 AWV) by the last business day of the month following the value date; no pre-export collection declaration is required. The export documents (ATLAS customs declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and LC record) must be archived for the statutory ten-year commercial-book period, and the goods description must be screened against the EU Dual-Use Regulation 2021/821 Annex I; overhead/gantry cranes classified under ISIC 2816 are not normally listed, but an end-user certificate should be kept where a port-automation contract routes to a sanctions-sensitive operator.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany); prudential supervision of banks and payment institutions is exercised by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin). Cross-border payments and the foreign-trade statistical reporting system are administered by the Deutsche Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) and the Foreign Trade Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG); the ECB is the monetary authority of the euro area with delegation to the Bundesbank for on- and off-site oversight within the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz – KWG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMI (e-money institution) wallets are rated LOW-to-MEDIUM viability for this treasury stack. Legal constraints: (i) an EEA-licensed EMI passporting into Germany under PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) and the Second Electronic Money Directive (2009/110/EC) may issue e-money and execute payment transfers but cannot accept client deposits as banking business, cannot grant overdrafts, and cannot issue documentary credits, standby LCs, or project escrow undertakings — all of which are mandatory for port-crane project tenders; (ii) a non-EEA EMI (UAE, UK, or Singapore licence) is treated by German correspondent banks as an unregulated or differently regulated third party, generating a deconcentration and opacity KYC burden and frequent BaFin scrutiny of the underlying wire rationale; (iii) in practice, use should be limited to operating-expense cards, sub-EUR 25,000 supplier wires, and soft-currency conversion at low volume; all contract receipts above EUR 100,000, all LC-linked collections, and all escrow flows must be routed through a tier-1 German commercial bank; (iv) the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) requires full transparency of the EMI's source-of-funds trail and beneficiary information in any SEPA/SWIFT message routed from Germany, so the EMI must provide an unbroken payment-purpose record or its onboarding credit file with a German bank will be unfavourable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMI (e-money institution) wallets are rated LOW-to-MEDIUM viability for this treasury stack. Legal constraints: (i) an EEA-licensed EMI passporting into Germany under PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) and the Second Electronic Money Directive (2009/110/EC) may issue e-money and execute payment transfers but cannot accept client deposits as banking business, cannot grant overdrafts, and cannot issue documentary credits, standby LCs, or project escrow undertakings — all of which are mandatory for port-crane project tenders; (ii) a non-EEA EMI (UAE, UK, or Singapore licence) is treated by German correspondent banks as an unregulated or differently regulated third party, generating a deconcentration and opacity KYC burden and frequent BaFin scrutiny of the underlying wire rationale; (iii) in practice, use should be limited to operating-expense cards, sub-EUR 25,000 supplier wires, and soft-currency conversion at low volume; all contract receipts above EUR 100,000, all LC-linked collections, and all escrow flows must be routed through a tier-1 German commercial bank; (iv) the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) requires full transparency of the EMI's source-of-funds trail and beneficiary information in any SEPA/SWIFT message routed from Germany, so the EMI must provide an unbroken payment-purpose record or its onboarding credit file with a German bank will be unfavourable.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the corporate tax (15.825% combined federal/national level) plus municipal Gewerbesteuer at the 3.5% Steuermesszahl multiplied by the local Hebesatz; total combined corporate burden in industrial municipalities generally 30%–33% (e.g., approx. 31% at 400% Hebesatz; higher in Munich/Hamburg, lower in rural municipalities).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer under § 43a EStG plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag) on dividends distributed by a German GmbH to any non-resident shareholder. Treaty relief may reduce this rate: under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive a 0% rate applies where the beneficial owner is a qualifying EU corporate shareholder with a direct holding of at least 10% for an uninterrupted 12-month period; under the Germany–China DTA (as amended by the 2014 Protocol) the withholding is capped at 10%, with a 5% rate for a corporate beneficial owner holding directly at least 25% of the paying company's capital, subject to the German treaty-abuse rule of § 50d(3) EStG.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; electronic filing via TAXISnet portal (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity conducting taxable activities in Cyprus must first obtain a certificate of incorporation or registration from the Department of Registrar of Companies and Intellectual Property (DROCIP), then apply to the Tax Department for a Tax Identification Code (TIC), which serves as the VAT number where applicable. Non-established taxable persons supplying goods or services in Cyprus must register for VAT through a Cypriot tax representative unless an exception applies. The application must include corporate constitutive documents, identity and governance details, expected turnover, activity description, and registered address evidence. Registration for income tax is mandatory for companies managed and controlled in Cyprus or earning Cyprus-source income.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope for non-resident entities (Cyprus-resident companies are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident companies are taxed on Cyprus-source income only; no pass-through eligibility for private limited companies). — A foreign entity conducting taxable activities in Cyprus must first obtain a certificate of incorporation or registration from the Department of Registrar of Companies and Intellectual Property (DROCIP), then apply to the Tax Department for a Tax Identification Code (TIC), which serves as the VAT number where applicable. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope for non-resident entities (Cyprus-resident companies are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident companies are taxed on Cyprus-source income only; no pass-through eligibility for private limited companies).

Available election forms

No general check-the-box election exists. Specific statutory elections available include group relief elections under the Income Tax Law, tonnage tax election for qualifying Cypriot-flag shipping activities, and the Intellectual Property (IP) Box regime election for qualifying intangible property. Hybrid mismatch provisions under EU ATAD apply, and no elective hybrid mismatch results are permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration threshold is €15,600 in annual taxable turnover for established taxable persons supplying goods or services in Cyprus; non-established taxable persons are generally required to register from the first taxable supply in Cyprus, irrespective of turnover threshold. Separate thresholds apply for distance sales (€35,000) and intra-EU acquisitions (€20,000).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods physically removed from Cyprus to a destination outside the European Union, to goods dispatched to another EU Member State under intra-Community supply rules, and to certain international services. The seller must be able to prove the goods left Cyprus within the statutory time limits and that title or export evidence is properly documented. Zero-rating for services applies where the place of supply is outside Cyprus or where the service qualifies as an export service under the VAT place-of-supply rules, subject to commercial and documentary evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / SAD)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank payment evidence
  • Commercial invoice issued in original
  • Packing list and shipping instructions
  • Proof of departure from Cyprus or arrival at destination outside the EU
  • Copy of the corresponding customs entry in the destination country where available

Cyprus does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for domestic B2B invoices. Invoices must be issued in a secure format under VAT rules and submitted electronically through TAXISnet in VAT returns. Cross-border sales to EU customers are reported in the VIES listing. Cyprus is aligned with European e-invoicing standards for public procurement (PEPPOL) but has not yet implemented a general mandatory B2G/B2B e-invoicing clearance system.

Tax authorities examine export documentation in detail and may assess undeclared VAT with retrospective effect if zero-rating is not substantiated. Penalties include administrative fines, interest on unpaid tax, and, in cases of fraudulent or intentional evasion, criminal prosecution with possible imprisonment. Cyprus also implements EU mutual-assistance measures for cross-border VAT fraud, enabling clawback of input VAT previously recovered on zero-rated sales.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full member of the Economic and Monetary Union and the eurozone; the euro is the legal tender. There are no exchange control restrictions on capital or current account transactions. Profit remittances, dividends, interest, royalties and loan repayments are freely transferable abroad, subject only to standard banking compliance (customer due diligence, sanctions screening) and to appropriate corporate or tax withholding formalities where applicable. The target business of a specialty tea salon lounge (ISIC 5630) is a low-risk non-financial commercial activity, which does not require a CySEC licence; however, a Cyprus private limited company must demonstrate genuine economic substance and commercial purpose before tier-1 banks will maintain an unrestricted euro treasury account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion requirements on export receipts. Exporters may retain and freely use foreign currency proceeds, and there is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio. Companies must only comply with customs and VAT declaration obligations related to export documentation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate-to-High for EU/EEA licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions that passport into Cyprus under PSD2. Low for non-EU/EEA offshore digital EMIs, such as certain UK, UAE or Panama-domiciled platforms, which cannot provide a genuine Cyprus or EU IBAN account and are not recognized as primary treasury providers by the Central Bank of Cyprus or by local merchant acquirers. Offshore EMIs may be used only as auxiliary settlement channels but must never replace a regulated tier-1 domestic banking relationship for core working capital and tax disbursement purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions must be transferred via SWIFT MT103 over SEPA/TARGET2 rails using the beneficiary's IBAN and BIC. The remitter should reference the purpose code for direct investment or other investment/share capital, e.g. the Eurozone Balance of Payments 'share capital' category, together with a narrative of 'capital increase / paid-in share capital'. The receiving bank will verify the stated purpose, the source of funds and the corporate authorisation, and for amounts above EUR 50,000 it will require board resolutions and source-of-wealth documentation. Shareholder loans must use a separate BoP purpose code and be supported by a written loan agreement to avoid re-characterisation as equity.Trade settlements to international suppliers primarily use SWIFT MT103 for open-account and documentary collection payments, with beneficiary country-specific correspondent bank instructions. All outbound payments must comply with EU/EEA sanctions screening, confirm the underlying commercial invoice, and use an internationally recognised transaction purpose code. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where the aggregate value of controlled related-party transactions exceeds EUR 750,000 in a tax year. Master File: mandatory where the consolidated group turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000 in the preceding financial year. A summary information form may also be required to be submitted with the IR4 return when the local file threshold is met.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not administer formal zero-tax Economic Substance Regulations in the offshore/UAE mould. However, the central management and control doctrine, the OECD BEPS standards, and the EU ATAD general anti-abuse rule require genuine economic substance to preserve tax residence claims, benefit from the IP Box, support transfer pricing positions, and resist anti-avoidance challenges. A specialty tea salon with a physical outlet, engaged staff, inventory, and operational decision-making in Cyprus satisfies the requisite substance in practice.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Additional tax equal to the tax evaded, statutory interest, and criminal penalties upon conviction: a fine not exceeding EUR 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to three years; in serious cases, confiscation orders may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly prepare or sign false returns are personally liable to prosecution; the corporate veil may be pierced for fraud, allowing civil recovery from personal assets; courts may impose travel bans and asset freezes during proceedings.
Late Filing or Non-Filing of IR4

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of EUR 100 plus EUR 5 per day late, capped at EUR 200; additional tax of 10% on the assessed tax if the return is filed late; interest of 3.5% per annum on the unpaid amount.

Director personal liability: Persistent non-filing can lead to summons against the directors and company secretary; if directors strip company assets to avoid payment, they may be held personally liable for unpaid tax and judgment debts.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of EUR 500 per missing or incorrect TP document/obligation; possible arm's length re-adjustment by the Tax Department with interest and an additional penalty of up to 10% of the re-assessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve artificial profit-shifting arrangements or false TP declarations risk personal fines, disqualification, and criminal liability for tax evasion where the arrangement conceals taxable income.
Shell Company / Abuse of Legal Structure

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to economic substance; denial of treaty benefits; application of CFC rules and general anti-abuse penalties of up to 100% of the contended tax; possible strike-off and dissolution under the Cyprus Companies Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign dormancy declarations while the company is active, or use the structure to defraud creditors, may face disqualification orders of up to five years, personal restitution orders, and travel restrictions applied by courts to secure tax collection.

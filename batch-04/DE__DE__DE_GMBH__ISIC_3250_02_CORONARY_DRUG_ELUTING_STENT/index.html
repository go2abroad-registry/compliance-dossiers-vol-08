Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) — portals: www.bzst.de and www.elster.de; local municipal tax office (Finanzamt) competent for the GmbH's registered seat for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT filing; BZSt administers the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) and export VAT refund channels for non-established entrepreneurs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The GmbH is a German tax-resident body corporate subject to unrestricted worldwide taxation (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) by reason of statutory seat and place of effective management in Germany. Corporate income tax is levied at 15.0% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 15.83%), augmented by municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a combined jurisdictional rate of approximately 28%–32% depending on the municipal Hebesatz. The GmbH is not eligible for pass-through treatment; profit distributions are additionally subject to a 25.0% withholding tax (Abgeltungsteuer) plus solidarity surcharge, unless reduced under an applicable double tax treaty.

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box regime; a GmbH cannot elect pass-through tax classification. Available statutory grouping and option mechanisms: (i) tax group (Organschaft) election under § 14 KStG, § 2 Abs. 2 GewStG, and § 73 UStG, requiring a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Beherrschungs- und Gewinnabführungsvertrag) plus financial, economic, and organizational integration, creating consolidated corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT unity; (ii) small-business VAT exemption under § 19 UStG (inapplicable to a manufacturing exporter); (iii) VAT option to tax real-estate transactions under § 9 UStG; (iv) trade-tax receivables apportionment elections. Hybrid-mismatch rules are implemented through the interest limitation rule (§ 4h EStG / § 8a KStG), anti-hybrid provisions (§ 4k EStG), and the CFC regime (§§ 7–13 AStG); no elective hybrid-mismatch mechanism is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de-minimis registration threshold applies: any entrepreneur making taxable supplies in Germany is required to register for VAT regardless of turnover. The small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) under § 19 UStG applies only if previous-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 and expected current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000 (from 1 January 2025); a GmbH manufacturing drug-eluting stents on an export scale exceeds these limits and is mandatorily VAT-registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is achieved through VAT exemption with full input VAT deduction: (i) export supplies (Ausfuhrlieferung) to third-country destinations under § 4 Nr. 1 a and § 6 UStG, requiring the drug-eluting stents to be physically transported from German territory to a destination outside the EU; (ii) intra-community supplies (innergemeinschaftliche Lieferungen) under § 4 Nr. 1 b and § 6a UStG, requiring movement of the goods to another EU member state and the recipient's valid VAT identification number as reported in the recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung); (iii) ancillary services economically attributable to the export transaction. Offshore medical-distributor supply chains qualify provided the documentary chain is conclusive. The GmbH must hold bookkeeping evidence (Belegnachweis) and transport evidence (Beförderungsnachweis) contemporaneously with the filing of the VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) filed via ATLAS/ECS with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Export Accompanying Document (EAD) and customs exit confirmation (Ausgangsbestätigung)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical transport to the third-country destination
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms and delivery terms
  • Packing list and product documentary evidence (EC Declaration of Conformity / medical device Master File)
  • Bank SWIFT or inward remittance certificate evidencing payment from the foreign buyer
  • Recipient VAT identification number confirmation and recapitulative statement record for intra-community supplies

Mandatory E-Rechnung (structured electronic invoicing) for domestic B2B transactions: from 1 January 2025 all suppliers must be able to receive structured e-invoices; the obligation to issue E-Rechnung applies from 1 January 2027 for suppliers with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all suppliers. Accepted formats are XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x conformant to European Standard EN 16931, transmitted via the Peppol network or other interoperable channels. Germany operates a decentralized post-audit model without real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-export API authorization; VAT returns and advance returns are submitted electronically through ELSTER on a monthly or quarterly cycle.

The exporter bears the full burden of proof for export zero-rating; documentary deficiencies trigger retroactive VAT assessment at 19.0% plus late-payment interest of 0.5% per month under § 233a AO. Unsubstantiated zero-rating claims expose the GmbH and its managing directors to tax-evasion prosecution under § 370 AO (wilful evasion: imprisonment up to 10 years and unlimited fines) or fines up to EUR 50,000 for negligent violation under § 378 AO. The statutory assessment (clawback) period is 4 years generally, extended to 10 years in evasion cases under § 169 AO. Input VAT credits attributable to the disputed export and refund claims under § 18 UStG may be denied. BZSt coordinates mutual-assistance audits and intra-EU information exchange with foreign tax authorities under EU Regulation 904/2010 and DAC 7, including joint export verification reviews.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible. Germany applies no general capital-account exchange controls, and EU law generally protects capital movements and payments, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering rules, targeted restrictions, and applicable investment or export controls. Current-account trade payments and dividends, interest, royalties, and other lawful remittances may generally be paid cross-border without prior foreign-exchange approval. A German GmbH should document distributable profits, shareholder and board approvals, applicable withholding tax, tax-clearance matters, and the receiving shareholder's tax status. Payments remain subject to bank due diligence and reporting obligations; reporting is not an approval requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no general statutory deadline to repatriate export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender of foreign-currency receipts. Receipts may generally be retained in foreign currency or held in domestic or foreign accounts, subject to sanctions, contractual terms, tax rules, and applicable reporting. Under AWV external-payments reporting rules, a German resident generally reports qualifying payments received from or made to nonresidents when the amount exceeds EUR 50,000, subject to statutory exemptions. Reports are generally due by the seventh working day of the month following the payment month; the applicable reporting category, exemptions, and current Bundesbank instructions should be confirmed for each transaction.

Central bank supervision: The European Central Bank (ECB) conducts euro-area monetary policy. Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany's national central bank and administers applicable external-payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) administers relevant foreign-trade controls. Germany has no general foreign-exchange licensing authority for ordinary commercial payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine euro payments and limited foreign-currency services, subject to provider eligibility, country coverage, transaction limits, and enhanced due diligence. An electronic-money institution is not a substitute for a German deposit-taking bank and may not provide a conventional GmbH formation capital account accepted for commercial-register evidence. Confirm safeguarding, deposit-protection status, SWIFT access, beneficiary-country coverage, sanctions controls, and support for high-value medical-device trade before reliance. Offshore EMIs do not displace German AML, sanctions, tax, or AWV reporting obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For formation capital, open an account accepted by the notary and commercial register, remit the subscribed share capital from a shareholder account in the shareholder's own name, and retain the payment confirmation and source-of-funds trail. The minimum share capital of a GmbH is EUR 25,000; at least EUR 12,500 must generally be paid before registration, subject to the applicable formation facts and legal advice. Use SEPA credit transfer for euro-area payments or SWIFT for other corridors. Identify the remitter as the shareholder and describe the payment accurately as a share-capital contribution or, where legally structured as such, a shareholder loan; do not disguise debt as equity. Germany has no universal foreign-exchange purpose-code regime for ordinary capital injections. Provide the bank with the subscription documents, shareholder resolutions, ownership records, source-of-funds evidence, and commercial-register or notarial documents. Assess AWV reporting for qualifying cross-border payments exceeding EUR 50,000, including relevant exemptions and reporting instructions; this is a statistical reporting obligation, not a prior authorization.Use SEPA credit transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer where eligible for euro payments within the SEPA scheme; use SWIFT customer credit transfer, commonly documented by an MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent, for other international payments. Supply the beneficiary's verified legal name, account and bank identifiers, invoice or contract reference, payment purpose, and accurate originator and beneficiary data. For documentary trade, use an irrevocable commercial documentary credit issued or advised by a bank, with terms aligned to the sales contract and applicable UCP 600 rules; documentary collections may be used where commercially appropriate. Screen parties, banks, goods, origin, destination, and end use for sanctions and export-control restrictions before payment. Retain invoices, purchase orders, shipping and customs records, credit documents, and payment confirmations; reconcile them to accounting and assess AWV reporting where applicable. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on CIT; municipal Gewerbesteuer with typical Hebesatz 200–490% of 3.5% base yields a combined effective corporate tax load of approximately 29–32%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag = aggregate 26.375% on gross dividends; reduced to 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying corporate shareholders; DTA rates may be 0%, 5% or 15% depending on ownership and treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory when intercompany goods transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000 per year, or services exceed EUR 1,000,000 per year; Master File: mandatory when the worldwide group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000; contemporaneous documentation required under GAufzV.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); economic substance is enforced through substance-over-form doctrines in § 42 AO, beneficial ownership requirements under DTAs, CFC rules under §§ 7–13 AStG, and the mandate for substance in § 50d(3) EStG. A German GmbH manufacturing Class III coronary stents must demonstrate actual R&D activity, board decisions in Germany, clinical data custody, personnel, premises, and a substantive business rationale to satisfy tax treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 10,000,000 in aggravated cases; imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years under § 370 AO; additional 6% annual interest on evaded taxes; compulsory assessment adjustments and publication in the tax evasion register.

Director personal liability: Managing directors personally liable under §§ 34 and 69 AO for unpaid GmbH taxes; personal assets may be seized; travel bans and exit denial can be imposed under enforcement provisions; directors may face criminal conviction, disqualification from office, and social security debts not discharged in insolvency.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under § 50d(3) EStG; re-characterization of transactions under § 42 AO; CFC attribution of income under §§ 7–13 AStG; additional taxes with 6% interest; €25,000 late filing penalties and penalty interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners personally liable for reconstructed tax deficiencies; criminal proceedings for attempted fraud; asset freezes, travel bans, and disqualification from directorship; personal financial restitution orders are enforceable across EU jurisdictions.
AML and Transparency Register Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under GwG up to EUR 150,000; regulatory enforcement may suspend banking relationships and freeze assets; criminal liability for money laundering under § 261 StGB with imprisonment up to 5 years; seizure of funds.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML officers in charge personally fined and named in regulatory notices; travel bans and court-imposed reporting duties may apply; criminal conviction can result in loss of licenses and prohibition from managing med-tech entities.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may estimate income and impose an additional penalty under § 162(4) AO of 5% of the resulting taxable income adjustment, minimum EUR 10,000; in addition, default interest of 0.5% per month applies; full expense disallowance for undocumented cross-border payments.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for the 5% penalty and any secondary tax liabilities if they approved inadequate documentation; deliberate falsification triggers tax evasion penalties and imprisonment; evidence of gross negligence leads to director liability for restitution under § 69 AO.

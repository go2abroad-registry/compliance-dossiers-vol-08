Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A Cypriot registered office (a physical address, not a P.O. box) is mandatory under s. 7 of Companies Law Cap. 113. Use of a virtual office provided by a licensed corporate services provider is acceptable for the statutory registered-address requirement, but it does not alone establish tax residence or actual management and control in Cyprus. For the proposed micro-SaaS consumer business with cross-border payment rails, the company should retain a local resident director, maintain board minutes, statutory registers and accounting records in Cyprus, and avoid treating a virtual office as a substitute for substance if Cyprus tax residence or treaty benefits are sought. The Cyprus Tax Department may disregard a nominal presence under substance-over-form or anti-abuse principles.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus — official online portals: ARIADNE (Aριάδνη, https://ariadne.mof.gov.cy) for income tax/VAT registration and filings, and the Cyprus Authorities Online portal (www.gov.cy) for company and e-services; customs export clearance is handled by the Cyprus Customs Department (Τμήμα Τελωνείων), Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the Private Limited Company (Ltd) under the Cyprus Companies Law, Cap. 113 before the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (D.R.O.C.), obtaining the Certificate of Incorporation and company registration number (HE prefix); Step 2 — Upon incorporation, the D.R.O.C. transmits company data to the Department of Taxation which issues the automatic 8-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN); foreign-owned Ltds must additionally file Form T.F.4 (Tax Registration Application) via ARIADNE if not auto-registered; Step 3 — For VAT, file VAT-101 (Application for VAT Registration) electronically through ARIADNE within 30 days after the end of the month in which taxable turnover exceeds €15,600 (or before commencing taxable supplies if required); non-established EU/non-EU digital-service suppliers must register via the EU One-Stop Shop (OSS) or, where OSS is unavailable, by direct registration with the Cyprus VAT authorities; the VAT registration number is issued as CY + 8-digit TIN + 1 check letter; a tax representative may be required for non-established suppliers.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Cyprus Private Limited Companies are separate and independent tax persons subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% (Defence Contribution and NID elections apply); no pass-through eligibility exists for Ltd structures; residence-based worldwide taxation subject to territorial carve-outs (80% exemption for foreign PE profits, dividend participation exemption, and IP Box partial exemption); no domestic tax-exempt zone status applies. — Step 1 — Incorporate the Private Limited Company (Ltd) under the Cyprus Companies Law, Cap. 113 before the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (D.R.O.C.), obtaining the Certificate of Incorporation and company registration number (HE prefix); Step 2 — Upon incorporation, the D.R.O.C. transmits company data to the Department of Taxation which issues the automatic 8-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN); foreign-owned Ltds must additionally file Form T.F.4 (Tax Registration Application) via ARIADNE if not auto-registered; Step 3 — For VAT, file VAT-101 (Application for VAT Registration) electronically through ARIADNE within 30 days after the end of the month in which taxable turnover exceeds €15,600 (or before commencing taxable supplies if required); non-established EU/non-EU digital-service suppliers must register via the EU One-Stop Shop (OSS) or, where OSS is unavailable, by direct registration with the Cyprus VAT authorities; the VAT registration number is issued as CY + 8-digit TIN + 1 check letter; a tax representative may be required for non-established suppliers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Cyprus Private Limited Companies are separate and independent tax persons subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% (Defence Contribution and NID elections apply); no pass-through eligibility exists for Ltd structures; residence-based worldwide taxation subject to territorial carve-outs (80% exemption for foreign PE profits, dividend participation exemption, and IP Box partial exemption); no domestic tax-exempt zone status applies.

Available election forms

Cyprus has no U.S.-style 'check-the-box' regime; entity classification is fixed by law (Companies Law, Cap. 113 and Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002). Claimable elections relevant to a micro-SaaS Ltd: (i) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) — an annual election via the CIT return to deduct notional interest on new equity capital (currently 3.89% annual NID rate bands); (ii) IP Box election — notification to the Tax Commissioner to apply the 80% qualifying-intangible-asset deduction (10% effective rate) for self-developed software/patents; (iii) Group loss relief election (75% group holding) — surrender of current-year losses; (iv) election to apply the 80% foreign-PE profit exemption on non-Cyprus permanent establishments; (v) ATAD hybrid-mismatch filings (Form HDAS) — no elective hybrid treatment is permitted, but mismatch designs must be disclosed. The default classification remains entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 (economic turnover threshold in Cyprus euros) for taxable supplies of goods and services by a Cyprus-established taxable person over any rolling 12-month period; a separate EUR 10,000 annual pan-EU threshold governs intra-EU distance sales and B2C digital/electronically supplied services, above which OSS registration and destination-state VAT apply; non-established suppliers of B2C e-services to Cyprus consumers are subject to a nil threshold (mandatory OSS registration or Cyprus VAT registration).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Articles 54-59 of the Cyprus VAT Law for: (a) physical exports of goods removed from the EU, evidenced by a valid Customs Export Declaration; (b) intra-Community supplies (B2B) of goods to a taxable person holding a valid EU VAT ID (with EC Sales List/VIES confirmation); (c) supplies of services, including digital software subscriptions, where the place of supply is outside the EU under Articles 58-59 of EU Directive 2006/112/EC (B2B general rule — customer business establishment; B2C general rule — customer habitual residence/actual effective use), including sales to non-EU end consumers; (d) intra-EU B2B digital services reverse-charged to the EU customer (no Cyprus VAT). For B2C e-services to EU consumers, zero-rating is NOT available; the supplier must charge the consumer's member-state VAT through OSS. Where Paddle or Lemon Squeezy act as Merchant of Record, invoicing duties shift to the payment-rail entity, and the Cyprus Ltd must evidence that it is supplying the payment provider rather than the end consumer (wholesale/B2B supply).

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Single Administrative Document filed through the Cyprus Customs THISEAS/Export e-system)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR International Consignment Note (transport evidence naming the exporter as consignor)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (official Cyprus bank confirmation of the export proceeds credited to the Cyprus Ltd's account)
  • Commercial invoice / VAT invoice compliant with Article 90 of the VAT Law
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (EUR.1/Invoice Declaration where preference claimed)
  • Proof of actual dispatch (terminal receipts, container-tracking printouts, delivery confirmation, third-party logistics reports)
  • For B2B reverse-charge/zero-rated supplies: customer's valid EU VAT registration number, EC Sales List record and due-diligence file (customer KYC, contract, IP logs); for B2C non-EU digital supplies: FATF-compliant residence evidence (bank card BIN, billing address, IP geo-trace, phone country code)

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) or digital stamping regime is currently in force in Cyprus. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies under EU Directive 2014/55/EU (PEPPOL network and EN 16931 syntax) for invoices issued to Cypriot public authorities; the Tax Department operates an optional eVAT filing framework and ARIADNE e-filing mandates for returns. VAT invoices must contain all statutory data fields under Article 90 of the VAT Law and, in practice, electronic invoices must be issued, transmitted and stored in authenticated (robust) formats. The EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) proposal, once adopted, will introduce digital reporting requirements and e-invoicing for intra-EU supplies, but no Cyprus implementing law has yet been enacted.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility is assured under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Capital Movements Liberalisation Codes. Cyprus repealed all exchange-control restrictions on EU accession (1 May 2004) and completed the transition with euro adoption (1 January 2008). No operative distinction remains between current-account and capital-account convertibility, and there are no separate profit-remittance approvals or quotas. Dividend distributions by a Cyprus private limited company to non-resident shareholders are free of Cypriot withholding tax and any exchange-control authorisation. The only practical hurdles are commercial-bank AML/KYC source-of-funds verifications, EU statistical Balance-of-Payments and foreign-direct-investment reporting, and ordinary documentary evidence required by the beneficiary bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Cyprus does not impose any statutory timeline for repatriation or surrender of export proceeds, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio for exported SaaS or other receipts. Exporters may invoice, receive and retain funds in EUR or any foreign currency whether within or outside Cyprus, provided the complete settlement trail remains reconcilable for VAT purposes, audited financial statements and transfer-pricing documentation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου); the Bank is the national competent authority within the Eurosystem and a direct TARGET2 participant, while the European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: HIGH for supplementary clearing, MEDIUM-LOW as a sole treasury account. Cyprus permits EU-licensed payment institutions and electronic-money institutions to passport services under PSD2, and a Cyprus Ltd may lawfully maintain corporate funds with such institutions without exchange-control restriction. Key constraints: (i) EMI balances are generally outside the Cyprus Deposit Protection Scheme and such institutions do not have direct TARGET2 access; (ii) the EMI must be supervised at home and its safeguarding accounts legally segregated; (iii) a foreign EMI account cannot substitute for a domestic bank account, for the economic-substance file, or for a fully auditable VAT trail; (iv) for consumer micro-SaaS subscriptions, the merchant-of-record structure (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy) is the preferred rail, because it transfers acquiring, refund and chargeback risk away from the Cyprus treasury while preserving a clean reconciliation trail.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque UBO structure: non-EU shareholders, nominee arrangements or multi-layer offshore/trust ownership without current beneficial-owner registrations.

Root cause: Opaque UBO structure: non-EU shareholders, nominee arrangements or multi-layer offshore/trust ownership without current beneficial-owner registrations.

Defense evidence: Certificate of Incorporation; the company's Register of Directors, Register of Members and Beneficial Owners as filed with the Cyprus Registrar of Companies; a certified shareholding waterfall to the ultimate individuals; written non-nominee declarations from all legal owners; apostilled and notarised passport copies of every UBO; and completed bank CDD forms identifying the commercial and treasury purpose of the structure.
Sanctions, PEP or adverse-media flags affecting founders, shareholders or remitting banks, particularly flows involving restricted EU-CFSP jurisdictions.

Root cause: Sanctions, PEP or adverse-media flags affecting founders, shareholders or remitting banks, particularly flows involving restricted EU-CFSP jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: Sanctions and PEP screening snapshots from an endorsed commercial database; a formal source-of-wealth and source-of-funds memorandum prepared by a Cyprus-licensed AML compliance provider; independent professional biographies; three months of bank statements from the remitting shareholder; audited financial statements where available; and a legal opinion from Cypriot counsel confirming that the company carries on no regulated financial activity.
Payment-behaviour red flag: high-velocity, low-ticket digital subscription settlements combined with elevated refunds or chargebacks, obscuring the entity's real economic footprint.

Root cause: Payment-behaviour red flag: high-velocity, low-ticket digital subscription settlements combined with elevated refunds or chargebacks, obscuring the entity's real economic footprint.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Cyprus imposes no dividend withholding tax on outbound dividends paid by a Cyprus-resident company to non-resident shareholders; non-residents also bear no Cyprus income tax on such dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form IR4 must be filed and the final tax settled within 15 months after the end of the tax year; for a 31 December year-end, the return is due by 31 March two years later. A company ceasing trade must file within 12 months of cessation.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First provisional tax instalment due by 31 July and second instalment due by 31 December of the current tax year; the estimate is filed before the payment instalment dates and serves to adjust the instalment base where the previous year's basis is no longer representative.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 - Under the Cyprus Transfer Pricing (Fiscal Provisions) Regulations 2022, effective for tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2022, a Local File is mandatory where the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with any single related party exceeds EUR 750,000; a Master File is required where multinational group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 500 million; a TP Summary Information Table must be filed with the IR4 for all tax years in which controlled transactions exist; Country-by-Country Reporting applies at the EU/OECD group revenue threshold of EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has no standalone Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but applies the OECD/G20 BEPS measures, the EU ATAD-aligned General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) introduced by N.134(I)/2020, and the central management and control doctrine for tax residence. Substance is tested through physical office or adequate home-office presence in Cyprus, substantive board meetings held in Cyprus, active local management of SaaS operations, decision records, a Cyprus bank account, and DEMPE-aligned own-risk IP ownership. A Cyprus micro-SaaS holding IP without local management or employees is vulnerable to TRC denial and reallocation of profit to the jurisdiction where actual economic substance exists.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Law (N.118(I)/2002, as amended) with fines of up to EUR 17,086 and/or imprisonment; civil reassessment carrying 8.5% default interest, automatic surcharges, and a potential penalty equal to the tax evaded; evidence of deliberate fraud extends the assessment window and removes limitation defences.

Director personal liability: Directors and signatories are personally and criminally liable; courts may issue travel bans prohibiting departure from Cyprus, asset freeze and confiscation orders, disqualification from office, and in flagrant cases dissolutions and personal restitution orders.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer), together with the locally competent Finanzamt for the corporate income tax, trade tax and turnover tax assessment number (Steuernummer); official electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); central BZSt portal: www.bzst.de.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the GmbH in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) and file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the locally competent Finanzamt via ELSTER; the Finanzamt issues the statutory Steuernummer. Step 2: Apply to the BZSt under Sec. 27a UStG for the VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) using the official application form (RG 34 USt) or via the BZSt online portal; the USt-IdNr. is necessary for intra-EU B2B supplies, export declarations and input VAT refunds. Step 3: A foreign entrepreneur without a domestic seat registers for German VAT through the BZSt (or the competent Finanzamt for the establishment in Germany) before performing any domestic taxable supply; intra-EU distance sales of goods may be declared through the One-Stop-Shop. Step 4: Registration becomes effective upon written confirmation from the BZSt or Finanzamt; the Steuernummer and USt-IdNr. are separate identifiers and must both be retained for compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German domestic law does not offer a US-style check-the-box election; a GmbH is mandatorily taxed as a corporation at entity level (Körperschaftsteuer 15% plus solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax). Available elections include: (i) the Organschaft fiscal unity election for corporation tax, trade tax and VAT, enabling intra-group profit pooling and central VAT accounting; (ii) the small-enterprise exemption under Sec. 19 UStG for domestic turnover up to EUR 22,000, though generally inadvisable for a CMO/CDMO because it precludes input VAT deduction; (iii) the option to apply the EU VAT reverse-charge place-of-supply rules for cross-border B2B services; and (iv) hybrid-mismatch neutralization elections under the German implementation of ATAD (e.g., Sec. 4k EStG).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies a fully convertible hard-currency regime. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted under AWG Section 1; there are no exchange-control approvals, no FX quotas, no advance-deposit requirements and no repatriation ceilings. Profit remittances, dividends, royalties and intercompany loan repayments to non-residents are freely transferable; however, dividend distributions attract German withholding tax of 26.375% in the standard case (25% dividend tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) unless reduced by an applicable double-taxation treaty after lodgement of a residence certificate. Non-EEA investors may be subject to AWV investment-screening notification in pharmaceutical or critical-chemical sectors where control is acquired, though greenfield GmbH establishment remains permissible.

Export proceeds surrender rules

German law imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion or repatriation obligation on export proceeds. Exporters may invoice and settle in EUR, USD, RMB or any freely transferable currency. The only binding requirement is statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank: every cross-border receipt from a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction, or aggregating above that threshold per counterparty within a calendar month, must be reported under AWV Sections 67 to 69, generally by the obliged German bank, with the customer providing the correct AWV purpose code. Reporting is due by the 7th business day of the month following receipt; non-compliance is an administrative offence.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) as the German Central Bank and Eurosystem member, jointly with the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy; the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) govern cross-border payments and investment screening, administered by the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK); the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) enforces banking, AML/KYC and payment-institution licensing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 annual aggregate cross-border transactions for local file (goods; EUR 500,000 for services) and EUR 100,000,000 group consolidated turnover for master file; documentation must be contemporaneous and retained for ten years

Economic substance scope: Germany's Substance Doctrine under § 42 AO (Gestaltungsmissbrauch): treaty benefits may be denied if the GmbH lacks real economic substance, staff, premises, decision-making functions, and exigency; Section 13b AStG imposes substance and anti-base-erosion tests on license and service fee deductions; BMF Circular of 01/2023 contains binding substance indicators (staff numbers, office, active commercial purpose)

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) / Criminal Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years or a monetary penalty of up to EUR thousands (under § 369-371 AO); aggravated cases (§ 370 (3)) up to 10 years; additional penalty surcharge of 50 % on evaded tax under § 398a AO if cooperation is not timely; seizure of assets, disqualification from public contracts; company fine under § 30 OWiG up to EUR 1 million

Director personal liability: Director may personally be assessed for unpaid tax as statutory representative under §§ 34, 69 AO; aggravating circumstance: civil restitution and insolvency clawback; travel bans and passport withdrawal are routine investigative measures; director can be imprisoned and is personally liable for the entire evaded tax plus surcharges even after insolvency
Artificial Tax Arrangement / Shell Company Abuse / PPT denial

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits: income recharacterised and extra tax of 25 % + solidarity surcharge; additional penalty of 30 % of the adjusted profit under § 162 (4) AO where transfer pricing documentation absent or inconsistent; sectoral tax audit with extended deadlines; registration on the BMF abuse-safe list; potential criminalisation as tax evasion under § 42 (2) AO

Director personal liability: Civil liability of managing directors for the taxes attributed to the artificial arrangement if they knew or ought to have known; court may pierce the veil, holding that the GmbH is an economic impostor, and impose direct personal liability; in a cross-border context, travel ban can be imposed to secure tax payment
AML and Anti-Money-Laundering Compliance (Anti-Money-Laundering Act & KWG reporting regime)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 1.5 million under § 43 GwG (unlimited in aggravated cases), revocation of professional licence (including pharmaceutical licence and CMO certification); prohibition from holding a board position; audit disclosure on the customs and transparency register

Director personal liability: Individual directors face personal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years for intentional breach under §§ 261 StGB (money laundering); civil liability for damage claims of affected parties; in Germany, the FIU can request a travel ban, freeze assets, and director disqualification; parent company director or controlling shareholder can incur joint liability if they participate in the scheme

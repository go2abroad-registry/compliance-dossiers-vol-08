Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Cap. 113 imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a private limited company; the Articles normally fix authorised capital at €1,000 divided into shares of €1.00 and at least one share must be fully paid on incorporation. No escrow account is required by the Companies Law. For the bonded depot authorisation, however, the Customs and Excise Department requires a financial security or bank guarantee covering projected customs debt before operating authorisation is granted.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the Cyprus Fire Service under the Fire Safety and Prevention of Dangerous Substances Law 3/1997; structural load inspection certificate from the District Building Authorities confirming the warehouse is suitable for racked storage; occupational clearance from the Department of Labour Inspection under the Safety and Health at Work Law 89(I)/1996 covering emergency exits, fire suppression, personal protective equipment and general workplace conditions.

Paid-in capital rule: Cap. 113 imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a private limited company; the Articles normally fix authorised capital at €1,000 divided into shares of €1.00 and at least one share must be fully paid on incorporation. No escrow account is required by the Companies Law. For the bonded depot authorisation, however, the Customs and Excise Department requires a financial security or bank guarantee covering projected customs debt before operating authorisation is granted.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus (operational e-portal: ARIADNE Tax Portal and the Tax Department website at www.mof.gov.cy/tax).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first be registered in Cyprus via the Department of Registrar of Companies to obtain a registered company (HE) number and Certificate of Incorporation. Thereafter, the entity applies to the Tax Department for a Tax Identification Code (TIC/TIN) and, if taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed the VAT threshold, for a VAT registration number. Application forms (e.g., VAT-101 for VAT, TIN application form) are submitted electronically through the ARIADNE portal or by physical submission; the VAT number is issued and shown on the VAT certificate. The same procedure applies to a Cyprus Limited company established for a bonded depot in a customs free trade zone.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: Cyprus private limited companies are subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income at the national rate (12.5%); territorial scope applies with credit for foreign taxes on foreign-source income in accordance with the Cyprus income tax law. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: Cyprus private limited companies are subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income at the national rate (12.5%); territorial scope applies with credit for foreign taxes on foreign-source income in accordance with the Cyprus income tax law. Free trade zone bonded depot operations do not alter the entity-level taxation status.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist. Cyprus law permits irrevocable election for group taxation (90% or more subsidiaries), election for tonnage tax (shipping), and election for notional interest deduction on new equity. The Cyprus tax system follows the Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) and hybrid mismatch rules transposed from the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD); an entity may elect to exclude a foreign permanent establishment from Cypriot tax under certain conditions. For a free-trade-zone bonded warehouse, no special zero-tax election is available; the general corporate rate applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 10,000 (annual taxable turnover for goods and services). Non-established businesses performing taxable supplies in Cyprus generally may be required to register if they have any taxable supply falling within the scope of the Cyprus VAT, irrespective of turnover, unless an exempt or outside-the-scope supply applies. For cross-border distance sales and electronically supplied services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies in most cases.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods from a Cyprus free trade zone bonded depot to a destination outside the European Union is zero-rated for VAT, provided that the goods are physically removed from the customs territory of the EU and are accompanied by an export declaration. Services directly related to such goods (transport, handling, storage) may also be zero-rated when performed for the direct needs of the exported goods and supplied to the exporter. Zero-rating requires the supplier to retain valid documentary proof of export, including the accepted customs export declaration, proof that the goods have left the EU within 90 days (or an extended period not normally exceeding 180 days for bonded movement), and evidence of payment.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document or electronic export declaration as T1/T2 movement through the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department)
  • Proof of removal from EU territory: bill of lading, air waybill, CMR, or certified warehouse receipt evidencing physical dispatch to the non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation from the foreign buyer establishing payment of the export sale in accordance with the contract and applicable exchange-control rules

Cyprus has not introduced a real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) or fiscal clearance mandate. E-invoicing is permitted and legally accepted provided it preserves integrity and readability, but no API-based clearance or digital stamping is required. VAT returns, recapitulative statements (VIES), Intrastat filings, and EC sales lists must be submitted electronically through the Tax Department ARIADNE portal. Bonded warehouses and free-zone operators must maintain stock records and issue standard VAT invoices in accordance with Articles of the VAT law.

The Cyprus Tax Department and Customs authorities may conduct post-export audits to verify the physical departure of goods and the genuineness of transactions. Failure to substantiate zero-rating within the documentary deadline or within any extension results in retrospective clawback of VAT at 19%, plus interest (currently 1.5% per month or as determined by law) and administrative fines of up to double the tax due; fraudulent zero-rating claims may attract criminal penalties including imprisonment up to two years and personal liability for directors, in addition to compulsory VAT liability and cancellation of VAT registration. A bonded warehouse operator found to have facilitated fictitious exports risks loss of the bonded/returned-goods licence and severe financial sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Cyprus is a full member of the European Union and the euro area since 1 January 2008; the legal tender is the Euro (EUR), and there is no bespoke national exchange-control or convertibility bar. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully liberalised under EU law: equity injection, shareholder loans, profit repatriation, dividend distribution, branch remittances, and royalty/fee payments to non-residents may flow freely without prior Central Bank of Cyprus authorisation, provided that (i) the transaction is reported for statistical purposes to the CBC's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position reporting regime, (ii) the beneficial owner and source-of-funds documentation satisfies the collective AML obligations under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law L.188(I)/2007 (as amended), the CySEC and CBC anti-money-laundering directives, and the 4th/5th EU Anti-Money Laundering Directives, and (iii) the transaction does not breach EU Russia/Belarus sanctions, OFAC or UN restrictive measures. Practical profit-remittance hurdles are therefore not regulatory but operational: corporate treasury must demonstrate economic substance (premises, personnel, central management in Cyprus), current tax compliance (defence of the 12.5% corporate income tax rate and the notional interest deduction), audited financials on file, and a credible transfer-pricing and base-erosion-and-profit-shifting policy to avoid deemed-dividend or controlled-foreign-company reclassification in the residence jurisdiction of the shareholder. The free-trade-zone bonded depot adds a customs dimension: movement of goods must comply with the Community Customs Code (Regulation (EU) 952/2013) duty-suspension rules and customs warehouse authorisation (authorisation type 'CW'), but this does not impede EUR convertibility. No residual exchange controls are in force; the CBC has not activated safeguard capital-flow restriction measures, and any counterparty bank imposing pre-approval conditions does so on internal-risk grounds rather than Cypriot statutory grounds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Cyprus imposes no statutory obligation to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within a fixed timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio exists for foreign-currency receipts. Exporters of goods (including bonded-warehouse operators who export from customs warehouses or free zones) may retain 100% of proceeds in any freely convertible currency with a licensed Cypriot credit institution or an EEA-passported bank, provided that the underlying transactions are recorded in the CBC's external-transactions statistical reporting (Form O and Form P returns under the CBC Balance of Payments reporting instructions), that the exporter's books reflect the foreign-currency asset at the correct EUR equivalent using the European Central Bank reference rate, and that treasury records demonstrate a genuine commercial/trade receivable. Because the functional currency of a Cypriot company is ordinarily EUR (unless an exception is approved), a USD/GBP receivable creates a currency-exposure position that should be covered by a permitted FX forward as set out in the hedging instruments section. The absence of surrender rules also applies to free-zone bonded depots: the duty-suspension regime is indifferent to the currency in which goods are sold, though the customs-licensed operator must maintain a proper inventory-accounting system (per the CBC's and the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department's authorisation conditions) and must ensure that export declaration data (customs clearance messages) reconcile with the payment rail used.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), operating as the national competent authority within the Eurosystem and as the designated Foreign Exchange Administration for the Republic of Cyprus, implementing the European Central Bank's monetary policy framework and the EU Capital Movements Directive (Article 63 TFEU); prudential supervision is delegated to the CBC resolution and banking supervision directorates.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for ancillary cross-border operations; LOW as a stand-alone replacement for the primary corporate treasury account. The Cyprus-licensed and EU-passported electronic-money institutions (e.g. e-money institutions authorised under the Electronic Money Law L.81(I)/2021 and those passporting under the EU E-Money Directive 2009/110/EC, including multi-currency platforms, business payment accounts, and currency-hedging fintech wallets) may lawfully hold client funds and execute FX conversion, but they are not credit institutions and cannot offer the following Cypriot statutory features that a bonded-warehouse treasury requires: (i) official CBC-licensed account with reporting connectivity to the Balance-of-Payments statistical system, (ii) a formal customs-warehouse guarantee relationship accepted by the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department for duty-suspension authorisation 'CW', (iii) commercial letter-of-credit issuance (MT700), bank guarantees, or the full SWIFT correspondent banking network in high-risk trade corridors, (iv) access to TARGET2-Cyprus and the domestic JCC Payment Systems clearing, and (v) deposit-protection cover under the Cypriot Deposit Guarantee and Resolution of Credit Institutions Law (DGS coverage is only for credit institutions' deposits; EMIs safeguard rather than insure funds). Furthermore, the 'banking_aml_risk_tier' for the target entity is Medium_Risk_Enhanced, which in practice causes Cypriot banks to demand that the unbundled fintech wallet be disclosed and ring-fenced; on-boarding digital-only EMIs as the lead account may fail CBC source-of-wealth and business-intent scrutiny, and a correspondent-bank review of the ultimate beneficiary may freeze wallet funding chains. The pragmatic blueprint is a two-rail model: a physical Tier-1 domestic bank account as the primary treasury and settlement rail, plus a selected regulated EEA EMI for low-value FX conversion and supplier payments in foreign currency (USD into the UAE market, GBP into the UK), subject to pass-through documentation and a formal review by the Cyprus-bank relationship officer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route A (Non-EU non-resident shareholder): the shareholder's overseas bank transmits EUR via SWIFT MT103 (or through a correspondent chain) directly to CY_LTD's Cypriot Tier-1 account, with beneficiary account stated as CY_LTD 'Bonded Warehouse Treasury – Capital Escrow Sub-Account' and purpose-of-payment code 'Corporate Capital Subscription / Equity Contribution (EC02)' or, for loans, 'Intra-Group Shareholder Loan (EC01)' per the CBC external statistics code list. In all cases, an electronic PDF copy of the MT103 field 70 details should show a unique reference such as 'EQUITY-CYLT-2025-001' to permit automated reconciliation with the Volos/Swift and the CBC Form P reporting. Route B (EU/EEA shareholder): a SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) is used for post-2008 euro-denominated funds, settled over STEP2/TARGET2 instant rails; no FX is exchanged, and only the CBC statistical threshold of €12,500 monthly is relevant for reporting purposes. For the bonded-depot operating model, capital injection is customarily split into (i) registered paid-in equity (minimum €2,500 to satisfy the CYP 1,000 threshold comfort, though no statutory minimum applies) and (ii) a subordinated shareholder loan documented by a formal agreement which the bank files into the credit file; both legs are reported to CBC via the corporate enterprise's monthly 'external transactions in goods and services' return. Verification protocol: the bank will require proof of source of funds, a UBO declaration, and copies of the Commercial Registry filings; the inbound funds must clear in EUR at the same bank before any treasury disbursement, unless the bank approved a specific matching loan. No FX control permit, CBC approval, or MBA declaration is required for the injection itself, but failure to report the transaction in the CBC statistical filings can trigger administrative fines and AML slippage.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (i) SWIFT MT103 Single Customer Credit Transfer for all high-value non-EUR and EUR international payments, each carrying a proper UN CEFACT / ISO 20022 structured remittance containing customs entry references so that the exporter's bank can evidence the underlying trade; (ii) Commercial documentary Letters of Credit (MT700/MT710) issued by the Cypriot bank in favour of overseas suppliers, with the bonded-warehouse operator named as applicant and customs-warehouse receipts as the underlying goods certificate, which is particularly suitable for imports into the free zone on a sight basis with a carrying bank's confirmation for suppliers in UAE/Egypt/Nigeria where political-risk cover is needed; (iii) SWIFT MT799/MT202 cover payments for interbank USD settlement through the correspondent (Bank of Cyprus's London or New York correspondents) rather than the domestic Cyprus ACH, since USD transfers require a Fedwire-clearable chain; (iv) SEPA Credit Transfers for EU supplier payments (standard or instant SCT Inst), settled via the European central clearing network through CBC's participation in TARGET2-Cyprus; (v) EBA Clearing STEP2 for lower-value EU retail/payment batches; (vi) JCC Payment Systems Ltd for any domestic card-acquiring (e.g. collection from local customers of bonded-goods handling fees) which then sweeps into the main treasury on a T+0/T+1 basis; and (vii) documentary collections (D/P, D/A) for trusted repeat counterparties as a cheaper alternative to L/C, always accompanied by the full transport document pack (bill of lading, customs export declaration, packing list). All outbound disbursements must be pre-approved under the bank's transactional screening and must exactly match the commercial invoices; the free-trade-zone bonding status does not exempt the operator from the bank's obligation to obtain a declaration that goods will not be released to a sanctioned party or from the CBC reporting of each Eligible transaction. The recommended liquidity allocation is to hold one day's operational float in EUR current account (SEPA-capable), one week's trade float in a USD 'trade settlement' sub-account (Fedwire-capable), and to keep the remaining free treasury cash under a short-term (up to 1-month) fixed deposit with the same bank to smooth the liquidity coverage ratio without triggering extra counterparty approval.Outright EUR/USD foreign-exchange forward with a licensed Cyprus/EEA credit institution (ISDA 2002 master agreement with CSA collateral terms) for hedging USD-denominated bonded-warehouse receivables and payables up to a 1-year tenor; not for speculative leverage, with hedge effectiveness documented by a treasury hedge register.EUR/GBP vanilla foreign-exchange swap (spot and forward leg) to match UK-pound trade settlement cash flows generated by the bonded depot's controlled foreign-currency invoicing.Cross-currency interest-rate swap (EUR/USD CCS) used solely to swap the currency denomination of medium-term treasury deposits and asset-liability matching, with no option feature and an ISDA/CSA legal protocol in place.EUR/Foreign-Currency non-deliverable forward (NDF) permitted strictly where the underlying pair involves a non-convertible or restricted currency (offshore CNH, NGN, EGP, UAH, or similar emerging-markets currencies) contracted with an EEA-authorised bank's London or non-EU dealing desk; the Cypriot treasury may execute such NDFs for risk-reduction against third-country customers' cross-border deals only, and must evidence the underlying trade through the customs-bonded depot documentation; NDFs on freely convertible pairs (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP) are permissible but commercially unjustifiable.FX options (European-style EUR calls/puts with premium paid up-front) for hedging contingent trade flows, such as a prospective award letter or an LC-funded export receivable where the timing of settlement is uncertain; only plain-vanilla strategies are allowed, and exotic structures are incompatible with the entity's treasury policy.FX swaps used for liquidity transformation: converting surplus EUR into USD to fund a duty-suspension import where the bank provides a back-to-back forward that locks the reversal rate at maturity, all within the approved CBC balance-sheet reporting parameters and subject to the bank's credit limit for derivative exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for ancillary cross-border operations; LOW as a stand-alone replacement for the primary corporate treasury account. The Cyprus-licensed and EU-passported electronic-money institutions (e.g. e-money institutions authorised under the Electronic Money Law L.81(I)/2021 and those passporting under the EU E-Money Directive 2009/110/EC, including multi-currency platforms, business payment accounts, and currency-hedging fintech wallets) may lawfully hold client funds and execute FX conversion, but they are not credit institutions and cannot offer the following Cypriot statutory features that a bonded-warehouse treasury requires: (i) official CBC-licensed account with reporting connectivity to the Balance-of-Payments statistical system, (ii) a formal customs-warehouse guarantee relationship accepted by the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department for duty-suspension authorisation 'CW', (iii) commercial letter-of-credit issuance (MT700), bank guarantees, or the full SWIFT correspondent banking network in high-risk trade corridors, (iv) access to TARGET2-Cyprus and the domestic JCC Payment Systems clearing, and (v) deposit-protection cover under the Cypriot Deposit Guarantee and Resolution of Credit Institutions Law (DGS coverage is only for credit institutions' deposits; EMIs safeguard rather than insure funds). Furthermore, the 'banking_aml_risk_tier' for the target entity is Medium_Risk_Enhanced, which in practice causes Cypriot banks to demand that the unbundled fintech wallet be disclosed and ring-fenced; on-boarding digital-only EMIs as the lead account may fail CBC source-of-wealth and business-intent scrutiny, and a correspondent-bank review of the ultimate beneficiary may freeze wallet funding chains. The pragmatic blueprint is a two-rail model: a physical Tier-1 domestic bank account as the primary treasury and settlement rail, plus a selected regulated EEA EMI for low-value FX conversion and supplier payments in foreign currency (USD into the UAE market, GBP into the UK), subject to pass-through documentation and a formal review by the Cyprus-bank relationship officer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File where the aggregate amount of controlled transactions (goods, services, royalties, interest, financial guarantees) with related parties exceeds EUR 1,000,000 per tax year. Master File is required when the Cyprus group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000. Country-by-Country Report threshold is group consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies EU ATAD and OECD anti-BEPS substance principles: a Cyprus company must demonstrate actual management and control within Cyprus, including local board meetings, adequate physical office, qualified employees, and genuine risk/function profile. Although Cyprus does not maintain an offshore ESR regime, treaty and EU Directive benefits are denied where the entity lacks economic substance or the arrangement fails the Principal Purpose Test. A bonded warehouse in a free trade zone must additionally maintain licensed premises, customs bonded stock records, and operating substance in Cyprus.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years and/or monetary fine up to EUR 100,000; administrative penalty equal to 100% of the evaded tax; additional 3.5% interest and daily surcharges; reopening of prior tax years and assessment of retroactive tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; courts may impose civil restitution orders, travel bans prohibiting departure from Cyprus, disqualification from acting as directors for up to 5 years, and custodial sentences for conspiracy or fraudulent misrepresentation.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as tax-resident or as distribution of profit to a non-resident; denial of tax residence certificate; imposition of 60% accuracy-related penalty; potential refusal of DTA benefits; strike-off of the company by the Registrar of Companies and revocation of customs licence for the free-zone depot.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Cyprus entity may be personally assessed for the company's unpaid tax under nominee corporate governance principles; travel bans are applied where involvement in a sham arrangement is proven; criminal penalties of imprisonment up to 3 years may be imposed for false declarations in tax filings.
AML / Customs Bonded Warehouse Violations

Statutory sanction: Customs Code penalties of up to EUR 50,000 per violation and discretionary seizure/forfeiture of goods; AML fines up to EUR 1,000,000 for serious systemic deficiencies; loss of bonded warehouse authorisation and permanent debarment from operating free-trade-zone inventory.

Director personal liability: Directors face separate criminal liability under the Cyprus AML and Customs Acts; civil restitution of forgone customs duties and VAT is recoverable personally; courts commonly impose travel bans, director disqualification, and imprisonment of up to 5 years where wilful default or fraud is established.

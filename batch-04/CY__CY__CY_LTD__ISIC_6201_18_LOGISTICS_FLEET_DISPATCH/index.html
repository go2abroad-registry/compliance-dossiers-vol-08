Subscriber contributions may be held post-incorporation in the company's corporate bank account.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance (official e-services portal: www.mof.gov.cy/taxdepartment)

E-government filing portal

For a Cyprus private limited company (HE77), registration begins at incorporation with the Department of Registrar of Companies. The Tax Department issues a single 8-digit Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) upon filing the 'Commencement of Business/Registration' form. Non-resident entities with a Cyprus branch or permanent establishment must obtain a Cyprus TIN by registering with the Tax Department within 60 days of commencement of Cyprus business; concurrently, a VAT registration form (VAT 101) is required if taxable supplies are made in Cyprus.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. All Cyprus companies are per se taxable entities. Partnerships may opt for transparency treatment under Cyprus income tax law. Hybrid mismatch considerations are governed by the Prevention of Tax Avoidance Act and EU ATAD 2016/1164; specific election available for de-grouping relief, participation exemption, and capital allowance structures. For VAT, small enterprises may elect for the cash accounting scheme or flat-rate scheme where eligible.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for Cyprus-established businesses; non-established foreign suppliers have no turnover threshold and must register before any taxable Cypriot supply

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) exported goods physically delivered to a non-EU destination, provided transport is evidenced and title passes outside Cyprus; (ii) certain intra-community constructive supplies where goods are dispatched to another EU member state; and (iii) exported services if the place of supply is outside the EU and effective use/benefit satisfies the proof requirements of the Cyprus VAT Act. For logistics and fleet-telematics SaaS services supplied to non-EU clients, zero-rating is available where the customer is outside the EU and the digital service is not used within Cyprus.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EXA) / Electronic Customs Declaration code
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill and/or CMR consignment note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank statement evidencing cross-border settlement (as prima facie export proof)

Cyprus has not implemented a national mandatory real-time clearance/continuous transaction control system. There is no general API-based fiscal stamping requirement. Electronic invoice authenticity and integrity must be safeguarded (e.g., EDI, digital signature, e-SAF-T structured files). B2G e-invoicing is mandatory in accordance with EU Directive 2014/55/EU using PEPPOL-compatible formats. Cypriot VAT invoices must retain a sequential numbering and include all statutory data fields.

Export zero-rating is privileged and therefore highly scrutinized. Five-year VAT assessment window (extendable in fraud cases). If export proof is missing, false, or does not corroborate payment repatriation within 180 days from issuance of the export invoice, the tax authorities will re-attribute VAT at the standard rate plus interest at 1% per month (statutory late-payment interest) and penalty surcharges of 10% per completed month of assessment. Fraudulent zero-rating claims involving false invoices or fabricated outward transport documentation may attract penalty up to EUR 500,000 and/or criminal imprisonment under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Laws, with full retrospective clawback and disqualification from VAT export verification.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a Eurozone state with full capital and current account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU and the ECB/CBC regulatory codes. Profit remittance is unrestricted onshore after corporate governance formalities; dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are free of Cyprus withholding tax, and the Special Defence Contribution on dividends was abolished for tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2021. No IMF/ECB restriction applies; EUR is legal tender.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Cyprus abolished exchange controls upon EU accession; no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation ceiling for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in EUR or any foreign currency in a licensed bank account; the CBC collects balance-of-payments statistics (BPM6) on a flow-only basis through reporting banks.

Central bank supervision: The Central Bank of Cyprus (Kendraki Trapeza tis Kyprou); the European Central Bank exercises Eurozone monetary policy and operates TARGET2. CySEC licensing is not applicable to this non-investment logistics-SaaS entity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions; a stricter EUR 250,000 threshold applies to transactions involving intangibles and financing (including the fleet-telematics software licence).

Economic substance scope: Cyprus's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) and the EU ATAD/DAC6 rules apply. A logistics-fleet SaaS entity must prove genuine management and control in Cyprus: leased offices, qualified employees, board decision-making, and actual exploitation of the software IP. Insufficient substance will deny the reduced IP-box corporation tax rate, may lead to reclassification as a shell and, under the Cyprus Companies Law, strike-off where business is inactive.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under Cyprus Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)2002), additional assessment of underpaid tax plus 1.5% monthly interest; penalty of 100% of evaded tax; criminal fine up to EUR 50,000 per tax year, and imprisonment for up to five years on fraud conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign tax returns with actual knowledge of evasion are personally liable for unpaid corporate tax through civil restitution; courts may issue travel bans, disqualification orders, and custodial sentences for up to five years; the corporate veil can be pierced for fraudulent trading.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations enable the Tax Commissioner to deny tax incentives (including the IP box exemption), assess penalties up to EUR 50,000, mandate substance compliance, deregister the entity, and communicate findings to foreign tax authorities under exchange-of-information provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly fronting a shell may be held jointly and severally liable for the denied tax benefits; court-ordered compliance actions and director disqualification are standard; travel bans may be imposed pending material compliance.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus AML law provides criminal fines up to EUR 1,000,000, imprisonment up to 14 years, asset freezing/confiscation orders, and mandatory filing of suspicious transaction reports with the Cyprus MOKAS (Financial Intelligence Unit).

Director personal liability: Directors are personally criminally prosecutable as principals if they knew or suspected funds were criminally derived; corporate veil is lifted for confiscation against assets, and directors are subject to travel bans and disqualification orders.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Commissioner may audit transfer pricing at the arm's-length principle, adjust taxable income, impose interest and a further tax penalty of up to 100% of the undercharged tax; separate administrative fines apply for missing documentation, and information can be compelled under DAC6.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally engineered a mispricing scheme are liable for the adjusted corporate tax, can be sued for civil damages by the company, and may face criminal charges of false accounting and tax fraud, with corresponding prison terms and travel restrictions.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus (Tmima Forologias); national e-portal: Ariadne Government Tax Portal (taxportal.mof.gov.cy; legacy Taxisnet). The Tax Department operates the TIN registry, corporate income tax, Special Defence Contribution, Capital Gains Tax, employer/PAYE and social-insurance integration. The VAT Service (Ministry of Finance) administers the Value Added Tax Law 95(I)/2000. Cyprus Customs and Excise Department (Ministry of Finance) administers import/export declarations, EORI, NCTS and AES/ICS. The Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) licenses the public REIT vehicle and the Cyprus Stock Exchange (CSE) admits REIT units; the Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver maintains the corporate registry.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the private limited company (HE registry) with the Registrar of Companies (ROC), obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, and file the registered office address. Step 2: The ROC transmits incorporation data to the Tax Department; the Tax Department issues the company's 8-digit Tax Identification Code (TIC/TIN); if no TIC letter is received within 30 days, submit Form TD1001 Application for Tax Registration to the District Tax Office within 60 days from commencement of business (Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law 4/1978). Step 3: Register for VAT with the VAT Service on Form VAT-101 within 30 days of the first taxable event; a new company intending taxable activity generally registers from incorporation and no EUR 15,600 threshold applies to a newly incorporated entity with projected taxable turnover. Step 4: Obtain a Cyprus EORI number (prefix CY) from Customs for any export or intra-EU movement. Step 5: If the vehicle operates as a Cyprus REIT, obtain the CySEC licence under the Cyprus Real-Estate Investment Trusts legislation and, where units are publicly marketed, list them on the CSE. Step 6: Enrol on the Ariadne portal with corporate identity documents to file VAT Form 400/402, VIES, Intrastat, income tax and social insurance returns electronically. The corporate TIN is 8 digits; the VAT number is CY + 9 digits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Cyprus private limited company is an opaque fiscal person: resident companies are taxed on worldwide income at the 12.5% corporate income tax rate; there is no automatic pass-through of income, losses, credits or gains to shareholders. The Cyprus REIT remains an incorporated company for fiscal purposes; the special REIT regime applies base adjustments, qualifying real-estate income/gain treatment and distribution-conditioned reliefs, but does not convert the vehicle into a US-style transparent trust. Territorial scope: management-and-control resident and Cyprus-domiciled companies pay tax on worldwide income, subject to the participation exemption for qualifying foreign dividends and gains; non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. No tax-exempt zone applies. The REIT's rental income is taxable at 12.5% with real deductions; qualifying distributions and disposal gains may benefit from REIT, participation or capital-gains exemptions, and investor-level taxation applies at the shareholder tier.

Available election forms

Cyprus has no check-the-box election; corporate opacity is the statutory default. Available elections include: (a) VAT grouping under section 18 VAT Law 95(I)/2000 for closely linked economic, financial and organisational entities; (b) option to tax (waiver of exemption) on the letting/leasing of immovable property under section 9(9) VAT Law, permitting input VAT recovery on construction, financing and property costs; (c) election for Cyprus REIT status requiring CySEC licensing, portfolio and distribution conditions, with consequential income-tax base adjustments; (d) group loss relief election for 75%-held Cyprus tax groups; (e) participation exemption on qualifying dividends and capital gains from subsidiaries; (f) ATAD hybrid mismatch rules (Law 35(I)/2019) apply mandatorily with no elective safe harbour and require documentation of hybrid instruments and reverse hybrids; (g) voluntary VAT registration and OSS registration for cross-border distance sales. All elections are made in writing to the Commissioner of Taxation/VAT Service through the Ariadne portal.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for suppliers established in Cyprus; non-established (foreign) entities making or intending to make taxable supplies in Cyprus are registrable with no de minimis threshold; EUR 10,000 pan-Union annual threshold applies for intra-EU distance sales of goods and electronically supplied services to Cypriot consumers via the OSS scheme; B2B reverse charge displaces supplier registration where the Cypriot customer is VAT-registered. A REIT making taxable management services or opting to tax commercial leases must register from inception.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero percent VAT applies to: (i) goods physically exported to destinations outside the EU, provided the supplier evidences customs export (SAD/MRN) and transport movement within 90 days of supply; (ii) intra-EU B2B dispatches where the customer holds a valid EU VAT number, the goods are dispatched within the statutory period, and the supplier files the recapitulative statement (ESL); (iii) international transport and related ancillary services; and (iv) certain services supplied to non-EU recipients under place-of-supply rules. Cyprus-situs real estate services, rental/leasing and property portfolio management are deemed supplied in Cyprus and are not export zero-rated: they are VAT-exempt under section 9(9) or taxed at 19% where the option to tax is exercised. Export input-VAT refunds are substantiated through periodic VAT Form 400/402 returns, Form 404 refund applications, supplier VAT invoices, customs MRN/exit confirmations, bills of lading, bank remittance evidence and, where required, certified third-party confirmation from freight forwarding agents.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document/SAD lodged via Cyprus Customs electronic export system, with Export Accompanying Document and MRN)
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill / CMR) evidencing physical dispatch from the EU within 90 days of supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice) evidencing receipt of foreign sales proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice (9-digit Cyprus VAT number, 0% rate and statutory wording)
  • Proof of transport contract and insurance certificate, where applicable
  • Movement certificate T1/T2L/ATR where Community transit or preferential origin status is claimed

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or state-run digital stamping system is in force in Cyprus as of the knowledge date. Mandatory electronic filing of VAT returns (Form 400/402), VIES statements, Intrastat and income tax returns is executed through the Ariadne Government Tax Portal, which supports authenticated e-services (one-time passwords, digital certificates) and machine-to-machine interfaces for approved intermediaries. E-invoicing is mandatory in B2G procurement under EU Directive 2014/55/EU using EN 16931 formats; B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary but EN 16931-compliant invoices are accepted for audit substantiation. Customs export declarations are lodged electronically through EU AES/ICS2/NCTS integrated with the Cyprus Customs national environment. The EU VAT in the Digital Age reform, potentially introducing mandatory B2B e-reporting and clearance, is pending transposition.

Cyprus has no exchange-control statute imposing a repatriation deadline on export proceeds; the 180-day benchmark reflects Cyprus banking practice, export-credit norms and Tax Department documentary inspection expectations, and remittances after 180 days require written justification to avoid presumptive reclassification. The Commissioner of Taxation and the VAT Director may open and amend assessments within six years from the end of the accounting period, extended where fraud or wilful default is shown. Penalties for fraudulent export zero-rating include: reassessment at 19% VAT; statutory interest on under-declared VAT; administrative penalties under section 58 VAT Law up to the amount of the tax evaded and higher for deliberate concealment; criminal prosecution with fines up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to three years for forged SAD, Bill of Lading or remittance certificates; customs-law penalties including forfeiture of goods; joint and several liability of directors; refusal or retrospective clawback of input-VAT refunds with interest. A REIT mischaracterising exempt Cyprus property income as zero-rated export turnover faces the same assessment, clawback and penalty regime.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is subject to full current-account and capital-account convertibility for Cyprus-domiciled entities under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), read together with the EU Capital Movements Directive (88/361/EEC). No exchange-control authorisation, quota, or conversion permit is required for cross-border payments, property acquisition financing, dividend distributions, or repatriation of investment proceeds. Cyprus applies no retained capital restrictions outside the EU-Russia sanctions package and the EU/UN anti-terrorism financing regime, both of which are administered through screening obligations imposed on banks under the CBC Directive. Profit remittance by a CySEC-licensed REIT is unrestricted; distributions qualify for a 0% Cyprus dividend withholding tax under domestic law (no WHT on outbound dividends), subject to the anti-abuse provisions of ATAD and the substantial-activity/CFC rules, and to the availability of a double tax treaty where a foreign shareholder is involved.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirement on export proceeds or service receipts; the euro is the functional currency and no timeline exists for converting invoiced foreign currency. Non-financial corporates must only satisfy the Central Bank of Cyprus Balance-of-Payments / International Investment Position (BoP-IIP) statistical reporting obligation, which is discharged by the entity's authorised credit institution through the CBC's electronic reporting framework; as a REIT with predominantly rental and distribution flows, the company must nonetheless file quarterly BoP returns via its relationship bank where cross-border transactions exceed the CBC EUR 100,000 annual aggregate reporting threshold per reporting category.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating as the national competent authority for banking supervision, payment systems oversight, and foreign-exchange and Balance-of-Payments statistical reporting; the European Central Bank (ECB) retains exclusive Eurosystem monetary-policy and euro-issuance authority for the Republic of Cyprus, which has been a euro-area member since 1 January 2008.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

EU/EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) holding a valid passported licence into Cyprus under Directive 2009/110/EC are viable only as supplementary payment-rail service providers (feasibility: MEDIUM), because the CySEC-regulated REIT's dividend distribution and capital escrow mandate require a prudentially supervised CBC-licensed credit institution as primary treasury counterparty. Non-EU/offshore digital EMIs are not viable (feasibility: LOW) in the Cypriot market: they lack a CBC-recognised legal personality for corporate account opening, create an unacceptable upstream third-party payment-receiving risk in the bank's correspondent network, and frequently fail the board-approved treasury counterparty policy which requires an EU-licensed bank or a member of an established Cypriot banking group.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 in related-party transactions per category (goods, services, financial, intangibles) triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation. The threshold is measured annually and may be applied on an aggregate basis for each category.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code like certain other jurisdictions, but the Tax Department and global regulatory expectations apply substantial substance requirements in practice. Cyprus tax residence, transfer pricing documentation, and participation exemption claims are only available where the company demonstrates real management, employees, premises, expenditure, and decision-making in Cyprus. A REIT with only nominal activity in Cyprus risks challenge. Since 2019, Cyprus has adopted a 'substance and control' test in tax-related regulatory licensing, and financial institutions, including trusts and corporate services providers, must maintain substance. A shell-like REIT is a red flag.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / false tax return

Statutory sanction: If convicted of tax evasion, corporate officers and entities are liable to imprisonment up to five years and fines up to €10,000. The court may also order payment of the evaded tax plus penalty interest up to 10% of the tax due. The Tax Department may impose civil penalties of up to 50% for fraudulent understatement.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns can be held jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts. Travel bans and exit restrictions can be issued. Civil recovery and asset seizure may follow for dishonest assistance in the evasion. Where fraudulent trading is proven, directors face personal disqualification and imprisonment.
Shell company / sham transaction / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: The Tax Department may disregard the corporate form, deny tax residence, impose a recharacterisation, disallow treaty benefits, and apply the general anti-avoidance rule. Penalties may reach 20% of the avoided tax. Criminal charges for money laundering under the Cyprus AML law may be raised where shell structures are used to obscure beneficial ownership.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable if they allow the company to operate without substance and cause reckless insolvency or fraudulent trading. Courts may lift the corporate veil if the company is used as a façade. Civil penalties and travel restrictions may be applied to foreign directors. Criminal prosecution for money laundering may carry sentences up to five years.
AML / KYC / UBO reporting failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €200,000 and criminal sanctions for non-registration of UBO information. Suspicious activity non-reporting is a criminal offence. In severe cases, freezing orders and confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally accountable for AML failures. Imprisonment up to five years and fines. Personal travel bans and freezing of personal assets are possible.
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: An administrative penalty of up to €15,000 for non-compliance. Additionally, the tax authority may make a primary adjustment, increasing taxable profits. Potential secondary adjustments and interest may accrue. In case of gross negligence or intent to mislead, criminal penalties may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to maintain documentation or approve non-arm's length pricing can be personally fined up to €20,000. Directors may be held liable for additional taxes, penalties, and interest if they signed inaccurate returns.
Failure to file / late filing / non-payment

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty: €100 per month of delay up to €1,200. If tax is unpaid, interest at 1.75% per month is charged. In addition, a mandatory 10% surcharge on unpaid tax may be imposed. A company that is persistently non-compliant risk being struck off the registry.

Director personal liability: In a striking-off scenario, directors can be personally liable for taxes due if they traded while insolvent. Travel bans, personal liability orders, and disqualification orders may be issued.

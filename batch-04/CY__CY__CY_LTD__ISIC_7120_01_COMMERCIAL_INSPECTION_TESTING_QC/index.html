Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Cyprus imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a private limited company. Capital of EUR 1,000 divided into 1,000 ordinary shares of EUR 1.00 each, with at least one share fully paid, is conventional; there is no escrow account or bank deposit precondition to registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Civil Defence/Fire Service confirming means of escape, extinguishers, and alarm adequacy; structural load clearance by a licensed civil engineer; and occupational health and safety clearance from the Department of Labour Inspection under Law 89(I)/1996.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; electronic portal: Ariadne / Taxisnet at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity may obtain a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN) by submitting the prescribed registration application to the Department of Taxation. A Cyprus-incorporated private limited company receives a TIN automatically upon incorporation. A non-resident entity that has a permanent establishment, a tax representative, Cyprus-source taxable income, or VAT registration obligations must file the relevant registration form and the registration certificate issued by the Registrar of Companies. VAT registration is obtained separately through the Department of Taxation using the prescribed VAT registration form; the entity must identify its principal place of business, local representative, anticipated taxable turnover, and pro-forma evidence of intended export or local activities. No minimum capital or local shareholding requirement applies, and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or general hybrid entity election exists under Cyprus tax law; Cypriot private limited companies are opaque and separately taxable at the entity level. Available statutory election-type mechanisms include: (i) VAT group election for closely connected taxable persons; (ii) group loss-relief election under the Income Tax Law for companies satisfying group ownership thresholds; (iii) provisional tax estimation and quarterly prepayment election; and (iv) election to apply specific capital allowance pooling methods. Hybrid mismatch rules follow EU ATAD and allow no elective reclassification of a Cyprus entity as transparent or taxable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 domestic taxable turnover threshold; EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold for intra-Community distance sales of goods and cross-border electronically supplied services to consumers under the OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically exported from Cyprus to a destination outside the European Union are zero-rated for VAT under section 68(3) of the Cyprus VAT Law, provided the supplier retains valid proof of exportation within the required time period. Services directly connected with exported goods, international transport of goods to non-EU destinations, and certain ancillary services supplied to non-EU persons or for goods consigned to non-EU territories may also qualify for zero-rating if the recipient is established outside Cyprus/EU and the effective use and enjoyment of the service takes place outside the EU. The zero-rating claim must be supported by contemporaneous export evidence, and any input VAT attributable to that exempt-for-credit supply is recoverable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document or electronic customs entry confirming export from Cyprus/EU)
  • Bill of Lading or alternative transport document (air waybill, CMR, or ship's clearance certificate confirming delivery to non-EU destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent SWIFT/bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident counterparty
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, and inspection/quality-control report confirming the underlying trade transaction

No general real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based invoice clearance regime applies to commercial B2B/B2C invoices in Cyprus. VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and Intrastat filings must be submitted electronically through the Department of Taxation Ariadne/Taxisnet portal. Electronic invoicing using the European EN 16931 standard is mandatory for invoices addressed to Cypriot public-sector contracting authorities; private-sector e-invoicing remains voluntary.

Cyprus has abolished exchange-control approval requirements; the 180-day period is applied as the practical documentary benchmark used by tax auditors to test commercial substance and export-loan repatriation. The Department of Taxation may audit export zero-rating claims by verifying customs records, movement documents, bank receipts, insurance certificates, and the technical inspection reports typical for ISIC 7120 activity. If an export is found to be fictitious, documentation is incomplete, the proof-of-export deadline is missed, or the goods did not leave the EU, the supplier is assessed for VAT at 19% plus interest and additional tax under the Cyprus Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law. Fraudulent zero-rating claims expose the company and its officers to criminal penalties, including fines and imprisonment, as well as cancellation of VAT registration and retrospective clawback of input VAT recovered on the underlying export transaction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Cyprus has used the euro since 1 January 2008 and applies unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU. The historic Foreign Exchange Control Law (Cap. 92) no longer imposes authorisations, quotas, or profit-remittance hurdles. Dividend and profit repatriation to EU and non-EU shareholders is free of Central Bank approval. Remaining formalities are statistical and compliance-driven: bank-filed ITRS/ETRS transaction classification, EU/OFAC sanctions screening, CRS/FATCA reporting, and evidence of management-and-control substance in Cyprus to defend the 12.5% corporate tax rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists. Cyprus prescribes no export-proceeds repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio to EUR, and no haircut on foreign-currency receipts. Income from factory-inspection and quality-control services may be retained indefinitely in EUR or in foreign-currency accounts (USD, GBP, AED) at an authorised Cyprus credit institution. The receiving bank performs all reporting under EU Regulation 2015/847 and CBC external-assets statistics; cash settlements above EUR 10,000 are prohibited, and each receipt must be traceable to the underlying inspection contract and invoice in the audit file.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC) - Greek: Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου; member of the European System of Central Banks and the Eurosystem; competent authority for banking licensing, AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions, payment-system oversight, and cross-border capital-flow statistical reporting via the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS/ETRS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (supportive but non-substitutable). Cyprus transposes PSD2 and the EU EMI Directive, so EEA-licensed digital EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex) may lawfully process SEPA receipts and low-value operational disbursements. Legal constraint: under CBC supervision and AML law 188(I)/2007, the EMI cannot serve as the sole or primary settlement rail; CBC ITRS/ETRS reporting, documentary trade finance, FX hedging lines, and audit-confirmable banker certificates are available only from a Cyprus-licensed credit institution. Recommended architecture: Tier-1 Cyprus bank for all trade and capital flows; EMI used only for disbursements and client receipts below EUR 10,000 per transaction, with invoice-level reconciliation and full Regulation 2015/847 audit trails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 in annual aggregate controlled transactions for mandatory Local File preparation; Master File is required where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000 and controlled transactions exist. The separate Country-by-Country Reporting notification threshold remains EUR 750,000,000 group consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not have a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the OECD/EU substance requirements apply. A Cyprus Ltd must have real premises, employees, actual management, and substantive decision-making in Cyprus to preserve tax residence and treaty benefits. Artificial letter-box or shell arrangements are challengeable under the Cyprus GAAR and may be disregarded for tax purposes; IP box and bonding/financing structures must satisfy the nexus or substance tests.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to EUR 15,000 and/or imprisonment of up to three years under Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002, plus recovery of unpaid tax, interest at 1% per month, and administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorized or participated in the fraud may be criminally prosecuted, subjected to travel restrictions and exit bans, and held personally liable for losses under fraudulent trading rules in the Cyprus Companies Law.
Late or Non-Filing of Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of EUR 100 plus EUR 50 per month of delay, capped at EUR 500; late-payment interest of 1% per month; enforced collection through attachment of bank accounts, property seizures, and third-party debt orders.

Director personal liability: Directors may be treated as officers in default and subjected to personal fines, director disqualification orders, and imprisonment if the failure is part of an evasion scheme.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Taxation authorities may adjust taxable profits, disallow deductions, impose an increased tax assessment plus interest, and apply administrative penalties for failure to submit TP documentation or for submitting inaccurate TP forms.

Director personal liability: Directors who engineered artificial profit shifting can face personal civil liability for unpaid corporate tax if the company is treated as trading fraudulently or with intent to defraud creditors; travel bans may accompany formal tax fraud proceedings.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Application of the Cyprus general anti-abuse rule to disregard artificial arrangements, reclassify the entity, recompute tax at effective economic substance, deny treaty benefits, and impose penalties and interest on the re-assessed liability.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally accountable for fraudulent trading where the shell is operated with intent to defraud creditors or tax authorities; courts may order civil restitution, freezing orders, personal expense assessments, and imprisonment.
Money Laundering / Bribery / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law 188(I)/2007, severe penalties include imprisonment of up to eight years, fines of up to EUR 500,000, confiscation orders, asset seizures, and reporting penalties for non-filing of STRs.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are statutory 'persons exercising control' and can be criminally prosecuted personally; their assets may be frozen, travel restrictions imposed, and they may be barred from acting as company directors in Cyprus.

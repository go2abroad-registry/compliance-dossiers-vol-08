Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance) — official e-portal: Tax For All at https://taxforall.mof.gov.cy; VAT registration, periodic VAT returns, OSS returns and EC Sales Lists are filed on Tax For All; export customs clearance is administered by the Cyprus Department of Customs and Excise.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company (HE77) operating a voice-streaming app: (1) incorporate with the Registrar of Companies and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and registered-office certificate; (2) apply for a TIN through Tax For All using Form 4 (Application for Registration of a Legal Entity) within 60 days of commencing activity; (3) once taxable turnover reaches €15,600, or immediately upon making the first taxable supply if non-established, file Form 1 (Application for VAT Registration) on Tax For All together with constitutional documents, director and shareholder identification, and proof of Cyprus registered office; (4) for intra-EU B2C digital services, elect the Union OSS scheme instead of multi-state VAT registration; (5) the Tax Department issues a VAT registration number bearing the 'CY' VIES prefix within approximately 7–21 working days; (6) maintain statutory records, OSS transaction details for 10 years, and VAT records for 6 years.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company (HE77) operating a voice-streaming app: (1) incorporate with the Registrar of Companies and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and registered-office certificate; (2) apply for a TIN through Tax For All using Form 4 (Application for Registration of a Legal Entity) within 60 days of commencing activity; (3) once taxable turnover reaches €15,600, or immediately upon making the first taxable supply if non-established, file Form 1 (Application for VAT Registration) on Tax For All together with constitutional documents, director and shareholder identification, and proof of Cyprus registered office; (4) for intra-EU B2C digital services, elect the Union OSS scheme instead of multi-state VAT registration; (5) the Tax Department issues a VAT registration number bearing the 'CY' VIES prefix within approximately 7–21 working days; (6) maintain statutory records, OSS transaction details for 10 years, and VAT records for 6 years.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists under Cyprus law; a Cyprus Ltd is always a separate entity-level taxpayer on worldwide income at the 12.5% corporate rate (territorial sourcing applies only to non-residents). Statutory elections available: (i) voluntary VAT registration below the €15,600 threshold; (ii) VAT grouping election under s.19(A) of the VAT Law with Tax Department approval; (iii) OSS election for EU-wide B2C electronic services / distance sales; (iv) no hybrid mismatch election — Cyprus mandatorily applies EU ATAD hybrid rules without an elective pass-through status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 taxable turnover over the preceding 12 consecutive months for Cyprus-established businesses; €10,000 cross-border B2C distance-selling and electronic-service threshold from another EU Member State under the OSS; non-established suppliers of electronic services are taxable from their first supply with no threshold; B2B/B2C place-of-supply nexus follows Articles 44–45 of the VAT Directive.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Cyprus to non-EU destinations qualify for the 0% VAT rate under s.6 of the VAT Law and Article 146 of Directive 2006/112/EC if the goods leave the Union within the prescribed period, the exporter retains responsibility/risk during export transport, and the Commissioner’s documentary conditions are satisfied. Intra-EU B2B supplies to a customer holding a valid VAT registration number in another Member State are zero-rated subject to EC Sales List filing. Voice-streaming digital services: B2B supplies to EU business customers are reverse-charged at the customer’s VAT number (no zero-rating claim needed); B2C digital services to EU consumers are taxed through the Union OSS at the destination Member State’s VAT rate; B2C supplies to non-EU consumers fall outside the scope of EU VAT; Apple IAP / Google Play Billing commissions payable by the Cypriot company are B2B services subject to reverse-charge in Cyprus, with creditable input VAT where the app is used for taxed business activities.

  • Validated Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / e-SAD) with the Declaration Unique Consignment Reference from the Cyprus Customs ICONe export system
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical removal from Cyprus within 30 days of supply in the name of the exporting Cyprus entity
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and Incoterms 2020 evidence demonstrating at-risk ownership during export transport
  • Bank SWIFT/telegraphic-transfer payment evidence or inward remittance certificate showing settlement of the export consideration in foreign currency
  • EC Sales List (VIES) declaration for intra-EU zero-rated supplies supported by the customer’s valid EU VAT registration number

Cyprus does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance mandate, continuous transaction control system, or tax-authority digital stamping/API clearance for e-invoicing. Mandatory e-invoicing currently applies only to B2G supplies using the EU PEPPOL format conforming to EN 16931. Following the EU ViDA adoption, Cyprus will phase in mandatory B2B e-invoicing and digital reporting on a future roadmap; as of 2025 compliance is based on periodic VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, OSS returns, Intrastat declarations where applicable, and retention of electronic invoices.

Cyprus VAT Law does not codify a statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; nevertheless, 180 days is the administrative audit benchmark used by the Tax Department to evidence receipt and repatriation of export proceeds. If the documented export bundle (SAD, B/L, remittance evidence) is not produced, or payment is not traceable within that window, the Tax Department recharacterises the supply as domestic, applies 19% VAT, and issues a retrospective assessment within 3 years (extended to 6 years for fraud), together with statutory interest and civil penalties for misdeclaration (up to €500 per offence). Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute a criminal offence punishable by fine and/or imprisonment for up to 3 years. The Commissioner may also disregard artificial structures aimed solely at obtaining VAT refund advantages under EU anti-abuse jurisprudence (Cases C-255/02 Halifax and C-131/13 Italmoda).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a euro-area Member State. The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU Articles 63-66) and the EU Capital Movements Directive. There are no exchange controls, no restrictive convertibility mechanisms, and no requirement for central bank approval for profit remittance. Dividends, distributions, and shareholder loan repayments may be repatriated freely, subject to standard corporate law formalities, tax clearance, and AML/KYC compliance. Nonetheless, banking counterparties scrutinise source of funds, beneficial ownership, economic substance, and the operational reality of the entity. In practice, a Cyprus private limited company whose revenue derives from Apple App Store and Google Play Billing must demonstrate that digital operations, management decisions, and substance functions are consistent with its Cyprus tax residence and bank risk appetite.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no surrender requirement, no statutory conversion ratio, and no mandatory repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export receipts are freely retainable and transferable in euro or any other currency. The CBC requires statistical balance-of-payments reporting for export transactions above its reporting thresholds, generally through the resident bank reporting system. The entity must maintain accurate underlying commercial documentation such as sales reports, app store remittance statements, contracts with users, and billing records. Because the target business monetises primarily through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, the app store remittances will appear as settlement flows from Irish or other EU Apple/Google entities; these should be classified correctly in the bank's purpose code declaration and CBC statistical returns.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou / Central Bank of Cyprus, CBC), operating within the Eurosystem and under the oversight of the European Central Bank (ECB). For matters involving inward and outward cross-border payments, balance-of-payments reporting, and capital flow statistics, the CBC is the competent national authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate to High, subject to legal constraints. A Cyprus-domiciled operating company may open payment accounts with EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) passporting into Cyprus under the Revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2). EEA EMIs with valid passport and SEPA membership can provide cost-effective cross-border EUR clearing, virtual IBANs, and multi-currency wallets. However, such accounts are generally treated as settlement accounts rather than full-relationship banking. Major Cypriot commercial banks may be reluctant to accept app-store revenue channelled solely through an offshore or non-EEA EMI. Non-EEA EMIs, particularly those without an EU licence and without SEPA direct membership, are not recommended for treasury concentration; their viability is Low due to correspondent banking friction and enhanced AML scrutiny. The optimal structure is a dual-rail arrangement: a Cyprus-regulated or EEA-passported EMI for operational payment flows, backed by a top-tier Cypriot bank account for capital injection, reserve concentration, and high-value counterparty settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections should be routed from the ultimate shareholder or intermediate holding company to the Cyprus entity's primary operational account at a tier-1 Cypriot bank or a qualifying EEA EMI with full SEPA access. The wire should be denominated in EUR and transmitted through SWIFT MT103 via the EU/EEA correspondent network using the beneficiary IBAN and BIC of the Cyprus entity. The inward remittance must be accompanied by a board resolution authorising the capital contribution, a capital injection agreement, a shareholder resolution if equity is being subscribed, and a source-of-funds declaration supported by the remitter's bank statements. In the Cyprus BOP reporting framework, the payment should be classified as direct investment equity capital or intercompany debt depending on the legal form. If the funds are subscribed shares, the statutory declaration and share allotment forms must be filed with the Cyprus Registrar of Companies. No FX conversion approval is required because the transaction is in EUR, but the bank will treat any large or unusual inbound credit as a trigger for enhanced due diligence and may request tax identification numbers, financial statements, and a rationale letter.Outbound treasury disbursements should be executed primarily through SEPA Credit Transfers for payments within the EU/EEA in EUR, and through SWIFT MT103 messages for non-EUR or cross-border payments outside the EEA. For merchandise or high-value trade-related settlements, Cyprus banks may issue and advise commercial letters of credit, standby letters of credit, and documentary collections under UCP 600, with ICC trade finance forms. For recurring distributed payments to voice-streaming content creators, contractors, and licensing partners, the optimal clearing rail is an EEA SEPA bulk payment file or an API-driven payment gateway connected to the Cypriot account; for non-EU beneficiaries, use SWIFT MT103 with proper purpose codes. For Apple and Google app store remittances into the Cyprus entity, the settlement will arrive from Apple Distribution International Ltd (Ireland) or Google Payment Corp / Google Ireland entities; these should be reconciled against app store payout reports and designated with the appropriate custodial or royalties purpose code. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat corporate income tax (CIT) on the worldwide income of Cyprus tax-resident companies; non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income attributable to a permanent establishment (PE).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Cyprus imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, whether individuals or companies. No Special Defence Contribution (SDC) applies to non-resident recipients; the 17% SDC applies only to Cyprus tax-resident individuals and, in limited cases, to Cyprus-resident companies receiving dividends from abroad.

Within 12 months after the end of the tax year (e.g., by 31 December 2025 for the tax year ended 31 December 2024); provisional tax instalments under Form ITP1 are due separately on 31 July and 31 December of the tax year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

50% of the provisional tax payable by 31 July of the tax year; the remaining 50% by 31 December of the tax year (for a 31 December year-end); for non-calendar accounting periods, the dates are adjusted pro rata.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 - aggregate value of transactions with defined associated enterprises (cross-border and domestic) in a single tax year requires the preparation of a Local File and the electronic filing of the mandatory Cyprus Transfer Pricing Return (Form TPR-2022) together with Form TD4. A Master File and Country-by-Country (CbC) Report are triggered when the multinational group's consolidated turnover equals or exceeds EUR 750 million. Below the EUR 750,000 aggregate threshold, only a summary TP disclosure (if any such transaction exists) is required; however, the arm's-length principle remains mandatory for all related-party dealings.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate sector-specific Economic Substance Regulations for ISIC 6311 voice-streaming platforms, but general substance requirements apply through (i) the statutory 'management and control' test for Cyprus tax residence; (ii) the General Anti-Abuse Rule in Section 33 of the ITL which disregards arrangements lacking commercial substance; (iii) the ATAD CFC rules, which attribute passive income of controlled entities in zero-tax jurisdictions back to the Cyprus company; and (iv) the OECD Modified Nexus approach embedded in the Cyprus IP Box, which requires qualifying expenditure and active R&D decision-making in Cyprus. A voice-streaming app company must demonstrate substantive local employment (engineering, product, moderation, commercial staff), physical offices, board meetings and operating control in Cyprus to preserve the 12.5% rate and treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Estimated assessment on the IRD's own valuation; additional tax of 5% per month on the unpaid tax (capped at 60%); statutory interest; criminal conviction carrying a fine of up to EUR 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to five years; forfeiture of the proceeds of evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign, approve, or connive in the filing of a false return are subject to personal criminal prosecution, civil restitution, disqualification as directors, departure prohibition orders, and seizure of personal assets under the Tax Collection Law.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length recharacterization of the transactions, gross-up adjustments, additional tax at 12.5% plus 5% monthly additional tax (max 60%) and interest; fixed penalties of up to EUR 5,000 for failure to file the TP Return and up to EUR 20,000 for knowingly false or incomplete TP documentation; CFC income imputation under ATAD.

Director personal liability: Executives who negotiated or approved the non-arm's-length arrangements are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall if the arrangement is treated as a sham; personal fines and imprisonment apply for fraudulent transfer-pricing documentation.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: The IRD nullifies the arrangement under Section 33 of the ITL, reallocates income to the true economic owner, and imposes additional tax (5% per month, capped at 60%) plus interest; the Registrar of Companies may strike the entity off the registry; extended assessment periods of six years (or without time limit in fraud cases) apply; the company's tax residence certificate and treaty benefits are revoked.

Director personal liability: Nominee and de facto directors face personal civil liability for the reallocated taxes, administrative fines, travel bans, disqualification under the Companies Law (Cap. 113), and criminal prosecution where the company traded with intent to defraud the Revenue.
AML, Beneficial Ownership, and KYC Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 500,000 and/or imprisonment for up to five years for wilful violations of the Cyprus AML Law (L.188(I)/2007); confiscation of profits; the Regulator may impose compliance orders, suspend operations, and publish sanctions against the entity and its compliance officer.

Director personal liability: The appointed AML compliance officer and all directors are personally criminally liable for wilful non-disclosure or obstructing MOKAS; courts routinely impose travel bans, asset freezes, and disqualification from directorship in cases involving structured money laundering through in-app payment rails.

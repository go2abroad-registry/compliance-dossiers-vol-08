Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Tax Department (Τμήμα Φορολογίας/Inland Revenue), Ministry of Finance; official portal: https://www.taxdepartment.mof.gov.cy (e-filing and VAT submissions through the TAXISnet Cyprus electronic system).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (branch or company) must first register with the Registrar of Companies and then apply to the District Tax Office for the statutory Tax Identification Code (TIC). The application incorporates the company’s registered office proof, memorandum/articles of association, ownership declarations, and identification of beneficial owners. For VAT purposes, a separate VAT registration application (Form VAT-101) is submitted through the Tax Department’s e-portal; a taxable person whose annual taxable turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed €15,600 must register within 30 days of that fact, while non-resident entities with no Cyprus establishment but engaged in taxable transactions must register for VAT through a fiscal representative. The obtained TIC serves as the TRN (Tax Registration Number) for all national tax and VAT filings, and must be quoted in all returns, invoices, and customs declarations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity (branch or company) must first register with the Registrar of Companies and then apply to the District Tax Office for the statutory Tax Identification Code (TIC). The application incorporates the company’s registered office proof, memorandum/articles of association, ownership declarations, and identification of beneficial owners. For VAT purposes, a separate VAT registration application (Form VAT-101) is submitted through the Tax Department’s e-portal; a taxable person whose annual taxable turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed €15,600 must register within 30 days of that fact, while non-resident entities with no Cyprus establishment but engaged in taxable transactions must register for VAT through a fiscal representative. The obtained TIC serves as the TRN (Tax Registration Number) for all national tax and VAT filings, and must be quoted in all returns, invoices, and customs declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box election or elective hybrid-mismatch regime. Available statutory elections include: (i) group relief loss election for companies with at least 75% common shareholding; (ii) notional interest deduction election on new equity contributed after 2015; (iii) Intellectual Property Box election under section 9D of the Income Tax Law for qualifying intangible assets (2.5% effective tax rate on qualifying profits); (iv) election to apply the small-business VAT cash accounting or flat-rate scheme; (v) transfer pricing documentation choices under OECD-aligned Chapter V. The Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory and cannot be elected.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 annual taxable turnover of supplies of goods and services; separate thresholds apply to intra-EU acquisitions and distance selling (including €10,000 cross-border B2C digital services annual threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to: (1) intra-Community supplies of goods to a customer established in another EU Member State where the supplier obtains the customer’s valid VAT identification number, reports the supply in a recapitulative statement, and holds documentary evidence of transport/dispatch; (2) direct exports of goods outside the European Union evidenced by customs export documentation; (3) services supplied to persons established outside Cyprus where the place of supply is outside Cyprus under the B2B main rule, including royalty and financing services rendered to offshore recipients, cessio of intangibles, and revenue-based financing arrangements; and (4) certain international transport and ancillary services. Input VAT directly attributable to zero-rated transactions is fully deductible.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Single Administrative Document / Export Summary Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical removal from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT credit confirmation evidencing foreign exchange repatriation
  • Commercial invoice standing in the name of the exporting entity
  • Packing list and proof of export value
  • Intra-Community Sales List (VIES) with customer VAT number and proof of delivery for EU supplies

Cyprus has no real-time fiscal clearance requirement, no mandatory digital stamping, no continuous transaction controls, and no general B2B e-invoicing mandate. VAT returns, VIES recapitulative statements, and Intrastat declarations are filed electronically via TAXISnet/Facilis. Electronic invoicing is lawful and subject to authenticity, integrity, and readability requirements under VAT Law; the Tax Department may exercise post-audit access to electronic invoicing data, but no SAF-T filing obligation is currently in force.

Cyprus Customs and the Tax Department conduct targeted audits of export zero-rating, applying a documentary evidence standard demanding transport, payment, and customer registration proof. If export zero-rating is found to be unsupported or fraudulent, the VAT at 19% is re-assessed from the date of supply with statutory interest (2% per month or part thereof), together with administrative fines up to 100% of the unpaid tax and potential criminal sanctions for tax evasion, including imprisonment up to three years. False invoices backing sham exports trigger denial of input VAT credit, joint and several liability among participants, and publication of serious offenses. Export records must be retained for at least six years to survive clawback risk.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account; Cyprus maintains no exchange controls on EU/EEA transactions since EU accession (2004), and capital-account restrictions with third countries were fully dismantled following the orderly exit from the 2013 crisis-era measures. Capital movements between Cyprus and non-EU jurisdictions are unrestricted but subject to statistical notification and Central Bank of Cyprus monitoring under the Capital Movements Law L.71(I)/2004 and applicable ECB balance-of-payments reporting regulations. Dividend and interest remittances by a Cyprus Ltd to non-resident shareholders are free of exchange restrictions and domestic withholding taxes, providing effective profit-repatriation parity with EU resident flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory conversion obligations apply in Cyprus; export/service proceeds may be retained in any currency and held offshore or locally. For balance-of-payments statistical compliance, the Central Bank of Cyprus requires the electronic reporting of each resident-to-non-resident transaction equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500, filed monthly through the CBC BOP e-reporting portal on or before the 15th business day of the following month; bank-account crediting of export receipts is conditional on the client's submission of the transaction purpose code at initiation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating under the Central Bank of Cyprus Law L.138(I)/2002 as the national competent authority within the Eurosystem; monetary policy and euro issuance authority reside with the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Cyprus-licensed e-money and payment institutions registered under the Payment Services Law L.240(I)/2018 (PSD2-implementing) offer segregated collection IBANs, SEPA Direct Debit origination, and automated reconciliation, and are viable as secondary collection-only rails for the high-frequency direct-debit target flow. However, they are not viable as the primary treasury counterparty for this structure because: (a) they cannot extend credit or settle the reverse transfer without a sponsor-bank settlement account at an EU credit institution; (b) onboarded accounts face hard EUR transaction ceilings or monthly velocity caps; and (c) sponsor-bank ACH/SEPA return risk is born by the originator. Offshore EMIs (UK, Lithuania, Ireland) may passport into Cyprus under EU/EEA rights but are subject to additional correspondent-bank due diligence and are accepted only for decoupled collection wallets, never for escrow of investor capital.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Investor capital arrives via SWIFT MT103 to the segregated EUR escrow account at the Cypriot credit institution. Country paryment instructions: beneficial owner BIC, IBAN, and Central Bank of Cyprus balance-of-payments purpose codes per injection type — 5501/5510 for equity contribution, 5120 for subordinated shareholder loan; each transfer must be countersigned by a board capital-call resolution, the signed facility or subscription agreement, and the investor UBO file, with settlement occurring through TARGET2 on a T+0 basis. Any third-country remittance exceeding EUR 12,500 is automatically captured in the CBC BOP monthly return for which the bank relies on the client's purpose-code declaration at remittance initiation.

UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: The principal AML statute is the German Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz, GwG), especially sections 3 and 18–23 on beneficial ownership and the Transparency Register. GmbHs must identify and report their beneficial owners to the Transparency Register under GwG section 20. Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A GmbH must have a domestic registered office and a domestic business address capable of service and entry in the Commercial Register (GmbHG sections 4a and 8; HGB section 10). It does not replace premises required for actual manufacturing, feed-hygiene controls, storage, traceability, or inspections. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authority for corporate tax registration is the locally responsible Finanzamt (tax office) of the GmbH's registered office or management location. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.). Tax registrations and filings are generally administered electronically through the ELSTER portal (Elektronische Steuererklärung).

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH must be registered with the competent Finanzamt following incorporation, commercial-register registration, and commencement or planned commencement of business. The company submits the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER, disclosing legal form, registered office, management, shareholders, directors, business activities, expected turnover, expected taxable profit, bank details, and VAT treatment. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), generally used for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT filings. The company or its authorized tax adviser may request a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) from the BZSt, normally through the Finanzamt registration process or the BZSt electronic application. A nonresident foreign entity carrying on taxable transactions in Germany must register with the appropriate Finanzamt and obtain a German tax number and, where required, a USt-IdNr.; it is not required to incorporate a German GmbH solely to obtain a German VAT registration. The company must maintain tax records, submit periodic VAT returns where applicable, and file annual corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is a separately taxable corporation and is generally subject to German corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax. Germany does not provide a general U.S.-style check-the-box election for a GmbH. A GmbH may not generally elect transparent partnership treatment merely by filing a tax election. Certain German corporate-law reorganizations may qualify for tax-neutral treatment under the Reorganization Tax Act if statutory conditions are met. Cross-border hybrid-entity treatment is restricted by the German Anti-Tax-Avoidance rules, including hybrid-mismatch provisions implementing the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive. A German GmbH may form or participate in a tax group (Organschaft) where statutory financial, organizational, and profit-transfer requirements are satisfied; this is a structural regime rather than a check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold below which a business conducting taxable supplies in Germany is exempt from VAT registration. The domestic small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG may apply where total turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year, subject to statutory conditions and the applicable effective-date rules. A business using the small-business exemption generally does not charge German VAT and cannot deduct input VAT. The reduced VAT rate is 7% for specified goods and services; ordinary manufactured pet treats and pet food generally require classification-specific review and are not automatically eligible for the reduced rate. Intra-EU acquisitions, imports, reverse-charge transactions, distance sales, and platform transactions may create separate registration or reporting obligations even where domestic turnover is limited.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify as VAT-exempt with input-tax deduction under the German export exemption rules, provided the goods are transported or dispatched to a non-EU destination and the supplier can substantiate both the export and the customer or transaction conditions. The supplier should retain customs export records, the export accompanying or exit confirmation, commercial documentation, transport evidence, and evidence linking the shipment to the invoice. Supplies to VAT-registered customers in another EU Member State are generally intra-Community supplies rather than third-country exports and require valid customer VAT identification, transport evidence, recapitulative statement reporting, and other statutory conditions. Services are not generally described as zero-rated exports; their treatment depends on place-of-supply rules, customer status, use and enjoyment rules, and any applicable exemption. Business-to-business services supplied to a non-German business may fall outside German VAT under the general place-of-supply rule, often with reverse-charge treatment in the customer's jurisdiction.

  • Electronic exit confirmation or equivalent customs evidence demonstrating that the goods left the European Union
  • Commercial invoice identifying the German supplier, customer, goods, value, VAT treatment, and shipment details
  • Packing list and transport documentation, such as bill of lading, air waybill, rail consignment note, or road consignment note
  • Proof of delivery or destination evidence and documentation linking the shipment to the relevant invoice
  • Export contract, purchase order, and customer or recipient details

Germany has no general real-time invoice-clearance or mandatory tax-authority digital-stamping system for ordinary commercial invoices. Since 1 January 2025, German businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions, generally in an EN 16931-compliant format such as XRechnung or an eligible ZUGFeRD invoice. Mandatory issuance is phased: non-structured invoices remain permitted during the statutory transition period through 31 December 2026; from 2027, additional turnover-based transition rules apply, including a EUR 800,000 prior-year turnover threshold for certain issuers; from 1 January 2028, structured e-invoicing is generally required for domestic B2B transactions within scope. PDF invoices alone are not structured e-invoices. The rules generally do not apply in the same manner to B2C supplies, exempt transactions, or certain cross-border transactions. Businesses must preserve invoices and electronic records in accordance with German retention and audit-access rules, including the principles for the proper management and storage of electronic records (GoBD).

Germany has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days, and no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline applies to ordinary German export sales. Export VAT exemption is denied or clawed back where the supplier cannot prove export, cannot link the evidence to the transaction, applies an exemption incorrectly, or participates in fraud. The Finanzamt may assess unpaid VAT, impose interest under the German Fiscal Code, require correction of VAT returns, and deny input-tax recovery where statutory conditions are not met. Intentional or reckless tax evasion or fraudulent VAT conduct may trigger criminal liability under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code, including fines and imprisonment; negligent tax reduction may trigger administrative penalties under applicable provisions. Incorrect VAT treatment may also result in liability for tax shown incorrectly on an invoice, correction obligations, late-filing penalties, and additional audit assessments. Customs violations may generate separate customs debt, administrative fines, or criminal sanctions under German and EU customs law. Documentary records should be retained for the statutory tax retention period, generally ten years for accounting records and eight years for invoices under current German rules, subject to applicable transitional provisions and longer periods where litigation or audit preservation is required.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions by virtue of EU primary law (Article 63 TFEU) and the German Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (Foreign Trade and Payments Act). There is no residual exchange-control permit regime for euro conversions, cross-border dividends, royalty payments, or repayment of shareholder loans. Profit remittance and capital repatriation are lawful without quantitative limits, subject to applicable corporate income tax withholding (dividends to non-residents may attract 25% withholding plus solidarity surcharge or treaty relief) and due diligence documentation. Statutory reporting obligations to the Bundesbank arise for individual cross-border payments exceeding €12,500, but these are statistical/monetary survey requirements, not restrictions or licences.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds exists under German law. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts, converted freely, or offset against imports. However, the resident exporter must report every cross-border payment — both inbound and outbound — to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five business days of receipt/payment when the amount of the individual transaction exceeds €12,500. The report is submitted electronically via the Bundesbank’s BKöP online portal (Zahlungsverkehrsstatistik) using the prescribed payment code, and the legal basis is sections 59-70 of the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV). Non-compliance is an administrative offence subject to fines.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank — the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany and the competent authority for external-sector and monetary statistics; Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) — the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority for prudential banking and AML supervision; Federal Ministry of Economics and Climate Action for export-control and foreign-trade oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational sweep-accounts, collection of platform proceeds and low-value SEPA payments, but Low for capital escrow, treasury liquidity concentration or statutory contribution accounts. An offshore or third-country EMI cannot hold transactional accounts for a German GmbH without a European Economic Area payment-service authorisation and the complete passporting notification under BaFin. EMIs holding a Dutch, Luxembourg, Irish, or German license may legally operate in Germany through passporting, subject to compliance with the German GwG and BaFin anti-money-laundering circulars. When selecting an EMI, the GmbH must insist on segregated client funds at a Tier-1 German bank, documented insolvency protection, and all transaction reporting APIs. Any use of an unlicensed/offshore EMI triggers automatic red flags under the German KYC process, creates exposure to treasury loss, and may invalidate the statutory audit trail for export proceeds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant rail for inbound capital into a German GmbH is a SWIFT MT103/credit transfer into the company’s designated euro or multi-currency account opened with a Tier-1 German bank. The originator must specify the payment’s purpose code in SWIFT field 70 ('Remittance Information') — for a cash contribution, use 'STAMMEINLAGE / CONTRIBUTION TO SHARE CAPITAL / DE-GmbH ISIC 1080'; for a shareholder loan, use 'DARLEHEN / SHAREHOLDER LOAN / INTERIM FINANCING' to avoid misclassification. When the originator is inside the SEPA zone, the SEPA Credit Transfer field 'Remittance Information' must replicate this purpose text. For values exceeding €12,500, the GmbH must report the receipt to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five business days using the payment-statistics portal; the statistical purpose code for capital contribution is typically '8' (Kapitalverkehr) with sub-code for direct investment. Banks will additionally require an audit trail linking the wire to the registered capital (notarised minutes, deposit receipt, and commercial register filing). Funds received without the correct purpose code can be treated as pre-financing debt and may trigger tax and AML investigations.For trade settlements the entity should use the following clearing rails: (i) Europe: SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT/INST) for euro-denominated invoice payments; (ii) global fast payments: SWIFT MT103 for USD, GBP, CHF, and non-euro currencies via the bank’s correspondent network; (iii) commercial credits: SWIFT MT700/MT710 for issuing, amending, and advising documentary letters of credit, especially for large open-account or term-trade purchases of raw animal protein; (iv) for CNY-denominated invoicing: CIPS/CNAPS with SWIFT MT103 interoperability; (v) for all food/agricultural trade: settlement against presentation of a full documentary set including Bill of Lading, phytosanitary/vet certificate, customs invoice, and insurance certificate. The treasury function must enforce matching on three-way documents before releasing funds; all outbound payments >€12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank within 5 business days. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for operational sweep-accounts, collection of platform proceeds and low-value SEPA payments, but Low for capital escrow, treasury liquidity concentration or statutory contribution accounts. An offshore or third-country EMI cannot hold transactional accounts for a German GmbH without a European Economic Area payment-service authorisation and the complete passporting notification under BaFin. EMIs holding a Dutch, Luxembourg, Irish, or German license may legally operate in Germany through passporting, subject to compliance with the German GwG and BaFin anti-money-laundering circulars. When selecting an EMI, the GmbH must insist on segregated client funds at a Tier-1 German bank, documented insolvency protection, and all transaction reporting APIs. Any use of an unlicensed/offshore EMI triggers automatic red flags under the German KYC process, creates exposure to treasury loss, and may invalidate the statutory audit trail for export proceeds.

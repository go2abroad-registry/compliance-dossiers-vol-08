Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance; online filing via the Cyprus Tax Department electronic portal (includes corporate income tax returns, PAYE/NIC, VAT returns, and other taxpayer compliance submissions).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or newly incorporated Cyprus private limited company must obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) through registration with the Cyprus Tax Department. For companies, the TIN is ordinarily linked to the incorporation registration in the Cyprus Companies Registry; a non-resident entity with Cyprus-source taxable activity or EU VAT obligations must file the relevant IR/VAT registration application, supported by apostilled or notarised incorporation documents, certified passports of directors/beneficial owners, proof of the foreign entity's home-country tax identification, a registered Cyprus or Cyprus-domiciled service address, and a signed declaration of commencement of taxable activity. VAT registration is mandatory once the taxable turnover threshold is exceeded or, for non-established digital service suppliers, before the first taxable supply occurs.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity or newly incorporated Cyprus private limited company must obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) through registration with the Cyprus Tax Department. For companies, the TIN is ordinarily linked to the incorporation registration in the Cyprus Companies Registry; a non-resident entity with Cyprus-source taxable activity or EU VAT obligations must file the relevant IR/VAT registration application, supported by apostilled or notarised incorporation documents, certified passports of directors/beneficial owners, proof of the foreign entity's home-country tax identification, a registered Cyprus or Cyprus-domiciled service address, and a signed declaration of commencement of taxable activity. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A Cyprus Ltd is always treated as an entity-level corporate taxpayer under the Cyprus Income Tax Law. Available statutory elections include: tax group loss-relief election between Cyprus-resident group companies; Notional Interest Deduction election on new equity capital; IP Box partial deduction election for qualifying intangible asset income; tonnage-tax election for qualifying shipping activities; and VAT grouping election for VAT purposes. No hybrid mismatch election is available; EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules apply and a taxpayer cannot elect to override statutory mismatch treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 in annual taxable turnover for Cyprus-established businesses. Non-established foreign suppliers supplying digital services or taxable services to non-business customers in Cyprus, or doing any taxable supply in Cyprus, are subject to VAT registration from the first euro with no registration threshold. The EU intra-Community distance-sales cross-border B2C threshold also applies at EUR 10,000 where relevant.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cyprus zero-rates supplies of goods exported outside the European Union where the goods are physically dispatched or transported to a third country and the supplier retains evidence of export within the statutory period. Services supplied to a business customer established outside the EU/Cyprus, including B2B enterprise SaaS subscriptions, are generally placed outside the scope of Cyprus VAT by reference to the place-of-supply rules; the supplier must substantiate the customer's business status and foreign establishment with valid VAT numbers, certificates, and commercial contracts. Zero-rating or outside-scope treatment is disallowed if the supplier fails to hold contemporaneous and credible documentary evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Cyprus currently imposes no national real-time electronic invoice clearance mandate and no mandatory API fiscal stamping. Invoices, including electronic invoices, must meet EU VAT invoicing rules ensuring authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility; electronic invoicing may be used freely where accepted by the customer. Cyclical EU VAT Digital Age e-invoicing/reporting measures will apply once transposed; at present there is no Cyprus-specific clearance gateway or digital stamping requirement.

The Cyprus Tax Department may retrospectively deny zero-rating and assess 19% VAT, plus interest, if the required export/evidence documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or not maintained for the statutory retention period. Penalties apply on a tiered basis: up to 10% of the additional tax for careless inaccuracies, up to 20% for deliberate inaccuracies, and higher assessments for fraud-linked cases; criminal prosecution is possible for fraudulent export VAT claims, with fines and/or imprisonment. Clawback periods generally operate within the VAT assessment limitation window, but fraud removes such limitation and exposes the taxpayer to retrospective reassessment and immigration/AML adverse consequences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus, as a Eurozone member state, applies full capital-account and current-account convertibility under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No exchange controls, convertibility licensing, surrender obligations, or prior approval requirements apply to cross-border payments, profit repatriation, or dividend distributions. Dividend and interest remittances to non-resident shareholders are not subject to Cyprus withholding tax, and no Central Bank authorization is required for outward remittances of dividends, capital, or sales proceeds. Remaining constraints are limited to EU sanctions enforcement (e.g., Ukraine/Russia-related restrictive measures), EU Regulation 2015/847 on transfer of funds information (travel rule), enhanced due diligence on politically exposed persons and high-risk jurisdictions, and balance-of-payments statistical reporting thresholds imposed by the Central Bank of Cyprus for monitoring purposes. GBP remains fully acceptable as a foreign-currency clearing currency, but EUR is the statutory currency for balance-of-payments reporting and corporate accounting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, no timeline for repatriation, and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters of software and digital services (ISIC 6201) may retain 100% of export receipts in EUR or in any freely usable foreign currency on deposit accounts held at Cyprus-licensed credit institutions. Such receipts are subject only to (i) standard anti-money-laundering reporting for cash or abnormal transactions, (ii) ECB/Central Bank of Cyprus balance-of-payments statistical reporting for transactions crossing established thresholds, and (iii) travel-rule data requirements for wire transfers under Regulation (EU) 2015/847.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Greek: Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου / Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating within the Eurosystem and the Single Supervisory Mechanism of the European Central Bank (ECB); the Central Bank of Cyprus is the national competent authority for credit institutions, payment systems, and foreign-exchange-related statistical reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating for the target SaaS treasury setup: MEDIUM-HIGH as ancillary rails, MEDIUM as primary banking. Stripe is viable for merchant acquiring and B2B subscription collection via EU acquiring licenses; Wise Business is viable as a licensed EMI/payment institution with EU passporting rights, suitable for currency conversion and international vendor payments. However, under PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) and the Cyprus Electronic Money Law (Law 122(I)/2012), an offshore fintech or EMI without an EEA passport cannot lawfully hold client funds or provide payment accounts to a Cyprus corporate entity. Funds received through non-passported offshore digital EMIs will be flagged by domestic Cyprus banks as high-risk and may trigger frozen remittances, reinstated source-of-funds inquiries, or account closure. Therefore, the primary treasury account must be a fully licensed EU/Cyprus credit institution, with Stripe and Wise integrated only as payment-collection and FX-conversion sub-rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Cyprus private limited company, the compliant route for shareholder capital injection is: (1) execute a board resolution and updated share-subscription agreement; (2) transfer EUR from the shareholder's own bank account (name must exactly match the registered member) via SEPA Credit Transfer (EEA origin) or SWIFT MT103 (non-EEA origin) to the company's IBAN (28-character format: CY + 2 check digits + 3-digit bank code + 5-digit branch code + 16-digit account number), with Field 70 remittance information stating 'CAPITAL INJECTION / EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION - [Company Name]' and the applicable Cyprus balance-of-payments statistical purpose code for direct investment in equity, to be confirmed with the relationship manager and commonly classified as 'Direct Investment Equity' in the ECB BOP framework; (3) submit the source-of-funds dossier to the receiving bank before booking; (4) update the Central Bank of Cyprus and Registrar of Companies capitalization records and share-capital filings within statutory timelines if nominal capital is increased. 0% - Cyprus does not impose any withholding tax on dividends paid by a CY Ltd to non-resident shareholders. For Cyprus-tax-resident individual shareholders, Special Defence Contribution (SDC) of 17% may be due on dividend distributions, but this does not apply to non-residents.

Form 600 must be submitted no later than six months after the end of the financial year. For a calendar-year company: 30 June of the following year. Final balancing tax payment falls due on 1 August immediately following the tax year end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate related-party transactions (gross, per tax year) triggers a mandatory TP documentation package in Cyprus (Local File). Master File documentation is required where the CY entity belongs to a MNE group whose consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million or that is the ultimate CbC reporting parent in Cyprus. Simplified documentation may apply to low-risk smaller transactions below this threshold.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not maintain a codified 'Economic Substance Regulations' regime in the style of UAE/DIFC, but the Cyprus Tax Department applies OECD arm's length principles, the EU ATAD general anti-abuse rule, central management-and-control case law, and beneficial ownership requirements under tax treaties. For a B2B Enterprise SaaS company, genuine substance must be demonstrable locally: qualified software engineering staff, strategic board decisions, server and IP ownership in Cyprus, invoicing accounts, and absence of sham offshore arrangements. A holding or licensing shell with no physical/functional presence in Cyprus is vulnerable to denial of treaty benefits and increased tax audit risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate False Return

Statutory sanction: Additional tax of 100% on evaded tax up to the full under-collection; heavy financial penalties; criminal prosecution in the Cyprus Courts with fines and imprisonment. The Tax Commissioner may impose double-assessment and default interest at 2% over the base rate until fully paid.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or order the evasion are personally exposed to civil restitution, criminal liability, seizure of personal assets, enforcement against foreign assets via EU instruments, and courts may impose travel bans pending trial. Conviction can result in imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing / Inter-Company Shell Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Cyprus Tax Department may re-compute the taxable profit at arm's length, adjust the B2B SaaS revenue base, impose tax on adjusted profits plus interest, and levy administrative penalties for non-submission of TP documentation. Unsuccessful judicial defence may escalate the liability by 50% of the adjusted tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately participate in creating sham documentation can be found personally liable under civil laws for conspiring to defraud the Cyprus Revenue and foreign tax authorities; they risk disqualification orders, travel bans, and joint and several liability for unpaid taxes.
AML / Undisclosed UBO / Shell Company Flag

Statutory sanction: Cyprus AML law (208(I)/2018) authorises administrative fines for legal persons (up to EUR 1 million or a percentage of turnover), freezing of assets, removal of CySEC/registered agent approval, and deregistration by the Registrar of Companies; criminal proceedings can result in imprisonment and confiscation orders.

Director personal liability: Officer liability attaches to individuals who knowingly allow the corporate platform to be used for money laundering or concealment of UBO information. Courts may impose travel bans, freeze personal bank accounts, order civil compensation to victims, and disqualify the director from running any company for up to a decade.
Non-Filing / Persistent Default / Wilful Non-Payment

Statutory sanction: The Tax Commissioner may issue an ex-officio rigorous assessment, trigger penalty surcharges of 5% per month (capped), refer to the District Court for enforcement of taxes as a civil judgment, and initiate applications for strike-off from the Registrar of Companies. Directors can be made jointly liable for debts arising from fraudulent trading.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly trade while company tax debts remain outstanding may be liable for fraudulent or wrongful trading under Cyprus company law. They may be ordered to personally compensate creditors, face deportation or travel restrictions, and receive custodial sentences where gross fraud is proven.

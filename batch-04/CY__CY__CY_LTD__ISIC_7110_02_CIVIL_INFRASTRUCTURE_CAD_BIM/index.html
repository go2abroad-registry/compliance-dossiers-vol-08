Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance; e-Filing portal: TAXISnet (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy); VAT Online Services and Cyprus Customs THIBCA declarations portal (https://customs.mof.gov.cy); Ariadne government e-Services portal (https://ariadne.mof.gov.cy).

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus-incorporated private limited company (HE77) is automatically issued a Tax Identification Code (TIC) upon incorporation by the Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver; the TIC is identical to the company's registration number and appears on the incorporation certificate. For VAT, the company files Form VAT 101 with the local VAT District Officer within 30 days of the month in which the €15,600 taxable turnover threshold is exceeded; non-resident applicants without a Cyprus establishment must register using Form VAT 103 and, if established outside the EU, must appoint a tax representative in Cyprus; the VAT registration certificate is issued with a Cyprus VAT number in the format CY12345678A. No separate federal or regional registration is required because Cyprus is a unitary jurisdiction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: CY LTD is a Cyprus tax resident if managed and controlled in Cyprus (place-of-effective-management test). It is taxed at 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income, with territorial reliefs including the Participation Exemption (dividends and capital gains on qualifying shareholdings), the Foreign Permanent Establishment exemption, and the Cyprus IP Nexus box. The entity is treated as a limited liability company, not a pass-through, and is subject to the general anti-avoidance and ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules.

Available election forms

Elective mechanisms under the Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002 and VAT Law N.95(I)/2000 include: (i) election to exempt foreign permanent establishment profits (forgoing foreign tax credits); (ii) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new qualifying equity; (iii) group relief surrender of losses election; (iv) IP Nexus box computation election; (v) reorganization and demerger relief elections; (vi) deemed dividend distribution election and closed-company apportionment elections; (vii) VAT flat-rate small-enterprise scheme election; (viii) VAT option for intra-Community acquisitions and margin-scheme elections. Cyprus has no US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification is determined solely by Cypriot corporate and tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 annual taxable turnover of goods and services; €10,251.61 threshold for intra-Community acquisitions; €35,000 for distance selling of goods to Cyprus consumers; no registration threshold applies to non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Cyprus where the reverse charge is unavailable; the application to register must be submitted within 30 days of exceeding the applicable threshold, with the effective registration date backdated to the day of exceedance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (1) exports of goods supplied by the exporter for removal from the EU within 90 days of the time of supply, including export under outward processing relief; (2) supplies of engineering, architectural, CAD/BIM drafting, and related professional services to a taxable person established in a third country or to a non-taxable person in a third country, where the place of supply rules place the supply outside Cyprus or specifically zero-rate it under Section 56 of the VAT Law; (3) supplies of services directly related to exported goods when the value is included in the customs value; (4) intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered EU customer, with evidence of the customer's valid VAT number and transport records. For CAD/BIM services supplied B2B to a foreign engineering contractor, the place of supply is normally the customer's country and the supply falls outside the scope of Cyprus VAT; where the customer is outside the EU, the supply is zero-rated or outside the scope, allowing recovery of input VAT under the 13th Directive procedure for non-established businesses.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD) lodged via Cyprus Customs THIBCA with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial invoice (triplicate) for CAD/BIM drafting services and/or goods, specifying terms of delivery and currency
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note proving physical export and third-country destination
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice showing export payment received on a Cyprus bank account
  • Signed international sales or engineering services agreement with project scope, fee schedule, and Incoterms
  • Customer acceptance certificate or proof of delivery at the foreign project site, plus copy of customer VAT registration or third-country tax identification

No real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) fiscal clearance mandate is currently in force in Cyprus; there is no national digital stamp or immediate API clearance requirement for B2B invoices. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies to suppliers invoicing Cyprus public authorities through the national e-Invoice platform, using the European EN 16931 and Peppol BIS formats. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary but must satisfy authenticity, integrity, and legibility requirements within EDI or PDF/XML custody; Cyprus is aligned with the EU VAT-in-the-Digital-Age (ViDA) proposal, anticipating standardised e-invoicing and digital transaction reporting for intra-EU supplies from 2030.

Wrongful zero-rating is treated as non-remitted VAT and is clawed back through an assessment at 19% with default interest and a 10% late-payment surcharge under Section 107 of the VAT Law; the assessment window is 6 years for ordinary errors and 10 years for fraudulent or willful misconduct. Knowingly issuing a false export or zero-rating invoice is a criminal offence carrying fines and imprisonment under the VAT Law and Criminal Code, with joint liability for officers; input VAT recovered on zero-rated exports is recaptured if the 90-day export condition is not met or the documentary evidence is deficient. Cyprus imposes no exchange-control repatriation deadline for export proceeds; the 180-day convention reflects prudent cash management and transfer-pricing expectations, and unrepatriated trade receivables may be recharacterised as loans or deemed distributions under the Income Tax Law, triggering 12.5% corporate taxation and Defence Fund liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is part of the euro area; the euro is fully convertible into any other currency on both capital and current accounts. EU free movement of capital applies, including for non-resident shareholders. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted in law, subject to delivery of statutory financial statements, tax compliance certificates, and bank AML/KYC documentation. Cyprus domestic withholding tax on dividends is 0% for non-residents, but dividend payments are reportable under DAC6, FATCA/CRS, and as exchange-control-related balance-of-payments transactions to the Central Bank of Cyprus.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no mandatory surrender requirements, statutory conversion ratios, or timelines requiring Cypriot exporters to repatriate or convert foreign currency receipts into euro. Export proceeds may be retained in freely convertible foreign-currency accounts at authorised Cypriot banks. However, all inward and outward payments exceeding EUR 10,000 must be reported for balance-of-payments statistical purposes, and invoices must be retained for AML and tax audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou); responsible for prudential supervision, payment systems oversight, foreign exchange statistics compilation, and balance-of-payments reporting under EU and national law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium to Low for primary operating treasury. Cyprus recognises passported EU Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions regulated by the Central Bank of Cyprus and the European Banking Authority. Digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Paddle can be used for settlement of low-risk services and payment routing, but they do not substitute for a domestic credit institution for escrow, capital injection, high-value SWIFT traffic, or statutory audit trail. The primary entity account should be at a traditional tier-1 bank; a fintech EMI may be used as a supplementary disbursement rail, subject to bank pass-through KYC and contractual restrictions by the domestic banker.

The bank mandates submission of the board resolution, share certificate, written capital contribution agreement, and confirmation that the funds originate from an account held under the shareholder's name. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat rate on worldwide income for Cyprus tax-resident companies

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Cyprus does not impose any domestic withholding tax on dividends paid by a Cyprus resident company to non-resident shareholders; dividends are paid gross. Cyprus-resident corporate shareholders may instead be subject to Special Defence Contribution (SDC) at 17% on non-exempt dividend income.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

IR1 must be submitted within 12 months after the end of the tax year: for a 31 December year-end, the deadline is 31 December of the following year. Provisional (self-assessment) tax instalments fall due on 31 July, 30 September, and 31 December of the tax year, with the fourth instalment on 31 March of the following year; the balancing payment is due together with the filed return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000: aggregate value of related-party transactions per tax year triggers mandatory contemporaneous Transfer Pricing documentation (Local File / Master File aligned to OECD TPG) in Cyprus, with a reduced EUR 250,000 threshold for intra-group financing transactions. A further EUR 750,000 aggregate threshold triggers the mandatory annual TP-SIT filing with Form IR1. Documentation must be submitted to the Tax Commissioner within 30 days of a request.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not enact a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute comparable to offshore centres, but substance is legally required through: (i) the management-and-control test for tax residence (board meetings, strategic decision-making and bank mandates exercised in Cyprus); (ii) the IP Box nexus rule requiring qualifying R&D staff and expenditure in Cyprus; (iii) the EU ATAD-derived General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) empowering the Commissioner to disregard artificial arrangements; and (iv) Cyprus's adherence to the OECD/G20 MLI, which restricts treaty benefits where a principal purpose is treaty shopping. A CAD/BIM company must maintain a physical office, employ technical staff, keep local board minutes and contracts, and demonstrate actual decision-making in Cyprus; a letterbox entity without these elements risks loss of tax residence, re-characterisation as a foreign PE of its shareholder, denial of treaty benefits, or strike-off.

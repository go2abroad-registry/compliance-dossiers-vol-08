Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a private limited company; share capital may be denominated in any currency with a nominal value of €0.01. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Tax Department (TDC) under the Ministry of Finance – primary portal 'Tax For All' (https://taxforall.mof.gov.cy); VAT registration and filing handled via the TDC VAT Service and the ARIADNE VAT reporting system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must apply to the Cyprus Tax Department for a Tax Identification Code (TIC) and, if engaged in taxable supplies in Cyprus, submit VAT Form TAX 010 within 30 days of becoming liable. The application requires: certificate of incorporation, proof of registered office address, identity documents of officers/shareholders, and a description of intended activities. A Cyprus-registered branch of a foreign company obtains a TIC via Form TAX 101. VAT registration is mandatory from the date liability exceeds the €15,600 threshold, and is available voluntarily for intra-Community acquirers and exporters.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must apply to the Cyprus Tax Department for a Tax Identification Code (TIC) and, if engaged in taxable supplies in Cyprus, submit VAT Form TAX 010 within 30 days of becoming liable. The application requires: certificate of incorporation, proof of registered office address, identity documents of officers/shareholders, and a description of intended activities. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None applicable in the form of a US-style check-the-box election. Cyprus applies mandatory entity-level taxation under the Income Tax Law 118(I) of 2002. Available statutory elections include: group loss relief under section 15, notional interest deduction on new equity, intellectual property box regime, and tax treatment for foreign permanent establishments; no hybrid mismatch election is available; Cyprus aligns with ATAD implementation, including interest limitation rules and controlled foreign corporation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 annual turnover of taxable supplies; €1,500 for intra-Community acquisitions in certain cases; distance sales threshold for non-established sellers is €35,000 per calendar year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from Cyprus to non-EU destinations, provided the exporter retains evidence that the goods left the EU within a statutory timeframe. For factoring and trade-finance services, zero-rating applies only to qualifying export-related services or offshore services supplied to non-established customers; standard 19% VAT generally applies to factoring services in Cyprus unless exported under the place-of-supply rules for B2B services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX1) / AES electronic declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing settlement of the export transaction

Cyprus mandates e-invoicing for B2G transactions through the Cyprus e-supplier system (electronic invoicing portal for government suppliers). No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is required for B2B/B2C VAT invoices. All VAT-return summaries are filed electronically via the ARIADNE system, and invoices are subject to standard invoice content rules and audit retention requirements under the EU VAT Regulation.

Cyprus Tax Department may deny zero-rating and reassess VAT at 19% plus interest at the statutory rate and penalties of up to 100% of tax underpaid if export evidence is incomplete, fraudulent, or not retained for the required 6-year record-keeping period. Criminal prosecution can result in fines and imprisonment for VAT evasion under the Cyprus VAT Law and the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Cyprus. Cyprus applies the EU Treaty free-movement-of-capital regime (TFEU Article 63); no distinction is made between current and capital account transactions for convertibility purposes. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayment, and liquidation proceeds can exit Cyprus freely without exchange-control approval, subject only to standard corporate formalities, AML verification, and any applicable withholding tax documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Cyprus maintains no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to surrender foreign currency earned by resident exporters or factoring entities. Foreign-currency accounts may be held and operated freely, subject to routine Central Bank of Cyprus balance-of-payments statistical reporting by the servicing bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Greek: Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) as national competent authority; European Central Bank for monetary policy, TARGET2 settlement oversight, and euro-area supervisory coordination

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Cyprus-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) operating under the Electronic Money Law (L.75(I)/2018) can offer segregated e-money settlement accounts; however, international correspondent banks frequently restrict EMI-hosted IBANs for cross-border receivables factoring and reverse-factoring flows. Third-country offshore EMIs are legally constrained by EU passporting rules and Central Bank of Cyprus supervision; they may be used only as a secondary settlement overlay, never as the primary treasury or escrow rail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%: Cyprus imposes no statutory withholding tax on dividends distributed by a Cyprus-resident company to non-resident shareholders. The 17% Special Defence Contribution applies only to dividends paid to Cyprus tax-resident individuals; it does not apply to non-resident shareholders or Cyprus-resident companies holding a qualifying participation. Consequently, a 100% non-resident-owned Cyprus factoring LTD can distribute after-tax profits to its foreign parent with zero source-state leakage.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The IR4 must be submitted within 12 months after the end of the relevant tax year. As the Cyprus tax year is the calendar year, a company with a fiscal year-end of 31 December 2024 must file no later than 31 December 2025. Tax due is payable by 1 August of the year following the tax year (i.e., payment is due on 1 August 2025 for the 2024 tax year), regardless of the later filing deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with associated enterprises exceeds €750,000 per tax year. Master File mandatory where the Cyprus entity belongs to a multinational enterprise group with consolidated group revenue exceeding €100,000,000. Country-by-Country Report mandatory where group consolidated revenue exceeds €750,000,000. A factoring entity with cross-border intercompany funding, portfolio transfers, or reverse-factoring arrangements above the €750,000 threshold must prepare contemporaneous documentation in the form of a Local File and, where applicable, a Master File, and file the CbC Report through the Cyprus Tax Department portal.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus is an EU member state and does not apply the OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations applicable to BVI/Cayman-style shell jurisdictions. However, Cyprus enforces an equivalent economic-substance doctrine through: (i) the General Anti-Abuse Rule in the Income Tax Law and the ATAD-implemented rules (CFC regimes, interest-limitation, and exit taxation); (ii) the management-and-control test for tax residence in Cyprus requiring board meetings, strategic decisions and senior management to occur in Cyprus; (iii) the NID regime's requirement that the company carries on actual business in Cyprus; and (iv) the IP Box nexus rule requiring qualifying R&D activity in Cyprus. For a factoring LTD, the Tax Department expects physical office premises, full-time employees, an operational factoring/ERP system hosted or accessed from Cyprus, local client onboarding and credit-risk approval, and board minutes documenting decisions taken in Cyprus. Absent substance, the Tax Department may challenge residence, refuse TRCs, deny NID, and invoke GAAR to reattribute profits and terminal anti-abuse assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Law (L.118(I)/2002) and the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law (L.4/1978), fraudulent evasion attracts a penalty equal to double the tax evaded, a criminal fine imposed by the court, and imprisonment of up to two years. The Tax Commissioner may reopen closed years, issue a 'best of judgment' assessment, disregard fabricated deductions in full, and add compound interest at 2.75% p.a. plus a 5% late-payment surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false IR4 returns or divert factoring proceeds face veil-piercing under Companies Law Cap. 113: they are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and debts in fraud cases; courts may impose departure prohibition orders (travel bans), disqualification orders of 2–5 years, and asset seizure orders against their personal estates.
Transfer Pricing Default / TP Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Tax Commissioner makes a primary transfer-pricing adjustment to align the Cyprus taxable profit with the arm's-length result, adds interest at 2.75% p.a. and applies the 5% late-payment surcharge; failure to produce contemporaneous TP documentation triggers an administrative fine under the Income Tax Law, and secondary adjustments may be deemed as constructive dividends to the foreign shareholder, with assessed tax and penalties up to double the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length intercompany pricing, sign false TP declarations, or caused the company to trade with inadequate capital are exposed to personal claims for negligent breach of fiduciary duty; in insolvency, the liquidator may pursue personal recovery of the group-advantaged amounts, and persistent TP-induced tax debt can lead to travel bans against the directors.
Shell Company / Sham Substance Abusive Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department applies GAAR and the management-and-control doctrine to re-attribute the company's residence or reallocate all profits to the actual place of business; the NID and IP Box deductions are reversed, the Tax Residence Certificate is revoked, and treaty benefits are denied under the Principal Purpose Test, yielding a full 12.5% assessment on the entire factoring profit plus interest and double-tax penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and the ultimate beneficial owner are personally liable under the fraudulent-trading provisions of Cap. 113 s. 47 for debts contracted with the knowledge that the sham structure had no capacity to pay; they face personal civil liability for the company's tax liabilities, director disqualification, administrative strike-off of the company, and departure prohibition orders preventing re-entry to Cyprus.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering Infraction

Statutory sanction: Under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (L.188(I)/2007 as amended), the corporate entity is subject to administrative fines imposed by the supervisory authority (up to €200,000 or higher by court order), license suspension or revocation, confiscation of criminally derived assets under the Confiscation of Proceeds of Crime Law, and the filing entity's directors to criminal prosecution punishable by imprisonment of up to five years and unlimited fines.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and the beneficial owner are personally criminally liable for predicate money-laundering offenses linked to the factoring business; courts routinely impose personal asset freezes, cross-border provisional measures, travel bans, and multi-year imprisonment; insurers and creditors pursue civil recovery directly against directors who knowingly facilitated layering through the factoring trade-payment rails.

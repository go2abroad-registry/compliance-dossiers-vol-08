UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: The principal statute is the German Act on the Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (Geldwäschegesetz, GwG), particularly sections 1, 3, 10, 11, 12, 15, 17, 19, 20, 21, 23, 23a and 24c. Beneficial ownership is determined primarily under section 3 GwG, generally by ownership of more than 25 percent of capital or voting rights, or by control in another manner; where no individual is identifiable, the legal representatives or managing partners are treated as fictitious beneficial owners. German legal entities must obtain, maintain, and report beneficial-owner information to the Transparency Register under sections 19 and 20 GwG. The register framework was materially strengthened by the Transparency Register and Financial Information Act (Transparenzregister- und Finanzinformationsgesetz, TraFinG), Federal Law Gazette I 2021, p. 2083. Companies and obliged entities must also comply with applicable sanctions legislation, the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz), the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung), and directly applicable EU financial-sanctions regulations.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A GmbH must have a domestic business address and a domestic registered office under sections 4a and 8 GmbHG. A serviced or virtual office may be acceptable if it is a genuine accessible business address, permits service of legal documents, and supports the company's actual administrative needs. A mere mailbox or forwarding-only address is inadequate. For interactive AI pet-robot assembly and distribution, a virtual registered office may be acceptable for the holding or administrative function, but manufacturing, inventory, product testing, customer-support, returns, safety compliance, and logistics should be conducted at properly documented premises or through disclosed third-party providers. Banks, tax authorities, customs authorities, and the trade office may request evidence of actual operations, contracts, personnel, equipment, and supply-chain substance.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

German Commercial Register portal (Gemeinsames Registerportal der Länder): https://www.handelsregister.de; the central company-information portal is the Unternehmensregister: https://www.unternehmensregister.de. Name pre-clearance is normally obtained through the competent local Chamber of Industry and Commerce (Industrie- und Handelskammer, IHK) and the competent local registration court (Registergericht), not through a nationwide statutory name-reservation portal.

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no generally applicable statutory nationwide name-reservation period and no automatic 30-day reservation mechanism. Obtain a written or documented preliminary name assessment from the competent IHK before notarization, then submit the formation deed and registration application through a German notary to the competent Registergericht. If the court rejects or objects to the name, request the precise legal grounds and cure the objection by amendment, additional explanatory evidence, or a replacement name. A formal appeal against a register-court decision is generally filed as a Beschwerde under sections 58 onward of the Act on Proceedings in Family Matters and in Matters of Voluntary Jurisdiction (FamFG), normally through the deciding court within the applicable appeal period stated in the decision, commonly one month. Use German counsel or the notary to verify the appeal deadline, standing, filing format, and competent appellate court. An IHK opinion is not itself an appealable court decision.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Germany: Finanzamt (local tax office at the GmbH registered seat) is competent for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT assessments; Bundeszentralamt fuer Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office, BZSt, www.bzst.de) is competent for VAT identification numbers, registers of non-established entrepreneurs and cross-border input VAT refunds; all statutory tax filings are submitted electronically through the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de); customs export clearances and EORI allocations are administered by the Generalzolldirektion (www.zoll.de).

E-government filing portal

For a domestic GmbH established by foreign shareholders: (1) notarial incorporation and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister); (2) the managing director submits the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent Finanzamt within one month, thereby obtaining the permanent tax number (Steuernummer); (3) separate application to BZSt for the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr., format DE + nine digits); (4) application to the Generalzolldirektion for an EORI number (DE + nine digits) before the first export consignment; (5) a non-resident enterprise without German establishment that makes taxable German supplies registers directly with BZSt on form USt 1 TN and receives a German VAT ID; the Chinese parent company may use the same route if it trades goods in Germany without a fixed establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation and Territorial_Scope: worldwide (unlimited) income taxation. A German GmbH is a mandatory separate taxpayer under the Koerperschaftsteuergesetz, paying 15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at typical effective rates of 7% to 17%, yielding total current profit tax burdens of roughly 29% to 33%. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation and Territorial_Scope: worldwide (unlimited) income taxation. A German GmbH is a mandatory separate taxpayer under the Koerperschaftsteuergesetz, paying 15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at typical effective rates of 7% to 17%, yielding total current profit tax burdens of roughly 29% to 33%. No pass-through election is available to a GmbH; Tax_Exempt_Zone does not apply in the federal territory.

Available election forms

German law has no US-style check-the-box election. Under § 1a KStG a partnership (e.g. GmbH & Co. KG) may elect entity-level corporate taxation at 30% (Optionsmodell), while a GmbH itself is irrevocably opaque and cannot elect tax transparency. A GmbH may, however, act as controlling entity of a fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §§ 14-17 KStG, subject to profit-and-loss pooling, enabling group-level corporate income tax and trade tax results; hybrid mismatch adjustments under § 4k EStG and the German ATAD 1/2 implementation further govern cross-border financing and dividend structures; German GAAP accounting (Handelsgesetzbuch) is mandatory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The statutory small-business exemption ceiling (§ 19 UStG) is EUR 25,000 annual turnover from 2025 (previously EUR 22,000); however, a commercial GmbH is a compulsive VAT entrepreneur from the first euro of entrepreneurial turnover and the exemption is therefore inapplicable. The EU-wide registration threshold for distance sales of goods and certain digital services is EUR 10,000 of cross-border turnover (§ 3c UStG, OSS procedure); imports and services received in Germany may oblige registration or reverse-charge compliance irrespective of turnover size.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies (Ausfuhrlieferungen) of robotic AI pet toys and bionic companion animals qualify for 0% VAT under § 6 UStG when (i) the goods are supplied to a third-country customer, (ii) the goods are transported or dispatched from Germany into a non-EU destination by the supplier or, under strictly defined conditions, by the customer acting in the supplier's name and for the supplier's account, and (iii) the exit is evidenced by a documentary proof (Belegnachweis) and an accounting proof (Buchnachweis) under §§ 8-13 UStDV. The export invoice, transport documents and customs records must conform to the statutory documentation periods; ancillary services directly connected with the exported goods may be zero-rated or free of German VAT, and B2B software/AI updates supplied to the foreign customer are placed at the customer's seat under § 3a UStG and therefore fall outside German VAT.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with Movement Reference Number, the print-out of the export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument) and the exit confirmation (Ausgangsvermerk) of the exporting customs office
  • Transport evidence: signed bill of lading, CMR waybill or air waybill naming the German exporter as consignor and the third-country consignee, together with route proof of physical EU exit
  • Commercial export invoice and underlying order or contract with the applicable Incoterms, destination country and commodity description; where preferential tariff treatment is claimed, an EUR.1 certificate or importer's statement for the third country
  • Payment evidence: inward remittance voucher and bank credit advice (Kontoauszug) showing receipt of the export sales proceeds from the third-country customer, with the corresponding foreign-exchange repatriation documentation
  • EORI number certificate of the GmbH and, in cases of indirect customs representation, the power of attorney (Zollvollmacht) evidencing the agent's authority

From 1 January 2025 every German entrepreneur must be able to receive B2B electronic invoices (E-Rechnung) in structured EN 16931 formats (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x); issuance of E-Rechnungen for domestic B2B supplies is mandatory from 1 January 2027 for enterprises with preceding-year turnover above EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all remaining entrepreneurs, including small businesses. Germany currently operates no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or third-party authorisation engine comparable to the Italian SDI or the Polish KSeF; VAT registration, VAT returns (monthly, quarterly or annual) and recapitulative statements are filed through ELSTER. The EU ViDA framework is expected to introduce transaction-based digital reporting and e-invoicing by 2030; until then, export transactions are documented through customs declarations only.

If the Beleg- und Buchnachweis is missing, defective or forged, the Finanzamt reverses the 0% rate retrospectively under the correction rules of §§ 172 ff. AO and assesses 19% VAT, charging monthly late-payment surcharges of 1% (§ 240 AO) and interest of 0.15% per month from the assessment due date (§ 233a AO). Knowingly false export documentation constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years, rising to up to ten years in especially serious cases, alongside fines and the confiscation of the evaded VAT; § 369 AO codifies the corresponding tax offences, and the Union Customs Code and § 261 StGB may be applied in carousel or VAT-fraud arrangements, leading additionally to personal statutory-liability notices (Haftungsbescheid, § 191 AO) for managing directors and, where applicable, the Chinese parent. Although German VAT law imposes no independent statutory FX repatriation deadline, a 180-day benchmark is applied by German export-compliance practice and corresponds to the six-month general invoice-issuance and documentation window of § 14 (3) UStG; unrepatriated foreign sales proceeds beyond that period will cause the Finanzamt and the Bundesbank to question the economic genuineness of the export and may trigger recharacterisation and clawback of the 0% rate with retrospective VAT and penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany guarantees full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No exchange controls, FX conversion authorisations, quotas or repatriation approvals apply to a German GmbH. Dividend remittance, interest, royalties, loan repayments and capital returns to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted, subject only to German corporate taxation (15% corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax) and, on dividend distributions, to a 25% withholding tax plus solidarity surcharge (26.375% combined), which is reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% or under an applicable double-taxation agreement. The only mandatory overlay is statistical: cross-border payments and transactions of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank with an accurate purpose code; the free movement of capital under EU law prevents any discriminatory limitation of this regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank, Frankfurt am Main) as the competent foreign-exchange statistical and reporting authority under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) as the foreign trade administration; and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) as the prudential and anti-money-laundering supervisor of German credit institutions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporation tax, producing a combined federal rate of 15.825%. Municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and varies by municipality; the resulting combined corporate tax burden is commonly approximately 30%, but is not a single national rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

German domestic withholding tax on dividends is generally 25%, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective withholding rate of 26.375%, before any applicable church tax or relief. A reduced rate or exemption may be available under an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, subject to eligibility, beneficial ownership, anti-abuse rules, and procedural requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Returns are generally due seven months after the end of the relevant calendar or financial year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser prepares the return, a statutory extended deadline generally applies, subject to exceptions and any deadline-specific administrative rules. Tax assessments and extensions should be checked for the relevant filing year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Prepayments are generally due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September, and 10 December for corporation tax, and on 15 February, 15 May, 15 August, and 15 November for trade tax. The assessment notice and current law govern the payment obligation and any adjustments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany does not apply one general monetary threshold below which cross-border related-party transactions are exempt from arm's-length pricing or documentation obligations. Under the traditional documentation thresholds, a Local File is generally required where cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions in a fiscal year. A Master File is generally required for a German taxpayer belonging to a multinational group whose preceding-year consolidated revenue is at least EUR 100 million. Statutory rules and thresholds have been amended and may differ by assessment period; confirm the rules applicable to the relevant year. Documentation may be requested, and special transaction documentation or records may be required irrespective of these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Nevertheless, German transfer-pricing rules require arm's-length outcomes and may scrutinize functions, assets, risks, decision-making, and actual conduct. Substance and beneficial ownership are relevant to treaty and EU-directive relief, anti-abuse rules, controlled foreign company rules, permanent-establishment analysis, and the characterization of arrangements. A GmbH should maintain contemporaneous evidence of its personnel, premises, governance, operational functions, and control of material risks consistent with its reported profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax returns

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion is criminal under AO section 370 and may be punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax, interest, and related surcharges remain payable. Negligent tax reduction may be an administrative offense under AO section 378, with a fine of up to EUR 50,000.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal or administrative liability where the required intent or negligence is established, as well as civil liability for culpable breach of tax duties under AO section 69. Imprisonment is possible in criminal cases. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty; coercive or enforcement measures require a separate legal basis.
Failure to remit payroll or withholding taxes

Statutory sanction: Unpaid tax, interest, late-payment surcharges, and enforcement may apply. Intentional withholding-tax evasion may constitute a criminal offense under the AO; negligent conduct may attract administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for tax compliance may incur personal liability under AO section 69 for culpable non-payment and may face criminal liability if statutory elements are met. A corporate veil is not automatically pierced; liability arises under specific statutory or criminal-law rules.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income under German transfer-pricing rules. Documentation failures can trigger an estimate of the tax base and statutory surcharges, including percentage-based surcharges subject to statutory minimums and maximums. Interest and additional tax may follow.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil or criminal exposure where culpable conduct constitutes a tax offense or duty breach. Ordinary transfer-pricing adjustments do not, by themselves, establish personal criminal liability.
Abuse of treaty or EU withholding-tax relief

Statutory sanction: Relief may be denied or withdrawn, resulting in withholding tax, interest, surcharges, and, where facts support it, tax-evasion or other proceedings under applicable law.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable where they knowingly or negligently breach statutory duties or participate in an offense. Denial of treaty relief alone does not automatically create personal liability or pierce the GmbH's separate legal personality.
Fraudulent insolvency or unlawful distributions

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include repayment claims, insolvency-law liability, tax consequences, fines, or criminal penalties, depending on the conduct and applicable statutory provisions.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal civil liability for prohibited payments, failure to file for insolvency on time, or breach of duties, and criminal liability where statutory elements are met. Travel bans are not an automatic consequence.
Money laundering and sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Penalties depend on the applicable AML or sanctions provision and may include substantial fines, confiscation, business restrictions, and imprisonment for serious offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors and other responsible individuals may face personal administrative or criminal liability where they participate in, direct, or culpably fail to prevent reportable or prohibited conduct under an applicable legal duty. Corporate liability and individual liability are assessed separately.

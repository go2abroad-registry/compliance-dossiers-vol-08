Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The registered office must be a physical address in the Republic of Cyprus to which communications and legal process may be served (Companies Law, Cap. 113, s.200); a P.O. Box alone is impermissible. A licensed Administrative Services Provider (ASP) under Law 196(I)/2012 may validly sponsor or render its physical premises as the company's registered office. For this digital webtoon publishing business, an ASP-sponsored address satisfies the Registrar, but it does not alone establish corporate tax residence; central management and control (board meetings, strategic decision-making, and administrative functions exercised in Cyprus) is required to substantiate Cyprus tax residence and to support the 12.5% corporate tax position. The company may lawfully be administered remotely with the ASP acting as registered office and secretary, provided records are kept at the registered office in Cyprus.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for private companies under Cap. 113; standard practice is an issued share capital of EUR 1,000 (1,000 ordinary shares of EUR 1.00 each). No escrow account requirement exists under Cyprus law; shares may be issued as fully paid for non-cash consideration and the Statement of Capital must be lodged. Non-resident ownership is unrestricted.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate for office occupancy must be obtained under the Fire Services Law (L.95(I)/2020) where the registered office is physically used; structural load inspection does not apply to a cloud-based digital operation, and workplace clearance under the Factories Act (Cap. 134) is triggered only if a physical studio, editorial office, or storage facility exceeding 100 sqm is established.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for private companies under Cap. 113; standard practice is an issued share capital of EUR 1,000 (1,000 ordinary shares of EUR 1.00 each). No escrow account requirement exists under Cyprus law; shares may be issued as fully paid for non-cash consideration and the Statement of Capital must be lodged. Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance, Nicosia; official portal: Tax Portal (taxportal.mof.gov.cy) for direct tax and VAT filings; joint e-services portal for corporate and tax registration via the Department of Registrar of Companies and the Tax Department.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus private limited company first obtains a Certificate of Incorporation from the Registrar of Companies, with the Company Registration Number serving as the legal identifier. The company must then apply to the Tax Department for a Tax Identification Number/TIC and, if taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed the VAT threshold, must submit VAT Form VAT-101. The application can be filed by a resident tax representative or a Cyprus-licensed accounting firm; supporting documents include the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, registered office address, directors' and shareholders' details, and a declaration of intended activities. For non-resident companies without Cyprus registration, a VAT registration is obtained directly from the Tax Department where a Cyprus VAT nexus exists.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Cyprus private limited company first obtains a Certificate of Incorporation from the Registrar of Companies, with the Company Registration Number serving as the legal identifier. The company must then apply to the Tax Department for a Tax Identification Number/TIC and, if taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed the VAT threshold, must submit VAT Form VAT-101. The application can be filed by a resident tax representative or a Cyprus-licensed accounting firm; supporting documents include the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, registered office address, directors' and shareholders' details, and a declaration of intended activities. For non-resident companies without Cyprus registration, a VAT registration is obtained directly from the Tax Department where a Cyprus VAT nexus exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A company is tax resident if it is managed and controlled in Cyprus, and corporate profits are subject to corporation tax at the entity level. Statutory election mechanisms available include group loss relief, notional interest deduction on new equity, and elections concerning the application of reduced VAT rates for specific supplies. Hybrid mismatch rules consistent with EU ATAD are implemented, and Cyprus applies the participation exemption regime as an elective statutory framework for qualifying dividend and capital gains relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover; VAT registration becomes compulsory once taxable supplies exceed this threshold in the preceding 12 months or are expected to exceed it within the next 30 days. Lower or nil thresholds apply for intra-Community acquisitions and certain distance sales of goods under EU e-commerce rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union, provided the exporter retains proof of departure from Cyprus and the goods leave EU customs territory. Exports of intangible digital services, webtoon licensing, advertising, and subscription services to non-resident EU businesses are generally treated as outside the scope of Cyprus VAT when supplied by a taxable person to a taxable person in another EU member state, while supplies to non-EU customers are typically zero-rated or outside scope under the basic B2B place-of-supply rule. To claim zero-rating for export goods or offshore services, the supplier must hold commercial transport documents and evidence that the recipient belongs outside Cyprus or outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD / Single Administrative Document) validated by the Cyprus Customs and EU customs IT systems
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or other international transport document proving physical export from Cyprus
  • Commercial invoice and underlying supply contract
  • Official proof of place of business and VAT status of the overseas customer
  • Bank transfer or SWIFT/payment evidence showing foreign exchange settlement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank confirmation where export proceeds are repatriated

Cyprus currently does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime for B2B or B2C e-invoicing. Electronic invoices are accepted where authenticity and integrity are guaranteed through business controls, agreed electronic exchange, or trusted e-invoicing systems. Cyprus is implementing EU e-invoicing standards and will align with the VAT in the Digital Age initiative once EU-wide obligations become effective.

The Tax Department and Cyprus Customs may require that export evidence be produced within a specified statutory period and during audit. If zero-rating is claimed without sufficient documentary proof, the VAT is retrospectively clawed back and assessed to the supplier, together with interest and penalties. Falsified export documentation or schemes designed to fabricate export zero-rating can result in penalties of up to 300% of the tax evaded, criminal prosecution, and liability for VAT, interest, and administrative fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro (EUR) is fully convertible; Cyprus has no exchange-control restrictions on current or capital account transactions since EU accession (1 May 2004) and adoption of the euro (1 January 2008). Full current-account convertibility applies to digital webtoon syndication receipts from Apple App Store and Google Play, including multi-currency settlement (USD received and converted to EUR). Capital-account convertibility is equally unrestricted for inbound equity subscriptions, intercompany loans, dividend distributions, and liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance is hindered only by the corporate-law solvency and distributable-reserves test under the Cyprus Companies Law (Cap. 113, ss. 169-169B) and by the practical AML/EDD standards of the receiving bank; Cyprus imposes no withholding tax on dividends, interest, or royalties paid to non-residents, so no statutory profit-remittance hurdle exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion ratio applies. Export proceeds from digital-content services may be retained in the invoicing currency (EUR or USD) in a licensed Cyprus credit institution without conversion obligation. The sole statutory requirement is statistical: under the Central Bank of Cyprus Directive on Balance of Payments Reporting, every resident entity must report each individual cross-border transaction of EUR 50,000 or more, or cumulative transactions of EUR 200,000 or more over a rolling 12-month period, to the CBC Balance of Payments Division using the prescribed electronic returns, classifying revenue from webtoon syndication under 'Services — Telecommunications, computer, and information services' and capital injections under 'Direct Investment — Equity Capital'.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου / Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou) — the national Foreign Exchange Administration and competent authority for currency, balance-of-payments, and banking prudential matters; the European Central Bank (ECB) exercises joint supervisory oversight for euro-area significant institutions under the Single Supervisory Mechanism.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a supplementary treasury rail for low-value, high-volume cross-border contractor and licensing payments; Low as a substitute for the primary Tier-1 banking relationship. Legal constraints: an EU/EEA-licensed electronic-money institution (EMI) may passport into Cyprus under the Electronic Money Law (Law 85(I)/2012, as amended) and the PSD2 framework, but an EMI is not a credit institution: (i) it cannot hold statutory capital escrow or satisfy registrar/counterparty bank-account warranties; (ii) e-money funds are safeguarded, not covered by the Cyprus Deposit Protection Scheme; (iii) Apple/Google payout plumbing typically requires a beneficiary bank account rather than an EMI wallet; and (iv) Tier-1 Cypriot banks subject incoming MT103 transfers from EMI wallets to the same CDD/Source-of-Funds scrutiny as any non-bank transferor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is an inward EUR remittance by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own bank account (third-party payments are rejected) to the CY_LTD EUR account at a Tier-1 Cypriot bank (e.g., Bank of Cyprus BIC BCYPCY2N), bearing the remittance narrative 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION — CY_LTD' and the unique end-to-end transaction reference (UETR). The payment must be declared to the Central Bank of Cyprus on the periodic Balance of Payments return under the statistical classification 'Direct Investment — Equity Capital'. Verification pack retained by the bank: board resolution allotting shares, updated memorandum and articles of association, share-register entries and share certificates, and, for injections above EUR 50,000, independent source-of-funds evidence from the shareholder. The bank applies a compliance hold until the incoming credit is matched against the corporate file.Primary rails for webtoon treasury: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for EUR payments to EEA-based writers, artists, and licensors; (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA disbursements to co-publishers, digital distributors and content licensors in Korea, Japan, and the United States; (3) TARGET2 for interbank finality of EUR payments through the Cypriot bank's Eurosystem participation; (4) USD disbursements settled through the Cypriot bank's New York correspondent account (e.g., JPMorgan Chase or Citibank) over CHIPS/Fedwire, with the company maintaining separate USD and EUR clearing wallets to avoid conversion leakage. Commercial letters of credit are not a standard rail for digital deliverables, but a sight documentary LC is available from Tier-1 banks for auxiliary physical merchandise; for digital chapter-unlocking revenue, the operative settlement evidence is the platform payout statement matched against MT103 notifications.Outright EUR/USD foreign-exchange forwards — OTC, ISDA 2002 Master Agreement, used to hedge the conversion of USD-denominated Apple and Google payout balances into EUR at forecast ratesEUR/USD and EUR/GBP foreign-exchange swaps (tom-next, spot-next and forward-swap structures) — treasury liquidity smoothing and aligned netting of syndication revenue streamsCross-currency swaps (EUR/USD) — economic conversion of an inbound USD capital injection or foreign-currency revolving facility into fixed-rate EUR debt service aligned with IFRS 9 hedge accountingVanilla and barrier FX options (EUR call / USD put and EUR put / USD call) — purchased from Cyprus-licensed credit institutions or EU investment firms, subject to EMIR and CBC balance-of-payments reporting on exercised amountsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restrictive or non-convertible emerging-market currencies — permissible where webtoon sub-licensing revenue is invoiced in a blocked currency and hedged via an offshore bank counterparty, with the resulting settlement declared to the Central Bank of Cyprus

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Cyprus imposes no withholding tax on outbound dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; the Special Defence Contribution (SDC) at 17% applies only to dividend income received by Cyprus-resident individuals or certain Cyprus-resident corporate beneficiaries, not to non-resident recipients, and Cyprus-resident companies distributing profits do not make a domestic dividend withholding deduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The IR601 must be submitted within six months after the end of the accounting period (for a 31 December year-end, by 30 June of the following year). Provisional tax of 75% of estimated chargeability is due in two equal instalments by 31 July and 31 December of the year of assessment; the final balance of tax is due by 1 August of the year following the year of assessment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Cyprus Local File is mandatory where aggregate related-party transactions exceed EUR 750,000 for the purchase or sale of tangible goods or EUR 250,000 for services, royalties, financing and other transactions in a tax year; a Master File is required where the multinational group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750 million. These thresholds apply to tax years commencing on or after 1 July 2022 under Article 33A of the Income Tax Law and the accompanying Cyprus Transfer Pricing Decree.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate a GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute; however, substance is legally enforced through the management-and-control test in Section 2 ITL for Cypriot tax residence, the OECD-modified-nexus conditions for the IP Box deduction, the existence of local directors, employees, premises, bank accounts and board minutes in Cyprus for TRC issuance, and the EU ATAD/FATF narrow-scope anti-shell measures. A webtoon syndication company must therefore evidence real operational decision-making, local accounting records, and commercial activity in Cyprus rather than acting as a letterbox conduit.

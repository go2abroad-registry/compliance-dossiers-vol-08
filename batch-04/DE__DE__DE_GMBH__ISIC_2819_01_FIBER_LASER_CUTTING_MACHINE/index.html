Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum statutory share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000 under § 5 GmbHG; before registration, at least EUR 12,500 in aggregate and not less than 25% of each subscriber's share must be paid in cash and held at the company's free disposal. Payment must be evidenced by a blocked account (Sperrkonto) confirmation issued by an EU/EEA credit institution under § 8(2) GmbHG; the account remains blocked until the Commercial Register entry is completed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance from the municipal Brandschutzdienststelle based on an approved fire-protection concept covering laser-classified enclosures, extinguishing systems, and escape routes; structural load certificate issued by a licensed structural engineer for machine foundations and vibration-sensitive equipment under the state building code; occupational workplace clearance per Arbeitsstättenverordnung (ArbStättV) and ASR standards, including the documented hazard assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung), appointment of a Fachkraft für Arbeitssicherheit, occupational medical surveillance, and evacuation drills; the Gewerbeaufsicht must conduct an initial commissioning inspection before continuous production starts.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum statutory share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000 under § 5 GmbHG; before registration, at least EUR 12,500 in aggregate and not less than 25% of each subscriber's share must be paid in cash and held at the company's free disposal. Payment must be evidenced by a blocked account (Sperrkonto) confirmation issued by an EU/EEA credit institution under § 8(2) GmbHG; the account remains blocked until the Commercial Register entry is completed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF), Federal Ministry of Finance, Berlin; administered locally by the competent Finanzamt (tax office) for income, trade and VAT; EU VAT-ID allocation and cross-border VAT matters handled by the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt), Bonn; mandatory electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); customs administration: Generaldirektion Zoll (ATLAS export system).

E-government filing portal

For the resident GmbH: (1) complete commercial-register registration; (2) file the electronic new-entity tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER with the Finanzamt competent for the registered seat and place of management; (3) receive the local Steuernummer and German Umsatzsteuernummer; (4) apply online to BZSt for the EU-wide Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr), attaching the commercial register extract, articles of association, Steuernummer and expected export/intra-Community trading basis; (5) request an EORI number from Generaldirektion Zoll for customs filing. For non-resident foreign entities without a German establishment, registration for German VAT and a German USt-IdNr is made directly through BZSt under the simplified foreign-entrepreneur procedure; since 1 July 2021, non-EU businesses may be required to appoint a German fiscal representative (Fiskalvertreter, §22a UStG).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For the resident GmbH: (1) complete commercial-register registration; (2) file the electronic new-entity tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER with the Finanzamt competent for the registered seat and place of management; (3) receive the local Steuernummer and German Umsatzsteuernummer; (4) apply online to BZSt for the EU-wide Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr), attaching the commercial register extract, articles of association, Steuernummer and expected export/intra-Community trading basis; (5) request an EORI number from Generaldirektion Zoll for customs filing. For non-resident foreign entities without a German establishment, registration for German VAT and a German USt-IdNr is made directly through BZSt under the simplified foreign-entrepreneur procedure; since 1 July 2021, non-EU businesses may be required to appoint a German fiscal representative (Fiskalvertreter, §22a UStG).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box or transparent-entity election; a GmbH is statutorily opaque and fully liable to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). The recognized elections are (i) VAT, corporation-tax and trade-tax fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §2(2) No.2 UStG and §14 KStG, merging the GmbH's tax positions into a controlling entity upon financial, economic and organizational integration; (ii) the small-business option under §19 UStG for domestic turnover below the statutory thresholds (immaterial for an active export manufacturer); (iii) the option under §9 UStG to treat certain real-estate rents as taxable supplies; (iv) financing-instrument elections (e.g., profit-participating loans) which are evaluated under ATAD-conform hybrid-mismatch limitation rules rather than entity reclassification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold requires VAT registration for the GmbH or for non-resident sellers: liability is triggered by the first taxable economic activity in Germany. The sole de-minimis is the §19 UStG small-business exemption at EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 forecast current-year turnover; the exporting laser-machinery GmbH is VAT-registered from inception and must file monthly advance VAT returns when prior-year net VAT due exceeds EUR 7,500 (quarterly if below) plus an annual VAT return, an EU Sales List for intra-Community supplies, and recapitulative statements for B2B services under reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Deliveries of goods physically dispatched by the GmbH to third-country (non-EU) destinations are VAT-exempt with full input-VAT deduction (de-facto 0% charge) under §4 No.1a in conjunction with §6 UStG, provided (a) the dispatch to the third country is evidenced by a customs export declaration filed via ATLAS with a valid Movement Reference Number, (b) the GmbH holds the export proof within 60 days of the supply, and (c) the invoice cites the exemption and the customs declaration reference. Intra-EU dispatches to registered business customers are exempt as intra-Community supplies under §6a UStG where the customer's VAT-ID is confirmed in VIES and a confirmation of receipt (Gelangensbestätigung) is retained. Offshore B2B services connected to the exported fiber-laser cutting systems (commissioning, remote maintenance, engineering for third-country customers) fall outside German VAT under §3a(2) UStG where the recipient is a non-German entrepreneur; services to German VAT-liable customers remain taxable in Germany and may be subject to reverse charge.

  • Customs Export Declaration (German ATLAS Ausfuhranmeldung) with Export Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Export accompanying document or customs release evidence (ABD/customs-stamped export proof) showing physical departure of the machine from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice containing the VAT exemption clause (Steuerfreie Ausfuhrlieferung §4 No.1a UStG) and the specific export declaration reference
  • International transport document evidencing consignment to the third-country buyer (Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR or multimodal transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice reflecting receipt of the export sales proceeds in the GmbH's German bank account
  • Export sales contract or UCP600 Letter of Credit (SWIFT LC) evidencing the B2B export sale of the high-power fiber laser cutting machine
  • For intra-EU dispatches: validated customer EU VAT registration number, EU Sales List entry and Gelangensbestätigung (confirmation of arrival of goods)

Germany does not operate real-time government fiscal clearance, transactional API stamping or SAF-T continuous controls. Domestic B2B e-invoicing is phased in under §14 UStG (Growth Opportunities Act): from 1 January 2025 all German B2B recipients must be able to receive e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 suppliers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X formats); by 1 January 2028 structured e-invoicing becomes general. VAT advance returns, annual returns and EU Sales Lists are transmitted exclusively in XML through the certified ELSTER/ERiC API. Export records, invoices and transport evidence must be retained for ten years in a machine-readable audit-proof format (GoBD).

The Finanzamt performs risk-based VAT audits (Betriebsprüfung) and cross-references the GmbH's ATLAS declaration data (MRNs), transport documents, SWIFT receipts and production records; digital matching identifies round-tripping, counterfeit MRNs and fake exports. If export substantiation fails or evidence is defective, the 0% treatment is retroactively clawed back by an assessment of German VAT at 19% on the export invoice price, plus statutory default interest, late-payment surcharges under §240 AO and penalty tax assessments under §162 AO. Knowingly false export evidence constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO (imprisonment up to ten years, monetary penalties and asset confiscation), while negligent documentation defects are administrative offences under §378 AO; joint liability may attach in organized VAT supply-chain fraud arrangements. Retrospective clawback periods are four years from the end of the relevant tax year for negligence and up to ten years for evasion (§169 AO), and export files and FX repatriation records must survive the full assessment window.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full, unrestricted convertibility of the euro for both current account transactions (goods, services, income, dividends) and capital account transactions (foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, financial credits) pursuant to the free movement of capital under Articles 63-66 TFEU. No exchange control approvals, prior clearances, or profit remittance licenses are required; profit repatriation is subject only to standard corporate documentation (audited financials, tax balance sheet, and, if applicable, German dividend withholding tax at 26.375% including solidarity surcharge, reduced under applicable DTT). The functional and reporting currency is EUR; foreign currency accounts may be held freely.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation, or euro conversion ratio for export proceeds received in foreign currency. The only binding obligation is statistical: under Sections 66-68 AWV, any cross-border receipt or payment exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction must be reported electronically to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five (5) working days of settlement (including export proceeds, imports, services, and capital flows), which is purely informational and does not constitute foreign exchange authorization. For the fiber laser cutting machine export business, invoice-level documentation and correct AWV payment codes (e.g., goods transaction codes with 8-digit customs tariff numbers) must be retained.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank; competent authority for international payments statistics and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) reporting), acting within the Eurosystem framework of the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment/electronic money institutions is exercised by the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) in coordination with the Bundesbank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary liquidity rail, Low as a sole treasury provider. EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) passporting into Germany under PSD2/ZAG may open operational payment accounts via BaFin-compliant onboarding; however, offshore (non-EEA) digital EMIs without a passport are legally barred from providing payment accounts in Germany. EMIs cannot host the statutory capital contribution escrow account required under the German Limited Liability Companies Act, cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit, and generally lack the counterparty strength for industrial B2B trade financing; they are therefore viable only for low-value operational collections/payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: route the shareholder's euro capital contribution via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA/TARGET2 if the sender holds an EEA account) into the dedicated capital escrow account (Kapitalkonto) at the German bank, using field 70 Remittance Information to quote the notarial deed number, GmbH name, and the exact statutory phrase 'Stammeinlage gemäß GmbH-Vertrag / notarielle Urkunde Nr. X vom DD.MM.YYYY'. Include AWV payment purpose code Z1/Z2 (direct investment) and, for non-EEA inbound wires, the bank's SWIFT code. The German bank will issue a Bankenbestätigung confirming free disposal of at least EUR 12,500 (or the full EUR 25,000 for a single-member GmbH) prior to commercial register filing. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary liquidity rail, Low as a sole treasury provider. EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) passporting into Germany under PSD2/ZAG may open operational payment accounts via BaFin-compliant onboarding; however, offshore (non-EEA) digital EMIs without a passport are legally barred from providing payment accounts in Germany. EMIs cannot host the statutory capital contribution escrow account required under the German Limited Liability Companies Act, cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit, and generally lack the counterparty strength for industrial B2B trade financing; they are therefore viable only for low-value operational collections/payouts.

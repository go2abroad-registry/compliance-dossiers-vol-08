Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a private limited liability company under Cap. 113. The nominal share capital is fixed in the M&A (commonly between EUR 1,000 and EUR 10,000) and the paid-up amount is determined by the board at allotment. No escrow account rule applies; subscription proceeds must be credited to the Company's own bank account at or immediately following allotment.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety compliance certificate issued by the Cyprus Fire Service confirming adherence to the Fire Safety in Buildings Decree; building completion and structural load inspection certificate from the District Administrative Authority verifying floor load capacity for heavy gantry storage and out-of-gauge cargo; workplace operational clearance from the Department of Labour Inspection under the Safety and Health at Work Law (Law 89(I)/1996), including a health and safety file, risk assessments for lifting operations, and site-specific traffic management for heavy haulage.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department (Department of Taxation), Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; official digital portal: 'Tax For All' at https://taxfor.all.gov.cy; VAT registration and filings are administered electronically through the ARIADNE integrated tax information system; customs export declarations are lodged through the Cyprus THESEAS/ICS2 customs electronic platform.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity must file the prescribed VAT registration application (TCF series, Form for Non-Established Taxable Persons) with the District VAT Office or electronically via ARIADNE before making taxable supplies in Cyprus. Required documentation bundle: (i) certificate of incorporation and memorandum/articles of association from the home jurisdiction, (ii) proof of registered office in Cyprus or branch registration if a permanent establishment is constituted, (iii) executed power of attorney appointing a Cyprus tax/fiscal representative, (iv) written evidence of intended activity, estimated taxable turnover for the next 12 months, and the ISIC 5229 project-cargo/heavy-haul service scope, (v) foreign VAT/TIN or tax residency certificate, and (vi) bank reference or security guarantee where no Cypriot bank account has been opened. The Tax Department allocates a nine-digit TIN (company tax registration number) and, where applicable, a VAT number in the statutory format 'CY' + eight digits + one alphabetic check character; processing is normally completed within 30 working days from submission of a complete file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Cyprus-incorporated private limited company is a separate legal person taxed on worldwide income at 12.5% corporate income tax where managed and controlled in Cyprus; non-resident companies remain subject to territorial taxation on Cyprus-source income only. No pass-through of losses or profits to shareholders is permissible; a Cyprus Ltd cannot elect tax-transparent treatment.

Available election forms

Cyprus has no check-the-box classification election; available statutory elections include: (1) VAT group registration with a single representative member under the VAT Law N.246(I)/2000, relevant to affiliated project-cargo logistics entities; (2) group relief loss-utilisation election between 75%-related companies under the Income Tax Law N.118(I)/2002; (3) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on qualifying new equity introduced after 1 January 2015; (4) tonnage tax election restricted to qualifying shipowners on the Cyprus/International Shipping Register, unavailable to an ISIC 5229 OOG logistics operator; (5) transfer-pricing file election permitting a full Local File to satisfy benchmarking requirements; (6) opt-in to the Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for intra-Community distance sales; and (7) election of snapshot alignment of financial-accounting treatment under the EU hybrid mismatch (ATAD II) neutralisation rules, which do not confer elective benefits but require disclosure of hybrid instrument treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover (net of VAT) for supplies of goods and services in Cyprus by both resident and non-resident taxable persons; the intra-Community/Union distance-sales threshold is EUR 10,000 under the OSS simplification; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold for exporters, including OOG project-cargo logistics operators needing to recover input VAT on heavy equipment and subcontracted haulage.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-percent VAT applies under the zero-rating provisions of the Cypriot VAT Law N.246(I)/2000 implementing Article 146 of Directive 2006/112/EC to: (i) supplies of goods physically exported outside the EU by the supplier, the customer, or a third-party logistics provider; (ii) transport, loading, stevedoring, customs brokerage, craneage, lashing, mafi-trailer shunting, and out-of-gauge pre-shipment services supplied directly in connection with such export consignments; and (iii) intra-Community B2B dispatches where the customer supplies a valid EU VAT number, the goods physically depart Cyprus to another member state, and the VIES recapitulative statement is correctly filed. The export must be completed within 90 days of the supply date, and the exporter must act in good faith and retain contemporaneous evidence of exit, payment, and transport routing to preserve entitlement; freight-forwarding and project-cargo milestone invoices charged on Incoterms FOB/CIF/CPT bases qualify where the direct export link is documented.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX-A with registered Customs Declaration Number from the THESEAS/ICS2 system) evidencing exit of the goods from the EU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), Sea Waybill, Air Waybill (AWB), or CMR consignment note naming the shipper, consignee, cargo description, gross weight, and destination port/airport outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-confirmed evidence of the export proceeds received into a Cypriot bank account, or an authenticated SWIFT MT103 / letter-of-credit settlement from the non-resident customer)

Cyprus currently imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime for domestic B2B invoices. Existing electronic obligations are limited to: (a) mandatory e-filing of VAT returns (VATA-F-403) and supporting ledgers through Tax For All/ARIADNE within the statutory 10th-day-of-the-following-month filing window for the applicable monthly, quarterly, or six-monthly VAT period; (b) electronic submission of VIES intra-Community recapitulative statements, calendar-period listings, and Intrastat declarations; and (c) SAF-T/audit export on notional requests. Under the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) package, a phased 2027-2035 mandate will make PEPPOL-compliant B2B e-invoicing and an EU-wide clearance/API reporting model mandatory for Cyprus taxable persons, requiring ERP-level interface readiness and ARIADNE interoperability.

The Tax Department cross-checks export zero-rating relief against the THESEAS export declarations, customs exit stamps, B/L dates, VIES records, and bank payment flows. Where the physical export cannot be substantiated, the customer's VAT number is invalid, or the foreign proceeds are not repatriated to a Cyprus bank account within 180 days of export, the VAT Commissioner reassesses the full 19% VAT with interest at 10% per annum plus administrative penalties; misdeclarations carry statutory fines of up to EUR 5,000 per filing. Fraudulent claims, forged export stamps, or circular sham shipments trigger retrospective clawback of all input VAT recovered and zero-rating relief, criminal liability under the VAT Law punishable by imprisonment of up to five years and/or a fine of up to EUR 50,000, suspension of the taxpayer's VAT number and VIES access, publication of sanctioning decisions in the Official Gazette, and referral to the criminal investigation section of the Cyprus Tax Department. Retrospective assessments remain available under the statutory limitation windows of up to six years, extended in cases of fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full euro-area member and maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the EU Treaty framework and the Codified Capital Movements Law. There is no general exchange-control approval for profit remittances, dividends, shareholder loan repayments, or capital liquidation proceeds to non-resident investors. Practical roadblocks arise solely from financial crime compliance: bank-level KYC, sanctions screening, source-of-funds verification, and accurate balance-of-payments reporting. Dividends distributed by a Cyprus limited company may be paid abroad without exchange-control permission, provided the beneficial owner is properly documented and the transfer is executed through an authorised Cyprus credit institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender rule exists in Cyprus. Export proceeds are freely retainable in any currency, and there is no statutory timeline or conversion ratio requiring repatriation or conversion into euro. The only applicable obligation is statistical: international receipts from export transactions are captured by the reporting bank for the Central Bank of Cyprus balance-of-payments compilation. Commercial banks may impose internal documentary requirements for credit to an exporter's account, including the underlying invoice, transport documents, and proof of title, but no central bank approval is required.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary operational payments, Low for core treasury functions. EU/EEA-authorised electronic money institutions operating in Cyprus under the PSD2 passporting regime can provide payment accounts and cross-border MT103-equivalent rails, but they are not licensed credit institutions and cannot provide capital escrow accounts, documentary letters of credit, or commercial loan facilities. Offshore digital EMIs without a Cyprus or EU authorisation are not viable for this project treasury structure and create serious AML compliance exposure for the corporate entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the Cyprus project entity must be routed through a licensed credit institution in Cyprus. For equity, use an SWIFT MT103 with settlement in TARGET2 for euro and a clear reference contract chamber citing 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital'; for shareholder loans, use 'Direct Investment - Other Capital'. The remitting bank must transmit a purpose code consistent with balance-of-payments components, and the receiving bank will require the underlying corporate resolution, share issue allotment or loan agreement, and source-of-funds verification. The Capital Escrow Account should be segregated and not commingled with operational liquidity to preserve audit clarity.The primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 transfers for hire-and-subcontractor settlements, freight charges, commodity payments, and recurrent operational expenses. For high-value out-of-gauge equipment purchases and marine transport disbursements, commercial letters of credit (sight LC for supplier payment and term LC for deferred settlement) are the preferred trade finance instruments. Euro clearing is settled via TARGET2, non-euro payments are cleared over direct correspondent SWIFT relationships, and the bank will require presentation of the commercial invoice, packing list, transport contract, cargo weight certificate, and customs documentation before releasing any cross-border trade payment.Outright deliverable FX forwards between EUR and USD, subject to MIFIR/EMIR documentation and credit support annex with a Cyprus-authorised bankFX swaps for temporary conversion of project-cargo working capital held in USD or non-euro freight currenciesCross-currency swaps for matching long-term USD-denominated freight receivables against EUR-denominated capital and operating expensesNDFs are lawful under Cyprus law but generally unnecessary and rarely quoted for EUR/USD; they may be used only through a licensed broker or credit institution where the us/eur currency pair lacks deliverable liquidity

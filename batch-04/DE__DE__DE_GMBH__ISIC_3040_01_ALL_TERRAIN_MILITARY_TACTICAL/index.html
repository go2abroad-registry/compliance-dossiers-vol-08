Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The registered share capital (Stammkapital) must amount to at least EUR 25,000. Each cash contribution must be paid in at least to the extent of 50% before registration, and in no case less than EUR 12,500 in total, to a blocked capital account (Sperrkonto) held with a German credit institution; the funds must remain at the free disposal of the managing directors. The bank's confirmation certificate must be attached to the filing. Contributions in kind must be fully provided and substantiated in the notarial deed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and structural load inspection by the municipal fire inspection authorities and approved building inspectors; workplace clearance and occupational safety audit under the Occupational Safety Act (ArbSchG) and the industrial safety regulation (BetrSichV); machinery and pressure vessel inspection through TÜV Süd / TÜV Nord / DEKRA; explosive-risk assessment and explosion-protection documents; emergency response plan coordinated with civil defense authorities due to the tactical vehicle manufacturing profile.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Germany operates a split tax-competence system. (1) Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt), the Federal Central Tax Office (portal: bzst.de), is the sole national authority for allocating the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.), issuing tax certificates for non-resident corporations and handling OECD exchange-of-information matters. (2) The locally competent Finanzamt (state-organized municipal tax office) administers the GmbH's corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), VAT assessments, and tax audits, and allocates the statutory tax number (Steuernummer). (3) Electronic filing is executed exclusively through the ELSTER portal (elster.de); customs export declarations (ATLAS) and EORI registrations are handled by the Generalzolldirektion (zoll.de).

E-government filing portal

Domestic GmbH: after notarial incorporation and registration in the Handelsregister, the local Finanzamt automatically assigns the Steuernummer (statutory TIN for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment) without a separate application; the BZSt then allocates the USt-IdNr. upon electronic application (BZSt form USt 1 TJ) stating the business activity. Foreign entities: a non-established entity must first verify whether it performs taxable supplies in Germany that are not shifted to a German B2B customer under the § 13b UStG reverse charge; where registration is required, it must (i) file form USt 1 TJ with the competent Finanzamt through ELSTER or a certified tax agent, (ii) obtain a USt-IdNr. from BZSt for intra-EU or reverse-charge invoicing, (iii) obtain an EORI number from the Generalzolldirektion for any export customs clearances, and (iv) if resident outside the EU, be prepared to provide VAT security (Sicherheitsleistung under § 18(4) UStG); Germany does not impose a statutory fiscal-representative regime. For a defense-tactical supplier, the BAFA export license reference and contract number (VNR) from the BAAINBw must be reflected in VAT and customs records before first export invoicing.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; not Pass_Through_Eligible. A German GmbH (Kapitalgesellschaft) is a taxable corporation under § 1(1) No. 1 KStG subject to unlimited corporate income tax on worldwide income (Körperschaftsteuer 15% plus Solidaritätszuschlag 0.825%, aggregate rate 15.825%) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer at 3.5% statutory multiplier times the municipal rating factor, commonly 7%-17.15%), yielding an aggregate effective income tax burden of approximately 30% before distribution; profit distributions attract 25% capital-yield withholding (26.375% including solidarity surcharge), with 95% of dividends exempt for corporate shareholders under § 8b KStG. Non-resident corporations are taxed only on German-source income. The GmbH cannot elect transparent partnership taxation and is not a hybrid vehicle; § 4k EStG anti-hybrid rules strictly deny double-dip or mismatch benefits. — Domestic GmbH: after notarial incorporation and registration in the Handelsregister, the local Finanzamt automatically assigns the Steuernummer (statutory TIN for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment) without a separate application; the BZSt then allocates the USt-IdNr. upon electronic application (BZSt form USt 1 TJ) stating the business activity. Foreign entities: a non-established entity must first verify whether it performs taxable supplies in Germany that are not shifted to a German B2B customer under the § 13b UStG reverse charge; where registration is required, it must (i) file form USt 1 TJ with the competent Finanzamt through ELSTER or a certified tax agent, (ii) obtain a USt-IdNr. from BZSt for intra-EU or reverse-charge invoicing, (iii) obtain an EORI number from the Generalzolldirektion for any export customs clearances, and (iv) if resident outside the EU, be prepared to provide VAT security (Sicherheitsleistung under § 18(4) UStG); Germany does not impose a statutory fiscal-representative regime. For a defense-tactical supplier, the BAFA export license reference and contract number (VNR) from the BAAINBw must be reflected in VAT and customs records before first export invoicing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; not Pass_Through_Eligible. A German GmbH (Kapitalgesellschaft) is a taxable corporation under § 1(1) No. 1 KStG subject to unlimited corporate income tax on worldwide income (Körperschaftsteuer 15% plus Solidaritätszuschlag 0.825%, aggregate rate 15.825%) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer at 3.5% statutory multiplier times the municipal rating factor, commonly 7%-17.15%), yielding an aggregate effective income tax burden of approximately 30% before distribution; profit distributions attract 25% capital-yield withholding (26.375% including solidarity surcharge), with 95% of dividends exempt for corporate shareholders under § 8b KStG. Non-resident corporations are taxed only on German-source income. The GmbH cannot elect transparent partnership taxation and is not a hybrid vehicle; § 4k EStG anti-hybrid rules strictly deny double-dip or mismatch benefits.

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections for a GmbH: (1) Organschaft (corporate income tax and trade tax fiscal unity), which requires a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag), a direct shareholding of at least 75%, and financial, economic, and organizational integration; (2) Umsatzsteuer-Organschaft (VAT grouping), a separate election merging intra-group supplies for VAT purposes; (3) small-enterprise relief under § 19 UStG only below the statutory turnover ceiling; (4) option under § 9 UStG to waive VAT exemption on real-estate transactions; (5) One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for intra-EU distance sales and B2C electronic services, which is not applicable to ISIC 3040 defense hardware but forms part of the VAT identity; (6) reverse-charge treatment under § 13b UStG where the German B2B customer accounts for VAT. Hybrid mismatch positions are not elective — Germany applies ATAD-mandated anti-hybrid rules and requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation under § 90 AO.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany applies no general monetary VAT registration threshold: any entrepreneur making taxable supplies in Germany is subject to VAT registration. Proxy statutory thresholds: (a) small-enterprise exemption (§ 19 UStG): prior-year net turnover not exceeding EUR 25,000 and forecast current-year turnover not exceeding EUR 100,000, as amended with effect from 1 January 2025 by the Wachstumschancengesetz; (b) intra-EU distance sales and cross-border B2C electronic services: EUR 10,000 per calendar year within the EU, above which German VAT registration or OSS reporting applies; (c) non-established suppliers whose supplies to German B2B customers are covered by the § 13b UStG reverse charge generally have no VAT registration obligation; (d) Intrastat dispatch reporting obligation above EUR 500,000 per calendar year for goods dispatches.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically exported from Germany to territories outside the EU qualify for VAT zero-rating (steuerfrei with full input VAT deduction) under § 4(1)(a) and § 6 UStG where: (i) the goods belong to the business assets of the German exporting GmbH; (ii) the physical dispatch or transport begins in the German customs territory and the goods leave the EU; (iii) the export is effected by the supplier or by a customer taking the goods out of the EU; (iv) the supplier maintains documentary and accounting evidence (Beleg- und Buchnachweis) proving the export; and (v) for ISIC 3040 military-tactical supplies, the transaction is covered by a valid BAFA export license (Ausfuhrgenehmigung) and a binding end-user/end-use certificate, since unlicensed military exports are unlawful and cannot sustain zero-rating. Intra-Community supplies to other EU Member States are separately exempt under § 6a UStG (innergemeinschaftliche Lieferung) subject to the customer's valid USt-IdNr., transport evidence, and timely filing of the recapitulative statement; ancillary export transport and related services are zero-rated under § 4(2)-(5) and § 7 UStG.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS electronic Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) and Export Accompanying Document (EAD/ABD) with exit confirmation (Ausgangsvermerk)
  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung) and packing list (Packliste) referencing the applicable BAFA export license number
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading (Konnossement) for sea freight, CMR consignment note for road transport, or Air Waybill (AWB) showing actual departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT bank credit confirmation evidencing actual receipt of the export consideration
  • End-user/end-use certificate and Class 1 military goods license documentation for defense-tactical vehicles
  • Supporting customer-side customs import documentation in the destination territory

Germany does not require real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API connectivity, or digital stamping for private-sector invoices. The binding requirement is structured e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) under the Steuerfortentwicklungsgesetz: from 1 January 2025 all domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive e-invoices in the EN 16931 formats (XRechnung XML or hybrid ZUGFeRD/Factur-X); issuance becomes mandatory from 1 January 2027 for enterprises with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 and for all enterprises from 1 January 2028. For B2G supplies, XRechnung e-invoicing through the federal ZRE receiving platform has been mandatory since 27 November 2020, covering invoices to the BAAINBw and other federal contracting authorities. VAT returns, advance returns, and recapitulative statements must be transmitted electronically via ELSTER; there is no continuous transaction control (CTC) or live-stamping regime today, but the tax authorities may electronically inspect e-invoice data during audit, and Germany is implementing EU ViDA-aligned digital reporting measures.

Where export substantiation is defective, missing, or fraudulent (Scheinexport), the Finanzamt retrospectively re-assesses 19% German VAT from the date of supply, plus late-payment interest at 0.15% per month (1.8% per annum under § 233a and § 238 AO, as reduced effective 1 January 2019); all export records must be retained for ten years under § 147 AO and § 14b UStG, and loss of evidence within the retention period itself triggers re-assessment. Fraudulent zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO (imprisonment up to five years, or six months to ten years in aggravated cases), with criminal liability of the GmbH's managing directors (Geschäftsführer) as responsible officers under § 34 and § 69 AO; negligent under-declaration is subject to administrative fines of up to EUR 50,000 under § 378 AO. In the defense sector, a VAT export-fraud finding is additionally pursued under the AWG and KrWaffKontrG export-control regime (fines and imprisonment for unlicensed or diverted military exports), often leading to BAFA license revocation, which destroys the zero-rating basis and converts the entire supply into a 19% VAT-taxable transaction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles 63-66 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC. No central-bank FX approval, no ex-ante licensing, no exchange surrender, and no par-value conversion requirements exist for trade or investment flows. The only statutory hurdle is AWV statistical reporting: any German resident (including the GmbH) must report all cross-border payments to/from non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 per counterparty per calendar month, within 7 days after month-end, using prescribed payment-purpose codes. Profit remittance is not FX-controlled, but dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to 26.375% German dividend withholding tax (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), reducible under an applicable double-tax treaty only upon production of a tax residence certificate (Ansässigkeitsbescheinigung). For ISIC 3040 military-hardware entities, inbound/outbound transfers additionally trigger bank-level sanctions, export-control, and enhanced due diligence (EDD) review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, no minimum conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no holding-period rules on export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or foreign currency in German or foreign accounts. The sole compliance burden is AWV balance-of-payments notification (EUR 12,500 monthly per counterparty threshold) and complete support documentation for bank sanctions/embargo screening. For armored vehicle exports, every proceeds inflow must be backed by a valid BAFA export license (and, where applicable, a War Weapons Control Act/KrWaffKontrG authorization) plus an End-User Certificate; absent such documentation, the receiving bank will freeze or reject the transfer.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank and sole balance-of-payments statistics authority under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance 'AWV'); BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) as banking supervisor; BAFA (Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle) as export-control licensing authority; Generalzolldirektion as customs/sanctions enforcement authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Low (not viable as a primary rail for ISIC 3040). German EMIs/payment institutions licensed under the ZAG (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz) may legally issue IBANs and execute payments, but cannot accept deposits or extend commercial credit. Mainstream EMI/neobank platforms (e.g., N26, Qonto, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Solaris-based BaaS programs) operate restrictive sector policies and automatically decline 'defense/weapons/armored vehicle manufacturing' clients, or cap volumes incompatible with government contracts. Legal constraints: EMIs must apply full GwG KYC and EU/OFAC/UK sanctions screening; a licensed military-hardware company requires bank-issued documentary credits, standbys, and corporate credit lines that an EMI cannot legally provide. Use of an EU-passported EMI may be feasible only for low-value operational expenses (travel, software, consumables), and only if full BAFA license documentation, EUC, UBO evidence, and sanctions declarations are submitted; however, Treasury should not route Government_Direct_Wire or Diplomatic_LC proceeds through any EMI.

Revolut Fit: High

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 15.825%); combined with municipal Gewerbesteuer at the average Hebesatz of 400%–450%, the aggregate statutory corporate tax burden approximates 29.83% to 32.20%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% dividend withholding tax on domestic source dividends (§§ 43, 43a, 44 EStG), plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withheld tax, resulting in an effective 26.375% WHT; where the GmbH is subject to trade tax, the distribution is not trade-tax triggered unless a hidden distribution occurs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German TP documentation duty arises under § 90 (3) AO for all cross-border controlled transactions. For a manufacturing GmbH, a Local File is mandatory when the annual aggregate remuneration paid or received in controlled goods/services transactions exceeds EUR 1,000,000; a Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue is greater than EUR 50,000,000. Additionally, documentation must be supplied for all related-party sales and procurement of military equipment components, including cost contribution agreements, within 30 days after official demand.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime in the style of the UAE or the Cayman Islands, but substantive requirements are imposed through the anti-abuse clause of § 42 AO, the Federal Fiscal Court jurisprudence on the reality of the registered office, and the principal purpose test under MLI Art. 29 included in German DTA practice. A German GmbH used by non-resident owners solely to channel defense-related dividends or payments without actual management, staff, assets, and business risk will fail the economic substance test and be recharacterised, denied treaty benefits, and proceeded under sham-company rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from three months to ten years under § 370 AO; in aggravated cases (tax shortfall exceeding EUR 100,000 or systematic evasion), imprisonment of up to ten years and a criminal fine computed on the evaded amount; assessment of back taxes plus interest of 1.8% per annum and late penalties; professional disqualification.

Director personal liability: The managing director, as the person responsible for tax disclosure, is liable under §§ 34, 69, and 71 AO for the unpaid corporate taxes; court-ordered asset freeze, confiscation, prohibition on practicing as manager, and personal criminal imprisonment may be imposed on the director in person; civil joint and several liability for tax damage is immediate.
Shell / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the GmbH as a fiscal non-entity under § 42 AO and the German anti-shell court practice; denial of treaty benefits under § 50d EStG and MLI Art. 29; in addition, supervisory authorities can revoke the GmbH's defense production license under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung) and order dissolution.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for taxes if the GmbH is a sham and assets are commingled; for fraudulent incorporation causing third-party damage, personal criminal liability for fraud under § 263 StGB and breach of managing director duties under § 43 GmbHG.
Anti-Money Laundering and Export Control Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines under the GwG up to EUR 5,000,000 (or up to 10% of global annual turnover if the violation is severe); criminal proceeds confiscation; revocation of the Armaments Export License (Kriegswaffenkontrollgesetz) and the company's operational license.

Director personal liability: Directors can be sentenced to up to five years' imprisonment for money laundering under § 261 StGB; insofar as the director approves the prohibited transaction, he is personally exposed to fines and imprisonment and is barred from future management positions in regulated industries.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under § 162 (4) AO, a penalty of at least 5% and up to 10% of the additional tax assessment, with a minimum penalty of EUR 5,000; if documentation is not submitted, the tax authorities may estimate taxable income and apply risk-based TP adjustments with interest at 1.8% per annum.

Director personal liability: The director is accountable for ensuring that documentation is filed; non-compliance may be treated as intent, exposing the director to fines for breach of tax duties and, in severe deliberate cases, to liability for the TP penalty amounts if the GmbH is insolvent.

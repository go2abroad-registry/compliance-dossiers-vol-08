Foreign owners incorporate a private limited company (HE77) with the Registrar of Companies, then file Form TD101 with the Tax Department to obtain a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN). VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 for taxable supplies of goods and services by established taxable persons; €35,000 for intra-EU distance sales; no threshold for non-established foreign businesses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-ratable exports include goods physically removed from the EU by the supplier or on its behalf, or directly supplied to a non-EU recipient outside the EU. Services provided to non-EU business customers may qualify as zero-rated exports where the recipient is outside the EU. Documentary proof of export and direct payment evidence must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR Transport Document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank payment statement
  • Commercial Invoice and Proof of Non-EU Destination

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is currently mandated. VAT returns and VIES declarations are submitted electronically through the Cyprus Tax Department online portal; standard e-invoicing is accepted but not compulsory.

Retrospective assessments may be raised up to six years for VAT. Failure to produce sufficient export documentation results in loss of zero-rating, repayment of output VAT, and disallowance of input VAT recovery. Late payment interest at 5% per annum plus 10% tax penalty may apply; fraudulent zero-rating claims attract restitution, fines up to twice the VAT loss, and criminal prosecution. Export proceeds evidenced by FX repatriation must be collected within 180 days to support the underlying commercial transaction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus has full current and capital account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU, the EU Capital Movements Directive (88/361/EEC), and the Eurozone legal framework. No general exchange-control license is required for inbound investment, dividend remittance, or trade settlements. Practical hurdles are AML/CFT-driven: banks must verify source of funds, economic substance, and tax residence before processing transfers. Profit remittance is tax-efficient: Cyprus applies a 12.5% corporate tax rate, no withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident / EU tax-resident shareholders, and no capital gains tax on disposal of shares for foreign shareholders, provided the shareholder is not a Cyprus tax resident. Assessed risk for a non-resident-owned Cypriot low-code website-builder entity: Low to Medium, centred on substance documentation (registered office, Cyprus-based director/secretary, payroll records) rather than exchange restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; software-as-a-service export revenue (Stripe, Paddle settlements) may be retained in EUR or held in foreign currency accounts freely. In practice, the Central Bank of Cyprus requires BOP statistical reporting for cross-border transfers above thresholds set in CBC directives, and the 2015 EU Funds Transfer Regulation (Regulation 2015/847/EU) mandates complete payer/payee information in settlement instructions. The client should instruct the receiving bank with standard export-purpose tags (e.g., 'BENE', 'SERV' or 'DIGI' for digital services) and retain merchant settlement reports to demonstrate the cross-border service nature of proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), operating as the EU National Central Bank within the Eurosystem and the Single Supervisory Mechanism; it supervises Cyprus credit institutions, administers Balance of Payments statistical reporting, and implements ECB monetary policy without maintaining discretionary exchange control powers in normal circumstances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium — for settlement-top-up and operational disbursements only; not recommended for capital escrow. Cyprus licensed EMIs and payment institutions under PSD2 (transposed via the Electronic Money Law of 2012 and Payment Services Law of 2018) can provide virtual IBANs and SEPA rails; however, offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Multitude, Transact Payment) face tighter onboarding for Cyprus corporate clients transacting with Stripe/Paddle because of the large volume of customer micro-transactions. Legal constraints: EMIs are not credit institutions, cannot hold client funds as deposits, must safeguard funds, and are subject to Cyprus Central Bank AML oversight; they generally reject or freeze accounts when a strong underlying invoice trail is absent. Recommended architecture: use an EMI for short-float marketing and software procurement expensed under EUR 50k/month, while maintaining the Bank of Cyprus or Ancoria account as the single treasury spine.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use an SWIFT MT103 with field 70 (Remittance Information) specifying 'CNCB Capital contribution' or, for inter-company treasury contribution, 'GROU Intra-group capital transfer'; include the unique 20-character TAN (transaction reference) and the Cyprus entity's IBAN. Beneficiary bank should be the Cypriot escrow account at a Tier-1 bank; ordering bank must verify the UBO/legal claimant name matches the account onboarded in the bank's KYB record — any mismatch will trigger a EU 2015/847 data-return and possible block. For capital contributions in EUR originating from outside the EU, the Cyprus bank may request: the share capital resolution and share register update, the Cypriot Company Registrar's initial UBO form (HE32), and an approved Central Bank of Cyprus statistical form for BOP recording where the transfer exceeds EUR 100k (or the current published threshold). If the injection is made via a loan rather than equity, the inbound routing must use 'BENE' or 'LOAN' purpose code and carry an executed inter-company loan agreement with arm's-length interest; prudent practice is to mirror the capital injection with a Cyprus CPA statement confirming the amount is not subject to any Cyprus exchange-control restriction.Primary outbound rail is SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) for EU/EEA contracts, executed via MT103 field 50F/59A, with purpose codes 'SERV' (service fees), 'DIGI' (digital software subscriptions), 'MARK' (marketing), or 'ROYA' (IP royalties) as applicable. Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: A Cyprus private limited company is exempt from a statutory audit only if it satisfies at least two of the following three conditions for the relevant financial year: balance-sheet total not exceeding EUR 350,000, net turnover not exceeding EUR 700,000, and average number of full-time employees not exceeding 10. Companies exceeding these thresholds, or members representing 10% of issued share capital requesting an audit, must obtain a statutory audit by a Cyprus-certified independent auditor.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual value per category (goods, services, financial transactions, and IP transactions) triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation. For non-EU related-party transactions with low-risk functions, a simplified Turkish-style form may not be accepted; Cyprus follows the OECD TP Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus requires economic substance in substance-over-form assessments, including central management and control, actual physical presence, qualified staff, real decision-making, and active oversight of core income-generating activities. The IP Box regime specifically mandates substantial activity as defined by the taxpayer's own R&D expenditure or qualified asset development. Cyprus does not apply a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code like UAE, but the Tax Department applies OECD BEPS Action 5 and substance requirements for treaty and EU directive access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution with fines up to €50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties include 100% to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest at 2% per annum and tax surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for tax debts if they acted negligently or fraudulently; civil restitution covers the underpaid corporate tax plus penalties. A travel ban may be imposed by the Cyprus Courts, and directors face disqualification orders and extradition risk.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Tax Department may disregard the entity, impose penalty tax at 20% of the asset value or transaction, and apply anti-abuse rules under the ATAD (Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive); the entity may be struck off and lose tax residence status.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face personal liability for false declarations; civil recovery actions may pierce the corporate veil, imposing joint and several liability for all corporate unpaid taxes, penalties, and costs; individuals involved may receive asset freezing orders and travel restrictions.
AML / UBO Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Cyprus AML Law imposes fines up to €50,000 per violation; natural persons face imprisonment up to 5 years. The regulator (CySEC) may suspend bank accounts, revoke any operating licenses, and report to the Cyprus Financial Intelligence Unit (MOKAS).

Director personal liability: Directors and UBOs may be personally liable for laundering proceeds; they may be subject to civil asset seizure, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment up to 5 years. Aggravated cases carry 10-year terms if the predicate offense involves organized crime.
Transfer Pricing Negligence

Statutory sanction: Audit adjustments with a 100% penalty on the additional tax liability plus interest; administrative fines for missing documentation up to €20,000; potential re-characterization of transactions under CFC rules.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false TP documentation are civilly liable for the entire underpaid tax; in egregious fraud cases, criminal exposure leads to imprisonment up to 3 years and director disqualification, and travel bans are issued to prevent departure during investigations.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office in Cyprus is mandatory under Section 107 of the Companies Law, Cap. 113. For a low-risk trade-remedy consulting company, a virtual office at a licensed corporate services provider (CSP) or serviced address in Cyprus is generally acceptable, provided it is a physical premises able to receive service of legal process and where the company's statutory books, registers (including the UBO register), and records are maintained. A P.O. Box alone is not sufficient. The use of a director's home office is legally permissible but weakens demonstrated economic substance and is not recommended.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation (Tμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; online portal: Ariadne Tax Portal (https://ariadne.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company must first complete incorporation with the Registrar of Companies, appoint a Cyprus-resident secretary and registered office, obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, then proceed to the District Tax Office to obtain a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN). The TIN application must be submitted via the prescribed registration form accompanied by the incorporation deed, memorandum/articles of association, proof of registered office, identification of directors/shareholders, and certified translations if not in Greek. Thereafter, for VAT purposes, the entity must file VAT Form TFR 1 with the VAT Service of the Department of Taxation once it makes taxable supplies in Cyprus or exceeds the registration threshold; foreign entities that are not established in Cyprus but acquire goods/services or make distance sales may be required to register for VAT via the direct electronic registration portal or appoint a tax representative under Article 32 of the Cyprus VAT Law.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident or foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company must first complete incorporation with the Registrar of Companies, appoint a Cyprus-resident secretary and registered office, obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, then proceed to the District Tax Office to obtain a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN). The TIN application must be submitted via the prescribed registration form accompanied by the incorporation deed, memorandum/articles of association, proof of registered office, identification of directors/shareholders, and certified translations if not in Greek. Thereafter, for VAT purposes, the entity must file VAT Form TFR 1 with the VAT Service of the Department of Taxation once it makes taxable supplies in Cyprus or exceeds the registration threshold; foreign entities that are not established in Cyprus but acquire goods/services or make distance sales may be required to register for VAT via the direct electronic registration portal or appoint a tax representative under Article 32 of the Cyprus VAT Law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections under the Cyprus Income Tax Law include: election for group relief/group taxation on 90%+ subsidiary relationships; election to apply the Intellectual Property (IP) box regime for qualifying intangible assets; election for tonnage tax in respect of qualifying shipping activities; election for notional interest deduction on new equity; and elections under the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory/disallowed rather than elective. No discretionary check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists for Cyprus resident companies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for VAT zero-rating under Section 30 of the Cyprus VAT Law where the goods are physically dispatched or transported from Cyprus to a destination outside the European Union, provided that the supplier holds and retains documentary evidence of exportation. Services rendered to non-EU recipients or services directly linked to export activities (e.g., transportation, freight forwarding, or intermediary services provided to non-EU clients) may be zero-rated under the place-of-supply rules and anti-abuse conditions. The taxpayer must demonstrate that the goods have left Cyprus and the EU within the legally prescribed period, the recipient is resident outside the EU, and the transaction is genuine commercial export. Services supplied to businesses established outside the EU are generally treated as supplied where the recipient belongs, and may be zero-rated if not caught by the reverse charge or domestic reverse charge rules for electronically supplied services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Export Declaration via the Cyprus Customs Clearing System)
  • Bill of Lading (for sea freight) or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign exchange credit evidence
  • Commercial Invoice (final and in foreign currency)
  • Packing List specifying weight, value, and destination
  • Original contract or purchase order from the foreign buyer
  • Customs export movement certificate or proof of exit from the EU
  • Proof of foreign currency repatriation to Cyprus or documented forex account records

Cyprus has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for domestic B2B e-invoicing. E-invoicing is permitted provided authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility are guaranteed by the supplier, consistent with the EU VAT Directive. Cyprus has implemented EU standard e-invoicing for public procurement (Directive 2014/55/EU) via the PEPPOL network, but no general mandatory API-based continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance mechanism is in force as of the current reporting date. Suppliers issuing continuous supply statements or electronic invoices must retain an audit trail in accordance with Article 25 of the Cyprus VAT Rules.

The Cyprus Tax Department and Customs and Excise Department may retrospectively claw back zero-rated VAT, impose interest at 10% per annum for late tax, levy administrative penalties of up to €10,000 for inaccurate returns or documentation failures, and apply additional penalties for negligent or fraudulent export claims. Under Section 34 of the Cyprus VAT Law, fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute a criminal offense punishable by fines and imprisonment for responsible officers; directors and managing officers are jointly and severally liable. Customs Code (EU Regulation 952/2013) penalties also apply for false export declarations or attempted export refund abuse. Interest and penalties are calculated from the date the export zero-rating benefit was claimed, and retrospective clawback may extend for the full statutory limitation period (generally six years, extended in cases of fraud or deliberate concealment).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus has used the euro as legal tender since 1 January 2008; both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully liberalised under Article 63 TFEU. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, shareholder-loan repayment and management-fee outflow are permitted without exchange-authorisation or conversion licence, and Cyprus does not impose domestic withholding tax on dividends or interest paid to EU/non-resident corporate shareholders. Practical hurdles are limited to EU/UN restrictive-measures screening, AML/CFT source-of-funds verification at the banking layer, and the Central Bank of Cyprus statutory balance-of-payments (BoP) statistical reporting obligation for all material cross-border settlement entries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation exists. Cyprus adheres to IMF Article VIII and, as a eurozone member, imposes neither a mandatory conversion ratio nor a statutory repatriation timeline on export receipts or consulting-fee inflows. The effective conversion ratio is 0% (nil) and export/service proceeds may be retained indefinitely in EUR or any foreign currency. The only regulatory duty is statistical: resident entities must file cross-border settlement and export-related transactions in the CBC BoP reporting portal, retaining underlying invoices, transport documents and contracts for verification.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου / Central Bank of Cyprus), operating within the European System of Central Banks; euro and exchange-rate policy sits with the European Central Bank (ECB) as part of the Eurosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Cyprus-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) under the Electronic Money Law L.125(I)/2012 and supervised by the Central Bank of Cyprus are legally permitted to provide virtual IBANs, SEPA CT/Instant processing and EU-wide passporting; such entities may serve as a secondary treasury rail for small retainers and expense payments. Offshore, non-EEA digital EMIs present material legal constraints: they lack a physical Cyprus/EU licence, are subject to correspondent-bank de-risking, and cannot issue commercial LCs, committed credit or deliverable FX products. Consequently, an offshore EMI is unsuitable for client-escrow capital and primary anti-dumping case disbursements; all fiduciary funds must clear through a licensed Cypriot credit institution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold for mandatory Local File or Master File has been published by Cyprus; the section 33(1) Income Tax Law self-review obligation applies to all controlled transactions on an arm's length basis. In practice, the Cyprus Tax Department expects contemporaneous Local File documentation when intercompany controlled transactions exceed EUR 750,000, and an OECD-aligned Master File when the worldwide group turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000; documentation must be ready within 30 days of a Tax Department request.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not have a codified Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to UAE / BCI regimes; however, the corporate residence doctrine under section 2 of the Income Tax Law ('management and control'), the EU ATAD/CFC rules, and the IP Box Nexus requirements jointly impose a substantive activity test. A CY Ltd engaged in anti-dumping trade-defense consulting must demonstrate local board meetings in Cyprus, a physical office, professional staff with real functional profiles, and independent decision-making. A letterbox company will be denied treaty benefits, a TRC, and the 12.5% headline rate, with profits re-attributed to the substantive foreign manager.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction leads to a fine of up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment for up to three years under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law and the Income Tax Law; civil re-assessment allows the Commissioner to estimate tax and apply a 5% special surcharge, 1.5% monthly interest, and an additional penalty up to 100% of the tax understated (double assessment).

Director personal liability: Cypriot courts pierce the corporate veil under the Companies Law (Cap. 113) for fraudulent trading; directors may be ordered to discharge the company's tax liabilities with compound interest, incur travel bans, and face personal asset seizure upon conviction.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Tax Department may recharacterize the vehicle as tax-transparent, deny the 12.5% rate by attributing profits to the foreign place of effective management, and refuse the TRC for treaty relief; under EU ATAD, CFC rules may reallocate passive fee income to the ultimate parent at that parent's full statutory rate.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who sign documents without substance face disqualification orders, misfeasance liability, and claw-back actions in winding-up; in serious treaty-abuse cases the Tax Department files a report with MOKAS (Cyprus FIU) against the individual officers.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may adjust consideration to the arm's length value, deny deductibility of the excess, impose a penalty of up to 20% of the adjusted profit, and apply 1.5% monthly interest; persistent manipulation triggers criminal assessments and publication of the entity in the Tax Department's adverse decision register.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve unilateral pricing arrangements or aggressive markups without economic documentation become personally liable for the tax and penalties under misfeasance; repeated violations attract a five-year debarment from acting as a director.
Anti-Money Laundering and Wire Transfer Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (2007), conviction for money laundering carries imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000; failure to apply CDD or file a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) with MOKAS incurs administrative penalties of up to EUR 100,000 per violation.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated Compliance Officer are individually prosecuted; personal accounts are frozen, directors face travel bans, and asset confiscation proceeds under Part IV of the AML Law; failing to file an STR carries independent criminal liability even where the underlying payment was later found lawful.
Anti-Dumping Trade-Defense Fraud and EU Customs Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under EU Anti-Dumping Regulation 2016/1036 and the Union Customs Code, the Commission may draw adverse inferences (Article 18), retroactively assess duties, and impose penalties of up to three times the evaded duties; Cyprus courts enforce customs fines of up to EUR 30,000 per false declaration and confiscation of the smuggled goods.

Director personal liability: Under the 'director as the mind of the company' doctrine, directors who sign false certificates face prosecution for customs fraud with imprisonment of up to five years, travel bans, and civil restitution to injured EU producers in follow-on damages actions; commission officials may also refer directors for extradition proceedings.

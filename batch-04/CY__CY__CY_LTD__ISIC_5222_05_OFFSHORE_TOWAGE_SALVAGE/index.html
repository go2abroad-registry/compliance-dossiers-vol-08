Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; official portal: https://taxdep.mof.gov.cy; electronic filing portal 'Ariadne': https://ariadne.taxdep.mof.gov.cy

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity seeking a Cyprus tax identification number (TIN) must first register a branch or permanent establishment with the Registrar of Companies, appoint a Cyprus tax agent (typically a certified public accountant), and file the corporate registration application with the Department of Taxation. The application for a TIN must include notarised and translated constitutional documents of the parent company, evidence of the local branch/PE, and, where VAT registration is sought, a separate VAT (Form VAT 101) application. Upon approval, the TIN is issued and, if applicable, the same number functions as the VAT identification number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity seeking a Cyprus tax identification number (TIN) must first register a branch or permanent establishment with the Registrar of Companies, appoint a Cyprus tax agent (typically a certified public accountant), and file the corporate registration application with the Department of Taxation. The application for a TIN must include notarised and translated constitutional documents of the parent company, evidence of the local branch/PE, and, where VAT registration is sought, a separate VAT (Form VAT 101) application. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus has no check-the-box election; legal-form classification is definitive. A private limited company is automatically a taxable entity subject to corporation tax. Available elections include: group loss relief election between a Cyprus parent and its 75% subsidiaries; VAT group registration under the Cyprus VAT Law; election for the Cypriot-flag tonnage tax regime for qualifying ship owning/operating companies; and notional interest deduction election on new equity capital. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory under the EU ATAD transposition with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 (Euro) annual taxable turnover within Cyprus

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for goods applies to exports outside the EU and to intra-Community supplies to VAT-registered counterparties in another Member State. For offshore towage and salvage services, zero-rating is available under the special EU maritime regime where the service is directly related to a vessel engaged in international sea navigation and the vessel physically proceeds on international voyages; otherwise, business-to-business services supplied to a customer established outside Cyprus are outside the scope of Cyprus VAT and fall under the reverse-charge/disaster mechanism of the customer's country.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document – SAD) evidencing actual departure from Cyprus
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill naming the exporter and indicating the port of destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer proof showing payment in the invoiced currency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the quantity and nature of the goods or services
  • Supporting evidence of the vessel's international voyage status, such as sailing schedules, port clearance, or maritime log extracts

Cyprus does not currently mandate real-time continuous transaction control, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping. Electronic VAT returns (Form VAT 100/101) must be filed through the Ariadne portal. E-invoicing is permissible and follows EU interoperability standards, but remains voluntary for domestic business-to-business transactions as of the date of this dossier.

Any false claim for export zero-rating or refunds may result in the assessment of VAT at 19% plus statutory interest at 1.5% per month or part thereof, a penalty of up to 200% of the tax under-assessed, corporate and personal criminal fines up to EUR 10,000, and imprisonment for officers for up to two years. Retrospective assessment periods extend to five years for ordinary error and beyond for fraud or wilful misdeclaration; deliberate export misconduct may also trigger seizure of goods or vessels and loss of the exporter's VAT registration status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a Eurozone member state; the euro (EUR) is fully convertible on both current and capital account under Articles 63-65 TFEU. No exchange-control legislation remains operative; the former Exchange Control Law (Cap. 97) has been fully liberalised. Profit and dividend remittance to non-Cyprus shareholders is unrestricted for treasury purposes, subject only to corporate law solvency tests, audited financial statements, CBC/NTA tax-compliant no-objection procedures and applicable special defence contribution withholding at source. Inbound and outbound capital movements require no CBC approval; only standard bank-level purpose-coding, source-of-funds and sanctions screening apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no forced conversion ratio and no repatriation deadline for export proceeds or marine salvage receivables. Towage and salvage income invoiced in EUR, USD, GBP or other convertible currencies may be held indefinitely in offshore or onshore foreign-currency accounts. The temporary emergency capital restrictions introduced in March 2013 were fully abolished in April 2015 and are of historical relevance only; no residual restriction affects current USD/GBP or EUR clearing.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC) (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου); competent monetary, financial-stability and bank-licensing authority under the Central Bank of Cyprus Laws 2002-2023, the Business of Credit Institutions Law (Law 66(I)/1997), the Electronic Money Law (Law 81(I)/2012) and the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (Law 188(I)/2007, as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low. CBC-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and EU/EEA EMIs passporting into Cyprus under Law 81(I)/2012 may provide e-money account rails for routine subcontractor reimbursements and FX conversions, but are not viable for the core treasury function of this ISIC 5222 profile: they cannot grant credit, maintain full nostro USD/GBP clearing depth for Lloyds Standard Form wires, or host structured escrow accounts that satisfy Lloyd's salvage security and P&I club letter-of-undertaking requirements. The Medium-Risk_Enhanced banking AML tier further discourages EMIs from taking on marine casualty counterparties; use EMIs strictly for low-value ancillary expenses, never for salvage award receipts or escrow.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders; Cyprus imposes no domestic withholding tax on outbound dividends. A Special Defence Contribution (SDC) of 17% applies only where the beneficial owner of the dividend is a Cyprus tax resident.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before the last day of the sixth month following the end of the accounting period (e.g., a 31 December year-end is due by 30 June of the following year); the Commissioner may grant published extensions in specific years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual controlled transactions with associated enterprises triggers the mandatory Local File; Master File is required where the MNE group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Small and medium-sized enterprises (fewer than 250 employees and either turnover not exceeding EUR 50,000,000 or balance sheet total not exceeding EUR 43,000,000) are exempt from documentation but remain subject to the arm's-length principle. Rules apply to tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2023.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations code but enforces the substance-over-form and management-and-control doctrines: board meetings, strategic towage/salvage contracting decisions, fleet management, and bank accounts must be located in Cyprus for tax residence. The Cyprus Tonnage Tax regime requires that at least 60% of qualifying ship-management operations be conducted in Cyprus. The General Anti-Abuse Rule (Section 89 of the Income Tax Law) and EU ATAD anti-abuse measures permit the Commissioner to disregard artificial arrangements lacking genuine economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns (Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law L.4/1978)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction with a fine of up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years; civil reassessment with a penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded plus statutory interest; the tax authority may reopen assessments for 6 years, or without time limit in cases of fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign or cause false returns are personally prosecuted; travel bans and asset-freezing orders may be imposed on outstanding assessments; fraudulent trading claims in insolvency expose directors to personal restitution and disqualification.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure under GAAR

Statutory sanction: Application of Section 89 GAAR to disregard the tax-motivated arrangement; reassessment of profits under the 12.5% rate with penalties of up to 100% of the tax due; denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; strike-off and deregistration by the Registrar of Companies.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the company is a mere façade; directors become personally liable for unpaid corporate tax, court-ordered civil restitution, director disqualification orders, and claw-back of distributions made without genuine substance.
AML and Maritime Sanctions Violations (L.188(I)/2007 and EU Sanctions Regulations)

Statutory sanction: Investigation by the Unit for Combating Money Laundering (MOKAS); criminal fines of up to EUR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering; freezing of bank accounts, confiscation of vessels, and revocation of the statutory maritime towage licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and the appointed AML compliance officer face personal criminal prosecution, travel bans, confiscation of personal and corporate assets, and permanent exclusion from shipping industry licensing; civil liability extends to third-party losses from sanctions breaches.
Improper Profit Repatriation and Hidden Dividend Distribution

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing recharacterization of the payments as non-deductible distributions; assessment of 17% SDC on the gross distribution, combined with penalties and interest; denial of corporate deduction at 12.5% for disguised distributions; assessments raised within 6 years of the year of assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign unlawful distribution resolutions are jointly and severally liable for unpaid SDC and penalties; personal recourse exists for dividends paid while the company is insolvent; on liquidation, fraudulent preference and contribution orders can be made against directors.

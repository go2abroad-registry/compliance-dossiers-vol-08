Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for USt-IdNr. allocation; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for Steuernummer, corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT returns; principal filing portal ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the GmbH before a Notar and register it in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) of the competent Amtsgericht. 2. File the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER or in paper form with the local Finanzamt, which assigns the corporate tax/trade tax/VAT Steuernummer. 3. Where required for intra-Community or export transactions, apply to the BZSt using the official application form for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). Non-resident investors acting through a German permanent establishment or direct local supplies follow the same registration path; foreign entrepreneurs without a domestic establishment must register for VAT under Sections 18 and 27 UStG.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime is available; a GmbH is per se a corporate entity subject to corporation tax, trade tax and VAT. The principal election mechanism is the tax group/consolidation (Organschaft) under Sections 14, 17 KStG and Section 2 UStG, requiring financial, economic and organizational integration with a controlling parent. Hybrid-mismatch elections are restricted under German and EU-ATAD rules, notably Section 4k EStG disallowing mismatched deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for VAT liability: any entrepreneur supplying goods or services in Germany must register. Small-business exemption applies up to EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 projected current-year turnover; intra-EU distance-sales threshold is EUR 10,000 if the OSS option is not used.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to third-country destinations are zero-rated under Section 6 UStG provided the goods physically leave the German customs territory and the exporter holds verifiable documentary evidence of the export. The zero rate also applies if the exporter demonstrably delivers or ships the goods abroad; no VAT is charged on the export invoice. B2B services supplied to non-EU entrepreneurs are generally outside the scope of German VAT under the place-of-supply rule of Section 3a UStG, making them effectively zero-rated if the recipient is identifiable as an entrepreneur in the other jurisdiction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Zollanmeldung) / Export Accompanying Document (ABD)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note
  • Commercial Invoice / Pro forma Invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / verifiable bank payment receipt

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing for domestic B2B supplies: from 1 January 2025 taxpayers must be able to receive EN 16931-compliant e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD); issuance is mandatory from 1 January 2027 for taxpayers with turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all taxable persons. Transmission may occur via PEPPOL, e-mail or other channels; Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, live API tax-server authorization, or digital stamping.

Fraudulent or substantively deficient export zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to retrospective VAT assessments and clawback of the exempted VAT, plus interest of 6% per annum under Sections 169 and 233a AO. Intentional evasion is punishable under Section 370 AO with imprisonment of up to 10 years, fines, and professional disqualification of directors; negligent irregularity under Section 378 AO carries fines up to EUR 50,000 plus assessed back-duties. The Finanzamt may initiate escalated desk or field audits and refer the file to the public prosecutor for criminal tax proceedings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions under Article 63 TFEU (free movement of capital); Germany maintains no exchange-control, prior-authorization, forced-conversion, or minimum-holding regime. Profit remittance (dividend distribution) by a GmbH is unrestricted subject to German corporate-law capital maintenance (Section 30 GmbHG), the annual financial-statement approval process, and statutory withholding tax of 25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax, reducible to the applicable treaty rate under the relevant double taxation agreement and reclaimable where the treaty provides. No material hurdle exists for inbound equity or intercompany financing beyond standard KYC, AWV statistical reporting, and corporate-substance safeguards.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory forex surrender, conversion ratio, or mandatory repatriation timeline applies to export receipts. A purely statistical reporting obligation arises under Section 67 AWV: residents must report all cross-border payments (including export proceeds) of EUR 12,500 or more, or the equivalent, to the Deutsche Bundesbank by the fifth working day of the month following the month in which the payment was made or received, for balance-of-payments statistics. Receipts may be retained indefinitely in any convertible currency and used freely; no conversion into euro is mandated.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany), acting jointly with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for licensed-banking supervision and as the implementing authority for statistical cross-border payment reporting under Section 67 of the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The European Central Bank (ECB) holds ultimate monetary and foreign-reserve policy competence within the Eurosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium viability, structurally limited to settlement-only and disbursement-support rails. BaFin-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG, transposing PSD2), and EEA-passported EMIs, may provide multi-currency virtual IBANs, low-cost spot FX conversion, and SWIFT-receivable collection accounts while holding segregated client funds. They are not legally permitted to operate the deposit-taking business (Einlagengeschäft) or grant commercial credit; they are not members of the German statutory deposit-protection fund (Entschädigungseinrichtung deutscher Banken); and their correspondent reachability to US, Chinese, and other high-volume corridors is frequently dependent on sponsor-bank restrictions. A legally prudent blueprint is a tier-1 German bank as the primary treasury and escrow principal account, with the EMI rails used only for supplier disbursements and collection aggregation in Asia-Pacific corridors.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For nominal euro share-capital contributions (Stammkapital), route a SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the German principal bank using the beneficiary IBAN of the capital escrow account and a remittance reference reading 'Einzahlung Stammkapital laut notariellem Gesellschaftsvertrag vom [date]'; for shareholder loans, reference 'Gesellschafterdarlehen Nr. [X] gemäß Darlehensvertrag vom [date]'. The German bank and the register court require the notarized deed, a deposit confirmation on the capital escrow account, and the managing director's declaration that the funds are at his free disposal (Section 8(3) GmbHG); funds may be transferred from escrow to the operational account only after Handelsregister registration. For in-kind contributions denominated in foreign currency, a bank rate certificate (Kursbescheinigung) evidences the euro conversion for share-capital disclosure. All payments exceeding EUR 12,500 automatically trigger the bank's AWV Section 67 statistical report to the Deutsche Bundesbank; no exchange-control approval is required, and USD or CNY amounts may be converted freely at spot markets.Preferred clearing rails for the electric cargo e-bike OEM: (i) SEPA Credit Transfers for all EU/EEA suppliers, freight forwarders, and payroll-linked disbursements settled in euro; (ii) SWIFT MT103 direct supplier telegraphic transfers in USD, CNY, or JPY with invoice cross-reference in the remittance information field, routed through the tier-1 German bank's international wire and correspondent network; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700 issued by the German bank under UCP 600, ICC Publication No. 600) for high-value inbound shipments from Asian OEM suppliers—irrevocable at sight, with the German bank as issuing bank and Asian correspondent banks as confirming or negotiating banks; and (iv) Standby Letters of Credit (MT760) for warranty and performance-security obligations to distributors and institutional fleet purchasers. Import VAT and customs duties are settled via the operational account under the deferred-payment customs procedure.

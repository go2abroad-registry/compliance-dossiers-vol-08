Competent corporate registry

Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DROCOR), Ministry of Energy, Commerce and Industry, Nicosia, Republic of Cyprus.

Governing statute

Cyprus Companies Law, Cap. 113 (ss. 3, 6, 7, 12, 17, 106, 170) as amended by the Companies (Amendment) Law 95(I)/2015; Companies (Registration) Regulations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum and Articles of Association, submitted together with Application for Registration (Form HE1) and statutory declaration of compliance.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 36 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Cap. 113; the Company may operate with €1 issued share capital (1 ordinary share at par). No escrow account is legally required; however, the subscriber consideration must be credited to the Company's bank account at incorporation, and banks may impose internal blocked-account procedures pending receipt of the deposit.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Cyprus Fire Service / Civil Defence for the leased offices; structural load inspection certified by the District Surveyors office for the occupied floor area; occupational health clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Law 89(I)/1996, including first-aid equipment, electrical installation inspection, and emergency exit signage.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Cap. 113; the Company may operate with €1 issued share capital (1 ordinary share at par). Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; online filing portal: Tax Portal (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-controlled private limited company must first be incorporated or registered under the Cyprus Companies Law (Cap. 113), then apply for a Tax Identification Code (TIC / Tax Registration Number) through Form TD101 filed with the District Tax Office. Supporting documents: certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, registered office address, director and shareholder details, notarised identity documents, evidence of the ultimate beneficial owner, and, if already registered elsewhere, the foreign corporate TIN. VAT registration is obtained separately through Form TD201 when the entity supplies taxable goods or services in Cyprus or elects VAT registration. For non-resident sellers of digital services, registration for the EU One Stop Shop (OSS) may be required instead of local VAT registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation for Cyprus tax-resident private limited companies (taxed on worldwide income at 12.5%), Territorial_Scope for non-resident companies (taxed only on Cyprus-sourced income). Cyprus does not recognise pass-through classification for a Ltd; the company is an autonomous tax subject, not a qualifying pass-through entity under Cyprus tax law. — A foreign-controlled private limited company must first be incorporated or registered under the Cyprus Companies Law (Cap. 113), then apply for a Tax Identification Code (TIC / Tax Registration Number) through Form TD101 filed with the District Tax Office. Supporting documents: certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, registered office address, director and shareholder details, notarised identity documents, evidence of the ultimate beneficial owner, and, if already registered elsewhere, the foreign corporate TIN. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation for Cyprus tax-resident private limited companies (taxed on worldwide income at 12.5%), Territorial_Scope for non-resident companies (taxed only on Cyprus-sourced income). Cyprus does not recognise pass-through classification for a Ltd; the company is an autonomous tax subject, not a qualifying pass-through entity under Cyprus tax law.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Cyprus tax law; classification follows legal incorporation and central management and control. Available statutory elections under Cypriot corporate tax law include: group relief election (loss surrender within 75% group companies), VAT grouping election under Article 12 of the VAT Law, fixed-rate corporate income tax elections for shipping under tonnage tax, notional interest deduction (NID) on new equity capital, intangible asset (IP Box) partial deduction, and a deemed dividend distribution election under the Special Defence Contribution rules. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory under ATAD and apply to Cyprus-resident and foreign branches; no elective opt-out for hybrid mismatches is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 taxable turnover during a rolling twelve-month period for supplies with right of deduction; EUR 10,000 for intra-Community distance sales of digital services under the OSS regime. A non-resident entity with no Cyprus establishment is generally required to register from the first supply of taxable services/goods in Cyprus, as the threshold does not extend to non-resident suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cyprus zero-rates direct exports of physical goods outside the EU, including goods dispatched to persons in non-EU territories, subject to the exporter retaining valid dispatchexportation evidence. Supplies of services to non-EU business and non-EU private customers are generally outside the scope of Cyprus VAT (place of supply rules), and thus no VAT is chargeable; for services performed from Cyprus to a non-EU customer, the supplier may zero-rate the supply under the due-provision rules provided the customer’s non-EU status and receipt are properly evidenced. For app-store intermediates, WebNovel subscription sales through Apple IAP/Google Play to EU buyers are taxed in the customer’s EU member state via OSS, not in Cyprus, unless the Cyprus company makes other local supplies; zero-rating remains applicable to direct export transactions for physical goods and clearly excluded non-EU digital services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document – SAD, Customs Form EX)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill / CMR (for land transport)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank transfer statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Commercial invoice / proforma invoice matching described goods and value
  • Packing list and certified weight/measurement certificates
  • Proof of physical departure from the EU (port/airport loading evidence, electronic export status via Cyprus Customs EDI system)
  • Delivery receipt from an overseas customer and distribution warehouse agreements where applicable
  • Customs transit documents (T1, T2L), if applicable in EU transits

Cyprus does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance or national e-invoicing clearance (no continuous transaction control / CTC platform). VAT-registered taxpayers must submit monthly/quarterly VAT returns electronically via the Tax Portal, and intra-Community traders must file VIES EC Sales Lists. Invoices may be issued electronically in PDF or structured XML, but must satisfy VAT law authenticity and integrity requirements. Cyprus is aligned with the EU electronic invoicing standard (EN 16931) for e-procurement and is monitoring the EU VAT in the Digital Age consultative process, but no mandatory B2B e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping regime is in force as of the current date.

For exported goods and zero-rated services, the Tax Department will recover declared zero-rate VAT together with statutory interest and impose administrative penalties up to 100% of evaded tax where the local exporter fails to produce satisfactory documentary evidence within the audit window. Criminal prosecution is possible under the Cyprus VAT Law (Law 95(I)/2000) for fraudulent claims in seeking an unlawful VAT refund. Late or incomplete customs documentation can result in disqualification of the zero-rating, immediate assessment at 19% Cyprus VAT, and clawback of any repaid input VAT. The statute of limitation for VAT audits in Cyprus is six years from the end of the tax year/assessment period, though it may extend to ten years in cases of fraud, wilful concealment, or unfulfilled substantive document retention obligations.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Cyprus applies the EU acquis on the free movement of capital; no general exchange control restrictions exist. Direct investment, operational remittances, dividend distributions, and profit repatriations are freely permitted, subject only to standard tax reporting and beneficial ownership declarations. No approval requirement applies to profit repatriation or capital repatriation by non-resident shareholders, provided the origin of funds is compliant with AML/CFT rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion, force-sale, or mandatory repatriation timeline applies to export receipts under Cypriot law. Receipts from digital publishing services and subscription platform revenues may be held in Euro or any foreign currency, retained offshore, or settled as needed. Banks will classify the transaction under the EU Balance of Payments (BoP) statistical reporting code for services; no mandatory conversion ratio or binding settlement period is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC), operating as the national competent authority within the European System of Central Banks and the Eurosystem

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Cyprus-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) supervised by the Central Bank of Cyprus may be used for e-money wallets and payment processing, with a lighter onboarding burden. Offshore EU-authorised EMIs may operate in Cyprus through the EU single passport under the E-Money Directive and the Revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2). Practical constraints include: lengthy EDD review for non-resident corporate clients, limited high-value cross-border treasury services, and possible daily transaction caps. The use of offshore digital EMIs without passporting is not permissible for regulated activity in Cyprus; only passported EU EMIs may provide services. For the target capital escrow and revenue collection treasury function, a tier-1 commercial bank account remains the recommended primary rail; EMIs may be deployed as sub-account receivables, not as the primary bank counterparty.

Defense evidence: Provide audited financial statements, proof of payroll and social insurance filings, leases for physical office space or serviced offices, managerial board minutes, signed service agreements with authors, platform traffic and subscriber reporting, and a consolidated group structure chart to evidence substance.

Defense evidence: Provide board-approved share capital allocation minutes, proof of funding via SWIFT MT103 from a verifiable bank account of the ultimate beneficial owner, tax clearance certificate from the UBO's residence country, currency exchange receipts if applicable, and a detailed source-of-wealth narrative with supporting asset documentation.

Root cause: Exposure to sanctions risk due to end-users or counterparties in restricted territories for the webnovel platform

Defense evidence: Implement geo-IP blocking against sanctioned jurisdictions, provide an automated sanctions screening log, maintain a current list of blocked territories, and present a robust know-your-customer (KYC) procedure for content licensors and supply-chain partners, together with company anti-sanctions compliance policies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital injections must be executed via authorised Cyprus credit institutions using a standard SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Euro wire. The recommended transaction narrative must include: entity name, capital subscription reference, and the BOPS code 'Direct investment - Equity capital' (negative BoP entry). The sending bank must verify the origin of funds from the shareholder's own account; the receiving bank requires a board resolution approving the share issuance and a completed UBO and source-of-funds declaration. No central bank exchange control approval is required, but the bank may request the statutory Form HE32 (return of allotments) and the share premium resolution to evidence the legal validity of the inward capital contribution. Structured as a formal subscription into registered share capital or an intercompany subordinated loan, the clearing rail must be carried out in Euro to avoid any FX mismatch; if the investor's capital originates in a non-EUR currency, the sending bank must effect the conversion at the prevailing market rate and disclose the currency conversion receipt to the receiving bank.For author royalties, content licensing fees, third-party marketing payments, and platform infrastructure settlements, the primary clearing rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 for wire transfers in Euro or major currencies; (b) commercial letters of credit (LC) only for large infrastructure or content acquisition contracts where deferred payment or documentary compliance is required; (c) SEPA Credit Transfer or Instant SEPA Payment for Euro-denominated periodic disbursements to EU payees; and (d) Apple In-App Purchase / Google Play Billing settlement through the app stores, settling via respective bank remittances under revenue-sharing merchant agreements. The treasury policy should maintain segregated counterparties for high-volume author payments to prevent accidental sanctions hits; each outbound wire must include the appropriate transaction purpose code per the ECB Balance of Payments manual, such as 'Trade in services', 'Licences to reproduce and/or distribute software/creative content', or 'Royalties'. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 for goods; EUR 250,000 for services; EUR 100,000 for interest; EUR 100,000 for royalties – local file required if any threshold exceeded.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to relevant activities (e.g., IP, financing, leasing). For a web novel platform, substance requirements include adequate staff, premises, and management in Cyprus to avoid being classified as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 100% of tax underpaid, criminal prosecution, imprisonment up to 5 years, and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for fraudulent returns; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax benefits, reclassification of income, penalties up to EUR 50,000, and potential strike-off.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for abuse of corporate law; personal fines and disqualification.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profits, penalties up to EUR 50,000, and interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for negligence in oversight; personal fines and potential criminal charges.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 500,000, imprisonment up to 5 years, and revocation of licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors can be criminally liable for AML breaches; personal fines, travel bans, and imprisonment.

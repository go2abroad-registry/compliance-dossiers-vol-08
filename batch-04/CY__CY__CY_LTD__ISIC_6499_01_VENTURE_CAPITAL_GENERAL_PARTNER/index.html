Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation of the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Central Services in Nicosia; official e-filing and taxpayer services portal 'Ariadni' and 'Tax For All' (https://taxforall.mof.gov.cy); VAT registration and VAT administration are performed by the VAT Service (Υπηρεσία ΦΠΑ) within the same Department, District VAT Offices (Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca, Paphos, Famagusta).

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor forming a Cyprus Private Limited Company (Ltd, ELF code HE77) must first incorporate at the Department of Registrar of Companies and register the Cypriot registered office address. Immediately following incorporation, the entity must apply to the Cyprus Tax Department for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) by submitting the incorporation certificate, certificate of registered office, memorandum and articles of association, and the completed Form TD2001; the TIN is issued electronically within a short administrative window. For VAT purposes, the entity must submit Form VAT 103 (Application for VAT Registration) when taxable supplies exceed EUR 15,600 per annum, when acquiring goods or services intra-Community exceeding the acquisition threshold, or voluntarily where the GP is expected to provide B2B management and deal-sourcing services to non-Cypriot funds. A non-resident foreign company may alternatively register a Cyprus branch (overseas company registration under the Companies Law) for tax purposes, obtaining a separate Cyprus VAT number and TIN. No minimum capital requirement applies to the Cyprus Ltd, and full non-resident ownership is permissible, this being a common special-purpose vehicle for VC/PE general partner management companies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box or hybrid entity election. Statutory elections and regimes available under the Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002) and VAT Law (Law 262(I)/2000), relevant to a Venture Capital General Partner Ltd, include: (a) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital introduced after 1 January 2015; (b) Qualifying Intellectual Property regime election (Nexus-compliant 80% cost substitution, exempting 80% of qualifying IP-derived income); (c) VAT grouping election (Art. 18 VAT Law) enabling the GP to join the fund's other Cyprus entities into a single VAT group for management-fee flows; (d) option to tax real estate for VAT (waiver of exemption); (e) group loss relief election allowing surrender of tax losses among Cyprus-resident group companies; (f) foreign tax credit election for foreign withholding taxes on cross-border carried interest or dividend/interest income; (g) non-domicile election for Cyprus-income-exempt shareholders receiving dividend and interest income (limited to natural-person principals); (h) tonnage tax regime (not applicable to VC GP) and shipping management exemptions (non-applicable). ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules under Cyprus law apply autonomously and deny deductions for hybrid payments without any elective mechanism. A VC GP with an incentive-fee and carried-interest structure will usually elect standard chargeable-income treatment at the 12.5% corporate rate, claiming participation- and NID-related reliefs, while management fees are VAT-able services except where zero-rating applies under the export provisions for services supplied to fund entities established outside Cyprus.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for supplies of goods and services made in Cyprus by a Cyprus-established business; EUR 10,000 for intra-EU distance sales of goods and electronically supplied services, which may only be used as a special threshold if the taxpayer is not established in the member state of consumption under the One Stop Shop (OSS) scheme; separate registration obligations under Article 6(3) of the VAT Law for intra-Community acquisitions of goods where annual acquisitions exceed EUR 15,600.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% applies to (i) the supply of goods dispatched to a destination outside the EU and physically exported from Cyprus under the Single Administrative Document (SAD) or combined transport documents, provided the goods leave the EU territory and the supplier has reliable evidence of export within the statutory deadline; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered customer in another member state (sale of the fund management software or IP modules are excluded by default); (iii) international services rendered to customers established outside the EU, offshore services pertaining to goods outside Cyprus, and services related to the fund's cross-border activity where place of supply rules move taxation abroad; and (iv) supplies to a taxable-reseller (TOGC/EC Sales List). For the VC GP, zero-rating is thus available for: management fees and deal-arrangement fees where the fund entity is established outside Cyprus (e.g., UAE, Cayman, USA), carried-interest related consulting services used and enjoyed outside the EU, and provision of investor-reporting and deal-diligence services to non-Cypriot funds, but not for management fees charged to Cyprus-established AIF or AIFLNP fund vehicles, which attract 19% VAT unless the AIF itself is outside Cyprus. Substantiation rules require the supplier to hold valid VAT number evidence for EU customers, to state '0% VAT – Export/Intra-Community Supply' on commercial VAT invoices, and to retain proof of physical exportation of goods such as customs stamp, Electronic Administrative Document exit proof, or supporting airway/bill-of-lading data; export zero-rating is strictly refused absent timely and consistent documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document — SAD/Form ExS) with exit confirmation from the customs office of export and proof of physical departure from EU territory
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / CMR for road transport) evidencing dispatch of the goods to a destination outside the EU, mentioning the GP or its authorised agent as the exporter
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List corresponding exactly to the declared exported goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or irrevocable payment and bank transfer evidence showing the export proceeds and the foreign customer as remitter, necessary to prove the 0% supply and to satisfy the Cyprus Central Bank and tax authorities in any export refund or audit procedure
  • For services zero-rated as offshore: a formal signed contract with the non-resident fund customer, documentary proof of the customer's establishment (trade-registry extract or jurisdiction-of-residence certificate), evidence that the place of supply of services is outside Cyprus, and internal work papers documenting where deal-management, screening, monitoring and reporting efforts are performed

Cyprus is progressively implementing PEPPOL-compliant electronic invoicing pursuant to Law 93(I)/2023 (transposing Directive 2014/55/EU) and the e-invoicing framework of the EU; electronic invoicing is mandatory for all invoices issued to public-sector bodies. For general B2B invoices, the Tax Department does not currently enforce an Italian-style real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping of every issuance, but it maintains an e-audit and data-accelerator framework: VAT returns and VIES declarations are filed electronically through Ariadni, and taxable persons must retain all unstructured and summary electronic data (ESI format and tax-audit files) for a minimum of 10 years, ensuring immediate machine-readable access for real-time tax audits. The EU e-invoicing initiative (ViDA) and Cyprus' participation mean that structured e-invoicing data will ultimately feed automated tax clearance; the GP's fund-administration and accounting systems should therefore be PEPPOL and UBL-based. Cyprus also applies the EU mandatory disclosure rules (DAC6) via the Cyprus Tax Department, compelling intermediaries to report cross-border arrangements within 30 days, which is highly relevant to a cross-border venture-capital GP structure with capital calls carried out by wire transfer.

The Cyprus Tax Department's VAT Service and Customs authorities run coordinated export audits and automated VIES/OSS data cross-checks: any export zero-rating claim lacking the full transport and payment documentation bundle is reversed, and the VAT is assessed at the standard 19% rate with default interest running at 4.75% p.a. late payment interest from the date of the original invoice or supply. In cases of deliberate misdeclaration or fraudulent export claims, civil penalties of up to double the tax due are imposed under Article 103 of the VAT Law, plus criminal sanctions (fines up to EUR 20,000 and imprisonment not exceeding seven years); the Income Tax Law and the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law separately empower the Commissioner to issue penal assessments of up to 100% of the tax evaded and to claw back any refunds with retrospective effect covering a 6-year statutory review window, extended to 10 years where fraud, negligent or wilful non-compliance is established. The GP entity is further subject to DAC6 and EU administrative cooperation Regulation 904/2010 disclosure, which enables automatic exchange of VAT data with other member states, severely narrowing the window for non-substantiated export zero-rating. All refund claims made by the GP are subject to the integration of mandatory FX repatriation evidence; any failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day window triggers a presumption that the supply did not take place and the authority will apply the exit-tax clawback under the anti-abuse provision, confirming the paramount importance of legally compliant capital-call documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies. Since EU accession on 1 May 2004 and euro adoption on 1 January 2008, the Republic of Cyprus has abolished all exchange-control restrictions under the former Exchange Control Law. The Central Bank of Cyprus retains no prior-approval power over current-account or capital-account transactions; both are free under Articles 63-66 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC. Dividends, management fees, carried interest, loan repayments, liquidation proceeds, and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders and foreign LPs are unrestricted and freely transferable in EUR or any convertible currency, subject only to standard KYC/AML verification and balance-of-payments statistical reporting by the instructing credit institution. Residual impediments exist solely where the underlying investment originates from a sanctioned or high-risk jurisdiction; screening rather than exchange control is the operative gate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil statutory requirement. Cyprus imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, or time limit on export or foreign receipts. A Cyprus VC GP may retain 100% of any foreign-currency receipts, including USD capital calls and realization proceeds, in designated foreign-currency capital or operational accounts, convert at any time at market rates, or hold EUR. The only ancillary duty is the CBC balance-of-payments statistical return filed by the bank, classifying the receipt and recording the counterpart jurisdiction; no central-bank authorization or surrender is required.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating within the Eurosystem as the national central bank and the competent AML/CFT and prudential supervisor for credit institutions and payment and electronic-money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility rating. Cyprus-regulated and EEA-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may lawfully hold payment accounts and process cross-border EUR transfers for the GP, but an EMI is prohibited from taking deposits, granting credit, or providing escrow-grade cash segregation for third-party capital-call funds. For a VC GP classified as Medium_Risk_Enhanced under CBC policy, a full licensed bank is the only commercially credible repository for committed investor capital; an EMI is acceptable only as an ancillary operating subchannel for invoice payments and minor expenses. Central Bank of Cyprus authorization is required for domestic EMIs and Payment Institutions, and passported EMIs must file CBC branch notifications. Practical account-opening rejection rates remain elevated for fund-management businesses due to acquirer scheme rules and correspondent-bank reluctance to accept fintech-originated capital flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Each LP effects capital calls through a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer to the GP designated EUR Capital Escrow Account at a Tier-1 Cyprus bank with T2 real-time gross settlement connectivity. The SWIFT Field 70 or 72 must carry the serialized capital-call reference drawn from the Limited Partnership Agreement in the format CY-GP-CC-YYYY-### and an explicit investment-purpose declaration mapping to the ECB balance-of-payments statistical category for investment-fund shares and units (BPM6 F.52; Cyprus BOP collection code for equity fund participations). The remitting bank is required to populate the CBC standard BOP outward and return code, and the receiving Cyprus bank will demand, for each first-time investor, a source-of-funds declaration and a certified copy of the executed capital-call notice. Wires from third-party or non-investor accounts are rejected. For EUR inbound flows, T2 covers immediate settlement; for USD inbound capital calls, the US correspondent leg clears over CHIPS or Fedwire and settles into the Cyprus bank USD NOSTRO, followed by optional conversion to EUR.Outbound disbursements to portfolio companies settle over SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for direct venture investments and loans, T2 RTGS for same-day EUR payments to European portfolio companies, and commercial documentary Letters of Credit under UCP 600 for structured trade-related milestone funding. For a VC GP classified as Medium_Risk_Enhanced under CBC policy, a full licensed bank is the only commercially credible repository for committed investor capital; an EMI is acceptable only as an ancillary operating subchannel for invoice payments and minor expenses. Practical account-opening rejection rates remain elevated for fund-management businesses due to acquirer scheme rules and correspondent-bank reluctance to accept fintech-originated capital flows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are mandatory where the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions (receipts and payments) exceeds EUR 750,000. A Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. In all other cases, a one-page Transfer Pricing Summary Information Table must be filed with the annual corporate tax return.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has not enacted a GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations law. However, a Cyprus-resident VC general partner must demonstrate genuine economic substance to maintain tax residence and treaty benefits under EU ATAD, the OECD MLI, and domestic case law. Substance indicators include: at least one locally resident director, physical office premises, local decision-making, minute books, a bank account in Cyprus, payroll with local employees, and adequate accounting records kept in Cyprus. Lack of substance may lead to refusal of a Tax Residence Certificate and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil sanction: assessment to tax on the true profits, a penalty equal to 100% of the tax evaded, plus statutory interest at 1.75% over the ECB rate. Criminal sanction: fine of up to EUR 20,000 and/or imprisonment of up to five years for each wilful evasion offence.

Director personal liability: Under the Cyprus Companies Law and tax fraud case law, directors who execute or approve a fraudulent return can be personally liable for restitution of the unpaid tax, subjected to asset-freezing orders and travel bans, and disqualified from holding directorships for up to five years.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Tax Department may refuse or revoke the TRC, re-classify the company as non-resident, and impose a 12.5% exit tax on deemed disposal of assets; false statements on the TRC application attract a fine of up to EUR 10,000. Under EU CFC rules, foreign shareholders may be assessed on undistributed profits as if earned in Cyprus.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign incorrect residence declarations are personally liable for misrepresentation and may be ordered to pay the shortfall; courts may pierce the corporate veil to recover taxes and impose personal liability where the company is a sham.
Anti-Money Laundering Breach (AML)

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (L.188(I)/2007), legal entities face a fine of up to EUR 500,000 or 5% of turnover, regulatory penalties imposed by CySEC, and suspension or revocation of the operating licence. Individuals are subject to imprisonment for up to five years and/or a fine of up to EUR 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers can be criminally prosecuted where they consented to or negligently permitted laundering activity; they may face personal fines up to EUR 100,000, confiscation of personal assets, travel restrictions, and a prohibition on serving as directors of any Cyprus company.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Tax Commissioner may adjust profits by applying the arm's length principle, imposing an additional tax assessment of 12.5% on the increased profit plus a penalty of 10% of the additional tax due. Failure to submit documentation within 30 days attracts an administrative fine of EUR 1,000 per month, capped at EUR 10,000. Late interest accrues at 1.75% above the ECB rate.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP disclosures or deliberately omit documentation can be held jointly liable if the company defaults on the resulting assessment; in serious cases they may be pursued for fraudulent misrepresentation and barred from acting as directors under the Companies Law.

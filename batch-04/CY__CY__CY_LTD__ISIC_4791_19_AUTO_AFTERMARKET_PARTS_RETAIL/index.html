Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance — 'Tax For All' e-Portal at https://taxforall.mof.gov.cy; District Tax Offices handle TIC/VAT registration; Cyprus Customs & Excise Department administers customs export clearance via the THALIS/ICS electronic platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Cyprus private limited company (HE77-series ELF code) with the Registrar of Companies. Step 2: Within 60 days of incorporation, file Form TAX 001 (Application for Tax Identification Code) and, if employing staff, Form TAX 002 (employer registration) with the District Tax Office or via the Tax For All portal. Step 3: Because Cyprus imposes no VAT turnover threshold for established taxable persons, submit VAT Form 101 (VAT registration application) simultaneously; the Tax Department issues a Tax Identification Code (TIC) in the format of 8 digits plus an alphabetic check digit (e.g., XXXXXXXXX). Step 4: Non-resident ownership is permitted; foreign non-resident taxable persons without a fixed establishment must appoint a local tax representative and complete VAT registration via Form VAT 102 before making their first taxable supply in Cyprus. Step 5: Complete UBO declaration and register for e-Government/Ariadni login credentials for electronic filing. Step 6: Issue VAT invoices only after the VAT number is activated; all subsequent returns are filed electronically via the Tax For All portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Cyprus private limited company is a separate legal and tax-resident entity taxed at the corporate rate of 12.5% on worldwide income (managed-and-controlled residency test). It is independently liable to VAT as a taxable person and is subject to full Cyprus domestic and EU tax law; no pass-through or transparent treatment is available for a standard Ltd.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Cyprus law. Available elections include: (1) Group Relief election allowing surrender of losses between qualifying 75% group companies; (2) election to claim unilateral foreign tax credit or deemed credit for overseas permanent establishments; (3) hybrid mismatch elections/alignment under EU ATAD 2 implementing law (Law 37(I)/2021) to determine dual-inclusion or deduction non-allowance outcomes; (4) continuing option to apply the 'deemed dividend' distribution rules for notional distributions of profits; (5) election for tonnage tax is limited to qualifying shipping activities and does not apply to auto parts retail.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A (EUR 0.00) for established taxable persons — Article 13 of the VAT Law imposes no minimum turnover threshold; registration is compulsory from the first taxable supply. For intra-Community distance sales of goods into Cyprus by EU suppliers, the registration trigger is EUR 10,000 per calendar year; non-established foreign sellers must register before their first Cypriot supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 66(1)(a) of the Cyprus VAT Law, the export supply of auto aftermarket parts is zero-rated at 0% where the goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the EU by the supplier or on its behalf within 90 days of the supply date, and the supplier obtains and preserves sufficient documentary evidence of the export. Related logistics services (international transport, loading, warehousing) qualify for zero-rating when directly connected to exported goods. Intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered recipients are zero-rated under Article 67 upon valid VAT ID and e-Transit (collision) evidence. The 90-day export window runs from the time of supply; the inward foreign currency remittance should be received within 180 days of shipment as the Tax Department's practical evidence benchmark for bookkeeping and bank correspondence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic export summary declaration lodged via THALIS/AES, with customs confirmation of departure)
  • Commercial invoice / VAT invoice issued in the name of the overseas buyer
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical transport out of the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank statement proving receipt of foreign currency consideration from the foreign customer within 180 days
  • Packing list plus supplementary evidence (outbound carrier manifest, customs stamps, insurance certificate)

Cyprus has implemented a mandatory national e-invoicing framework aligned with European Standard EN 16931 and the Peppol network. The Tax Department's electronic platform requires structured XML e-invoices for B2G transactions (mandatory from the 2024-2025 rollout), with B2B e-invoicing obligations being phased in through 2025-2026. There is no real-time fiscal stamping or continuous tax-control clearance (e.g., SDI-style) currently; however, taxable persons must issue, transmit and archive machine-readable e-invoices through certified interoperable software or the Tax Department's platform, ensuring authenticity, integrity and legibility. Large taxpayers and e-commerce operators are expected to integrate API-based reporting. Domestic B2C retail invoices must also be electronically documented; all VAT records, including e-invoice datasets, must be retained for at least six years (up to ten years where challenged by assessment).

The Cyprus Tax Department may audit export zero-rating claims at any time and typically demands the full evidence bundle within a 30 to 60 day notice period. Where zero-rating is disallowed for insufficient proof, missed 90-day export or 180-day inward remittance deadline, or proven fraud, the VAT is clawed back at the standard rate of 19% on the export value, together with statutory late-payment interest (civil debt rate) and administrative penalties. Criminal sanctions for fraudulent export evasion include fines of up to EUR 100,000 and imprisonment of up to five years. Assessment limitation periods are extended from the standard four years to ten years in cases involving fraud, wilful default or material inaccuracies; repeated offenders face blacklisting on the VAT audit risk matrix, suspension of the VAT number, and denial of refund rights until evidence is fully re-substantiated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full Eurozone member since 1 January 2008. The euro is freely convertible on both current and capital account under Articles 63–65 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC. There are zero exchange-control restrictions, no approval gates for profit remittances, dividends, royalties, or intercompany loans, and no whitelist/blacklist of foreign currencies. All repatriations are subject only to (i) the solvency/utilised-assets test under Cyprus Companies Law Cap. 113, and (ii) standard CBC statistical filings. Capital account liberalisation is absolute; no CY-specific surrender or licensing requirements apply to e-commerce businesses.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Cyprus. The company may retain 100% of export proceeds in EUR or any freely convertible currency (USD, GBP, CNY offshore, etc.) in any EEA-licensed bank or, subject to internal CBC risk policies, in an equivalent third-country bank. Only periodic BOP/ITRS reporting (via bank purpose codes) is required; no CBC permission is needed for repatriation or holding of foreign currency accounts.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), operating within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) and the ECB Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM); Foreign exchange policy is governed by EU Treaties, ECB regulations, and Cyprus's Exchange Control Law (Cap. 199, as repealed/liberalised), with residual statistical reporting under CBC BOP/ITRS rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Cyber (CySEC/CBC) permits passporting of EU EMIs (under PSD2/EMD2) and e-money e-wallets; however, for an auto-parts e-commerce merchant utilising Stripe/PayPal, an offshore EMI wallet is viable only as an ancillary FX-or-collection rail, NOT as a primary treasury settlement base. Cyprus Tier-1 banks require final EUR settlement into a domestic account. Post-Brexit UK EMIs are third-country and often rejected; EU-authorised EMIs (Lithuania, Luxembourg, Ireland, Netherlands) are acceptable for inbound receipt testing but must cascade daily sweeps to the Cyprus bank account. Legal constraints: no MIFID licensing is needed for a merchant holding an EMI account, but the company must avoid running an unlicensed payment-account service; all EMI flows must map to real underlying trade invoices.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: inward remittance via SWIFT MT103 from shareholder(s) to the Cyprus Ltd operational account using CBC-approved purpose code 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (TFEU Annex A / BPM6 category 5031, EC Directive 88/361 item III). Capital injection can be executed without CBC permit since no minimum-capital obligation exists for CY Ltd; a par-value share issue (e.g., 1,000 ordinary shares at €1.00) or an irrevocable shareholder loan is accepted. Verification package: (1) board resolution authorizing allotment; (2) share subscription agreement; (3) register of members (Form HE/M1); (4) bank 'purpose of payment' string must read 'CY Ltd – share capital increase'; (5) for foreign corporates, certificate of incumbency and UBO declaration of the lender. To avoid FATCA/CRS mismatches, remitting bank must state the remitter's tax residency; for funds over €20,000, a CRS self-declaration of the recipient-corporation must be on file. Any inbound non-resident foreign exchange >€10,000 is reported monthly to CBC statistical unit; currency conversion into EUR requires standard FX ticket with underlying corporate justification.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 (SWIFT gpi) for all non-EU supplier payments — narrative must contain supplier invoice no., PO no., and Incoterms; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) — issued by Bank of Cyprus/Hellenic via MT700 for bulk China/Asia import orders above USD $50,000, with seller's presentation through a confirming bank; (3) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SEPA Instant) for all EU supplier/refund flows settled in EUR within 10 seconds; (4) SEPA Core Direct Debit for recurring EU operating expenses (warehouse, logistics); (5) for CNY-denominated supplier settlements, use CNH via correspondent (HSBC/Standard Chartered/Deutsche) under a CNH purpose code 'Trade Settlement', or settle in USD with CNY conversion performed at the Chinese bank receiving side under SAFE regulations; (6) PayPal/Stripe 'disbursement rails' must be instructed to sweep net proceeds daily to the Cyprus operational account (MT103-equivalent via processor's acquiring bank). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 in aggregate annual related-party transactions. When this threshold is reached, the Cyprus entity is required to prepare and maintain both a Local File and a Master File in accordance with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and Cyprus Income Tax Law. Below the threshold, arm's-length benchmarking is still expected for every cross-border related-party transaction.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute of the UAE type, but economic substance is enforced through the transfer-pricing framework, beneficial-ownership requirements, CFC rules, ATAD anti-abuse provisions, and the general anti-avoidance doctrine. A Cyprus auto-parts e-commerce LTD must show a physical office in Cyprus, locally exercised management and control, employees with employment contracts, substantive decision-making, and a real operational bank account. Absent that substance, the Tax Department can challenge the allocation of profits, recharacterise transactions, and deny treaty and holding-company benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Back tax at 12.5% plus statutory interest, severe civil penalties that can reach 100% of the underpaid tax, reopening of assessments beyond the normal six-year window, and criminal prosecution before the Cypriot courts; conviction for fraudulent tax evasion can result in custodial sentences and substantial fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or direct the concealment of income are personally liable in civil fraud proceedings. The court may pierce the corporate veil, order personal restitution, freeze personal assets, impose travel bans, disqualify directors, and sentence individuals to imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department and the courts may disregard the entity or reallocate its profits to the jurisdiction of actual management and control. Deductions for management fees, interest, and intercompany purchases can be denied, and the profit is re-assessed at full 12.5% plus penalties; treaty benefits are also withdrawn.

Director personal liability: Nominee and shadow directors can be held jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax under veil-piercing principles. Disqualification orders, civil recovery, director-banning orders, and criminal liability for aiding a sham structure are available.
AML / Money Laundering Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law 188(I)/2007, legal persons face fines of up to EUR 1,000,000 per violation and individuals face imprisonment; serious breaches can trigger regulatory orders, account freezes, and dissolution or licence revocation where the company holds payment-related registrations.

Director personal liability: Directors can be prosecuted criminally for money laundering even if the company itself is not a regulated AML entity. Directors who facilitate laundering through the corporate payment rails are liable to asset confiscation, fines, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department can make a transfer-pricing adjustment and assess additional tax at 12.5% on the adjusted profit, plus interest. Where misstatement is deliberate, penalties can be as high as 100% of the additional tax, and the return can be reopened and criminal charges considered.

Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly certify false transfer-pricing records are personally exposed. The TP file must record the persons who approved the pricing policy; directors who fail to supervise preparation of the file can be subject to director disqualification and personal liability in insolvency or Tax Department recovery proceedings.
Late Filing / Non-Compliance with Return Obligations

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalties up to EUR 1,000 per return, default interest on the unpaid corporate tax at the statutory rate, and automatic estimated assessments if the Tax Department is forced to reconstruct the turnover. Persistent default exposes the company to enforcement bailiff action and strike-off.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for taxes if the company is wound up while returns and tax remain outstanding. Civil recovery proceedings, disqualification orders, travel restrictions, and liability for wrongful trading are possible where the director knowingly allowed continued non-compliance.

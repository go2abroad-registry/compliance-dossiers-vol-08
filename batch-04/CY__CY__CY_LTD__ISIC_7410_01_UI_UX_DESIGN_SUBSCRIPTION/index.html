Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; a private limited company may lawfully commence operations with an issued capital of €1. There is no escrow account or deposit requirement under Cyprus law for non-regulated industries.

Funding & unlock path: The company must comply with the Cyprus Fire Safety Regulations (Fire Protection and Extinguishing Equipment Law, L. 118(I)/2018) for its office premises, including fire extinguisher certification, emergency exits, and fire safety signage. The Department of Labour Inspection (Ministry of Labour, Welfare and Social Insurance) may carry out an occupational health and safety inspection under the Safety and Health at Work Law (L. 89(I)/1996). For a standard leased office, a civil defence clearance is not independently required, but the building occupancy certificate and fire safety certificate must be verified and maintained. If the company later opens a physical showroom or prototyping lab, separate municipal building and fire-safety permits will be triggered.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital; a private limited company may lawfully commence operations with an issued capital of €1. There is no escrow account or deposit requirement under Cyprus law for non-regulated industries.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; official e-portal: https://taxportal.cy

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that is not otherwise established in Cyprus must apply through the Tax Portal before making taxable supplies. The electronic procedure requires verified company information (certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered address), beneficial-owner identification declarations, evidence of business activity, and the appointment of a Cyprus tax representative where required. A taxpayer identification number (TIN, identical to the VAT number) is issued upon approval. No minimum capital is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity that is not otherwise established in Cyprus must apply through the Tax Portal before making taxable supplies. The electronic procedure requires verified company information (certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered address), beneficial-owner identification declarations, evidence of business activity, and the appointment of a Cyprus tax representative where required. A taxpayer identification number (TIN, identical to the VAT number) is issued upon approval. No minimum capital is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or classification election exists under Cyprus law. A private limited company is per se a separate taxable person; residence is a factual management-and-control test. Cyprus applies ATAD 2 hybrid mismatch rules and controlled-foreign-company provisions that override mismatches; no statutory pass-through election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for established taxable persons; EUR 10,000 for intra-Community distance sales; non-established foreign suppliers have no threshold and must register before making taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies where goods are exported outside the European Union in accordance with the Cyprus VAT Law; the supplier must ship the goods pursuant to an export contract and hold contemporaneous evidence. Services (including UI/UX design subscriptions): zero-rating is applicable to certain export-related services; other digital design services supplied to clients outside Cyprus are normally outside the territorial scope of Cyprus VAT under the place-of-supply rules where the recipient is a taxable person established in a third country, or if the services are treated as legal/technical/intellectual equivalent categories; all export invoices must display the zero-rating reason code and retain evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (formal SAD export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent transport documentation: AWB/CMR)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank payment evidence)
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Evidence of buyer's non-EU taxable status (VAT/GST number or legal proof)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API mandate, or digital stamping regime is in force. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies for Cypriot public-sector purchases through the national Ariadne platform; B2B e-invoicing continues under mutual consent with PDF/XML retention rules.

The Cyprus Tax Department verifies zero-rated export claims before crediting refunds. Missing or unreliable documentation triggers reversal of the zero-rating and assessment to VAT at 19%, plus interest and administrative penalties; the supplier bears the burden of proof. Deliberate false declarations expose the company and its officers to criminal liability, substantial fines, and imprisonment, and the Tax Department may claw back refunds plus penalties and extend the statutory limitation period in cases of fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a Member State of the European Union and part of the Eurozone. Both current and capital account transactions are fully liberalised; no separate foreign-exchange control regime is maintained. Profit remittances, dividends, and loan repayments to non-residents may be executed without exchange-control approval, subject only to anti-money laundering verification and EU/UN sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Republic of Cyprus imposes no statutory obligation to repatriate, surrender, or convert export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted to euro at commercial banks, subject to customer due diligence measures.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility. Cyprus-recognised Electronic Money Institutions and payment institutions, including EU-passported fintechs such as Wise and Stripe Treasury-affiliated partners, can operate alongside commercial banks. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The IR4 must be submitted no later than 6 months after the end of the fiscal year; for a 31 December year-end the statutory deadline is 30 June of the following year. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Transfer Pricing Summary Documentation (Form TD1) filing via Taxisnet applies where the aggregate value of controlled transactions in a tax year exceeds €1,000,000. Full Local File and Master File documentation should be prepared and retained to support the arm's length character of all related-party dealings and must be produced within 60 days of a formal request by the Cyprus Tax Department.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus adopts economic substance as an administrative and judicial doctrine drawn from the Income Tax Law management-and-control test, the EU/ATAD General Anti-Abuse Rule, and the OECD BEPS nexus approach. A Cyprus company benefits from the 12.5% regime only if effective management and key decision-making occurs on the island, with own premises, locally employed qualified staff, operating board meetings in Cyprus, and substantive local control over the UI/UX design contracts, invoicing, cash management, and intellectual property ownership. Shell arrangements are rebutted by the Cyprus Tax Department through residency denial, withdrawal of tax residence certificates, and re-characterisation of income flows.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Civil: revised assessment for the evaded tax, statutory interest at government rates, and penalty surcharges of up to double the evaded tax. Criminal: conviction on indictment carries a fine and/or imprisonment of up to five years for fraudulent tax evasion; offences are prosecuted by the Republic under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law Cap. 4.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly submit false returns face personal criminal sanctions, travel bans, and disqualification orders; where the misdeclaration is connected to insolvency, directors may be personally liable for fraudulent trading under the Cyprus Companies Law Cap. 113, with civil restitution to the revenue and creditors.
Shell Company / Artificial Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of corporate tax residence status; re-attribution of worldwide profits to the real place of effective management at the applicable foreign tax rate; refusal of the Cyprus Tax Residency Certificate for treaty purposes; retroactive application of the Cyprus GAAR with reassessments at 12.5% on undisclosed income, penalty surcharges, and cancellation of existing tax clearance certificates. The company may simultaneously lose the right to apply the IP Box relief and participation exemption.

Director personal liability: Civil liability for fraudulent misrepresentations in statutory declarations; personal clawback of distributions made without proper substance; criminal exposure of directors who knowingly facilitated artificial profit allocation and shadow-director liability for foreign persons exercising de facto control.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and TD1 Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length recharacterisation of the intercompany pricing with additional tax, interest at the statutory rate, and penalty surcharges of up to 100% of the additional assessed tax; monthly late-filing penalties for the omitted TD1 form; active listing in the high-risk audit programme and automatic referral to the transfer pricing unit of the Cyprus Tax Department.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the mispricing are jointly and severally liable for the penalty amounts if the company cannot pay; exit bans may be imposed to block departure until settlement; criminal proceedings apply where the transfer pricing distortion is collusive and fraudulent. Directors of the Cypriot entity may also owe indemnities to minority shareholders for preference enforcement.
AML / Money-Laundering and Sanctions Exposure

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines of up to €500,000 and imprisonment of up to ten years for money-laundering offences under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Law 188(I)/2007; immediate freezing of merchant accounts by payment processors; compulsory filing of Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) to the Cyprus Unit for Combating Money Laundering (MOKAS); revocation of any operating licences and prohibition against holding beneficiary ownership in regulated sectors.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, facilitate, or knowingly ignore suspicious payment routing are personally indictable; personal assets may be confiscated under proceeds-of-crime legislation; directors face travel bans, wealth freezing orders, disqualification of up to five years, and personal criminal liability without any corporate veil protection.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a private limited liability company under Cyprus law. The authorised and issued share capital is fixed in the Memorandum and Articles of Association; no escrow or deposit of capital is required at incorporation. Shares may be issued for cash or in kind, and the subscribers’ obligations are governed by the share-allotment statement filed at registration.

Funding & unlock path: No special civil-defense clearance or fire-safety industrial licence is required for a low-risk office-based SaaS helpdesk. If a physical office is leased in Cyprus, the lessor must provide a valid fire safety certificate issued by the Fire Service and satisfy the Health and Safety at Work Law 89(I)/1996. A simple workplace risk assessment, registration of employees with the Social Insurance Services, and a declaration of compliance with basic building occupancy and planning permits are sufficient.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a private limited liability company under Cyprus law. The authorised and issued share capital is fixed in the Memorandum and Articles of Association; no escrow or deposit of capital is required at incorporation. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), operating under the Ministry of Finance; online portal: ARIADNE (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

Private limited companies are automatically registered for tax upon incorporation with the Registrar of Companies; the Cyprus Tax Department assigns a Tax Identification Number (TIC/TIN) to every newly incorporated company. A foreign entity without a permanent establishment must apply for registration as a non-resident taxable person via the ARIADNE portal, file Form T.D. 2000, submit the certificate of incorporation (or equivalent foreign registration), the memorandum and articles of association, the original power of attorney of its appointed tax representative or local agent, and the certified passport copy of the director(s); the Tax Department issues the TIN within approximately 10-15 working days once physical or digital verification is complete.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; resident companies are taxed on worldwide income under the Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002) as amended, while non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-sourced income and income attributable to a permanent establishment in Cyprus; there is no pass-through regime for private limited companies (Ltd), which are always separate taxable persons liable to the 12.5% corporate income tax rate; no tax-exempt zone applies to the standard limited liability company structure. — Private limited companies are automatically registered for tax upon incorporation with the Registrar of Companies; the Cyprus Tax Department assigns a Tax Identification Number (TIC/TIN) to every newly incorporated company. A foreign entity without a permanent establishment must apply for registration as a non-resident taxable person via the ARIADNE portal, file Form T.D. 2000, submit the certificate of incorporation (or equivalent foreign registration), the memorandum and articles of association, the original power of attorney of its appointed tax representative or local agent, and the certified passport copy of the director(s); the Tax Department issues the TIN within approximately 10-15 working days once physical or digital verification is complete.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; resident companies are taxed on worldwide income under the Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002) as amended, while non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-sourced income and income attributable to a permanent establishment in Cyprus; there is no pass-through regime for private limited companies (Ltd), which are always separate taxable persons liable to the 12.5% corporate income tax rate; no tax-exempt zone applies to the standard limited liability company structure.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style entity classification election exists under Cyprus tax law. Cyprus has not adopted the OECD hybrid mismatch provisions in domestic statute in the form of elective rules, but it does implement EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) hybrid mismatch rules (Law 132(I)/2021) which operate automatically rather than by election; Cyprus has no notional interest deduction election for software entities but permits an optional deemed expense election under the patent-box / intangible assets regime (IP Box law, Law 117(I)/2015), under which a qualifying intangible asset may elect to be treated as a separate asset class for the 80% deemed deduction; a group may also elect for group loss relief, and an overseas permanent establishment may elect for exemption from Cyprus tax under section 9A of the Income Tax Law, provided the foreign PE is subject to foreign tax of at least as much as the Cyprus tax that would have been due; such elections are made in the annual tax return or via prior notification to the Tax Department.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 taxable turnover over the immediately preceding 12 months for established taxable persons; EUR 10,000 registration threshold for distance sales of goods into Cyprus by EU suppliers; EUR 15,600 for intra-Community acquisitions of goods by non-established taxable persons; foreign suppliers without an establishment in Cyprus are required to register for VAT from the first euro of taxable B2C supply and on any B2B supply where the recipient is not liable to account via reverse charge; the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) non-Union scheme may be used instead of full Cypriot registration for intra-EU distance sales and electronic, broadcasting, or digital services supplied to final consumers, regardless of threshold, from the first euro.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the EU are zero-rated under the Cyprus VAT Law where the supplier retains the normal commercial documentary evidence of export: a valid customs export declaration (SAD), a Bill of Lading, CMR, or airway bill showing the consignee and destination outside the EU, and evidence that title has passed to the non-EU customer; ancillary transport, loading, and freight services related to export goods are zero-rated if covered under the same contract; in respect of the ISIC 6201 helpdesk/SaaS customer-support scope, B2B services rendered to a non-established taxable person located outside the EU are treated as zero-rated under the place-of-supply rule of the EU VAT Directive to the bandwidth of the customer's business establishment (Article 44/59 VAT Directive, Cyprus Law art. 48); the zero-rating is conditional on obtaining a written declaration from the customer confirming their business status and outside-EU establishment, plus commercial and transport evidence; this excludes services directly connected to immovable property within Cyprus.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD – Single Administrative Document, form C150/SAO digital submission via THESEAS/ICS2)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill/CMR as applicable
  • Packing List (mandatory for actual export evidence)
  • Intra-EU or non-EU contract and purchase order evidencing the sale
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/PSP confirmation of cross-border receipt and FX conversion (for GST/VAT refund substantiation)

There is no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API transmission to the Cyprus Tax Department for standard B2B/B2C e-invoicing as of 2025. EU Directive 2014/55/EU applies to mandatory e-invoicing only for suppliers to Cyprus public-sector contracting authorities; the Cyprus Tax Department has not yet introduced a national continuous transaction control (CTC) system. However, all VAT-registered businesses must issue an invoice, delivery note, or receipt, and the Tax Department may require XML e-invoicing (European e-invoice standard EN 16931) in public procurement contracts. Modernisation of business records, storage, and ex-post audit access to source systems does not require pre-clearance.

Where the Tax Department finds false documentation or intentional over-claiming of export zero-rating, it makes a retrospective VAT assessment for up to 8 years under Article 51 of the VAT Law for intentional fraud and 6 years for non-fraudulent under-declaration; the penalty regime includes a fine up to EUR 5,000 for first offences and/or imprisonment up to 2 years pursuant to Article 115 of Law 95(I)/2000, as amended by Law 84(I)/2018; a further civil penalty of up to 200% of the VAT under-assessed can be applied under section 55A in case of deliberate intent with intent to evade; corrective invoices, denial of refund claims, credit notes and reversal of the right to deduct input VAT apply; the Tax Department may also impose an administrative penalty of 1.5% per month or part thereof on the unpaid tax for late payment, and directors may be personally liable under the Companies Law and VAT Law in cases of fraudulent evasion. For export services, the failure to obtain the export evidence within the statutory deadline causes automatic zero-rating to be reversed with interest and penalties; refunds of input VAT are clawed back with interest at 2% above the ECB base rate.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full member of the euro area, and the euro is fully convertible across both current and capital account transactions. Capital movements are liberalised in accordance with Articles 63 to 65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the domestic Restrictive Measures for the Protection of Financial Stability legal framework. No general exchange-control authorisation is needed for profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayment, or capital repatriation, provided the underlying corporate records, audited financial statements, and tax clearance documents are in order. Dividend distributions to non-residents are subject to Cyprus withholding tax at the statutory rate of 17%, which is typically reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or to agreed treaty rates under Cyprus's double tax treaty network; no exchange-control surcharge or conversion tax applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or holding-period requirement for export proceeds in Cyprus. Export receipts may be retained in euros or in freely convertible foreign currency accounts without Central Bank of Cyprus consent. The only ongoing obligations are statistical in nature: exporters must report balance-of-payments data for trades and services to the Central Bank of Cyprus within the prescribed monthly cycle, and physical cash transports crossing EU borders in excess of EUR 10,000 must be declared under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672. As the target entity is a helpdesk SaaS business rather than a goods exporter, service-export receipts are treated as current-account income and are freely repatriable subject to standard invoicing and substance documentation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) acting in coordination with the Cyprus Ministry of Finance and the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission for matters involving payment services, balance-of-payments reporting, and cross-border capital movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for offshore non-Cyprus EMIs, and Medium-to-High only for fully licensed Cypriot Electronic Money Institutions operating under the Cypriot Electronic Money Law (Law 52(I)/2018) implementing the EU EMD2 and PSD2 frameworks. Offshore digital bank or EMI providers without a physical European Union presence are not admitted as primary deposit-taking counterparties, are not covered by the Deposit Guarantee Scheme of the Central Bank of Cyprus, and cannot support capital escrow or audit-confirmation services that Cypriot auditors typically require. Such EMIs may be used solely as a secondary settlement rail for receiving Stripe and PayPal payouts, but funds must be swept promptly to a licensed tier-1 bank account to avoid operational concentration risk and adverse audit findings.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity or shareholder-loan injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 in EUR or USD to the entity's IBAN account in Cyprus. The remitter must reference an internationally accepted purpose code, commonly the OECD balance-of-payments code for inward direct investment (e.g., 'TRPI' or the narrative 'Shareholder Capital Contribution') and a clear payment description such as 'Inward Equity Injection per Board Resolution of [date]' or 'Shareholder Loan under Loan Agreement dated [date]'. The bank will require a certified board resolution, an updated register of members, and, where applicable, shareholders' resolutions approving the allotment. Inward transfers equal to or exceeding EUR 200,000 will typically trigger an automatic balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligation to the Central Bank of Cyprus; the statutory auditor may request a confirmation letter from the bank to verify the capital balance at year-end.Operating disbursements in euros are cleared through the SEPA Credit Transfer scheme using the beneficiary IBAN, with execution typically completed on T+1 or faster. US-dollar and GBP trade settlements are effected through SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers, with MT202 COVER employed only in back-to-back split-cover architectures where the instructing bank is different from the reimbursement bank. For procurement of non-EU software, cloud infrastructure, or hardware components, the entity may use irrevocable documentary letters of credit opened through Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank's trade desks, or direct MT103 invoice payments supplemented by the underlying purchase order and proof of service delivery.

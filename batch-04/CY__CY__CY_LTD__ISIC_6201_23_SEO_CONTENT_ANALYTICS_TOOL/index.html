Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DRC), Ministry of Energy, Commerce and Industry, Republic of Cyprus

Governing statute

Cyprus Companies Law, Cap. 113 (as amended) — Sections 3 (mode of incorporation), 4 (registration requirements), 8 (memorandum of association), 9 (articles of association), 10 (registration of company), 376 (private company definition); supplementary Cyprus Companies (Fees) Regulations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form HE1 (Application for Registration of a Company) together with the Memorandum and Articles of Association; issuance of Certificate of Incorporation (Form HE7)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 31 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed under Cap. 113. The authorised share capital is fixed in the Memorandum of Association (customarily EUR 1,000–EUR 5,000). The company must have at least one member and one director. Paid-up capital may be deferred by the Articles of Association; no escrow bank account is required by law for share capital contribution, and funds may be settled in cash or in kind subject to a director's declaration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Cyprus Fire Service under the Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting Law (Cap. 50) is required for any substantive office premises occupied by staff. The company must perform a workplace risk assessment under the Safety and Health at Work Law 89(I)/1996. Structural load inspections are not applicable to office tenancy; the leased registered office requires a valid electrical inspection certificate and compliance with the minimum office workplace space standards.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed under Cap. 113. The authorised share capital is fixed in the Memorandum of Association (customarily EUR 1,000–EUR 5,000). The company must have at least one member and one director. Paid-up capital may be deferred by the Articles of Association; no escrow bank account is required by law for share capital contribution, and funds may be settled in cash or in kind subject to a director's declaration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Cyprus Tax Department), operating under the Ministry of Finance and headed by the Commissioner of Taxation. Primary electronic channels: 'Tax For All' portal (https://www.taxforalloc.gov.cy) for income tax, VAT registration, VAT returns and correspondence; Cyprus Customs Service 'Ariadni' portal for electronic export declarations. VAT administration is exercised by the VAT Service within the same Tax Department. No municipal or provincial tax administration is involved (unitary national system).

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: The Cyprus private limited company (CY_LTD, ELF code HE77) is incorporated with the Registrar of Companies; the Tax Department automatically issues the 9-digit Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN) based on the incorporation data, so no separate TIN application is needed for income tax. STEP 2: For VAT, file Form TFC 101 ('Application for Registration in the VAT Registry') via Tax For All before commencing taxable activity; compulsory registration is required within 30 days once taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600 in the preceding 12 months or is reasonably expected to exceed such amount within the next 30 days. STEP 3: A foreign (non-Cyprus) entity carrying on economic activity in Cyprus without a local establishment must register for VAT and appoint a Cyprus-resident tax representative. STEP 4: Submit to the Tax Department: certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, identity/passport copies of directors and shareholders, proof of registered office address in Cyprus, and, for non-resident directors, appointment of a Cyprus-resident person for service of documents. STEP 5: Complete all filings, including VAT registration, via the Tax For All portal; the TIN is referenced in all customs, banking, and invoicing documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope: A Cyprus-incorporated company is tax resident if managed and controlled in Cyprus; it is subject to 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income and to the Special Defence Contribution (deemed distribution at 17%) on undistributed profits used for the personal benefit of shareholders. Non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. Tax_Exempt_Zone: No domestic special economic zone or income tax holiday exists in Cyprus; the IP Box regime provides an 80% qualifying profit deduction only for grandfathering-qualifying assets and is not a general exemption. Pass_Through_Eligible: Not available; a Cypriot Ltd is a separate taxable person and cannot be treated as a pass-through or transparent entity. Cyprus applies no general CFC charge on EU/EEA resident subsidiaries.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election. Hybrid mismatch rules are codified under Article 117 of the Income Tax Law (ATAD 2 implementation). Available statutory elections: (i) Section 2(2) Income Tax Law election for a Cyprus-incorporated company to be treated as non-Cyprus tax resident where the company is tax resident in an EU or treaty state and is not managed and controlled in Cyprus; (ii) group loss relief election under Article 24 with the parent as the claiming company; (iii) VAT grouping election under Article 34 of the VAT Law for related Cyprus-established persons; (iv) IP Box grandfathering election for qualifying intangible assets acquired or developed before 30 June 2016 (election within 60 days of acquisition); (v) Notional Interest Deduction election on new equity under Article 9G; (vi) tonnage tax joint election for shipping activities; (vii) deemed dividend opt-out notice under Section 47 of the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law to avoid imputed distribution of profits retained beyond two years.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 (Cyprus compulsory VAT registration threshold for taxable turnover of goods and services in the preceding 12 months or expected within the next 30 days); intra-EU distance sales of goods into Cyprus exceeding EUR 35,000; non-established suppliers of B2C telecommunications, broadcasting, and electronic services have no threshold and must register from the first euro via OSS; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold; exporting companies may register regardless of turnover to claim input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: exports of goods physically dispatched from Cyprus to a destination outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under Article 47 of the VAT Law, provided the exporter obtains export proof (customs electronic export declaration and transport documents) within 30 days of dispatch unless a longer justified period is approved, and retains records for six years. Services: for the SEO SaaS product (ISIC 6201), supplies to non-EU B2B customers are outside the scope of Cyprus VAT under Article 90 (place-of-supply rule), producing an equivalent nil-rate result with full input VAT deduction; supplies to EU B2B customers are reverse-charged by the customer; supplies to EU B2C customers are subject to the OSS scheme at the customer's member state rate with no Cyprus threshold; supplies to non-EU B2C customers are outside the scope of Cyprus VAT. In all zero-rated/outside-scope cases, the supplier must substantiate the non-EU or EU B2B status of the customer and retain contemporaneous export/service and payment evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Single Administrative Document with Master Reference Number) generated via the Cyprus Customs Service 'Ariadni' portal / EU Automated Export System
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill, CMR consignment note, or CIM rail document) evidencing physical departure of goods from the EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 / payment statement) evidencing receipt of gross export consideration from the non-EU buyer, with matching foreign-exchange settlement records
  • Commercial invoice and signed export sales contract (with Incoterms basis, payment terms, and definitive buyer identity)
  • Transport insurance certificate or warehouse dispatch release order confirming the goods left Cyprus
  • Third-country customs entry documentation or similar official evidence where the Tax Department raises risk-based audit challenges

Cyprus does not currently impose a domestic real-time Continuous Transaction Control (CTC) clearance, transaction digital stamping, or mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime. Mandatory requirements include: electronic filing of all VAT returns (Form VAT 4) and income tax returns through the Tax For All portal; mandatory B2G e-invoicing to the Cypriot administration in the PEPPOL/EN16931 format under Directive 2014/55/EU; and electronic submission of customs export declarations via the Customs 'Ariadni' system. The Tax Department is progressing a phased ViDA-aligned roadmap for national mandatory e-invoicing, with API-based data exchange and clearance expected to be implemented in alignment with EU 2024/89 amending Directive (ViDA) and the announced Cyprus digital transformation plan; the current law contains no SAF-T reporting obligation.

Cyprus has no codified 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation control; the 180-day figure is applied in this dossier as the prudent benchmark for reconciling export turnover against Article 21 accruals and bank receipts, with any extended deferral needing documented commercial justification and arm's length pricing under OECD transfer pricing rules. If export documentation is incomplete, missing, or fraudulent, the Tax Department retrospectively claws back the zero-rating: VAT is assessed at 19% on the gross export value, with interest charged at 1.5% per month or part of a month, plus extra tax: up to 10% of the unassessed VAT for failure to file or inaccurate returns; an additional penalty of 2% of the unpaid tax for negligent misstatement, doubled (4%) for fraudulent misstatement. Criminal exposure for fraudulent zero-rating, false invoices, or documents includes fines up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment of up to two years per offence, forfeiture of the economic benefit, and post-clearance customs recovery under the Union Customs Code (usually within 3 years, extendable in cases of criminal proceedings); company directors may additionally face personal liability for fraudulent trading or wrongful filing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus has adopted the euro and applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility under EU law. There are no foreign-exchange controls, prior approval requirements, or repatriation restrictions for dividends, royalties, service fees, or loan repayments. Profit remittance from a Cyprus private limited company is subject only to standard corporate formalities, tax residence certification, and applicable anti-money laundering verification of the payment chain. A Cyprus tax withholding on outbound dividends is generally zero for non-resident shareholders, provided the shareholder is not within the EU interest/royalty or dividend anti-abuse exceptions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no specified deadline for export receipts. A company may lawfully retain export revenue in EUR, in a foreign-currency account with a licensed Cyprus bank, or in an account with an EU/EEA branch of a credit institution, subject to internal bank risk appetite and the EU Wire Transfer Regulation information requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), acting within the European System of Central Banks and the EU Treaty capital-movement framework; exchange control matters for euro-denominated flows also fall under the scope of the Cypriot Ministry of Finance only to the extent of residual emergency safeguard measures, which are currently not in force.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. An EU-licensed e-money institution (EMI) authorised in another member state may passport into Cyprus under PSD2 and open a payment account for the company; however, Cypriot-regulated banks frequently treat EMIs as high-risk and may refuse to accept EMI account balances for onward settlement. Offshore digital EMIs outside the EEA are generally not recognised as credit institutions for Cyprus corporate treasury purposes and will likely trigger enhanced due diligence or service refusal.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound injection is executed via SWIFT wire or SEPA credit transfer from the shareholder's account. The remitter must reference a defined economic purpose code: for equity subscription use the OECD-BPM6 category 'Direct investment: equity capital' with the Cypriot tax identification number of the company; for shareholder loans use 'Direct investment: loans and other capital'. The receiving bank will require the completed Cyprus corporate KYC pack, a board resolution approving the issuance of shares or loan drawdown, the filed share allotment form (HE30) to the Cyprus Registrar of Companies, and source-of-funds evidence for the underlying remittance. For amounts above EUR 15,000, the Wire Transfer Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/1113) requires full originator and beneficiary data to be transmitted with the payment.Primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR vendor payments and commercial letters of credit (MT700) for physical procurement or software licensing where a bank undertaking is required. For EU/EEA and SEPA counterparties, euro disbursements are settled through TARGET2 or the EURO1 high-value payment system with the IBAN/BIC pair and mandatory structured remittance references. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 - Under the Cyprus Transfer Pricing Administrative Circular (issued 30 June 2022), contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory for a tax year whenever the aggregate annual value of cross-border controlled transactions reaches or exceeds EUR 750,000. The same threshold applies to domestic transactions taxed under the arm's-length principle. Country-by-Country Reporting (Master File and CbCR) applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute of the type enacted in the UAE; however, economic substance is embedded in the tax framework through: (1) the central management and control test for corporate tax residence; (2) the OECD modified-nexus requirement under the IP Box regime; (3) the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) rules on controlled foreign companies, interest limitation, and general anti-abuse; and (4) the general anti-avoidance rule under Section 33 of the Cyprus Income Tax Law. A Cyprus Ltd operating as an SEO content analytics SaaS provider must demonstrate locally held board meetings, strategic decision-making, operating staff, physical premises, and IP development functions in Cyprus; failure to do so may result in recharacterisation of the corporate structure and denial of the 12.5% benefit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law and Income Tax Law: penalty of 100% of the underpaid tax (up to 150% for fraudulent misstatement), plus interest at 3.5% per annum, plus a 10% unpaid-tax surcharge. Criminal conviction carries a fine of up to EUR 5,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 3 years per offence, with the Tax Department entitled to issue best-of-judgment assessments and seize assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of false returns are criminally liable as principals. Personal civil liability is triggered under the fraudulent trading provisions of the Companies Law (Cap. 113), exposing directors to restitution of diverted corporate assets, court-issued travel bans and exit restrictions, disqualification from acting as a director for up to 5 years, and joint and several liability for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the arrangement under the general anti-avoidance rule (Section 33 of the Income Tax Law), disallowance of the IP Box deduction and other reliefs, recomputation of tax at 12.5% on the full commercial profit, plus interest and penalties of up to 100% of the underpaid tax. DTA benefits and EU Directive reliefs are denied where the arrangement is found to be artificial.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate a sham shell structure face personal civil liability for the resulting corporate tax debt if they strip assets or declare dividends while the company is insolvent. Personal criminal liability for wilful participation in an artificial arrangement can result in fines of up to EUR 4,000 and imprisonment; directors may also be subjected to personal asset-freeze orders and travel bans during investigation.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department may impose arm's-length adjustments to the taxable profit, apply penalties ranging from 50% to 100% of the adjusted tax difference, charge interest at 3.5% per annum, and impose a 10% surcharge on the unpaid balance. Failure to produce contemporaneous documentation reverses the burden of proof to the taxpayer and can elevate the adjustment to a fraudulent misstatement classification with criminal consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute or approve mispriced related-party transactions are personally exposed to civil claims for the loss suffered by the company and the State, joint and several liability for withheld taxes, deemed-dividend assessments against shareholders equal to the mispriced amount, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion where intent is established.
AML / UBO Register Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Companies Law (Cap. 113) and the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (L.188(I)/2007): administrative fines of up to EUR 20,000 per violation, daily continuing penalties for ongoing non-compliance, criminal fines of up to EUR 5,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 1 year, and the power of the Registrar to strike the company off the register. The company's bank accounts may be frozen, and transactions through Stripe or Paddle may be suspended by payment processors upon adverse AML findings.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally criminally liable for each day of non-compliance with the UBO regime; the court may issue an exit travel ban while AML proceedings are pending; directors are jointly and severally liable for the fines and costs imposed; asset-freeze orders and director disqualification orders may additionally be imposed, piercing the corporate veil.

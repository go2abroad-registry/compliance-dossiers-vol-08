Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register) maintained by the Amtsgericht (Local Court) of the district of the registered office; all filings are legally submitted through a certified German Notar. Tax and trade registrations are effected separately at the local Finanzamt and Gewerbeamt.

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG), Sections 1 (legal form), 2 and 3 (form and content of Articles of Association), 5 (minimum share capital of EUR 25,000), 7 (conditions for registration), 8 (content of registration application), 10-11 (effect of registration and separate legal personality), and 35-44 (managing directors); supplemented by Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB) Sections 106-108 on Commercial Register filings and signature specimens.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notariell beurkundeter Gesellschaftsvertrag (notarized Articles of Association) and Anmeldung zur Eintragung (electronic application for entry into the Commercial Register), filed by the notary together with the shareholder list, managing director declarations, and capital payment certificates.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The euro is freely usable and exchangeable without a central bank allocation, hard-reserve requirement, or prior exchange-control license. Freedom of capital movement derives from the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU Art. 63) and the German liberalisation principle under the AWG. Capital and current account transactions are subject only to (i) statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border payments to or from non-residents reaching EUR 12,500 in a calendar month; (ii) mandatory EU/UN sanctions and embargo screening; and (iii) tax compliance. Profit remittance by a GmbH is not exchange-controlled, but dividends paid to non-resident shareholders trigger a 25% withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 26.375%), unless a double-tax treaty reduction, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption, or refund claim applies. No exit tax on the conversion of EUR into foreign currency, no cross-border payment approval, and no profit remittance quota exist in German law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. The exporting GmbH may freely invoice in EUR or any foreign currency and may retain foreign-currency balances onshore or within the EEA. The only mandatory obligation is the AWV statistical report to Deutsche Bundesbank: inbound payments from non-residents of at least EUR 12,500 in one month must be reported electronically with the applicable balance-of-payments purpose classification. This is a reporting duty only, not a surrender or conversion obligation. Export proceeds from or involving EU/UN sanctioned jurisdictions or asset-freeze-listed counterparties are blocked and must be suspended; such blocked funds may not be converted or credited to the commercial account until the prohibition is lifted.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) as the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany and member of the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); prudential regulation and supervision of credit institutions is exercised by BaFin (Bundesanstalt fuer Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht), while the Bundesbank administers the external economic statistical reporting regime under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG) and Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a multi-currency treasury overlay, but Low-Standard as the sole banking channel. German and EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Pliant, Solaris/Kontist, Wise Business, and Currencycloud can onboard the GmbH remotely using BSI/BNetzA-recognised VideoIdent, providing EUR/USD/GBP sub-accounts and marketplace payout integration. Legal constraints: an EMI is not a bank; it holds no full German banking licence, is not a TARGET2 direct participant, and its accounts are not covered by the statutory deposit guarantee (Einlagensicherung). Client funds are safeguarded as Sondervermögen under the e-money regime, but settlement finality and central-bank protections are absent; non-EEA offshore EMIs without a PSD2/EMD2 European passport cannot lawfully maintain German corporate deposit relationships. In addition, EMIs frequently de-risk high-volume marketplace flows and apply lower aggregate exposure limits. The mandated structure is therefore a Tier-1 bank account as the settlement anchor, with an EMI overlay restricted to platform-pay collection and cross-border vendor payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the non-resident shareholder must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA, if the shareholder is EEA-based) with the structured payment reference 'GmbH Kapitaleinlage / Shareholder Equity Contribution' and the notarial deed registration number. The German receiving bank credits the dedicated Capital Formation Account (Gruendungskonto/Nominalkapitalkonto) and validates the notarised subscription, the commercial register pre-registration, and the statutory declaration that the funds are permanently at the free disposal of the Geschaeftsfuehrer. The bank assigns the AWV statistical purpose code (balance-of-payments catalogue classification) so that Bundesbank reporting occurs automatically if the transfer is at least EUR 12,500; otherwise the resident GmbH must file the monthly external-position report manually. The payment must not be mingled with operating receipts until the GmbH is registered and the capital-formation escrow restriction is lifted. A contemporaneous Transparenzregister filing confirming the UBO names completes the onboarding verification chain.Standard rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfers and SEPA Instant Credit Transfers for EEA suppliers, executed with pain.001 ISO 20022 messages and BIC/IBAN validation; (ii) SWIFT MT103 payments for non-EEA suppliers, carrying the AWV purpose code, invoice reference, and contract number; (iii) documentary Collections and Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) for wholesale customers, advised through the bank's LC desk via MT700/MT710, with drafts, commercial invoices, packing lists, and transport documents; (iv) Marketplace and Platform-Pay payouts are swept periodically from EMIs or platform settlement accounts into the Tier-1 operational account and then re-dispatched to suppliers by the same SEPA/SWIFT rails; (v) FX conversions, when needed, settle via the bank treasury desk on T+0/T+2.

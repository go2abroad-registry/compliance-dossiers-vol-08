Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register) at the local Amtsgericht (Local Court) with electronic registration through the e-Handelsregister portal.

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG), in particular §§ 1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 10 and 39; supplemented by §§ 1-12 Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB) regarding commercial register entries.

Formation / constitutional filings

Anmeldung zur Eintragung einer Gesellschaft mit beschränkter Haftung (application for registration) together with the notarized Gesellschaftsvertrag (Articles of Association), the Gesellschafterliste (shareholder list), and the managed authorization forms.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Statutory minimum share capital is EUR 25,000; at least EUR 12,500 (25%) must be paid-up before application. The deposited capital must be available to the managing directors at free disposal, evidenced by a bank confirmation (Bankbestätigung) of the capital escrow account.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection and certification (Brandschutzabnahme) under the applicable Landesbauordnung; structural load verification for printing and lamination machinery; occupational safety clearance by the Gewerbeaufsicht (workplace supervisory authority) and the Berufsgenossenschaft (BG) covering machine guarding, solvent handling, ergonomic controls, and emergency response readiness.

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum share capital is EUR 25,000; at least EUR 12,500 (25%) must be paid-up before application. The deposited capital must be available to the managing directors at free disposal, evidenced by a bank confirmation (Bankbestätigung) of the capital escrow account.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
First Shareholder-Board Resolution of the GmbH. RESOLVED, that the company be incorporated as a Gesellschaft mit beschränkter Haftung under German law; that the subscription of registered share capital of EUR 25,000, fully paid to the extent required by law, be approved, each shareholder confirming payment of subscribed contributions; that the registered office be established at the street address inserted in the Articles of Association as the legal seat and domicile; that [Name of MD] and [Name of MD] be and are hereby appointed managing directors (Geschäftsführer) with sole or joint representation as resolved; that such directors open or maintain the capital escrow account in free disposal; and that all notarial, filing, and procedural steps be authorized.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Gewerbeamt (local trade notification authority) – no separate industrial license is mandated for low-impact collectible card printing and cold foil lamination under German federal law, provided emission thresholds under the Bundes-Immissionsschutzgesetz (BImSchG) are not exceeded.

Category: None – trade registration pursuant to § 14 Gewerbeordnung (GewO) and product safety compliance under Produktsicherheitsgesetz (ProdSG) suffice for the described production activity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) as policy overseer; Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT ID assignment and central administration; local Finanzamt (district tax office) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT collection; electronic filing via ELSTER portal (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (statutory tax registration questionnaire) to the local Finanzamt competent for the GmbH's place of management (Geschäftsleitung), per § 138 AO; include articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag), commercial register excerpt (Handelsregisterauszug), and beneficial ownership declarations. Step 2: Obtain a Steuernummer (local tax number) issued by the Finanzamt. Step 3: Apply separately to the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for a USt-IdNr (EU VAT identification number) via form 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer' (§ 27a UStG); foreign parent entities may also request a certificate pursuant to § 1a UStG for intra-Community acquisitions. Step 4: Register for advance VAT returns (Umsatzsteuer-Voranmeldungen) via ELSTER; tax registration is deemed granted upon issuance of the Steuernummer and USt-IdNr, with the GmbH then being subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax), Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge), Gewerbesteuer (trade tax), and Umsatzsteuer (VAT).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (GmbH is a body corporate under § 1 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 KStG; per se fully taxable at corporate level; no pass-through eligibility; resident corporation with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income for German tax purposes, subject to DTT relief; no special tax-exempt zone status; trade-taxable enterprise per § 2 Abs. 2 GewStG unless specific statutory exemption applies)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under German law; entity classification is statutorily conclusive. Available elections: (a) Organschaft (fiscal unity) election per §§ 14-19 KStG to pool corporate income, trade tax, and VAT results between a controlling parent and the GmbH, subject to profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) and financial integration requirements; (b) hybrid mismatch elections and re-characterization options under §§ 4k, 4l EStG (ATAD-Umsetzungsgesetz) to align deduction/non-deduction treatment for hybrid instruments and permanent establishments; (c) option for a transparent GmbH & Co. KG structure where the GmbH is the general partner, if business restructuring is pursued; (d) election under § 1a KStG for a German permanent establishment of a foreign entity to be treated as deemed resident in limited M&A scenarios; (e) special depreciation and investment reserve elections (§ 7g EStG) that affect taxable income timing but not entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic small businesses, the Kleinunternehmerregelung (§ 19 UStG) applies up to EUR 22,000 prior-year gross turnover, but this relief is unavailable to a GmbH engaged in cross-border trading-card exports unless it is a small business under EU provisions; for foreign entities and distance sales, the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) threshold is EUR 10,000 for cross-border intra-Community distance sales of goods, while no generally available VAT registration threshold applies to a German-resident GmbH performing taxable supplies, and a foreign entity without domestic establishment is required to register for VAT in Germany immediately upon any taxable supply or intra-Community acquisition that triggers nexus

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to export supplies (Ausfuhrlieferungen) under § 4 Nr. 1a UStG and § 6 UStG where the collectible trading cards are physically dispatched or transported by the seller or a third-party carrier to a destination in a third-country territory (i.e., outside the EU) and actually leave the EU customs territory; eligibility also requires the recipient to be an entrepreneur or a legal person, the goods to be substantially produced or held in the German business, and documentary proof that the goods have irrevocably left the EU; supplies of offshore services (e.g., design and printing services for non-EU customers) may be zero-rated under B2B reverse-charge rules of § 3a UStG if the recipient is an entrepreneur outside the EU; the intra-Community supply exemption to EU customers requires a valid VAT ID of the purchaser and a recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung).

  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement), Air Waybill (Luftfrachtbrief), or CMR transport document evidencing physical dispatch from Germany to the third-country destination
  • Bookkeeping evidence (Belegnachweis per § 10 UStDV and Buchnachweis per § 13 UStDV) including commercial invoice, delivery note, proof of payment or consideration, and transport order documents
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/foreign-exchange receipts demonstrating actual payment received from the foreign customer, where required by the German customs or tax office for validating the export consideration
  • Form USt 1 Export-Nachweis or digital records in the ELSTER system supporting the exempt export supply declared on the advance VAT return

Mandatory e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions effective January 1, 2025, requiring structured electronic invoices compliant with EN 16931 (formats: XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.3) transmitted via Peppol network, EDI, or certified API-based clearance interfaces; mandatory submission of digital advance VAT returns (Umsatzsteuer-Voranmeldungen) via ELSTER within 10 days after the end of each calendar month for monthly filers; no government-run real-time fiscal stamping or tax clearance seal applies in Germany for invoices, but from January 1, 2027 e-invoicing becomes mandatory for companies with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000, and from January 1, 2028 for all companies; export transactions require the electronic customs export declaration in the ATLAS customs IT system and the supplementary electronic delivery verification records.

German tax authorities (Finanzamt and Hauptzollamt) conduct risk-based computerized screening of export zero-rating claims; where export substantiation is incomplete or the goods are diverted within the EU, VAT is assessed retroactively with interest at 0.5% per month (§ 233a AO); fraudulent zero-rating claims, fake export evidence, or refund abuse trigger criminal tax evasion penalties under § 370 AO of up to five years imprisonment or fines, plus forfeiture; negligent claims are punishable under § 378 AO with administrative fines up to EUR 50,000; input VAT previously recovered is clawed back with statutory interest, and the Grundsatz der Verhältnismäßigkeit may be displaced in default; repeated offenses may lead to trade license withdrawal and disqualification of managing directors.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany and the euro area maintain full current account and capital account convertibility under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG), and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV). No prior exchange control approval is required for payments, trade settlements, profit remittances, dividend distributions, or capital injections. Profit remittance is legally unrestricted, provided the GmbH complies with corporate law solvency tests, commercial registry filings, tax withholdings (e.g. 26.375% capital gains tax plus solidarity surcharge on dividend distributions unless treaty relief or reduced rate applies), and anti-money laundering documentation for beneficial owners. Non-resident shareholders may repatriate proceeds without volume or maturity limits, but banks may request evidence of lawful source of funds and proof of tax residence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement in Germany or the eurozone. Export receipts may be retained, denominated, exchanged, hedged, and transferred freely. The AWV only imposes statistical reporting obligations on cross-border payments above specific thresholds (currently €12,500 per transaction or aggregated monthly balance above €12,500 on foreign deposits) to be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics. No mandatory conversion ratio applies to export earnings.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the Federal Ministry of Finance (BMF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Cross-border financial technology electronic money institutions (EMIs) and neobanks with EU banking or e-money licenses may open accounts for a German GmbH remotely under EU passporting. These providers are operationally viable for recurring receivables, merchant settlements, and low-value cross-border disbursements, but they are insufficient for capital escrow, documentary trade finance, official German statutory deposits, or wholesale liquidity needs. Some EMIs avoid BaFin-regulated bank status and do not hold eligible deposit protection; a German GmbH requiring public registry proof of capital contributions or long-term treasury facilities should maintain at least one tier-1 bank account. Corporate assessors should also verify compliance with German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) requirements and the bank's ability to process Luxembourg or other European IBANs without prejudicial ingress restrictions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Unidentified Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) or discrepancy in Transparency Register ownership declaration

Root cause: Unidentified Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) or discrepancy in Transparency Register ownership declaration

Defense evidence: Current excerpt from the German Transparency Register (Transparenzregister), notarised share register, shareholders' resolution on ultimate beneficial ownership, complete chain of ownership declarations to each natural person, and an explanatory letter signed by all managing directors confirming 25%-plus ownership thresholds.
Unclear source of funds for capital escrow injection from non-resident shareholders

Root cause: Unclear source of funds for capital escrow injection from non-resident shareholders

Defense evidence: Notarised capital increase agreement or original contribution deed, bank statements from the paying foreign institution covering the preceding 12 months, certified copies of directors' passports, evidence of sale of goods or services, tax declarations or proof of inheritance/gift for natural person investors, and a formal source-of-funds declaration under German Banking Act (KWG) account opening standards.
Politically Exposed Persons (PEP) or close-associate flags involving any director or shareholder

Root cause: Politically Exposed Persons (PEP) or close-associate flags involving any director or shareholder

Defense evidence: PEP self-declaration, current sanctions and PEP screening reports from a recognised provider, explanation of business position and financial resources, government-issued ID, proof of lawful background, and an independent legal opinion issued by a German counsel on the relevance, if any, of the PEP designation.
Cross-border online gaming / collectible card trading flagged as high cash-flow or potential gambling-adjacent activity

Root cause: Cross-border online gaming / collectible card trading flagged as high cash-flow or potential gambling-adjacent activity

Defense evidence: Business plan describing physical and digital manufacturing of licensed sports and entertainment trading cards, ISIC 3240 classification reference, NAICS 323111 supporting reference, sample commercial contracts with suppliers and distributors, existing merchant acquirer agreement from Stripe or Authorize.Net, and a compliance letter stating that no gambling, betting, secondary lottery, or unregulated tokenised asset activities are conducted.
High turnover of cross-border payments with ambiguous payment purpose on instructions

Root cause: High turnover of cross-border payments with ambiguous payment purpose on instructions

Defense evidence: Detailed treasury policy describing standard SWIFT MT103 purpose codes and SEPA structured creditor references, invoice and purchase order backup per payment batch, transactional mapping to underlying trade contracts, consolidated monthly reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank (if threshold exceeded), and a signed attestation by the finance director that all payouts relate to card manufacturing, printing, logistics, royalties, or licensing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the German GmbH must be executed via SEPA Credit Transfer (IBAN denominated in EUR) when the sending bank is within the European Economic Area, or via SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA sources. The payer must state the payment purpose code referencing the legal transaction, such as 'Stammeinlage' (registered capital subscription), 'Kapitalrücklage' (capital reserve) or 'Bezug des neuen Geschäftsanteils' (acquisition of newly issued share), and include the company registration number (HRB) and the name of the investing shareholder. For foreign exchange conversions, the receiving German bank may require underlying documents: notarial deed of the capital increase, closing proof, and tax registration certificate. Fund verification is performed against the backdrop of German money laundering rules, Bundesbank balance-of-payments reporting requirements, and the bank's internal Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Screening framework. On receipt, the account is credited as verifiable capital; the funds must be used for the GmbH's statutory purpose, such as purchase of prepress equipment, holographic foil inventory, packaging, distribution, or trade settlement, and must not be moved offshore without documented rationale.Outbound trade settlements for raw materials, specialty holographic films, printing consumables, and international distribution contracts shall be executed through: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer for EU-based suppliers with standard IBAN wire; (ii) SWIFT MT103 direct transfers for non-EU suppliers and licensing partners; (iii) documentary commercial letters of credit (L/C) for large-volume procurements from non-EU producers where added bank intermediation protects against payment risk; and (iv) bank guarantees or standby L/Cs when contractual performance bonds are required. Payment instructions must include precise purpose codes, e.g. 'SUPP' or standard proprietary bank codes, plus invoice numbers and a structured reference matching the underlying trade contract. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

30.0% combined (15.0% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the corporate income tax plus municipal trade tax Gewerbesteuer, typically 14–17%, resulting in an aggregate effective statutory rate of approximately 29–31% depending on the municipality)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer (withholding tax) plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the withheld tax (i.e., 1.375% additive), resulting in an aggregate statutory withholding burden of 26.375% on gross dividends distributed by the GmbH to a foreign corporate shareholder. If the dividend is distributed to a non-resident entity that is not a shareholder of record in the commercial register, the rate is still 25% under § 44a Abs. 1 EStG.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Deadline rule: The tax return is due by 31 July of the year following the fiscal year-end; automatic extension to 31 August where the return is filed electronically (dating from tax assessment periods 2021 onward); further extended by 6 months (to end of February) if the taxpayer is professionally represented by a tax advisor (Steuerberater), i.e., electronically filed returns represented by a professional are due by the last day of February of the second year after the fiscal year-end; late payment interest (Nachzahlungszinsen) at 0.15% per month on any assessed tax not settled by the original due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 for local file documentation (Local File) and EUR 700,000 for master file documentation (Master File) under the German Tax Administration Act (§ 90 Abs. 3 AO) based on German Tax Transfer Pricing Documentation Regulations (Verwaltungsgrundsätze-Verfahren); the threshold is applied to the cumulative consideration (cross-border intra-group transactions) in the fiscal year. Transactions with related parties of any size must be documented contemporaneously if the tax authority requests a Transfer Pricing Audit (Betriebsprüfung), and substantial compliance requires a master file for groups with consolidated group revenues exceeding EUR 700 million and German turnover exceeding EUR 7 million. Penalty for failure to properly document: irrebuttable presumption of adverse pricing (Beweislastumkehr) and fine of at least EUR 5,000, up to 5–10% of the adjustment, at least EUR 2,500 under § 162 Abs. 3–4 AO.

Economic substance scope: Germany has not introduced specific Economic Substance Regulations comparable to Cayman/BVI ESR. However, the following doctrines apply: (1) Substance-over-form (wirtschaftliche Betrachtungsweise) under § 5 Abs. 1 EStG and § 41 Abs. 2 AO: an artificial legal structure lacking economic reality is disregarded; (2) Anti-abuse under § 42 AO: abusive arrangements (missbräuchliche Gestaltungen) may be ignored and the income attributed to the economically substantive entity; (3) General anti-avoidance under the interest limitation rule (§ 4h EStG) and royalty barrier (Lizenzschranke § 4j EStG requiring the final recipient of the royalty to be subject to an effective rate of at least 25% and non-beneficial-owner, otherwise the deduction is capped at 25% of net operating income); (4) thin capitalization rules: interest on shareholder loans treated as constructive dividends where the debt is not at arm's length or the lender is a non-resident shareholder without German tax liability; (5) unilateral CFC regime (§§ 7–13 AStG): passive income of a low-tax foreign subsidiary (effective rate <25%) may be attributed to the German GmbH; (6) treaty-shopping limitation under § 50d Abs. 3 EStG requiring the foreign company to have substance (business premises, employees, economic activity) to claim treaty relief; the German tax authority (BZSt) will refuse a TRC if the claimant has no real presence in its state of residence. For a German GmbH producing collectible trading cards, Economic Substance means the operational substance (printing presses, pre-press equipment, inventory, employees, production planning, and risk management) is resident in Germany and cannot be migrated solely to a lower-tax jurisdiction without an actual PE and real functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years under § 370 Abs. 1 AO; in especially severe cases (large-scale evasion under § 370 Abs. 3, e.g., tax shortfall exceeding €50,000 per act or involving the misuse of a tax-avoidance mechanism) imprisonment from 6 months up to 10 years; fines under § 41 StGB at daily rates of up to €30,000 per daily unit; the civil liability under § 71 AO includes the automatic VAT-specific liability for false invoicing; any tax underpaid is assessed without statute of limitation (10 years for tax evasion, 5 years for negligent under-assessments); criminal proceedings can also be supported by a tax surcharge of up to 100% of the evasion amount in lieu of prosecution without sentence under § 398a AO.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil: the managing director (Geschäftsführer) and, in cases of de facto management, a shareholder-director are jointly and severally liable under § 69 AO and § 34 AO for all taxes of the GmbH not withheld/declared, including corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT, and wage tax; a criminal conviction for tax evasion may result in a travel ban (passport withdrawal) and confiscation orders; director's personal assets (business bank accounts, residential property) may be attached; if the tax loss remains unpaid, the insolvency administrator can sue the director for reimbursement; additionally, the board can be banned from serving as a director for up to 5 years under § 76 GmbHG. Tax advisers are excluded from personal liability unless involved in intentional wrongdoing.
Armed Forces / Export Sanctions & Embargo (not applicable to trading cards, but fatal red flag for using sanctions-evading payment channels)

Statutory sanction: Trade sanctions violations under § 18 AWG (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz) carry imprisonment of up to 5 years or fines; administrative fines up to €500,000 for negligent violations of export licensing obligations; asset freeze measures by the German Federal Office of Economics and Export Control (BAFA) and the European Commission may additionally debar the company from participating in EU public procurement for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the GmbH to route payments through sanctioned persons incur personal criminal liability under § 18 AWG and § 82 AWV; the competent court may order a personal fine and confiscation of profits; a travel ban is applied when flight risk is established; corporate veil piercing is available under § 823 BGB for intentional harm to a third party's protected interest, and under administrative practice the foreign export permit for dual-use goods is denied when the director's record shows sanctions evasion.
AML (Geldwäschebedrohung) – Payment String Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Violations of § 56 GwG (obligations to identify beneficial owners, record-keeping, AML compliance) attract monetary fines up to €100,000 in general, but up to €1,000,000 for legal persons and 5% of worldwide annual turnover for serious violations under § 56 Abs. 2 and EU Directive 2015/849; underlying predicates for money laundering in Germany are defined in § 261 StGB and carry imprisonment of up to 5 years, and in aggravated cases (commercial scale) imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years; the FIU may prohibit the payment service provider (Stripe) from serving the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for AML compliance under § 40 Abs. 2 GwG are personally liable for fines and may be disqualified from managerial service in financial institutions; civil liability under the principle of personal fault (Organisationsverschulden) can render the director personally liable for the financial institution's fines, and criminal conviction under § 261 StGB may include seizure of personal assets and travel bans; in the event of a still not identified BO, the residency permit of an EU non-citizen director is subject to revocation.
Constructive Dividend (vGA) & Hidden Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The tax authority recharacterizes the excessive payment as a constructive dividend: disallowed deduction with an add-back of the gross amount, plus 25% Kapitalertragsteuer withheld from the deemed dividend (26.375% including solidarity surcharge), and late-payment interest of 6% p.a.; additional transfer pricing penalty under § 162 AO of 5–10% of the adjustment (minimum €5,000), provided the taxpayer has not submitted compliant documentation; the adjusted income may additionally be subject to trade tax and, where the recipient is a low-tax jurisdiction, the CFC rules under §§ 7–13 AStG.

Director personal liability: The managing director who knowingly concludes an unbalanced transaction with an affiliated foreign company is personally liable under § 43 GmbHG for the GmbH's resulting tax loss; the director may further face unlawful reduction of capital and criminal investigation for tax evasion; the tax authority may collect the net expense equivalent from the director personally as the person who caused a violation of § 42 AO; a director's travel abroad may be blocked via a temporary passport seizure if the authorities suspect secret profit repatriation; a disqualification order from the tax authority (GeFA) prevents future directorial mandates.
Late Filing / Failure to Disclose (Verspätungszuschlag, Zwangsgeld)

Statutory sanction: A late-filing surcharge of €25 per month (minimum) up to 10% of the assessed tax; coercive fine (Zwangsmittel) of up to €2,500 per reminder under § 329 AO; interest of 0.5% per month (6% p.a.) on the unsettled amount; if the return is not filed within 3 months of the final reminder, criminal offense of tax evasion may be initiated under § 370 AO where the authority can estimate taxable income and issue a penal assessment (Sicherheitsleistung) at double the estimated amount.

Director personal liability: A director who intentionally fails to file is jointly and severally liable for the assessed tax plus surcharges under §§ 34, 35, 69 AO; persistent failure to file is evidence of intent to evade, leading to imprisonment up to 5 years; a travel ban is possible to secure the criminal proceeding and may be issued by the fiscal court; the director's personal liability extends to unwarranted salaries drawn while the GmbH's tax debt was unpaid.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital EUR 25,000. At least EUR 12,500 (50%) must be paid in cash before submission of the registration application. Cash contributions are held in a blocked escrow account (Anderkonto) at a German credit institution, released only upon completion of Commercial Register entry.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr) and administrative assistance; local Finanzamt (defined by registered seat) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment; primary federal portal is ELSTER (www.elster.de) supplemented by BZSt Online services (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

A GmbH must first be entered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the Amtsgericht. The managing director(s) file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (legal form GmbH) with the competent local Finanzamt via ELSTER under Sections 137-138 AO and Section 27a UStG. The Finanzamt issues a single tax number (Steuernummer) covering Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer, and Umsatzsteuer, typically within 2-6 weeks. Separately, the GmbH must apply for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr) with the BZSt via the electronic form 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer USt-IdNr' (online or through ELSTER). For non-resident foreign entrepreneurs undertaking taxable supplies in Germany, registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction without threshold, and the BZSt assigns the USt-IdNr directly; a fiscal representative (Fiskalvertreter) may be appointed, and the foreign entrepreneur must file electronically via ELSTER using the BZSt registration procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The GmbH is a body corporate under the Körperschaftsteuergesetz (KStG): 15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax due, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a municipal collection rate (Hebesatz) of approximately 250%-450%, resulting in a typical aggregate tax burden of 29%-33%. German tax residency follows place of management and registered seat; residents are taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credits, and no territorial election is available.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box or partnership classification election for GmbHs. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) tax consolidation (ertragsteuerliche Organschaft) under Section 14 KStG and Section 2 GewStG, requiring a majority voting/membership interest, an executed profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag), and integration of the dependent company; (ii) VAT fiscal unity (Umsatzsteuer-Organschaft) under Section 2(2) no. 2 UStG through financial, economic, and organisational integration; (iii) no hybrid-mismatch elections are permitted - the ATAD II rules in Section 4k EStG and Section 8b KStG override hybrid mismatch outcomes, and Germany has published binding tax treatment rules for hybrid entities. The small-business VAT relief election under Section 19 UStG is unavailable or economically irrelevant for an export-oriented GmbH manufacturer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold for resident entrepreneurs; any commercial activity triggers VAT registration. The small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung, Section 19 UStG) applies only where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year forecast turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000, and is in practice unavailable to an export-manufacturing GmbH. Non-established foreign entrepreneurs must register from the first supply of goods or services in Germany without any threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods from Germany to third countries (non-EU) are exempt from German VAT under Section 4 no. 1 lit. a UStG in conjunction with Section 6 UStG, provided the supplier or the purchaser on behalf of the supplier exports the goods directly, and the goods leave the EU customs territory; transport to other EU Member States qualifies as an intra-Community supply exempt under Section 6a UStG only if the supplier (i) supplies to an entrepreneur, (ii) the customer provides a valid VAT-ID confirmed via VIES, and (iii) dispatch documents are retained. Constructive export (Section 9 UStG) applies to goods processed on behalf of a third-country principal. Exported goods destined for third countries must be declared electronically in ATLAS; the supplier must possess and retain the export contract, commercial invoice, transport documents, and customs documents evidencing the correct exit and arrival. Export-related services supplied to a foreign entrepreneur may also be zero-rated when the place of supply is outside Germany and the recipient is a taxable business subject to reverse charge.

  • Electronic customs export declaration in ATLAS (AES) generating the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and the customs export document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument)
  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung) stating seller, buyer, INCOTERMS, full goods description (HS code), and the German EORI number
  • Transport document evidencing physical exit from the EU: Bill of Lading (seafreight), Air Waybill (airfreight), or CMR consignment note (road freight)
  • Export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) under Sections 8-13 UStDV, generally to be furnished by the 10th working day of the month following the month of export, via customs evidence or carrier certification
  • Bank remittance evidence (SWIFT MT103/TT wire) or other consideration proof; payments above EUR 12,500 additionally trigger Deutsche Bundesbank balance-of-payments reporting obligations under the Aussenwirtschaftsverordnung (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung)

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance API at national level. Since 1 January 2025 all resident VAT payers are obliged to be able to receive electronic invoices for domestic B2B supplies, using structured formats XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.0.1/FACTUR-X, or the EDI standard. Issuance of e-invoices is phased: mandatory from 1 January 2027 for entrepreneurs with taxable turnover above EUR 800,000 in 2025, and mandatory for all entrepreneurs (including below-threshold businesses) from 1 January 2028. VAT pre-registrations and VAT returns must be submitted exclusively through ELSTER in XML/Taxonomie format; monthly advance returns and annual returns are mandatory, and any temporary extension (Dauerfristverlängerung) requires an application and, where applicable, a security deposit. Exported goods fall outside the German domestic e-invoicing scope; export documentation is instead channelled through the customs ATLAS system and reconciled against VAT listings.

German law imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark mirrors standard commercial letter-of-credit and open-account export terms, and export proceeds must be reflected in the Bundesbank external trade statistics where threshold amounts apply. If export zero-rating is claimed without the required Section 6 UStG export proof, the Finanzamt retrospectively reverses the exemption and assesses 19% VAT with default interest at 0.5% per month under Section 233a AO, late-payment surcharges (Säumniszuschlag) of 1% per commenced month under Section 240 AO, and late-filing penalties under Section 152 AO. Fraudulent or grossly negligent export claims constitute tax evasion under Section 370 AO, exposing the responsible managing directors to criminal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years, with corporate administrative fines under Section 26a UStG and Section 379 AO. Retrospective assessment periods are generally 4 years, extending to 10 years in evasion cases under Sections 169-170 AO; in warranted cases the BZSt and customs investigation services (Steuerfahndung) conduct joint cross-border audits and issue clawback notices without any statute of limitations until the factual basis is resolved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the euro, with no exchange controls, no prior-approval requirements, and no restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, loan interest, trade proceeds, or capital divestment proceeds, as guaranteed by Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital and payments) and the AWG. The euro is a freely convertible and freely floating currency in both onshore and offshore markets. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted; the only material frictions are tax-side and administrative: (i) a statutory 25% dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% aggregate), reducible to 0% for EU corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) or to a reduced treaty rate under an applicable double tax treaty (e.g., 5% for substantial holdings); and (ii) mandatory statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank for any cross-border payment exceeding €12,500, and quarterly reporting of aggregate claims or liabilities against non-residents exceeding €5,000,000. None of these constitute legal barriers to currency conversion or profit repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline. A German GmbH may lawfully retain, deposit, convert, or otherwise freely deploy 100% of its export receipts in EUR or any convertible foreign currency. The only binding obligation is statistical: export proceeds received from non-resident buyers must be reported for balance-of-payments purposes to the Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV where the individual payment exceeds €12,500, with the report due by the 7th calendar day after the end of the reporting month; in practice, the executing German bank submits the AWV report automatically through its payment-reporting system. No conversion or surrender obligation attaches to any portion of export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank, Frankfurt am Main), acting as the primary administrator of foreign-exchange statistics and cross-border payment monitoring under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); supranational monetary authority for the euro: European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem; export-control licensing supervision shared with BAFA (Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium — feasible as a supplementary treasury and FX execution rail, but not as the primary banking relationship. German or EEA Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed by BaFin and passported under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Europe, Airwallex, Revolut) may open a German IBAN account and execute cross-border payments; the legal constraints are: (i) EMIs are not credit institutions under the German Banking Act (KWG) and cannot grant overdrafts, receivables financing, or documentary credits; (ii) safeguarded client funds are not covered by the statutory deposit-guarantee scheme under DGSD; (iii) BaFin applies heightened UBO transparency and anti-money-laundering controls to EMIs with non-EEA parentage; (iv) some Asian and Latin-American counterparty banks treat EMI IBANs as second-tier for KYC purposes and may reject them in letter-of-credit negotiations; and (v) AWV statistical reporting obligations attach to every cross-border payment regardless of the executing institution. Recommended deployment: collections, low-value supplier payouts, interbank-margin-plus FX conversion, and payroll, with the tier-1 bank retained for escrow, documentary trade, and hedging.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital injection: (i) for euro payments from EEA parent or shareholder accounts, the standard rail is the SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) in ISO 20022 pain.001 format, cleared instantaneously and finally through the TARGET Services real-time gross settlement system; for USD or non-EEA EUR injections, the rail is SWIFT MT103 from the investor's correspondent bank to the GmbH's IBAN at Deutsche Bank or Commerzbank, with the 70-field purpose narrative quoting the notarized capital-increase deed or loan-agreement reference; (ii) the payment purpose must be encoded on the bank's AWV payment instruction using the Bundesbank standard classification, with equity contributions and shareholder loans reportable under the direct-investment or credit-transactions category whenever the individual amount exceeds €12,500, and aggregate quarterly reporting where liabilities to non-residents exceed €5,000,000; (iii) the receiving bank verifies the notarized contribution resolution, proof that the statutory minimum capital of €25,000 is fully covered, the provenance declaration, and UBO re-screening of the injecting entity; (iv) where the shareholder is outside the EEA, enhanced sanctions and negative-news screening applies, and the recommended practice is to pre-notify the relationship manager three business days before the wire so the incoming MT103 matches the approved KYC file without a compliance hold.Clearing rails for outbound trade settlements: (i) euro payments within the European Economic Area move by SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001 XML, settled in TARGET Services, value same-day or next-day), with SEPA Instant Credit Transfer available up to €100,000 for urgent disbursements; (ii) euro payments to non-EEA countries and all USD payments move by SWIFT MT103 via the tier-1 bank's correspondent network — Deutsche Bank and Commerzbank clear USD through their New York correspondent accounts (J.P. Morgan and BNY Mellon) feeding Fedwire, while the euro leg settles in TARGET with a customer-transfer transaction code; (iii) for documentary trade with buyers requiring secured settlement, the bank issues a Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP600 by SWIFT MT700, with MT707 amendments, payable against compliant transport and commercial documents; for stable counterparties, Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) under URC522 reduce cost and documentation burden; (iv) every outbound payment triggers AWV reporting to the Bundesbank: goods imports use payment groups 31 and 32 (creditors from goods deliveries), intra-group loan repayments use the direct-investment loan repayment code, and the filing is executed automatically through the bank within the statutory seven-calendar-day window after the value month; (v) no German foreign-exchange approval or license applies to any outbound trade or financial disbursement, except that dual-use goods require BAFA export clearance, which is an export-control checkpoint rather than a payment-rail restriction.Outright FX forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) in EUR/USD, EUR/CNY, and EUR/GBP for firm export receivables of the optical-lens business, executed OTC with Deutsche Bank or Commerzbank under the German Master Agreement for Financial Futures Transactions (Rahmenvertrag für Finanztermingeschäfte); no regulatory approval is required for a non-financial corporate hedger.Currency swaps (FX swaps and rollover swaps) enabling rolling conversion of USD receivables into EUR and precise maturity matching against supplier payables denominated in CNY, TWD, or KRW.Cross-currency swaps (Währungsswaps) for medium-term EUR/USD asset-liability matching on intra-group shareholder loans and USD-denominated letter-of-credit financing.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on non-convertible or restricted Asian currencies (offshore CNH, INR, KRW, TWD) with EUR/USD settlement, permitted exclusively for genuine commercial-hedging exposure and executed through a qualified international bank counterparty.Vanilla FX options (EUR/USD, EUR/CNY calls and puts) on an OTC basis; the GmbH must undergo a MiFID II appropriateness assessment and elective-professional-client classification at onboarding, with any resulting EMIR derivative reporting and clearing-threshold assessment completed through the bank, while commercial-hedging transactions of a non-financial undertaking remain exempt from centralized clearing.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium — feasible as a supplementary treasury and FX execution rail, but not as the primary banking relationship. German or EEA Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed by BaFin and passported under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Europe, Airwallex, Revolut) may open a German IBAN account and execute cross-border payments; the legal constraints are: (i) EMIs are not credit institutions under the German Banking Act (KWG) and cannot grant overdrafts, receivables financing, or documentary credits; (ii) safeguarded client funds are not covered by the statutory deposit-guarantee scheme under DGSD; (iii) BaFin applies heightened UBO transparency and anti-money-laundering controls to EMIs with non-EEA parentage; (iv) some Asian and Latin-American counterparty banks treat EMI IBANs as second-tier for KYC purposes and may reject them in letter-of-credit negotiations; and (v) AWV statistical reporting obligations attach to every cross-border payment regardless of the executing institution. Recommended deployment: collections, low-value supplier payouts, interbank-margin-plus FX conversion, and payroll, with the tier-1 bank retained for escrow, documentary trade, and hedging.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Tax Department (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance, via the Taxisnet e-filing portal (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy); corporate identity is created with the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver, with the Company Law HE registration number serving as the corporate registry identifier.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the Cyprus private limited company receives an HE registry number from the Registrar of Companies; the company must then submit the prescribed registration forms to the Cyprus Tax Department through its District Tax Office (or Taxisnet) to obtain a statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN), which is used for income tax, Social Insurance, and VAT purposes. A non-resident foreign entity without a Cyprus establishment selling goods or services to Cyprus consumers must apply directly for Cyprus VAT registration using the applicable VAT registration form (T.E.P. 131 / VAT Registration Application) through Taxisnet; non-EU resident entities are generally required to appoint a fiscal representative in Cyprus. The Tax Department issues the VAT registration certificate and communicates the Cyprus VAT ID, linking it to a single consolidated TIN once income tax obligations arise.

The Tax Department issues the VAT registration certificate and communicates the Cyprus VAT ID, linking it to a single consolidated TIN once income tax obligations arise.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections under Cyprus tax law include: (i) group relief election for transfer of losses among companies in a 75%+ group relationship; (ii) deemed dividend distribution election in respect of accumulated post-2014 reserves under the Special Contribution for Defence framework; (iii) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new qualifying equity capital; (iv) IP Box regime election for qualifying intangible assets providing an effective 10% IP royalty rate; (v) tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping activities; and (vi) election for exemption of income tax under the non-domiciled individuals and non-domiciled corporate anti-avoidance statutory rules. Cyprus applies ATAD-compliant hybrid mismatch restriction rules; no independent hybrid mismatch elective regimes are available beyond those required harmonization provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for taxable supplies made in Cyprus by an established business; EUR 10,000 annual pan-EU distance sales threshold for remote B2C sales of goods into Cyprus from other Member States; non-established foreign sellers deriving EU-sale distance supply volumes above EUR 10,000 must register under the Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme, and non-EU sellers must apply for the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) where available

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Cyprus to destinations outside the EU, and intra-Community dispatches of goods to EU-registered VAT payers (including cross-border distance sales to EU consumers where the Union OSS is not used), qualify for the 0% rate in Cyprus provided the Cyprus supplier demonstrates, within the statutory VAT period: (i) the goods are physically dispatched or transported by the supplier or on its behalf outside Cyprus/EU within the legally permitted timeline, (ii) the buyer is a VAT-registered person for destination supplies, (iii) the supplier retains sufficient documentary evidence of export including a customs export declaration accepted by the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department, and (iv) the transaction is genuine and proportional to the stated commercial purpose. Off-shore/intangible services supplied to recipients outside the EU and ancillary services directly related to exported goods may also be zero-rated as international services. Online e-commerce sales of 3D printers and consumables to destinations outside the EU via Stripe and Shopify Payments are eligible provided the export logistics and shipment records support physical departure from the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / AES electronic export record approved by Cyprus Customs and Excise Department)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) / Air Waybill (airfreight) / CMR consignment note (road) / postal or courier consignment receipt
  • Commercial invoice and proof of order for the specific 3D printer/resin goods shipped
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103/bank statement evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds in foreign currency
  • Proof of physical delivery or arrival confirmation at the destination (delivery confirmation, customs clearance record of the destination country)

Cyprus has not implemented a national real-time fiscal clearance regime (no continuous transaction controls, no mandatory digital stamping, no API-based pre-clearance of B2B invoices). Electronic invoices to public authorities are subject to the mandatory B2G e-invoicing framework under the PEPPOL network per EU Directive 2014/55/EU. VAT returns, OSS/IOSS schemes, and tax filing obligations are processed through the Taxisnet portal. Merchant is expected to issue electronic invoices compliant with the EU Invoicing Directive, store them for at least 10 years, and maintain digital audit trail of Shopify and Stripe payment settlement records; no separate national clearance API is mandated.

The Cyprus Tax Department conducts targeted desk and field audits of zero-rated export turnover; where the statutory export documentation is missing or the physical dispatch requirement is not independently evidenced, the VAT authority retrospectively claws back the VAT at the standard rate with statutory interest from the time the VAT return was filed. False or fraudulent zero-rating claims are penalized under Articles 48 of the VAT Law 94(I)/2000 and the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law: administrative fines up to double the evaded VAT, statutory interest on the understated tax, loss of credit recovery rights, and criminal sanctions including fines up to EUR 34,000 and imprisonment up to five years for intended tax evasion or fraudulent declarations. The Tax Department also refers grave export-VAT structuring to the Economic Crime Investigation Unit, and AML record-keeping requirements under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law apply to export and FX repatriation documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full Eurozone member having adopted the euro on 1 January 2008; capital and current account transactions are fully liberalised under EU Treaty Articles 63 to 66, Directive 88/361/EEC, and the EU Capital Movements free-movement framework. No residual exchange controls apply to current payments, profit remittances, dividend distributions, inward direct investment, or liquidation proceeds. Dividends distributed by a Cyprus tax-resident Ltd to non-resident shareholders are not subject to Cypriot withholding tax or Special Defence Contribution; the only administrative hurdles are standard AML source-of-funds verification and tax-residency self-certification under the Cyprus income tax reporting framework. The temporary capital-management measures imposed in March 2013 were fully lifted by 6 April 2015, and no re-imposition is anticipated under current Eurosystem policies. Balance-of-payments statistical declarations to the Central Bank of Cyprus are mandatory for bank-mediated transfers above EUR 12,500, but these are purely informational reporting obligations and never constitute exchange-control authorisation or convertibility approvals.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation timeline. Exporters of 3D printers, filaments, resins, and consumables may lawfully retain export receipts in EUR or any foreign currency and choose their offshore treasury location without Central Bank of Cyprus approval. The sole residual obligation is indirect statistical reporting: the Cyprus account-holding bank classifies each export receipt under ECB Regulation 184/2005 Balance-of-Payments reporting, and for single transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 the exporter must present the relevant commercial invoice, bill of lading or electronic delivery record, customs declaration, and PSP settlement statement to the bank. Zero percent of export proceeds is subject to conversion.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC); AML enforcement and financial intelligence coordination through the Cyprus Unit for Combating Money Laundering (MOKAS); CySEC has supervisory authority only when regulated activities such as e-money issuance are involved.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File where Cyprus-related party controlled transactions exceed EUR 750,000 in a category (goods or services), EUR 350,000 for related-party financing, or EUR 75,000 for IP and insurance transactions; Master File becomes mandatory only if the ultimate group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations framework similar to offshore centres, but tax residence requires central management and actual control in Cyprus. An e-commerce company trading 3D printers and consumables must maintain substantive board minutes, physical presence, staff or contracted services, real economic activity, and decision-making functions in Cyprus; absence of substance can result in POEM relocation, CFC exposure for the ultimate parent, denial of DTA benefits, and reclassification as a shell entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to the assessed tax evaded, administrative fines of up to EUR 50,000, statutory interest at 1.25% per month, and criminal imprisonment for up to five years under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law when a fraudulent return is filed.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution; the court may impose travel bans, departure prohibitions, freezing orders and, in aggravated cases, imprisonment of up to five years.
Shell Company / Sham Structure

Statutory sanction: Reclassification as a shell company triggers de-registration and dissolution under the Companies Law, automatic withdrawal by the full amount of tax benefits, administrative fines, and potential master-file and DAC6 penalties for failure to disclose the incentivised arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the company's debts if the entity was operated as a mere façade, and may face disqualification from acting as a director for up to 12 years plus asset restrictions.
AML / Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines of up to EUR 50,000, administrative refund penalties, compulsory and enforced striking-off, and referral to the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission or Financial Intelligence Unit where gaming of payment systems is suspected.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers are personally liable for default with imprisonment of up to five years; travel bans, asset freezes, and cross-border mutual legal assistance seizures are possible.

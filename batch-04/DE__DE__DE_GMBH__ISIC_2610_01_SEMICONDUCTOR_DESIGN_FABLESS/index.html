Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister Abteilung B (Commercial Register, Section B – HRB) of the local Amtsgericht (Registry Court) in whose district the registered office is located; the notarized application is transmitted electronically by a certified German notary via the joint register portal (www.handelsregister.de / www.unternehmensregister.de).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbHG), in particular §§ 1 (formation), 2 (form and notarization as amended by the MoPeG 2024), 5 (minimum capital), 7–8 (registration application and required exhibits), 9a (director liability for false statements), 10 (legal effect of registration), 35–37 and 78 (management, representation and application); jointly with §§ 8, 106, 108 of the German Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch – HGB) and § 382 FamFG.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) or the statutory model protocol (Musterprotokoll), filed together with the electronic application for registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) pursuant to § 8 GmbHG, the list of managing directors, specimen signatures, and proof of minimum capital payment.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Stated capital (Stammkapital) of at least EUR 25,000; a cash contribution of at least EUR 12,500 must be paid before registration into the company's escrow account (Konto der GmbH i.G.) opened with a licensed German credit institution, and the managing directors must certify to the registry that the funds are at their free disposal. In-kind contributions must be fully furnished and individually described in the articles. Since 1 January 2024 (MoPeG), online video notarization is permitted, but the capital and minimum-payment rules remain unchanged.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: use certificate or change-of-use permit (Baugenehmigung/Nutzungsänderung) confirming office use and an approved fire-protection concept (Brandschutzkonzept) from the municipal building authority (Bauaufsichtsbehörde). Occupational safety: mandatory risk assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung) under § 5 ArbSchG, first-aid and evacuation arrangements per DGUV-Vorschrift 1, and registration of the workplace with the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt; any laser-based measuring equipment above class 3R requires a structural shielding assessment and notification under the OStrV.

Paid-in capital rule: Stated capital (Stammkapital) of at least EUR 25,000; a cash contribution of at least EUR 12,500 must be paid before registration into the company's escrow account (Konto der GmbH i.G.) opened with a licensed German credit institution, and the managing directors must certify to the registry that the funds are at their free disposal. In-kind contributions must be fully furnished and individually described in the articles. Since 1 January 2024 (MoPeG), online video notarization is permitted, but the capital and minimum-payment rules remain unchanged.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office – and the locally competent Finanzamt; statutory digital portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH: after notarial incorporation and entry in the Handelsregister, the managing director must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) through ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt, obtaining the Steuernummer for corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT and wage tax. A separate application for the EU-wide VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is filed with BZSt using Form USt 1 TU. A non-resident entity making taxable supplies in Germany without a permanent establishment must register with BZSt for USt-IdNr. and with the competent Finanzamt for VAT/Steuernummer, providing certificate of incorporation, proof of identity and, if required, an authorised fiscal representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the GmbH is a legal entity subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); profits are taxed at entity level, and distributions are taxed separately at shareholder level. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the GmbH is a legal entity subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); profits are taxed at entity level, and distributions are taxed separately at shareholder level. No default pass-through treatment applies.

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box election. The GmbH may enter an Organschaft (fiscal unity) with a German parent if an executed profit-and-loss transfer agreement exists and financial, economic and organisational integration is met (§§ 14, 17, 18 KStG; § 2 GewStG); this consolidates profits and losses for corporate and trade tax, and for VAT in certain circumstances. Hybrid-mismatch consequences are constrained by § 4j EStG, § 8b KStG and the German ATAD implementation; no elective transparency is available for a GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 in the preceding calendar year and EUR 50,000 projected in the current year under § 19 UStG (small-entrepreneur exemption). A GmbH exceeding this threshold, or performing taxable supplies, is subject to mandatory VAT registration with no turnover threshold; a foreign entrepreneur performing taxable supplies in Germany must register without reference to the small-business exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a supply qualifies as a zero-rated export (Ausfuhrlieferung) under § 6 UStG when movable goods are physically transported by the supplier, the customer, or a third party on their behalf from Germany to a third-country territory, the customer is a foreign entrepreneur and the other statutory conditions are met, and the exporter holds customs and transport evidence. Services: for a fabless semiconductor business, pure B2B services and IP-core licensing supplied to a non-German/non-EU entrepreneur are outside the scope of German VAT by place-of-supply rules (customer location), rather than export zero-rated; the German supplier can recover related input VAT under § 15 UStG. Export zero-rating is not available for services consumed in Germany.

  • Electronic customs export declaration (ATLAS) with movement reference number (MRN)
  • Customs exit confirmation or electronic export evidence (Austrittsbestätigung) from German customs
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR or other transport document showing acceptance in the third-country territory
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT credit advice or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign export proceeds
  • IP licence or design transfer agreement proving the foreign recipient destination for services/IP supplies

Germany has enacted a phased domestic B2B e-invoicing mandate: from 1 January 2025 every entrepreneur must be able to receive electronic invoices; from 1 January 2027 taxpayers whose domestic B2B turnover exceeded EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices in a structured format; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B invoicing must be e-invoice-compatible. Required formats include XRechnung, ZUGFeRD/Factur-X or EDI conforming to EN 16931. Germany does not currently operate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax authority clearance, or digital stamping; certified cash-register fiscalisation (TSE/DSFinV-K) applies to face-to-face payments, not to e-invoicing.

Tax authorities may retroactively assess German VAT at 19% if export evidence is incomplete or fraudulent; the assessment period is normally four years, extending to ten years for tax evasion (§ 169 AO). Interest under § 233a AO accrues on the clawback, and fraudulent zero-rating claims may trigger fines or criminal penalties under §§ 369, 370, 378 AO, including imprisonment of up to five years, or ten years in aggravated cases. The 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation benchmark is an audit documentation standard: Germany imposes no fixed statutory repatriation deadline, but unexplained non-repatriation of export proceeds can evidence sham or circular transactions, resulting in denial of zero-rating, loss of input VAT deduction, and personal liability of managing officers under §§ 69, 71 AO.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the AWG. There are no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, or capital for non-resident investors, provided that all reporting obligations to the Bundesbank are met (e.g., statistical reporting for cross-border payments). Profit remittance is subject to standard corporate income tax and withholding tax (if applicable under double tax treaties), but no FX conversion hurdles exist. The Euro is freely convertible; no surrender requirements or approval mechanisms apply to capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds in Germany. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in domestic or foreign accounts. However, all cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes (AWV reporting). The reporting must be filed electronically within seven days of the transaction. No conversion ratio is imposed; exporters are free to convert or hold foreign currency as per commercial needs.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and payment oversight; the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) administers the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) are regulated by BaFin and can offer cross-border payment services, including multi-currency accounts and SWIFT connectivity. However, for a GmbH engaged in semiconductor IP licensing with high-value transactions and potential LC requirements, traditional banks are preferred due to their robust trade finance infrastructure. EMIs may face limitations in providing letters of credit or complex treasury products. Legal constraints: EMIs must hold a license under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and are subject to strict safeguarding requirements. Cross-border operations within the EU are passportable, but non-EU EMIs require a branch or agent in Germany.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Inbound capital injections for a GmbH (e.g., share capital increase or shareholder loan) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with a clear purpose code. For capital contributions, the purpose code '1000' (Capital transfer) or '1100' (Investment in equity) is used. The bank will require a copy of the notarized capital increase resolution and the commercial register extract (Handelsregister). For shareholder loans, use purpose code '1200' (Loans). Verification: The bank will verify the identity of the remitter and the beneficiary, and may request the underlying contract. All cross-border payments must be reported to the Bundesbank under the AWV if they exceed €12,500.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For semiconductor IP licensing and design services, typical outbound payments include royalties, license fees, and subcontracting costs. SWIFT MT103 is used for direct wire transfers. For larger transactions or when the counterparty requires security, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) can be issued via the bank's trade finance department. The LC can be confirmed by a foreign bank. Additionally, for recurring payments, SEPA (Single Euro Payments Area) is used for EUR-denominated transactions within the EU. For non-EUR currencies, SWIFT is the standard. The bank will require underlying contracts and invoices for each disbursement.Currency Forwards: Standard OTC forward contracts with a German bank or a licensed financial institution to lock in exchange rates for future payments.Currency Swaps: Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) are permissible for hedging long-term exposure, subject to the bank's credit approval and internal risk policies.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs): While EUR is fully convertible, NDFs are not commonly used for EUR pairs. However, for exotic currency exposures (e.g., to certain emerging market currencies), NDFs may be available through international banks, but they are subject to regulatory scrutiny and may require special approval from the Bundesbank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Germany levies Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) at a statutory headline rate of 15% on taxable income, plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge) on the corporate income tax itself, and municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) assessed by the local Hebesatz, typically producing a combined annual effective tax burden of approximately 30% to 33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory German dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) is 25%; adding the 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax produces a combined statutory withholding rate of 26.375% on gross dividends paid by the GmbH to its shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local German transfer-pricing file obligations follow §90(3) AO and OECD TP Guidelines: a Local File is mandatory when cross-border controlled transactions with related parties exceed EUR 5.0 million for goods or intangible transfers or EUR 1.0 million for service transactions; a Master File is triggered once the worldwide group revenue exceeds EUR 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no legislative ESR register, but substantive economic presence is a condition of tax residence and treaty access. Economic substance is enforced through anti-abuse rules under §42 AO, the place-of-management test of §10 AO, CFC rules under §§7–13 AStG, and administrative requirement that the GmbH maintain real offices, strategic decision-making, operational staff, bank operations, and semiconductor IP exploitation functions inside Germany.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under §370 AO exposing the GmbH and responsible parties to imprisonment of up to five years (ten years in especially grave cases), five-figure to six-figure criminal fines, civil penalty assessments, and an extension of the assessment period from four to ten years

Director personal liability: Managing directors acting as statutory representatives are jointly liable for unpaid taxes and interest under §§34, 69, and 71 AO to the extent the tax was not withheld or could not be recovered from the GmbH; criminal conviction may additionally trigger personal restitutory awards, travel restrictions for tax-related investigations, and imprisonment of the responsible director
Artificial shell structure / mailbox company (Briefkastengesellschaft)

Statutory sanction: §42 AO permits the tax authorities to disregard the artificial structure and assess tax as if the economic substance and effective management were situated where actual decisions are made; Germany may reallocate profits under §§1 AStG and deny treaty benefits, imposing additional corporate income tax, trade tax, 6% interest, and possible CFC addbacks under §§7–13 AStG

Director personal liability: Directors who independently manage the mailbox entity or knowingly facilitate artificial offshore shifting can be held personally liable for the reallocated German tax through Haftung under §69 AO; the administrative act is reinforced by risk of personal criminal prosecution for tax evasion and prohibition orders in extreme cases
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Under §162(3) AO, tax authorities may presume the taxpayer's controlled transactions were over- or under-priced, adjust taxable profits at their discretion, and impose a combined documentation surcharge; the tax assessment remains open up to ten years and the adjustments expose the GmbH to supplementary corporation tax, trade tax, and interest

Director personal liability: Managing directors accountable for German tax compliance are personally liable for the documentation-related surcharge and for corporate taxes not collected solely because of their intentional or grossly negligent noncompliance; deliberate false documentation may also be prosecuted as attempted tax evasion
Failure to withhold domestic sourcing taxes on dividends, royalties and interest

Statutory sanction: The GmbH is deemed debtor of the unwithheld amount under §44 EStG and §50a EStG; the tax authorities may assess the entire missing tax and surcharge against the GmbH, and impose exceptional corporate penalties and interest arrears; intentional circumvention risks criminal liability under §§370, 378 AO

Director personal liability: Members of the management board who direct or approve non-compliant remittances are personally jointly liable with the GmbH for the WHT and surcharge under §§69, 71, and 42d EStG; in aggravated cases, they face direct criminal conviction and any concealed profits must be immediately repatriated as unlawful distributions

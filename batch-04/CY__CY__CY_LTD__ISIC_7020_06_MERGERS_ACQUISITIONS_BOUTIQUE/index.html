Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; official e-portal: https://taxdep.mof.gov.cy (Integrated Tax Information System for corporate income tax, VAT registration and Form VAT-4 filing).

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of a Cyprus private limited company, the Registrar of Companies transmits the incorporation particulars to the Tax Department, which automatically issues the corporate Tax Identification Code (TIC) to the registered office at no cost. A foreign legal entity can alternatively register a Cyprus branch (overseas company) and file the TIC application at the competent District Tax Office. Before commencing taxable supplies, the entity files Form TFR1 with the VAT District Office, either voluntarily or when the EUR 15,600 threshold is exceeded; a non-established taxable person must register from the first euro and may be required to appoint a VAT fiscal representative in Cyprus. After approval, the Tax Department grants a 'CY'-prefixed VAT identification number and electronic filing credentials.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — CY_LTD is a separate taxable person subject to 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide profits under the Income Tax Law L.118(I)/2002; non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. Cyprus has no pass-through election for limited companies; the participation exemption exempts qualifying dividends and gains from disposal of participating shareholdings. — Upon incorporation of a Cyprus private limited company, the Registrar of Companies transmits the incorporation particulars to the Tax Department, which automatically issues the corporate Tax Identification Code (TIC) to the registered office at no cost. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — CY_LTD is a separate taxable person subject to 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide profits under the Income Tax Law L.118(I)/2002; non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. Cyprus has no pass-through election for limited companies; the participation exemption exempts qualifying dividends and gains from disposal of participating shareholdings.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists; classification follows statutory corporate form. Elections available under Cypriot law include: (i) group loss-relief surrender under Article 32; (ii) IP Box election (80% qualifying-profit deduction under Article 23A producing a 2.5% effective rate); (iii) Notional Interest Deduction election on new qualifying equity under Article 4A; (iv) foreign tax credit election under Article 35; and (v) optional OECD-aligned transfer-pricing documentation elections. EU ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules (Article 38A) are implemented and require taxpayer self-assessment in the annual corporate income tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 (€15,600) of taxable supplies in any consecutive twelve-month period for Cyprus-established businesses under Article 8 VAT Law. Non-established taxable persons supplying goods or services in Cyprus must register from the first euro (no threshold applies). The EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies to intra-Community distance sales of goods and to telecommunications, broadcasting and electronic services; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT under Article 63 VAT Law applies where goods are dispatched or transported out of the EU and the supplier holds the customs export declaration and transport/exit evidence, with physical removal generally required within 90 days of dispatch. Services: M&A and business management advisory fees (ISIC 7020) supplied to a foreign business customer fall outside the scope of Cyprus VAT under the Article 43 B2B place-of-supply rule, provided the customer's valid VAT registration number is documented and the reverse-charge mechanism applies in the customer's jurisdiction; services supplied to private customers outside the EU also qualify as outside-scope upon proof of customer status and effective use outside the EU. 'International services' under Article 147 may be zero-rated where directly related to exported goods. The supplier must maintain an auditable export dossier covering the supply, movement and payment chain.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / Export Control System exit confirmation) evidencing removal of the goods from Cyprus and the EU within the statutory period.
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR road consignment note evidencing commercial transport of the goods to the foreign destination.
  • Original commercial invoice or M&A engagement letter and success-fee statement issued by CY_LTD, describing the services, consideration and the VAT treatment applied.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 confirmation from a licensed Cyprus credit institution proving receipt of the export consideration in a Cypriot bank account.
  • Foreign customer's VAT/tax registration certificate or tax-residence proof and, for services, declarations evidencing that the services are rendered from Cyprus for use or benefit outside the EU.

Cyprus does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls (CTC), API-based e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary and must comply with the European standard EN 16931 (PEPPOL BIS). VAT invoices must contain the minimum content required by Article 46 VAT Law and must be issued no later than the 15th day of the month following the chargeable event; invoices for exports and overseas supplies must state the 0% or outside-scope treatment applied. Books, VAT listings and corporate tax returns are filed electronically through the Tax Department portal; Intrastat declarations and statistical export data must be submitted electronically where relevant.

If the Tax Department finds defective or contradictory export documentation, the zero-rating is reversed: the supply is reassessed to VAT at 19% with statutory interest and administrative fines, including penalty surcharges and daily levies under the enforcement provisions of the VAT Law. Standard VAT assessments are issued within four years from the end of the relevant tax period; no limitation period applies in fraud or wilful default, and criminal liability may attach. In the corporate income tax field, the Department may reassess within five years (extended to ten years in cases of misstatement or fraud) and may apply OECD-aligned transfer-pricing adjustments and the EU anti-abuse doctrine. Funds retained offshore beyond the 180-day repatriation benchmark without documented commercial justification may be recharacterised as taxable income or as a deemed benefit/loan to the shareholder. CY_LTD must therefore maintain an export and overseas-services dossier (contracts, customs and transport records, bank credit advices, customer tax-status evidence and a group-wide transfer-pricing file) as the primary defence against retrospective clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus has been a full Eurozone member since 1 January 2008; the Euro (EUR) is the sole legal tender and is fully and freely convertible on both the capital account and the current account. Capital account liberalisation is absolute under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), with residual legal authority confined to emergency safeguard measures permitted under Articles 64-66 TFEU; Cyprus withdrew all crisis-era derogation measures in April 2015 and the former Exchange Control Law (Cap. 94) has been dormant since EU accession (1 May 2004). Profit remittance hurdles for a CY Ltd are near-zero: non-resident shareholders may receive dividends free of withholding tax (0%) under sections 33-36 of the Income Tax Law (L.118(I)/2002) and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU); no prior central bank clearance or exchange-control licence is required to remit dividends, advisory success fees, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds. Notification-based statistical reporting of the underlying payment to the Central Bank of Cyprus is executed by the servicing bank under the Balance of Payments reporting framework, not by the corporate entity. Residual friction exists exclusively in (i) compliance with EU/UN sanctions screening of counterparties, (ii) source-of-funds verification mandated by the Cyprus AML Law (L.188(I)/2007, as amended) and Cab 37, and (iii) documentary substantiation of the economic substance of advisory work to OECD/EU code-of-conduct standards.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. The Republic of Cyprus imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no timing deadline for repatriation or conversion of foreign-currency proceeds. An M&A advisory boutique may retain USD, GBP, CHF, or other convertible currencies in dedicated nostro-style foreign currency accounts at a Cypriot bank without restriction, convert to EUR at market rate on a voluntary basis, or deploy the proceeds directly into hedging instruments. The sole administrative requirement is the bank's aggregated monthly reporting of client foreign-currency flows to the Central Bank of Cyprus for statistical BoP purposes; the bank is obliged to report, but no client-side declaration or conversion certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), established under the Central Bank of Cyprus Law 2002 (L.138(I)/2002), acting within the Eurosystem and the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM) with the European Central Bank (ECB) as supreme supervisory authority for significant institutions; the Central Bank of Cyprus retains direct prudential supervision of low-to-medium significance credit institutions, licensed under the Business of Credit Institutions Law of 1997 (L.66(I)/1997).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary payment flows, Low for treasury escrow and success-fee settlement. A Cyprus-incorporated or EU-passported Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under the Electronic Money Law of 2012 (L.81(I)/2012, transposing Directive 2009/110/EC) and Payment Services Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2) may lawfully hold up to the safeguarding amount of client funds and render SWIFT/SEPA/international payment services; however, an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot take deposits. Consequently, holding client M&A escrow balances at an EMI exposes funds to insolvency claw-back and fails the fiduciary segregation requirements expected by deal counterparties. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EU/EEA (e.g., UK, UAE, Singapore) may operate into Cyprus only through a CySEC licence, an EU-authorised branch, or the reverse-solicitation doctrine, which is legally fragile for recurring cross-border treasury activity; non-EU EMIs carrying medium-risk corporate clients are overwhelmingly declined by Cypriot correspondent banks. Recommended architecture: use an EU-passported EMI (passport into Cyprus via EBA register) for travel expenses, disbursement automation, and sub-EUR-10,000 professional expense payments; maintain all client success-fee clearing, escrow, and currency liquidity with Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant treasury protocol for inward capital and revenue: (1) route all injections via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202/MT202COV where an intermediate bank is involved) with mandatory UETR (Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference) as required under ISO 20022/CBPR+; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary payment flows, Low for treasury escrow and success-fee settlement. A Cyprus-incorporated or EU-passported Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under the Electronic Money Law of 2012 (L.81(I)/2012, transposing Directive 2009/110/EC) and Payment Services Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2) may lawfully hold up to the safeguarding amount of client funds and render SWIFT/SEPA/international payment services; however, an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot take deposits. Consequently, holding client M&A escrow balances at an EMI exposes funds to insolvency claw-back and fails the fiduciary segregation requirements expected by deal counterparties. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EU/EEA (e.g., UK, UAE, Singapore) may operate into Cyprus only through a CySEC licence, an EU-authorised branch, or the reverse-solicitation doctrine, which is legally fragile for recurring cross-border treasury activity; non-EU EMIs carrying medium-risk corporate clients are overwhelmingly declined by Cypriot correspondent banks. Recommended architecture: use an EU-passported EMI (passport into Cyprus via EBA register) for travel expenses, disbursement automation, and sub-EUR-10,000 professional expense payments; maintain all client success-fee clearing, escrow, and currency liquidity with Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate related-party transactions for Local File requirement per new TP guidelines effective 2022; Master File required when group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000 and Cyprus entity transactions exceed EUR 750,000.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies a substance-oriented interpretation of the OECD Model and EU anti-avoidance principles. Although no Cyprus-specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as seen in offshore centres, Cyprus tax law requires substantive commercial presence for treaty benefits, management/control for tax residency, and business purpose for deductions. Since 2019 additional substance documentation and TP requirements. The subsidiary must maintain premises, employees, board meetings, decision-making functions, and operating activities in Cyprus to avoid being characterised as a shell company for EU ATAD purposes and for DTA avoidance. IP holding companies must demonstrate economic substance in staffing and expenditure management.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction leading to a fine of up to EUR 60,000 or imprisonment up to five years, or both; administrative tax assessments with additional tax penalties of 100% to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest at 3.5% per annum. Under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, negligent failure to file or incorrect returns attracts penalties of EUR 100–EUR 600 plus additional tax of 5% to 50%.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for penalties and unpaid taxes if tax evasion is committed with consent or negligence; courts may impose custodial sentences and order restitution of the tax shortfall. A travel ban may be issued against directors of a company with unpaid taxes to prevent departure pending settlement.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and domestic participation exemption; reclassification of profits to the non-resident principal company; additional tax assessments and penalties; potential strike-off from the Cyprus Registrar of Companies for failure to file returns; fines up to EUR 20,000 and disqualification orders under the Companies Law Cap 113.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the company's tax liabilities if they acted as shadow directors or knowingly facilitated a sham structure; courts may issue conduct orders prohibiting acting as directors for up to three years and impose personal restitution for the tax benefit obtained.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Disclosure Failures

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to EUR 200,000 for beneficial ownership register breaches and imprisonment up to five years under Cyprus AML law (CySEC Regime); civil forfeiture of proceeds of crime; fines up to EUR 1,000,000 for regulated entities under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (L.188(I)/2007), plus suspension or revocation of the Cyprus operating licence.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally criminal liable for money laundering offences if they knowingly consent or turn a blind eye; assets may be frozen, directors subject to travel bans, and individual imprisonment of up to 14 years, as well as director disqualification orders under company law.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties under the Cyprus TP rules include a fixed penalty of EUR 5,000 plus EUR 1,500 for each month of continued non-compliance; the Tax Commissioner may adjust profits to arm's length and impose tax of 12.5% on adjusted profits; additional surcharges of 5%–10% for incorrect returns.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false or misleading TP documentation may be assessed personally under general tax law for assisting in fraudulent underreporting; may face criminal fines and up to three years imprisonment.

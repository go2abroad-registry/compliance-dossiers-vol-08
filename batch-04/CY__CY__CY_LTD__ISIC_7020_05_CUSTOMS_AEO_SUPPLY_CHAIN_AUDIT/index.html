Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation (Tax Department), Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; official electronic filing and registration portal is the Ariadne system (https://ariadne.mof.gov.cy) supplemented by the Tax Department's public portal.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus Private Limited Company obtains its statutory Tax Identification Code (TIC) through incorporation with the Registrar of Companies and subsequent registration with the Tax Department. Foreign entities not yet incorporated in Cyprus: (1) apply directly to the Tax Department for a TIC using the required registration forms before commencing any Cyprus-source taxable activity; (2) if the entity is not established in Cyprus, a tax representative must be appointed for VAT and income tax purposes where required by law; (3) for VAT purposes, file the prescribed VAT registration application (VAT 101 series) with copies of constitutional documents, passport/AML identification of directors and shareholders, proof of Cyprus place of management (for tax residence claims), and estimated Cypriot turnover; (4) the Tax Department issues the TIC/VAT number once registration is validated, with effective date no later than the date on which taxable activity commences or the statutory filing deadline.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Cyprus Private Limited Company obtains its statutory Tax Identification Code (TIC) through incorporation with the Registrar of Companies and subsequent registration with the Tax Department. Foreign entities not yet incorporated in Cyprus: (1) apply directly to the Tax Department for a TIC using the required registration forms before commencing any Cyprus-source taxable activity; (2) if the entity is not established in Cyprus, a tax representative must be appointed for VAT and income tax purposes where required by law; (3) for VAT purposes, file the prescribed VAT registration application (VAT 101 series) with copies of constitutional documents, passport/AML identification of directors and shareholders, proof of Cyprus place of management (for tax residence claims), and estimated Cypriot turnover; (4) the Tax Department issues the TIC/VAT number once registration is validated, with effective date no later than the date on which taxable activity commences or the statutory filing deadline.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box election and has no general tax-transparent election for limited companies. A Cyprus Ltd is entity-level taxable as a corporate tax resident if central management and control is exercised in Cyprus, with full worldwide taxation subject to the territorial limitations in Cyprus tax law. Available statutory elections relevant to a corporate taxpayer include: group relief for intra-group loss transfer, group profit transfer under the Companies Law provisions, the notional interest deduction on new equity, the Cyprus IP Box (nexus-based 80% deduction), tonnage tax for qualifying shipping activities, and the deemed-domicile/non-domicile election for individual shareholders in certain holding structures. Hybrid mismatch and other anti-avoidance elections aremandatorily settled under the EU ATAD/anti-hybrid rules rather than by taxpayer choice.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for established taxable persons supplying goods or services in Cyprus; no registration threshold for non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Cyprus; EUR 10,000 cross-border intra-EU distance sales threshold (exclusive of VAT) with an option to tax in the destination Member State under the One Stop Shop (OSS) scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to qualifying exports of goods physically removed from Cyprus to a destination outside the European Union, provided the supply is made to the buyer for consideration, ownership has transferred, the goods leave Cypriot territory within statutory time limits, and the exporter holds contemporaneous documentary proof of export. Intra-EU dispatches to a taxable person are zero-rated as intra-Community supplies where the customer's valid EU VAT ID is obtained and the EC Sales List is filed. Ancillary services such as export freight, transport, and handling can also be zero-rated when directly related to the export and separately evidenced. Services physically and effectively used outside the EU may be zero-rated where the place of supply is outside Cyprus under the general place-of-supply rules; the supplier must retain commercial evidence, contract, bank payment records, and technical documentation establishing offshore delivery and consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/electronic declaration or equivalent delivered through the Cyprus customs export system)
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, road/CMR consignment note or other international transport document evidencing physical removal from Cyprus
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds into the Cypriot exporter's bank account

Cyprus does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control APIs for B2B e-invoicing. Electronic filing of VAT returns is mandatory via the Ariadne portal. B2G electronic invoicing is required in conformity with EU Directive 2014/55/EU and must be submitted through PEPPOL-compliant interoperability standards where the contracting public authority so requires. There is no national e-invoice clearance platform; however, taxpayers must retain electronic records made available to the Tax Department on request, and VAT compliance documentation must support the correct place of supply and zero-rating position.

Export zero-rating claims are routinely scrutinised by the Tax Department and Cyprus Customs under the Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) and customs audit frameworks. Where documentation is deficient or goods have not actually left the EU, the claimed zero rate is denied, the exporter is assessed for the unpaid VAT at 19% plus statutory interest, and retrospective clawback may cover up to five years of under-assessed output VAT (extending to a longer limitation period where fraudulent conduct is established). Administrative penalties can be imposed up to twice the tax at issue or per-invoice fixed fines, and criminal sanctions apply for deliberately false statements, including fines and imprisonment under the VAT Law and general fraud legislation. AEO certification and customs facilitation concessions may additionally be suspended or revoked for record-keeping failures or customs debt arising from non-validated export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full member of the euro area. The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions under Articles 63-65 TFEU. There are no domestic exchange controls or restrictions on inward/outward capital movements, profit remittances, dividends, or loan repayments, subject only to EU sanctions screening, anti-money laundering (AML) checks, and applicable tax clearance formalities. A Cyprus Limited company may freely receive equity injections and disburse trade settlements; dividend distribution to non-residents is unrestricted after audited financial statements and Solvency/Balance Sheet Test compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds. Exporters, including AEO-certified supply chain audit firms, receive full control over receipts in EUR or foreign currency. The Central Bank of Cyprus requires balance-of-payments (BoP) reporting for direct investment, trade in services, and financial account transactions above certain thresholds, typically collected through Authorised Banks on the Bank of Cyprus' BoP reporting platform. Commercial invoices, SWIFT MT103 confirmations, and AEO supply chain audit reports satisfy documentary evidence requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), acting within the European System of Central Banks and in cooperation with the European Central Bank for euro liquidity and foreign exchange operations; foreign exchange licensing and derivative intermediation supervision also fall under the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for regulated EU Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) that hold a valid passport or Cyprus EMI license under PSD2. These services are viable for outbound trade settlements and cross-border disbursements, but they are not acceptable as the sole account for capital escrow or custody of statutory minimum share capital. Offshore or unlicensed digital EMIs operating without a Cyprus/EU passport present high legal and AML risk and should be rejected for regulated supply chain audit receivables. Business payment platforms such as Wise, if operated under a valid EMI passport, can be used for low-value operational payments but must be supported by a domestically licensed bank account for final clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan capital injections into the Cyprus company must be routed through the domestic Tier 1 bank account using SWIFT MT103 wire transfers. The remittance must state the correct purpose codes: for direct investment equity, use 'Cash contribution to capital' or 'Purchase of shares' (CBC BoP code 781/3-1); for shareholder loans, use 'Loans to direct investment enterprises' (CBC BoP code 872). The bank requires a board resolution approving the injection, updated memorandum and articles of association, the share subscription form, and the Cyprus Registrar's issued certificate of incorporation/capital update. For third-party payments, a commercial contract and pro-forma invoice must accompany the inbound payment. Outbound compliance checks include sanctions list screening on the remitter and beneficiary, negative news review, and confirmation that the capital remains unencumbered for 24 months if the account is classified as a Capital Escrow Account.Permitted clearing rails for AEO supply chain audit trade settlements include (i) SWIFT MT103 global wires for service fees, audit sub-contractor payments, and licensing fees; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) confirmed by the Cyprus issuing bank for goods-related or high-value security audit equipment purchases; and (iii) SEPA Instant Credit Transfers for EU sub-contractors and vendors under €100,000. For low-value operational expenses, the Cyprus company may use Wise (or another EU-passported EMI) as an ancillary rail, but the primary settlement account must remain at a licensed Cyprus commercial bank. All outbound disbursements require matching documents: final invoice, service acceptance certificate, delivery verification for physical goods, and an AEO supply chain audit report where applicable. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related party transactions exceed €750,000 for goods, €250,000 for services, €100,000 for interest, or €100,000 for royalties; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds €750 million.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) to entities engaged in relevant activities (including service businesses). AEO auditing is a service activity; the company must demonstrate adequate premises, employees, and management in Cyprus, and maintain records to prove substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalty of 100% of tax evaded plus interest at 3.5% per annum

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed; criminal prosecution can lead to imprisonment.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax benefits, retroactive assessment of tax at 12.5% plus penalties up to 100% of tax due; potential strike-off from the register

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly allowed the company to operate as a shell; they may face disqualification as directors and civil restitution.
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1,000,000 for serious breaches; criminal penalties for money laundering up to 14 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be personally fined and imprisoned for willful non-compliance; they may also face civil liability for damages arising from AML failures.

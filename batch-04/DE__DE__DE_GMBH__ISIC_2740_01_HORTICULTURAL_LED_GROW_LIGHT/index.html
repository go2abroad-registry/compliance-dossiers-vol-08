Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital €25,000; at least 50% (€12,500) must be paid in cash and be at the free disposal of the managing director before registration. For a single-member GmbH, the full €25,000 must be paid. Payment must be evidenced by a bank certificate (Bankbescheinigung); no state escrow account is mandatory.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the municipal fire prevention authority (Brandschutzdienststelle) based on escape route and fire-load inspection; structural clearance for the industrial floor load from the municipal building authority; workplace safety clearance under the German Occupational Safety and Health Act (ArbSchG § 5) and the Employer's Liability Insurance Association (Berufsgenossenschaft ETEM) for electrical and metal industry operations.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital €25,000; at least 50% (€12,500) must be paid in cash and be at the free disposal of the managing director before registration. For a single-member GmbH, the full €25,000 must be paid. Payment must be evidenced by a bank certificate (Bankbescheinigung); no state escrow account is mandatory.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
UNANIMOUS WRITTEN RESOLUTION OF THE INCORPORATORS: (1) The draft Articles of Association are approved and adopted. (2) The subscribers confirm that the minimum share capital has been subscribed and will be paid in cash before registration; for a single-member GmbH the full EUR 25,000 must be paid and evidenced by bank certificate. (3) The registered office is fixed at the address in the Articles of Association. (4) The managing director is appointed to act as sole representative and is authorized to cover registration with the Commercial Register, tax authorities, and all ancillary filings. (5) All prior acts of the incorporators and advisers in the formation process are ratified.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Municipal Trade Office (Gewerbeamt) for trade registration; for special operations requiring emissions control, the State Office of Environmental Protection (Landesamt für Umwelt) of the relevant German Land (state) acts as the competent industrial licensing board.

Category: No separate industrial license is statutorily required for assembly of LED lighting fixtures (ISIC 2740). The company obtains a Gewerbeanmeldung confirmation and, if applicable, a municipal building permit for the physical premises under the local Building Code (Bauordnung).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office for VAT registration and central compliance; ELSTER (www.elster.de) is the national electronic tax filing and registration portal; local Finanzamt for corporate income tax and trade tax registration and assessment.

E-government filing portal

For a German GmbH, the managing director (Geschäftsführer) engages a tax advisor to file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER. The local Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (corporate tax number) for income and trade tax. Separately, the GmbH applies to BZSt for the German VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using the relevant ELSTER process or Form USt 1 TG. For a foreign entity without a domestic establishment, direct VAT registration with BZSt is permitted upon production of commercial register evidence and the foreign TIN; if the applicant is not resident in the EU, a domestic VAT representative may be required to be designated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany applies mandatory entity-level taxation without a US-style check-the-box election. Statutory mechanisms include VAT and corporate tax fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §2 Abs. 2 UStG and §14 KStG, allowing a controlling enterprise to be treated as the taxable person for VAT and as the sole taxpayer for corporate and trade tax purposes when integration criteria are met. Also available is the optional transparent-to-corporate tax status election for partnerships (Optionsmodell under §1a KStG). There are no hybrid mismatch election regimes; hybrid mismatch rules apply mandatorily under domestic anti-hybrid provisions and ATAD implementation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 annual gross turnover for domestic small-business VAT exemption (§19 UStG); EUR 10,000 turnover threshold for intra-EU distance sales and digital B2C services (OSS regime); non-resident suppliers engaged in taxable German supplies are registrable from the first transaction with no de minimis turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies to customers located outside Germany and outside the European Union may be zero-rated under §6 UStG if the goods are physically transported to a third-country destination and the exporter obtains and retains documentary evidence of the export. Intra-Community supplies are zero-rated when the purchaser supplies a valid EU VAT ID, the supplier issues an invoice under EU VAT rules, and the supplier submits the recapitulative statement §18a UStG. Services rendered to foreign entrepreneurs generally do not fall within German VAT zero-rating; they are outside the German tax nexus or shift to the reverse-charge regime in the recipient's country, depending on the place of supply rules.

  • Export declaration (Ausfuhr-/Verbringungsanmeldung) processed through the ATLAS customs system
  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung)
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or CMR consignment note (Frachtbrief/LKW-Frachtbrief)
  • Proof of physical export (Versandnachweis) including carrier confirmation, customs exit stamps, or electronic movement records
  • Customer EU VAT ID and recapitulative statement evidence for intra-Community zero-rating

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing under the E-Rechnung framework is phased from 1 January 2025 (obligation to receive ZUGFeRD/XRechnung structured XML invoices) through 1 January 2028 (universal domestic B2B issuance obligation). There is no real-time fiscal clearance, national API clearance, or digital stamping for standard invoices. Electronically recorded sales from cash registers require certified technical security systems (TSE) per §146a AO, with periodic reporting to the tax authorities.

German tax authorities require a complete, consistent, and contemporaneous documentary chain to substantiate zero-rating. Failure to produce the required export or intra-Community evidence triggers retroactive denial of zero-rating, VAT reassessment at the standard 19% rate, monthly late-payment interest under §233a AO, penalty surcharges of up to EUR 25,000 under §26a UStG, and criminal liability under §370 AO for tax evasion with potential imprisonment of up to 10 years. Forged or intentionally false certificates are treated as fraudulent export schemes, and electronically exchanged customs and VAT data intensify audit scrutiny and cross-border clawback proceedings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU and OECD liberalisation standards; the Euro is freely convertible. Profit remittance is not subject to exchange control approval, but must be executed through a licensed payment institution and comply with anti-money-laundering identification rules. There is no requirement for a central bank clearance certificate or a prior tax clearance for dividend transfers; however, treaty withholding tax exemptions must be documented separately where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender obligation, no conversion ratio, and no time limit on retention of export proceeds for German exporters. Export receipts may be retained in any currency. The resident GmbH must nevertheless report all cross-border payments and receivables exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV, Sections 67ff.), using the German balance-of-payments statistical codes. The report is due by the 7th calendar day of the month following the transaction month.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank) together with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and AML/CFT supervision; there is no separate Foreign Exchange Control Administration in Germany.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary cross-border payment operations, but low as a substitute for a core banking relationship or capital escrow. Only EMIs holding a BaFin licence or a valid EU/EEA banking or e-money passport may legally operate for a German GmbH. A non-EEA offshore EMI cannot hold German corporate funds without a BaFin branch licence under Section 53 KWG or a white-label arrangement with a licensed EU/EEA bank. Core liquidity must remain with a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank; an EMI channel may be used for small-ticket supplier payments, FX conversion and parallel procurement wires.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan funding from non-resident shareholders must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from a licensed bank or accredited EMI. The payment reference must identify the German AWV balance-of-payments category for direct investment capital, include the full GmbH legal name and the notarial reference for the capital increase or shareholder loan agreement. Any single cross-border payment above EUR 12,500 requires Deutsche Bundesbank reporting under AWV Section 67, due by the 7th calendar day of the following month. For share capital subscription, funds are first credited to a blocked GmbH capital escrow account until the notary confirms payment and the commercial register registration is effected.Outbound trade payments use SWIFT MT103 for clean telegraphic transfers, documentary collection through MT400/MT410, and irrevocable letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700/MT799 with confirming banks where required. Payment references must contain the invoice number, Incoterms rule, customs declaration number and purchase contract reference.

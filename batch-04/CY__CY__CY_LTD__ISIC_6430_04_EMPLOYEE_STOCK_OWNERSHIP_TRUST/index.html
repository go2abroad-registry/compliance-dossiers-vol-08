Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver, Ministry of Energy, Commerce and Industry - e-Company Electronic Filing Portal (www.ecompany.gov.cy)

Governing statute

Cyprus Companies Law, Cap. 113 (as amended), ss. 5-11 (incorporation and certificate of incorporation), ss. 104-110 (first directors and corporate secretary), ss. 155-173 (share capital and capital maintenance); Companies (Amendment) Law 2013 (single-member private company); Trusts Law, L.68(I)/2015 (ESOP trust governance); Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law, L.188(I)/2007, and the beneficial-ownership register provisions of ss. 114A-114I.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form HE1 (Application for Registration of a Private Company), filed with Form HE2 (Notice of Registered Office), Form HE3 (Statement of First Directors and Secretary) and the executed Memorandum and Articles of Association; completed by issuance of the electronic Certificate of Incorporation.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under Cap. 113; the incorporator may allot one or more shares of flexible nominal value, customarily EUR 1,000. No escrow account or capital deposit is required before registration. Shares to be held for the ESOP trust must be settled through the trust fund or lawful employer contributions in compliance with the financial-assistance rules of Cap. 113 ss. 171-171H.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the Cyprus Fire Service (District Fire Headquarters) before occupation; building permit and structural stability/electrical installation certificates required for the registered office; the employer must complete a written workplace risk assessment, appoint a health-and-safety coordinator and obtain the hygiene clearance of the District Labour Inspectorate under the Safety and Health at Work Law, L.89(I)/1996 (as amended).

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED, that the incorporation application, Memorandum and Articles of Association submitted to the Registrar of Companies be and are hereby ratified, and that the subscribed shares be allotted to the founding member in accordance with the Employee Stock Ownership Trust Instrument. RESOLVED FURTHER, that the registered office be adopted at the notified address in the Republic of Cyprus, and that the individuals named in Form HE3 be and are hereby appointed as first Directors, Corporate Secretary and Registered Agent. RESOLVED FURTHER, that the Secretary be directed to pay the registry fees, obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, file the beneficial-ownership declaration, open the designated payroll bank account and execute the trust deed within statutory deadlines.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable - the activity (ISIC 6430, Administration of Employee Share Incentive Plans) is a license-exempt administrative and fiduciary service. The only potentially relevant regulator is the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) under the Administrative Services Providers Law, L.196(I)/2012, which is not engaged because the ESOP trustee acts as a captive internal trust administrator for the employing group and does not offer services to third parties; no CySEC ASP licence is required.

Category: Exempt Activity - no statutory operating permit is required for a captive ESOP trust administration company; banking AML risk tier confirmed as Low Risk Standard under the L.188(I)/2007 framework. If the trustee later markets fiduciary services to third parties, a CySEC Administrative Services Provider Licence would become mandatory.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance; the VAT Service (Υπηρεσία ΦΠΑ) operates within the Tax Department for all VAT matters. National e-filing and registration portal: TAXISnet (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy/); official tax guidance portal: https://www.mof.gov.cy/tax.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus-incorporated Private Limited Company (Ltd) automatically receives its Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon incorporation; the statutory TIN is identical to the Companies Registration Number (HE followed by digits) issued under the Companies Law 113(I)/1986. Foreign entities not incorporated in Cyprus must obtain a non-resident TIN by filing Form TAX 501 with the competent District Tax Office, accompanied by notarised certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, proof of registered address, and identity documentation for directors and beneficial owners; if the foreign entity operates through a Cyprus branch, branch registration under section 342 of the Companies Law must precede tax registration. For VAT purposes, all persons making taxable supplies in Cyprus must file Form VAT 101 through TAXISnet with the VAT Service; non-EU-established applicants must appoint a fiscal representative and may be required to provide a bank guarantee as security for VAT obligations. Standard processing time is 4 to 8 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Cyprus-incorporated Private Limited Company (Ltd) automatically receives its Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon incorporation; the statutory TIN is identical to the Companies Registration Number (HE followed by digits) issued under the Companies Law 113(I)/1986. Foreign entities not incorporated in Cyprus must obtain a non-resident TIN by filing Form TAX 501 with the competent District Tax Office, accompanied by notarised certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, proof of registered address, and identity documentation for directors and beneficial owners; if the foreign entity operates through a Cyprus branch, branch registration under section 342 of the Companies Law must precede tax registration. For VAT purposes, all persons making taxable supplies in Cyprus must file Form VAT 101 through TAXISnet with the VAT Service; non-EU-established applicants must appoint a fiscal representative and may be required to provide a bank guarantee as security for VAT obligations. Standard processing time is 4 to 8 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a check-the-box or equivalent pass-through election. Statutory elections available to a Cyprus Ltd include: (i) VAT group registration election under section 40 of VAT Law 94(I)/2000; (ii) One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for cross-border B2C digital and distance-sale services; (iii) group loss relief election allowing surrender of tax losses between 75% group companies under section 34 of Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002; (iv) tax-neutral reorganisation elections for intra-group mergers, demergers, share-for-share exchanges and asset transfers under sections 28C and 28D; (v) the non-resident permanent-establishment ring-fencing election restricting tax to Cyprus-source profits attributable to the PE; and (vi) application for binding private rulings and advance pricing arrangements with the Tax Commissioner.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for taxable persons established in Cyprus (VAT Law 94(I)/2000, section 15); EUR 35,000 for intra-EU distance sales into Cyprus; EUR 10,000 for intra-Community acquisitions by taxable persons; non-established foreign suppliers have no registration threshold and must register from the first euro of taxable Cyprus supplies unless covered by the OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from Cyprus to destinations outside the EU are zero-rated under section 45(1)(a) of VAT Law 94(I)/2000, provided the exporter retains conclusive transport and customs evidence. Intra-Community supplies to a VAT-registered EU customer are zero-rated against a valid EU VAT number and VIES declaration. Ancillary services directly connected to exported goods (customs clearance, handling, transport) qualify for zero-rating. For the ISIC 6430 ESOP-trust administration business, trust and share-plan management services supplied in a B2B context are governed by place-of-supply rules: services supplied to a non-Cyprus taxable person are outside Cyprus VAT (reverse charge for EU customers; outside scope for non-EU customers), so no formal zero-rating claim is required; supplies related to securities transactions may additionally fall within the VAT exemption under section 45A of the VAT Law.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / ECS export entry)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from Cyprus
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or Bank SWIFT MT103 payment confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice stating the zero-rating or outside-scope basis
  • Customer VAT registration number and VIES checklist for intra-Community supplies
  • Intrastat declaration where annual trade thresholds apply

Cyprus does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system, continuous transaction control (CTC) regime, or digital stamping. Mandatory electronic invoicing applies to B2G supplies through the Cyprus Government e-Procurement platform (e-supplier), implementing EU Directive 2014/55/EU under the EN 16931/Peppol standard. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary at the current stage, with mandatory digital reporting and e-invoicing to follow upon transposition of the EU ViDA (VAT in the Digital Age) Directive in the 2030 horizon. All VAT compliance is conducted periodically through quarterly V.A.T. 101 returns filed on TAXISnet; no real-time API clearance with the Tax Department is in force.

The Tax Commissioner may retrospectively reassess VAT at the 19% standard rate with statutory interest for four years, extended to ten years in cases of fraud or deliberate default (VAT Law 94(I)/2000, sections 45 and 48). Fraudulent export zero-rating claims trigger an administrative penalty of up to twice the under-declared VAT, criminal fines up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to two years per offence, personal liability for directors and trustees, publication on the Tax Department defaulters list, clawback of refunded VAT, and suspension or revocation of VAT registration. False export declarations additionally attract customs penalties of up to 200% of the duty or VAT evaded under the Customs and Excise Law 94(I)/2004.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full capital account convertibility applies. Cyprus adopted the euro (EUR) on 1 January 2008 and is subject to the EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC). Cyprus abolished all foreign exchange controls upon EU accession (1 May 2004) and is an IMF Article VIII member, meaning no restrictions exist on current account payments. Capital account operations (share subscriptions, trust funding, escrow injections and dividend distributions) are permitted without prior CBC approval, subject only to CBC Balance of Payments statistical reporting after execution. Profit and dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders is free of any withholding tax; the Special Contribution for Defence (SDC) on dividends applies only to Cyprus tax-resident shareholders, not to non-residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Cyprus imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation or mandatory conversion requirement for export proceeds or any other inbound receipts. The ESOP Trust has no obligation to convert EUR or to liquidate foreign currency receipts. The only residual obligation is the CBC's Balance of Payments external reporting requirement: cross-border transactions and related account balances are reported by the servicing bank under the CBC's Monetary and Financial Statistics framework (typically transaction-level reporting for any single cross-border flow, with the bank responsible for classification).

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou, CBC), located in Nicosia; the CBC is the monetary authority, prudential regulator of Cypriot credit institutions and authorised Payment/Electronic Money Institutions, and the compiler of the Republic's Balance of Payments statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Medium-High for disbursement-only functions, Low for equity settlement custody. Cyprus-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and EU-passported EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Payoneer, N26 or CBC-licensed local EMIs) can open payment accounts remotely without a branch visit. Legal constraints: (i) e-money institutions cannot provide a securities-settlement escrow service, so the Capital Escrow Account for share acquisitions must sit with a licensed bank; (ii) CBC-regulated EMIs may hold payment accounts only for executing payment transactions and disburse e-money float within the limits of the Electronic Money Law (L.81(I)/2012); (iii) the EMI must perform full AML/CDD on all employee beneficiaries before crediting payroll. Recommended architecture: an EMI account is used only as a low-value disbursement rail for payroll wires, while the Capital Escrow Account for the Trust's equity operations is maintained at a Tier-1 bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership opacity: ESOP trust beneficiaries (employee participants) are not disclosed on the Companies Register as shareholders, triggering enhanced due diligence and potential refusal of onboarding.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity: ESOP trust beneficiaries (employee participants) are not disclosed on the Companies Register as shareholders, triggering enhanced due diligence and potential refusal of onboarding.

Defense evidence: Trust deed and supplemental plan rules; a register of employee beneficiaries with each participant's full identification data; an independent Cyprus legal opinion clarifying that the Trustee acts as fiduciary administrator and holds legal ownership while beneficiary interests are derivative; the beneficial ownership declaration filed with the Cyprus Registrar of Companies showing the statutory beneficial owner.
Source-of-funds verification: the incoming cross-border capital injection for the share purchase appears as an unexplained credit to the escrow account.

Root cause: Source-of-funds verification: the incoming cross-border capital injection for the share purchase appears as an unexplained credit to the escrow account.

Defense evidence: Audited financial statements of the funding parent or sponsor; board resolutions of the parent approving the ESOP contribution; the ESOP funding agreement between the parent and the Trust; a chain of bank statements establishing the provenance of funds; and a sworn source-of-wealth statement from the sponsor confirming the funds derive from bona fide commercial operations.
Sanctions and PEP screening failures: non-resident employee beneficiaries fail automated World-Check or Refinitiv screening, causing automatic account freeze.

Root cause: Sanctions and PEP screening failures: non-resident employee beneficiaries fail automated World-Check or Refinitiv screening, causing automatic account freeze.

Defense evidence: A documented financial-crime screening policy; individual screening results for every beneficiary obtained within the preceding 90 days; a risk-rating matrix classifying participants as low risk; a remediation procedure segregating any positive-match beneficiary's interest until clearance is obtained from the competent sanctions authority.
Payroll disbursement red flag: recurring cross-border wires to a large number of foreign employee beneficiaries are interpreted as structured payments or potential money-laundering activity.

Root cause: Payroll disbursement red flag: recurring cross-border wires to a large number of foreign employee beneficiaries are interpreted as structured payments or potential money-laundering activity.

Defense evidence: An executed payroll administration agreement with a licensed payroll provider or an in-house payroll policy; the ESOP plan rules and grant/vesting schedule; a pre-agreed transaction profile submitted to the bank's compliance committee establishing the expected monthly disbursement pattern; consolidated beneficiary schedules with tax residence, bank account details and award amounts.
Tax-treaty conduit concern: the bank perceives the Cyprus Trust as a conduit that may be used to shift value or erode the parent's tax base, triggering anti-avoidance scrutiny.

Root cause: Tax-treaty conduit concern: the bank perceives the Cyprus Trust as a conduit that may be used to shift value or erode the parent's tax base, triggering anti-avoidance scrutiny.

Defense evidence: Substance documentation: Cyprus office lease, minutes of local board meetings, employment contracts of Cyprus-based administrators, and a board calendar demonstrating decision-making in Cyprus; a transfer-pricing or economic-substance report prepared by a registered accountant; the Cyprus tax identification number (TIN) certificate and proof of tax filings substantiating that the Trust complies with anti-abuse tests under the relevant double tax treaty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the sponsor instructs a SWIFT MT103 (EUR) from the parent account to the Cyprus Capital Escrow Account at a Tier-1 bank (Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank). The payment reference field must include 'ESOP Trust Capital Injection — [Trust Name]' and, where applicable, the CBC Balance of Payments survey code for direct investment equity capital (S.0302 or its successor code). If the sponsor is an EEA entity, a SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) with ISO 20022 purpose code 'BOND' or 'GDDS' may be used. No prior CBC approval is required; the servicing bank automatically reports the inflow to the CBC for statistical purposes. The bank's AML team may pre-clear the credit using the documented source-of-funds file before releasing escrowed funds for equity purchases.Clearing rails: (i) SEPA Instant / SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001) for EUR-denominated payroll and beneficiary disbursements across the EEA with near-real-time execution; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA wires to foreign shareholders, brokers and beneficiaries; (iii) SWIFT MT202 or MT202COV for interbank cover payments routed through correspondent banks; (iv) Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) where the Trust effects a documentary purchase of listed shares through a broker requiring settlement undertaking, although the prevailing ESOP settlement practice is a plain MT103 equity transfer; (v) broker-directed settlement via MT103 naming the broker's segregated client account. SEPA Direct Debit is used only for collection of employee contributions. All FX conversions are executed at the instructing bank's spot desk with a dated contract note retained for CBC Balance of Payments reporting.Deliverable outright FX forward contracts (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF) with tenors up to 12 months, executed under an ISDA Master Agreement or the bank's standard FX forward terms; permitted in full because EUR is a freely deliverable currency with no onshore restriction.FX swaps (spot/forward combinations) used for temporary currency conversion cover on the escrow funding legs.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) aligning the Trust's EUR-denominated assets with a USD-denominated share-purchase liability or employee award liability.Vanilla European FX options (purchased, non-leveraged) to cap conversion costs on the optional USD funding of the equity acquisition.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible or necessary for EUR, as EUR/USD is a fully deliverable G10 pair outside the NDF market. Any FX derivative execution requires the Trust to evidence sufficient net asset backing and to complete the bank's MiFID II appropriateness or eligibility assessment before execution.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A Cyprus private limited company is exempt from a mandatory statutory audit if, for two consecutive years, it meets at least two of three small-entity thresholds: balance-sheet total not exceeding EUR 2,000,000; net turnover not exceeding EUR 5,000,000; and average number of employees not exceeding 50. The exemption does not apply to public-interest entities (banks, insurance, listed companies) or if shareholders holding at least 10% of the issued shares request an audit. An ESOP trust that qualifies as a small company may use the exemption; otherwise an independent certified audit is required.

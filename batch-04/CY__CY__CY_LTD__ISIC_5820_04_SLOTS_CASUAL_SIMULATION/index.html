Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation of the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus (Nicosia); taxpayer portal: ARIADE (Tax For All) at ariade.mof.gov.cy; official tax department website: www.mof.gov.cy/taxdep.

E-government filing portal

For a Cyprus private limited company (HE prefix): the company is incorporated with the Registrar of Companies and the Department of Taxation automatically issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) that mirrors the HE registration number; no separate income tax registration is needed. A foreign-owned company that will make taxable supplies in Cyprus must file Form VAT-1 through the ARIADE portal, declaring anticipated CY taxable turnover, or register under the Union OSS / non-Union schemes for cross-border electronic services. Mandatory VAT registration is required within 30 days of exceeding the EUR 15,600 annual threshold; voluntary registration is available for the export-oriented game publisher. Non-resident companies with no Cyprus fixed establishment may be registered directly or through a tax representative in Cyprus.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - Cyprus private limited company is taxed as the liable taxable person at 12.5% corporate income tax; no pass-through or transparency is permitted, and a modified territoriality exemption applies to foreign dividends and foreign permanent establishment profits.

Available election forms

Cyprus has no check-the-box regime, but offers statutory elections: (i) deemed-dividend distribution election under Income Tax Law section 37 (17% special defence contribution on the distribution of otherwise undistributed profits); (ii) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital introduced after 1 January 2015; (iii) IP Box regime election (80% tax deduction on qualifying IP profits); (iv) group loss relief and group gain elections; (v) VAT OSS/IOSS and EC Sales List election schemes; and (vi) hybrid-mismatch elections and disclosures required under the EU ATAD provisions as implemented in Cyprus.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover of supplies of goods and services with a place of supply in Cyprus (mandatory registration within 30 days of the threshold being reached); EUR 10,000 annual remote intra-EU distance sales threshold for micro-businesses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0%) applies to (i) exports of goods physically dispatched or transported to a non-EU destination; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to a business customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number and reported in the EC Sales List; and (iii) international transport services and ancillary loading services. Electronic services (social casino games, virtual slots) supplied to non-EU B2C consumers fall outside the scope of Cypriot VAT under the place-of-supply rules rather than being zero-rated; supplies to EU B2C consumers are taxed in the consumer's Member State via the OSS scheme, and B2B supplies to foreign businesses are reverse-charged. The exporter must retain conclusive evidence that goods left the EU within the statutory time frame and that the economic benefit accrued to the Cypriot entity. No Cyprus statutory foreign-exchange repatriation period is codified, but the mandated 180-day window in this dossier is used as the bank compliance benchmark for inward remittances from Apple IAP and Google Play.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document) with exported Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial invoice and proforma or contract evidencing the export sale
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note or Air Waybill proving physical departure from the European Union
  • Packing list and customs or port arrival proofs where requested
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank swift or credit confirmation linking payment to the specific export transaction
  • Customer's valid EU VAT number and EC Sales List / VIES confirmation for intra-Community supplies

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing or digital stamping is currently in force in Cyprus. Electronic invoices are accepted on equal footing with paper invoices under the VAT Law, provided authenticity, integrity and legibility are preserved. Cyprus is aligning with the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package: a mandatory EU-wide B2B e-invoicing and digital reporting framework is expected by 2030; Cypriot businesses should maintain e-invoicing capability for Apple IAP and Google Play Billing ledgers.

The Department of Taxation may assess VAT at 19% where zero-rating is claimed without an adequate documentary trail, with a correction period normally of five years from filing (no time limit in fraud cases), plus statutory interest and administrative penalties under Article 53 of the VAT Law. Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating claims attract criminal prosecution, fines (up to EUR 15,000), imprisonment (up to three years), retrospective clawback of input VAT, revocation of approved exporter status, and publication of penalties. Approved economic operators are subject to continuing customs and VAT audits; the social casino publisher's Apple IAP and Google Play remittance records are included in the audit perimeter.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus, as a euro-area member state, maintains full currency convertibility. Both current and capital account transactions are unrestricted under the EU Capital Movement Directive (Directive 88/361/EEC) and the Cypriot Exchange Control Law framework, which has been effectively suspended/abolished since EU accession (2004). Profit remittances, dividends, capital repatriation, and loan servicing may be freely executed via licensed credit institutions without prior central bank or ministry approval, subject only to standard anti-money laundering ('AML') due diligence, tax withholding compliance (where applicable), and international sanctions screening. Restrictions would only arise from EU/UN sanctions, judicial orders, or a bank's internal risk appetite. No general convertibility hurdles or material profit remittance impediments exist for a Cyprus private limited company in good standing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus imposes no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion of foreign currency receipts into EUR, and no specified timeline for repatriation of export earnings. Export proceeds may be held in foreign-currency accounts, retained abroad for treasury purposes, or converted at market rates, subject to bank-level account monitoring, intra-EU customs documentation, and VIES/Intrastat reporting where applicable. For reasonableness, banks may apply internal guidelines requiring supporting invoices/Bill of Lading to justify large inbound credits.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) as the national competent authority within the Eurosystem, with the European Central Bank (ECB) as the ultimate monetary and foreign exchange policy authority for euro-area members

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for treasury facilitation, but not a substitute for a licensed commercial bank settlement account. Cyprus-licensed and EEA passported Electronic Money Institutions are permissible under the Electronic Money Law (L.87(I)/2012) and PSD2, and may provide multi-currency IBANs, low-cost payment collection, FX conversion, and enhanced payment API integration for social-casino revenue flows (Apple/Google payouts). However, offshore digital EMIs from non-EEA jurisdictions without a Central Bank of Cyprus passport or EU equivalent cannot lawfully provide payment services to Cypriot entities; using such unfinancially regulated entities would constitute an AML red flag and likely breach local licensing rules for payment services. For capital escrow and large trade settlements, a regulated Tier-1 bank remains mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses a single-leg or dual-leg SWIFT MT103/MT202 TT from the investor's bank to the Cyprus account, with the payment purpose coded as 'CAPT' (capital contribution) or 'MCNE' (equity injection), and a beneficiary reference containing the company name and project code. The receiving Cyprus bank will require a certified board/shareholder resolution authorising the capital increase, an updated Companies Registry extract reflecting the issued share capital, an AML/beneficial ownership declaration, and evidence of source of funds. The transfer must be free from sanctions-tainted jurisdictions and should be accompanied by a formal currency conversion instruction if the investor holds non-EUR funds. For amounts exceeding EUR 100,000, the bank will also conduct a false-prescriptive vetting under the Central Bank's AML directives. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 (annual intercompany transactions of goods, services, royalties or loans for local file preparation), and Master File required when Group aggregate turnover exceeds EUR 75,000,000 and intercompany transactions exceed the threshold prescribed by administrative practice; Cyprus TP rules (s. 51 Income Tax Law, Defence Law, and Administrative Guidelines) align with OECD TPG, and documentation is compulsory when the transaction value exceeds EUR 750,000 per category or when the enterprise operates 'high-value' intangible and financing arrangements; smaller firms must still prepare contemporaneous TP documentation in case of audit, as penalties apply even without threshold.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has implemented Economic Substance Rules under the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive 1 (ATAD) via the Interpretation Guidelines and the Cyprus Income Tax Law; all Cyprus-resident companies (including tax-exempt entities) that derive relevant income (or have assets and risk) from IP, financing, leasing, or high-risk functions must demonstrate real economic activity commensurate with the business: physical premises, qualified full-time employees (appropriately senior to decision-making), actual board meetings in Cyprus, and local management discretion. The rules are primarily triggered for entities holding IP assets or performing intellectual property functions; in a social-casino/gaming holding structure generating digital royalties or management fees, absence of such substance creates a fatal red flag. The Cypriot authorities apply the OECD/EFTA approach and require detailed local substance documentation, substance reports, annual evidence of bank activity and management minutes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Cyprus Income Tax Law s.93 and Criminal Code, conviction renders imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fine up to EUR 3,000 for individuals; corporate fine up to EUR 30,000; additional tax assessment at 100% of the deficient tax plus interest at 1.75% per quarter and a statutory penalty up to EUR 3,000 per false return; publication/reputation sanctions under anti-money laundering framework.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the company is a mere façade and director controls routing of payments; directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes under registration/collection orders if they signed fraudulent returns or are involved in the preparation of falsified accounts; civil restitution orders, travel bans (departure prohibition orders issued by tax authorities), disqualification as director (up to 10 years), and personal criminal liability can attach even for limited-liability companies.
Transfer Pricing Mismatch / Absence of Arm's Length File

Statutory sanction: Tax Authority may upward adjust taxable profits by the arm's length deviation, impose additional tax at 12.5% with interest at 1.75% per quarter (or current quarterly rate) and administrative penalties of up to EUR 5,000 for missing/incorrect documentation; in serious cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion with imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors approving non-arm's length arrangements without documented board rationale and economic substance may be held jointly liable for penalty tax, and their actions may trigger the reverse onus of proof in tax fraud proceedings; personal asset freezing or worldwide freezing orders are available.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Cyprus Directorate of Tax and Customs may disregard the company's taxable presence and re-assess profits to the beneficial owner under the Anti-Avoidance Rules (s.61A Income Tax Law, section 66/67 AAD); penalties include denial of treaty benefits (under MLI and DTA abuse limitation), issuance of a 'special assessment' with 5% monthly penalties (capped at 50%), and revocation of tax residence certificate; if the company enters insolvency, there is no limited-liability protection and creditors/authorities can apply for striking-off and disqualification orders.

Director personal liability: Directors can be subject to loss of limited liability for wrongful trading, personal liability for tax debts under s. 48 of the Companies Law, Cap. 113 (if distributions were made while insolvent), and criminal sanctions for operating a company as a vehicle of fraud; additional 'official liquidator' investigations may lead to 12-year restriction orders and travel bans.
AML / Sanctions Violation (Digital Payments)

Statutory sanction: Cyprus Unit for Combating Money Laundering (MOKAS) may impose administrative penalties up to EUR 200,000 or 5% of annual turnover (under AML Directive implementation), criminal fine for money laundering to EUR 100,000 or imprisonment up to 10 years; assets may be forfeited under Section 3 and 4 of the AML Law; CySEC (if auxiliary services provided) may revoke licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and Compliance Officers (AMLCO) are individually criminally liable for failing to report suspicious transactions or executing payments for persons on sanctioned lists; travel bans, property seizure, personal fines, and imprisonment up to 10 years; civil liability for recovery of misappropriated funds.
Late Filing / Non-Filing Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of EUR 100 per un-filed return; where returns outstanding for consecutive years, EUR 200 per return; late payment interest at 1.75% per quarter and a 10% additional tax for unpaid tax at due date; removal of ability to obtain a tax clearance/certificate; restriction imposed on carrying forward tax losses.

Director personal liability: Under s. 43 of Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, the directors of a company are jointly and severally liable for tax debts if the company's failure occurs after winding up proceedings or during a distraint; personal insolvency procedure may be instigated; refusal of travel clearance for outward movement.

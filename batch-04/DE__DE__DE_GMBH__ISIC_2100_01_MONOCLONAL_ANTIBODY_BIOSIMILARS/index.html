Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Department (Handelsregisterabteilung) of the Local Court (Amtsgericht) in the district of the company's registered office, under the state justice administration; the Public Notary (Notar) acts as the mandatory filing intermediary via electronic submission. Official extracts are retrievable through the national registry portal www.handelsregister.de.

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbH-Gesetz – German Limited Liability Companies Act), in particular § 1 (legal personality), § 2 (notarized Articles of Association), § 3 (mandatory contents), § 4a, § 5 (minimum share capital), § 7 (application for registration), § 8 (content of application), § 9c (registry court review), § 10 (legal effect of registration), § 14, §§ 35–46 (managing directors); in conjunction with §§ 8, 9, 9c, 106–110 of the German Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch – HGB).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified Deed of Incorporation and Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) together with the Registration Application (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister), signed by all managing directors and submitted electronically by the notary.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Statutory minimum share capital (Stammkapital) is EUR 25,000. At the time of registration, at least 50% of the nominal amount of each cash share must be paid in, provided that aggregate cash contributions reach at least EUR 12,500. For a single-shareholder GmbH (Einpersonen-GmbH), the cash portion must be paid in full (EUR 12,500). Contributions must be deposited into a dedicated bank account held in the name of the GmbH in formation (GmbH i.G.), with a written bank deposit confirmation (Einzahlungsbestätigung) attached to the registration application; the funds must remain at the free disposal of the managing directors post-registration. Non-cash contributions (Sacheinlagen) require a notarized contribution report and full transfer of assets.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate (brandschutztechnische Abnahme) issued by the local fire protection authority confirming structural fire resistance, approved alarm and extinguishing systems, emergency exits, and evacuation routes; structural load and vibration clearance (Standsicherheitsnachweis) by the building supervision authority for bioreactor vessels, cleanroom floors, and heavy process equipment; workplace clearance under the German Occupational Health and Safety Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz) and the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung), covering hazardous substance assessments, noise, ventilation, and GMP cleanroom classification (Grades A/B/C/D); occupational clearance under the Biological Agents Ordinance (Biostoffverordnung) for personnel handling infectious or genetically modified biological materials, including medical surveillance and containment training records.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate the GmbH in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). Step 2: apply online to the Generalzolldirektion for the EORI number, mandatory before the first ATLAS export declaration. Step 3: file the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent local Finanzamt via ELSTER within four weeks of commencing business; the Finanzamt assigns the economic tax number (Steuernummer), sets corporate tax and trade tax prepayments, and defines the VAT filing cycle. Step 4: apply to the BZSt via ELSTER for the USt-IdNr. (format DE + 9 digits), required for intra-Community B2B invoicing, the EC Sales List and Intrastat. Step 5: a foreign parent holding shares in the GmbH without a German permanent establishment does not require a separate German TIN; if the foreign parent operates a German fixed establishment (Betriebsstätte), that establishment must be registered locally and must obtain its own Steuernummer. Step 6: all VAT and corporate tax returns, including monthly or quarterly preliminary VAT returns (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung) and annual returns, must be submitted electronically through ELSTER with a qualified electronic signature.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in German tax law; the GmbH is an opaque capital company subject to corporate income tax at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and trade tax at the municipal Hebesatz applied to a 3.5% tax base. Available elective mechanisms: (i) Organschaft (corporate and trade tax group) with the parent under §§14, 17 KStG, requiring majority shareholding, financial/economic/organisational integration and a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag); (ii) trade tax allocation elections between municipal jurisdictions under §28 GewStG; (iii) reverse option under §1a KStG whereby a partnership, such as a GmbH & Co. KG, may elect to be taxed as a corporation; (iv) hybrid-mismatch compliance and limitation rules under §4k EStG (ATAD 2 implementation) covering hybrid financial instruments and reverse hybrid entities; (v) reinvestment reserve elections under §6b EStG for qualifying movable manufacturing assets. No election is available for the GmbH itself to be treated as a transparent pass-through entity.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro qualifies as a fully convertible currency under EU and German foreign exchange law. Capital account transactions, including profit remittances and capital contributions, are unrestricted and freely transferable subject only to standard statistical reporting obligations. No capital controls or surrender requirements apply to resident or non-resident entities operating within the EU single market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to the German entity within twelve months of shipment date. No mandatory surrender to the central bank or conversion at official rates; repatriation occurs at prevailing market exchange rates. Statistical reporting via the Bundesbank is mandatory through the electronic reporting system (ELSTER or equivalent), with supporting documentation including commercial invoices and shipping documents.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs face significant legal constraints under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and EU PSD2 framework. Licensed German payment service providers or partnerships with BaFin-supervised entities are required; pure offshore structures without local licensing are generally prohibited for high-risk biopharma transactions.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a combined federal rate of 15.825%. Municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional; its rate depends on the municipality’s multiplier. The combined corporate and trade tax burden is commonly approximately 30%, but is not a uniform statutory rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital income withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax, producing a combined domestic withholding rate of 26.375%, before any applicable church tax. A reduced rate, exemption, or refund may be available under an applicable double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, subject to eligibility, beneficial-ownership, anti-abuse, and procedural requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the annual tax returns are generally due seven months after the end of the tax year. Where a tax adviser prepares the returns, the statutory extended deadline is generally the last day of February of the second following calendar year, subject to statutory exceptions and any applicable administrative extensions. The tax office may set an earlier deadline in an individual case.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German transfer-pricing documentation requirements include transaction-based thresholds generally of EUR 6 million per fiscal year for supplies of goods and EUR 600,000 per fiscal year for other transactions, including services. A Master File is generally required where the taxpayer’s preceding-year turnover exceeds EUR 100 million. These thresholds do not exempt a taxpayer from the arm’s-length principle or from the duty to provide information. Documentation must be prepared and supplied within the statutory period following a request; special rules and shorter deadlines may apply in tax audits.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. German tax law nevertheless applies the arm’s-length principle under section 1 AStG, general anti-abuse rules under section 42 AO, beneficial-ownership and treaty anti-abuse rules, and specific substance and business-purpose tests where relevant. Intra-group financing and intellectual-property arrangements require evidence of actual functions, decision-making, control of risks, personnel, assets, and capacity to bear risks. A contractual label or foreign incorporation alone does not establish substantive entitlement to income or treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 AO is punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; in especially serious cases, imprisonment may be from six months to ten years. Unpaid tax, interest, and related assessments may also be recoverable. Negligent tax reduction under section 378 AO is an administrative offence and may be fined.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal or administrative liability for their own conduct and personal tax-payment liability under section 69 AO where a culpable breach of tax duties causes tax claims to be unpaid. Corporate status does not shield a director from personal criminal liability.
False transfer pricing and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income under section 1 AStG, impose additional tax and interest, and apply documentation-related surcharges under section 162 AO, generally subject to statutory minimums and maximums. Intentional or reckless false reporting may also trigger tax-crime or administrative proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for culpable tax-duty breaches and may face criminal or administrative penalties where they knowingly authorize or submit false information.
Dividend withholding tax failure

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may remain liable for the withholding tax, solidarity surcharge, interest, and applicable penalties. False applications or intentional non-remittance may lead to tax-crime or administrative proceedings.

Director personal liability: A managing director responsible for withholding and remittance may incur personal liability under section 69 AO for a culpable failure and may face criminal liability for intentional evasion.
VAT fraud or fictitious input-tax claims

Statutory sanction: Input-tax deductions may be denied and VAT, interest, and penalties assessed. Intentional tax evasion may be punishable under section 370 AO, including imprisonment of up to five years or, in especially serious cases, six months to ten years.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability for their own participation and personal tax-payment liability for culpable breaches of VAT duties. A director’s lack of knowledge is fact-specific and does not eliminate the duty to maintain appropriate controls.
Insolvency-related tax and payment misconduct

Statutory sanction: Failure to file for insolvency without undue delay may constitute an offence under section 15a InsO and is punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to three years, or up to one year where negligent. Prohibited payments after insolvency may create restitution and liability exposure under insolvency law.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal civil liability for prohibited payments, tax liabilities under section 69 AO, and criminal liability for insolvency offences. Any travel restriction or imprisonment depends on a specific legal order or criminal conviction and is not an automatic consequence of tax non-compliance.
Shell company, sham arrangements, or abusive treaty claims

Statutory sanction: Relief may be denied, withholding tax and interest recovered, and anti-abuse provisions applied, including sections 42 AO and 50d(3) EStG where relevant. Intentional false declarations may lead to tax-evasion proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability for their own knowing participation, false certifications, or culpable failure to perform statutory tax duties. There is no automatic piercing of the GmbH’s limited liability solely because it is foreign-owned or has limited operational substance.
Money laundering and sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under section 261 StGB is generally punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years, with higher exposure in aggravated cases. Sanctions and foreign-trade violations may attract criminal or administrative penalties under applicable German and EU law; the specific sanction depends on the transaction and governing measure.

Director personal liability: Directors and employees may be personally prosecuted for knowing participation or other individual offences. Corporate fines and confiscation may also be imposed where statutory conditions are met; travel bans and imprisonment are not automatic and require the applicable legal process.

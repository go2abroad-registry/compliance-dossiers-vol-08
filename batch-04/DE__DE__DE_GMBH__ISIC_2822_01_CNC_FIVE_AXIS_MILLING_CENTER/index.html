Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Finanzamt (local tax office) under the Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF) and Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt); electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned GmbH must register with the local competent Finanzamt (tax office determined by seat/Jurisdiction of business) on Form 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (§ 137 AO) via ELSTER or in writing to obtain a Steuernummer; the GmbH then applies to the BZSt (via ELSTER) for the USt-IdNr. (VAT registration number) under § 27a UStG and for the customs EORI number; if importing/exporting, the EORI number is granted automatically by the Generalzolldirektion upon registration. Registration is mandatory before commencement of business; online portal is ELSTER and customs portal ATLAS.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — German GmbH is a body corporate with unlimited corporate income tax liability (unbeschränkte Körperschaftsteuerpflicht, 15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the KSt and Gewerbesteuer, ~16.5% effective CIT) and a permanent Establishment trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) with municipal multiplier (Hebesatz) of 200%-490%; distributions to shareholders are subject to 26.375% Abgeltungsteuer (withholding tax), reduced under any treaty or EU parent-subsidiary directive; no check-the-box; per se per se entity-level taxation also for CFC and hybrid purposes.

Available election forms

(1) Corporate tax group election (Organschaft) under §§ 14 ff. KStG and VAT fiscal unity under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG allowing financial and indirect tax consolidation with a German parent; (2) Small-business option (Kleinunternehmer) under § 19 UStG exempt from VAT if turnover ≤ EUR 22,000 prior year and ≤ EUR 50,000 current year, with waiver of input VAT recovery; (3) Optional registration and VAT invoicing under § 9 UStG (option to waive VAT exemption for real-estate transactions); (4) European OSS/IOSS regimes and the Union-wide distance-sale threshold election of EUR 10,000 (cross-border B2C); (5) Hybrid mismatch structural elections via the German implementation of ATAD 1 (Section 4k EStG, § 14 KStG) — Germany does not provide check-the-box classifications but recognizes foreign entity classification for hybrid mismatch analysis under the OECD BEPS Action 2 rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general compulsory VAT registration threshold exists: any entrepreneur (Unternehmer) making taxable supplies in Germany must register from its first euro of turnover. Only the small-business exemption (§ 19 UStG) is elective where prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover ≤ EUR 50,000. For distance sales of goods into Germany, the EU Union-wide threshold of EUR 10,000 applies (excluding own-basis VAT OSS); for imports of low-value consignments (≤ EUR 150), the import VAT exemption has been abolished and the IOSS mechanism is used.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0% VAT, tax exemption with credit) applies to (a) physical exports of goods to destinations outside the EU Customs Territory (§ 4 Nr. 1 lit. a, § 6 UStG) and (b) intra-Community supplies to EU businesses possessing a valid German USt-IdNr./VIES registration (§ 4 Nr. 1 lit. b, § 6a UStG). The 0% rate requires proof that the goods left the EU within strict documentary time frames and, for intra-EU, inclusion of the buyer's VAT ID and the EC Sales List. Related offshore services for the CNC five-axis milling center (e.g., installation, maintenance and technical services physically rendered abroad or supplied B2B to third-country entrepreneurs under the place-of-supply rule in § 3a UStG) are outside the scope of German VAT or qualifying as tax-free supplies; the benefit is conditional on maintaining the statutory Export Proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) under §§ 8-13 UStDV.

  • Electronic customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) filed in the ATLAS customs system (or via the exporter/proxy) with the Export Accompanying Document (EAD)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Combined Transport Document evidencing transport out of the EU customs territory
  • Inward remittance certificate and cross-border payment register report (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik / AWV Meldung) filed with the Deutsche Bundesbank for every export payment above EUR 12,500
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) and the customs export confirmation (Ausfuhrbestätigung) of the German or border customs office
  • For five-axis CNC milling centers: end-user certificate and dual-use/export-control documentation where the machine is classified under EU Dual-Use Regulation (EU) 2021/821 Annex I Category 2B001; an EC Teaching File or End-User Undertaking may be required

Germany follows a phased e-invoicing mandate (Wachstumschancengesetz / E-Rechnungsverordnung): from 1 January 2025, every domestic B2B supplier must be able to electronically receive structured e-invoices; issuing obligation becomes mandatory for companies with turnover > EUR 800,000 from 1 January 2027 and for all other taxable persons from 1 January 2028. Accepted formats are EN 16931-compliant structured data formats (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD/Factur-X); transmission through PEPPOL, qualified e-mail, or certified data-interchange channels. This is a post-audit 'report-to-retain' model — Germany has NOT yet implemented a real-time fiscal clearance/CTC model (no Italian-type SdI digital stamping); VAT returns (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldungen) are filed electronically via ELSTER (monthly with Dauerfristverlängerung or quarterly depending on prior-year tax burden) and annual VAT returns are due by year-end plus 7 months. Further obligations: Intrastat, EC Sales Lists and e-Services reporting are filed via ELSTER/IDV.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a full-convertibility, unrestricted-capital euro-area jurisdiction: both current-account and capital-account operations are liberalized under TFEU Articles 63-66, with no exchange-control approvals, no convertibility limits, no profit-repatriation licensing and no surrender requirements applicable to a GmbH owned by a non-resident. The only statutory constraints on profit remittance for a non-resident shareholder are commercial-law limitations on distributing unreleased reserves (HGB §§ 268(8), 253 - the minimum-underwrite restrictions; GmbHG §§ 29-30 creditor protection) and the dividend withholding tax of 26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), reduced to the treaty rate (typically 0-15%, most often 5% for a corporate 10% shareholder holding) upon presentation of the SEPA/national-FTGS declaration to the client adviser. An additional two-pronged check exists for intra-group financing transactions, where the tax authorities may reclassify an excessive shareholder loan as equity (German GAAP and Treaty anti-articification principles), so profit remittances should be supported by contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds are not subject to surrender, earmarking, forced conversion, or foreign-exchange rationing in Germany; EUR and foreign-currency export receipts are freely disposable. Statutory discipline comes from the AWV statistical and notification statutes: (i) any cross-border receipt/payment equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 must be individually reported to Deutsche Bundesbank within 5 business days (§§ 66, 67 AWV) by the resident bank or the GmbH, under the Bundesbank IPS portal; (ii) goods exports and imports with third countries are aggregate-reported monthly under § 60 AWV when the monthly flow is EUR 12,500 or more; (iii) each MT103/MT202 wire must include a meaningful German 'Verwendungszweck' and the bank assigns the Msg/Bundesbank code. For CNC five-axis milling centers, the proceeds will be cleared only after the BAFA dual-use permission/EUC inspection is on file, but no percentage of the proceeds is retained or converted by the authorities.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main), acting as the German foreign-exchange and cross-border-payments statistics authority under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV); prudential conduct and AML/CFT supervision is shared with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin; Bonn) pursuant to the Kreditwesengesetz (KWG) and the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG). Export-control and sanctions jurisdiction is exercised by the Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Klimaschutz (BMWK) and the Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle (BAFA, Eschborn).

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium feasibility. BaFin-licensed fintech EMIs and digital payment institutions (Wise, Airwallex, Penta, Qonto, Solarisbank) are legally capable of holding segregated client funds and executing SEPA/MT103 payments, but they are not licensed to take deposits or issue documentary credits, and their AML risk appetite excludes High_Risk_Special_License manufacturers in ISIC 2822 with dual-use exposure. They are suitable only for low-value ancillary disbursements, never for inbound equity subscription, export proceeds collection, or LC-based settlements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Koerperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax (0.825%) plus Gewerbesteuer at municipal rates typically 14-17%; aggregate effective statutory burden approximately 30% in most municipalities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge); reduced to 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if holding >=10% for 12 months; DTA rates commonly 5-15% depending on ownership and country.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 annual cross-border related-party sales/transactions triggers full Local File and Master File obligation; CbCR required for consolidated groups with revenue >= EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; however substance requirements are enforced through ATAD rules (interest limitation, CFC rules, GAAR under Sec. 35 EStG), BZSt anti-shell company rulings (Sec. 50d EStG), and OECD Pillar Two GloBE. The GmbH must demonstrate actual management premises, qualified staff, operational control, and complete accounting records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine; if large-scale because of gross self-interest, imprisonment up to 10 years under Sec. 370 AO; additional back taxes plus penalty up to EUR 25,000 and statutory late interest.

Director personal liability: Managing director (Geschäftsführer) personally liable under Sec. 69 AO and Sec. 34 AO for taxes not withheld or paid; criminal conviction can lead to travel ban, extradition within EU, seizure of assets, and civil restitution for corporate tax loss.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, deemed hidden dividend distribution, minimum withholding assessment, penalty up to EUR 25,000; BZSt may cancel VAT ID; corporate registration struck off for insolvency.

Director personal liability: Directors held liable under Sec. 42 AO abuse doctrine, payment liabilities under Sec. 64 GmbHG; travel ban under Sec. 132 AO if tax debts persist; personal bankruptcy and disqualification for management positions.
Transfer Pricing Audit Violation

Statutory sanction: Documentation penalty plus interest; upward TP adjustment (Sec. 1 AStG) with 6% annual compounding interest (Sec. 3a AStG); penalty up to 10% of adjusted income, minimum EUR 5,000; possible criminal prosecution for false documentation.

Director personal liability: Geschäftsführer is statutorily obligated to maintain transfer pricing records; personal liability for penalty surcharge and interest if taxes remain unpaid due to negligent documentation; criminal conviction under Sec. 370 AO for false records leads to imprisonment up to 5 years and personal civil liability for tax deficiencies.
AML / Financial Compliance Breach (GwG)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 5,000,000 per violation or 10% of annual turnover in serious cases; license revocation for export-related CNC sales; criminal penalties under Sec. 54 GwG for intentional violations.

Director personal liability: Directors face imprisonment up to 2 years for negligent AML violations, up to 5 years for intentional violations; travel bans and surrender of passports ordered by German prosecutors; liability for fines if company cannot pay; personal forfeiture of illicit profits.

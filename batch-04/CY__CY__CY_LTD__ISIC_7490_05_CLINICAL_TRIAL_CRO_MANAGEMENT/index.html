Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department (Department of Taxation), Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; online portal: Tax Portal ARIADNE (https://taxportal.cy / https://ariadne.mof.gov.cy) and Cyprus VAT service

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity must be incorporated under the Cyprus Companies Law Cap.113 with the Registrar of Companies to obtain an HE registration number. Following incorporation, the Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN) is automatically aligned with the company's registration number for corporate income tax purposes. For VAT purposes, the company must submit a VAT registration application (Form TD 101 / VAT-101) through the Tax Portal, attaching the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, proof of registered office address, and a declaration of intended activities. The Registration Officer issues a VAT number in the format CY + 9 digits (CY12345678X) within a few weeks. For non-resident entities without a Cyprus establishment, a fiscal representative may be required for VAT registration; a Cyprus branch or subsidiary is standard for permanent establishment status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election is available for a Cyprus private limited company. Cyprus resident companies are subject to entity-level corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide income. Non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. No hybrid mismatch election is provided under the Income Tax Law; EU ATAD-aligned hybrid mismatch rules are applied mandatorily and do not offer elective treatment. Certain standard elections under the Income Tax Law (e.g., group relief, capital allowances, loss relief) are available as ordinary tax mechanisms, not entity classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 / €15,600 (annual taxable turnover; mandatory VAT registration applies when the taxable supply of goods or services by an established or non-established trader exceeds this threshold in any consecutive 12-month period). Separate threshold: EUR 10,000 for intra-Community distance sales and certain digital services / e-commerce supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to: (i) sales of goods physically exported from Cyprus to a destination outside the EU or dispatched/transported to another EU Member State, provided the goods leave Cyprus within the statutory period and proof of movement is retained; (ii) direct exports of goods by the supplier or on behalf of the customer; (iii) supply of services with the place of supply outside Cyprus (e.g., clinical trial services, CRO services, R&D services provided to non-resident pharmaceutical clients) when supplied to a business customer established outside Cyprus; (iv) certain internationally related services (e.g., transport, ancillary services) strictly subject to export conditions. Zero-rating is not automatic for intra-Community services without a valid customer VAT number. For exempt or zero-rated CRO services, a formal documentary trail proving the non-EEA establishment and commercial substance of the recipient is mandatory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / AES-export submitted through Cyprus Customs)
  • Bill of Lading (sea) / Air Waybill (air) / CMR (road) evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT confirmation of foreign exchange crediting to the Cypriot company's account
  • Commercial invoice
  • Contract/Service Agreement (for cross-border CRO services)
  • Proof of the customer's non-resident status (e.g., foreign VAT registration certificate / valid TIN)
  • Intra-EU sales list (VIES) or written statement that goods were dispatched to another Member State (if applicable)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping regime exists in Cyprus as of 2025. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory for suppliers to the public sector via the Pan-European Public Procurement On-Line (PEPPOL) network. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary; standard EDI/XML/PDF in electronic form is accepted provided authenticity and integrity are guaranteed. Cyprus is not yet implementing the EU ViDA real-time e-invoicing mandate; future obligations are anticipated post-2028.

The Cyprus Tax Department and Customs Authorities apply strict documentary review to zero-rated export claims (goods and services). If the required evidence is not available within 180 days of the supply, VAT at 19% is clawed back together with statutory interest (currently 9.5% per annum, revised by ministerial order) and administrative penalties under Articles 49-50 of the VAT Law. Penalties range from 50% to 200% of the tax underpaid, up to EUR 5,000 per assessment, and further fines for failure to register or incorrect e-invoicing records. Fraudulent claims for export zero-rating expose the company and directors to criminal liability, imprisonment, unlimited fines, and retrospective reassessments of up to ten years for fraud. Even in good faith, an unsupported zero-rating claim is treated as an incorrect return and attracts retroactive clawback for the six-year limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions under EU free-movement-of-capital rules (TFEU Art. 63). Cyprus operates no general exchange-control regime; the Exchange Control Law was repealed in 1994 and no post-2013 emergency controls remain in force. Profit remittance is unrestricted: no withholding tax applies on dividends, interest, or royalties to non-residents under Cyprus domestic law and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. The only practical hurdles are standard AML/KYC verification of the beneficiary and CBC statistical reporting (Balance-of-Payments) for cross-border flows. A clinical-trial CRO may invoice sponsors worldwide in EUR or foreign currency, maintain multi-currency accounts, and transfer funds abroad without prior regulatory consent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rules apply. Cyprus imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation obligation for export/service proceeds. CRO export revenue (clinical-trial services rendered to foreign sponsors) may be held fully in EUR or in foreign currency in Cyprus accounts. The sole compliance duty is indirect: quarterly BOP reporting by resident banks for transactions exceeding the CBC statistical threshold, with zero effect on convertibility or liquidity.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) as the national competent authority within the Eurosystem; European Central Bank (ECB) holds monetary-policy and payment-systems oversight. Exchange-control administration rests with the CBC's Financial Stability and Foreign Exchange Departments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility. An EU/EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institution (EMI) may passport into Cyprus under the E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and PSD2, enabling remote account opening, SEPA/Instant SEPA clearing, and multi-currency IBANs for milestone wires. Legal constraints: (i) EMI accounts are not CBC-licensed deposits, hence no Deposit Guarantee Scheme protection; (ii) large-volume clinical-trial flows (patient compensation, investigator grants) can trigger enhanced due diligence and card/network restrictions at the EMI; (iii) Cyprus-licensed banks may later reject EMI-sourced funds as unverified. Recommended use: complementary payment-on-behalf-of (PBO) rail for low-value EEA site disbursements, not as the primary treasury account. Preference: AdvCash- or Wise-type EMIs regulated by an EU authority with a valid EBA passporting notification to the CBC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: shareholder sends EUR via SWIFT MT103 to the CY Ltd capital escrow account at a licensed Cyprus bank, using payment purpose code 'CAPL' (Capital Contribution) or EU BOP code 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (ECB BOP category 301/310). Supporting documents: board resolution approving share allotment, share register update, shareholder loan agreement if applicable, and CBC BOP statistical form submitted by the bank. Verification steps: (i) the bank confirms the beneficiary is the CY Ltd account and the remitter matches the UBO/register; (ii) capital is booked to the share capital account and reported to the Cyprus Registrar via your annual return (HE32); (iii) no exchange-control approval is required. Injunction: if the injection originates from a non-CY EU bank, route via SEPA with purpose code 'EQUI' (Equity purchase); if non-EU, attach source-of-funds pack to enable the capital-account credit.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for high-value/Overseas sponsor-to-site wires and investigator milestone payments; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant for EEA site payments under €100k with same-day settlement; (3) Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) and Standby LC (MT760) for procurement of medical supplies or equipment where the supplier demands bank-backed payment; (4) batch bulk payments via the bank's host-to-host channel (e.g., SWIFT FileAct/EBICS) for monthly site reimbursements. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File: a Cyprus-resident taxpayer must prepare, retain and file a transfer-pricing study and Local File for a tax year when the aggregate value of its controlled (related-party/associated-introducing) transactions, including CRO milestone fees, subcontracting payments and inter-company charge-outs, equals or exceeds EUR 1,000,000. Mandatory Master File: a multinational group whose consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000 and which has a Cyprus resident entity in the above threshold must also submit a Master File to the Cyprus Tax Department. Below these EUR thresholds, contemporaneous documentation is still recommended to prevent an adjustment.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus imposes tax residence on a company only when 'management and control' is exercised in Cyprus (Section 35 of the Income Tax Law). Following the EU Code of Conduct for Business Taxation, Cyprus applies an Economic Substance Doctrine under which a CRO company must show real business activity through local staff, operating premises, risk management, and board-meeting minutes. The Doctrine applies to all Cyprus HE77 companies, including those that conduct clinical trial management through a low-tax BVI subsidiary; if the Cyprus vehicle is merely a paper conduit, the Commissioner can reallocate income to the real decision-making seat abroad and deny the exclusive benefit of the 12.5% rate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Reporting

Statutory sanction: The Cypriot Tax Commissioner assesses the underpaid corporate income tax at 12.5% plus daily statutory interest (3.5% p.a.), a 10% surcharge, and, where the return is fraudulent, an additional assessment of up to 300% of the evaded tax. Under the Cyprus Criminal Code and the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, the company is liable to a fine not exceeding EUR 20,000 and the responsible officers to imprisonment not exceeding five years.

Director personal liability: Where the fraud contributes to a winding-up, the court lifts the corporate veil and orders directors jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and creditor restitution; personal travel bans and conservatory seizures apply to prevent disposal of personal assets; custodial sentences of up to three years are possible for dishonest support of the evasion.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may apply the management-and-control test under Section 35 of the Income Tax Law and recharacterise payments to the foreign PE, assessing tax at 12.5% plus interest and penalties; the Registrar may impose administrative fines of EUR 200 to EUR 500 for failing to maintain required statutory records, and the company can be struck off the register under Companies Law (Cap. 113) without levy of damages.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable in reckless trading for the company's post-shell-spending liabilities, disqualified for up to 10 years from serving as directors, and their freedom of movement restricted by a court order to prevent cross-border exit where the SHELL held sponsorship funds in escrow.

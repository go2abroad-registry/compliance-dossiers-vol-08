Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (Federal Ministry of Finance) for policy; local Finanzämter (tax offices) administer corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT; Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, Federal Central Tax Office) issues the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) and manages EU/international administrative cooperation; customs export procedures run through the Generalzolldirektion via the ATLAS system; all tax filings are transmitted through the ELSTER online portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned GmbH: (1) notarial incorporation and entry in the commercial register (Handelsregister); (2) Gewerbeanmeldung to the municipal Gewerbeamt, which electronically notifies the locally competent Finanzamt; (3) electronic submission of the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) on ELSTER within four weeks of business commencement, declaring the intended EV-manufacturing activity, accounting year and expected turnover, whereupon the Finanzamt assigns the statutory Steuernummer covering corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT advance filings; (4) separate application to BZSt for the DE-prefixed USt-IdNr. (VAT ID) required for intra-Community statements and export invoice substantiation; (5) registration with the Generalzolldirektion for an EORI number enabling ATLAS customs export declarations; (6) if employing staff, registration with the employer liability accident insurance association and the statutory health, pension and care carriers.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the GmbH is an opaque legal person subject to unlimited tax liability; profits are taxed at entity level at 15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on such tax (15.825% combined), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) typically yielding 7% to 17%, for an effective total burden of roughly 30% to 33%. Dividends distributed to shareholders attract 25% withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% all-in) unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. No pass-through, transparent, or S-corporation-type classification exists for a GmbH under German domestic law. — Foreign-owned GmbH: (1) notarial incorporation and entry in the commercial register (Handelsregister); (2) Gewerbeanmeldung to the municipal Gewerbeamt, which electronically notifies the locally competent Finanzamt; (3) electronic submission of the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) on ELSTER within four weeks of business commencement, declaring the intended EV-manufacturing activity, accounting year and expected turnover, whereupon the Finanzamt assigns the statutory Steuernummer covering corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT advance filings; (4) separate application to BZSt for the DE-prefixed USt-IdNr. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the GmbH is an opaque legal person subject to unlimited tax liability; profits are taxed at entity level at 15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on such tax (15.825% combined), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) typically yielding 7% to 17%, for an effective total burden of roughly 30% to 33%. Dividends distributed to shareholders attract 25% withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% all-in) unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. No pass-through, transparent, or S-corporation-type classification exists for a GmbH under German domestic law.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or discretionary entity-classification election is available. Statutory elections relevant to an EU industrial manufacturing group: (i) corporate income tax and trade tax grouping (Organschaft) under §§ 14, 17 KStG and § 2 GewStG, requiring a controlling majority, financial integration and an effectively implemented profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) maintained for at least five years; (ii) VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG integrating controlled enterprises for single VAT accounting; (iii) small-business VAT exemption under § 19 UStG, available only below EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 expected current-year turnover and practically unavailable to an exporter; (iv) option to waive VAT exemption on certain real-property supplies under § 9 UStG; (v) mandatory, non-discretionary anti-hybrid treatment under § 4k EStG and restricted § 8b KStG participation relief that neutralizes hybrid instrument, hybrid entity and reverse-hybrid mismatches in the GmbH's cross-border financing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany imposes no general de-minimis VAT registration threshold domestically: any entrepreneur making taxable supplies is a VAT registrant from the first euro; the only statutory relief is the small-business exemption under § 19 UStG for turnover of EUR 25,000 or less in the previous calendar year and not expected to exceed EUR 100,000 in the current year, which an industrial exporter practically never qualifies for. Full VAT registration with monthly or quarterly advance returns is therefore mandatory for the GmbH's EV-charger sales. For remote suppliers selling goods into Germany from another EU member state, intra-EU distance sales trigger German VAT once EU-wide distance sales exceed EUR 10,000 in the current and preceding calendar years, unless the supplier elects OSS; goods fulfilled from German stock require immediate German VAT registration from the first euro with no threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries (Ausfuhrlieferungen) of DC fast chargers to non-EU third-country territories are exempt with full input-VAT recovery at a 0% effective rate under § 4 Nr. 1a and § 6 UStG if (i) the goods physically leave the EU customs territory; (ii) the GmbH as seller transfers the right to dispose of the goods to the third-country buyer; (iii) the seller or a commissioned forwarder instigates or procures the transport; and (iv) the export is evidenced by an electronic ATLAS export declaration and the statutory book-and-document proof (Buch- und Belegnachweis per §§ 8-14 UStDV). Intra-EU B2B dispatches to a customer holding a valid VAT ID are exempt under § 4 Nr. 1b and § 6a UStG, conditional on reporting in the recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung). Offshore services billed to a non-EU entrepreneur (e.g., installation or commissioning physically performed outside Germany, remote monitoring, embedded software or license fees) fall outside German VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule in § 3a Abs. 2 UStG; services physically performed in Germany (domestic installation, certification or maintenance) remain subject to the standard rate. Input VAT on related procurement is creditable in the same VAT return only once the export substantiation is complete and traceable to specific goods.

  • ATLAS electronic customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with the Export Accompanying Document (EAD) reflecting customs release for physical exit from the EU
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing departure from EU customs territory, together with destination-country import or clearance evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank settlement documentation (SWIFT MT103/MT202, letter-of-credit concurrence, or account statement) linking export proceeds to the specific transaction
  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung) and packing list (Packliste) corresponding to the export declaration and transport particulars
  • Buch- und Belegnachweis register under §§ 13-14 UStDV reconciling the sales ledger, transport documents and customs data; retention for 10 years pursuant to § 147 AO

Germany has no Italian-style SdI authorization, no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping and no national transaction database; tax filings are electronic only via ELSTER. Mandatory B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) entered into force on 1 January 2025: every German B2B supplier must be able to receive EN 16931-compliant structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X); issuance becomes mandatory from 1 January 2027 for suppliers with more than EUR 800,000 turnover in the preceding calendar year and from 1 January 2028 for all suppliers, with qualified EDI processes grandfathered until 31 December 2028. Export invoices to non-EU customers and B2C invoices remain format-free during the transitional period. The pending EU ViDA initiative may later introduce EU-wide digital reporting and e-invoicing, but it is not yet in force.

Export zero-rating is audited ex post by the competent Finanzamt in cooperation with the Generalzolldirektion; missing, late, contradictory or fabricated export evidence reverses the 0% rate and triggers assessment of 19% VAT on the invoice value, disallowance of the corresponding input-VAT recovery, and default interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum) under § 233a AO. Negligent false export declarations are administrative offences under § 378 AO; intentional evasion, including circular export, sham exporter or missing-trader arrangements, constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to 10 years, with a 10-year extended assessment period and the 10-year documentary retention duty of § 147 AO. Customs authorities may impose forfeiture, issue penalty assessments and suspend preferential clearance treatment. Undue deferral of export-claim collection beyond the 180-day benchmark invites transfer-pricing interest adjustments under § 1 AStG and, in cross-border shareholder situations, may be recharacterized as a constructive dividend subject to 25% withholding plus solidarity surcharge; export receipts must additionally be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV for balance-of-payments statistics, with separate administrative fines for non-compliance.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under EU free movement of capital (Articles 63–66 TFEU) and the German AWG/AWV legal framework. No prior approval or exchange-control permit is required for foreign capital injection, retained earnings, or profit and dividend repatriation by a GmbH. The euro is fully convertible; all corporate transactions may be denominated in EUR or any freely convertible currency. Practical hurdles are limited to bank-level KYC/AML documentation and statutory reporting thresholds, not convertibility restrictions. Profit remittances are subject to German withholding tax at 25% (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) unless reduced or exempted under an applicable tax treaty (DTT), but this is a tax, not an FX-control, impediment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory requirement for export proceeds to be surrendered to the central bank and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may remain in EUR or foreign currency and are fully disposable. Obligations are limited to balance-of-payments statistical reporting under AWV: German-resident exporters must report cross-border exports and related payments to Deutsche Bundesbank when the invoice value or payment exceeds EUR 12,500 (or equivalent). Reports must be submitted electronically via the Bundesbank 'Meideverfahren' system; for goods exports to third countries, periodicity is typically monthly with deadlines shortly after month-end, while intra-EU deliverances fall under Intrastat thresholds and customs export declarations supplement this for non-EU trade.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank, operating within the Eurosystem) and the German Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) as principal foreign trade and payments supervisory authorities; FX administration is primarily statistical and surveillance-based, executed through Deutsche Bundesbank pursuant to the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditionally viable for ancillary or operational disbursements (e.g., supplier payments below high-value thresholds, customer refunds, remote expenses) via BaFin-regulated E-Money Institutions (EMIs) under PSD2; however, viability rating is 'Low' for core treasury, L/C issuing, escrow/capital verification and high-value cross-border settlements. Legal constraints: EMIs are not 'Kreditinstitute' under the German Banking Act (KWG) and therefore cannot accept deposits, issue commercial documentary credits, or provide treasury lending; their licenses restrict them to payment/e-money services. German tier-1 banks will not accept an EMI as confirmation or title-holder bank for documentary collections, and an EMI is not a recognised proof point for GER. GmbH capital deposit verification or equity injection evidence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection into the GmbH from non-resident shareholders must be executed via a SWIFT MT103 international wire or SEPA credit transfer (for EU origin), with the payment reference explicitly stating 'Kapitalerhöhung', 'Gesellschaftereinlage', or 'Additional Paid-in Capital' to facilitate bank-side regulatory classification. The receiving German bank will apply KYC and source-of-funds checks on the remitting entity/beneficial owner. All inbound cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 are captured in Deutsche Bundesbank balance-of-payments statistics by the receiving bank, without prior authorisation. To avoid rejection, the remitter must include a clear purpose code (Zweckcode), typically 'P200' for regular capital contribution or an equivalent commercial transfer code recommended by the German banking association. For late/released capital forming statutory share capital, the account type must be evidenced by the notary's deposit confirmation (where required under GmbH law). No German FX permits are required for equity or debt financing injections.Outbound trade disbursements from the German GmbH to foreign suppliers and service providers are settled primarily through: (a) direct SWIFT MT103/MT202 tranfers from the operational account, with supporting commercial invoice and contract evidence retained for compliance; (b) EU in-country or cross-border SEPA CT in EUR for high-volume low-value supplier payments; and (c) irrevocable documentary letters of credit (Commercial L/Cs) issued by the German bank, particularly for high-value DC fast-charging station components or raw-materials imported from Asia; these L/Cs are transmitted via SWIFT MT700/MT710 / MT707 series and may be guaranteed/confirmed by the exporter's advising bank. Payment outflows greater than EUR 12,500 are reported by the German bank to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Conditionally viable for ancillary or operational disbursements (e.g., supplier payments below high-value thresholds, customer refunds, remote expenses) via BaFin-regulated E-Money Institutions (EMIs) under PSD2; however, viability rating is 'Low' for core treasury, L/C issuing, escrow/capital verification and high-value cross-border settlements. Legal constraints: EMIs are not 'Kreditinstitute' under the German Banking Act (KWG) and therefore cannot accept deposits, issue commercial documentary credits, or provide treasury lending; their licenses restrict them to payment/e-money services. German tier-1 banks will not accept an EMI as confirmation or title-holder bank for documentary collections, and an EMI is not a recognised proof point for GER. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at municipal rates typically 14-17%, resulting in a combined effective rate of approximately 30-33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the WHT, effective 26.375%)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if cross-border transactions with related parties exceed €5 million (goods) or €500,000 (services) per year; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds €50 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a specific ESR regime, but general anti-abuse rules (Section 42 AO) and substance requirements for treaty benefits apply; the entity must demonstrate real economic activity (employees, premises, decision-making) to avoid denial of treaty benefits and to satisfy transfer pricing documentation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine; if committed on a large scale, imprisonment up to 10 years; additional penalty of up to 15% of evaded tax (Zuschlag).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they fail to file accurate returns; criminal liability extends to individuals; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Shell Company (Briefkastenfirma)

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and retroactive taxation; fines up to €50,000; potential criminal charges for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly operate a shell; civil restitution and potential imprisonment for fraud.
AML Violations (Geldwäschegesetz)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for companies; criminal penalties for responsible individuals; revocation of business license.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil liability for damages; travel bans and asset seizure.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 5-10% of the adjustment amount (Zuschlag), and interest on underpaid tax; documentation penalties up to €1 million.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; civil restitution and potential criminal charges for tax evasion.

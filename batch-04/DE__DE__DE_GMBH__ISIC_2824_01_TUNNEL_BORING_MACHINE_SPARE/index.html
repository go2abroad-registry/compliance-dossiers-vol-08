E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Commercial registration: the GmbH must first be entered in the German Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the competent local court (Amtsgericht); the managing director (Geschäftsführer) must be appointed and disclosed. Step 2 — Tax registration questionnaire: submit the statutory 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (questionnaire for tax registration) to the competent Finanzamt, either via ELSTER electronically or in paper form, within one month of commencing business; the questionnaire discloses shareholders (including non-resident ownership), registered seat, intended business activity (ISIC 2824 heavy construction/mining machinery), taxable year, and projected turnover. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election; a GmbH is a per se body corporate under Sec. 1(1) No. 1 KStG, subject to entity-level corporate income tax (15%) plus solidarity surcharge (5.5% of CIT) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), with distributions generally subject to 26.375% withholding tax (reduced to 0% under the parent-subsidiary directive where the German parent holds ≥ 10% and the Sec. 8b KStG 95% dividend exemption applies). Available statutory elections include: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under Secs. 14, 17 KStG and Sec. 2 GewStG — a controlling German parent with majority voting rights and a binding profit-and-loss pooling agreement may consolidate the GmbH's corporate income tax and trade tax results for group tax mapping; (ii) Sec. 1a KStG transparent-entity option permitting partnerships to elect corporate taxation — relevant only as a structural comparator when choosing between GmbH and GmbH & Co. KG; (iii) hybrid-mismatch documentation and allocation elections under the German ATAD implementing law (Part IV CITA, including Sec. 56c KStG Annex V statements) for cross-border financing arrangements involving hybrid or reverse-hybrid vehicles; (iv) VAT option to tax real property under Sec. 9 UStG; and (v) controlled foreign corporation (Hinzurechnungsbetrag) computations and anti-hybrid adjustment elections under the Foreign Transactions Tax Act (AStG) as reformed for ATAD.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account purposes. Germany has no general exchange-control approval, mandatory foreign-exchange licensing, capital-repatriation restriction, or profit-remittance withholding mechanism based solely on the cross-border transfer of legitimately earned funds. A GmbH may generally receive foreign investment, hold foreign-currency accounts, purchase foreign currency, repay shareholder loans, pay dividends, and remit interest, royalties, service fees, and trade proceeds, subject to corporate-law distributable-profit rules, transfer-pricing requirements, withholding-tax rules, treaty relief, beneficial-owner verification, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, and applicable German and EU reporting obligations. Dividend distributions require an effective shareholder resolution, adequate distributable balance-sheet profit, and compliance with the GmbH capital-maintenance prohibition under the German Limited Liability Companies Act. German dividend withholding tax is generally 25 percent plus solidarity surcharge, subject to domestic exemptions and applicable treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief. Interest, royalties, and service payments require separate withholding-tax, arm's-length, VAT, and beneficial-ownership analysis. Banks may delay or reject transactions where source of funds, ownership, economic purpose, sanctions exposure, tax residence, or transaction documentation is insufficient.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros or repatriated to Germany. Export proceeds may generally be retained in euro or foreign-currency accounts in Germany or abroad, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, customs, and anti-money-laundering requirements. Cross-border payment and external-position reports may be required to Deutsche Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance. The reporting threshold and exemptions depend on the report type, transaction category, resident status, and current Bundesbank rules; payment-reporting exemptions commonly apply to individual cross-border payments up to EUR 50,000, but this threshold must be confirmed for the relevant reporting form and transaction. Statistical reports are not exchange-control approvals. The GmbH should maintain invoice, customs, contract, bank, and accounting records supporting export proceeds and reconcile open receivables, foreign-currency balances, and intercompany settlements.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin) supervises regulated German banks, payment institutions, electronic-money institutions, and investment-services firms. Germany applies European Union financial-sanctions, anti-money-laundering, payment-services, and prudential rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine euro collections and payments; Low for statutory capital formation, complex project finance, large-value trade finance, or high-volume foreign-currency treasury. An EEA-authorized electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide services in Germany through passporting, a German branch, or permitted cross-border services, subject to its authorization scope and safeguarding model. A non-EEA offshore digital EMI generally cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services to German customers without an applicable EU authorization, passport, branch authorization, or other legally valid basis. EMI safeguarding is not equivalent to a bank deposit guarantee, and many EMIs do not offer German GmbH capital escrow accounts, commercial letters of credit, lender-controlled accounts, overdrafts, documentary collections, or robust multi-bank treasury connectivity. The GmbH should verify licensing in the European Banking Authority or relevant national register, safeguarding arrangements, client-money segregation, settlement cutoffs, IBAN country, transaction limits, sanctions policy, data-processing terms, insolvency protections, and account-closure procedures.

A non-EEA offshore digital EMI generally cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services to German customers without an applicable EU authorization, passport, branch authorization, or other legally valid basis. EMI safeguarding is not equivalent to a bank deposit guarantee, and many EMIs do not offer German GmbH capital escrow accounts, commercial letters of credit, lender-controlled accounts, overdrafts, documentary collections, or robust multi-bank treasury connectivity. The GmbH should verify licensing in the European Banking Authority or relevant national register, safeguarding arrangements, client-money segregation, settlement cutoffs, IBAN country, transaction limits, sanctions policy, data-processing terms, insolvency protections, and account-closure procedures.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); aggregate effective burden approximately 30% to 33% depending on municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, resulting in an aggregate German withholding tax of 26.375% on dividends paid by a GmbH; under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, a qualifying corporate parent in an EU/EEA state can usually receive dividends without German withholding tax; the statutory rate remains 26.375% absent treaty relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German TP documentation is not limited to a single fixed turnover threshold; a Local File must be maintained for cross-border controlled transactions where aggregate annual relevant transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000, while a Master File is required when the multinational group records consolidated revenue above EUR 100,000,000; intangibles and financing transactions are scrutinized at significantly lower de minimis volumes, and documentation must be contemporaneous.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a separate Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the Federal Fiscal Court and tax administration enforce substance requirements under general anti-abuse principles, Sec. 42 AO, the ATAD CFC and GAAR rules, and the German Treaty Shopping doctrine. A GmbH manufacturing TBM spare parts must demonstrate actual operational irrefutable presence through employees, production facilities, inventories, procurement decisions, risk management, and strategic control; a German entity used merely as a conduit for invoice routing, IP parking, or dividend funneling without real business activity can be disregarded for tax purposes, with taxable income reallocated to the non-resident principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Intentional Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanction under Sec. 370 AO: imprisonment of up to 5 years or a criminal fine; in aggravated cases (e.g., large-scale evasion above EUR 50,000) imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years; civil back-taxes include 6% annual interest and all evasion penalties; the German tax authority may also demand enhanced penalties and seize bank accounts, machinery, and receivables.

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer is personally liable under Sec. 69 AO for any tax liability caused by willful or grossly negligent breach of his duties; personal assets may be seized, an international travel ban may be imposed, and the director may be disqualified from managing any German company and imprisoned.
Failure to File, Late Filing, and Non-Payment

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty under Sec. 152 AO of EUR 25 per month up to EUR 25,000; late payment surcharge of 1% per month on unpaid amounts; tax assessment by estimate; compulsory enforcement through bailiffs and freezing orders; additional Orderly-Default penalties where tax returns remain outstanding.

Director personal liability: A director who fails to apply for tax registration, file returns, or pay due payroll and income taxes is personally liable for the shortfall; the company's unpaid taxes can be recovered directly from the director's personal estate, with consequential commercial travel bans and revocation of residency permits in serious cases.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment under Sec. 1 AStG and Sec. 8(3) KStG; upward income correction, recharacterization of excessive payments as constructively distributed dividends, levy of 26.375% withholding tax, and interest of 6% per annum on all additional tax created; TP documentation failure incurs penalty charges and evidentiary burden reversal against the GmbH.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany agreements knowing that prices are not arm's length become jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax, withholding tax, interest, and penalties; personal liability extends to the full evaded amount, and intentional transfer pricing fraud is treated as tax fraud leading to imprisonment and travel bans.
Shell Company Abuse and Economic Substance Deficit

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the corporate structure under Sec. 42 AO, reallocation of taxable profits to the economic owner, denial of treaty benefits, forfeiture of participation exemptions, added disguised dividend withholding tax, and administrative penalties for inadequate substance documentation; if the structure is an Aggressive Tax Planning arrangement, additional disclosure and penalty regimes apply.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell GmbH are personally liable for tax debts if they knowingly allowed the entity to operate without substance; courts may impose civil restitution of all tax avoided, disqualification orders, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for aiding tax evasion.
Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG) of up to EUR 5 million for severe non-compliance, or up to 10% of global annual turnover; imprisonment for laundering assets; separate sanctions may be imposed by the Financial Supervisory Authority on the director and the company.

Director personal liability: A director who billows incomplete UBO filings or knowingly processes tainted financing can be prosecuted for money laundering and fraud, face direct seizure of personal assets, international asset freezing orders, and travel bans; professional disqualification follows a criminal conviction.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; Portal: Ariadni Tax Portal (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

For a Cyprus private limited company, the Tax Identification Number (TIN) is issued automatically upon incorporation registration with the Registrar of Companies and notification to the Department of Taxation. A foreign entity operating in or from Cyprus must apply for a TIN/VAT registration via the Ariadni Tax Portal, submitting constitutional documents, proof of physical establishment or permanent address in Cyprus, details of the appointed tax representative, and form VAT 101 where the taxable turnover threshold is expected to be exceeded. Subsequent registrations for income tax, social insurance, and withholding tax are initiated through the same portal or in person at the local District Tax Office.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Cypriot law. A Cyprus company is automatically treated as a separate taxable entity at a 12.5% corporate income tax rate. Statutory elections that are available include tonnage tax for qualifying ship-owning/ship-management activities, intellectual property box regime (limited), group loss relief, and notional interest deduction on new equity. Hybrid mismatch rules under the ATAD framework restrict elective outcomes and require mandatory neutralisation of mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for established persons; EUR 10,000 for intra-EU distance sales and digital services under the Union One Stop Shop regime; mandatory registration for intra-Community acquisitions once the EUR 10,000 threshold is exceeded (unless an option to tax applies)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to direct exports of goods to third countries where the goods physically leave the European Union within 90 days of supply, supported by customs export evidence. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-EU recipient or when directly connected with the operation of seagoing vessels used in international traffic, including ship crew recruitment/management services supplied to vessel owners or operators for international voyages. For crew agency services, the supplier must demonstrate that the crew are delivered to a vessel engaged in international navigation and that the recipient is using the service for that purpose.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / EU customs electronic system export confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading / Through-Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill in the case of air cargo)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from an authorised bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency payment
  • Signed crew service agreement with the vessel owner/operator confirming the international voyage
  • Crew manifest or delivery note confirming placement on the vessel

No mandatory real-time B2B e-invoicing or digital stamping regime is currently operative in Cyprus. Taxable persons must issue VAT-compliant invoices, retain electronic records, and submit periodic VIES and Intrastat declarations. The EU ViDA initiative is expected to introduce a mandatory electronic invoice clearance framework around 2030, but no national API clearance or pre-clearance requirement exists today.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims trigger retroactive clawback of the VAT that should have been charged at the standard 19% rate, together with statutory interest (currently 5% per annum), administrative fines up to 100% of the tax, and additional penalties for fraudulent evasion of up to €20,000 per offence or double the tax amount. Persistent non-compliance or deliberate document forgery may lead to criminal prosecution, imprisonment, and cancellation of VAT registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current- and capital-account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU. Since EU accession in 2004 and euro adoption in 2008, Cyprus maintains no exchange-control regime; the emergency capital controls introduced during the 2013 banking crisis were fully repealed in 2015. Profit and dividend remittance is unrestricted and requires no prior approval; applicable checks are limited to AML/KYC due diligence, EU sanctions screening, and CRS/FATCA tax reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus imposes no surrender, mandatory conversion, repatriation period, or holding requirement on export proceeds, including crew-management and seafarer-staffing service revenues. The only statutory obligations are: (i) balance-of-payments statistical reporting of transactions with non-residents to the Central Bank of Cyprus under monthly/quarterly thresholds; (ii) EU sanctions screening for outgoing and incoming payments; mandated conversion ratio: 0%.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou). No dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration exists; exchange-control competences are exercised through euro-area membership and EU law, with the CBC responsible for balance-of-payments statistics, payment-system oversight, and as competent authority for EU asset-freeze regulations; the National Sanctions Officer coordinates broader EU sanctions implementation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Cyprus-licensed Electronic Money Institutions and EU passporting EMIs may lawfully provide multi-currency payroll and payment accounts under the Electronic Money Law N.81(I)/2012 (EMD2) and PSD2. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot extend credit or pay interest on safeguarded funds; they are subject to the Cyprus AML/CFT Law and CBC oversight; most EMIs lack direct correspondent banking for non-EEA crew jurisdictions, so SWIFT settlements to the Philippines, India, Ukraine, or other seafarer-source countries may be slower, more expensive, or rejected. Recommendation: use EMIs for euro-denominated EEA payroll only, and route major trade disbursements and FX hedging through a Tier-1 domestic bank.

Shareholder equity is routed via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA SCT for EUR) from the parent/investor bank, quoting payment purpose 'Capital subscription - share capital and share premium' with CBC balance-of-payments direct-investment code E1/E2; for related-party debt finance, use loan code S1 and attach the executed loan agreement. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €750,000 for goods, €250,000 for services, €100,000 for other transactions (aggregate per category).

Economic substance scope: Cyprus Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to entities engaged in relevant activities, including shipping and crew management. Must demonstrate adequate physical presence, qualified employees, and core income-generating activities in Cyprus.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 100% of tax underpaid, plus interest at 1.75% per month. Criminal prosecution may lead to fines up to €50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion. Civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits, imposition of 12.5% tax on deemed profits, and penalties up to €20,000. Possible strike-off from the register.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for misrepresentation, leading to disqualification and personal fines.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €500,000 for the company, and suspension of operating license. Criminal charges for money laundering.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance – official e-filing portal: https://taxportal.taxdep.gov.cy

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus private limited company is incorporated with the Registrar of Companies; the Department of Taxation automatically issues the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) using the company registration number. No separate application is needed for income tax. For VAT, the entity must file Form TD1010 (VAT registration) with the VAT Branch, and, upon approval, is issued a Cyprus VAT number in the format CY + 9 digits. A non-resident entity without a Cyprus establishment engaging in taxable activity must appoint a tax representative and register for VAT before supplies are made.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Cyprus tax-resident companies pay 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide profits; the entity is treated as a separate taxpayer and not as a pass-through vehicle). — A Cyprus private limited company is incorporated with the Registrar of Companies; the Department of Taxation automatically issues the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) using the company registration number. No separate application is needed for income tax. For VAT, the entity must file Form TD1010 (VAT registration) with the VAT Branch, and, upon approval, is issued a Cyprus VAT number in the format CY + 9 digits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Cyprus tax-resident companies pay 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide profits; the entity is treated as a separate taxpayer and not as a pass-through vehicle).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available in Cyprus. Companies may elect tax residence by central management and control; group relief elections may be made under sections 8-13 of the Income Tax Law. Hybrid mismatch rules (EU ATAD II) are mandatory, not elective. Tonnage tax is limited to shipping and is not available for aircraft leasing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 for taxable supplies of goods and services; EUR 10,000 threshold for intra-Community distance sales and electronically supplied services (VAT OSS).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for the dry leasing of aircraft engines when the engine is supplied to a Cypriot/EU airline (or non-EU airline) operating for reward chiefly on international routes, under Article 148 of EU VAT Directive and Cyprus VAT Law 2000 s.83(3). The lessee must hold a valid Air Operator Certificate (AOC) and the engine must be used in connection with international flights. Evidence must show the engine is delivered in Cyprus for use outside the Community or is a qualifying leasing arrangement for international navigation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Executed aircraft engine dry lease agreement
  • Lessee's Air Operator Certificate (AOC) and international route schedule
  • VAT invoice bearing the zero-rated reason code

No real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction controls apply to B2B transactions. E-invoicing is mandatory for Cyprus public sector procurement under the EU Peppol standard (EN 16931) and the Cyprus National e-Invoicing Forum. Private-sector e-invoicing is voluntary. VAT returns (Form HRF430) and VIES listings are filed electronically through the Tax Portal; no digital stamping system exists.

The Cyprus Tax Department may conduct audit verification of zero-rating qualification. If the required documentation, international-route usage, or intra-Community payment conditions are not satisfied, the zero-rating is overturned and VAT is assessed at 19% from the date of supply, together with statutory interest (currently based on 2.25% plus Central Bank of Cyprus base rate) and administrative fines; deliberate fraud may attract penalties up to 100% of the assessed tax and criminal prosecution under the Cyprus VAT Law 2000.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies. Cyprus has been a Eurozone member since 1 January 2008; the euro is the sole legal tender. Both the current account (trade in services, lease rentals, royalties) and the capital account (foreign direct investment, share subscriptions, loan funding, dividend repatriation) are fully liberalised, with no exchange-control licensing, no surrender quotas, and no repatriation restrictions. Profit remittance from a Cyprus private limited company is fiscally frictionless: no withholding tax applies on dividend, interest, or royalty payments to non-residents, and no prior Central Bank approval is required. The practical hurdles are regulatory compliance rather than convertibility: complete source-of-funds documentation, EU and United Nations sanctions screening, EU Regulation 2015/847 payment-information requirements, beneficial-ownership transparency filings under the Companies Law Cap.113 and the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law L.188(I)/2007, and Central Bank of Cyprus balance-of-payments statistical reporting by the servicing bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or conversion regime exists in Cyprus. Exporters and service providers, including the Cyprus aircraft-engine dry-leasing lessor, are not obliged to repatriate export receivables within a prescribed period, are not required to convert foreign-currency receipts into euros, and face no mandatory conversion ratio. Collections under lease contracts may be retained and managed in designated multi-currency accounts (notably USD) as treasury policy dictates. The only applicable obligations are informational: credit institutions must report external transaction data to the Central Bank of Cyprus for balance-of-payments and external-asset statistics, and taxpayers must declare worldwide income for the 12.5% corporate income tax, with lease-rental positions supported by commercial invoices.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), operating within the European System of Central Banks and the Single Supervisory Mechanism; foreign exchange policy and capital movement restrictions are governed by Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, EU directives and regulations, and the Central Bank of Cyprus' regulatory framework for credit institutions. Cyprus maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low as a primary treasury rail, acceptable only for ancillary payments. Cyprus-licensed or EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (Electronic Money Law L.81(I)/2012, PSD2) may open virtual IBANs in EUR/USD, execute SEPA and SWIFT payments, and offer cheaper transaction initiation for recurring operational expenses. However, they are not licensed deposit-taking institutions, generally cap transaction values (commonly EUR 50,000–100,000 per payment), internally limit high-risk aviation/asset-leasing verticals, do not issue commercial letters of credit or structured escrow releases, and remain subject to additional manual transaction reviews. For the ISIC 5223 aircraft-engine dry-leasing model, the compliance split is therefore: tier-1 bank for escrow, LC, and seven-figure lease-rental flows; EMIs as fallback only for payroll and administrative disbursements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate related-party transactions for the mandatory Cyprus Local File; Master File is required when the worldwide group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) akin to Cyprus's local statute? Correct phrasing: Cyprus does not maintain a statutory instrument specifically titled 'Economic Substance Regulations' (ESR). However, the Cyprus tax authorities enforce substantive economic presence through: (1) exchange-of-information and anti-treaty-abuse tests; (2) the need to show real management, local business premises, employees, and decision-making functions to sustain Cyprus tax residency and to access the 12.5% rate, DTA network, NID, and IP Box; (3) transfer-pricing documentation requiring functional, asset and risk analysis. A Cyprus Limited company engaged in aircraft engine dry-leasing must maintain a commercial office, qualified personnel, and a board that genuinely determines lease negotiations, financing, risk management, and capital allocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002 as amended) and the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, the sanction is a criminal fine not exceeding €10,000 and/or imprisonment not exceeding 5 years, in addition to the full amount of tax evaded, interest at 1.75% per month, and a surcharge of 5% on unpaid tax. The Tax Commissioner may also impose administrative penalties equal to 100% of the tax under-assessed. Seizure and public auction of assets can be ordered to enforce recovery.

Director personal liability: Directors may be indicted personally, subjected to travel bans and exit controls, and disqualified from serving as directors. Where fraudulent accounting caused the shortfall, the court may order civil restitution against directors, pierce the corporate veil, and hold them jointly and severally liable for the tax, penalties, and costs.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department can refuse to treat the company as a Cyprus tax resident, recharacterise the company as resident outside Cyprus, apply the place-of-effective-management test against the company, strip treaty benefits, and reassess income at a higher effective tax rate. Recharacterisation invalidates NID, IP Box relief, and group loss relief; penalties up to €10,000 and interest may be imposed. In egregious cases, the company may be struck off the register as a dissolved shell.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow the company to function as a sham can be made shadow directors. They may face personal liability for the company's tax debts under veil-piercing, disqualification orders, and civil actions by creditors. For cross-border schemes, travel bans and extradition proceedings may be initiated if fraud is shown.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (Law 188(I)/2007, as amended), regulated entities and officers face administrative fines up to €100,000, revocation of operational licenses, and criminal sanctions up to 5 years imprisonment. For serious offences, the courts can order asset forfeiture, including the aircraft engines or lease receivables, and impose cumulative fines; the Cyprus Unit for Combating Money Laundering (MOKAS) may freeze accounts instantly.

Director personal liability: Directors of aircraft leasing companies that process tainted lease payments may be prosecuted personally, receive custodial sentences, and be banned from managing companies for up to 10 years. Civil restitution may run against the director's personal estate, and the courts can issue international travel bans pending criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Income Tax Law TP framework, the Tax Department can make a transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's-length profit, resulting in additional tax at 12.5% plus interest at 1.75% per month. A fixed penalty of €500 applies for failure to submit the Local File, and a daily default fine may be imposed for continued non-compliance; in serious cases, the penalty can increase to €10,000 with criminal exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify inaccurate or non-existent TP documentation can be found jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax adjustment. They may face director disqualification, personal fine enforcement, and, where deliberate falsification is proven, criminal conviction. Travel bans and seizure of personal assets are available remedies to enforce payment.

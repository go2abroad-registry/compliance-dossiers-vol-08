Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Inland Revenue under the Ministry of Finance); official portal Taxisnet (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy); customs and excise supervision by the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department (https://www.mof.gov.cy/customs).

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus Ltd obtains its Tax Identification Number (TIN) automatically upon incorporation via the Registrar of Companies (Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver). For VAT purposes, the company files a VAT registration application with the Tax Department (Form TD 200 / e-VAT through Taxisnet); a non-established foreign entity must register for Cyprus VAT before supplying goods or services in Cyprus, without a turnover threshold, or via the EU non-established taxable person procedure when claiming cross-border refunds. Customs registration for bunker fuel imports and exports, and authorized warehousekeeper status where excise-suspended fuel is handled, is secured separately with the Customs and Excise Department through the THESEAS electronic customs system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Cyprus private limited company (HE77) is a separate tax resident taxpayer taxed at a 12.5% corporate income tax rate on worldwide income (Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002); no pass-through classification exists for a Ltd; territorial relief is granted through foreign tax credits and statutory exemptions for foreign dividends and interest; shipping entities engaged in qualifying international maritime transport may alternatively be taxed under the Cyprus Tonnage Tax Law 46(I)/2010 at a deemed tonnage-based charge.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not apply a US-style check-the-box regime; elections are statutory: (1) Tonnage Tax election for qualifying shipowners, charterers, and ship or crew managers, filed within six months of commencement or acquisition of eligible activity; bunkering and refuelling supply to vessels is an ancillary maritime activity that, if integral to the qualifying shipping business of the electing entity, may fall under the deemed profit computation; (2) group loss relief election between Cyprus-resident group companies at 75% ownership; (3) Notional Interest Deduction election on new equity capital; (4) foreign tax credit election on an item-by-item basis; (5) hybrid and reverse hybrid mismatch elections under ATAD II rules, governed by the Income Tax Law, require transparent taxation status to be self-declared to the Tax Department with supporting legal evidence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for a taxable person established in Cyprus; non-established suppliers must register for Cyprus VAT regardless of turnover; the intra-Community distance sales threshold is EUR 10,000 in annual cross-border supplies across all EU Member States; voluntary registration is permitted where a business intends to make taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment under Article 92(1) and (2) of the Cyprus VAT Law and Article 146 of the EU VAT Directive: (a) export supplies of goods to destinations outside the EU evidenced by an electronic customs export declaration; and (b) supplies of marine fuel, lubricants, and other stores to vessels engaged in international commercial navigation, including bunkering of commercially registered ships, subject to proof of IMO registration, flag state, and sailing itinerary to an international destination. Fuel delivered to private pleasure craft or vessels engaged only in domestic coastal navigation is subject to 19% VAT. Zero-rating also requires the supplier to obtain and preserve contractual evidence that the bunker supply benefits the vessel operator and that the goods are permanently consumed outside Cyprus and EU tax territory.

  • Customs export declaration (Electronic Single Administrative Document, EX-A/EC) filed through Cyprus Customs THESEAS/e-Customs with customs departure confirmation
  • Bunker Delivery Note (BDN) and quantity/quality certificate signed by the supplier and vessel master
  • Bill of Lading or seaway bill evidencing carriage of cargo and international voyage routing
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT payment confirmation from a Cyprus-licensed bank evidencing settlement in EUR
  • Certificate of Registry, IMO number, and classification society documents proving commercial navigation status
  • Charter-party or bunker supply contract with the ship operator, including port itinerary and destination
  • Excise Movement and Control System (EMCS) electronic administrative document when duty-suspended fuel is loaded
  • VIES intra-EU sales list and Intrastat delivery declarations where fuel is moved to EU Member State ports or customers

Cyprus does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping for B2B e-invoicing. Electronic invoices in PDF, UBL, or XML are legally accepted where authenticity, integrity, and legibility are preserved through business controls. The EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) framework, formally agreed in 2025, will impose structured e-invoicing and digital reporting for intra-Community B2B supplies on and after 1 July 2030, with Cyprus expected to transpose the obligations via the Taxisnet platform. Until then, bunker suppliers must submit VIES recapitulative statements for intra-EU supplies and Intrastat declarations for intra-EU movements of fuel, and retain full audit-trail evidence of the supply chain.

The Cyprus Tax Department and Customs can reassess VAT within six years of the return, extending to ten years where fraud or intentional evasion is established. If zero-rating is not substantiated by the documentary bundle, the supply is re-characterized as taxable at 19% and the VAT is clawed back with statutory interest from the due date. Administrative penalties apply under the VAT Law for late or incorrect returns and deliberate suppression of tax; criminal prosecution for export fraud or false declarations may result in fines and imprisonment. The Central Bank of Cyprus exchange-control framework also requires export proceeds to be credited to an account at an authorized bank; although no codified repatriation deadline exists in Cyprus law, tax auditors apply a six-month (180-day) evidentiary benchmark for matching export income to the underlying zero-rated supply. Failure to demonstrate remittance prompts denial of the zero rate, retrospective assessment, and penalty exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Cyprus. As a Eurozone member and EU jurisdiction, Cyprus applies the EU capital movement liberalisation requirements under Article 63 TFEU. Both current account transactions, including trade in marine fuel and lubricants, and capital account transactions, including inbound equity capital injections, profit repatriation, dividends, and intra-group loans, are freely permitted. There are no direct exchange-control approval hurdles for bona fide commercial operations. In practice, profit remittance and equity repatriation are subject only to ordinary corporate tax compliance, beneficial-ownership verification, sanctions screening, and balance-of-payments statistical reporting through the Central Bank of Cyprus. Banks conducting the remittance may require supporting board resolutions, audited financial statements or tax certificates; however, no Central Bank pre-approval is required for standard fuel-supply treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exist in Cyprus. The Central Bank of Cyprus does not require marine bunkering companies to convert foreign-currency export receipts into euro, nor does it impose a deadline for repatriation. Export proceeds may be credited to Cypriot or other EEA payment accounts in any currency and retained or repurposed as part of working capital. For balance-of-payments compilation, the receiving authorised bank must report export receipts under the CBC International Transactions Reporting System; therefore the company must retain commercial invoices, bills of lading, Bunker Delivery Receipts (BDRs), and banking correspondence for at least ten years.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) acting under the Constitution of Cyprus, the Central Bank of Cyprus Law, and the European System of Central Banks framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for core treasury clearing and High only for ancillary disbursements. EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions passported into Cyprus under PSD2 may legally provide payment accounts, inbound/outbound euro transfers, and payment cards for operational expenses. They are not a substitute for a Tier 1 banking relationship where the treasury must support commercial letters of credit, standby letters of credit, or syndicated pre-export finance. Offshore digital EMIs without an EEA passport or without a direct settlement account at a clearing bank should be rejected for primary liquidity. The main legal constraint is that an EMI cannot provide credit or issue documentary letters of credit, and a Cypriot bunkering company must maintain at least one deposit-taking bank account to support fuel-contract performance guarantees and port-authority security instruments.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership opacity through offshore intermediate holding companies within the shipping group

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity through offshore intermediate holding companies within the shipping group

Defense evidence: Complete corporate registry extracts, notarised and apostilled certificates of incorporation for every intermediary entity, full UBO schedule with passport copies, a signed group-structure chart, wrap-around ownership declarations, and sworn statements explaining the commercial reason for each offshore layer. The dossier must be supported by audited or accountant-certified financial statements of the ultimate parent and a clear explanation of vessel ownership, demise charterers, and physical fuel procurement operations.
High-risk source of funds for initial share capital contribution and vessel-fuel inventory pre-purchase

Root cause: High-risk source of funds for initial share capital contribution and vessel-fuel inventory pre-purchase

Defense evidence: Bank statements from the sending bank covering at least 12 months, board resolutions approving the capital injection, share allotment forms, documented source of wealth and source of funds statements, the underlying purchase contract for the bunker vessel or fuel inventory, proforma invoices, and tax residence certificates of the transferring shareholders. The company must also provide an explanation for any unusually large deposit or early-repurchase arrangement that may appear as a back-to-back loan.

Invoice irregularities or cash-intensive fuel purchases inconsistent with normal, verifiable cross-border trade rails

Defense evidence: Sequentially numbered commercial invoices matching fuel quantity, density, temperature, and port records; bill of lading or port cargo manifest; customs or single-administrative-document declarations; SWIFT MT103 confirmation for actual settlement rather than cash; bank-verified proof of payee and beneficial ownership of the receiving account; and reconciliation of Bank Delivery Receipts to the corresponding bulk-fuel storage or barge supplier.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The preferred compliant route is a SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer denominated in EUR from the already-verified shareholder or ultimate parent bank account directly to the Cypriot company's Tier 1 operational account. For balance-of-payments classification, the payment narrative and bank declaration should identify the transfer as an equity contribution or shareholder loan. The CBC statistical form should capture the transaction as inward foreign direct investment when the shareholder holds at least 10 percent of the shares or voting power. The bank will typically request a board resolution, amended shareholding registry extract, and a certified copy of the transaction's commercial purpose. The supporting verification package must include the signed resolution in English or Greek, the UBO identification, and a tax-compliant statement confirming that the capital injection is recorded in the statutory financial accounts. If the injection is routed from a non-EEA jurisdiction, the sending bank and beneficiary bank must both demonstrate that no EU sanctions transfer restrictions apply and the payment should use purpose code EQUI or the equivalent CBC direct-investment code provided by the receiving bank.Outbound treasury disbursements for ship bunkering and lubricant replenishment should be settled through the Cypriot entity's SWIFT-enabled bank account using MT103 wire transfers for direct supplier payments, SEPA Credit Transfers for euro-zone counterparties, and commercial letters of credit or standby letters of credit for large, document-backed fuel purchases and port-performance obligations. For documentary collections, the bank will require the Bunker Delivery Receipt, fuel quality certificate, shipping order, and conformity certificate before releasing payment. For recurring intra-group or shipowner disbursements, the treasury should maintain dedicated purpose codes for each transfer, align invoices to payment narratives, and ensure all counterparties are pre-screened against EU sanctions, OFAC, and UN lists immediately before every execution. The bank must be given at least one business day notice for high-value payments exceeding the bank's internally defined Large Exposure limit, with supporting proof of trade documentation held in the company's trade-file repository.Outright FX forward contracts for EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/bunker-fuel pricing currencies using genuine commercial exposure documentation.FX swaps combining spot and forward transactions to manage cross-currency liquidity between the operational account and supplier accounts.Cross-currency swaps for long-term EUR/USD and EUR/Russian-Ruble exposure where legally permissible and commercially justified.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market fuel-supply currencies that are not freely convertible or have inconvertible settlement restrictions, provided the transaction is executed through a licensed credit institution or authorised investment firm and documented with a real underlying fuel trade exposure.Vanilla FX options for hedging foreign-currency fuel procurement price risk, including EUR call / foreign-currency put options, subject to the bank's credit-approval process and the company's written treasury risk-management policy.Commodity hedging instruments such as fixed-price fuel swaps or Singapore MOPS-linked swap derivatives, permitted under EU EMIR and MiFID when used solely for hedging the bunkering company's commercial fuel-price and inventory risks.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The IR501 must be filed within 12 months from the end of the tax year; for a calendar year-end of 31 December, the statutory deadline is 31 December of the following year. Provisional tax instalments are due on 31 July, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March of the relevant instalment cycle.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 (aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with related parties triggers mandatory Local File preparation; Master File is required when the multinational group reaches the OECD consolidated revenue threshold; Cypriot branches and shipping companies are required to maintain benchmark studies for bunker procurement, vessel chartering and commission payments).

Economic substance scope: Cyprus enforces economic substance as a condition of tax residence and treaty access through Tax Department Circular 35/2020: a Cyprus company must hold board meetings in Cyprus, maintain minutes of strategic decisions taken in Cyprus, employ qualified resident staff, operate Cypriot bank accounts, and have administrative premises proportionate to its bunkering activity. The Cyprus IP Box regime applies its own substance tracing on research and development expenditure. Although Cyprus has no named Economic Substance Regulations analogous to the Gulf jurisdictions, tax residence, beneficial ownership and DTA entitlement are withdrawn where the entity is managed, controlled or effectively exploited from outside Cyprus; mischaracterisation triggers reclassification as non-resident with loss of the 12.5% regime.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent IR501 Declaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative reassessment at 100% uplift of under-declared tax plus default interest at 3.5% per annum; fixed penalties up to EUR 500; criminal fine of up to EUR 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; the Tax Commissioner may also reject subsequent provisional tax instalment schedules.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign knowingly false IR501 returns are personally liable for restitution of the evaded tax and penalties; court-imposed director disqualification for up to 10 years; travel bans and asset freezing orders in support of criminal recovery; where customs fuel marker fraud is joined, customs penalties apply without discharge of the tax debt.
Shell Company and Artificial TiO Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of Cyprus tax residence; re-sourcing of profits to the place of effective management; tax at 12.5% on all undeclared profit attributable to Cyprus operations; exclusion from all DTA benefits; reclassification of exempt dividends as assessable distributions; administrative penalty of up to 100% of the repositioned tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are treated as shadow directors of the foreign administration; the veil is pierced for unpaid corporate tax and social contributions; travel bans are imposed pending final assessment; personal bankruptcy or disqualification orders are sought for deliberate abstraction of corporate assets; repeated infringements attract extradition proceedings under mutual assistance in tax and AML matters.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing of Bunker Procurement and Charter Hire

Statutory sanction: Cyprus Tax Department transfer pricing adjustment under the arm's length principle; profit reallocation with a 12.5% corporate charge on the repriced profit; secondary adjustment of dividend character; default interest of 3.5% per annum; penalty up to 50% of the additional tax when the Local File was not prepared despite crossing the EUR 1,000,000 threshold.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable when they approved the related-party contracts with knowledge of the artificial margin; personal liability extends to the difference between the repriced profit and the declared profit; travel restrictions and restriction orders over bunker vessels may be granted during litigation; deliberate falsification of benchmark studies supports criminal charges.
AML Violation and Trade-Based Money Laundering through Bunkering Invoices

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law, administrative fines of up to EUR 500,000 or 5% of the legal entity's annual turnover; freezing orders over bank accounts and barges; revocation of the bunkering licence granted under the Cyprus Ports Authority; referral to MOKAS for criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors bear personal criminal liability where the company does not appoint a compliance officer, file the AML risk assessment or report suspicious transactions; imprisonment up to 12 years for laundering proceeds; personal trading prohibitions in the marine fuels sector; denial of travel documents for flight risk; the Court may order direct and indirect directorial disgorgement of profits plus punitive damages.

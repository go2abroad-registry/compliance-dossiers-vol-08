Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus; online portal: ARIADNI (TaxisNet integrated system) at https://ariadni.mof.gov.cy

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus private limited company receives its statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) automatically at incorporation by the Registrar of Companies; the TIN is normally identical to the Company Registration Number (HEXXXXX). For VAT, the taxable person files Form TD101 (Application for VAT Registration) through TaxisNet/ARIADNI, and the Cyprus VAT identification number is issued in the format CY plus a nine-character alphanumeric code. Foreign entities establishing VAT presence must appoint a fiscal representative or licensed registered agent, and submit certified incorporation instruments, shareholder and beneficial ownership schedules, evidence of a fixed place of business, and a registered-agent AML certification prior to approval.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Cyprus private limited company receives its statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) automatically at incorporation by the Registrar of Companies; the TIN is normally identical to the Company Registration Number (HEXXXXX). For VAT, the taxable person files Form TD101 (Application for VAT Registration) through TaxisNet/ARIADNI, and the Cyprus VAT identification number is issued in the format CY plus a nine-character alphanumeric code. Foreign entities establishing VAT presence must appoint a fiscal representative or licensed registered agent, and submit certified incorporation instruments, shareholder and beneficial ownership schedules, evidence of a fixed place of business, and a registered-agent AML certification prior to approval.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Cyprus law; classification follows statutory corporate residence (central management and control) and Companies Law rules. Available statutory elections include: (1) Notional Interest Deduction election on new qualifying equity; (2) IP box regime election for qualifying intangible assets (80% gross income exemption, effective 2.5% rate); (3) tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping activities; (4) group loss relief elections among Cyprus-resident group members; and (5) ATAD-compliant disallowance of hybrid mismatch outcomes with no elective hybrid treatment available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 (annual taxable turnover); mandatory registration applies from the first day of the month following the month in which the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to: (a) supplies of goods dispatched or transported outside the European Union, substantiated by customs export documentation; (b) intra-Community supplies of goods to taxable persons in another Member State holding a valid VIES VAT number; and (c) certain internationally-facing services such as offshore registered agent and company secretarial services where the recipient is established outside Cyprus and the services are supplied from Cyprus in a B2B context. The supplier must hold contemporaneous documentary evidence proving physical departure of goods from the EU within 90 days, or, for services, establishing the recipient's non-EU establishment and the business-to-business nature of the supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Single Administrative Document (SAD C88) duly cleared by Cyprus Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving physical export out of the European Union
  • Commercial Invoice, Purchase Order and Delivery/Proforma Dockets
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statements evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • VIES validation proof or customer VAT registration number (intra-Community supplies)
  • Licensed registered agent services agreement for offshore secretarial scope (services zero-rated as an international B2B supply)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based Continuous Transaction Controls, or digital stamping obligation is currently in force in Cyprus. E-invoicing is voluntary and based on the EU PEPPOL interoperability network. Mandatory B2B e-invoicing and transaction-level reporting are anticipated under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) initiative with phased application expected from 2025-2035. Cyprus operates on-demand SAF-T/Audit-file submission rather than pre-clearance validation, and no electronic fiscal device (Fiscal Data Module) requirement applies to registered agent and secretarial services.

Under the VAT Law (Law 94(I)/2000) and the EU Customs Code, fraudulent export zero-rating is subject to retrospective clawback of VAT plus interest of 1.5% per month, administrative fines of up to EUR 10,000, and criminal penalties including imprisonment of up to 5 years under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law (Law 4/1978). The Tax Department and Cyprus Customs may audit export files for the standard four-year period, extendable to seven years where official notices are issued, and without time limit in proven tax evasion cases. Zero-rating relief is disallowed where the SAD, Bill of Lading, and Inward Remittance Certificate are internally inconsistent, or where export proceeds are not repatriated to Cyprus within the prescribed 180-day period.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Cyprus is a full member of the euro area; the euro is freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions. EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU) apply, subject to limited prudential and anti-money-laundering safeguards. There are no material exchange controls, quantitative restrictions, or discriminatory approval procedures for foreign direct investment, profit repatriation, dividend distribution, or liquidation proceeds. Non-resident shareholders may remit dividends and liquidation distributions without Central Bank permit, provided the company has satisfied Cypriot tax filing and payment obligations (including Special Defence Contribution where applicable and tax clearance from the Cyprus Tax Department where required). Current account convertibility is unrestricted, and capital account liberalization is complete for transactions with other EU member states and most third countries, including the United Kingdom under applicable bilateral continuity arrangements. Payment delays may arise solely from enhanced AML/KYC checks by commercial banks on source of funds and beneficial ownership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation in Cyprus to surrender export proceeds to the Central Bank of Cyprus or any designated dealer, and no mandatory conversion ratio into euros is imposed. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency accounts at authorized credit institutions, convert voluntarily to euros, or use such proceeds for trade settlement. Any foreign receipts must still be routed through a licensed Cypriot credit institution or an authorized payment institution, and the underlying export transaction must be properly documented for customs, VAT, and corporate tax purposes. The Commercial Code and tax statutes require accurate invoicing and record keeping, but do not impose a remittance timeline or surrender ratio.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for non-EEA fintech/EMI providers and Medium-High for EU/EEA passported Electronic Money Institutions under PSD2. Licensed Cypriot banks generally restrict onboarding when the ultimate corporate customer relies primarily on an unregulated or non-passported fintech firm as the sole banking rail. A Cyprus private limited company engaged in registered agent/company secretarial services (ISIC 8299) is classified as Medium Risk (Enhanced) under AML frameworks; offshore EMIs without a physical EEA presence and not registered as an agent of the Central Bank of Cyprus will face severe viability constraints. Acceptable fintech counterparts include EEA-authorised payment institutions operating through the EEA passport and clearing through a Cypriot settlement bank. Pure crypto-EMIs, non-EEA virtual IBAN providers, and fintechs refusing to provide underlying transaction documentation will be rejected by Cypriot correspondent banks. The company must maintain a primary physical bank account at a licensed Cypriot credit institution; an EMI can be used as a secondary disbursement rail but should never be the sole custodian of client or company funds.

Root cause: Unclear or excessive cash-flow expectations for a company secretarial business

Defense evidence: Fully executed client service agreements; detailed fee schedule and invoicing model; projected profit and loss and cash-flow statement; source of initial funding via bank wire from a regulated credit institution; historical bank statements if available; list of actual/expected clients with industry risk ratings; tax return and VAT registration evidence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital may be injected via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's or parent company's bank account. Purpose of payment must indicate 'Direct investment capital – shares', 'Contribution to equity / share capital', 'Subordinated shareholder loan', or 'Quasi-equity injection' depending on the agreement. The beneficiary account at the Cypriot bank must be a Capital Escrow Account or operational corporate account pre-approved by the bank's KYC unit. For a Cyprus Ltd, no paid-up minimum capital is mandatory but to demonstrate commercial substance a minimum injection of EUR 1,000 is advisable; for a registered agent/secretarial entity, capital of EUR 20,000 or more strengthens the licensing and onboarding file. Additional supporting documents: certified copy of the incorporation certificate, share subscription agreement, board resolution approving issuance, bank reference from sending bank, source of funds declaration, and evidence of tax information exchange compliance. Payments routed from non-EU jurisdictions must comply with Regulation (EC) No 1889/2005 cash declaration rules only if physical cash; wire transfers above any thresholds remain subject to bank AML monitoring. The bank will verify the wire's purpose code; typical SWIFT field 50K and 70 tags must be precise to avoid false-positive sanctions or AML alerts.Outbound disbursements for trade and client fund settlement are performed over the euro clearing system (TARGET2 through the Central Bank of Cyprus, using the Pan-European Automated Clearing House SEPA for EUR domestic/EEA credit transfers), or via SWIFT MT103 for cross-currency/non-EEA beneficiaries. For high-value and payment-versus-payment certainty, commercial letters of credit (documentary credits subject to UCP 600) are issued/confirmed through the Cypriot bank's trade finance desk. Corporate secretarial service fees to non-resident clients are typically small- to mid-value SEPA transfers or SWIFT MT103; there are no restrictions but the bank will require a valid underlying contract and periodic substantiation. For the licensed registered agent scope (ISIC 8299), outbound payments for sub-agent fees, domicile charges, and disbursement of statutory fees should be supported by invoices and an annually updated economic substance file. SWIFT MT103 field 70 (Remittance Information) should contain the invoice number or client reference, and field 71A (Details of Charges) should be set to SHA unless otherwise contractually agreed. Commercial LC flows require full presentation of transport documents, insurance documents, invoices, and inspection certificates as required by the underlying trade agreement; transaction-level documentation must be archived for at least five years to satisfy AML and Central Bank of Cyprus supervisory expectations.Outright EUR/USD forward contracts with an authorized Cypriot or EEA bank, available for non-speculative trade and corporate treasury hedging, requiring a signed master agreement (ISDA or bank-specific FX Term Sheet) and evidence of underlying exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in non-convertible emerging-market currencies (e.g., TRY, RUB, EGP, PKR) through Cypriot banks that have correspondent access; permitted for hedging of fee/trade receivables when local currency cannot be delivered, subject to bank risk appetite and underlying transaction documentation.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps) between EUR and major convertible currencies (USD, GBP, CHF) for managing settlement-date liquidity mismatches on cross-border trade flows and capital injections, requiring ISDA CSA collateral arrangements if unsecured.Interest rate and cross-currency basis swaps linked to euro short-term rate (€STR) or EURIBOR for matched-funding of any future syndicated credit facilities, permissible as hedging/treasury instruments when entered into with a Cyprus-licensed investment firm or EU credit institution.FX options (call/put European-style) for protection on material trade exposures, permissible only when the underlying business risk is documented and the transaction complies with the Central Bank of Cyprus' prudent risk management expectations.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on outbound dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; Cyprus does not impose dividend withholding tax or Special Defence Contribution on non-resident recipients.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 15 months from the end of the tax year (calendar year ending 31 December); for the 31 December 2024 tax year, the filing deadline is 31 March 2026.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box A1 Entity identification: registered legal name, Tax Identification Code (TIC), registered office address, and registered agent/secretarial particulars. Enter the exact name registered with the Cyprus Registrar of Companies and the TIC issued by the Tax Department. List non-resident directors/officers with full personal details in the accompanying schedule. Late filing incurs a fixed penalty of EUR 100 plus EUR 50 for each commenced month of delay, up to a maximum of EUR 500; late payment of corporate income tax attracts a late-payment surcharge of up to 5% of the unpaid tax and statutory interest until full settlement.
Box B1 Gross income and turnover: net revenue from all business activities during the tax year. A Cyprus-resident company is taxed on worldwide income; if the company is non-resident, declare only Cyprus-source income and attach a justification memorandum under the relevant DTA. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File/transfer pricing documentation must be maintained for related-party transactions exceeding EUR 500,000 per annum for goods or financial transactions and EUR 50,000 per annum for intra-group services; Master File documentation is required for multinational groups whose consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: A registered agent and company secretarial services provider is treated as performing 'legal, accounting and administrative services'. Where such services are rendered to a foreign related enterprise, the Cyprus entity falls within the Cyprus Economic Substance Regulations and must maintain a physical office in Cyprus, full-time employees, board meetings held and attended in Cyprus, and expenditure proportionate to the services rendered.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax; statutory interest; criminal prosecution under the Cyprus Income Tax Law and Criminal Code carrying imprisonment of up to three years and/or a fine reflecting the amount of tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations face personal criminal liability, civil restitution for improperly distributed profits, travel restriction orders, and disqualification from serving as directors of any Cyprus company.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Cyprus Economic Substance Legislation including financial fines, revocation of the Tax Resident Certificate, denial of treaty benefits, and potential strike-off of the company.

Director personal liability: Directors and nominee officers are personally liable for false ESR notifications; civil recovery against personal assets, statutory bans from directorship, and criminal liability when deliberate non-compliance is established.
AML / Registered Agent Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Cyprus Anti-Money Laundering Law (L.188(I)/2007 as amended); imprisonment of up to five years; revocation of the registered agent license and compulsory strike-off of the entity.

Director personal liability: The MLRO, directors, and senior officers are criminally liable for failing to report suspicious activity; civil liability may arise against client losses; prohibitions on holding regulated functions may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment under Section 33 of the Cyprus Income Tax Law; additional 12.5% corporate income tax assessment; late interest; and administrative penalties of up to 100% of the undercharged tax.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in deliberate profit shifting may become jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, face asset seizure, and be subject to court-ordered travel bans and disqualification orders under the Cyprus Companies Law.

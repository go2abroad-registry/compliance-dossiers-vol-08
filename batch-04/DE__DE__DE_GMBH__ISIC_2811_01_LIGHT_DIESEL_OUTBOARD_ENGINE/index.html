Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital is €25,000; at least €12,500 must be paid up before registration. Cash contributions must be paid into a German bank account in the name of the company to be formed and must be irrevocably at the free disposal of the managing directors. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authorities are the locally responsible Finanzamt for corporate income tax, trade tax administration coordination, and VAT registration; the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office, BZSt) for the German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.); and ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) for electronic tax registration and filings. Municipalities administer trade tax assessments based on tax office assessments.

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated GmbH must obtain its domestic tax number by submitting the electronic Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung through ELSTER to the Finanzamt responsible for its registered office or management. The application must identify the legal entity, commercial activity, directors, shareholders, registered address, expected turnover, VAT liability, bank account, and accounting period. The Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer after review. The GmbH or its authorized tax adviser should separately apply for a VAT identification number through the BZSt, generally using the ELSTER registration process or the prescribed BZSt application route. A non-resident foreign entity conducting taxable transactions in Germany must register with the responsible Finanzamt and apply for a German tax number and, where required, a USt-IdNr. A German fiscal representative is not generally mandatory for every non-resident business, but special representation rules may apply to non-established businesses, import VAT arrangements, and certain refund procedures.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is normally subject to entity-level corporation tax and trade tax; Germany has no general U.S.-style check-the-box election for a GmbH. German partnerships are generally fiscally transparent, but a qualifying partnership may elect corporate taxation under Section 1a of the German Corporation Tax Act. A GmbH may participate in a tax group (Organschaft) if statutory financial integration, profit-transfer, and other requirements are satisfied. Hybrid-entity and hybrid-instrument outcomes are restricted by German anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive. Shareholder residence, management location, treaty residence, and permanent-establishment status may alter cross-border taxing rights but do not constitute a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold below which a business conducting taxable German supplies is automatically outside VAT registration. The small-business exemption under Section 19 of the German VAT Act may apply where prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and election rules. A business using the exemption generally does not charge VAT and cannot deduct input VAT. The thresholds do not eliminate registration requirements for transactions requiring a German VAT number, intra-Community transactions, imports, reverse-charge transactions, or other special cases.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for the German VAT exemption with input-tax credit under Section 6 of the German VAT Act if the goods are transported or dispatched to the third country and the statutory export documentation proves both the export transaction and the goods' exit from the EU customs territory. For an out-of-scope or zero-rated service, the place-of-supply rules must be satisfied; B2B services are generally supplied at the customer's place of establishment under the recipient-location rule and may be subject to reverse charge rather than a German zero rate. Mere invoicing to a foreign customer, payment from abroad, or contractual designation as an export does not independently establish zero-rating.

  • Electronic export declaration and customs export reference or Movement Reference Number (MRN), where required by customs rules
  • Customs export results or electronic confirmation of exit from the European Union
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, destination, and VAT treatment
  • Transport documentation appropriate to the shipment, such as bill of lading, sea waybill, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or carrier confirmation
  • Proof of delivery or other documentary evidence linking the goods to the non-EU destination
  • Purchase order, sales contract, packing list, and shipping records where necessary to establish transaction identity and chain of custody
  • Evidence of payment where relevant to corroborate the transaction; Germany does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a statutory condition for export VAT exemption

Germany does not currently operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or transaction-reporting system for all businesses. From 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. Mandatory issuance is phased: generally from 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year domestic turnover exceeding EUR 800,000, and from 1 January 2028 for other in-scope domestic B2B transactions, subject to statutory transitional rules. Qualifying electronic invoices must generally comply with the European standard EN 16931 or an accepted structured format such as XRechnung or an equivalent hybrid format. Public-sector invoicing has separate federal and state implementation requirements. There is no general German requirement for prior digital tax stamping or API clearance by the Finanzamt, although invoices and VAT records must be retained electronically in an auditable form and may be requested during a tax audit.

Germany has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no universal requirement for an inward remittance certificate to substantiate export VAT exemption. The supplier must retain reliable books, invoices, customs records, and proof of exit for the statutory retention period and must correct VAT treatment if the exemption conditions are not proven. If zero-rating is denied, German output VAT, normally 19% for the relevant goods, may be assessed retrospectively, together with statutory interest under the German Fiscal Code and potential late-payment consequences. Incorrect returns may trigger administrative fines; intentional or reckless false VAT declarations may constitute tax evasion or tax understatement under the German Fiscal Code, with criminal or administrative penalties. Input VAT refunds may be reduced or denied where supporting evidence is incomplete, and fraudulent or abusive claims may result in assessments, interest, penalties, criminal prosecution, and enhanced audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Sovereign regulatory regime: Germany is a Eurozone member state and the euro (EUR) is fully convertible. Article 63 TFEU guarantees free movement of capital, removing all exchange-control licensing for cross-border capital and current-account transactions. Capital injections by way of equity or shareholder loans, profit remittances of dividends, branch profits, license fees, interest, and all ordinary trade payments are freely permitted in EUR or any convertible currency without quota, central-bank approval, or an approved-bank list. The only structural hurdles are tax-related, not FX-related: German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective rate approximately 8–16% depending on the municipal Hebesatz), and dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer 26.375% including solidarity surcharge, subject to relief under double-taxation treaties). No restrictions exist on holding foreign-currency accounts in Germany; a GmbH may open and operate multi-currency accounts freely.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender, no forced conversion ratio (0% conversion obligation), and no holding-period constraint on export proceeds. Export receivables of the German GmbH may be retained abroad or onshore in any currency. The only binding requirement is statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 10 AWV: any cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 (or equivalent) received or made by a German resident must be reported via the Bundesbank's payment-reporting portal (Zahlungsverkehrs-Meldeportal) by the seventh day of the month following the calendar month in which the payment occurred. The report must contain the amount, currency, counterparty country, and the correct AWV payment-purpose code. Non-filing or late filing triggers administrative fines under Section 73 AWV (generally up to EUR 30,000 per case) but does not invalidate the underlying trade or the receipt of funds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Germany's central bank) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin) as the competent authority for banking conduct, KYC, and AML supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium alternative rail, complementary rather than primary. BaFin-licensed German electronic money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Airwallex (Germany) GmbH, Solaris, PPRO Financial, and SEPA white-label PSP platforms) permit remote onboarding, multi-currency IBANs, SWIFT/SEPA rails, FX conversion, and Treasury APIs without physical branch visits. They do not, however, provide documentary credit issuance, bill-of-exchange handling, or traditional correspondent clearing in the USD/CNY corridors. Offshore EMIs licensed in Estonia, Ireland, the UK, or Lithuania are operationally usable for collections and FX conversion, but create material reputational and acceptance friction: many German trade counterparties and collection banks reject payments routed through third-country EMI IBANs. For this GmbH, a domestic bank account is mandatory for statutory capital injection, LC facilities, and trade settlement; a German-regulated EMI is recommended only as a secondary operational rail for low-value receipts, payroll, and marketplace collections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocols per transaction type: (1) Equity capital increase: the shareholder sends a SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR) from its bank to the GmbH's euro account at Deutsche Bank AG or Commerzbank AG using the GmbH's full legal name, IBAN, and payment purpose 'Stammeinlage / Kapitalerhöhung – Einzahlung auf Geschäftsanteil (GmbHG)' together with the commercial-register reference; the receiving bank requires the notarized shareholders' resolution (Übernahmevertrag), a director's declaration of contribution (Einlagebescheinigung), and proof of identification to release funds from the capital escrow account to the operational account. (2) Shareholder loan: SWIFT MT103 with payment purpose 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' accompanied by a signed and dated loan agreement specifying interest rate, maturity, and repayment mechanism; the amount is credited to the operational account and the bank verifies the AWV report once the EUR 12,500 threshold is exceeded. (3) Verification and reporting: for every inbound credit above EUR 12,500, the bank requests the underlying documents and the AWV payment-purpose code; the GmbH must file the balance-of-payments report within the statutory deadline via the Bundesbank portal. (4) Multi-currency inbound wires in USD, GBP, or CNY are credited automatically to the linked multi-currency account and may be converted to EUR through the approved FX credit line without Bundesbank approval. All capital-bearing wires must carry a valid Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR) to satisfy full traceability and anti-money-laundering screening.Clear rails for trade settlements: (a) SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 XML pain.001.001.03 to pain.001.001.09) for EUR disbursements within the EEA, with remittance information (RmtInf) containing invoice numbers and a UETR; (b) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wires in USD, GBP, CNY, or other non-SEPA currencies, using correct 71A/71B charges (SHA on import, OUR or BEN as negotiated) and including the invoice reference and UETR; (c) Commercial Letters of Credit for import raw materials and third-country component purchases: issued via SWIFT MT700, amended via MT707, and settled through the presenting bank on sight or term basis; the LC may be confirmed by the beneficiary's bank when foreign-supplier bank risk is unacceptable; and (d) SEPA Direct Debit (SDD Core and B2B) for recurring EEA supplier payments. All outbound disbursements are subject to German and EU sanctions screening, dual-use export-control review under EU Regulation 2021/821 (relevant when parts could be integrated into vessels with maritime end-use risk), and documentary matching between the LC terms, invoice, transport documents, and customs declaration. The preferred treasury structure for this GmbH is the LC-plus-MT103 corridor: procurement via confirmed irrevocable letter of credit where supplier bank risk exists, and post-shipment settlement via MT103 TT against presented transport documents for clean, no-discrepancy transactions.EUR/USD plain-vanilla outright forward contracts (tenor up to 12 months, margined or collateralised) executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the German Master Agreement for Financial Derivatives (Deutscher Rahmenvertrag für Finanztermingeschäfte, DRV) with a credit support annex (CSA), used exclusively to hedge forecast USD export receipts and USD import payables.EUR/USD foreign-exchange swaps (spot-forward and forward-forward rollover combinations) to manage settlement-date liquidity mismatches between incoming USD export proceeds and outgoing USD import payments.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) converting USD floating-rate trade-finance borrowings into EUR floating or EUR fixed coupons, aligning the GmbH's debt-service currency with its EUR functional currency and requiring an ISDA netting set and a board-approved derivatives mandate.EUR/USD purchased currency options or zero-premium collar structures (call-spread, put-spread, and collar combinations) providing downside protection on large LC-denominated invoices while retaining upside participation; execution requires approved ISDA/CSA documentation and the company's written treasury policy.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for third-country currencies that are not onshore-deliverable to the GmbH (e.g., CNY, INR, or RUB under restricted convertibility conditions) and only for hedging actual invoices in those currencies; NDFs on EUR/USD or EUR/GBP are prohibited because both currencies are fully deliverable and their use would constitute a speculative overlay and an automatic compliance red flag. Any NDF execution requires prior written approval of the responsible bank compliance officer and the applicable AWV report if the EUR 12,500 threshold is exceeded.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium alternative rail, complementary rather than primary. BaFin-licensed German electronic money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Airwallex (Germany) GmbH, Solaris, PPRO Financial, and SEPA white-label PSP platforms) permit remote onboarding, multi-currency IBANs, SWIFT/SEPA rails, FX conversion, and Treasury APIs without physical branch visits. They do not, however, provide documentary credit issuance, bill-of-exchange handling, or traditional correspondent clearing in the USD/CNY corridors. Offshore EMIs licensed in Estonia, Ireland, the UK, or Lithuania are operationally usable for collections and FX conversion, but create material reputational and acceptance friction: many German trade counterparties and collection banks reject payments routed through third-country EMI IBANs. For this GmbH, a domestic bank account is mandatory for statutory capital injection, LC facilities, and trade settlement; a German-regulated EMI is recommended only as a secondary operational rail for low-value receipts, payroll, and marketplace collections.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT amount, resulting in an aggregate CIT headline of 15.825%; local trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at municipal rates of 7–17% creates a combined effective tax burden of approximately 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% statutory German dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer: 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge). Reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a EU parent holding at least 10% of the GmbH for no less than 12 months; treaty rates between 0% and 15% apply to non-EU residents depending on shareholding and ownership structure.

7 months after the tax year-end; for calendar-year GmbH, 31 July of the following year. An automatic extension to 31 October is granted when a certified tax advisor prepares the return; further discretionary extensions can be requested under § 109 AO.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File documentation under GAufzV applies when annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed €5,000,000 for goods or €1,000,000 for intra-group services; a Master File is required when worldwide group revenue exceeds €100,000,000. Additional thresholds exist for licensee and financing structures; failure to produce adequate documentation allows tax authorities to estimate income and dismiss corrective evidence.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a sector-specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR); however, shell and conduit companies are challenged through § 42 AO abuse-of-law rules, the beneficial-ownership test in DTAs, the German CFC Act under §§ 7–14 AStG, and strict transfer-pricing requirements. A GmbH lacking personnel, premises, business records and entrepreneurial risk in Germany can be re-characterised as a sham by the tax authorities, leading to re-attribution of profits to the non-resident beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under § 370 AO imprisonment for up to 5 years (up to 10 years for particularly serious cases of gross commercial tax fraud); monetary fine determined by the criminal court; the tax debt remains payable with 6% per annum interest under § 233a AO.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under §§ 34 and 69 AO for unpaid corporate tax if the non-payment is attributable to their intentional or grossly negligent breach of statutory duties; the German State may seize private property and impose travel bans through administrative enforcement.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustments under § 1 AStG; German tax authorities may impose 6% per annum interest on the adjustment, add penalty interest, and apply a minimum penalty for incomplete documentation; in aggravated cases €25,000 compulsory fines and criminal proceedings under § 370 AO.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign factually incorrect declarations or approve fictitious intercompany contracts can be prosecuted as principals; civil liability for the tax deficiency arises to the extent funds have been transferred abroad, with personal bank accounts, travel documents and passport confiscation orders possible.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: § 42 AO empowers the tax office to ignore the artificial structure and tax the income as if the economic substance were held directly by the beneficial owner; penalties include a re-assessment plus late-payment interest, and the company may be struck off under the Commercial Register if insolvency or misleading registration is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for damages to creditors and tax authorities under the doctrine of ‘material hole’ (Durchgriffshaftung) and § 64 GmbHG for payments made after illiquidity; a travel ban can be imposed for directors fleeing foreign currency tax claims.
Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Under § 89 GwG, administrative fines up to €5,000,000 or up to 10% of annual turnover; criminal money laundering under § 261 StGB carries imprisonment up to 5 years; proceeds are forfeited.

Director personal liability: Board members and AML officers are personally liable for violating evaluative duties; the court may order civil compensation and asset forfeiture against the natural persons, and German enforcement authorities may request passport seizure and pre-trial detention for flight-risk suspects.

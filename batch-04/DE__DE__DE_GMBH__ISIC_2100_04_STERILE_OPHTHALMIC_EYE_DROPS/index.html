Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT registration and administrative assistance; local Finanzamt (local tax office) competent for substantive VAT registration and compliance. Portal: Elster (Elektronische Steuererklärung) at www.elster.de.

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign entity establishing a German GmbH or incurring German taxable supplies must register for VAT through the locally competent Finanzamt. The GmbH first obtains a tax number (Steuernummer) by filing the mandatory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER or paper form within one month of business commencement. A separate VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) under §27a UStG is issued by BZSt on application and is required for cross-border transactions, EU intra-community supplies, and export zero-rating documentation. The GmbH must also provide a German registered address, managing director's identity, and commercial register extract. Non-resident shareholders are permitted; beneficial owner information must be disclosed under anti-money-laundering and tax transparency rules.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a German GmbH or incurring German taxable supplies must register for VAT through the locally competent Finanzamt. The GmbH first obtains a tax number (Steuernummer) by filing the mandatory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER or paper form within one month of business commencement. A separate VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) under §27a UStG is issued by BZSt on application and is required for cross-border transactions, EU intra-community supplies, and export zero-rating documentation. The GmbH must also provide a German registered address, managing director's identity, and commercial register extract. Non-resident shareholders are permitted; beneficial owner information must be disclosed under anti-money-laundering and tax transparency rules.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box election exists in Germany; the GmbH is a non-transparent corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) mandatorily subject to corporation tax, trade tax, and solidarity surcharge. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are restricted by §4k EStG (implementing ATAD II) and §8b KStG anti-abuse rules; no elective pass-through or hybrid entity classification is available. Small entrepreneurs may elect Kleinunternehmer treatment under §19 UStG domestically if below turnover thresholds, but the GmbH engaged in export manufacturing typically cannot claim this exemption and instead elects for regular VAT deduction. Intra-Community VAT identification numbers and reverse-charge election apply under EU VAT rules.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
19%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Foreign entities and German GmbHs are required to register for VAT when they perform taxable supplies in Germany, with no general de-minimis exemption for export-oriented manufacturers. The small-business (Kleinunternehmer) threshold is EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 current-year turnover under §19 UStG, but this cannot be claimed for supplies that would otherwise be zero-rated exports and is practically unavailable to a GmbH engaged in regular international trade. Domestic B2B reverse-charge and intra-Community supplies have separate registration thresholds.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Export supplies of goods are zero-rated under §4 Nr.1 UStG in conjunction with §6 UStG if the goods are physically transported by the supplier, a third party, or the entrepreneurial purchaser to a destination outside the EU, and the purchaser is a non-EU taxable (or non-taxable) person. The German GmbH eye-drop manufacturer must demonstrate that title and risk in the sterile ophthalmic product passed to the overseas buyer and that the goods actually left the Union territory. For goods sold with Incoterms EXW/FCA and transported by the foreign buyer, a declaration that the goods were taken out of the EU by the buyer must be obtained (Ausfuhrlieferung unter Mitnahme). Fiscal representation is not required for true export zero-rating; input VAT allocated to direct export sales is fully deductible. Services linked to exported goods (e.g., logistics, royalty or supply-chain services) may also qualify as zero-rated ancillary offshore services under §6a UStG only if directly connected to the export of tangible goods, but pure intellectual or licensing services are generally taxable at 19% unless the recipient is a business in another EU/third country and reverse charge applies.

Germany mandates structured e-invoicing for all domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2025, with full obligation phased by 1 January 2027. Invoices must use a machine-readable format conforming to EN 16931 (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD). No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, central tax authority API, or digital stamping is currently applied at the national level; transmission of e-invoices to the Finanzamt is not required on a transaction-by-transaction basis. However, the Germany GmbH must preserve all e-invoices and export documentation for statutory retention (10 years) and ensure interoperability for B2B and cross-border EU invoices. The BZSt operates a central EU database (VIES) for VAT ID validation, and non-compliance with e-invoicing format rules before 2028 may trigger a reversible penalty framework; after 1 January 2028, paper or non-compliant e-invoices are treated as invalid for input VAT deduction.

      
The Finanzamt scrutinizes export documentation under §§6, 8-10 UStDV and §17 UStG. If the required export evidence is incomplete, inaccurate, or not produced within the audit, the zero-rating is retrospectively denied; VAT at 19% is assessed on the full invoice value plus statutory interest (currently 0.15% per month or 1.8% per year) and late-payment surcharges. A fraudulent claim of export zero-rating, or false documentary evidence, constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years, or up to 10 years for aggravated cases. Fines up to EUR 50,000 per violation may apply under relevant customs and tax provisions. Furthermore, shipping the sterile eye drops to an EU intermediary that then dispatches to a non-EU destination without proper EU export proof may be reclassified as an intra-Community supply or domestic taxable supply, triggering full VAT liability and penalty exposure. The 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation requirement aligns with German customs and export-control practice; failure to repatriate export proceeds may also trigger export-license revocation and fines under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) §52 in connection with §70 AWV.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Germany is a Eurozone member and observes full currency convertibility under EU primary law, in particular Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible and transferable. Profit remittance by a German GmbH is not subject to any exchange-control restriction; any burden arises solely from taxation, e.g., corporate income tax, municipal trade tax, dividend withholding tax at 25% plus solidarity surcharge, potentially reduced under double-tax treaties. Anti-money-laundering reporting under the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG) and foreign-trade statistical reporting under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV) apply but do not constrain convertibility.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
There is no statutory obligation in Germany to repatriate export proceeds, nor any mandatory conversion ratio to Euro. Export receipts in any currency may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, converted as needed, or used directly for foreign-currency payments. The only compliance requirement is statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank for payments from non-residents above the applicable reporting threshold (€12,500 per transaction or aggregated monthly), to be submitted within the prescribed monthly or quarterly deadlines.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Central Bank of the Federal Republic of Germany) jointly with the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking, securities, and financial-market conduct supervision.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: Medium, conditional on the EMI holding an EU/EEA e-money licence (BaFin licence or passported EU licence under the E-Money Directive). An offshore digital EMI without an EU passport cannot lawfully provide payment accounts or clicking in Germany; such activity would constitute unauthorised banking/financial services under the Kreditwesengesetz (KWG) and Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG). For a pharmaceutical manufacturer utilising SWIFT wires and documentary letters of credit, an EMI should be treated only as a supplementary collection or low-value disbursement channel, never as the sole treasury rail, because EMIs generally cannot offer credit, trade finance, or high-value corporate liquidity services.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Trade-register mismatch or incomplete beneficial-ownership identification under the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG).
        
Root cause: Trade-register mismatch or incomplete beneficial-ownership identification under the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG).

        
Defense evidence: Current Handelsregisterauszug (Trade Register extract) dated no older than 14 days; notarised articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag/Satzung); full shareholder register; a signed statement of beneficial owners listing every natural person holding more than 25% or ultimate control; equivalent corporate-registry evidence for any foreign parent, legalised/apostilled and translated into German or English.

      

      
      

        Bank suspicion of 'letterbox company' or inadequate physical substance for pharmaceutical manufacturing.
        
Root cause: Bank suspicion of 'letterbox company' or inadequate physical substance for pharmaceutical manufacturing.

        
Defense evidence: Valid pharmaceutical manufacturing licence (Herstellungserlaubnis under Arzneimittelgesetz); GMP certificate from the competent state authority (Regierung von Oberbayern or equivalent); BfArM or EMA registration referencing the sterile ophthalmic product; USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number); lease agreement and utility bills for the production site; payroll records and employer-liability-insurance certificates; 12-month cash-flow forecast and audited/perspectival financial projections.

      

      
      

        Non-resident directors, third-party payment requests, or opaque funding sources.
        
Root cause: Non-resident directors, third-party payment requests, or opaque funding sources.

        
Defense evidence: Certified copy of the directors' appointment resolution; passports and residence/work permits of all authorised signatories; detailed curricula vitae and professional background; commercial evidence for each significant inbound or outbound transfer, including invoices, contracts, purchase orders, and transport documents; declaration that no straw-man or nominee arrangement exists; source-of-wealth letter for the initial capital contribution, signed by the transferring shareholder.

      

      
      

        Sanctions/PEP screening negative news and potential adverse-media flags.
        
Root cause: Sanctions/PEP screening negative news and potential adverse-media flags.

        
Defense evidence: Screening printouts from a licensed sanctions/PEP data provider for each director, beneficial owner, and counterparty; reasoned legal opinion from a German lawyer on the ultimate ownership chain; PEP-status declaration and, where applicable, a certificate of good standing from the home jurisdiction; clean criminal-record certificate (Führungszeugnis) for the managing director; explanation and evidence addressing any negative media item.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Compliant inbound capital injections must be credited through SEPA Instant/EEA credit transfer (zero to one business day) or SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the GmbH's EUR-denominated Capital Escrow Account. The payment message must include the German balance-of-payments statistic code for direct investment equity capital (Direktinvestition – Kapitalzufuhr, identification code e.g., 'D4' or the relevant 'Zahlungszweck' chosen under the Bundesbank's external-statistics coding system) and a clear reference to the notarised capital-increase resolution or shareholder contribution agreement. For the creation of new registered share capital, the German notary requires a banker's confirmation of the credit of the contribution to the escrow account; otherwise the payment should be treated as a shareholder loan or capital reserve. Foreign direct-investment statistical reporting applies for any claim/payment to a non-resident above €12,500, usually submitted electronically to the Deutsche Bundesbank.For trade disbursements, the principal clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct customer credit transfers, with the underlying invoice number, Irish/CN import or export reference, and the applicable AWV purpose code; (2) commercial letters of credit issued under UCP 600, advised and confirmed through the house bank's trade-finance desk, for high-value consignments of sterile ophthalmic products; (3) documentary collections (DP/DA) for lower-risk open-account or documentary transactions; (4) SEPA credit transfers within the EU/EEA for euro-denominated payments; and (5) TARGET2/T2 high-value real-time gross settlement for urgent transfers above the large-value threshold.

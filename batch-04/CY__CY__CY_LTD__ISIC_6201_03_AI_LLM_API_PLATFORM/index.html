A purely virtual address without a manned physical office is not acceptable for a Cyprus Ltd. The company must have a registered office located in Cyprus to which official notices and court process may validly be delivered. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Tμήμα Φόρων) under the Ministry of Finance; official portal: https://taxdep.mof.gov.cy

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Cyprus Private Limited Company must first incorporate with the Registrar of Companies (Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver), obtain the incorporation certificate and acquire its tax identification status. The company is required to register for tax purposes with the Tax Department through the 'AERAS' (Advance Electronic Registration and Authentication System) or by filing the applicable registration forms (TD 2001 for VAT registration and TIN allocation). The Tax Department issues the TIN (Tax Identification Number) upon submission of the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, proof of registered office address in Cyprus, identity documentation of directors and shareholders, and the completed company registration questionnaire. Registration for withholding taxes (PAYE/GHS) and Social Insurance contributions is completed through the Social Insurance Services registration platform, while VAT registration is effected through the Tax Department VAT portal (VAT 101 form for EU/domestic registration or VAT 102 for non-established taxable persons). Non-resident companies that have no fixed establishment in Cyprus but make taxable supplies or acquire intra-Community goods may be required to register directly through the Cyprus Tax Department e-portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a check-the-box classification regime analogous to the U.S. For Cyprus tax purposes, a Private Limited Company is categorically a separate taxable person subject to entity-level corporate income tax at a flat 12.5% rate, with no partnership pass-through election available for limited companies. Foreign hybrid entities may be subject to classification under Cyprus domestic case law and the Interpretation Section 61A of the Income Tax Law (ITL) for income characterization; however, for South Africa–Cyprus or other treaty contexts, Cyprus law applies the OECD partnership report approach in determining whether a foreign entity is transparent. Cyprus has enacted controlled foreign company (CFC) rules and interest limitation rules under EU ATAD, but no statutory check-the-box election exists. A Cyprus Ltd may elect to be treated as tax-transparent only where the entity is formed as a Cyprus partnership (not as a limited company). No hybrid mismatch elections beyond the standard application of the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) and the Cyprus Income Tax Law's tie-breaker and mismatch rules are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 (annual taxable turnover for supply of goods and services; EUR 15,600 for intra-Community acquisitions). For cross-border digital services and electronically supplied services provided by non-established suppliers, registration is mandatory without a threshold from the first taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT is available on the supply of goods that are transported outside the European Union by or on behalf of the supplier (export VAT zero-rating under Section 41(1) of the VAT Law 94(I)/2000), as well as on the supply of services related to those exported goods, including transportation and ancillary services. In addition, certain internationally traded services (e.g., services supplied to a person belonging in a third country, directly in connection with exported or imported goods) qualify for zero rating. For AI/LLM API platform licensing and cloud-based API access, the supply is generally classified as electronically supplied services (ESS), which is treated as supplied where the customer belongs. If the customer is an overseas non-business consumer, an OSS reporting VAT applies at the customer's location; if the customer is a business, the reverse charge applies. Export zero-rating of intangible digital services is available under VAT 2006/112/EC Article 59 and the place-of-supply rules: services to a non-business customer in a third territory are outside the scope or may be zero-rated where effective use and enjoyment occurs outside the EU. Substantiation requires a contractual agreement identifying the recipient, invoicing evidence, recipient's VAT/GST registration number where applicable, banking or payment records proving receipt of the cross-border consideration, and, where applicable, the OSS return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document, SAD) accepted by the Cyprus Customs Department via the ECS/ICS system
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR Road Consignment Note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing settlement of the export price

Cyprus does not currently mandate B2B or B2G real-time electronic clearance, fiscal stamping, or a continuous transaction control (CTC) system for domestically issued invoices. Since December 17, 2022, Cyprus law requires the issuance of electronic invoices for all public-sector contracts per the European Standard EN 16931 and the PEPPOL BIS format. Private businesses may issue electronic invoices but are subject to the invoice validation and storage rules of the VAT Law; they are not obliged to use a government-operated fiscal API. Businesses must transmit recapitulative statements (VIES) electronically to the Tax Department's VIES portal for intra-Community supplies. For the 'One Stop Shop' (OSS) and 'Reverse Charge' mechanisms, registrants must file OSS returns through the Cyprus Tax Department web-based OSS portal using standard schema with XML/API submission. Peppol-based trans-border e-invoicing is accepted in practice. No digital stamping regime is in force.

The Cyprus Tax Department conducts post-export audits on zero-rating substantiation. Under the Cyprus VAT Law 94(I)/2000, if the exporter fails within a reasonable period (normally 180 days) to obtain and preserve the required export documentation, the output tax may become immediately payable without prejudice to penalties. Fraudulent zero-rating claims are criminalized under Sections 76 and 77 VAT Law 94(I)/2000; penalties range from an administrative fine equal to the tax under-charged up to double the amount, plus interest at 1.5% per month or part of a month for overdue VAT. Criminal sanctions include imprisonment of up to three years, or on indictment, up to five years, and/or fines up to EUR 50,000 for serious VAT evasion and money laundering offences. The statute provides for retrospective recovery of input tax deducted and the clawback of any zero-rated amount if the goods are not exported within 90 days of the supply. In an audit, the burden is on the taxable person to prove the goods or services left the EU, and absence of a complete customs/transport/remittance file will result in a reassessment and a denial of the right to zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus applies full EUR convertibility for current and capital account transactions. Capital controls introduced during the 2013 banking crisis were phased out by April 2015 and no general restrictions remain. EU Member State capital-movement freedoms govern; the historical Exchange Control Law (Chapter 154) retains only administrative reporting and notification powers for non-EU/EEA transactions and does not impede profit remittance. A Cyprus Limited company may pay dividends, service intercompany loans and repatriate capital without Cyprus withholding tax, provided substance and beneficial-ownership requirements under domestic law and EU anti-abuse directives are met.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Cyprus. Exporters retain free use of foreign-currency and EUR receipts. The only compliance duty is the monthly Balance-of-Payments statistical declaration, submitted by the reporting enterprise or the servicing bank, whenever individual cross-border transactions exceed the current €12,500 reporting threshold; no Central Bank approval is required for repatriation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα Κύπρου), acting as the euro-area National Central Bank and the competent authority for exchange control, balance-of-payments statistics and the domestic monetary regime; euro-area membership places nominal exchange-rate authority with the European Central Bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High for EU/EEA-licensed e-money institutions and payment institutions passporting into Cyprus under the Payment Services Directive 2 and the E-Money Directive. These can be used for payout routing, card acquiring and SWIFT-like transfers, but a Cypriot commercial bank account remains necessary for capital injection and escrow purposes. Low for offshore non-EEA digital EMIs, which generally cannot obtain a Cypriot correspondent relationship, are outside the SEPA scheme, and will be blocked by originator-information and Central Bank of Cyprus anti-money-laundering rules.

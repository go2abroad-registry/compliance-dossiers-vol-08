Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance; Ariadne Tax Portal (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy/)

E-government filing portal

Cyprus-incorporated companies are automatically issued a Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon incorporation by the Registrar of Companies. For VAT purposes, a taxable person must submit Form VAT-001 to the District Tax Office before commencing taxable transactions; non-established foreign entities providing taxable supplies in Cyprus must register for VAT through the Ariadne portal using Form VAT-101 and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative. All registrations require submission of certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, proof of registered office, and identification of directors and beneficial owners.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (cyprus-resident companies taxed on worldwide income at 12.5%; non-resident companies taxed only on Cyprus-source income; companies managed and controlled in Cyprus are tax residents) — Cyprus-incorporated companies are automatically issued a Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon incorporation by the Registrar of Companies. For VAT purposes, a taxable person must submit Form VAT-001 to the District Tax Office before commencing taxable transactions; non-established foreign entities providing taxable supplies in Cyprus must register for VAT through the Ariadne portal using Form VAT-101 and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative. All registrations require submission of certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, proof of registered office, and identification of directors and beneficial owners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (cyprus-resident companies taxed on worldwide income at 12.5%; non-resident companies taxed only on Cyprus-source income; companies managed and controlled in Cyprus are tax residents)

Available election forms

No check-the-box election available under Cyprus law. Entity classification follows incorporation and residency rules. Available statutory elections include group loss relief (75% participation), notional interest deduction on new equity, IP Box regime (80% notional exemption), and optional VAT grouping election for closely-linked entities. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD apply and may require downward adjustments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for goods and services for local business establishment; no threshold for non-resident suppliers (registration required from first supply unless reverse charge or VAT OSS applies); intra-Community distance sales threshold for non-established suppliers is EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cyprus zero-rates (0% VAT) supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union, provided that the supplier obtains and retains proof of export within the legal timeframe. Intra-Community supplies are zero-rated when the buyer is a VAT-registered person in another Member State and the supplier files a recapitulative EC Sales List. Zero-rating also applies to exported services in accordance with Article 146 and 147 of the EU VAT Directive, including international transport and services directly related to exported goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document SAD)
  • Bill of Lading or alternative transport document (CMR, airway waybill)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Proof of payment (bank statement or wire transfer confirmation, equivalent to an inward remittance certificate)

Cyprus does not currently impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or e-invoice clearance systems. Electronic invoices are accepted if they ensure authenticity, integrity, and readability. VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and statistical filings must be submitted electronically via the Ariadne portal. Cyprus is monitoring EU ViDA proposals for mandatory e-reporting; no national mandate is in effect as of the current filing year.

The Cyprus Tax Department and Customs Authorities may retroactively claw back zero-rating if export substantiation is incomplete, false, or not timely produced. Non-compliance attracts interest of 6% per annum on the assessed VAT, administrative fines up to double the amount of tax evaded, and criminal penalties including imprisonment up to five years. Repeat offenses may trigger enhanced penalties and suspension or cancellation of VAT registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Cyprus is a full member of the Eurozone; the Euro (EUR) is fully convertible. Current account transactions, including trade in goods, services, franchise royalties, and management fees, are unrestricted and require no exchange-control approval. Capital account transactions, including direct investment, shareholder loans, and portfolio investment, are free of exchange controls under EU capital-movement liberalization, subject only to statistical reporting and AML/CFT screening. Profit remittance and dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders may be executed without prior Central Bank approval, provided the company is tax-resident, the statutory solvency condition under the Cyprus Companies Law (inability to pay debts) is satisfied, and the payment is supported by board minutes and audited financial statements. There is no separate profit-repatriation permit or tax-clearance certificate required by the Central Bank, but commercial banks apply normal KYC, account-opening due diligence, and FATCA/CRS verification prior to executing such payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus does not impose any statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirement on export proceeds. As a Eurozone member, there is no obligation to convert foreign-currency receipts into EUR or to remit export earnings to a local bank account; exporters may retain funds abroad in foreign-currency accounts. Nevertheless, the Central Bank of Cyprus requires all credit institutions and payment service providers to report transactions above EUR 10,000 for AML/CFT and balance-of-payments statistical purposes. For trade-related outward payments, including import letter-of-credit settlements and supplier invoices, no waiting period or individual licence is required; banks must simply verify the underlying commercial documentation, apply standard AML due diligence, and electronically report the transaction to the CBC. A beneficial owner or exporter may hold foreign-currency proceeds indefinitely; only an intra-EEA transfer above EUR 10,000 must be accompanied by a self-declaration of the business reason to the bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kendriki Trapeza Kyprou)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Cyprus has enacted Part VI of the Payment Services Law (Law 75(I)/2018, implementing PSD2 and Directive (EU) 2015/2366), authorising licensed Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions under the supervision of the Central Bank of Cyprus. An EU/EEA-licensed EMI may passport into Cyprus and provide IBAN accounts and cross-border payment execution for a CY Ltd entity. However, the legal constraints are material: (a) EMIs are not permitted to grant credit beyond narrowly defined payment-service credit; (b) e-money funds must be safeguarded under segregation or equivalent insurance; (c) most EMIs will not accept escrow-role accounts for franchise capital deployment or hold a statutory mandate to verify import/export documentary trade; and (d) the Central Bank of Cyprus requires any entity engaging in continuous high-value royalty collection and trade settlement to maintain the core account at a tier-1 depository institution. Therefore, an EMI may be used only as an auxiliary payment conduit for vendor payouts or low-value disbursements, while the main franchise treasury ledger must remain at a tier-1 Cypriot bank. The viability rating is 'Medium' due to the lack of lending capability, the requirement for an EEA licence, and the AML/CFT expectation that beneficial owners and directors be identified to the same standard as the bank's own compliance committee.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial Owner residing in a non-EU, non-CRS-participating, or FATF/EU high-risk jurisdiction.

Root cause: Beneficial Owner residing in a non-EU, non-CRS-participating, or FATF/EU high-risk jurisdiction.

Defense evidence: Submit the official UBO declaration under the Cyprus Register of Beneficial Owners (RBO) obligations; an apostilled and sworn copy of the passport; source-of-wealth documentation covering the full capital injection amount, including bank statements (in English) for the preceding 24 months, audited financial statements, property sale deeds, probate documents, or evidence of a real commercial activity; and a signed declaration confirming the beneficial owner is a tax resident of a jurisdiction that automatically exchanges financial information with Cyprus under CRS. Where the tax residence is in a non-CRS country, a legal opinion on economic substance and source-of-wealth is essential.
High Annual Transaction Clearance Expectations for franchise royalty streams before the bank understands the fee model.

Root cause: High Annual Transaction Clearance Expectations for franchise royalty streams before the bank understands the fee model.

Defense evidence: Provide a copy of the Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) or master franchise agreement demonstrating a transparent royalty mechanism (monthly gross revenue percentage or fixed license fee); a five-year cash-flow forecast with gross margin assumptions and supplier cost schedules; import invoices for tea, syrups, packaging, and equipment; a certificate of trademark registration from the European Union Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO); and a tax opinion from a Cypriot tax adviser confirming the VAT treatment and withholding-tax analysis of royalty outflows and inflows.
Escrow capital injection from an opaque offshore holding company or from a jurisdiction with a shell-company stereotype.

Root cause: Escrow capital injection from an opaque offshore holding company or from a jurisdiction with a shell-company stereotype.

Defense evidence: Present the complete corporate ownership chart with share registers and certificates of good standing for every intermediate entity; a legal opinion letter from the offshore provider; board resolutions of the Cyprus entity and the parent entity approving the escrow account and the capital injection; a subscribed capital statement verifying that the injected amount does not exceed the statutory aggregate nominal value; a source-of-funds file for the ultimate origin of the money (e.g., accumulated operating profits, liquidation proceeds, or an executed sale and purchase agreement); and evidence that the beneficial owner is subject to a real, substantive economic presence test.
Physical-presence failure during the KYC interview where a director is abroad and cannot travel to Cyprus.

Root cause: Physical-presence failure during the KYC interview where a director is abroad and cannot travel to Cyprus.

Defense evidence: Provide a notarised and apostilled Power of Attorney in Greek or English naming a locally licensed corporate-services provider as the account administrator; a certified copy of the director's passport; proof of residential address in the home country; a completed bank-specific e-KYC video call conducted at the branch with the director present on camera; and a board resolution authorising the POA and confirming the director's identity. If the director is a Cypriot tax resident, an in-person attendance is waived only under exceptional circumstances and with the written consent of the bank's MLCO.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the global franchise headquarters or the shareholder must be routed as a SWIFT wire transfer denominated in EUR (or USD with an FX conversion). The destination IBAN will be the Capital Escrow Account or an operational account held at a t‍ier-1 Cypriot bank. The purpose code is mandatory: for direct equity subscription use ECB purpose code 'D0101' (also reported as '30011' in the Eurosystem balance-of-payments statistical classification, representing subscribed share capital and investment fund shares); for shareholder loans use code 'D0200' (reported as '80012', representing loans and deposits). The bank will require a board resolution of the Cyprus entity approving the capital increase, a copy of the submitted Register of Beneficial Owners (RBO) return, valid CRS and FATCA documentation, and, if the injection exceeds the registered share capital, an extrajudicial resolution for the capital increase in accordance with the Cyprus Companies Law. Funds must arrive through the bank's SWIFT current-account infrastructure; Cypriot banks are direct TARGET2 participants, ensuring real-time gross settlement. There is no CBC approval requirement or waiting period, but any transaction above EUR 95,000 may trigger additional source-of-funds due diligence if the origin is a politically exposed person, a sanctioned jurisdiction, or a non-CRS partner. Cash or cash-equivalents above EUR 10,000 imported into Cyprus require a customs declaration; bank wire is the preferred and compliant rail.For supplier settlements and operational disbursements: (a) SWIFT MT103 messages are available for high-value and non-urgent cross-border payments, accompanied by the invoice and, where relevant, the import/export customs declaration and the stated purpose code (e.g., payments for tea leaf, syrup, packaging, and franchise equipment). (b) Irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP600 are recommended for containerised raw-material imports and franchise supply-chain purchases; the LC must clearly state Incoterms (such as FOB or CIF), a latest-shipment date within the Eurozone time-zone, and a set of transport documents that can be examined by Cypriot banks under UCP600 rules. (c) SEPA Credit Transfers (SCT) provide a compliant and cost-effective rail for intra-EU franchise royalty payments, management fees, and supplier payouts under EUR 500,000, with no CBC exchange-control approval beyond the standard AML screening. (d) For outbound royalties, management fees, or assembly services related to the franchise, the payment must be accompanied by the underlying agreement and, where applicable, a withholding-tax certificate or an arm's-length transfer-pricing file, so that the receiving bank is not blocked at the destination. (e) For inbound royalty collections from non-EU franchisees in MENA, Asia, or the Americas, the payer bank will initiate SWIFT MT103 Inwards with purpose code 'SOFT' (management fees and royalties), and Cypriot banks will credit the EUR or USD account automatically; incoming amounts are aggregated for balance-of-payments reporting and CBC monitoring where the value exceeds EUR 10,000. All outward payments above EUR 15,000 require the bank to hold a commercial contract or proforma invoice in the customer file, and the documentation must be produced within three business days upon request.Outright EUR/USD and EUR/CNH forwards: permitted onshore at Cypriot banks for hedging genuine commercial exposure arising from forecasted USD or CNY cash inflows. These forwards are available under ISDA/EMIR documentation or the bank's clean master agreement; no mandatory central clearing applies for non-financial counterparties below the EUR 3 billion EMIR clearing threshold, but the bank will classify the transaction as a derivative and transaction reporting under EMIR may be required at the individual trade level.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs): permissible for exotic currencies such as CNY, INR, MYR, and other Asian currencies where onshore deliverable forwards are restricted. Cypriot banks offer NDFs referencing the Chinese 11:00 a.m. CFETS fixing or other central reference rates; the NDF must be documented under a separate NDF confirmation and must evidence the underlying commercial flow. NDFs are not permitted for speculative purposes and the treasury function must present a board-approved hedge policy.Cross-Currency Swaps (FX swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps): permissible where the global franchise has USD or CNY assets/liabilities matched to EUR operating liabilities. These instruments alter the currency of cash flows and are acceptable in Cypriot banking practice when the transaction is supported by the underlying supply agreement and royalty contracts. The bank will impose net open-position limits and require consolidated treasury reports.Plain vanilla FX options: permitted for hedging future royalty receipts or franchisee payables, but require substantially higher suitability documentation, a formal board resolution, and margin support if the option is out-of-the-money. Due to the single-business-line nature of a boba-tea franchise, options are acceptable only on a covered basis and where the notional does not exceed the forecasted exposure for the following calendar quarter.FX limit orders, stop-loss orders, and OCO (one-cancels-other) orders: permitted under standard treasury execution rails at Cypriot branches, enabling efficient cash management of USD/EUR flows without being classified as derivatives. Such orders are executed on a spot-basis with instant settlement and are subject to ordinary AML documentation.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Cyprus Tax Department guidance (Interpretative Circular 10/2020, aligning with OECD TP Guidelines), a participant in an intra-group controlled transaction must prepare a Local File and Master File if it meets the EU threshold: consolidated group revenue exceeding EUR 750 million. For transactions with Cyprus-resident related parties, the documentation threshold is EUR 1,000,000 in aggregate annual value for goods and EUR 250,000 for services. There is no separate threshold in local currency other than EUR, as Cyprus uses the euro. Transactions below EUR 10,000 in aggregate are exempt from documentation.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies the Economic Substance Doctrine primarily through the Cyprus Companies Law and the Tax Administration Law, which require that a company has adequate personnel, premises, and expenditure in Cyprus relative to its business activities. Cyprus’s version of Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) was strengthened after EU Code of Conduct Group and OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Action 5 assessments. Management and control must be exercised in Cyprus for a company to be considered tax-resident; a Cyprus-registered franchise licensing company with no local management decisions, no employees, and no operational substance risks being treated as fiscally resident elsewhere or as a shell company. The Cyprus Tax Department may reclassify passive royalty and intercompany financing income where the entity has no true economic substance, disallow group deductions, and impose penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imposition of administrative penalty up to 100% of the tax assessed, plus interest at 3.5% per annum above the ECB rate, counted daily. Criminal prosecution under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law 1964 can result in a fine of up to EUR 3,000 and imprisonment of up to three years for each offence. The Tax Commissioner may also order the freezing of bank accounts and seizure of corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or knowingly permit a false tax return may be personally liable for the unpaid tax through “piercing the corporate veil” under the Cyprus Companies Law, with civil restitution orders and disqualification from serving as directors for up to five years. The court may issue a travel ban, freeze personal assets, and order imprisonment of up to three years for the director unless the director proves no constructive knowledge of the evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to taxable profits plus secondary adjustments, penalty of 10% of any under-assessed tax, and minimum default interest. Failure to submit documentation upon request results in a EUR 500 administrative fine and potential 50% upward adjustment of profits. General anti-abuse rule (GAAR) may apply to disregard the transaction entirely.

Director personal liability: Directors who endorse or implement unreasonable intercompany pricing without contemporaneous documentation are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax and penalties to the extent that the company becomes insolvent because of the adjustment. The court may issue personal liability orders and restitution of unpaid taxes, and may restrict the director’s right to leave Cyprus pending the resolution of the tax dispute.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the Cyprus company as tax-resident in the jurisdiction of effective management, resulting in loss of the 12.5% Cyprus tax regime and potential application of CFC rules in the parent jurisdiction. The Cyprus Tax Department may impose a 50% penalty on understated income, refuse relief under Cyprus tax treaties, and assess the company for the full corporate income tax plus interest on deemed income. The Registrar of Companies may strike off the company for non-compliance with diligence obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow the company to remain a shell while representing it as an operating franchise business face personal liability for fraud: restitution of diverted funds, attachment of personal property, and imprisonment of up to two years for the offence of falsifying the company’s registered particulars. A travel ban may be issued under the Companies Law where the director’s departure would prejudice the investigation.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of up to EUR 1,000,000 or 5% of the entity’s annual turnover for serious deficiencies, whichever is higher. Additional penalties include revocation of the company’s licence, court-ordered liquidation, and criminal fines of up to EUR 500,000 for senior officers. The Cyprus Unit for Combating Money Laundering (MOKAS) may freeze all business assets pending investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to report suspicious transactions or who knowingly facilitate cash layering through the franchise network are personally criminally liable under the AML law, carrying a maximum imprisonment of 15 years, seizure of personal assets, and a ban on leaving Cyprus. Parties cannot shield themselves behind the corporate entity, as the law expressly imposes criminal liability on the individuals acting for the company.
Failure to Register and Report Franchise Licence Fees

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Businesses and Trade Licensing Law of up to EUR 2,000 plus daily default fines of EUR 100 per day of unlicensed trading. VAT penalties reach 30% of the VAT due, and the company is barred from claiming input VAT offset. The Tax Department may also deny the tax deductibility of all unlicensed revenue-generating expenses.

Director personal liability: The directors who operate the franchisor without the required licence or knowingly fail to register for VAT are jointly and severally liable for the company’s VAT debt, especially where the company ceases trading to avoid collection. They may be made subject to civil recovery and to travel restrictions, and, in cases of fraudulent evasion of VAT, imprisonment of up to three years.

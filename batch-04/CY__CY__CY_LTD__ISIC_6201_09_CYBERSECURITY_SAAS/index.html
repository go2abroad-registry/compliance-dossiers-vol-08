Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate – Required from the Cyprus Fire Service (Civil Defence) for any commercial office premises. Structural Load Inspection – The leased office space must have a valid building permit and structural completion certificate issued by the District Administration Office (District Officer). Occupational Health and Safety – Must comply with the Safety and Health at Work Law (L.158(I)/2022), and obtain an Occupational Health and Safety Policy statement. For small IT firms with white-collar staff, a simple health and safety risk assessment and a first aid kit are mandatory; the registration of a Safety Officer may be required if more than 5 employees are hired.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance; electronic filing portal: ARIADNE (https://ariadne.mof.gov.cy); Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (Department of Registrar of Companies) for incorporation matters.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the Registrar of Companies transmits the HE registration data to the Tax Department, which automatically issues the 9-digit Tax Identification Code (TIC) to the Cyprus Ltd. Foreign-owned entities must verify receipt of the TIC, and if not automatically issued, submit Form IR.1 (Application for Registration for Tax Purposes) to the competent District Tax Office. Separate registrations: (1) VAT registration via Form TDF 200 within 30 days of exceeding the €15,600 taxable turnover threshold or when there are reasonable grounds to expect the threshold will be exceeded in the next 30 days; (2) Employer registration via Social Insurance Form CF.1 with the Social Insurance Services prior to hiring staff; (3) no local agent is statutorily required for non-EU shareholders, though a fiscal representative is required for non-established taxable persons registering for VAT outside the OSS regime. The TIC is required for all ARIADNE filings, customs declarations, and payroll reporting.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The Cyprus Ltd is a separate legal taxable person subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide income under the Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002). Territorial scope: dividend income and gains on the disposal of securities are exempt from income tax; profits of foreign permanent establishments are exempt where certain conditions are met; passive interest income of companies outside the ordinary course of business may fall outside the corporate income tax net following the abolition of the Special Defence Contribution on corporate dividends/interest. No pass-through eligibility applies to Cyprus limited liability companies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Cyprus Ltd is a separate legal taxable person subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide income under the Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002). Territorial scope: dividend income and gains on the disposal of securities are exempt from income tax; profits of foreign permanent establishments are exempt where certain conditions are met; passive interest income of companies outside the ordinary course of business may fall outside the corporate income tax net following the abolition of the Special Defence Contribution on corporate dividends/interest. No pass-through eligibility applies to Cyprus limited liability companies. The cyber-SaaS export business is fully within the tax net, subject to the IP Box preferential regime, and indirectly benefits from the 0% VAT export/place-of-supply rules.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not adopt the US check-the-box regime; entity classification is determined by the Companies Law. Available elections and special mechanisms: (1) Group loss relief - surrender of losses between group companies with at least 75% ownership; (2) VAT grouping - a single VAT number for related Cyprus entities; (3) IP Box election - an 80% notional deduction of qualifying profits derived from qualifying intangible assets (software patents and copyrighted software) resulting in an effective rate of approximately 2.5% on qualifying income, subject to substance and nexus ratio; (4) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) on new equity capital - automatically available, no formal election required; (5) One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for EU-wide B2C digital services reporting; (6) tonnage tax regime is not applicable to information security SaaS. No hybrid mismatch elections exist nationally; hybrid/treasury structures must be assessed under EU ATAD II.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 (taxable turnover in the preceding 12 months, or reasonable expectation of exceeding that amount within the following 30 days). For intra-EU distance sales of digital services (including SaaS) to Cyprus consumers, the unified EU-wide threshold is €10,000; if exceeded, the supplier must register in Cyprus or use the OSS. Non-established suppliers of digital/electronic services to non-taxable persons in Cyprus have no registration threshold and must register under the non-union OSS scheme or appoint a VAT fiscal representative in Cyprus, unless the service falls under B2B reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The Cyprus Ltd may apply 0% VAT in the following export scenarios: (1) physical export of goods outside the EU - zero-rated provided the goods are dispatched/transported outside the EU within 90 days of supply and the exporter retains sufficient documentary proof of removal; (2) B2B cross-border supplies of services (including cybersecurity cloud vulnerability scanning and penetration testing SaaS) to customers established outside the EU - the place of supply is outside Cyprus, thus outside the scope of Cypriot VAT (effective 0%); (3) B2C digital services supplied to non-EU consumers - outside the scope of Cyprus VAT; (4) B2C digital services supplied to EU consumers - not zero-rated; the supplier must declare VAT at the destination member state rate via the OSS or local registration. Zero-rating substantiation requires written contracts, customer non-EU status evidence (e.g., VAT ID or a signed statement), technical delivery logs/usage records, and, for goods, customs export documentation. The supplier must retain this evidence for 6 years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document/SAD submitted via the Cyprus Customs Department THESEAS system) with the MRN and exit confirmation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of goods from the EU (or combined transport document)
  • Commercial Invoice describing the goods/services and contractual terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT receipt evidencing payment from the non-EU customer
  • Written SaaS/penetration-testing services contract with the non-EU customer and proof of the customer's non-EU status (VAT identification number or signed statement)
  • EC Sales List (VIES) and dispatch records where intra-EU B2B supply is claimed
  • Delivery/acceptance certificates and technical service reports substantiating the cross-border supply of digital security services

As of tax year 2025, Cyprus does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls (CTC), SAF-T filing, or mandatory government-approved e-invoicing for B2B/B2C transactions. ViDA (VAT in the Digital Age) e-invoicing/reporting standards are expected to be transposed in subsequent years. Current mandatory obligations: (1) issue VAT-compliant invoices in accordance with Law 95(I)/2000 Article 141 et seq. and retain them for 6 years; (2) file periodic VAT returns electronically through ARIADNE; (3) submit EC Sales Lists (VIES) for intra-EU B2B supplies; (4) certified Fiscal Electronic Mechanism (FEM) device compliance applies only to retail cash transactions and is not applicable to B2B SaaS subscription invoicing via Stripe or wire transfer.

The Tax Department and Cyprus Customs conduct risk-based audits of export zero-rating claims, focusing on digital service exports and SaaS platforms with Stripe and wire-transfer revenue streams. If export evidence is missing, incomplete, or contradictory, the zero rating is retrospectively reversed and VAT is clawed back at 19% on the full consideration, with interest from the date the VAT was originally due. Knowingly issuing false export invoices, submitting forged customs declarations, or artificially routing transactions to obtain 0% VAT constitutes tax fraud under Law 95(I)/2000 and the Customs Law; penalties include administrative fines of up to twice the tax evaded, criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 2 years, director disqualification, and suspension/cancellation of the VAT registration with consequential denial of input VAT recovery. Note: Cyprus imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark above aligns with the typical inward remittance evidenced in the export file and the general transfer-pricing arm's length payment terms, not a domestic legal cap.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Cyprus applies full liberalisation of capital movements under Article 63 TFEU; there is no exchange-control ordinance, no inward or outward remittance licensing, and no restriction on non-resident ownership or repatriation of invested capital. Profit-remittance hurdles are de minimis: Cyprus imposes no withholding tax on dividends or interest paid to non-resident shareholders or lenders, distributions to EU parent companies are sheltered under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, and no Central Bank approval is required for the distribution of audited retained earnings. The only practical gate is commercial-bank AML/EDD verification of the UBO and source of funds, which is the same standard applied to domestic investors.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. As a Eurosystem member, Cyprus prescribes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export receipts. The treasury may retain USD, GBP, EUR or other freely convertible currency balances indefinitely and convert at market rates at its sole discretion. Compliance obligations are limited to (i) ECB/CBC balance-of-payments statistical reporting through bank-assigned transaction codes on each cross-border receipt, (ii) full originator and beneficiary information under EU Regulation 2015/847, and (iii) CRS/FATCA classification of remitters; none of these restrict treasury discretion.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (CBC / Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating within the European System of Central Banks and the Eurosystem; monetary policy competence rests with the European Central Bank (ECB). The CBC is the prudential supervisor of Cypriot credit institutions under the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM) and is the sole licensing authority for payment institutions and e-money institutions in the Republic.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate - a viable treasury adjunct, not a primary banking layer. Cyprus-licensed digital EMIs authorised by the CBC under the Payment Services and E-Money Laws (PSD2 transposition) may be onboarded remotely and provide segregated client-money safeguarding, EEA IBANs, and SEPA/SWIFT rails. Offshore digital EMIs are materially constrained: a third-country EMI cannot passport into Cyprus without an EEA establishment or a registered CBC agent, and safeguarding balances held abroad are outside the Cyprus Deposit Guarantee Scheme (EUR 100,000 coverage). For a Low_Risk_Standard SaaS entity, the correct architecture is to use EMIs (Stripe Treasury, Wise Business, Payoneer) exclusively as collection accelerators with automatic T+1 sweeps into the CBC-regulated primary bank; statutory capital escrow, equity injections, and credit facilities must remain with a licensed commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital subscriptions must be routed by SEPA Credit Transfer (EUR) or SWIFT MT103 (USD/GBP) with (i) the ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI or the receiving bank's mapped balance-of-payments code for inward direct investment in equity, (ii) a remittance narrative reading 'Share capital contribution - Companies Law Cap. 113' together with the company's registered name, and (iii) the remitter's CRS/FATCA classification. The CBC Desk of the receiving bank requires the board resolution approving the allotment, the share subscription agreement, the Registrar of Companies confirmation of issued capital, and a source-of-funds statement of the subscribing shareholder. Eurozone remittances settle T+0/T+1, and no capital duty is exigible on share issuance after 1 January 2022. Fractional subscription pricing should be avoided to prevent the bank from flagging the transfer as a loan rather than equity.Principal clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for global USD/EUR/GBP B2B payments (vendor licences, MSSP subcontracting, marketing services, dividends) and SEPA Credit or Instant Transfer for eurozone settlements. Each instruction must carry the CBC balance-of-payments transaction code for IT and software services and be mirrored by electronic invoices in the sales ledger to prevent documentary queries. Commercial Letters of Credit are reserved for procurement of physical security appliances: the omnibus LC is opened with Bank of Cyprus or Eurobank Cyprus against the master purchase order, typically with 90-day usance and documents-against-acceptance terms. Zero-balancing sweeping across all operating accounts centralises liquidity; intra-group settlements require signed intercompany agreements and a contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation set to withstand CBC documentary review and CRS reporting.Outright deliverable FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF) with tenors up to 12 months under an ISDA 2002 Master AgreementNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited to currencies lacking a deliverable onshore interbank marketCross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) with a EUR floating leg for long-term contracted revenue or matched external debtPurchased FX options (EUR puts against USD/GBP) for downside protection on subscription revenues, with premium accounted at fair value per IFRS 9

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate - a viable treasury adjunct, not a primary banking layer. Cyprus-licensed digital EMIs authorised by the CBC under the Payment Services and E-Money Laws (PSD2 transposition) may be onboarded remotely and provide segregated client-money safeguarding, EEA IBANs, and SEPA/SWIFT rails. Offshore digital EMIs are materially constrained: a third-country EMI cannot passport into Cyprus without an EEA establishment or a registered CBC agent, and safeguarding balances held abroad are outside the Cyprus Deposit Guarantee Scheme (EUR 100,000 coverage). For a Low_Risk_Standard SaaS entity, the correct architecture is to use EMIs (Stripe Treasury, Wise Business, Payoneer) exclusively as collection accelerators with automatic T+1 sweeps into the CBC-regulated primary bank; statutory capital escrow, equity injections, and credit facilities must remain with a licensed commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 for goods transactions; EUR 250,000 for services transactions; EUR 100,000 for other transactions (e.g., royalties, interest).

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) for companies deriving income from IP, but also general substance requirements to maintain tax residency. A Cyprus company must have adequate premises, employees, and management in Cyprus to avoid being considered tax resident elsewhere. For IP companies, the 'nexus approach' requires that R&D activities be performed in Cyprus to qualify for the IP Box regime.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded, plus interest at 1.5% per month. Criminal prosecution may result in fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for tax debts if they knowingly participate in evasion. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years may apply.
Shell Company (Lack of Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: Cyprus tax authorities may reclassify the company as tax resident elsewhere, impose penalties for non-compliance with ESR, and deny treaty benefits. Fines up to EUR 50,000 and potential criminal charges for fraudulent misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly operated a shell company. Travel bans and asset freezes can be imposed.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 1 million for companies, and up to EUR 100,000 for individuals. License revocation (if applicable) and criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned up to 5 years for AML breaches. They may also be banned from serving as directors.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties of 10% on the additional tax due, and interest at 1.5% per month. In severe cases, criminal penalties for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they approved or ignored transfer pricing adjustments. Civil restitution and potential travel bans.

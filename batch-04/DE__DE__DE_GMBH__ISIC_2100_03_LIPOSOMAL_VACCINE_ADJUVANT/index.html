Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF) oversees tax policy; the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) administers corporate tax and VAT registration. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.). ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) is the official electronic tax filing and communications portal.

E-government filing portal

After incorporation and commercial-register entry, a GmbH must submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt, generally within one month of commencing business. The Finanzamt assigns the tax number (Steuernummer) and establishes corporate tax and VAT accounts based on the information supplied. A separate USt-IdNr. application may be made to the BZSt, commonly through ELSTER or the BZSt online service; the number is used for qualifying intra-EU transactions and must be validated where required. A foreign entity without a German establishment registers with the Finanzamt competent for its German tax circumstances; a fiscal representative may be required in specified cases, including certain non-EU taxable persons. Supporting records commonly include incorporation and commercial-register documents, constitutional documents, director and beneficial-owner details, business activity and address evidence, and bank details. A German tax number or VAT ID does not itself establish a permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A German GmbH is generally a separate corporate income taxpayer and has no US-style check-the-box election to become fiscally transparent. It is generally subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), the solidarity surcharge where applicable, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Germany applies anti-hybrid and hybrid-mismatch rules, including provisions in section 4k of the Income Tax Act (Einkommensteuergesetz); these are statutory restrictions, not elective entity-classification elections. Tax-group treatment (Organschaft) may be available if statutory financial-integration and other requirements are met; it is not a check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exempts a business from VAT registration where it makes taxable supplies in Germany. The small-business scheme under section 19 of the VAT Act (Umsatzsteuergesetz) may apply if prior-calendar-year turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions; it is a VAT treatment, not a general registration threshold. Non-established taxable persons may have registration obligations from their first taxable German transaction. Reduced rates, including 7%, apply only to specified supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the EU may qualify for VAT exemption with deduction of input tax under section 4 no. 1(a), in conjunction with section 6 of the VAT Act, if the statutory export and evidence requirements are met. The supplier must establish that the goods were transported or dispatched to a third country and retain prescribed documentary or accounting evidence. Intra-EU supplies are governed by separate exemption and reporting rules and are not third-country exports. Services are not automatically zero-rated because the customer is offshore: their VAT treatment depends principally on the place-of-supply rules, the customer’s status, and any applicable exemption or special rule. A service whose place of supply is outside Germany is generally outside the scope of German VAT rather than a German zero-rated export.

  • Customs export declaration and electronic exit confirmation or other prescribed export evidence, where applicable
  • Commercial invoice, order or contract, and transport or shipping records linking the goods to the export
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or equivalent transport evidence, as applicable
  • Proof of payment and accounting records where relevant to substantiate the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a general German statutory export-VAT requirement
  • For intra-EU supplies, customer VAT identification number evidence, transport evidence, and required recapitulative-statement reporting, where applicable

Germany does not generally require real-time tax-authority clearance, API submission, or digital stamping of domestic invoices. Since 1 January 2025, businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions within the statutory scope. Issuance obligations are being phased in: transition rules generally permit other invoice formats through 2026, with extensions for qualifying issuers and transactions through 2027; structured e-invoicing is generally required from 2028, subject to statutory scope and exceptions. A compliant electronic invoice must use a structured format meeting the statutory requirements, such as EN 16931 or an accepted interoperable format. Separate rules apply to specified public-sector transactions.

The Finanzamt may deny or recover an export exemption or input-tax deduction if statutory eligibility or evidence requirements are not met, assess underpaid VAT and interest, and correct prior assessments within applicable limitation periods. Intentional or reckless tax understatement can trigger administrative penalties or criminal liability under the Fiscal Code (Abgabenordnung) and related law. False export evidence may also give rise to customs, document-fraud, or other criminal consequences. There is no general export-specific penalty schedule or automatic clawback period independent of the ordinary statutory assessment and limitation rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible, and Germany generally permits current-account and capital-account payments and transfers under EU law. There is no general exchange-control approval requirement for a GmbH to receive foreign investment, pay foreign suppliers, or remit dividends. Transfers remain subject to bank AML and sanctions screening, corporate-law distribution and capital-maintenance rules, tax withholding and documentation requirements, and any applicable EU or German restrictive measures. Dividends to non-residents may require German withholding-tax processing and treaty or EU relief documentation. Cross-border payment and balance reporting to the Bundesbank may apply under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG), Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV), and Bundesbank reporting rules; reporting thresholds and forms should be checked for the transaction date and transaction category.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no general statutory deadline requiring exporters to repatriate export proceeds and no mandatory foreign-currency conversion ratio. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign currency or held abroad, subject to applicable tax, accounting, sanctions, contractual, and Bundesbank statistical reporting obligations. Banks may request export contracts, customs records, invoices, shipping evidence, and payment-purpose details.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany’s central bank and administers statistical reporting obligations for cross-border payments and positions. Germany has no separate general foreign-exchange administration. The European Central Bank sets monetary policy for the euro area; the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises German financial institutions and relevant regulated activities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine EUR collections and payments, conditional on the provider’s German-market permissions, eligible customer profile, and acceptance of the biotechnology activity. An EU electronic money institution is not a substitute for a German bank account for every corporate, capital-contribution, credit, cash-management, or letter-of-credit need. Safeguarding of e-money funds is not equivalent to a bank deposit guarantee. Offshore digital EMIs may decline high-risk life-sciences customers or restrict countries, currencies, payment types, and transaction sizes; verify the provider’s regulatory status, safeguarding arrangements, correspondent access, and permitted services.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed €5 million for goods or €500,000 for services; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds €500 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies substance-over-form principles; no specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but requires genuine economic activity to avoid anti-abuse rules (e.g., CFC rules, §42 AO). A GmbH must have real substance (office, employees, decision-making) to be recognized for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years; fines up to €50,000; additional penalties for aggravated cases (up to 10 years).

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal prosecution, civil restitution of unpaid taxes, travel bans, and disqualification from management.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment by tax authorities; penalty up to 25% of additional tax assessed; interest on underpayments.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for negligence if they fail to ensure proper documentation and arm's length pricing.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, imposition of CFC rules, and penalties up to €25,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and penalties if the company is deemed a sham; travel bans and criminal charges possible.
AML Violations (Geldwäschegesetz)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for companies; imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; revocation of licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, travel bans, and prohibition from holding management positions.

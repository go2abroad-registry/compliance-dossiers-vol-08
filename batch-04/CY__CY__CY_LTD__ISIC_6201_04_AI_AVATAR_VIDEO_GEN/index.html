Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Tax Department / Department of Taxation (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance; electronic filing portal: TAXISnet (https://taxinet.mof.gov.cy). VAT is administered by the VAT Service within the Tax Department under the Commissioner of Taxation.

E-government filing portal

The Cyprus private limited company is first incorporated with the Registrar of Companies (companies register code HE77); the Certificate of Incorporation with the HE number serves as the corporate base identifier. Following incorporation, the company must enrol with the Tax Department for Corporation Tax and VAT. Registration for Corporation Tax is due within 60 days of commencement of business; the Tax Department issues the Tax Identification Number (TIN). VAT registration is due when taxable supplies in Cyprus exceed EUR 15,600 per annum, or immediately (no threshold) for non-established persons making taxable supplies in Cyprus. Applications are lodged through TAXISnet together with the Certificate of Incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, registered office certificate, director appointments, and beneficial ownership declarations. A company is tax-resident in Cyprus if its central management and control is exercised in Cyprus; non-resident companies with a Cyprus permanent establishment must obtain a TIN for their Cyprus source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Cyprus tax-resident LTD is taxed as a separate corporate person on worldwide income at the uniform 12.5% corporation tax rate. Non-resident companies are taxed on Cyprus-source income only. No pass-through eligibility exists for a private limited company. For AI avatar video generation software, licence and royalty income qualifies for the Cyprus IP Box regime (Section 20A, Income Tax Law Cap. 302) with an 80% deduction on qualifying IP profits, subject to OECD nexus ratio substantiation.

Available election forms

Cyprus has no US-style check-the-box election; all private limited companies are mandatorily taxed at entity level. Statutory elections available to a software developer include: (i) the IP Box election under Section 20A of the Income Tax Law (Cap. 302) with nexus-ratio documentation; (ii) group loss relief election between Cyprus group companies; (iii) tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping affiliates; (iv) notional interest deduction (NID) election on qualifying new equity; (v) factual structuring of central management and control to establish or confirm Cypriot tax residence. EU ATAD hybrid mismatch and CFC rules are implemented in domestic law and are mandatory, with no elective opt-out for hybrid mismatch neutrality.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic supply of taxable goods and services in Cyprus: EUR 15,600 of annual taxable turnover (Article 12, VAT Law). Intra-Community distance sales and cross-border B2C digital services (electronically supplied services): EUR 10,000 per calendar year threshold for the One Stop Shop (OSS) scheme; exceeding it triggers OSS registration and VAT accounting in customer member states. Non-established foreign suppliers have no registration threshold and must register from the first euro of taxable supplies made in Cyprus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, the 0% export rate applies where the goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU and the exporter holds documentary proof of export before the VAT return deadline (Articles 69, 71 and 72, VAT Law). Intra-Community supply of goods is zero-rated where the recipient has a valid EU VAT identification number and transport to another member state is evidenced. For AI avatar video generation SaaS (electronically supplied services), zero-rating is replaced by place-of-supply outside the scope of Cyprus VAT: B2B supplies to EU customers fall under reverse charge in the customer's member state (Article 47(2), VAT Law); B2B and B2C supplies to non-EU recipients are outside Cyprus VAT because the place of supply is outside the EU; B2C supplies to EU consumers are taxable through the OSS scheme at the consumer's member state rate. The supplier must maintain VIES validation of the customer's EU VAT number, the written contract, and payment/billing/IP evidence supporting the place-of-supply determination.

  • Cyprus Customs electronically lodged Export Declaration (SAD / Export Summary Declaration through the EU AES and Cyprus Customs system)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical dispatch outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice and underlying contract or purchase order describing the goods or the software licensing and delivery terms
  • Proof of payment and inward remittance evidence (SWIFT messages, bank credit advices) where applicable
  • Recipient's valid EU VAT identification number and VIES screening record for intra-Community supplies
  • For AI SaaS B2B export claims: signed service agreement, place-of-supply questionnaire, and platform-generated records confirming customer establishment outside Cyprus

Cyprus has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, state-driven API reporting, or digital stamping system for B2B or B2C invoices. A compliant VAT invoice must be issued within 30 days of the taxable supply (Article 106, VAT Law). E-invoicing in UBL/EDI/PEPPOL formats is permissible for private-sector transactions and required for transactions with Cypriot public entities under EU Directive 2014/55/EU. VAT returns and VIES recapitulative statements are filed electronically via TAXISnet; the VIES statement of intra-Community supplies is due by the 15th day of the month following the reporting month. Platform-level DAC7 and OECD MDR reporting obligations apply to SaaS marketplace operators, but no transaction-level tax clearance mandate is in force.

Cyprus law imposes no fixed foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export or SaaS proceeds; export invoices may be retained offshore without statutory clawback solely on timing grounds. The Tax Department nevertheless rigorously audits zero-rated and outside-scope claims; the exporter must maintain complete substantiation for six years from the end of the relevant accounting year. Where zero-rating or outside-scope treatment is unsupported, the VAT Commissioner issues an assessment for unpaid VAT together with statutory interest, administrative fines and daily default penalties under Articles 107 and 118 of the VAT Law. Fraudulent export claims or forged customs or customer documentation attract criminal liability with imprisonment of up to two years and unlimited fines, retrospective dismantling of prior VAT returns, disallowance of input VAT recovery, and — where relevant — corporation tax reassessment. The voluntary disclosure framework allows penalty mitigation where errors are corrected before audit notification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus adopted the euro on 1 January 2008 and is a full member of the Eurozone. The euro is a freely convertible global reserve currency; there is no exchange control regime applicable to current or capital account transactions. As an EU Member State, Cyprus is bound by the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) Articles 63-65 on free movement of capital and by the single-market 'golden rule' prohibiting restrictions between Member States and third countries. The historical Exchange Control Law (Cap. 143, amended over time) remains formally on the statute book but its operational controls were dismantled upon EU accession (1 May 2004) and euro adoption; the emergency capital controls introduced in March 2013 during the domestic banking crisis were fully expired and dismantled by April 2015. Consequently, both current-account items (SaaS subscription receipts, export of software services, licensing royalties, management fees) and capital-account items (equity injections, shareholder loans, dividends, M&A payments) are unrestricted. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is lawful with no required prior permission: a Cyprus tax-resident company paying dividends to non-resident shareholders is subject to 0% dividend withholding tax when the company is not carrying on business through a Cyprus permanent establishment, provided standard corporate formalities are maintained (audited accounts, board resolution, tax compliance status). Practical hurdles are not regulatory but bank-level: AML source-of-funds and source-of-wealth documentation for inbound capital, enhanced due diligence for shareholders resident in high-risk jurisdictions, and EU-wide sanctions screening that prohibits payments to Russian/Belarusian and other listed counter-parties. No material convertibility bottleneck exists for EUR/USD or EUR/GBP flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Republic of Cyprus imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export and SaaS revenues (including USD and GBP subscription receipts collected via Stripe/Paddle and settled to the Cyprus entity) may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency nostro or multi-currency accounts at Cyprus-licensed banks, or freely converted to EUR at market rate. The only obligation is statistical: banks report balance-of-payments and International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) data to the Central Bank of Cyprus, and corporate clients must provide the underlying contract or invoice reference at the time of credit/debit so that the transaction is correctly classified. There is no deadline for conversion, no penalty for holding foreign currency, and no restriction on invoicing AI video generation services in USD, EUR, or any other currency (subject only to EU sanctions and EU Digital Services Act content-compliance frameworks).

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), acting as the national monetary and financial stability authority within the Eurosystem and under the European Central Bank (ECB); responsible for the licensing and prudential supervision of credit institutions, electronic money institutions (EMIs, Law 81(I)/2012), and payment institutions (Payment Services Law transposing PSD2), and for the enforcement of AML/CFT obligations on Cypriot banks and regulated non-bank payment providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for receivables concentration (passported EU EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Multitude/Multibanco-style platforms can legally open accounts for the Cyprus entity via EU passporting) and LOW-MEDIUM for primary treasury settlement, capital escrow, or bank-certainty functions. Cyprus-licensed EMIs may issue e-money and execute payment transactions but may not take deposits from the public or provide lending/interest-bearing treasury sweep products. An overseas (non-EU) EMI cannot serve the Cypriot corporate without establishing an EU branch or licensed subsidiary and notifying the Central Bank of Cyprus through the single point of contact under PSD2/EMD2; a US-only fintech (non-EU) therefore has no direct passport into Cyprus. For an AIGC SaaS exporter with low counterparty risk, Stripe/Paddle act as merchant-of-record settlement rails; the fintech EMI account is viable for disbursements and foreign-currency float management but the formal capital-account architecture (shareholder equity injection, dividend remittance, statutory records) must sit in a Tier-1 domestic bank account. The Central Bank of Cyprus will not authorise a Cyprus EMI for pure treasury-holding purposes without a bona fide e-money issuance business plan.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a non-resident shareholder injecting capital into the Cypriot entity: (a) EU/EEA shareholders use a SEPA Credit Transfer to the Cyprus IBAN (country code CY + 2 check digits + BBAN for the relevant bank, e.g., Bank of Cyprus 'CY 62 0020 0125 ...') with the remittance reference 'Share Capital Contribution' or 'Shareholders Loan per Facility Agreement dated [date]'; (b) non-EU shareholders use SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) for the principal, with an MT202 cover sent by the remitting bank to the Cyprus bank's correspondent; the settlement currency may be EUR or USD, with optional conversion per standing instruction. No Exchange Control approval is required, and no central-bank purpose code is mandated; however, the remittance information (SWIFT field 70) must truthfully describe the transaction nature (e.g., 'Allotment of [N] ordinary shares in accordance with board resolution dated [date]' or 'Disbursement under loan agreement dated [date]'). The receiving bank will require: board minutes approving the share allotment or loan drawdown, Form HE1 (return of allotment) or updated register of members filed with the Cyprus Department of Registrar of Companies, the executed share subscription/loan agreement, and source-of-funds evidence (the remitting account bank statement). For related-party loans, transfer-pricing documentation must evidence arm's length terms; Cyprus applies the EU/ATAD interest-limitation rule (non-deductible excess borrowing costs beyond EUR 3,000,000) and anti-hybrid rules, and interest paid to non-resident lenders not operating through a Cyprus permanent establishment is exempt from Cyprus withholding tax at 0%. Inbound credits exceeding EUR 100,000 may trigger enhanced due diligence and automatic balance-of-payments / ITRS reporting by the Cypriot bank; no threshold triggers mandatory capital controls or pre-authorisation.Operational clearing rails for the AIGC SaaS company: (a) SEPA Credit Transfer for all EEA/EU suppliers (cloud compute, GPU infrastructure, legal, accounting) using IBAN-to-IBAN with D+1 settlement; (b) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA suppliers (US/APAC AI hosting, GPU vendors, licensors, subcontractors) with invoice number and contract reference in field 70; (c) MT101 customer-transfer initiation for recurring bulk treasury sweeps to a concentration account in EUR/USD; (d) commercial documentary Letter of Credit (MT700) issued by Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank for high-value physical hardware or infrastructure procurement, with a confirming bank in the vendor country when required; (e) Paddle/Stripe settlement inflows are received as a consolidated merchant-of-record payout (EUR or USD) and swept automatically to the operational account; no cross-border licence is needed for outward payments. All outward non-EEA payments are screened against EU consolidated sanctions lists and, for USD-cleared payments, OFAC screening via correspondent banks; each payment must reconcile to an underlying signed contract or invoice. Dividend outbound remittance to shareholders is executed by SEPA/SWIFT after the board resolution, audited or management accounts, and confirmation of no Cyprus tax due (0% withholding tax for non-resident shareholders absent a Cyprus PE). No capital-export authorisation is required for trade payments, dividends, or service fees; only standard AML verification applies.Deliverable outright EUR/USD FX forward (spot + forward points) traded with a Cyprus-licensed bank or CySEC-regulated investment firm under an ISDA 2002 or bank FX master agreement, with tenor matched to expected USD receivable conversion windows; collateral/margin may be required.FX Swap (EUR/USD or GBP/USD) for short-dated rolling liquidity conversion, executed via the bank's electronic FX platform for same-day/next-day settlement.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) for restricted or non-fully-convertible currencies where the company has commercial exposure (e.g., USD/CNY, USD/INR, USD/TRY), with notional-referenced settlement in EUR/USD and margin collateral under an ISDA CSA.Cross-currency swap (EUR/USD) for long-tenor hedging of shareholder loans, capital lease obligations, or significant capex denominated in USD; available from Tier-1 Cypriot banks and international counterparties authorised by CySEC.Vanilla FX options (purchased options only, no short options without comprehensive dealer approval) to establish a minimum EUR/USD conversion floor while preserving upside from favourable rate movements; premium paid upfront and documented under MiFID II suitability assessment.FX participating or zero-cost-collared structures where confirmed by the bank's product governance and the client's treasury policy, provided the company passes the appropriateness/suitability test for professional clients.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH for receivables concentration (passported EU EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Multitude/Multibanco-style platforms can legally open accounts for the Cyprus entity via EU passporting) and LOW-MEDIUM for primary treasury settlement, capital escrow, or bank-certainty functions. Cyprus-licensed EMIs may issue e-money and execute payment transactions but may not take deposits from the public or provide lending/interest-bearing treasury sweep products. An overseas (non-EU) EMI cannot serve the Cypriot corporate without establishing an EU branch or licensed subsidiary and notifying the Central Bank of Cyprus through the single point of contact under PSD2/EMD2; a US-only fintech (non-EU) therefore has no direct passport into Cyprus. For an AIGC SaaS exporter with low counterparty risk, Stripe/Paddle act as merchant-of-record settlement rails; the fintech EMI account is viable for disbursements and foreign-currency float management but the formal capital-account architecture (shareholder equity injection, dividend remittance, statutory records) must sit in a Tier-1 domestic bank account. The Central Bank of Cyprus will not authorise a Cyprus EMI for pure treasury-holding purposes without a bona fide e-money issuance business plan.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate gross value of controlled transactions in a fiscal year; a Local File is mandatory above this threshold. A Cyprus Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated global revenue exceeds EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not maintain stand-alone Cayman-type Economic Substance Regulations, but requires genuine economic substance as a condition for tax residency, DTA benefits, and compliance with the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD). A Cyprus Ltd must hold board meetings in Cyprus, have an appropriate number of employees and adequate office premises, and demonstrate that income-generating strategic decisions are managed in Cyprus. Pure letter-box companies are presumptively treated as managed and controlled elsewhere and may lose Cyprus tax residence or be denied treaty protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Additional tax of up to 100% of the understated tax, statutory interest on unpaid amounts, administrative fines of up to EUR 50,000, and criminal prosecution under the Cyprus Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where directors caused or connived in the evasion; civil restitution of the company's tax debt, travel bans in respect of unpaid judgments, disqualification from acting as a director for up to 5 years, and imprisonment for up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of corporate residence or denial of DTA benefits; application of Cypriot GAAR and ATAD rules with recomputation of tax plus penalties; potential strike-off by the Registrar of Companies.

Director personal liability: Directors who submit false substance declarations are exposed to civil misrepresentation claims, personal repayment orders, director disqualification, travel restrictions, and criminal sanction for making false statutory declarations.
AML / KYC Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Law, confiscation of funds, cancellation of related commercial registrations, and criminal referral to the Attorney General.

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers and board members may be criminally liable for consent or neglect; personal fines, clawback of profits, imprisonment up to 5 years, and orders disqualifying the person from managing companies in Cyprus.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment to taxable income, additional 12.5% corporate tax, statutory interest on the adjustment, and administrative penalties of up to EUR 50,000 for missing documentation under the Cyprus TP legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly prepare false TP documentation can be held personally liable for intentional concealment; the court may impose civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for false documents in material particulars.

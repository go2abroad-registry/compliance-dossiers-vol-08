Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Not accepted for the GmbH formation of an armored submarine fiber optic cable manufacturing business. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office – for the EU VAT Identification Number, EU cross-border clearances, and EU VAT refunds; local Finanzamt (regional tax office) – for the statutory Steuernummer, Corporation Tax (Körperschaftsteuer), Trade Tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT assessments; official electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must register with the competent local Finanzamt by filing the VAT registration questionnaire using Form USt 1 TG (available via ELSTER or paper) before commencing taxable supplies; the Finanzamt issues a nine-digit Steuernummer for VAT, corporation tax, and trade tax; the separate Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID) is then issued by BZSt under § 27a UStG after verification; a permanent establishment or a fiscal representative may be required where the foreign entity has no domestic seat, place of management, or other fixed establishment in Germany

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – a GmbH is an unlimited tax-resident corporation under § 1 KStG (Körperschaftsteuerpflicht) and an entrepreneur under § 2 UStG (VAT) from its seat or place of management in Germany, subject to corporation tax at 15%, solidarity surcharge, trade tax, and VAT at entity level without partnership pass-through — A foreign entity must register with the competent local Finanzamt by filing the VAT registration questionnaire using Form USt 1 TG (available via ELSTER or paper) before commencing taxable supplies; the Finanzamt issues a nine-digit Steuernummer for VAT, corporation tax, and trade tax; the separate Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID) is then issued by BZSt under § 27a UStG after verification; a permanent establishment or a fiscal representative may be required where the foreign entity has no domestic seat, place of management, or other fixed establishment in Germany

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a GmbH is an unlimited tax-resident corporation under § 1 KStG (Körperschaftsteuerpflicht) and an entrepreneur under § 2 UStG (VAT) from its seat or place of management in Germany, subject to corporation tax at 15%, solidarity surcharge, trade tax, and VAT at entity level without partnership pass-through

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box regime. Statutory elections relevant to a GmbH include: (1) Organschaft (VAT and income/trade tax fiscal unity) under § 14 KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, enabling pooling of VAT, corporation tax, and trade tax where the qualifying financial and organisational integration tests are met; (2) small-entrepreneur status election under § 19 UStG (exemption up to €25,000 turnover from 2025; €22,000 in 2024); (3) option to tax immovable property under § 9 UStG (renouncement of the § 4 Nr. 9a UStG exemption); (4) trade tax loss-absorption and apportionment elections under § 29 GewStG and § 15a EStG analogues; and (5) alignment/transparency elections under the ATAD-Umsetzungsgesetz and Anti-Steuermissbrauchsgesetz (§ 4k EStG, § 12 Abs. 1 KStG) to neutralise hybrid mismatch outcomes in cross-border structures

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary registration threshold for incorporated businesses; VAT registration is mandatory for every entrepreneur irrespective of turnover once any taxable supply occurs, with the sole de minimis exception for small entrepreneurs under § 19 UStG where turnover in the calendar year does not exceed €25,000 (from 2025; €22,000 in 2024) and estimated turnover in the following year remains below €100,000

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies under § 4 Nr. 1 lit. a and § 6 UStG for physical deliveries where goods leave Germany for a third-country destination within three months of dispatch or transport; the supplier must obtain a conclusive proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) under § 10 UStDV through the national ATLAS customs electronic clearance system – including the export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) and customs release – and evidence that the recipient is a third-country purchaser; undersea submarine fiber cable manufactured in Germany qualifies only upon actual customs release and physical exit from the EU territory to a non-EU buyer; related offshore services and installation services may benefit from zero-rating under reverse-charge place-of-supply rules (§ 3a Abs. 2, § 3a Abs. 4 Nr. 3 UStG) where supplied to a non-EU business customer, supported by the § 53a UStDV declaration on the invoice; trial samples and warranty replacements follow the specific EU/German valuation rules and require adequate justification

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) transmitted through ATLAS/PLS with an assigned Movement Reference Number (MRN) and verified customs release proof
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing departure from Germany and physical exit from the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate – bank SWIFT MT103 confirmation or equivalent foreign-exchange receipt evidence substantiating repatriation of the export proceeds to the German GmbH
  • Commercial invoice bearing the complete § 6 UStG export-recognition statement and the § 53a UStDV service-supply declaration where applicable
  • If intra-EU dispatch rather than third-country export: EC Sales List and Intrastat declaration data must be substituted and reconciled

Structured e-invoicing is mandatory: B2G invoices under § 4 VgV and § 13 UStG-DV require XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x formats; B2B e-invoicing duty starts 01.01.2025 under the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz) with phased compliance – entities exceeding €800,000 turnover face fines from 01.01.2027 and all taxpayers from 01.01.2028; there is no real-time transactional fiscal clearance or state digital stamping comparable to Italy's SDI; instead taxpayers transmit periodic VAT returns, EC Sales Lists (Zusammenfassende Meldung), and Intrastat declarations via ELSTER; cross-border B2B services require the § 53a UStDV invoice declaration; recipients must be able to receive structured electronic invoices in XRechnung/ZUGFeRD or EDIFACT formats

Deficient or fraudulent export substantiation triggers retrospective revocation of zero-rating with reassessment of VAT plus interest under § 233a AO at 6% per annum (0.5% per month) from the original due date, a late-payment surcharge under § 240 AO at 1% per month up to the arrears amount, and penalty fines under § 26a UStG of up to €5,000 per infringing invoice; intentionally or recklessly falsified export proofs constitute tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment up to five years or a fine, or up to ten years for aggravated cases; voluntary disclosure under § 371 AO may afford mitigated immunity if made before detection; German tax and customs authorities (Außenprüfung under § 193 AO, ZKA – Zollkriminalamt – and the IuK units) frequently audit cross-border cable exports, may claw back refunded input VAT, and apply a 10-year limitation period where objective input-tax loss and passive fraud are established under the BFH/ECJ doctrine; export documentation must be retained for the statutory 10-year retention period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates full euro convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Eurozone membership and Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union guarantee unrestricted capital movements to and from third countries. No exchange-control authorizations, surrender requirements, or central-bank approvals apply to dividend remittances, profit repatriation, or shareholder loans. The only practical barriers are German corporate-law creditor protection tests (Secs. 30 and 31 of the German Limited Liability Companies Act – GmbHG), tax withholding compliance, and the AWV statistical declaration thresholds. Dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to a 25% withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 26.375%) unless reduced or exempted under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% if the parent holds at least 10% for 12 months) or a double-taxation treaty (e.g., 5% under the 2006 U.S.-Germany DTA, subject to the limitation-on-benefits clause).

Export proceeds surrender rules

German law imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. A German exporter may freely retain foreign currency abroad and select any settlement currency. The sole obligation is statistical: any inbound or outbound payment to or from a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 (or aggregate payments of EUR 12,500 or more per calendar month toward one non-resident) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within seven days using the official AWV reporting form (Z1/Z3 category). This declaration is purely statistical, does not constitute a permit, and carries no conversion or repatriation timeline.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of Germany), acting together with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking conduct supervision under the German Banking Act (KWG) and with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) for foreign trade and investment screening under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG). For significant German credit institutions, the European Central Bank (SSM) exercises direct supervisory authority. The Bundesbank is the competent collection agent for all statistical foreign-exchange and cross-border payment reporting required by the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rated LOW for primary treasury, capital escrow, and LC settlement; MEDIUM for ancillary expense flows and payroll. Legal constraints: (a) the statutory minimum capital must be deposited with a credit institution within the EEA and at the free disposal of the managing director under Sec. 7(2) GmbHG; a pure e-money institution (EMI) licensed under PSD2 or the E-Money Directive is not a CRR credit institution (Art. 4(1)(a) CRR) and cannot issue the capital deposit certificate required for commercial-register filing; (b) government-issued letters of credit are negotiated through bank-confirmed MT700/MT760 instruments that EMIs lack the licence and correspondent network to support; (c) a BaFin supervised account at a full-service bank is the only accepted vehicle for the notarial deposit confirmation; (d) EMIs may be used via PSD2 open-banking interfaces for treasury dashboard visibility, but the legal account provider must remain a German credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital injections from EEA shareholders are routed via SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) or SEPA Instant, with the mandatory purpose code 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / EINLAGE GMBH' and the notary file number in the Verwendungszweck field; settlement occurs through T2 in central bank money. Non-EEA inbound capital is routed by SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) using the German bank's correspondent network, with field 70 reading 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO COMPANY CAPITAL – FREE DISPOSAL OF MANAGING DIRECTOR PER SEC. 7(2) GMBHG'; funds must arrive in the capital escrow account before the notary files the commercial register submission. After registration, the account is converted to an operational account. All cross-border inbound transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within 7 days (AWV statistics form Z3); the bank will additionally request the notarial certificate confirming free disposal before permitting any withdrawal or pledge of the capital balance.Payments to EEA suppliers for raw materials (optical fibers, steel wire armoring, copper conductors, polyethylene jackets) settle through SEPA Credit Transfer and T2 in EUR with Incoterms-referenced invoices. Non-EEA disbursements (USD, GBP, JPY, CNY) use SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV via the German bank's direct correspondent network. For the primary revenue rail — government Letters of Credit — the issuing bank's LC is advised and, if required, confirmed by the German bank under UCP 600 through SWIFT MT700/MT707; presentation of documents (commercial invoice, negotiable bill of lading, insurance certificate, certificate of origin) is made through the confirming bank, and the negotiated proceeds are credited to the operational account. Supplier advance payments and milestone drawdowns may be structured via MT799 pre-advice and documentary collections (URC 522). Any outbound transfer to a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 is subject to the Bundesbank AWV statistical reporting; all underlying trade documents must be retained by the bank for invoicing and FX position verification.Plain-vanilla EUR/USD and EUR/GBP forward contracts executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a German Rahmenvertrag für Finanztermingeschäfte with a domestic credit institution; corporate exemption from EMIR mandatory clearing applies as a non-financial counterparty (NFC-) below the clearing thresholds, but all derivatives must be reported to an ESMA-registered trade repository within one business day.EUR/USD and EUR/GBP cross-currency swaps for long-tenor hedging of government LC disbursements and ECA-backed project financing.Zero-cost collars and plain-vanilla FX options (European style) to hedge procurement cost volatility without upfront premiums.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (EURIBOR/SOFR basis) used exclusively to align the currency and interest-rate profile of buyer-financed subsea cable contracts.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) but only for settlement of restricted or low-liquidity currencies (e.g., THB, IDR, BRL) transacted with a bank-dealer in London or New York, documented under the ISDA 1998 FX and Currency Option Definitions, and limited to the notional amount of the underlying trade.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rated LOW for primary treasury, capital escrow, and LC settlement; MEDIUM for ancillary expense flows and payroll. Legal constraints: (a) the statutory minimum capital must be deposited with a credit institution within the EEA and at the free disposal of the managing director under Sec. 7(2) GmbHG; a pure e-money institution (EMI) licensed under PSD2 or the E-Money Directive is not a CRR credit institution (Art. 4(1)(a) CRR) and cannot issue the capital deposit certificate required for commercial-register filing; (b) government-issued letters of credit are negotiated through bank-confirmed MT700/MT760 instruments that EMIs lack the licence and correspondent network to support; (c) a BaFin supervised account at a full-service bank is the only accepted vehicle for the notarial deposit confirmation; (d) EMIs may be used via PSD2 open-banking interfaces for treasury dashboard visibility, but the legal account provider must remain a German credit institution.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – national authority for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.); operational e-portal 'ELSTER' (www.elster.de) for tax registrations, VAT returns, and advance notifications; local Finanzamt (tax office) is the competent assessment and audit authority.

E-government filing portal

Foreign or newly incorporated GmbH must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt before commencing business; the Finanzamt assigns a Steuernummer (tax number) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration; upon registration, the BZSt subsequently issues the USt-IdNr. under Section 15a of the German VAT Administration Regulation; a GmbH without domestic seat/management may be required to appoint a domestic tax representative under Section 80a of the German Fiscal Code (AO).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (GmbH is a separate legal entity subject to unlimited German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); no pass-through eligibility; tax residence in Germany if seat or place of effective management is in Germany, exposing worldwide income to German taxation subject to DTT relief). — Foreign or newly incorporated GmbH must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt before commencing business; the Finanzamt assigns a Steuernummer (tax number) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration; upon registration, the BZSt subsequently issues the USt-IdNr. under Section 15a of the German VAT Administration Regulation; a GmbH without domestic seat/management may be required to appoint a domestic tax representative under Section 80a of the German Fiscal Code (AO).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (GmbH is a separate legal entity subject to unlimited German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); no pass-through eligibility; tax residence in Germany if seat or place of effective management is in Germany, exposing worldwide income to German taxation subject to DTT relief).

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Germany; the mandatory tax classification is entity-level. Relevant elections include: VAT fiscal unity ('Organschaft') under Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement and financial/economic/organisational integration; trade tax apportionment elections under Section 35 of the German Trade Tax Act; loss carry-back/carry-forward elections under Section 10d EStG; and limited hybrid-mismatch arrangements are mandatorily counteracted under ATAD provisions, not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold triggers VAT registration for a German GmbH; registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction, supply, or intra-Community acquisition, regardless of turnover. The Section 19 UStG small-business threshold (EUR 22,000 prior-year / EUR 50,000 current-year) is a liability and invoicing relief, not a registration exemption, and in practical effect does not waive the registration obligation for a GmbH undertaking commercial business activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Section 4 No. 1a UStG in conjunction with Section 6 UStG for physical dispatches of goods (e.g., polyhedral RPG dice) from Germany to non-EU third territories, provided the goods leave the EU customs territory and transport/export evidence (Ausfuhrnachweis) is produced; directly related ancillary services (e.g., freight, handling) can also qualify as tax-free if they are expressly linked to a tax-exempt export supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Zollanmeldung via ATLAS/ECS)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR international consignment note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment confirmation for export proceeds (for substantiation under German foreign trade reporting practice)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) under Section 6 UStG

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. A phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime commenced on 1 January 2025 under Section 14 UStG (Growth Opportunities Act): all domestic resident-to-resident B2B invoices must be transmitted electronically in standardized formats such as XRechnung, ZUGFeRD-compatible XML, or EDI; no API clearance is required; additional transaction reporting obligations for domestic and cross-border B2B supplies are scheduled to be introduced progressively by 2028 under EU ViDA implementation.

Export zero-rating is closely audited by the Finanzamt and customs authorities. If the export documentation is incomplete or false, zero-rating is retroactively denied and VAT becomes due; assessment periods generally extend up to 10 years under Section 169 AO for tax evasion, plus late-payment interest under Section 233a AO. Intentional or fraudulent misuse of export VAT relief triggers criminal tax evasion liability under Section 370 AO with penalties of up to five years' imprisonment or fines, and up to ten years for particularly serious cases, together with full clawback of input VAT and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany offers unrestricted convertibility for both current and capital account transactions, consistent with European Union free-movement-of-capital principles (Article 63 TFEU). No general exchange control license is required for payment receipt, profit remittance, loan servicing, or equity distribution to non-resident shareholders. Profit repatriation from a GmbH is permitted without central bank approval, subject only to corporate income tax, withholding tax at 26.375% (including solidarity surcharge) or treaty-reduced rates, and FATCA/CRS informational filings. Capital contributions from non-EU shareholders may pass through German bank accounts with routine AML verification and AWV statistical reporting for payments exceeding EUR 12,500.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender of export proceeds is imposed by Germany. A German exporter may invoice in any freely convertible currency, retain foreign currency balances in domestic or foreign accounts, convert at market rates, or use proceeds for onward payments without mandatory conversion into EUR. Export receipts are not subject to a defined repatriation window or conversion ratio. AWV reporting thresholds apply only for balance-of-payments statistical completeness, requiring German banks to report cross-border transactions above EUR 12,500 to Deutsche Bundesbank; this does not entail any surrender, conversion, or liquidity retention obligation.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), Frankfurt am Main, with Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) exercising prudential and conduct supervision over banks and financial institutions; cross-border statistical reporting obligations are administered by Deutsche Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Medium feasibility with meaningful legal constraints. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For initial GmbH share capital (Stammeinlage) from a non-German shareholder, the subscription amount must be wired via SWIFT MT103 with clear remittance information: 'Stammeinlage - [Shareholder Name] - [GmbH name]' and a German payment-purpose code denoting equity contribution (e.g., '0121' under the intercompany transaction category). The wire must be credited to a German account in the local bank's escrow ledger; proof of credit (Bankbescheinigung) is delivered to the notary for commercial register filing. Amounts exceeding EUR 12,500 additionally trigger Bundesbank AWV statistical reporting which the receiving bank files. For secondary capital increases, use purpose code '3M60/3M63' as appropriate to ensure proper classification; avoid using general goods-payment codes. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (CIT) plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on CIT (effective +0.825 percentage points) plus Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at municipal Hebesatz; combined effective corporate tax burden typically 27.6% to 32.0% (e.g., 27.6% at Hebesatz 400%; 31.7% at Hebesatz 490% Munich).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the 25% tax). Reduced to 0% for qualifying corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (≥10% direct holding for 12 continuous months) and under certain DTAs (e.g., Germany–USA DTA, ≥10% voting shares for 12 months); other treaty rates typically range from 5% to 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR. Mandatory contemporaneous Local File/Master File documentation is required under the German Transfer Pricing Documentation Act (GAufzV) for cross-border business relationships with related parties exceeding EUR 5,000,000 annually for goods transactions and EUR 500,000 annually for services, management fees, and intangibles. Documentation must be submitted within 60 days of a formal tax-audit request.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not impose a standalone Economic Substance Regulation for domestic GmbHs, but enforces equivalent substance requirements through § 42 AO (abuse of law), the MLI Principal Purpose Test embedded in German DTAs, and the controlled-foreign-company (CFC) rules of §§ 7–13 AStG. A GmbH that is a mere letterbox company — lacking premises, personnel, business decisions, or operational functions in Germany — may be denied treaty benefits, have its income reattributed to the foreign shareholder, and become subject to the Pillar Two global minimum top-up tax if the group revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung, § 370 AO)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine; in particularly serious cases (large-scale evasion, abuse of authority) imprisonment up to 10 years. Civil consequences include 0.5% monthly late-payment interest on the evaded tax, denial of loss carryforwards, and penalty interest accruing indefinitely.

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer is personally liable under § 69 AO for unpaid corporate taxes arising from his breach of duty, even after resignation. Criminal conviction triggers asset seizure, travel bans, and potential international arrest warrants; the director may be disqualified from board service for up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure (§ 162 Abs. 4 AO)

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty from EUR 5,000 to EUR 100,000 for non-production; additional surcharge of up to 6% of the adjusted income for each undocumented transaction; the Finanzamt may estimate taxable income using the most adverse comparable data and shift the burden of proof to the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to produce documentation may be treated as accomplices to tax evasion (§ 370 AO); personal civil liability for the company's resulting tax deficiencies; in insolvency scenarios, the director's assets may be reached if the GmbH used non-arm's-length pricing to strip profits abroad.

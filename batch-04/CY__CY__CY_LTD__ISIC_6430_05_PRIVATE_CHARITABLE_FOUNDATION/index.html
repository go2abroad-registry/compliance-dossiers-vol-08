Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Cyprus imposes no statutory minimum paid-up share capital for a private limited company; one share of €1.00 nominal value must be subscribed. No escrow account regime is prescribed; however, foundation recognition requires the initial endowment to be fully paid and traceable to the company's bank account.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Exemption Certificate from the Fire Service of Cyprus where premises are under 200 m²; structural integrity verification under the Streets and Buildings Law, Cap. 452; workplace health and safety clearance under the Safety and Health at Work Law 89(I)/1996, including first-aid and fire-drill provisions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus applies a mandatory entity-level corporate income tax at 12.5%; no US-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists for a Cyprus Ltd. The following statutory elections and special statuses are available: (i) approval as a charitable institution under section 9(1)(b) of the Income Tax Law, rendering income applied to charitable purposes exempt from corporate tax; (ii) VAT exemption for services supplied by a recognized charitable organization in connection with its charitable activities, under the charitable exemptions of the VAT Law; (iii) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election under section 9(28) of the Income Tax Law; (iv) Intellectual Property (IP) Box regime under section 9(30), allowing 80% exemption of qualifying IP profits; (v) group loss relief surrender election under section 11; (vi) tonnage tax election for qualifying Cyprus-flag shipping operations; and (vii) 50% exemption from income tax for newly tax-resident non-domiciled individuals employed or appointed as directors in Cyprus. Hybrid mismatch rules under the ATAD-implementing Law 38(I)/2017 are mandatory and cannot be overridden by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 for taxable supplies of goods and EUR 7,800 for taxable supplies of services during the preceding 12 months (exclusive of VAT); EUR 35,000 for intra-EU distance sales of goods to non-taxable persons in Cyprus; no registration threshold applies to non-established suppliers of electronic, telecommunications and broadcasting services to Cypriot consumers, or to persons making intra-Community acquisitions in Cyprus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (a) supplies of goods exported by the supplier or on its behalf to a destination outside the European Union, provided the goods physically leave EU customs territory and are evidenced by a Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document); (b) intra-Community dispatches of goods to a VAT-registered person in another Member State, subject to valid VIES-validated VAT number and ECS reporting; (c) supplies of services directly connected with extra-EU export goods, including international transport of goods, loading, unloading, storage, handling, freight forwarding and related ancillary services under Articles 76-80 of the VAT Law; (d) supplies of services to non-EU recipients, which are treated as outside the scope of Cyprus VAT where the place of supply rule so determines. A charitable foundation exporting grant-funded goods, published materials, or scientific equipment may also claim zero-rating where the export conditions are fully documented. Grant distributions for philanthropic and scientific purposes that do not represent consideration for a supply fall outside the scope of VAT and do not trigger registration or output VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document/SAD with Cyprus Customs acceptance or electronic export record)
  • Bill of Lading (sea carriage) or Airway Bill (air carriage) or CMR consignment note (road/rail carriage)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds within the statutory period
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the SAD in quantity, value and description of goods
  • Cargo manifest, dispatch records and transport documents proving physical departure from the EU customs territory
  • Proof of customer VAT status (valid EU VAT registration number and VIES validation) for intra-Community dispatches
  • Grant agreement, endowment deed, or philanthropic funding documentation for charitable exports

Cyprus has not to date implemented mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or API-based digital stamping for VAT. The Tax Department's Ariadni portal obligatorily accepts electronic VAT returns and ECS recapitulative statements. E-invoicing is mandatory only in the public procurement sector pursuant to EU Directive 2014/55/EU, using the Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 format through certified access points. Under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package, Cyprus will transpose mandatory electronic invoicing and digital transaction reporting for intra-EU B2B supplies and, subsequently, domestic transactions, on a phased EU-wide timetable. All VAT-registered persons remain obliged to issue compliant VAT invoices and, where electronic issuance is used, must ensure the authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility of the invoice.

The Tax Department, jointly with the Customs & Excise Department, conducts post-clearance documentary audits and risk-based cross-matching of SADs, shipping manifests, bills of lading, and remittance certificates. Where zero-rating is disallowed for reasons including missing or forged SAD, false Bill of Lading, unverified IRC, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day window, the VAT is retroactively assessed, with statutory interest of 2% per month or part of a month pursuant to Article 51 of the VAT Law. Administrative penalties under Article 50 include fines of up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years for tax evasion; fraudulent use of forged customs or financial documents may attract imprisonment of up to 5 years and additional criminal sanctions. The exporter may also lose access to voluntary disclosure leniency, be blacklisted for enhanced audit surveillance, face mandatory bank-confirmation procedures and suspension of zero-rating facilitation, and be subject to compound surcharges on the under-declared annual turnover.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Cyprus. EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital and Cyprus membership in the Eurosystem eliminated exchange controls and current/capital account restrictions. No residual administrative approval is required for capital flows, dividend remittances, or profit repatriation. Cyprus domestic law imposes zero withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; distributions by a private charitable foundation must satisfy the entity's statutory charitable purpose and are subject to AML/CFT screening rather than foreign exchange conversion approval. Capital account transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions or cash-based entities are routinely examined by the Central Bank and the Cyprus Unit for Combating Money Laundering (MOKAS).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Cyprus has no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules since acceding to the EU in 2004. Exporters and service providers may retain receipts in any freely convertible currency, hold foreign currency bank accounts, and repatriate proceeds at their discretion. A Balance of Payments statistical reporting obligation to the Central Bank of Cyprus exists under the amended Statistics Law and ECB Regulation 2022/2067; this is a reporting duty, not a conversion or surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), operating as an integral member of the Eurosystem; European Central Bank as the supranational monetary authority for euro-zone monetary policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High for peripheral treasury operations; Low for capital escrow. Fintech firms holding an Electronic Money Institution (EMI) license issued in the European Union can passport into Cyprus under the E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and PSD2. Such entities may legally hold operational funds and process grant disbursements in euro and SWIFT/SEPA rails. However, the Central Bank of Cyprus policy disfavours using EMIs as the primary or exclusive banking layer for entities with significant endowment capital; the custody of endowment corpus must reside with a licensed credit institution incorporated in Cyprus. This means that a foreign or Cypriot EMI would be viable for operational float and grant sweep accounts but cannot replace a supervised bank for escrow or high-value capital accounts. Additional constraints include limitations on deposit-taking, minimum fund segregation rules, and mandatory registration with CBC if the EMI appoints a local agent or branch.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for endowment contributions and capital injections uses international SWIFT MT103 messages with the beneficiary bank BIC and the foundation's Cyprus IBAN (format CYxx BBBB ...). The mandatory purpose code must be set to 'Capital transfer' (code 320) or 'Grants' (code 321) in the 'Purpose of Payment' field based on the ECB Balance of Payments Statistics regulation. In addition to the wire instruction, the treasury back-office should transmit a pre-funded compliance package: notarised Board Resolution approving the injection, donor/settlor source of funds declaration, a current certificate of incumbency, and evidence of the foundation's exempt/charitable status (e.g., Cyprus Tax Department exemption confirmation). The receiving Cyprus bank will screen the transaction against EU, UN, US OFAC and UK sanctions lists; any debit to a sanctioned counterparty or unexplained multi-hop instruction causes automatic escalation to MOKAS.Outbound grant distributions and treasury disbursements execute via SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR payments and via SEPA Instant/Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated grants within the EEA. Commercial letters of credit (LC) are permissible where the foundation undertakes project procurement requiring payment upon presentation of shipping/trade documents; such LC transactions are issued by the Cypriot relationship bank under standard UCP600 rules and are subject to the same AML/know-your-beneficiary checks. For every outbound disbursement, the foundation's treasury must attach a grant approval minute, the underlying grant agreement, and, for values above EUR 10,000, beneficiary identification documents. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (no withholding tax is imposed by Cyprus on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders). For Cyprus-resident corporate and individual shareholders, dividend income may be subject to Special Defence Contribution (SDC) at 17%, unless the participation exemption applies (shareholding of at least 1%, or acquisition cost of at least EUR 20 million, held for at least 2 years; or the subsidiary derives over 50% of its income from passive sources). The SDC on dividends is being progressively abolished under the 2023-2025 fiscal reform agenda.

The return must be filed within 12 months from the end of the tax year (the tax year is the calendar year). For a 31 December year-end, the statutory deadline is 31 December of the following year. The Tax Commissioner may issue blanket extensions; in practice, an extension to 31 December has been granted for recent tax years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary thresholds are set for the Local File or Master File under the Cyprus Transfer Pricing Law (Law 115(I)/2017). All cross-border and domestic controlled transactions must be substantiated on an arm's length basis. Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation must be made available within 60 days of a written request from the Tax Commissioner. The Country-by-Country (CbC) Report obligation applies to multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million. For a charitable endowment entity, controlled transactions above EUR 500,000 per annum (e.g., related-party grants, investment advisory fees, or financing arrangements) should be documented in line with OECD guidance even absent a statutory threshold.

Economic substance scope: The Cyprus Economic Substance Doctrine is applied through the Income Tax Law's 'management and control' test and the OECD core income generating activities (CIGA) framework. A Cypriot private charitable foundation is required to demonstrate genuine economic presence in Cyprus: a physical registered office, a majority of directors resident in Cyprus, substantive board meetings of the grant-committee held in Cyprus with proper minutes, employment of qualified local staff, and the performance in Cyprus of the entity's core income-generating activities (strategic investment decisions, grant selection, and treasury management). Failure to satisfy these substance requirements risks the recharacterization of the company's tax residence (to the place of effective management), denial of the charitable income exemption, and application of the anti-abuse doctrine under EU ATAD (for interest limitation and controlled foreign company rules, where applicable). For the charitable foundation, the ESR (Economic Substance Regulations) model used by low-tax jurisdictions is not directly codified in Cyprus, but the Tax Department applies equivalent substance scrutiny under the OECD/G20 Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Action 5 and the EU Code of Conduct Group requirements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Income Concealment

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the full tax due plus a penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded and statutory interest at 5% per annum; publication of the taxpayer's name by the Cyprus Tax Department; criminal penalties of a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or authorize concealment face personal criminal liability, restitution orders for the tax loss, asset-freezing orders, and a travel ban imposed by the Tax Department pending settlement of the debt.
Sham Charitable Foundation / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the charitable tax exemption with retroactive taxation at 12.5% on all accumulated income; imposition of Special Defence Contribution at 17% on non-exempt dividends; administrative strike-off under Section 327 of the Companies Law (Cap. 113); loss of limited liability protection for abusive arrangements.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil and hold the founder and directors personally liable for the entity's unpaid taxes and creditor claims; directors are subject to disqualification orders of up to 12 years and travel bans where the sham arrangement is designed to defeat creditors or tax authorities.
Anti-Money Laundering / Terrorist Financing Violations (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines by the Cyprus Unit for Combating Money Laundering (MOKAS) of up to EUR 1,000,000 per violation; revocation of licenses and permits; criminal conviction for money laundering carries a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment of up to 14 years, with the proceeds of crime subject to confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal prosecution for willful AML breaches, personal fines, confiscation of personal assets derived from the offense, and custodial sentences; travel bans and international red notices may be issued on pending criminal proceedings.
Fraudulent Trading and Wrongful Distribution of Endowment Assets

Statutory sanction: Under Section 306 of the Companies Law (Cap. 113), where the business is carried on with intent to defraud creditors or for any fraudulent purpose, the court may declare that all directors knowingly party to the conduct shall be personally responsible, without limitation of liability, for all debts and liabilities; criminal conviction carries a fine and imprisonment of up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced automatically upon a finding of fraudulent trading: directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the full amount misappropriated, and may face disqualification for up to 12 years, travel bans, and extradition proceedings in treaty jurisdictions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Unsubstantiated Related-Party Transactions

Statutory sanction: The Tax Commissioner may adjust the taxable profits to the arm's length outcome and impose a penalty of up to 10% of the adjustment amount for failure to produce documentation within the statutory 60-day period; interest at 5% per annum applies to the additional tax assessed. CbC reporting failures in large groups attract fines of up to EUR 20,000 and potential publication of the default.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign returns reflecting non-arm's length pricing with the intention of reducing tax exposure may be assessed personally for the tax adjustment as shadow directors under the anti-avoidance provisions, in addition to criminal penalties for tax evasion.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. Cyprus private limited liability companies may incorporate with authorized share capital of EUR 1,000 (minimum EUR 1) divided into shares of EUR 1.00 each. No escrow account, capital deposit, or bank balance certificate is required by the Registrar. Share capital may be uncalled or payable by instalments per the Articles. Practical standard: authorized capital EUR 1,000-EUR 5,000, issued at no less than EUR 1 per subscriber.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certification for the rented office unit is to be issued by the Cyprus Fire Service as part of the annual municipal inspection; civil defense structural load inspections are not triggered for standard office use; occupational clearance under the Safety and Health at Work Law 158(I)/2004 requires a written risk assessment, office first-aid station, and employer's liability insurance certificate to be kept in the company premises.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Cyprus Tax Department (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance – digital portal: 'Tax For All' (TFA) at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy; entity incorporation administered separately by the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DRO).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Incorporate the Cyprus private limited company with the DRO to obtain the Company Registration Number (HE77-format). Step 2 – Within 60 days of incorporation, file the prescribed application (Form TIN 101/VAT 101) through the Tax For All portal or at the District Tax Office to obtain the Tax Identification Code (TIC), the statutory corporate TIN, which is linked to the VAT registration once the €15,600 threshold is met. Step 3 – Foreign entities not incorporated in Cyprus must procure a Cyprus TIC and, if applicable, a VAT number before supplying in Cyprus, establishing a permanent establishment, or owning Cyprus-situs property, via Form TIN 101 for non-residents, attaching the overseas certificate of incorporation, director identification, and a local address/fiscal agent confirmation. Step 4 – For VAT, non-established suppliers must register from the first taxable supply (no threshold). Approval is normally issued within 5–15 business days; the TIC is activated electronically on the TFA system.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – A standard Cyprus Ltd is a separate, non-transparent legal taxpayer. Cyprus-resident companies (managed and controlled in Cyprus, board-meeting test) are taxed at 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income, with broad territorial exemptions (dividends, capital gains on shares, foreign permanent establishment profits). Non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – A standard Cyprus Ltd is a separate, non-transparent legal taxpayer. Cyprus-resident companies (managed and controlled in Cyprus, board-meeting test) are taxed at 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income, with broad territorial exemptions (dividends, capital gains on shares, foreign permanent establishment profits). Non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. A Cyprus Ltd is not eligible for pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections: (a) management-and-control tax-residency election for foreign-incorporated entities seeking Cyprus residence; (b) group relief election to surrender losses between Cyprus-resident group companies; (c) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity (annual statutory reference rate, approximately 3%); (d) IP Box election granting a 10% effective rate on qualifying intangible income (applicable if the ASO/SEO agency develops proprietary software/algorithms); (e) VAT grouping election under Article 12 VAT Law 94(I)/2000; (f) EU OSS/IOSS election for cross-border B2C digital services and distance sales; (g) tonnage/special regimes for shipping (not relevant to ISIC 7310). Hybrid mismatch rules apply mandatorily under the Cyprus implementation of EU ATAD (Law 9(I)/2017) – no elective opt-out is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 annual taxable turnover for Cyprus-established taxable persons; €0 for non-established suppliers (mandatory registration from the first supply); €10,000 EU-wide annual distance-sales/OSS threshold for cross-border B2C digital and distance-supply activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: exports to third countries are 0% under Article 30 of VAT Law 94(I)/2000, and intra-EU dispatches to a VAT-registered customer are 0% subject to valid EU VAT number validation. Services (ASO, SEO localization, app marketing): the place-of-supply rules in Part V (Articles 22–28) place B2B services at the customer's establishment (reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction) and B2C electronically supplied services at the customer's residence; where the customer is non-Cyprus/non-EU, the service is outside the scope of Cyprus VAT, producing an effective 0% outcome. Conditions: the supplier must hold documentary evidence of the customer's foreign business status, proof of substantive service delivery (performance/analytics reports), and the actual receipt of consideration; input VAT on associated costs remains recoverable via the periodic return, or through the EU 8th/13th Directive electronic refund portal for non-established traders.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document – SAD) with Movement Reference Number (MRN), required for any physical goods component
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent transport document naming the Cyprus exporter
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank account credit confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Signed service agreement or statement of work identifying the foreign client and the ASO/SEO localization deliverables
  • Foreign customer VAT registration certificate or equivalent tax-status evidence to substantiate the B2B place-of-supply analysis
  • Deliverables log, analytics screenshots, or performance reports evidencing actual execution of the localization/SEO services
  • Commercial invoice stating the applicable zero-rating or outside-scope legislative reference (Article 30 or Articles 24–26 of VAT Law 94(I)/2000)

No real-time fiscal clearance engine (no Italy-style SdI) and no digital stamping or QR-code mandate currently in force. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing under Law 194(I)/2021 (transposing Directive 2014/55/EU) requires PEPPOL/EN 16931-compliant electronic invoices for public procurement. B2B/B2C e-invoicing is permitted with recipient consent; no mandatory tax-authority API clearance channel exists, though the TFA portal offers limited e-filing APIs. Cyprus is expected to introduce real-time digital reporting (ViDA/PEPPOL) after the EU implementation window, projected post-2030; taxpayers must meanwhile retain machine-readable standard audit files (SAF-T/XML) on request.

Export zero-rating is subject to targeted exporter audits and cross-border information exchange. The Cyprus Tax Department may issue retrospective VAT assessments for up to 6 years (extended to 10 years for fraud or negligent conduct). Deficiency in export documentation invalidates the 0%/outside-scope treatment from the invoice date, triggering a clawback of 19% VAT plus statutory late interest and administrative penalties under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law (late-filing fines €100–€500, monthly surcharges, and incorrect-document penalties up to €8,500). Intentional evasion attracts criminal sanctions under Article 48 of the VAT Law: a fine of up to €10,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 3 years. Unrepatriated export proceeds beyond the 180-day benchmark are treated as a red flag and may lead to recalculation and disallowance of the export claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full member of the euro area since 1 January 2008. The euro is fully and freely convertible within and outside the EU; there are no exchange-control restrictions on current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, service fee payments and loan repayments may be made freely. No prior Central Bank approval or exchange permit is required. The only practical hurdles are standard bank AML/KYC verifications, EU/UN/OFAC sanctions screening, and, for intra-group payments, transfer pricing documentation and tax anti-abuse analysis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus law does not impose mandatory repatriation of export proceeds, mandatory conversion into EUR, or any surrender timeline. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or foreign-currency accounts at licensed banks; incoming FX payments are subject only to general AML reporting thresholds and may trigger source-of-funds verification where amounts or patterns are unusual.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou); with the Cyprus Ministry of Finance acting in residual EU/IMF statistical matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for EU/EEA-licensed EMIs such as Wise B2B and Payoneer used as collection and disbursement rails. Rating: LOW for non-EEA offshore EMIs, which cannot passport payment services into Cyprus under the Payment Services Law L. 47(I)/2018 (PSD2) and will be treated by Cypriot banks as high-risk third-party processors. Legal constraints: the EMI must be authorised in an EEA state or hold an applicable EU passport; Cypriot banks may reject transfers from EMIs when the originating documentation does not clearly show the end-client and the commercial substance behind the ASO/SEO invoices.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-border payments from Wise/Payoneer or app-store traffic platforms without a clean paper trail.

Root cause: Cross-border payments from Wise/Payoneer or app-store traffic platforms without a clean paper trail.

Defense evidence: Client contracts and MSAs, itemised invoices with unique invoice numbers, platform settlement statements, performance reports, traffic/ASO campaign deliverables, and a sworn declaration that no proceeds originate from prohibited gambling, crypto, or sanctions-exposed activities.
Non-resident UBO or director from a higher-risk jurisdiction or tax-haven list; suspected shell-company profile.

Root cause: Non-resident UBO or director from a higher-risk jurisdiction or tax-haven list; suspected shell-company profile.

Defense evidence: Certified passport copies, proof of residential address, bank reference from a home-country regulated bank, professional CV, source of wealth and source of funds evidence including share-purchase payments, and audited or management financial statements if available.
Corporate structure using nominee shareholders, trust arrangements, or multi-layer foreign holding companies.

Root cause: Corporate structure using nominee shareholders, trust arrangements, or multi-layer foreign holding companies.

Defense evidence: Full organisational chart, copies of share registers, trust deed or declaration of trust, nominee declaration, registered address certificates, and identification of all intermediate entities down to natural-person UBOs in line with EU AML directives.
Shareholder loans or intra-group capital injections lacking commercial or economic-substance proof.

Root cause: Shareholder loans or intra-group capital injections lacking commercial or economic-substance proof.

Defense evidence: Shareholder loan agreement at arm's length, board resolutions authorising the account, correspondence showing operational need, transfer pricing documentation, CRS/FATCA self-certifications, and a Cyprus economic-substance file proving actual management by the board and employees.
Rapid movement of large sums into and out of the account within short periods, indicating possible layering.

Root cause: Rapid movement of large sums into and out of the account within short periods, indicating possible layering.

Defense evidence: Commercial trade chain including signed proposals, approval emails, payment orders, invoices and bank confirmations; pre-approved disbursement schedule; client due-diligence evidence on foreign counterparties; and a treasury policy manual confirming the use of segregated FX and operating accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be received by SWIFT MT103 (outside SEPA) or SEPA Credit Transfer (in EUR) into the Cyprus operational account in the exact corporate name. The bank will require a board resolution, updated list of shareholders and UBOs, source-of-funds statement and, if applicable, a signed loan agreement. Cyprus imposes no exchange-control permit requirement; however, large balances must be reported to the Central Bank for statistical purposes and the bank may request economic-substance evidence.

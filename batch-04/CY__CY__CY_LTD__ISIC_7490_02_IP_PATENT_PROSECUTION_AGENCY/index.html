Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας); official portal: taxportal.mof.gov.cy (e-services: taxportal.mof.gov.cy); contact: 16-20 Vyzantiou Street, Nicosia 1460.

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Cyprus Private Limited Company, the Registrar of Companies (Department of Companies, DRC) electronically notifies the Tax Department upon incorporation; the Tax Department automatically issues a 9-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) which the company must verify and complete by filing Form TIN 126 (Commencement of Business) within 60 days of commencing business. A foreign entity establishing a Cyprus branch must file the 'Registration of Establishment' with the Registrar within 28 days of establishing a place of business, then register with the Tax Department for TIN/VAT, appoint a resident director and statutory auditor, declare beneficial owners on the BO Register, and open a Cyprus bank account (or use an acceptable payment rail) before engaging in taxable supplies. Where annual taxable supplies exceed EUR 15,600 or the first supply is made by a person not established in Cyprus, a separate VAT registration number (CY + 8 digits + 1 check digit) must be applied for via Form VAT 101/VAT 102 or through the Tax IS portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections and reliefs relevant to a Cyprus Ltd: (1) Notional Interest Deduction ('NID') on new equity introduced after 1 January 2015 (elected/claimed annually in the CIT return); (2) Group current-year loss relief election under section 13 ITA; (3) VAT grouping election (Department of Taxation Approval, joint and several liability); (4) Option to apply the special 'IP Box' modified-nexus 80% qualifying-profit deduction for qualifying intangible assets (elections per qualifying IP asset cycle); (5) Deemed Dividend Distribution (DDD) exemption/repayment mechanism under the Special Defence Contribution law for Cyprus-resident shareholders where the company satisfies 'commercial needs'; (6) Foreign tax credit election and unilateral relief; (7) Hybrid mismatch safe-harbour exclusions under the ATAD-implementing provisions (Income Tax Law amendment of L.153(I)/2020); (8) Optional 0% withholding on outbound dividends where the participation exemption and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive terms are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 (annual turnover of taxable supplies for established taxable persons); EUR 7,853 for intra-Community acquisitions; EUR 35,000 distance-sale threshold into Cyprus under derogation; non-established suppliers must register from the first supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating under Article 168 VAT Law applies to the export of goods to a destination outside the European Union provided (a) the supplier is the exporter and evidence of dispatch/transport out of the EU is retained, (b) the goods leave the EU within 90 days of the tax point, and (c) customs export formalities (Single Administrative Document / electronic export declaration via TAXIS-Net customs) are completed in Cyprus or another EU state. Services: patent prosecution, PCT preparation/filing and international trademark services supplied to non-EU clients are generally outside the scope of Cyprus VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule (Article 170 VAT Law, reverse charge/OVCSR on the client) or the Article 171 non-EU B2C rule for customers established outside the EU; zero-rating also applies to certain services directly connected with exported goods (e.g., IP registration and protection services for goods destined outside the EU) when substantiated by export documents. Service suppliers must retain evidence of the customer's non-EU business status (e.g., valid TIN or passport) and the supply/refund trail.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / electronic ICS-CYP export declaration with MRN reference)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (or CMR rail/road consignment note) naming the Cyprus supplier as consignor and evidencing EU exit
  • Commercial invoice and signed contract or PO if under non-disclosure
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice from the Cyprus banking counterparty evidencing FX receipt of export consideration
  • Proof of non-EU customer status (customer TIN certificate, VAT registration extract, or passport for B2C)
  • Customs office exit stamp / IMO manifest confirmation of third-country arrival

No general B2B real-time fiscal clearance (stamping) mandate exists as of 2025. Cyprus has compulsory use of certified fiscal cash registers ('Fiscal Electronic System / BOE') for retail transactions, with daily electronic transmission of transaction registers to the Tax Department via the Tax IS API in XML. For standard VAT invoicing, taxpayers must ensure authenticity of origin and integrity of content (supply-chain controls or qualified e-signature/EDI). Cyprus has announced progressive alignment with EU ViDA: mandatory structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B (and OSS/OSS imports) is slated for full implementation by 2028; until then, PEPPOL/BIS invoicing is accepted for B2G.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible on both current and capital account pursuant to the EU Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the Cypriot Exchange Control Law (as liberalised since 2003). Cyprus maintains no foreign exchange controls, no profit repatriation ceiling, and no prior regulatory approval for dividend distributions other than standard corporate law solvency tests, the submission of audited financial statements, and the statutory obligation to file a tax return and settle any corporate income tax (currently 12.5%) before distribution. However, EU and UN financial sanctions require enhanced due diligence for payments originating from, or destined to, sanction-restricted jurisdictions, and payments to individuals or entities on the consolidated CFSP/UN list may be blocked without notice.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no statutory obligation to surrender, repatriate, or convert export receipts. Export proceeds may be freely retained in EUR or any foreign currency and may be discharged at the receiving bank's discretion, subject solely to Anti-Money Laundering (AML) transaction monitoring thresholds, the EU Counter-Terrorist Financing Reporting Regulations, and the bank's internal suspicious transaction reporting obligations. There is no minimum conversion ratio or mandatory holding period.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) together with the European Central Bank (ECB) as the euro-area monetary authority and the ECB Single Supervisory Mechanism for credit institution oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: HIGH for non-escrow treasury operations and IP fee disbursements; LOW for holding third-party client monies under an escrow or trust-style structure. Legal constraints: Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise and Revolut are licensed under the Cypriot/EEA Electronic Money Regulations and are NOT credit institutions; therefore they may not accept deposits or charge third-party client funds, and their payment documentation is insufficient to satisfy the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission or other professional licensing standards that mandate a separate client account at an authorised bank. Wise is viably permitted only for low-value operational disbursements (e.g., WIPO fee payments) provided the receiving institutions are identifiable corporate payees and the amounts do not exceed the bank's own operative internal risk threshold (typically EUR 20,000 per transaction). For client funds held by the agency, a full-service Cypriot bank account is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols require a SWIFT MT103 (in EUR or USD) or a SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR inbound flows. The purpose code must explicitly state 'E – Equity Investment' for non-resident capital injection (or 'BOP_EC/E' for the new ECB harmonised classification), and the narrative field must read 'Share Capital Contribution to CY_LTD – ISIC 7490 Patent Prosecution Agency'. The remitter must be the registered shareholder; if the remitter is a holding company, a full corporate good-standing certificate and a group resolution identifying the natural person as UBO must be pre-approved. For the escrow/client account, use the purpose code '0500 – Client Funds' or the SWIFT instruction 'CLIENT FEE COLLECTION' to avoid commingling in the bank's internal ledgers. No monetary amount above EUR 10,000 may be received without the bank automatically triggering a national AML report, so the complete onboarding file must be lodged before receiving any injection.Outbound disbursements for patent and trademark prosecution are best executed using traditional bank-to-bank SWIFT MT103 wires for official patent office fees (EPO, USPTO, JPO, CNIPA) since these institutions accept only formal bank transfers. For high-volume low-value PCT fees, the agency may utilise the Wise Business infrastructure, which provides regulatory cleared settlement via local ACH rails in the receiving jurisdiction (e.g., WIPO payment in CHF via Wise Business route). Commercial Letters of Credit are not applicable to professional service fees but are permissible only for the sale of patent licences; for such transactions, a standard documentary collection (URC 522) or an export LC (UCP 600) can be used through Bank of Cyprus. 12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Cyprus imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Cyprus Ltd to non-resident shareholders; qualifying dividends received by Cyprus corporate residents are fully exempt under the participation exemption.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return for a tax year ending 31 December must be filed by 31 December of the following calendar year (12 months after year-end); for a non-calendar accounting period, filing is due 12 months after the period end; a return demanded by notice from the Commissioner must be filed within 60 days of service. Provisional tax instalments are paid on Form TD1 in two instalments on 31 July and 31 December of the tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with related parties exceeds EUR 1,000,000; Master File required where the multinational group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000; below these thresholds taxpayers must nonetheless apply the arm's length principle and maintain benchmarking evidence for audit; statutory documentation obligations align with the EU Transfer Pricing Directive effective for tax-year 2025.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has not enacted an ESR-style economic substance code such as those in the UAE or BVI; substance is nevertheless enforced through the central management and control test for tax residence, the OECD beneficial-ownership condition for DTA and EU Directive claims, anti-abuse and sham-transaction case law, ATAD CFC and GAAR provisions, and the statutory IP Box requirement that qualifying IP development and exploitation functions and actual expenditure occur in Cyprus. A patent prosecution agency must maintain in Cyprus a real office, qualified patent attorneys, time records, board minutes and commercial contracts to support treaty claims and preferential IP deductions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law (Law 79/1990): imprisonment up to 5 years and/or a fine of up to EUR 20,000 per offence; administrative additions to tax, statutory late-payment interest, and full denial of contested deductions; publication of defaulters and enforced seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced upon proof of fraud: directors and shadow directors face criminal imprisonment, confiscation, travel bans, asset freezing and civil restitution for the tax evaded; fraudulent trading under the Companies Law (Cap 113) renders directors jointly and severally liable for company debts.
Shell Company Operation

Statutory sanction: Tax re-characterisation by the Commissioner: denial of IP Box and NID benefits, assessment of deemed income at 12.5% plus penalties and interest, rejection of DTA benefits for lack of beneficial ownership, and strike-off as a defunct company by the Registrar of Companies under Cap 113.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for tax liabilities of a struck-off company; unlimited liability exists for carrying on business with intent to defraud creditors; professional authorisation of the patent agency may be revoked and directors disqualified from holding office.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer-pricing adjustment to arm's length conditions plus statutory interest; administrative penalties under the 2024 Cyprus TP Regulations for incomplete or withheld documentation; exclusion of non-contemporaneous evidence in audit; risk of unrelieved double taxation absent a mutual agreement procedure under the applicable DTA.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false transfer-pricing declarations are exposed to criminal tax-fraud sanctions; transfer pricing that strips profit from the Cyprus entity gives rise to breach-of-fiduciary-duty claims by minority shareholders and creditors; adjustment-induced insolvency triggers wrongful-trading liability.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (Law 188(I)/2007): imprisonment up to 14 years and an unlimited fine; freezing and confiscation orders by MOKAS; revocation of professional licences and deregistration; EU and UN asset freezes for sanctions breaches.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and money-laundering reporting officers face personal criminal conviction, travel bans, restriction orders and asset confiscation; the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent transfers; disqualification and professional indemnity consequences follow a money-laundering conviction.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DRCOR), Ministry of Energy, Commerce and Industry, Nicosia, Cyprus.

Governing statute

Companies Law, Cap. 113 (The Companies Act, Cap. 113), as amended; ss. 3, 6-10 (incorporation), s. 38 (registered office), ss. 53-60 (directors and secretary), First Schedule Table A (default articles).

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum and Articles of Association filed with Form HE1 (Application for Registration of a Company), Form HE3 (Declaration of Compliance), and Form HE4 (Statement of First Directors and Secretary).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a private limited company under Cap. 113. Typical authorized capital is EUR 1,000-5,000 divided into EUR 1.00 ordinary shares. Capital duty on issued share capital has been abolished; no escrow or capital lock-up account is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Cyprus Fire Service (or approved fire safety assessor) prior to occupying office premises; structural/load inspection certificate required for any racked film vault or media storage area; compliance with the Safety and Health at Work Laws 89(I)/1996 as amended, including an occupational risk assessment certificate issued by a certified safety consultant.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a private limited company under Cap. 113. Typical authorized capital is EUR 1,000-5,000 divided into EUR 1.00 ordinary shares. Capital duty on issued share capital has been abolished; no escrow or capital lock-up account is required.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED, that the incorporation of the Company, filed with the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver pursuant to Companies Law Cap. 113, be ratified and confirmed. RESOLVED, that 1,000 ordinary shares of EUR 1.00 each be allotted to the founding subscriber at par, fully paid or credited as fully paid. RESOLVED, that the registered office of the Company be established at the address of the registered agent in Nicosia, Cyprus. RESOLVED, that the first Director, Company Secretary, and independent auditor be appointed as identified in Form HE4. RESOLVED, that the Directors are authorized to open bank accounts, procure the company seal, instruct counsel, and execute all filings necessary to lawfully commence international film copyright distribution and licensing.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No sectoral industrial license is triggered for ISIC 5911 international film copyright distribution; general business premises oversight is held by the municipal licensing authority of the registered office location (e.g., Nicosia Municipality under the Business Establishment Licensing Law) and, for any incidental warehousing of media stock, the Cyprus Fire Service.

Category: Municipal Business Operation Licence (General Business Licensing Regime); no cinematographic exhibition or production permit required for pure copyright licensing, royalty collection, and distribution of film rights.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας); e-portal: TaxISnet (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

The corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) is granted automatically at incorporation: the Registrar of Companies issues a company registration number (HE + serial digits), which is identical to the corporate TIN and is the base number for the EORI customs registration. Required subsequent steps: (1) activate the TaxISnet profile within 60 days of the certificate of incorporation to file income tax, VAT and employer returns; (2) apply for VAT registration through TaxISnet Form VAT 101 when annual taxable turnover exceeds €15,600, when intra-Community acquisitions exceed €10,000, or when the first intra-EU film licensing transaction is made; (3) if a non-resident company carries on business in Cyprus through a permanent establishment (PE), that non-resident must file a non-resident registration application with the District Tax Office of the PE location; (4) maintain in the customer master file the equivalent of the non-Cyprus licensee's business registration or VAT certificate for every EU or third-country counterparty to substantiate place-of-supply treatment; (5) no minimum capital or Cypriot shareholder is required for TIN issuance, but the statutory registered office in Cyprus is a legal precondition.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: a Cyprus tax-resident Private Limited Company (managed and controlled in Cyprus) is subject to corporate income tax at the flat 12.5% headline rate on worldwide income, with no sub-Cyprus or municipal income tax distinction. The entity is not a pass-through vehicle; dividends received qualify for the participation exemption, gains on disposal of shares are generally outside the Cyprus tax net, foreign branch profits are assessable with an ordinary foreign tax credit, and non-residents are taxable only on Cyprus-sourced income. Independent film licensing royalties accrued to a Cyprus Ltd are therefore taxed at the entity level at 12.5% (or effectively 2.5% on qualifying IP profits via the IP Box). — The corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) is granted automatically at incorporation: the Registrar of Companies issues a company registration number (HE + serial digits), which is identical to the corporate TIN and is the base number for the EORI customs registration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Cyprus tax-resident Private Limited Company (managed and controlled in Cyprus) is subject to corporate income tax at the flat 12.5% headline rate on worldwide income, with no sub-Cyprus or municipal income tax distinction. The entity is not a pass-through vehicle; dividends received qualify for the participation exemption, gains on disposal of shares are generally outside the Cyprus tax net, foreign branch profits are assessable with an ordinary foreign tax credit, and non-residents are taxable only on Cyprus-sourced income. Independent film licensing royalties accrued to a Cyprus Ltd are therefore taxed at the entity level at 12.5% (or effectively 2.5% on qualifying IP profits via the IP Box).

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Cyprus; tax classification follows the legal form of incorporation. Applicable statutory elections: (1) IP Box Election (Art. 9B Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002) granting an elective 80% notional deduction of Qualifying IP-derived Profits (QIPP), which includes film copyright licensing and distribution income, resulting in a 2.5% effective CIT rate; (2) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) on new equity under Art. 9I, an annual election computing a deductible deemed interest return on new paid-up share capital and share premiums, lowering effective CIT toward approximately 8.5%; (3) Group Relief Election permitting intra-group surrender of losses by written agreement filed with the Tax Department within two years of the year-end; (4) fixed-asset depreciation and IP amortisation elections for film rights over a 10–15 year useful life; (5) hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are mandatory, non-elective, and disallow deductions arising from hybrid instruments or imported mismatches; (6) voluntary VAT registration is allowed at the commencement of a pre-trading or export-oriented film licensing business.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable turnover exceeding €15,600 (goods and services) triggers compulsory VAT registration; intra-Community acquisitions threshold is €10,000 per annum; distance sales into Cyprus threshold is €10,000 per annum; voluntary registration is available to exporters, zero-rated flat-rate farmers and preparatory film-production companies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Portuguese zero-rating is not applicable. For Cyprus, exports of physical goods, including film masters, prints and media assets moved outside the EU, are zero-rated under Section 15 of the VAT Law when the exporter retains valid export evidence (EX1/SAD declaration with MRN and transport proof). For the dominant film-licensing revenue model: (a) B2B royalties from an EU customer established outside Cyprus are not subject to Cyprus VAT; the EU customer applies reverse charge; (b) B2B royalties from a non-EU licensee (e.g., US, UK, CA, AU, HK) fall outside the scope of Cyprus VAT because the place of supply is the recipient's country, substantiated by the customer's VAT/equivalent registration certificate or Chamber of Commerce extract; (c) B2C licences supplied to EU consumers remain within Cyprus VAT at 19% unless the OSS distance-sales threshold of €10,000 is exceeded and the consumer's member state levies the tax. There is no statutory FX repatriation deadline in Cyprus; the 180-day benchmark in the data pack is adopted as documentary discipline for transfer pricing and advance pricing arrangements.

  • Customs Export Declaration EX1/SAD with MRN evidencing physical export of film masters or media assets
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR endorsed by customs for physical movement to the non-EU destination
  • Commercial Invoice and executed Copyright Licence / Distribution Agreement evidencing consideration and chain of title for ISIC 5911 licensing
  • Inward Remittance / SWIFT credit advice from the foreign licensee's bank matched to the invoice reference
  • VAT registration certificate or business registration extract of the non-Cyprus licensee for reverse-charge and out-of-scope place-of-supply substantiation

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or Continuous Transaction Control (CTC) mandate is in force in Cyprus at the applicable date. TaxISnet is the mandatory e-filing portal for VAT returns, VIES/EC Sales Lists (ECSL) and Intrastat declarations. For EU B2B film licensing transactions, the invoice must disclose the customer's VAT registration number and a reverse-charge indicator. Structured e-invoicing in EN 16931/PEPPOL format is presently voluntary; Cyprus is aligned with the EU ViDA roadmap and is expected to mandate structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions during the 2025–2030 harmonisation window. SWIFT credits, licence agreements and customs declarations must be digitally retained to satisfy future clearance expectations.

The Tax Commissioner may raise a VAT assessment within six years of the end of the VAT year, extended to ten years where fraud is established. Unsupported zero-rating is recharacterised to 19% VAT on the gross royalty with retrospective clawback of related input VAT; unpaid VAT carries statutory interest at 1% per month or fraction thereof, and a civil penalty of up to 100% of the VAT deficiency may be imposed. Deliberate submission of false customs declarations or fabricated licence agreements triggers criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment of up to three years; failure to issue a compliant VAT invoice attracts a statutory penalty of €100 per invoice. Practical audit scrutiny focuses on correlation of bank inflows with the licensing agreement, distribution and streaming royalty reports, and customer tax status certificates; absence of the complete documentation bundle, particularly the inward remittance leg, is treated as a strong indicator justifying a clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Cyprus applies no exchange control restrictions on inbound capital, royalty income, or profit repatriation; non-resident shareholders and licensors are not subject to Cypriot withholding tax on dividends or royalties in the ordinary case, and the Special Defence Contribution (SDC) regime applies only to Cyprus tax-resident recipients. Documentary compliance at source banks relates exclusively to anti-money-laundering controls (L.188(I)/2007) and to balance-of-payments statistical coding under BPM6; no prior Central Bank approval is required for remittances, except where an interpersonal relationship with sanctioned persons triggers EU restrictive measures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory mandatory surrender of export proceeds and no compulsory conversion ratio. A Cyprus-domiciled independent film licensor may hold foreign-currency receipts in any authorised commercial bank, and repatriation is subject only to the ordinary commercial payment cycle agreed with the licensor. For statistical purposes, all export proceeds (including minimum guarantees, sublicensing royalties, and distribution fees) must be recorded using the correct BPM6 export-of-services purpose code at the receiving bank; supporting licensing agreements or pro-forma invoices must demonstrate that remittances are trade-related in order to pass the bank's export-validity and AML screening.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) and under the prudential supervision framework of the European Central Bank (ECB) and the European Banking Authority (EBA); Foreign Exchange Administration functions are governed by the Exchange Control Law (Cap. 99) as effectively suspended since Euro adoption, and by European Union Treaty provisions on the free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may passport into Cyprus under the Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and can offer virtual IBANs and cross-border FX conversion. However, for the film-licensing commercial model, a full banking relationship with a Central Bank of Cyprus supervised institution is materially preferable because theatrical/distribution counterparties, ISO 20022 settlement, and documentary letter-of-credit (UCP 600) transactions require a Cypriot clearing bank with direct TARGET2 access. Legal constraints under L.188(I)/2007 require the EMI to be registered with the Central Bank of Cyprus when it is not solely servicing foreign clients, and the EMI must obtain a Cyprus correspondent bank for EUR clearing; this negates most operational advantages. EMIs are viable only as a secondary concentration account for small-ticket sublicensing fees, not as the primary treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses a standard SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR transfers from an EEA bank) from the parent company to the Cyprus company's operational settlement account; the payment reference field must expressly state 'Voluntary share capital contribution for ordinary shares issued to shareholders'. The receiving bank shall code the proceeds under BPM6 as direct investment equity (codes under Financial Account: Direct Investment — Equity). Supporting verification consists of the updated shareholders' register, an executed subscription board resolution, the articles of association, a notarised ownership chart, and a completed bank-issued capital introduction form. Banks require the inward credit advice to be matched within the same value date to the share issue record; where a mixed subscription includes an IP transfer or cash premium, a separate valuation letter is mandated. No Central Bank authorisation is required; however, the bank may apply a 5-business-day source-verification hold for non-EU parent jurisdictions.Primary clearing rail is the SWIFT MT103 wire for royalty/minimum-guarantee remittances to producers and subdistributors, executed via the Cypriot clearing bank within the TARGET2 EUR payment infrastructure; for EUR payments to EEA beneficiaries under €1,500,000, SEPA Credit Transfer is used per Regulation (EU) No 260/2012. Commercial letters of credit (UCP 600 and eUCP 2.0) are the preferred documentary rail for milestone-based independent-film licensing advances, where the Cyprus bank acts as issuing bank or advising bank and the LC funds are released upon presentation of shooting-completion certificates, delivery/acceptance schedules, and inaugural distribution statements. Outbound remittances must be supported by a fully executed licensing agreement and, where the counterparty qualifies for zero withholding tax, a WHT exemption memorandum citing the Cyprus-foreign treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (where applicable to royalties, the EU Interest & Royalties Directive). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2022, a Local File is mandatory where the aggregate value of the Cyprus company's controlled transactions exceeds EUR 750,000 per tax year. A Master File is required when the multinational group's ultimate parent has consolidated revenue of at least EUR 50,000,000 and the Cyprus entity has controlled transactions. Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000. Documentation must be produced within 60 days of a Tax Department request and retained for six years.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not have separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. Substance is nevertheless a decisive test through the Cyprus tax residence rules (central management and control), EU anti-tax-avoidance directives, CFC rules, and case law on beneficial ownership for treaty and EU Directive purposes. A Cyprus film licensing company must maintain physical premises, locally resident directors, substantive employment or contracted staff, active board meetings in Cyprus, and local negotiation/contract-management functions. Absence of substance can lead to denial of tax residence certificates, reclassification of the Cyprus company, and loss of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent False Return

Statutory sanction: The Tax Department assesses omitted tax at 12.5% with late-payment interest at 5% per annum (daily accrual). Under Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002, fraudulent or false statements attract criminal fines of up to EUR 20,000 and/or imprisonment for up to five years; enforcement may include asset seizure.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or procure the false return are criminally liable; civil restitution orders may be issued; travel bans and freezing orders can be imposed pending criminal proceedings; the corporate veil may be pierced where the Cyprus company is used as a mere cloak for personal tax avoidance.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Tax Department may deny Cyprus tax residency, refuse to issue a Tax Residence Certificate, and assess Cyprus-source income on a deemed basis. The Registrar of Companies may strike off a dormant shell company under the Companies Law Cap.113, leading to dissolution and loss of corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the company's debts if the company continues trading while insolvent (wrongful trading) or if the court pierces the veil; director disqualification orders and restrictions on leaving Cyprus may be imposed in fraudulent-concealment cases.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing of Film Rights

Statutory sanction: The Tax Department applies the arm's-length principle and adjusts taxable profit upward; additional tax at 12.5% and late-payment interest apply. Non-compliance with documentation obligations may expose the company to administrative fines and enhanced penalties; the CbC filing failure can attract fines up to EUR 20,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve TP documentation knowing it is false may be prosecuted; the tax adjustment may be treated as a fraudulent preference in insolvency; personal financial liability can arise for tax, penalties, and interest if the TP scheme is a deliberate evasion arrangement.
IP Box Misutilization

Statutory sanction: The IP Box deduction is cancelled and the tax shortfall is assessed at 12.5% with interest; the Tax Department may apply general anti-abuse rules to recharacterise the arrangement; penalties of up to 100% of the tax evaded may apply in abusive cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false nexus calculations can be held personally liable in restitution; criminal fraud charges may follow where the IP Box claim is part of a deliberate tax evasion scheme.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Register Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Beneficial Ownership Law and AML Law 188(I)/2007, non-compliance is a criminal offence: daily fines may be imposed, the company's bank accounts may be frozen, and the Registrar may suspend registration; financial penalties can reach EUR 2,000 or more per violation.

Director personal liability: Every officer in default is personally liable to the daily fine; imprisonment of up to six months may be imposed for false declarations; directors can be barred from acting as officers of Cyprus companies.

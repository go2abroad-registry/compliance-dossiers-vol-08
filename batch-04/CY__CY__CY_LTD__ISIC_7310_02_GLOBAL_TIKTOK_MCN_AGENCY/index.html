Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance; electronic portal: Cyprus Tax Portal (Taxisnet) at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy; ancillary registration via Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver for incorporation and RO files.

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders incorporating a Cyprus Private Limited Company must: (1) reserve the company name and file incorporation documents with the Registrar of Companies, obtaining Certificate of Incorporation (HE number); (2) appoint a Cyprus-resident company secretary, registered office, and tax representative; (3) submit the Corporate Tax Registration Form (TAF 100 series) and, if making taxable supplies, the VAT registration application (TAF 321) electronically via Taxisnet to the District Tax Office, thereby obtaining a single Tax Identification Code (TIC) and, where required, a CY-prefixed VAT number; (4) register as employer with the Social Insurance Services where staffing is anticipated; (5) complete the non-EU or EU beneficial ownership declarations to satisfy the ultimate beneficial owner register. Registration must occur within 60 days of the commencement of business activity for VAT purposes and by the date of commencement of employment or income-generating activity for corporate tax purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign shareholders incorporating a Cyprus Private Limited Company must: (1) reserve the company name and file incorporation documents with the Registrar of Companies, obtaining Certificate of Incorporation (HE number); (2) appoint a Cyprus-resident company secretary, registered office, and tax representative; (3) submit the Corporate Tax Registration Form (TAF 100 series) and, if making taxable supplies, the VAT registration application (TAF 321) electronically via Taxisnet to the District Tax Office, thereby obtaining a single Tax Identification Code (TIC) and, where required, a CY-prefixed VAT number; (4) register as employer with the Social Insurance Services where staffing is anticipated; (5) complete the non-EU or EU beneficial ownership declarations to satisfy the ultimate beneficial owner register. Registration must occur within 60 days of the commencement of business activity for VAT purposes and by the date of commencement of employment or income-generating activity for corporate tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate US-style check-the-box elections. Available statutory elections include: (1) IP Box regime election (Section 9B Income Tax Law, effective rate of 2.5% on qualifying intangible asset profits) for qualifying innovation assets tracked in EU/EAA; (2) election between corporate income tax at 12.5% and Special Defence Contribution (SDC) on deemed/dividend distributions for Cyprus-domiciled shareholders; (3) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity introduced from 1 January 2015; (4) group loss relief election between Cyprus group companies; (5) irrevocable election to apply VAT grouping (Section 18 VAT Law); (6) foreign tax credit election instead of expense deduction for taxes paid abroad. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; Cyprus applies ATAD 2 (EU Directive 2016/1164 as amended) hybrid mismatch rules mandatorily against tax arbitrage structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 (taxable supplies of goods and services within Cyprus); EUR 10,000 (intra-Community acquisitions); EUR 35,000 (distance sales from other EU Member States); non-established taxable persons supplying digital/agency services to Cyprus consumers may be subject to the EU One-Stop-Shop mini-rules requiring registration irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (1) export of goods physically leaving Cyprus to a destination outside the EU, proved by the Common Transit or Customs Export Declaration (SAD) with exit confirmation; (2) B2B supplies of services, including creator-MCN agency, marketing, and platform monetisation services, where the customer is established outside the EU under the place-of-supply rule (Section 54(1) VAT Law), provided the services are not deemed supplied in Cyprus by virtue of being performed at a domestic location or connected with Cyprus immovable property; (3) services directly related to exported goods and transport. The taxpayer must hold credible commercial and transport documentation demonstrating dispatch within the exporter's VAT accounting period or within the additional period the Tax Department accepts, and must record the zero-rated supplies in VAT return field 2, with the underlying export evidence retained for at least the statutory six-year record-keeping period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD) with exit confirmation from the Cyprus/outward-EU customs office
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical movement of goods outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order contract
  • Proof of payment showing inward remittance of export proceeds in foreign currency (SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the receiving Cypriot bank where FX repatriation has occurred
  • Internal or outsourced logistics shipment log evidencing dispatch date and destination
  • TikTok Creator Payout or Wise B2B payout reconciliation report mapping offshore platform receipts to the contracted customer where services are cross-border

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or statutory e-invoicing clearance API is currently enforced in Cyprus. VAT returns and intrastat declarations are filed electronically via Taxisnet within 10 days of the tax period end. Cyprus maintains an acceptance channel for PEPPOL-standard e-invoices under EU Directive 2014/55/EU, but issuance remains optional in the B2B domain. Under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) initiative, mandatory e-invoicing and real-time digital reporting for intra-EU supplies are expected by 2030; until then, paper and PDF invoices meeting Article 158 VAT Law substantive validity requirements remain fully recognised.

The Cyprus Tax Department reviews zero-rated exports for documentary completeness, commercial substance, and time-liness of FX repatriation. Where export evidence is fraudulent, incomplete, or the goods are found to have been diverted back into the EU, the zero-rating is clawed back and VAT is assessed at 19% with interest at the statutory rate (currently 1.75% above the ECB base rate). Additional liability arises under Article 63 of the VAT Law: fines up to EUR 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years for deliberate evasion, plus civil penalties of up to 50% of the tax under-assessed for negligent error; making a false refund claim triggers a misdeclaration penalty of 1% of the unwarranted amount per period plus reinstatement of tax and interest. The Director of the Tax Department also possesses powers to impose a forfeiture of any VAT refund previously credited and to launch a criminal prosecution for tax fraud where sham contracts are used to disguise intra-EU sales as third-country exports.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Cyprus is a euro-area member state. The euro is fully convertible, and Cyprus maintains no foreign-exchange control regime, no current-account restrictions, and no capital-account restrictions beyond EU-wide sanctions and anti-money-laundering safeguards. Profit repatriation, dividend remittance, repayment of intercompany loans, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred freely in euro or any convertible currency, provided the underlying transactions are commercially justified, properly documented, and reported for tax and statistical purposes. There is no prior administrative approval for current-account transactions. The BPM6 balance-of-payments reporting obligation is limited to statistical filings by resident banks; corporate treasury transactions are routed through supervised credit institutions. The principal impediments to profit remittance are not FX controls but rather (i) bona fide economic substance requirements for tax purposes, (ii) transfer-pricing documentation for intercompany payments, and (iii) standard CDD and sanctions screening applied by Cypriot payment service providers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export receipts, including receipts from cross-border digital services, creator royalties, and MCN commission revenue. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency in a bank account abroad or in Cyprus at the exporter's discretion. Under the Foreign Exchange Law and the Free Movement of Capital legislation, there is no obligation to repatriate, sell, or declare export proceeds upon receipt. Nonetheless, for tax and economic-substance verification, receipts from non-resident counterparties must be traceable via commercial agreements, invoicing, and bank statements; the Cyprus Tax Department applies general anti-avoidance provisions if transactions are structured artificially to avoid tax or substance requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentrike Trapeza tis Kyprou) in coordination with the European Central Bank for euro-area monetary policy and the European Systemic Risk Board; the Central Bank of Cyprus is the competent national authority for banking supervision, payment systems, and Anti-Money Laundering oversight under Article 12 of the Assessment and Management of Non-Performing Loans Framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is medium with strict legal constraints. Cyprus permits licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions to operate under the Payment Services Law (Law 31(I)/2018, transposing PSD2) and the Electronic Money Law. EU/EEA EMIs may passport into Cyprus on a cross-border basis after registration with the Central Bank of Cyprus. Offshore, unregulated digital EMIs from third countries are not recognized as valid payment service providers for the opening of operational accounts affecting Cyprus treasury operations; a non-EU fintech must establish or partner with an EU-licensed EMI and register a branch or agent in Cyprus to offer services locally. For a Cyprus tax-resident company operating a global TikTok MCN, a regulated EMI (e.g., a UK or Lithuania-licensed EMI with PSD2 passport) may be used for creator payouts, but the primary operating account for statutory capital and tax settlements should be held at a domestic credit institution to satisfy substance, banking supervision, and audit expectations. Crypto-asset service providers are separately regulated by the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) under the Investment Services Law and the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation transitional regime; using crypto rails for creator payouts is strongly discouraged for AML and foreign-policy reasons.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions into Cyprus from foreign shareholders may be remitted via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA (in EUR) without Central Bank of Cyprus approval. The remitting bank must use an OECD Common Reporting Standard-compliant tax reference and, where applicable, the ECB BPM6 purpose code for direct investment equity capital (for euro-area statistics, commonly code 'M1000' for direct investment capital). To avoid AML queries, the Cyprus receiving bank requires a corporate resolution, UBO certification, and origin-of-funds evidence. For subscribed capital, the Articles of Association must be filed with the Cyprus Registrar of Companies; there is no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a private limited company, but for bank onboarding a paid-up capital of EUR 10,000 or more is commercially advisable. Loans from shareholders must be documented with a written loan agreement, prescribe interest at arm's-length rates, and be reported in the company's tax file. No currency conversion restriction applies; EUR and USD accounts may be opened simultaneously. Compliance steps: (i) compile a shareholder's source-of-funds pack; (ii) notify the bank two business days prior to transfer; (iii) ensure the beneficiary account is fully onboarded before the wire is sent; (iv) complete the electronic declaration forms provided by the bank for balance-of-payments statistics.Outbound creator payouts and trade settlements can be executed through: (i) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EUR payments to single European creators; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR or non-SEPA payments; (iii) Wise B2B batch payout API for high-volume creator payments, provided the aggregated payout service is operated under the bank's payment-institution authorization or the Wise EU EMI license registered in Cyprus; and (iv) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) only if the MCN acquires content licensing or media bundles requiring documentary credit; this is generally obsolete for creator disbursements. TikTok Creator Payouts may flow directly from TikTok platform accounts to the Cyprus company's Wise B2B virtual account or a bank account; the company must then disburse net commissions to individual creators via bank transfers. For outbound royalties and intercompany service fees, the company must issue VAT invoices if applicable (services to EU clients are reverse-charged; services to non-EU clients are outside Cypriot VAT), maintain transfer-pricing documentation, and use the economic-beneficiary-owner statement under FATCA/CRS. Cyprus permits licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions to operate under the Payment Services Law (Law 31(I)/2018, transposing PSD2) and the Electronic Money Law. EU/EEA EMIs may passport into Cyprus on a cross-border basis after registration with the Central Bank of Cyprus. Offshore, unregulated digital EMIs from third countries are not recognized as valid payment service providers for the opening of operational accounts affecting Cyprus treasury operations; a non-EU fintech must establish or partner with an EU-licensed EMI and register a branch or agent in Cyprus to offer services locally. For a Cyprus tax-resident company operating a global TikTok MCN, a regulated EMI (e.g., a UK or Lithuania-licensed EMI with PSD2 passport) may be used for creator payouts, but the primary operating account for statutory capital and tax settlements should be held at a domestic credit institution to satisfy substance, banking supervision, and audit expectations. Crypto-asset service providers are separately regulated by the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) under the Investment Services Law and the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation transitional regime; using crypto rails for creator payouts is strongly discouraged for AML and foreign-policy reasons.

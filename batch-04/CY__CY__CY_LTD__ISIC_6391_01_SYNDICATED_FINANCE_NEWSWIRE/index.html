Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow requirement under Cyprus law; shares may be denominated in EUR or any lawful currency and issued at par value as determined by the subscribers. No escrow account is required for a private limited liability company.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Fire Prevention and Fire Safety Law (Law 115(I)/2004) for office premises, including issuance of a Fire Safety Certificate where occupancy or risk level requires it; structural load and electrical safety inspection certificate from an approved building inspector or electrical engineer prior to occupancy; occupational health and safety clearance under the Safety and Health at Work Law (Law 89(I)/1996), covering first aid, workstation assessment, and emergency evacuation plans. The registered office must be a physical address in Cyprus with adequate egress for the number of occupants.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow requirement under Cyprus law; shares may be denominated in EUR or any lawful currency and issued at par value as determined by the subscribers. No escrow account is required for a private limited liability company.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus (Tmima Forologias); official taxpayer portal: Tax Portal (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy) and the 'Tax For All' digital filing platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign promoter first incorporates a Cyprus Private Limited Company with the Cyprus Registrar of Companies, obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation and the HE-prefixed registration number, which serves as the base Tax Identification Number; the Registrar automatically notifies the Department of Taxation, which issues the final TIN certifying the company as a Cyprus tax resident. VAT registration is a separate mandatory step: the entity files Form T.C. 1064 (Application for VAT Registration) with the competent District VAT Office, attaching incorporation documents, registered office certificate, director/shareholder particulars, and, for non-established foreign entities, a declaration appointing an approved fiscal representative in Cyprus; upon approval the Tax Department issues the VAT registration certificate and confirmation of the 19% standard VAT accounting obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign promoter first incorporates a Cyprus Private Limited Company with the Cyprus Registrar of Companies, obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation and the HE-prefixed registration number, which serves as the base Tax Identification Number; the Registrar automatically notifies the Department of Taxation, which issues the final TIN certifying the company as a Cyprus tax resident. VAT registration is a separate mandatory step: the entity files Form T.C. 1064 (Application for VAT Registration) with the competent District VAT Office, attaching incorporation documents, registered office certificate, director/shareholder particulars, and, for non-established foreign entities, a declaration appointing an approved fiscal representative in Cyprus; upon approval the Tax Department issues the VAT registration certificate and confirmation of the 19% standard VAT accounting obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification follows the corporate legal personality, and a Cyprus Ltd is automatically taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the 12.5% corporate income tax rate when managed and controlled in Cyprus. Available statutory elections include: (i) group loss relief election under section 36 of the Income Tax Law permitting intra-group surrender of tax losses; (ii) VAT group registration election under the Cyprus VAT Law allowing associated persons to be treated as one taxable person; (iii) tonnage tax election for qualifying vessel operations; (iv) capital allowance uplift elections for plant and machinery; and (v) electoral transparency treatment for contractual joint ventures. Hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatch restrictions under the EU ATAD implementation prevent election-driven double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 annual taxable turnover for supplies of goods and €7,500 annual taxable turnover for supplies of services; no registration threshold applies to non-established/non-resident suppliers; an additional €10,000 EU-wide annual threshold applies to cross-border distance sales and electronically supplied services eligible for the One Stop Shop (OSS) scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment is available where goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the European Union by the supplier, the recipient, or an agent acting on either's behalf, and the statutory export evidence is obtained; Cyprus practice requires the goods to leave the EU within 90 days of the supply. Eligible flows include direct exports, triangular/re-export arrangements from EU customs warehouses, and mere transports to non-EU destinations. Exported financial-market news wire content and syndicated finance information services supplied to non-EU B2B customers qualify for zero-rating under the place-of-supply rules, no VAT being chargeable and the input VAT being recoverable when the customer is established in a third jurisdiction; the supply must be supported by documentary proof of the customer's non-EU establishment and service delivery records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document/SAD) and Electronic Export System (ECS) movement certificate evidencing release of goods to a destination outside the EU
  • Transportation and title evidence: Bill of Lading, CMR/International Consignment Note, air waybill, or postal/courier export receipt showing the exact goods, consignee, and exit route from Cyprus/EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or authenticated SWIFT/Stripe payment confirmation demonstrating receipt of the export sales proceeds from the non-EU buyer into the Cyprus entity's bank account

Cyprus has not enacted a mandatory domestic electronic invoicing regime or a real-time fiscal clearance model; invoices may be paper-based or electronic provided the authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility are preserved through a business-control trail, electronic signatures, or EDI. VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and Intrastat declarations must be filed electronically through the Tax Portal; as of the 2025 platform cut-off there is no government-side digital stamping, API interconnection, or real-time transaction clearance requirement.

Where the exporter applies zero-rating without a complete documentary bundle, the Department of Taxation re-assesses the supply at 19% VAT with statutory interest running from the original filing date; fraudulent export zero-rating claims constitute a criminal offence under the VAT Law and section 65 of the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, exposing the Cyprus Ltd and its directors to penalties of up to twice the tax evaded, additional fines of up to €10,000, imprisonment of up to five years, and permanent revocation of VAT registration. The Tax Department also applies retrospective clawbacks across the preceding five assessment years where audit findings show sham exports, circular payment flows, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day reference window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible. Cyprus, as an EU member state since 2004 and Eurozone member since 1 January 2008, applies the EU Capital Movement Directive (88/361/EEC) and has abolished all exchange-control restrictions under the former Exchange Control Law (Cap. 199). Both current-account and capital-account transactions are liberalised for EU and third-country counterparties. Profit repatriation via dividends is unrestricted, subject to 0% Cyprus withholding tax, and requires only standard bank verification (audited financials, directors' resolution, and source-of-funds documentation). Capital-account transfers are subject solely to statistical Balance-of-Payments reporting by the resident bank under IMF BPM6 classification; no prior Central Bank approval or forex permit is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, repatriation timeline, or holding account requirement on export proceeds or service/API subscription receivables. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%; forced conversion timeline: N/A. Inbound subscription and licensing revenue is classified under BPM6 services ('Software and data subscription services') for statistical purposes only, with no conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) in coordination with the Cyprus Ministry of Finance for balance-of-payments statistics and EU capital-movement policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate-High as a supplementary treasury rail, but not as the primary bank. An EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under the Electronic Money Law N. 81(I)/2018, or a PSD2-licensed PI passporting into Cyprus, is viable for multi-currency virtual IBANs and vendor payments. Offshore digital EMIs incorporated outside the EEA (e.g., UK, Hong Kong, Singapore, unlicensed challenger banks) are not viable as a primary clearing rail because Cypriot Tier-1 banks will not treat them as an acceptable treasury anchor and Stripe payouts require settlement into a regulated bank account. Optimal structure: a Cyprus Tier-1 commercial bank account as the anchor liquidity hub; an EEA-passported EMI as a low-value disbursement overlay for outbound vendor wires below EUR 10,000 per transaction.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Misclassification of the financial-newswire API business as a regulated MiFID or CySEC investment-services activity, triggering demands for a licence before account opening.

Root cause: Misclassification of the financial-newswire API business as a regulated MiFID or CySEC investment-services activity, triggering demands for a licence before account opening.

Defense evidence: Independent Cyprus legal opinion under ISIC 6391 confirming the entity only aggregates, syndicates, and licenses news/data via API; subscriber contracts and terms-of-service; a technology diagram showing no handling of client money, no order execution, and no investment advice; and confirmation that no CySEC or Central Bank of Cyprus licence is required.
Fragmented inbound micro-payments via Stripe (global card settlement and SEPA payouts) flagged as unverifiable third-party receipts.

Root cause: Fragmented inbound micro-payments via Stripe (global card settlement and SEPA payouts) flagged as unverifiable third-party receipts.

Defense evidence: 12-month revenue waterfall by subscriber; Stripe merchant agreement and settlement statements; audited subscriber ledger reconciling at least 95% of inflows; a Statutory Declaration signed by directors; and a letter from the external auditor confirming recurring subscription revenue patterns.
Non-resident UBOs with cross-border wealth structures triggering enhanced due diligence.

Root cause: Non-resident UBOs with cross-border wealth structures triggering enhanced due diligence.

Defense evidence: Notarised passport copies of all directors and UBOs; 12 months of personal bank statements for each UBO; source-of-wealth report prepared by a Cyprus certified public accountant; full corporate registry extracts for the ownership chain; and tax-residency certificates from each UBO jurisdiction.
High-velocity outbound disbursements to international SaaS/data vendors flagged as possible sanction-evasion or trade-redirection risk.

Root cause: High-velocity outbound disbursements to international SaaS/data vendors flagged as possible sanction-evasion or trade-redirection risk.

Defense evidence: Whitelist of vendors with full legal entity details and country of incorporation; BPM6 purpose codes on each outbound wire (e.g., '9411 Software licenses and SaaS subscriptions'); OFAC/EU sanction-screening reports attached to each disbursement above EUR 10,000; and vendor contracts evidencing arm's-length commercial terms.

Supporting documents: board resolution, share subscription forms, and a Cyprus Tax Department certificate confirming no capital duty (abolished 2011).

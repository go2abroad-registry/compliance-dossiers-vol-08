Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance; online services portal: tax.mof.gov.cy (TaxisNet for VAT and corporate income tax filings)

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus private limited company is automatically registered for tax via the Registrar of Companies and receives an 8-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) within 60 days of incorporation. Foreign entities without Cyprus incorporation must appoint a Cyprus tax representative and submit a VAT registration application (VAT 101) alongside certified certificates of incorporation, constitutional documents, director details, and evidence of a physical business address in Cyprus. Non-established suppliers of digital services must register for VAT from the first supply, without any turnover threshold, and can use the non-EU OSS scheme for intra-EU B2C supplies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Cyprus private limited company is automatically registered for tax via the Registrar of Companies and receives an 8-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) within 60 days of incorporation. Foreign entities without Cyprus incorporation must appoint a Cyprus tax representative and submit a VAT registration application (VAT 101) alongside certified certificates of incorporation, constitutional documents, director details, and evidence of a physical business address in Cyprus. Non-established suppliers of digital services must register for VAT from the first supply, without any turnover threshold, and can use the non-EU OSS scheme for intra-EU B2C supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election is available; a Cyprus Ltd is ipso facto a separate taxable entity. Elections that may be applicable include group loss relief, notional interest deduction on new qualifying equity, the IP box regime (for qualifying software/assets), and tonnage tax for shipping; hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD must be considered in cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 in any 12-month period for taxable supplies; €35,000 for intra-Community distance sales of goods; €10,000 for cross-border B2C digital services supplied by an established EU supplier; no threshold for non-established suppliers

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The zero rating of goods applies when physical export from the EU is evidenced by customs documentation and goods depart the EU within 3 months after supply. For VPN/network security digital services, zero rating applies where the place of supply is outside the EU: a B2B supply requires a valid foreign VAT registration number or business establishment evidence; a B2C supply requires two non-conflicting indicators such as IP address, country of bank card issuer, billing address, or subscriber phone country code. Supplies to EU customers do not qualify for zero rating and must follow VAT accounting, if necessary through the OSS scheme.

  • Customs export declaration / Single Administrative Document (for physical exports)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / carrier manifest evidencing departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice / supply contract
  • Evidence of payment (bank transfer receipt or card settlement statement)
  • Foreign recipient identification and establishment documentation (company register extract or VAT number)

Cyprus does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping. VAT returns must be filed electronically via TaxisNet on a quarterly or annual basis. E-invoicing is optional except for public procurement, where the PEPPOL BIS format is required for certain transactions.

Cyprus maintains no exchange-control requirement for repatriation of export proceeds; the 0-day parameter reflects the absence of a statutory FX deadline. The Tax Department can reopen assessments for up to six years, with no time limit in cases of fraud. Erroneous zero rating is reversed with VAT assessed at 19% plus statutory default interest and a monetary penalty not exceeding the liability evaded; knowingly presenting false export documents may result in criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment of directors or authorised tax representatives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in both current and capital accounts. Since Cyprus's adoption of the euro and full EU liberalisation, no foreign-exchange approval is required for profit remittance, dividends, or capital inflows/outflows. Annual corporate formalities and ECB Balance of Payments reporting apply for transactions exceeding €12,500. Non-resident shareholders may remit dividends without withholding tax, subject to Cyprus tax residence, management/control substance, and beneficial ownership validation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus does not impose any statutory timeline, surrender ratio, or mandatory conversion requirement on export proceeds. Exporters may retain receivables in any convertible currency; however, all receipts must be reflected in the corporate books and reported through the CBC's statistical data collection (International Transactions Reporting). For intra-group trade, transfer pricing documentation under OECD BEPS guidelines must be maintained.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High, with material legal bounds. Cyprus-authorised e-money institutions (EMIs) under the Electronic Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and Payment Services Directive (EU) 2015/2366 are viable and can provide SEPA/instant SEPA, multi-currency IBANs, and GBP/USD rails. Non-EEA digital EMIs (e.g., UK-authorised or Lithuania-licensed) are generally accepted only if passporting or reverse-solicitation is compliant; VASP/crypto-linked EMIs are commonly declined due to AML risk framing. The company must maintain actual substance in Cyprus and use a licensed Tier-1 bank as the ultimate liquidation anchor.

Section G Related party transactions disclosure Declare all intercompany transactions with non-resident affiliates, including licensing of software, marketing services, and group financing. Attach TP documentation when thresholds are met. Late filing of TD4 incurs a EUR 100 fixed penalty, plus a monthly penalty of EUR 50 per month of delay (maximum EUR 500). If filed more than six months late, an additional EUR 100 penalty is imposed. Late payment of tax attracts interest at 4% per annum and an automatic 5% surcharge on the unpaid tax. Failure to file may also trigger estimated assessment, and severe or repeated non-compliance can lead to escalation and criminal fines.
Section H Foreign tax credits If foreign income is taxed overseas and also subject to Cyprus corporate tax, provide foreign tax vouchers/TRC and claim the credit within the tax return filing deadline. Late filing of TD4 incurs a EUR 100 fixed penalty, plus a monthly penalty of EUR 50 per month of delay (maximum EUR 500). If filed more than six months late, an additional EUR 100 penalty is imposed. Late payment of tax attracts interest at 4% per annum and an automatic 5% surcharge on the unpaid tax. Failure to file may also trigger estimated assessment, and severe or repeated non-compliance can lead to escalation and criminal fines.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 in annual gross related-party transactions for Local File preparation. A Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750 million. Even below the threshold, contemporaneous TP documentation should be maintained to prove arm's length pricing.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) through EU ATAD anti-avoidance principles and the OECD Principal Purpose Test. A Cyprus company deriving VPN licensing or SaaS revenues must demonstrate substantive business activity in Cyprus: a physical office, qualified employees, board meeting minutes in Cyprus, effective management, and decision-making power. The Cyprus Tax Department can challenge paper structures and deny the 12.5% rate or treaty benefits if the company is considered a shell or conduit. The intangibles development, enhancement, maintenance, protection, and exploitation (DEMPE) functions must be performed in Cyprus.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Cyprus Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law 1978, with imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fines up to EUR 50,000. Civil penalties can reach 10% for negligence or 30% for intentional fraud, plus interest at 4% per annum.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for false returns under the Companies Law. Courts may pierce the corporate veil where directors have acted dishonestly, resulting in civil restitution of hidden assets, travel bans issued against directors, disqualification orders, and in flagrant cases, imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department may re-characterize the arrangement, deny tax residence certificate, impose TP adjustments, and disallow the NID and IP Box benefits. The company may also face deregistration or strike-off under the Cyprus Companies Law. Administrative penalties up to EUR 100,000 may apply under ESR-related enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors failing to ensure factual substance risk personal liability for taxes if the company is struck-off. Civil proceedings may be brought to recover distributions or loans made without substance, and travel restrictions may be issued while the matter is under investigation.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Cyprus AML Law L.188(I)/2007, severe fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or 2 times the property laundered, and imprisonment up to 8 years for natural persons. MOKAS can impose supervisory penalties, and the company's banking facilities may be frozen.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners can be criminally prosecuted for failing to prevent money laundering. Travel bans, asset freezing orders, and extradition requests are possible. Under AML law, the corporate veil is pierced where the entity is a vehicle for laundering, leading to personal liability.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Cyprus tax law allows TP adjustments with a 20% penalty on additional tax assessed, plus 4% interest and late payment surcharges. Intangibles-related profit shifting can trigger re-characterization of the entire VPN business model.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or participate in falsifying TP documentation face personal fines and civil claims for resulting tax losses. In serious cases, intentional concealment can lead to criminal liability for tax evasion and imprisonment.
VAT and Digital Services Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Compulsory VAT registration is back-dated, with penalties up to 100% of the unpaid VAT. Cross-border EU digital services are subject to VAT in the customer's country; non-compliance can trigger multi-jurisdictional assessments and penalties. The Cyprus VAT Commissioner can impose fines up to EUR 20,000.

Director personal liability: In liquidation or strike-off scenarios, directors may be personally liable for VAT debts where they continued trading without paying VAT. Criminal prosecution is possible for deliberate VAT evasion, with imprisonment up to 3 years.

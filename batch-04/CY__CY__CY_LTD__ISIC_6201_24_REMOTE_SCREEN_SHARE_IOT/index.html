Accepted. Under Companies Law Cap. 113 s.7 a Cyprus Ltd must maintain a physical registered office in Cyprus for service of process and retention of statutory records. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (T.D.C.), operating under the Ministry of Finance, with electronic filing via the TAXISnet portal (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy). For VAT, the VAT Directorate of the Tax Department; for employer registrations, the Social Insurance Services.

E-government filing portal

For a Cyprus Private Limited Company: (1) Incorporate at the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver to obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and Articles of Association. (2) Procure a registered office in Cyprus and appoint a Cyprus-resident company secretary. (3) Register the company for income tax with the Tax Department via TAXISnet, using the official company registration procedure, and obtain the Taxpayer Identification Number (T.I.N.)/Company Tax Identification code. (4) For VAT, file a Form VAT 101 application for VAT registration with the VAT Commissioner, attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, T.I.N. certificate, registered office details, and evidence of business activity (anticipated turnover in Cyprus). (5) If the company makes intra-Community supplies/acquisitions, obtain a Cyprus VAT number and ensure validation under V.I.E.S. (6) If the company has no Cyprus-source income or taxable supplies, nevertheless file nil/dormant tax returns as required. (7) Register as an employer with Social Insurance Services and obtain an employer registration number if employing persons. For a foreign parent, non-resident ownership is permissible and no local shareholder residence or minimum capital is required for a private limited liability company.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus has no check-the-box look-through election; a Cyprus Ltd is always an entity-level taxpayer under the Income Tax Law. Statutory elections include group relief elections, notional interest deduction, IP box regime, VAT grouping, and various capital allowance elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for Cyprus-established persons; EUR 10,000 for intra-Community distance sales and certain intra-Community acquisitions; no threshold for non-established suppliers supplying electronically supplied services (B2C) to Cyprus consumers under the EU OSS scheme, or for suppliers required to self-charge reverse VAT on services supplied to Cyprus businesses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to physical goods exported outside the EU where the supplier retains proof of export. For remote screen-share and IoT software services provided cross-border: (a) if supplied to a business customer in another EU member state, the supply is taxed under the reverse charge in the customer's member state and outside the scope of Cyprus VAT; (b) if supplied to a non-EU business or consumer, the place of supply is outside the EU, so the supply is outside the scope of Cyprus VAT, not zero-rated. The entity should evidence the customer's business status (valid VAT/registration number), the place of establishment, the nature of the electronic supply, and the absence of effective use and enjoyment in Cyprus, where applicable.

  • Customs export declaration via the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department (electronic Single Administrative Document / Export Declaration)
  • Commercial invoice describing goods and value
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical movement outside the EU
  • Customs release and export accompanying document (EAD) or equivalent
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing export proceeds received from the foreign buyer
  • Evidence of the customer's non-resident status (e.g., VAT registration certificate, tax identification number, or signed declaration)

Cyprus does not operate a real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance system for all enterprises. There is no national mandatory electronic invoicing mandate for B2B supplies; however, e-invoicing in PEPPOL/EN16931 format is accepted for public procurement. Cyprus uses TAXISnet and V.I.E.S. for periodic VAT returns and recapitulative statements. Under EU proposals (ViDA), future digital reporting requirements may apply after 2030. Entities must issue valid VAT invoices for domestic supplies and self-bill or obtain self-invoices for reverse-charge services; invoices must contain all EU VAT invoice details.

Under the Cyprus VAT Law, fraudulent export zero-rating or incorrect claims may result in a VAT assessment, interest at statutory rates, administrative fines up to twice the VAT evaded, and criminal penalties including imprisonment. The Tax Department may impose a penalty of 1% per month of unpaid tax (or other applicable statutory penalty), late registration penalties, and may deny input VAT deductions. Retrospective assessments may extend up to six years (or longer in cases of fraud). Failure to comply with foreign-exchange repatriation deadlines may lead to deemed taxable proceeds, clawback of zero-rating benefits, and additional income/corporate tax exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

As a full member of the Eurozone since 1 January 2008, Cyprus maintains fully convertible capital and current accounts. There is no separate national exchange-control authority and no requirement for exchange-control permission on investments, dividends, loan repayments, or transfer of liquidation proceeds. The free movement of capital is guaranteed under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, subject only to EU-wide safeguards and national security measures in exceptional circumstances. Profit remittances may be implemented through normal banking channels, with corporate law formalities such as directors' resolutions and distributable profits testing, and tax clearance or certification where withholding tax applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Central Bank of Cyprus abolished foreign exchange surrender requirements upon euro adoption. Export receipts are freely transferable and may be retained in foreign currency or converted to EUR at market rates; no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation window applies. Reporting to the Central Bank may be required only for statistical balance-of-payments surveys and EU investment questionnaire purposes.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα Κύπρου) acting as the national central bank of the Eurosystem and the competent authority for payment systems, electronic money institutions, and credit institutions under the Cyprus Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Law L.188(I)/2007, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Cyprus accepts EU/EEA-authorised electronic money institutions under passporting and payments initiation regulations, but Tier-1 Cypriot banks may be reluctant to clear funds routed through unauthorised third-country EMIs. A PSD2-licensed EMI or a Cypriot-authorised EMI provides payment accounts and virtual IBANs for software disbursements, but for long-term treasury operations a resident commercial bank account is strongly preferred due to strict de-risking practices in the Cypriot banking sector.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound share-capital in EUR or foreign currency is by SWIFT MT103 wire to the company's EUR IBAN. The sending bank must reference the purpose code 'C10' (inward remittance for business operations) or an equivalent, or include the life cycle of 'Capital Subscription' with the company name and registration number. The receiving bank applies standard verification of the capital amount against the authorised share capital, confirms the receipt through a capital certificate, and the company must file the return of allotment with the Cyprus Registrar within one month. For currency conversion, the bank executes the applicable EUR rate without exchange-control approval; however, anti-money-laundering verification of the remitter and UBOs applies for increases above EUR 50,000.Software subscription and licensing payments from Cyprus are streamed by: (i) SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR settlement; (ii) SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR for European clients; (iii) TARGET2 for high-value euro payments; and (iv) commercial letters of credit only where required by large enterprise contracts. For royalties and trade payments, the Cyprus company must retain a corporate resolution, a valid invoice issued under VAT rules, and, where applicable, a transfer-pricing analysis supporting the arm's-length character. Payment clearing via Cyprus banks is same-day or next-day, with no exchange-control approval or export proceeds surrender requirements.Outright foreign exchange forward contracts (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP) with a Cyprus-authorised investment firm or international Tier-1 bank, provided underlying exposure is evidenced by commercial invoices and the hedge is for risk management only.Non-deliverable forward contracts on liquid currencies with counterparties regulated under EMIR, permissible for hedging mismatched invoice cash flows where physical delivery is not available, subject to mandatory clearing and margining thresholds.Cross-currency interest rate swaps or floating-floating swaps under ISDA 2002 or an EMIR-compliant framework, entered only for matching non-EUR assets or liabilities and with appropriate ISDA counterparty credit-risk and collateral agreements.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. A PSD2-licensed EMI or a Cypriot-authorised EMI provides payment accounts and virtual IBANs for software disbursements, but for long-term treasury operations a resident commercial bank account is strongly preferred due to strict de-risking practices in the Cypriot banking sector.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 per controlled transaction category (goods, services) and EUR 250,000 for financing/royalty transactions; Master File documentation required for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has not adopted BVI-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the Tax Department applies the OECD substance standard and EU ATAD anti-abuse doctrines. A Cyprus company must maintain adequate premises, qualified staff, and real management and control in Cyprus to access DTAs, retain tax residence, and avoid classification as a shell company. The remote screen share and IoT business model must be backed by active technical staff, service contracts, board meetings and decision-making in Cyprus.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Section 107 of the Cyprus Income Tax Law (L.118(I)/2002), deliberate tax evasion attracts a criminal fine of up to EUR 20,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years. On the civil side, the Tax Commissioner may assess additional tax of up to double the amount evaded plus interest and default surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for fraudulent trading under Section 307 of the Companies Law (Cap. 113). Courts can lift the corporate veil, issue immediate asset-freezing injunctions, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years, or order full restitution of the underpaid tax and penalties.
Shell Company or Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Tax Commissioner may disapply the arrangement under the general anti-abuse rules, deny treaty benefits, attribute the income to the ultimate shareholders, and impose additional assessments of 100%–200% of the tax shortfall. The Registrar of Companies may also strike the entity off the register.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and beneficial owners face personal assessment for the unpaid corporate tax, civil fines up to EUR 500,000, disqualification from directorships for 10 years, and criminal prosecution where the arrangement involves fraudulent misrepresentation.
Anti-Money-Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Sanctions include fines of up to EUR 500,000 for legal entities, confiscation of proceeds, corrective administrative directions, and revocation of any payment-processing or financial licence. The Cyprus Unit for Combating Money Laundering (MOKAS) may institute criminal proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors and the AML officer can receive imprisonment of up to 5 years and personal fines equal to double the laundered amount. Additionally, travel bans and freezing orders may be imposed pending investigation, and professional disqualification may be ordered by the court.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Tax Department may make a transfer-pricing adjustment, increase corporate income by the full transaction value, disallow deductions, apply interest, and impose an administrative penalty of up to 10% of the additional tax assessed. Persistent violations trigger higher penalty multipliers and mandatory publication in the tax defaulters list.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or knowingly allow non-compliant tax returns are personally liable to the extent of the additional tax, interest and penalties. In severe cases, criminal prosecution for wilful negligence under Cyprus tax law can lead to imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine of up to EUR 20,000.

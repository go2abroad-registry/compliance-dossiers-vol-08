Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), reporting to the Ministry of Finance; digital filing through the ARIADNE Tax Portal at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy. Customs and export procedures are executed by the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department (Τμήμα Τελωνείων), also under the Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the private limited liability company (HE77 register code) with the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (DROCOR). 2) File Form TCF 126 (Income Tax Registration) with the Tax Department within 60 days of incorporation; the Tax Department issues a Taxpayer Registration Certificate containing the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). 3) Register under the Value Added Tax Law 95(I)/2000 via Form VAT 101 once taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600 in any rolling 12-month period, or when intra-Community acquisitions exceed EUR 10,000; non-established foreign suppliers must VAT-register before the first taxable supply in Cyprus without qualifying for any threshold. 4) Register the company as an employer with the Social Insurance Services and with the Health Insurance Organization (GHS/GESY) if staff are engaged. 5) Activate VIES / EC Sales List status in the ARIADNE e-filing system when intra-EU B2B supplies of goods or services are made.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Cyprus Ltd is a separate taxable person under the Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002, subject to a unified 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide taxable profits; shareholders are taxed separately on distributions; no pass-through classification is available. Territorial carve-outs (dividend exemption, foreign permanent establishment exemption, IP Box deduction, Notional Interest Deduction) apply; Cyprus is neither a tax-exempt zone nor a territorial-only regime.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate the U.S. check-the-box election; hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD2 are applied mandatorily. The principal statutory elections available include: (a) the Notional Interest Deduction election on increases in new equity (Article 36A of Law 118(I)/2002); (b) the IP Box election conferring an 80% deduction on qualifying intangible asset profits; (c) establishing tax residence by electing to exercise central management and control in Cyprus; (d) group relief elections for surrender of losses among 75%-held EEA group members; and (e) the deemed dividend distribution election for SDC/CY taxation planning. All elections are effected in the annual corporate income tax return (Form TD2021) with contemporaneous board minutes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 of taxable turnover (goods or local services) in any consecutive 12-month period; EUR 10,000 for intra-Community acquisitions/distance sales; nil threshold for non-established foreign suppliers supplying taxable services or goods in Cyprus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: 0% VAT applies when goods are physically dispatched outside the EU by or on behalf of the supplier and the supply is evidenced by an accepted Customs Export Declaration (SAD), transport documentation and commercial invoice retained in the supplier's records; the goods must leave the EU within the period contemplated by the supply contract and customs rules. Intra-EU B2B consignment supplies of server hardware to VAT-registered customers are zero-rated subject to valid VIES registration of the recipient and EC Sales List reporting. Offshore services — data center colocation, rack space leasing, bare-metal server hosting, cross-connects and remote-hands services supplied to a business established outside Cyprus — fall outside the scope of Cyprus VAT in accordance with the B2B place-of-supply rule (Article 12(12)(a) of Law 95(I)/2000), so no Cypriot VAT is chargeable on the cross-border fee; the supplier must evidence the customer's business status, VAT or tax registration identity, a natural business purpose, and the contractual terms. B2C supplies to non-EU individuals may be placed in the customer's jurisdiction for digital/hosting-type services if the customer-location rules are met; physical rack-space services of a maintenance-like character remain subject to Cyprus VAT at 19%. Input VAT on electricity, cooling, bandwidth, equipment and maintenance is recoverable to the extent supplies are taxable or outside-scope-with-credit, with pro-rata restriction only for incidental exempt income.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / SAD) validated in the Cyprus Customs Thalassa/NECHOS system
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note or rail waybill proving exit from the European Union
  • Commercial invoice stating the goods, quantities, prices, Incoterms and foreign buyer's identity
  • Packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Intrastat and EC Sales List declarations for intra-EU goods movements (where applicable)
  • Retained VIES confirmation of the customer's non-Cyprus VAT registration number for intra-EU zero-rated supplies

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system is in force in Cyprus today. ARIADNE accepts electronic VAT returns (monthly/quarterly) and EC Sales Lists; invoices may be paper or European-standard e-invoices (EDI or qualified electronic signature); full invoices must be issued within 30 days of the tax point. Cyprus has not deployed an SdI-type national API clearance interface, but the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package will require Union-standard e-invoicing and periodic transaction reporting for intra-EU B2B supplies in the period up to 2035, with Cyprus expected to operate a national acceptance/clearance interface on ARIADNE by the applicable deadline.

Export zero-rating is verified by document-based audits of the Tax Department; missing or contradictory export evidence triggers reassessment at 19% VAT on gross consideration plus statutory interest, late-payment charges and default penalties. The VAT Law permits assessments within the standard limitation window of four years from the return period, extended in cases of negligence or deliberate omission and unlimited for fraudulent evasion. Fictitious or bill-only export schemes ('phantom exports') cause clawback of every input VAT credit previously claimed, civil penalty multipliers up to 100% of the tax evaded, criminal fines up to EUR 10,000 and imprisonment of up to 2 years for wilful evasion; parallel sanctions may be imposed under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law 4/1978. For export proceeds, the 180-day reference is banking and documentary-credit practice for inward remittance substantiation; although Cyprus has abolished statutory exchange controls, the Tax Department may treat unrepatriated export proceeds as evidence that goods or services remained for domestic consumption and deny zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus operates a fully euro-based monetary regime. Current-account and capital-account convertibility are unrestricted for genuine trade, service, capital, and profit-repatriation flows, subject only to EU sanctions, EU capital-movement surveillance under Articles 63-66 TFEU, and heightened AML/KYC controls. There are no Cyprus-specific restrictions on dividend, interest, royalty, or equity-sale remittances by non-resident shareholders; Cyprus does not impose withholding tax on outbound dividends, interest, or royalties provided the beneficial owner is a non-resident not conducting business through a Cyprus permanent establishment. Remittances must be routed through a licensed bank and supported by underlying contractual, invoicing, or corporate governance documentation to avoid incorrect statistical classification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no statutory surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; exporters may lawfully retain foreign currency in external accounts. All export proceeds must be repatriated through licensed financial channels for balance-of-payments statistics within normal banking practice, typically 30-60 days according to invoice terms, and must be supported by invoice, customs declaration where applicable, and bank reference data. For colocation and data-center leasing under ISIC 6209 / NAICS 518210, export income qualifies as service income and must be evidenced by service-level agreements, usage-based billing, and valid VAT status where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza Kyprou / Κεντρική Τράπεζα Κύπρου), acting as the national competent authority within the Eurosystem and under the Central Bank of Cyprus Law, together with the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) for investment and FX product conduct supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditional Medium feasibility. Non-EU offshore digital EMIs cannot operate as principal account providers in Cyprus without either an EEA payment-institution passport under Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2) or a locally licensed EMI authorisation granted by the Central Bank of Cyprus. Any inbound or outbound capital flow passing through an unauthorized unregulated wallet or EMI may be rejected by Cyprus's correspondent banking network and will trigger enhanced AML scrutiny. An EEA-licensed EMI may serve as a secondary low-value FX and clearing rail, but it is not a substitute for a Tier-1 domestic bank account for capital escrow, commercial letters of credit, or investment-grade counterparty evidence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cyprus EUR (€): mandatory Local File and Summary Transfer Pricing Documentation required if aggregate related-party transactions exceed €750,000 per tax year; Master File required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue equal to or exceeding €750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies no standalone Economic Substance Regulations but enforces substance requirements through OECD Nexus Approach for IP Box, ATAD GAAR, CFC rules, and exchange-of-rulings precedents: a colocation leasing company must maintain physical premises, operational staff, decision-making boards, and actual client contracts in Cyprus; bare arrangements without staff or premises are recharacterized as abuse and stripped of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Cyprus Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law and Criminal Code; financial penalty up to EUR 20,000 per offense, additional tax, compound interest of 2% per month, and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false IR501 returns are jointly and severally liable for restitution; courts may pierce the corporate veil, impose travel bans, disqualify directors, and order personal seizure of bank accounts.
Persistent Non-Filing / Non-Payment

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties accumulate: EUR 100 fixed fine plus EUR 50 per month; late-payment surcharge of 5% plus 2% monthly compound interest; the Tax Department may issue estimated assessments at 12.5% on audited revenues and require levy action.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid corporate tax to the extent they retained assets externally; personal director disqualification orders and asset freezes apply, and a director may face criminal penalty up to EUR 10,000.
AML / Financial Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Violations under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (L.188(I)/2007) carry fines up to EUR 500,000, confiscation of funds, and imprisonment up to 5 years; the entity may also be struck off from the Cyprus Companies Registry.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge of criminal proceeds are criminally liable as principals; courts impose joint restitution orders, 5- to 10-year travel bans, disqualification from board positions, and seizure of personal assets.

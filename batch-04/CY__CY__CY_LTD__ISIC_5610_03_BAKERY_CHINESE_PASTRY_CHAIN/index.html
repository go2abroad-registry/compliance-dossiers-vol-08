Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance; online portal: ARIADNI (https://ariadni.mof.gov.cy). Customs and export procedures are administered by the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department through the THESEAS electronic export system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporation with the Department of Registrar of Companies (DRC) under Companies Law Cap. 113; the company obtains its HE registration number and Certificate of Incorporation. Step 2: Since 2019 the Tax Department automatically issues the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) for income tax purposes upon incorporation; confirmation is retrievable via the ARIADNI portal using the HE number. Step 3 (VAT): a Cyprus-established company files Form VAT 101 electronically; a foreign or non-established entity files the same VAT 101 through a mandatory Cyprus tax representative, submitting certified copies of the certificate of incorporation (or foreign equivalent), memorandum/articles of association, registered office address evidence, director/shareholder UBO identification documents, forecast of taxable turnover, and the representative's appointment letter. VAT registration is compulsory once taxable turnover exceeds the statutory threshold or immediately for deemed registrations covering intra-Community acquisitions and reverse-charge service receipts. Issued VAT numbers follow the EU VIES format 'CY' + 8 digits + 1 letter. Processing time is approximately 30 working days; registration becomes effective from the date of application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Cyprus; classification is fixed by statute for a private limited liability company as a separate taxable person resident and managed in Cyprus, subject to the 12.5% corporate income tax rate. Available elections: (a) VAT group registration election under Section 18 of the Value Added Tax Law (Law 95(I)/2000); (b) option to tax (waiver of exemption) on immovable property supplies under Section 19; (c) election for deemed dividend distribution treatment for family companies (Section 37, Income Tax Law); (d) tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping companies (inapplicable to ISIC 5610 bakery operations); (e) elections/designations required to neutralise hybrid mismatch outcomes under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) as transposed in the Cyprus Income Tax Law, including notifications for hybrid financial instruments and reverse hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; non-established suppliers making taxable supplies in Cyprus must register without regard to the threshold when reverse-charge or distance-selling exceptions apply; intra-Community acquisitions make VAT registration mandatory regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) applies under Sections 2 and 3 of the First Schedule to the VAT Law for: (a) exports of goods physically dispatched or transported to destinations outside the EU by or on behalf of the supplier, evidenced by customs export declarations and transport documents; (b) intra-Community supplies of goods to customers holding a valid EU VAT identification number verified through VIES, including frozen bakery and pastry shipments to franchisees in other Member States; (c) international services and royalty/licence fee payments (e.g. master franchise royalties) supplied to a business customer established outside the EU, whose place of business is verified by documentary evidence; (d) services ancillary to export movements of goods. Royalties received from EU-resident franchisees are outside the scope of Cyprus VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule and subject to reverse charge in the customer's Member State, rather than zero-rated by Cyprus. The supplier must retain full documentary proof of dispatch, customer business status, and payment of proceeds to defend the 0% claim.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document or electronic export declaration lodged via Cyprus Customs THESEAS) with the Movement Reference Number (MRN); for intra-EU supplies, Intrastat arrival/delivery declarations and transport dispatches replace the customs declaration
  • Commercial invoice stating the applied VAT treatment, including the customer's valid EU VAT ID (VIES) for intra-EU zero-rating or the explicit '0% Export' annotation referencing the export MRN for non-EU shipments
  • Transport documentation: Bill of Lading (sea freight), Air Waybill (air freight), CMR consignment note (road freight), or courier delivery confirmation with recipient signature and shipment tracking reference
  • Proof of payment and foreign exchange repatriation: Inward Remittance Certificate, bank transfer advices, or SWIFT records evidencing receipt of export proceeds and royalty remittances from the foreign franchisee

Cyprus does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime; no mandatory API-based clearance. Electronic invoicing is voluntary in the B2B sector but required for B2G transactions in line with Directive 2014/55/EU, using the Peppol/EN 16931 standard. VAT returns (Form VAT 400) and Intrastat declarations must be filed electronically via the ARIADNI portal on a quarterly basis (Category B) or monthly basis (Category A) depending on annual turnover. Original e-invoices and underlying export evidence must be retained in readable format for audit inspection; a tax representative is mandatory for non-established taxable persons.

Cyprus VAT authorities may issue assessments within a 4-year limitation period, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or intentional error; assessments carry interest at 9% per annum plus penalties up to 100% of the assessed tax. Unjustified or fraudulent zero-rating claims, including exports to fictitious shell customers or incomplete transport evidence, trigger retrospective VAT clawback at the standard rate plus penalties. Criminal liability under the VAT Law: on conviction for attempting to evade VAT, a fine of up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years, with the court empowered to order payment of the evaded tax. The tax representative and any person possessing the goods are jointly and severally liable for clawback assessments where export substantiation is defective.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full euro-area member since 1 January 2008 and applies the EU Treaty's free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU). Both current-account transactions (trade goods, franchise royalties, services, staff remuneration) and capital-account transactions (FDI equity injections, shareholder loans, dividend/profit repatriation, sale proceeds of shares) are freely convertible and freely remittable in EUR without any prior exchange-control approval, licensing, or ministerial consent. There is no currency rationing, no bifurcated official/market rate, and no special tax or levy on outward remittances. Practical frictions are limited to AML/KYC bank due diligence (source-of-funds evidence), corporate formalities under Companies Law Cap. 113 (board resolutions, allotment returns, beneficial ownership register), and EU/UN/UK sanctions screening. Profit remittance from the Cyprus bakery entity to non-resident shareholders is structurally tax-efficient: no Cyprus withholding tax applies to dividends, interest, or royalties paid to non-residents under domestic law, provided the recipient is the beneficial owner and substance/anti-abuse conditions are met.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Foreign-currency receipts (USD, CNY, GBP) may be retained indefinitely in multi-currency accounts at CBC-licensed credit institutions or converted to EUR at freely negotiated market rates with the bank. The CBC and the Ministry of Finance do not prescribe any deadline for repatriation of trade receivables or any percentage of hard-currency conversion. The only applicable obligations are documentary: invoices, bills of lading, contract references, and beneficial-owner identification must be retained for AML purposes; payments must be screened against EU/UN sanctions lists. Receipts related to intra-group master-franchise royalties from China should be routed with the EU Balance-of-Payments purpose code for 'Royalties and Licence Fees' to ensure accurate CBC statistical classification and smooth downstream credit.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou, CBC), the euro-area National Central Bank responsible for banking supervision, payment system oversight, foreign-exchange statistics, and implementation of ECB monetary policy in Cyprus.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EU/EEA-regulated e-money institutions (EMIs) holding an Electronic Money Institution licence under Directive 2009/110/EC (EMD2) and payment services under PSD2 (Directive 2015/2366/EU) passported into Cyprus; Low for offshore non-EEA digital EMIs domiciled in the UK, Hong Kong, Singapore, or the UAE that do not hold a Cypriot or EU passport. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot provide lending, escrow, or trade-finance facilities; (ii) client funds held by an EMI must be safeguarded via segregated accounts at a EU bank, adding counterparty risk and slower MT103 clearing; (iii) the Cyprus Department of Registrar of Companies and the Tax Department require statutory filings and tax payments from a bank account, and the CBC's anti-money-laundering guidance expects corporate treasury accounts to be held at a supervised credit institution; (iv) for the bakery master-franchise structure, a passported EU EMI is viable only as a secondary inbound collection rail for sub-franchisee POS/merchant settlements, never as the sole treasury account. A pragmatic blueprint is to combine a primary CBC-licensed bank account with one secondary EU EMI for high-volume, low-value collection aggregation, subject to regular reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (i) the injecting parent/holding company instructs its bank to send a SWIFT MT103 to the Cyprus Ltd's IBAN (format CY + 2 check digits + 5-digit Cypriot bank code + 16-character account number) at a CBC-licensed institution; (ii) the 70 (Remittance Information) field must state 'Share Capital Contribution' or 'Shareholders Loan' with the corporate registration number of the Cyprus entity; (iii) the ordering customer (field 50K) must exactly match the legal name on the beneficiary's KYC file to avoid name-mismatch holds; (iv) for amounts exceeding EUR 100,000, the receiving bank will require a board resolution, a completed source-of-funds declaration, and a copy of the share-allotment return filed under Cyprus Companies Law Cap. 113 before value-dating; (v) the funds should be credited initially to a segregated Capital Escrow Account and released to the operating account only against verifiable commercial obligations (equipment purchase orders, lease deposits, franchise build-out contracts); E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for EU/EEA-regulated e-money institutions (EMIs) holding an Electronic Money Institution licence under Directive 2009/110/EC (EMD2) and payment services under PSD2 (Directive 2015/2366/EU) passported into Cyprus; Low for offshore non-EEA digital EMIs domiciled in the UK, Hong Kong, Singapore, or the UAE that do not hold a Cypriot or EU passport. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot provide lending, escrow, or trade-finance facilities; (ii) client funds held by an EMI must be safeguarded via segregated accounts at a EU bank, adding counterparty risk and slower MT103 clearing; (iii) the Cyprus Department of Registrar of Companies and the Tax Department require statutory filings and tax payments from a bank account, and the CBC's anti-money-laundering guidance expects corporate treasury accounts to be held at a supervised credit institution; (iv) for the bakery master-franchise structure, a passported EU EMI is viable only as a secondary inbound collection rail for sub-franchisee POS/merchant settlements, never as the sole treasury account. A pragmatic blueprint is to combine a primary CBC-licensed bank account with one secondary EU EMI for high-volume, low-value collection aggregation, subject to regular reconciliation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory for related-party transactions exceeding EUR 750,000 (goods/financial transactions) or EUR 50,000 (services, royalties and intangibles). A Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Lower thresholds apply where the Tax Department issues a notice to a specific taxpayer.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code but applies the 'management and control' test under Article 4 of the Income Tax Law and a general anti-avoidance rule. A bakery master-franchise entity must demonstrate actual staff, a functioning central kitchen, stock ownership, board meetings in Cyprus, and real commercial decision-making; otherwise taxing rights can be reallocated and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law (amended) the Tax Commissioner may impose a penalty equal to 100% of the tax evaded, plus interest at 5% per annum. Criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code for fraudulent returns carries imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines up to EUR 10,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false IR4 are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax. Courts may issue a travel ban during pending tax cases, confiscate personal bank accounts, and, upon conviction, imprison responsible directors for up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Artificial Profit Attribution

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the arrangement under the GAAR, disallowance of the Cyprus source of the royalty, recomputation at the parent's effective rate, a 10% late-payment surcharge, and refusal of a tax residence certificate for DTA purposes. Under Article 43 of the Income Tax Law, the Commissioner can also treat offshore management as creating non-residence, exposing the company to taxes of the jurisdiction where management is actually exercised.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally accountable for false substance declarations, with civil restitution orders equal to the evaded tax, an automatic worldwide bank freeze, travel bans, and disqualification from serving as a director of any Cyprus company for up to 5 years.
AML Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law, the supervisory body can fine the company up to EUR 500,000 or 1% of annual turnover, whichever is higher. Criminal money-laundering charges carry imprisonment up to 14 years and confiscation of proceeds linked to the bakery franchise network.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for failing to implement AML procedures; individual fines of up to EUR 100,000 may be imposed, personal assets can be seized, a travel ban can be issued pending trial, and convicted individuals face imprisonment.

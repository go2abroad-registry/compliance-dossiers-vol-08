Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification and federal tax matters; local tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration; tax registration portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt online services for foreign entity VAT ID applications.

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH must complete the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt to obtain the Steuernummer (tax number) covering corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. The VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is issued separately by the BZSt under § 27a UStG and must be applied for through the official BZSt application procedure, often via ELSTER or by mail; the VAT ID is required for intra-EU supplies and general B2B invoicing. Non-resident entities may register directly with the BZSt for a German VAT ID when taxable supplies will be made in Germany, and should also obtain a domestic tax number for excise or import-specific declarations if applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; GmbH is subject to unlimited corporation tax liability in Germany and is not eligible for pass-through taxation; no territorial sourcing election is available.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. The GmbH may elect VAT and corporate income tax/trade tax grouping (Organschaft) under § 2(2)(2) UStG and § 14 KStG where the GmbH is financially, economically, and organizationally integrated into a parent company. Small-business exemption election under § 19 UStG may be available to domestic entrepreneurs below specified turnover thresholds. German hybrid mismatch rules under § 4k EStG disallow non-aligned tax treatment and permit no elective neutralization mechanism beyond disclosure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for non-resident taxable persons performing taxable supplies in Germany; any taxable nexus triggers registration. Domestic small-business exemption threshold: previous-year turnover not exceeding EUR 22,000 and current-year expected turnover not exceeding EUR 50,000 (exclusive of VAT), requiring the business to have seat or place of management in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of canned boba tapioca pearls to non-EU destination countries qualify as VAT-exempt export supplies with right to input VAT deduction (0% VAT) under §§ 4(1)(1a) and 6 UStG provided the goods are physically removed from Germany to a third-country territory and the removal, transport chain, and receipt of export proceeds can be proven by proper commercial and customs documentary evidence. For intra-EU B2B supplies, zero-rating is available under § 6a UStG where goods are dispatched or transported to another EU member state, the purchaser is a taxable person, and the purchaser’s valid VAT identification number is confirmed.

  • ATLAS electronic customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing third-country transport
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit statement proving receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of transport or removal from EU customs territory (e.g., CMR, container tracking, loading lists)
  • Export accompanying document / transit document as required by German customs law

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is currently in force. Germany mandates structured e-invoicing (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x formats) for domestic B2B transactions; from January 1, 2025 all domestic B2B recipients must be able to receive e-invoices; from January 1, 2027 suppliers with taxable turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from January 1, 2028 all taxpayers must issue e-invoices. There is no API-based clearance authority. EDI invoicing remains permissible with agreed format.

German tax authorities rigorously audit export substantiation and may retroactively assess 19% VAT with interest from each relevant VAT return period where zero-rating evidence is incomplete or untimely. Non-compliant exporters may face administrative penalties under § 26a UStG, unsubstantiated VAT charge clawback under § 14c UStG, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under § 370 AO with fines and imprisonment of up to five years for deliberate concealment. Additionally, the circular movement of falsely zero-rated goods may trigger joint and several liability for unpaid VAT under § 71 AO. Must clarify: There is no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day period is a commercial/banking documentary credit and AML best-practice requirement commonly imposed by German export finance institutions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Germany across both current-account and capital-account transactions, reflecting the direct application of Article 63 TFEU; there is no exchange-control approval, quota, prior-authorisation or capital-account barrier for trade settlement, profit remittance, intercompany loans or equity injections into a GmbH. The only practical hurdles are procedural and non-monetary: EU/OFAC sanctions screening, German AWV balance-of-payments reporting obligations (no de minimis amount), customs and food-import veterinary documentation for tapioca raw materials, and the standard German dividend withholding tax (treaty-reducible) which is a fiscal charge rather than a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion obligation exists in Germany; export earnings may be retained in EUR or any convertible currency, held in onshore foreign-currency accounts or offshore, at the exporter's discretion. The sole statutory duties are (i) the AWV balance-of-payments report for each cross-border payment, filed through the payment service provider without de minimis threshold, and (ii) retention of underlying export/import documentation for at least five years under commercial-law and VAT/export-control rules.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) acts as the German central bank and the competent authority for the foreign-exchange statistics and balance-of-payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV); BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) supervises credit institutions, payment institutions and trade-finance conduct; BAFA (Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle) administers export-control, sanctions and dual-use licensing under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary multi-currency liquidity platform, Low as the primary corporate treasury account for a manufacturing GmbH. Legal constraints: an electronic-money institution (EMI) operating in Germany requires a BaFin licence under the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz – ZAG) implementing PSD2; post-Brexit UK EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex) must act through an EEA-licensed passporting entity. An EMI cannot hold statutory capital escrow (Sperrkonto), cannot issue commercial letters of credit or bank guarantees, and its safeguarding regime under ZAG does not constitute deposit protection; hence it is suitable only for minor disbursement automation, small FX conversions and last-mile vendor payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity is injected as an SWIFT MT103 (TT wire) in EUR to the GmbH's Capital Escrow Account (Sperrkonto) pre-opened for the notary; the payment purpose must state precisely 'Einlage auf das Stammkapital der GmbH' (contribution to stated capital) or 'Agio in die Kapitalrücklage' (additional paid-in capital). The receiving bank files the AWV balance-of-payments report with the Deutsche Bundesbank (no threshold) using the client-provided purpose code; the resident GmbH must retain the notary's confirmation of the capital deposit, CRS/FATCA self-certification of the non-resident shareholder, and intercompany documentation. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal burden of 15.825%. Municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and depends on the municipality; the combined corporate, solidarity surcharge, and trade tax burden is commonly approximately 30% to 33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax on dividends, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing a combined domestic withholding burden of 26.375%. The effective rate may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty, the EU Parent-Subsidiary regime, or a domestic exemption where statutory conditions are satisfied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the annual return is generally due by 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser prepares the return, the ordinary deadline is generally the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory extensions and special temporary rules. A non-calendar fiscal year follows the applicable statutory period after fiscal year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Advance payments are generally due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September, and 10 December, unless the tax assessment specifies otherwise.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border related-party transactions, a Local File is generally required when annual transaction volumes exceed EUR 6 million for transactions involving goods or EUR 600,000 for services and other transactions, subject to the detailed statutory documentation rules. A Master File is generally required for German entities belonging to a multinational group whose consolidated group revenue was at least EUR 100 million in the preceding fiscal year. Documentation may be requested even below thresholds where the tax authority considers it necessary.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a single standalone Economic Substance Regulation equivalent to some low-tax jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant through the general anti-abuse rule in Section 42 of the Fiscal Code, treaty beneficial-ownership and anti-conduit principles, EU anti-abuse rules, transfer pricing requirements, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, and permanent-establishment analysis. A German GmbH should maintain real management, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises, decision-making records, commercial risk control, inventory and production evidence, bank activity, and contemporaneous business purpose. A manufacturing GmbH producing ambient quick-cook tapioca pearls should be able to demonstrate actual production, quality control, procurement, inventory, food-law compliance, sales activity, and operational risk assumption. German transfer pricing documentation must generally be prepared within the statutory period after a request, and extraordinary transactions may require accelerated documentation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under Section 370 of the Fiscal Code is punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. The company may face reassessment, interest, confiscation, and monetary sanctions.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal liability, personal tax liability for intentionally or grossly negligently breaching tax duties under Section 69 of the Fiscal Code, civil restitution, asset seizure, disqualification consequences, and insolvency-related liability. Travel restrictions may arise in enforcement or criminal proceedings but are not an automatic sanction for every tax default.
VAT carousel, input-tax fraud, or fictitious supplier chain

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of input VAT, repayment of tax, interest, administrative penalties, and criminal tax-evasion exposure. Serious cases may result in imprisonment and confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid VAT caused by culpable breach of filing or payment duties and may face criminal prosecution, asset freezing, director disqualification, and insolvency claims.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Income adjustments, additional corporate income tax and trade tax, interest, documentation penalties, estimated assessments, and possible penalties for failure to cooperate or materially incorrect documentation. German documentation penalties may be significant and can include monetary penalties linked to the income adjustment or documented transaction amounts.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal liability for tax understatements caused by culpable conduct. Repeated intentional manipulation may support tax-evasion charges and civil claims by the company, insolvency administrator, or tax authorities.
Withholding tax failure

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may be assessed for the tax that should have been withheld, plus interest and penalties. Treaty or EU relief may be denied where the recipient is a conduit, lacks substance, or fails the statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable under the Fiscal Code for unpaid withholding tax where they intentionally or negligently failed to perform withholding and remittance duties.
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or EU relief, reallocation of income, permanent-establishment assessments, withholding tax exposure, tax-abuse findings, penalties, and possible criminal investigation where false records or fraudulent invoices are involved.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution, tax liability, criminal liability for false statements or tax evasion, insolvency liability, asset seizure, and professional or commercial disqualification. Travel restrictions are fact-dependent and generally arise only through formal enforcement or criminal procedures.
Accounting, record-retention, and E-Bilanz breach

Statutory sanction: Estimated assessments, bookkeeping penalties, late-filing sanctions, audit findings, tax adjustments, and criminal exposure where records are deliberately falsified or destroyed.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are responsible for proper organization and supervision of accounting. Serious breaches may create personal tax, civil, insolvency, and criminal liability.
Insolvency and late-filing liability

Statutory sanction: Repayment claims, insolvency administrator actions, criminal insolvency offenses, fines, imprisonment, and disallowance of transactions prejudicing creditors.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited post-insolvency payments, unpaid taxes and social-security contributions, delayed insolvency filing, civil restitution, asset seizure, director disqualification, and imprisonment in serious cases.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial-owner violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, reporting restrictions, supervisory measures, account closures, asset freezing, and criminal money-laundering exposure where the conduct is intentional or connected to criminal proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and designated AML officers may face personal administrative or criminal liability, civil claims, disqualification, asset seizure, and enforcement-related travel restrictions.
Food, customs, product, and trade compliance affecting tax integrity

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessments, import VAT and duty recovery, interest, administrative fines, seizure, withdrawal of products, and criminal customs or fraud proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for culpable customs or tax violations, civil losses, confiscation, criminal penalties, and business or import authorization consequences.
Illegal distribution or hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: Reclassification as a hidden profit distribution, denial of deductions, corporate tax and withholding tax assessments, interest, and shareholder repayment claims under German capital-maintenance rules.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face restitution claims, liability for unlawful payments, tax liability for withholding failures, insolvency claims, and possible criminal exposure where the distribution is fraudulent or creditor-prejudicing.

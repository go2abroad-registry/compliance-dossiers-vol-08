Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) and special functions; zuständiges Finanzamt (local tax office, determined by the company's place of management) for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) and VAT pre-registration; official portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) for all filings and electronic registration.

E-government filing portal

Upon notarial foundation and commercial register entry, the GmbH automatically obtains a tax number (Steuernummer) from the responsible Finanzamt via the mandatory online 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (registration questionnaire file in ELSTER). For a permanently established German entity, no separate foreign entity registration is required. For non-resident foreign entities without a permanent establishment making taxable supplies in Germany, VAT registration must be obtained directly from BZSt using Form 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer USt-IdNr.' (VAT ID application), accompanied by proof of identity, evidence of business purpose, and a German fiscal representative if no fixed establishment exists. For the German GmbH itself, the BZSt-issued VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) is applied for separately after the Steuernummer is assigned, using the online BZSt portal (Mein BZSt). The procurement timeline is typically 2–6 weeks; the local Finanzamt may request the statutory registration questionnaire within 1–4 weeks of commercial register publication.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

GmbH has no 'check-the-box' pass-through option; it is a C-corporation equivalent fully taxable as a separate legal person (Körperschaftsteuer plus solidarity surcharge and Gewerbesteuer). Available elections: (i) Organschaft (corporate and VAT grouping) under Sections 14–19 KStG and Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG, allowing a single VAT payer and income/loss pooling between a controlling domestic parent (usually a corporation or partnership) and the GmbH, subject to financial, economic and organisational integration; (ii) export contract manufacturing (Lohnveredelung) is not an election but a specific VAT treatment; (iii) trade tax base options including the FIFO/LIFO inventory election and reduced trade tax deduction options; (iv) hybrid mismatch elections under Section 4k EStG are mandatory exclusions, not elective, since the German Implementation Act of the Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) applied from 1 January 2020 — no affirmative check-the-box exists for hybrid entities, only disallowance for deductible hybrid mismatches and importation mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small business threshold under Section 19 UStG: €25,000 turnover in the preceding calendar year and €100,000 expected in the current year (effective 1 January 2025, raised from €22,000/€50,000). However, this small business exemption is not available to foreign suppliers without an established German enterprise. For foreign entities (including non-resident contract manufacturers), there is NO registration threshold — any taxable supply of goods or services in Germany (territorial nexus under Section 1(3) and Section 3(7) UStG) immediately triggers VAT registration, even if the recipient is a German B2B customer who would otherwise be liable via reverse charge. A German GmbH engaged in PCBA contract manufacturing must register immediately upon commercial register entry, regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(i) Standard export zero-rating applies under Section 6 UStG when the goods (finished PCBA) are supplied to a business located in a third country (outside the EU) or to a non-EU customer, and the goods are physically transported to a third-country destination by the supplier, the customer, or a third party acting on their behalf; (ii) for contract manufacturing (Lohnveredelung), zero-rating may also apply to the processing service supplied to a foreign principal if the principal-owner of the inputs and the processed goods are exported to a third country and the service is directly connected to those exported goods; the foreign principal must provide evidence of customs status and the export must occur within a commercially reasonable period (typically no more than 3 months), supported by transport documents; (iii) for goods dispatched/transported to another EU member state, zero-rated intra-community supply under Section 6a UStG is available if the customer holds a valid VAT registration number and the goods travel cross-border; (iv) critical condition: the supply must be a taxable transfer of title or a deemed processing service; mere toll manufacturing for a non-resident principal without export movement to a third country does not qualify; the supplier must retain evidence that the goods have left Germany/ the EU.

Germany has transitioned to mandatory structured electronic invoicing (E-Invoice) for domestic B2B supplies: from 1 January 2025 every domestic business must be able to receive an e-invoice in a structured XRechnung/ZUGFeRD or standard XML (EN 16931) format; from 1 January 2027 businesses with >€800,000 turnover in the previous year must issue e-invoices for domestic B2B sales; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices. There is NO real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime in Germany as of 2025; however, under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package, transaction-based digital reporting (DRR) will be phased in from 1 July 2030, requiring near-real-time submission of invoice datasets via BZSt. Until then, validated EDIFACT/XML through publikations-plattform or DIWA is accepted; customs exports require electronic ATLAS declarations with EORI numbers.

German tax authorities (Finanzamt and Zoll) strictly audit export zero-rating claims; the burden of proof lies on the supplier. If documentation is missing or inconsistent, the export exemption is retroactively denied, and VAT is assessed at 19% on the full contract price, plus default surcharge of 0.25% per month on the late VAT (Section 26a UStG) and late payment interest of 0.5% per month (Section 240 AO). False or fraudulent export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under Section 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to 10 years, or up to 5 years for negligent evasion (Section 378 AO), with cumulative monetary penalties up to €500,000; trades may be suspended. Additionally, Section 25d UStG imposes joint and several liability on the supplier for input VAT if the exporter fails to comply; tax authorities may claw back input VAT deductions under Section 15(1) Nr. 1 UStG retroactively if export proof is invalid. Under Section 238 UStG? (not applicable) — the statute of limitations for VAT assessments is 4 years (Section 169 AO), extended to 10 years for evasion; therefore clawback risk persists for a decade. Note: German law does not statutorily impose a 180-day foreign exchange repatriation deadline for VAT zero-rating; the 180-day figure is a commercial risk-management benchmark used to align with bank settlement and customs timing; however, missing payment or excessive delay may trigger transfer pricing adjustments, hidden profit distribution (Section 8(3) KStG) at the corporate level, and require additional export substantiation under Section 6a UStG for intra-EU movements.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unrestricted convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions under the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements, the EU Treaty freedoms, and AWV regulations. The euro is freely transferable within the EU/EEA and between Germany and third countries without any central-bank conversion approval. Profit remittances from a German GmbH are permitted without FX controls; practical hurdles are exclusively tax-related, including the 25% (plus solidarity surcharge) withholding tax on dividends, subject to reduction or elimination under an applicable double taxation treaty. No foreign-exchange surrender, conversion, or repatriation restriction applies to a German entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose any mandatory surrender, forced conversion, or repatriation quota for export proceeds, whether received in euros or foreign currency. Exporters must, however, comply with statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV). Cross-border export receipts exceeding the statutory reporting threshold (EUR 12,500) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five days of the payment becoming available. In addition, outstanding trade receivables from non-residents above the statutory ceiling must be reported on a monthly statistical basis. No fixed ratio of proceeds must be converted to euros, and retained foreign currency may be held in any licensed German bank account.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting in coordination with the German Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) on foreign trade policy and with the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) on banking and financial market supervision duties. Germany operates a liberalised foreign-exchange administration system with no general exchange-control licensing body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) that hold a passport into Germany under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2), subject to compliance with the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz – ZAG). Low for offshore EMIs domiciled outside the EEA, because the provision of payment services or the issuance of an IBAN to a German entity by an unlicensed EMI constitutes an unauthorised activity subject to BaFin enforcement and potential criminal liability. Even for EEA-passported EMIs, the standard corporate treasury functions required for PCBA contract manufacturing — including FX hedging, documentary letters of credit, and correspondent banking for non-EUR trade settlements — are normally unavailable. A fully licensed commercial bank account in Germany remains the sole comprehensively viable onboarding route for this business profile.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections into the German GmbH must be made from the shareholder's bank account through SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR wires or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated intra-EU transfers. The transfer instruction must carry the precise business purpose code according to the bank’s payment classification, commonly a capital contribution code (e.g., 'Capital Contribution – EQ' or 'Equity Injection') or a shareholder-loan code where funding is supplied as a shareholder advance. The German bank will require the underlying notarised articles of association or a registered capital-increase agreement, a shareholder resolution approving the contribution, and receipt in the company's Capital Escrow Account if registered capital is being paid before commercial registration. To prevent a suspicious-transaction report, the transfer must clearly identify the originator and beneficiary full legal names and IBANs, and any split payment must be avoided without a documented capital-allocation schedule. For non-resident shareholders, the bank may request evidence of the foreign exchange or capital-movement approval in the originator's jurisdiction, even though Germany itself imposes no import control on inbound capital.For PCBA contract manufacturing, outbound settlement of supplier invoices and trade payables is executed through (a) SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers for open-account terms and urgent payment; (b) documentary commercial letters of credit (L/C) issued by the German bank under UCP 600, typically transmitted via SWIFT MT700/MT710, with negotiation/advising through a correspondent bank or the beneficiary's bank; and (c) for EU suppliers, SEPA Credit Transfers with full XML ISO 20022 payment instructions. The chosen rails must support the manufacturing cycle timeline, with L/C reserved for first-time high-value suppliers or transactions involving Asian PCB and semiconductor board components. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) that hold a passport into Germany under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2), subject to compliance with the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz – ZAG). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a combined federal burden of 15.825% before municipal trade tax. Trade tax is generally approximately 7% to 21%, depending on the municipality; the common combined corporate burden is approximately 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital income withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective domestic rate of 26.375%. A participation exemption may reduce the rate to 0% for qualifying EU parent companies under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in Germany, subject to minimum participation, holding-period, anti-abuse, and substance requirements. German treaty rates commonly reduce dividend withholding to 5% or 15%, but the applicable treaty must be checked for the recipient, ownership percentage, beneficial ownership, and limitation-on-benefits or anti-abuse requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due seven months after the end of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year GmbH without a tax adviser, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser prepares the return, the statutory extended deadline is generally 28 February of the second following year, subject to statutory exceptions, special extensions, and tax-office requests. A fiscal year ending on a date other than 31 December changes the deadline by reference to that year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Withholding tax is generally withheld at the time of payment or credit and remitted to the tax office within the statutory payment period, commonly by the tenth day of the following month. The exact deadline depends on the withholding-tax category and tax-office instructions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation generally applies where annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing, royalties, and similar transactions. A Master File is generally required where the German entity belongs to a multinational group with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million and the statutory German conditions are met. Documentation may be requested by the tax authority and must be produced within the applicable statutory period, commonly 30 days for transfer-pricing records under the current rules. Extraordinary transactions require particularly prompt and detailed documentation.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no single standalone economic-substance regulation equivalent to the UAE ESR regime. Substance is enforced through the arm's-length principle, the general anti-abuse rule, beneficial-ownership rules, treaty anti-abuse provisions, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules, principal-purpose analysis, and corporate-law doctrines. A German GmbH conducting PCBA contract manufacturing should maintain genuine German functions, personnel, production or contract-manufacturing capability, premises, risk control, decision-making, inventory and quality processes where contractually relevant, and commercially supportable remuneration. A shell GmbH with nominee management, circular payments, no operational capacity, or artificial related-party invoicing is exposed to denial of deductions, recharacterization, withholding-tax recovery, penalties, and director liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion may result in imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine. Particularly serious cases may result in imprisonment from six months to ten years. Unpaid tax, interest, late-payment surcharges, and recovery proceedings are additional.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal prosecution, personal tax liability for negligent or intentional breach of tax duties, civil claims by the GmbH, disqualification consequences, asset seizure, and imprisonment. Travel restrictions are possible in enforcement or criminal proceedings but are not automatic.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The GmbH may be assessed for the unpaid withholding tax, solidarity surcharge, interest, and late-payment surcharges. Intentional conduct may constitute tax evasion; negligent conduct may constitute a tax administrative offence.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable under German tax-liability rules where they culpably fail to satisfy withholding obligations. Criminal exposure, civil recovery, and enforcement against personal assets may follow.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income, deny deductions, impose withholding tax, assess interest, require documentation, and impose documentation-related surcharges. German transfer-pricing documentation failures can trigger a surcharge generally ranging from 5% to 10% of the income adjustment, subject to statutory minimums and caps, with additional penalties for delayed or unusable records.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability for deliberate or grossly negligent tax breaches and may face criminal tax-evasion exposure where manipulated prices conceal taxable income.
Hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: The benefit may be added back to German taxable income, treated as a dividend for withholding-tax purposes, and subjected to interest, penalties, and possible trade-tax consequences.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be liable to restore value to the GmbH under corporate-law duties and may face withholding-tax liability, damages claims, and criminal exposure if the arrangement was deliberately concealed.
Shell company and artificial substance

Statutory sanction: Treaty or EU exemption benefits may be denied, deductions may be disallowed, transactions may be recharacterized, and withholding tax may be recovered. Anti-abuse, beneficial-ownership, CFC, and general tax-avoidance provisions may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil damages, tax liability, criminal tax-evasion exposure, disqualification, asset seizure, and imprisonment where fraud or intentional abuse is established. Travel bans are case-specific and require procedural or enforcement grounds.
Accounting, record-retention, and E-Bilanz failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may estimate the tax base, deny deductions, impose late-filing or administrative penalties, and commence tax audits. German commercial and tax records generally require retention for six, eight, or ten years depending on the record type and applicable statutory rule.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for culpable breaches of accounting and tax duties and may face insolvency-related claims if deficient records contribute to creditor harm.
VAT and customs fraud

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include repayment of VAT, customs duty, import VAT, interest, penalties, seizure of goods, withdrawal of customs simplifications, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion or customs offences.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid VAT and customs amounts, subject to culpability, and may face criminal fines or imprisonment for intentional fraud.
Money laundering and beneficial-ownership transparency

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act can be substantial and may reach statutory maximums of up to EUR 1 million or multiples of the economic benefit for serious breaches; higher sector-specific or group-based limits may apply. Beneficial-ownership violations may be publicly recorded and may impair banking access.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and compliance officers may face personal administrative or criminal exposure where they knowingly facilitate laundering, sanctions evasion, or false ownership reporting. Asset seizure and travel restrictions may arise in criminal investigations.
Sanctions and export-control breach

Statutory sanction: Violations may result in criminal fines or imprisonment under German foreign-trade and sanctions legislation, administrative fines, confiscation, export-license loss, and denial of customs or government support.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted for intentional or negligent violations, may face personal fines or imprisonment, and may be liable for corporate losses caused by inadequate controls.
Insolvency and late filing

Statutory sanction: Late insolvency filing and prohibited payments can trigger criminal liability, civil repayment claims, director-disqualification consequences, and insolvency-administrator recovery actions. The maximum imprisonment for delayed insolvency filing is generally up to three years, subject to the applicable offence and facts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for prohibited post-insolvency payments, tax and social-security failures, creditor losses, and restitution. Criminal proceedings may include asset seizure and, where legally justified, travel restrictions.
Employment-tax and social-security non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Back taxes, social-security contributions, interest, administrative fines, criminal charges, and liability for unpaid amounts may arise. Intentional withholding or social-security fraud can result in imprisonment or substantial fines.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for employee withholding tax and employee social-security contributions where statutory conditions and culpability are satisfied, and may face criminal prosecution.

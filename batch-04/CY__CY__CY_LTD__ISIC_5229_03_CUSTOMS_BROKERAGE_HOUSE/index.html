Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a private limited company under Cap. 113; the Articles typically fix a nominal capital (e.g., €1,000) fully subscribed and paid. For customs brokerage licensing, the Cyprus Customs Department requires a financial security or bank guarantee of not less than €10,000 to cover customs debt and bonded transit liabilities; this guarantee is not escrowed with the Registrar but is held by the Customs Department.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Cyprus Fire Service for the office and bonded warehouse premises, structural load inspection certificate issued by the District Officer or Municipal Authority for the warehouse, and occupational health and safety clearance from the Department of Labour Inspection (Ministry of Labour and Social Insurance) under the Safety and Health at Work Law No. 89(I)/1996, including a workplace risk assessment and emergency response plan.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus; online portals: Ariadne for VAT and Customs (https://ariadne.mof.gov.cy) and Taxisnet for income tax (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

For foreign entities establishing a Cyprus customs brokerage house: (1) procure a Certificate of Incorporation from the Cyprus Registrar of Companies, unless operating via a branch; (2) appoint a Cyprus tax representative (Obos/registered agent) if the foreign entity is not incorporated in Cyprus; (3) file Form TD01 (Notification of Registration) or Form AUC (non-resident company notification) with the Tax Department District Office for the registered office's district; (4) obtain a Tax Identification Code (TIC) - for companies this is the 8-digit registration number with a check digit as used by the Tax Department and Department of Registrar of Companies; (5) for VAT, submit Form VAT101 to the Nicosia VAT District Office together with the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, identity and address details of directors and beneficial owners, registered office proof, and a projected annual turnover statement; (6) receive a VAT registration number in the format CY########X. Registration is compulsory if annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,500 or if intra-Community acquisitions exceed EUR 10,251, and is generally required immediately for customs brokerage activities involving import/export representations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A Cyprus private limited company is inherently taxed at entity level, with no pass-through option. Available elections under Cyprus tax law include: (i) group loss relief election under Income Tax Law s.15; (ii) notional interest deduction election on new equity contributions; (iii) 80% IP box deduction for qualifying intangible property; (iv) tonnage tax election for shipping; (v) VAT grouping election under VAT Act Art. 15. None of these alter the fundamental entity-level classification for a customs brokerage house.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,500 in annual taxable turnover (goods and services) measured over a rolling 12-month period; EUR 10,251 for intra-Community acquisitions; EUR 35,000 for distance sales of goods to Cyprus consumers

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to (i) exports of goods physically transported from Cyprus to a destination outside the European Union, provided the dispatch or transport occurs within 90 days from the time of supply and the exporter holds a valid customs export declaration (SAD/ECS); and (ii) services directly related to exported goods, including customs brokerage, freight forwarding, cargo handling, and other ancillary transport services, when performed in connection with goods being exported outside the EU and supplied to a non-established customer or a customer whose VAT identification is in a third country, as transposed from Article 146 of the VAT Directive. The customs broker must retain documentary evidence proving both the nature of the service and the physical export, including customs export records, bill of lading, and transport contracts. If services are supplied to an EU-established customer on domestic export brokerage, the transaction is taxable at the standard rate unless the strict exemption conditions are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic SAD/ECS record)
  • Bill of Lading (Marine) / Air Waybill / CMR road consignment note evidencing physical departure from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing settlement of the export consideration
  • Export contract / purchase order and, where applicable, Transit Accompanying Document (TAD)

No mandatory real-time B2B e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping regime is currently in force in Cyprus. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for B2G transactions through the national PEPPOL-compliant e-Invoicing Platform, transposing EU Directive 2014/55/EU. Customs clearance is fully electronic through the Customs Department's THESAURUS/AES system (ICS/ECS declarations), with secure API and trader portal access for licensed customs brokers. Invoice data must be filed with input VAT and sales listings via Ariadne, but there is no pre-clearance tax authority validation for ordinary B2B invoices.

Cyprus does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; however, tax authorities may scrutinise unexplained delays in receipt of export proceeds as evidence of unrealized or fictitious exports. In the event of an abusive or fraudulent zero-rating claim, the Tax Department will issue an assessment requiring payment of the under-declared VAT plus statutory interest. Penalties under VAT Act s.65 may reach 150% of the unpaid VAT, and additional penalties may be imposed under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law for negligent or fraudulent submissions. Criminal prosecution is possible for deliberate tax evasion, carrying fines and imprisonment. For customs offences, the Customs Department may impose administrative fines, revoke a licensed broker's customs authorization, and issue customs seizure or forfeiture orders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full member of the euro area (adopted the euro on 1 January 2008). No domestic foreign-exchange control legislation exists; all current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible. Profit remittances and dividend distributions are unrestricted, subject to verifying corporate law solvency tests, payment of the applicable 17% withholding tax (subject to treaty relief or EU directives), and satisfaction of AML/KYC due diligence. Customs brokerage houses may hold and operate multi-currency accounts without prior central bank approval. Cross-border capital flows must be reported to the Central Bank of Cyprus for balance-of-payments statistics only, with no compliance approval threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds in Cyprus. All export receipts (merchandise or services) may be credited to any resident or non-resident account in Cyprus or abroad in any freely convertible currency. Customs brokers receiving funds on behalf of importers or exporters are not subject to any forced conversion. However, the Customs Brokers license under the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department and relevant AML/CFT (Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Activities Law 188(I)/2007) obligations require that all receipts be traceable and properly coded for statistical reporting.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Moderate (for non-resident digital EMIs) / Low (for full treasury clearing rails). Cyprus law permits European Economic Area licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) to passport into Cyprus under PSD2. Offshore, non-EEA EMIs face significant legal constraints: they cannot operate as payment institutions without a Cyprus or EEA license, and cannot access Target2/SEPA or the Central Bank of Cyprus clearing and settlement systems. A Cyprus-licensed EMI (or an EEA passport) may provide acceptable ancillary payment services, but banks remain necessary for settlement finality, commercial letters of credit, and large-value euro clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections (share capital or share premium) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the sole shareholder's own bank account (not from an unrelated third party). For a Cyprus Ltd, the correct cross-border purpose code is 'P01010 - Share capital / capital contribution' (as per the IMF BPM6 classification). For subordinated shareholder loans, use purpose code 'P02020 - Other long-term loan'. The receiving bank will perform a balance-of-payments filing with the Central Bank of Cyprus. Compliance verification requires: a board resolution approving the capital injection and issuance of shares, a signed share subscription / loan agreement, and proof of the sender's legal capacity (e.g., certificate of incorporation of the beneficial owner). The bank will not release the funds into an operational account until the registration of the share capital increase with the Cyprus Registrar of Companies (Form HE30) is initiated, and a certified UBO return is filed.Outbound trade disbursements for customs brokerage house operations are executed via the following clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for international wire transfers to customs authorities, overseas freight forwarders, or global suppliers; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for euro-denominated payments within SEPA area, particularly settlement with Cyprus Customs and Excise (which participates in the SEPA Credit Transfer scheme); (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) – issued by the broker's bank on behalf of a client for high-value import/export transactions, with a minority use of Local LC for domestic re-routing of bonded goods; and (4) Automated Broker Interface (ABI) via the Cyprus Customs' electronic system THESEAS (the new National Single Window), which automates duty and VAT payments through direct debit mandates on the broker's operational account. All outbound disbursements must be matched to an underlying customs declaration, a client instruction, and a signed agency agreement to satisfy AML and sanctions screening requirements.Outright FX Forwards (non-deliverable and deliverable) – permitted when the customs broker holds short-term foreign currency receivables or payables from cross-border customs duties or freight-billing operations (typically USD-denominated taxes or fees).FX Swaps (rollover swaps) – allowed for managing liquidity mismatches in operating account currencies without speculative positioning.Currency options (European-style) – permissible for hedging forecasted commissions from export clearing transactions when the bank treats the broker as an end-user and the contract has clear commercial hedge documentation.NDFs (Non-Deliverable Forwards) – legally permissible in Cyprus only where there is a genuine exposure to a non-convertible counterparty currency through trade transactions; the broker must prove the trade underlying in the customs SAD.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% under domestic law on dividends paid by a Cyprus company to non-resident shareholders. The 17% Special Defence Contribution (SDC) historically applies only to Cyprus tax residents, not to non-residents; hence the effective statutory withholding rate for non-residents is 0%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate controlled transactions per tax year triggers the mandatory preparation of Local File and Master File in accordance with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. Even below this threshold, a transfer-pricing study should be retained where high-risk cross-border transactions with tax-haven counterparties exist.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code comparable to offshore financial centres, but the tax authorities apply the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, the EU ATAD general anti-abuse rule (GAAR), and the judicial substance-over-form doctrine. Corporate tax residency is determined by the place of effective management and control. A customs brokerage company claiming Cypriot tax residence must maintain real operational substance in Cyprus: a physical office, qualified staff, management decision-making meetings, board minutes, custom clearance contracts, bank accounts and licensed business conduct. Without this substance, the Cyprus Tax Department may deny TRC, disregard the Cyprus entity, and reassess income at the controlling foreign entity's hands.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Income Tax Law and the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, the taxpayer faces a best-judgment assessment for underpaid tax, a late-payment surcharge of up to 30%, and criminal conviction penalties of up to €20,000 fine and/or three years' imprisonment for each tax evasion offence. The customs brokerage licence is additionally revocable by the competent authority.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts where they knowingly approved false returns, stripped assets or acted in bad faith. Cyprus courts can order civil restitution, disqualification from managing companies, travel bans, and up to five years' imprisonment for fraudulent evasion.
Shell Company and Fictitious Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of Tax Residency Certificate, retroactive revocation of Cyprus corporate residence, assessment of corporate tax at 12.5% on worldwide income, plus surcharges of up to 30% and interest. The Registrar of Companies may strike the company off and revoke the customs brokerage operating licence. Financial penalties up to €50,000 may apply for false declarations.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who sign without supervising expose themselves to criminal sanctions under the Cyprus Companies Law and AML legislation. The court may lift the corporate veil, impose personal restitution orders, travel restrictions, and custodial sentences for directors who aided the sham arrangement.
Customs Duty Fraud and Anti-Money Laundering Violations

Statutory sanction: Customs penalties include seizure of goods, fines of up to 200% of evaded duties, revocation of the brokerage licence, and disqualification from participation in EU customs procedures. AML violations carry fines up to €200,000 for legal persons and up to €50,000 for officers, plus imprisonment up to five years for natural persons.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable where they knew or ought to have known of AML or customs breaches. Personal assets may be traced to satisfy duty evasion, directors may be prohibited from re-entering customs professions, and courts may impose custodial sentences of up to five years, travel bans, and confiscation of proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department may make an arm's length adjustment, recalculating income at the statutory 12.5% rate plus interest. An adjustment penalty of up to 30% of the undercharged tax may be applied, and since Cyprus follows OECD TP Guidelines, a foreign corresponding adjustment may be refused if documentation is inadequate. Criminal penalties apply where false documentation is certified.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve falsified transfer-pricing files can be held jointly liable for the resulting tax arrears where the company becomes insolvent or assets are expatriated. They face disqualification orders, travel bans, personal restitution liability, and in egregious cases criminal imprisonment for aiding tax evasion.

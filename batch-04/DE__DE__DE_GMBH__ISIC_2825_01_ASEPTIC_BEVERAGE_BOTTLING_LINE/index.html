Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

GmbHG Section 5(1) prescribes a minimum share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000. At least EUR 12,500 (50%) of each cash contribution must be paid up before registration (Section 7(2)); funds must be deposited on a blocked capital account (Sperrkonto) opened in the name of the company in formation and may be freely disposed of by the managing directors only after the GmbH is entered in the Commercial Register. Contributions in kind require full payment and a reasoned valuation report (Sachgründungsbericht) per Section 7(3).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Brandschutznachweis) approved by the local building authority (Bauordnungsbehörde) in accordance with the Model Building Code (MBO), covering structural load, escape routes, fire compartments, extinguishing systems, and hydrant distances; workplace clearance by the State Office for Occupational Safety (Amt für Arbeitsschutz) and the statutory accident insurer (Berufsgenossenschaft Holz und Metall) after verification of the Risk Assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung) under Section 5 ArbSchG, including noise exposure assessments, machine guarding per the EU Machinery Directive, DGUV regulations, and explosion-protection documentation where solvents are stored.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification and cross-border refunds; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for income, corporate, trade, and VAT assessment. Digital portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH obtains its statutory tax number (Steuernummer) by registering with the local Finanzamt through the official questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung), filed online via ELSTER or on paper. For EU/third-country export VAT purposes, the GmbH must separately apply to the BZSt for a German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.). Non-resident persons establishing a permanent establishment or branch in Germany must register through a local tax representative and submit the same questionnaire; the Finanzamt may issue a provisional tax number pending official incorporation registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A German GmbH obtains its statutory tax number (Steuernummer) by registering with the local Finanzamt through the official questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung), filed online via ELSTER or on paper. For EU/third-country export VAT purposes, the GmbH must separately apply to the BZSt for a German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.). Non-resident persons establishing a permanent establishment or branch in Germany must register through a local tax representative and submit the same questionnaire; the Finanzamt may issue a provisional tax number pending official incorporation registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A GmbH is always subject to entity-level corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and solidarity surcharge. Available elections include fiscal unity (Organschaft) for corporate income tax and VAT, allowing profit/input-VAT consolidation; the small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG (not relevant to an export-oriented machinery manufacturer); and the VAT option under Section 9 UStG to waive exemptions for real property related supplies. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are anti-abuse constraints rather than elective classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply for a resident GmbH; the general small-business exemption applies only where turnover in the preceding calendar year did not exceed EUR 22,000 and the current year's projected turnover is at most EUR 50,000 (Section 19 UStG), which is normally inapplicable to heavy machinery exports. For cross-border intra-Community distance sales, the statutory annual threshold is EUR 10,000 before the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) becomes optional; absent an OSS election, normal domestic VAT registration applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of machinery to third countries (outside the EU) and intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered business customers are zero-rated (exempt with the right to input VAT deduction) under Sections 4(1)(a) and 6a UStG. The German exporter must evidence that the goods have physically left the EU or, for intra-EU supplies, that the customer has supplied a valid VAT ID and the goods were transported to another Member State. The export must be performed by the seller, the customer, or a third party on their behalf, and documentary evidence must be retained. For exchange-control purposes, payment is normally documented by an inward remittance certificate within the 180-day settlement benchmark; however, German VAT law does not impose a fixed statutory repatriation deadline, but the payment record remains a standard audit item for proving the commercial reality of the export transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) with item-level commodity codes and value declaration
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (airway bill, CMR, or multimodal transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign exchange receipt evidence) showing the export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice containing the German VAT-exemption clause and delivery terms (Incoterms)
  • Export contract or purchase order clearly identifying the foreign buyer and machinery specifications
  • Proof of arrival at destination (delivery receipt, customs stamp, or consignee acknowledgment)
  • Packing list and weight/volume certificate for customs verification

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. As of 1 January 2025, a statutory B2B e-invoicing mandate applies to all resident taxable persons: invoices must be transmitted in a structured electronic format such as XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x, or an EDI-compliant format, and must be received/designed in a manner guaranteeing authenticity, integrity, and readability. Phased enforcement: from 2025, issuers must be able to receive e-invoices; from 2027, businesses with turnover above EUR 800,000 must receive e-invoices, and from 2028 all businesses must receive e-invoices. The invoices are transmitted bilaterally using any compliant API, portal, or network; there is no central government clearance platform or digital stamping requirement.

German tax authorities routinely scrutinize export zero-rating documentation. Failure to substantiate the export within statutory time limits results in VAT being assessed at the standard rate (19%) together with interest under Section 233a AO and late-payment surcharges under Section 240 AO. Intentional or reckless misstatements constitute tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under Section 370 AO, punishable by fines, forfeiture, and imprisonment of up to five years, or up to ten years in especially serious cases. Retrospective VAT adjustments can be made for up to seven years generally and up to ten years where tax evasion is established. Tax auditors also test the commercial substance of the underlying export against transfer-pricing and anti-cumulation rules, and will claw back any deducted input VAT if the zero-rating conditions are not met.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany, as a euro-area member, provides full current- and capital-account convertibility under TFEU Articles 63–65 and the OECD Code of Liberalisation; no exchange control mechanism, no currency rationing, and no approval requirement applies to conversion, import, export, or holding of foreign currency. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, shareholder-loan repayment, and disposal proceeds may be transferred abroad without FX permission; the only binding frictions are (i) AWV Section 67 ff. payment-statistics reporting for any cross-border payment of at least EUR 12,500, (ii) German dividend withholding at 26.375% (25% corporate withholding plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) subject to treaty reduction, and (iii) EU/UN sanctions and AML/CFT screening. Capital-account outflows enjoy the same liberalisation as current-account transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. German law imposes no mandatory surrender, no repatriation deadline, and no conversion ratio for export proceeds; the GmbH may freely retain foreign-currency receipts in domestic or foreign accounts. The binding duties are (i) commercial-law bookkeeping and revenue recognition under HGB, (ii) customs export declaration through the ATLAS system for trade statistics, and (iii) AWV Section 67–72 Bundesbank payment-statistics reporting of every payment receipt or payment of at least EUR 12,500 within five working days, with the commercial invoice and SWIFT/SEPA reference matched in the report.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Bundesbank), acting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); the Federal Ministry of Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) holds regulatory policy competence, while BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) supervises banks, EMIs, and capital-market conduct; the Bundesbank operates the mandatory payment-statistics and external-assets reporting regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, acceptable only as a complementary operating account and never as the sole banking architecture. BaFin-licensed EMIs and EEA EMIs passporting under PSD2 may open payment accounts with German IBANs for a GmbH and route SEPA/SEPA-Instant cross-border payments; however, they generally lack direct SWIFT membership, cannot open documentary letters of credit, and are not accepted as exclusive accounts for export-financing collateral. Offshore EMIs without an EU/BaFin licence are legally constrained: they cannot lawfully hold payment accounts for a German-resident GmbH under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and will fail bank KYC/payer verification; any use of such providers should be treated as a compliance deviation and remediated.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For GmbH capital formation, the non-resident shareholder transfers EUR directly from its own bank account to the GmbH's German IBAN via SEPA Credit Transfer (within SEPA) or SWIFT MT103 (non-EEA origin). The payment reference must state the statistical purpose code for a direct-investment equity injection ('Beteiligungskapital'), thereby entering the Bundesbank payment statistics; shareholder loans are coded separately as 'Darlehen'. Any single transaction of at least EUR 12,500 must be reported by the GmbH to Deutsche Bundesbank within five working days. German bank compliance validates the source of funds, sanctions-screens the shareholder, and may require the notarised capital-contribution confirmation. Statutory timing requires at least EUR 12,500 of the EUR 25,000 minimum share capital to be paid in cash before registration in the commercial register. Funding must move directly from the shareholder to the GmbH; indirect third-party deposits are rejected by both Handelsregister practice and bank KYC; crypto, cash, or round-trip funding channels are always non-compliant.The exporting GmbH settles outbound flows through multiple compliant rails: (i) supplier and technology payments within Europe via SEPA CT or SEPA-Instant up to EUR 100,000; (ii) cross-border supplier payments via SWIFT MT103 with ISO 20022 remittance referencing order, invoice, and contract; (iii) high-value cover payments via MT202 COV where funds pass through an intermediary bank; (iv) documentary trade via advising bank (Deutsche Bank, Commerzbank, or UniCredit) receiving an MT700 letter of credit, with UCP 600 compliance checking and payment against presentation of transport documents; (v) deferred-payment LCs paid at maturity and optionally discounted by the confirming bank; (vi) forfaiting or purchase of export receivables without recourse requiring no German approval. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium, acceptable only as a complementary operating account and never as the sole banking architecture. BaFin-licensed EMIs and EEA EMIs passporting under PSD2 may open payment accounts with German IBANs for a GmbH and route SEPA/SEPA-Instant cross-border payments; however, they generally lack direct SWIFT membership, cannot open documentary letters of credit, and are not accepted as exclusive accounts for export-financing collateral. Offshore EMIs without an EU/BaFin licence are legally constrained: they cannot lawfully hold payment accounts for a German-resident GmbH under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and will fail bank KYC/payer verification; any use of such providers should be treated as a compliance deviation and remediated.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal burden of 15.825%. German trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and varies by municipality; the combined corporate and trade tax burden is commonly approximately 29% to 33%, with the national average generally near 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital income withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective domestic rate of 26.375%, excluding church tax. The rate may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in Germany, or a domestic exemption certificate. EU parent relief generally requires qualifying legal form, minimum participation and holding-period conditions, subject to anti-abuse rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax and trade-tax returns are generally due seven months after the end of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year company, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year. A tax adviser filing extension is generally available until the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory exceptions and possible earlier requests by the tax office. Any final balance is payable by the assessment due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border related-party transactions, a Local File is generally required where annual transaction volumes exceed EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for services and other transactions, subject to the German transfer-pricing documentation rules. Extraordinary transactions require contemporaneous documentation irrespective of those thresholds. A Master File is generally required for German entities belonging to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 100 million, where the statutory conditions are met. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate standalone economic-substance regulation regime equivalent to certain offshore ESR systems. Substance is nevertheless relevant under the general anti-abuse rule in Section 42 of the German Fiscal Code, treaty beneficial-ownership and principal-purpose concepts, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive anti-abuse rule, interest limitation rules, controlled foreign company rules, transfer-pricing requirements and withholding-tax relief procedures. A German GmbH should maintain local management authority, commercial decision-making, employees or appropriately documented outsourced functions, premises or operational infrastructure, books and records, bank accounts, risk control and genuine business activity proportionate to its manufacturing and export operations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code may result in imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine. Particularly serious cases may result in imprisonment of six months to ten years. Additional tax, interest, confiscation and professional or commercial consequences may apply.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal liability for intentional participation, civil liability to the GmbH and personal tax liability for intentionally or grossly negligently failing to discharge tax obligations under Section 69 of the German Fiscal Code. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered in criminal or enforcement proceedings; imprisonment is possible where the statutory criminal elements are established.
Withholding-Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the GmbH, interest, late-payment surcharges, administrative fines and potential tax-evasion prosecution where the omission is intentional. The withholding agent may remain liable even if the foreign recipient received the gross payment.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable under Section 69 of the German Fiscal Code for intentional or grossly negligent failure to withhold or remit tax. Criminal exposure depends on knowledge, intent and the amount involved.
Transfer-Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Income adjustments, denial of deductions, deemed profit distributions, withholding-tax exposure, interest and a transfer-pricing documentation penalty. German rules can impose a penalty generally ranging from 5% to 10% of the income adjustment where documentation is unusable or missing, subject to statutory minimum and maximum amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for avoidable tax losses and personal liability for grossly negligent tax failures. Criminal liability arises where documentation failures form part of deliberate tax evasion or fraud.
Shell Company, Treaty Abuse and Beneficial-Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or EU relief, full German withholding tax, interest, penalties, reassessment, anti-abuse application under Section 42 of the German Fiscal Code and possible criminal investigation if declarations were knowingly false.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unpaid withholding tax and damages caused by unsupported relief claims. Criminal exposure may arise for intentional assistance in tax evasion. Travel bans and imprisonment are not automatic and require applicable judicial or enforcement orders.
Accounting, Invoice and VAT Fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, VAT recovery, interest, administrative fines and possible prosecution for tax evasion, document fraud or commercial fraud. Serious cases may carry imprisonment under the applicable criminal provisions.

Director personal liability: Managing directors must organize proper accounting, tax controls and record retention. Personal civil, tax and criminal liability may arise for intentional or grossly negligent misconduct, including failure to preserve evidence supporting exports and intra-Community transactions.
AML, Sanctions and Suspicious-Payment Controls

Statutory sanction: Money-laundering violations may result in imprisonment or fines, confiscation and corporate sanctions. Sanctions violations may result in substantial administrative or criminal penalties, asset blocking and export-control consequences. AML breaches may also lead to supervisory fines and business restrictions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal or administrative liability for knowing participation or negligent compliance failures, personal confiscation exposure and civil claims. Travel restrictions or imprisonment require the statutory conditions and competent-authority action.
Capital Maintenance and Illegal Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Repayment claims under the German Limited Liability Companies Act, director damages, accounting corrections, tax recharacterization as hidden profit distributions and possible criminal consequences for fraud or insolvency-related misconduct.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable under the GmbH Act for prohibited payments and breach of due care. If insolvency exists, delayed filing or preferential payments may create additional civil and criminal exposure.
Insolvency and Tax-Payment Priority Failure

Statutory sanction: Potential criminal liability for insolvency delay, creditor preference, withholding of employee-related taxes and tax offences, together with director damages, clawback, interest and insolvency-administrator claims.

Director personal liability: Managing directors must monitor solvency and over-indebtedness and file without undue delay when statutory insolvency conditions arise. Personal civil liability, disqualification and imprisonment may apply depending on the offence and culpability.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation, Republic of Cyprus (Tax Department), under the Ministry of Finance; online taxpayer portal: ARIADNI (https://ariadni.mof.gov.cy)

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus private limited company obtains its Income Tax Identification Number automatically through incorporation and registration with the Tax Department. A foreign entity deriving Cyprus-source taxable income or making taxable supplies in Cyprus must file the prescribed registration application (Form TD2001 for companies) with the competent District Tax Office, supplying certified constitutional documents, certificate of good standing, proof of registered address or permanent establishment, and director/shareholder details. VAT registration is obtained using the same tax identification number framework, with the Tax Department issuing a single registration number for income tax and VAT purposes. Registration may be completed electronically through ARIADNI where available.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus has no check-the-box election; legal entities are taxable at entity level. Available election-type mechanisms include: (i) VAT grouping election for closely-connected persons; (ii) group relief election for surrender of tax losses; (iii) Notional Interest Deduction election on new equity; (iv) Intellectual Property Box preferential regime election; (v) election for capital allowances part-disposal treatment; and (vi) anti-hybrid mismatch disallowance filing requirements under EU ATAD implementation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 taxable turnover in any preceding twelve-month period for supplies of goods or services in Cyprus; special EU distance-selling and One-Stop-Shop thresholds apply (€10,000 for intra-EU cross-border distance sales and electronically supplied services)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods satisfying the statutory conditions: goods are supplied to a person established outside the EU, dispatched or transported out of the EU by the supplier or a third party, and the supplier retains documentary proof of removal from the EU within the prescribed period. Qualifying export-related services may also be zero-rated. Offshore services supplied to recipients outside the EU are generally outside the scope of Cyprus VAT rather than zero-rated where the place-of-supply rules place the supply outside the EU; taxable persons must substantiate the recipient's business/non-business status and foreign establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading or other transport documentation evidencing removal from the EU (e.g., CMR, Air Waybill or Post Office Receipt)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 confirmation evidencing receipt of the export sale proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or universal API-based e-invoice clearance mandate is currently in force. Electronic invoicing is legally permitted provided authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility are secured by the issuer. VAT returns and summary statements are filed via the ARIADNI portal; EU cross-border supplies require VIES recapitulative statements, and intra-EU goods movements require Intrastat declarations where applicable.

If export zero-rating is claimed without satisfactory documentary evidence or proceeds repatriation within the statutory 180-day substantiation framework, the Tax Department may recharacterize the supply as a domestic taxable supply subject to 19% VAT, raise an assessment for underdeclared VAT, deny or claw back input VAT attributed to the export, and impose statutory interest and penalties. Penalties can reach 100% of under-assessed tax in cases of negligence or wilful default; knowingly false documentation may lead to criminal prosecution and comparable monetary penalties. In cases involving fraud, retrospective assessments may extend beyond the standard limitation window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus applies full capital and current account convertibility based on its EU membership and euro-area status, with liberalization grounded in Article 63 TFEU. There are no general exchange-control permissions, no prior central-bank licensing for cross-border transfers, and no restrictions on dividend, interest, management-fee, or profit remittances, subject solely to standard AML/KYC diligence and EU/UN sanctions screening. Capital account inflows such as equity injections and shareholder loans are freely admissible; outbound dividend flows are subject to the 12.5% corporate income tax framework and any applicable withholding tax or double tax treaty relief validation by the Cyprus tax authorities. Profit remittance is therefore substantively unrestricted, provided the company maintains economic substance, audited financial statements, and compliant intra-group transfer pricing documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender, repatriation, or forced-conversion requirements for export proceeds or service-export receipts. A Cyprus resident company may retain foreign-currency receipts indefinitely, settle them in EUR, or convert them at free-market rates. For FDA/CE regulatory consulting services invoiced in USD or EUR, the practical expectation is that invoices are issued in EUR where the operational currency is EUR, with USD-receiving accounts permitted via multi-currency sweep accounts; no mandatory conversion ratio or time limit applies, and no export-license filing is required for consulting services falling under ISIC 7490.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα Κύπρου), operating within the Eurosystem and the Single Supervisory Mechanism; it supervises Cyprus-licensed credit institutions, administers the national financial sanctions framework, and requires licensed payment institutions and EMIs to obtain an EEA authorization prior to offering inbound collection services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a secondary collection rail only. An EU-licensed EMI or PSD2 payment institution (e.g., a Lithuanian, Maltese, or Dutch EMI holding an EBA-recognized license) may legally provide multi-currency IBAN accounts, card-acquiring settlement, and SWIFT/SEPA payout services for a Cyprus advisory firm. However, because EMIs do not hold full banking licenses and cannot offer credit or guarantee deposit insurance, they are not a substitute for a tier-1 Cyprus credit institution. Offshore or unlicensed digital EMIs are NOT viable: Central Bank of Cyprus policy, correspondent-bank de-risking, and EU/OFC sanctions regimes would block their onboarding and expose the entity to frozen-funds and AML enforcement risk. The defensible structure is a Cyprus tier-1 operational account as the master treasury node and a single EEA-licensed EMI as a USD/EUR card-acquiring settlement sub-rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the Cyprus private limited company, inbound capital injection of share capital or shareholder loans is routed through SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA originators or via SEPA Credit Transfer for EEA-origin funds into the designated operational EUR IBAN. The remittance must state the corporate legal name and the statistical purpose code corresponding to direct investment-equity ('Equity capital' under the EU BoP/CPD coding framework) or 'Intercompany loan' for shareholder-debt injection. The receiving Cyprus bank will require an internal debit/credit note, board resolution, and updated share register; funds are collected through TARGET2 with no exchange-control authorization. For USD-origin wires from US medical-device clients, the wire should include the purpose code for consultancy fees (services) and reference the FDA 510(k)/CE engagement contract number; the bank posts the receipt at the prevailing ECB reference FX rate, and no currency surrender is required.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements are executed via SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA beneficiaries, via SEPA Instant for EEA suppliers, or through an irrevocable documentary commercial letter of credit (UCP600) issued by the Cyprus bank for large contract milestones with US/UK manufacturers. For regular supplier and subcontractor payments in EUR, SEPA Credit Transfer offers TARGET2 settlement with next-day finality. Card-acquiring settlement for advisory fees is cleared by the Cyprus-licensed acquirer through the Visa/Mastercard networks and posted to the operational EUR account gross of interchange and acquiring fees. There is no requirement to obtain Central Bank of Cyprus approval for any outbound payment, but the bank's transaction monitoring system will attach a standard purpose code and automatically screen the beneficiary against EU and UN sanctions lists.Deliverable outright forward foreign-exchange contracts (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP) up to 12-month tenors under an ISDA/CSA master agreementFX swaps for temporary balance-sheet liquidity rebalancingCross-currency interest-rate swaps for USD-denominated receivables hedging, subject to EMIR clearing obligationsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted currencies on a back-to-back basis for exceptional client dealings only

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory TP documentation and TP form submission apply where the aggregate value of controlled transactions with related parties exceeds EUR 750,000 per tax year. The Master File obligation applies where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750 million, aligned with OECD BEPS Action 13.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies economic substance requirements through the Income Tax Law's central management and control test and the EU/ATAD substance framework. A Cyprus Ltd providing FDA/CE regulatory consulting must have actual business premises in Cyprus, full-time professional staff, supervisory board decisions taken in Cyprus, and genuine local execution of contracts. Nominally registered entities without real substance face tax residence reallocation, denial of treaty benefits, and potential realignment to a Cyprus branch taxable in the foreign management jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: In addition to repayment of underpaid tax, the taxpayer incurs administrative penalties of up to 100% of the tax understated, default interest, and criminal liability with fines of up to EUR 50,000 on conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved misdeclarations may be jointly and severally liable for the diverted corporate tax. Cyprus courts can issue travel bans, attachment orders against personal assets, and impose imprisonment of up to five years.
Transfer Pricing / Permanent Establishment Risk

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department will re-characterize the transactions, adjust taxable income, disallow artificial deductions, and assess additional tax plus interest. Penalties of up to 30% of the adjustment may be imposed for transfer-pricing documentation failures.

Director personal liability: Directors who negligently or knowingly approved artificial intercompany arrangements face civil recovery actions and disqualification under the Cyprus Companies Law, with personal liability where the arrangements render the company insolvent.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Cyprus tax residence shifts to the place of actual central management and control, exposing the foreign corporate structure to Cypriot audit and penalties. Failure to maintain substance may invalidate tax residence certificates and trigger the application of foreign controlled-foreign-company rules.

Director personal liability: Nominee and shadow directors may be held criminally liable for enabling a shell structure; substantive directors can be prohibited from acting as directors and personally assessed for unpaid Cypriot taxes if the corporate veil is pierced.
AML / International Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Cyprus AML legislation imposes fines of up to EUR 400,000, confiscation orders, freezing of bank accounts, and revocation of the company's registration. Sanctions breaches can trigger EU-wide asset freezes and removal from the approved registrant register.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability for deficient AML controls or intentional sanctions evasion, with imprisonment of up to eight years, travel bans, and worldwide asset freezing orders.

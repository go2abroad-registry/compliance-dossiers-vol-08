Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office (foreign VAT registration and issuance of German USt-IdNr.), together with the locally competent Finanzamt (tax office) for domestic corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment. Electronic filing and official communication are conducted through the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

1. A German GmbH must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt immediately after notarization and entry in the Commercial Register; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (statutory tax number). 2. To obtain a cross-border VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), the GmbH submits the corresponding application to the BZSt, usually via ELSTER or Finanzamt forwarding; the BZSt verifies identity, registered address, and place of business in Germany and issues the VAT ID (format DE123456789). 3. A foreign entity not established in Germany applies directly to the BZSt through the Foreign VAT Registration procedure, providing certified copies of the foreign registration document, constitutional documents, proof of the foreign place of business, expected German taxable supplies, and, if applicable, evidence of a fiscal agent or local establishment; after validation, the BZSt assigns a German VAT ID and an appropriate domestic tax reference for VAT accounting. Non-resident ownership of a GmbH is permitted and does not restrict tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a GmbH is a legal entity subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT; it is not a pass-through entity for German tax purposes).

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election. A GmbH is per se taxable as a corporation. The relevant statutory mechanisms are: (i) Organschaft (tax and VAT group) under Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG and Sec. 14 KStG, available where a parent undertaking is financially, economically, and organizationally integrated with the GmbH; (ii) the Kleinunternehmer (small business) VAT exemption under Sec. 19 UStG if the prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 and the current-year turnover is expected to remain below EUR 50,000, with a possible waiver of the exemption; and (iii) standard anti-hybrid rules under Sec. 4k EStG, which deny elective hybrid mismatch outcomes. No optional hybrid entity classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany has no general quantitative VAT registration threshold for entrepreneurs; any person making taxable supplies in Germany is required to register for VAT before commencing the activity. The statutory small-business exemption ceiling is EUR 22,000 in the preceding calendar year and EUR 50,000 in the current calendar year under Sec. 19 UStG. Cross-border distance sales and foreign e-commerce sellers have no low-value VAT registration threshold for German domestic supplies; the EU One-Stop-Shop mechanism is available for intra-EU distance sales and cross-border B2C supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero-rated export supply (Ausfuhrlieferung) under Sec. 4 No. 1a and Sec. 6 UStG requires: (i) tangible goods, such as hard enamel lapel pins, are physically transported from Germany or the EU customs territory to a third-country destination; (ii) the movement is caused by the supplier or by the recipient on the supplier's instructions; (iii) the export is evidenced by a customs export declaration accepted by German customs (ATLAS) and, where the recipient arranges transport, the recipient is a non-resident entrepreneur; and (iv) the goods are not used, installed, or consumed in Germany before leaving the EU. If those conditions are met, the sale is taxed at 0% and the input VAT attributable to exported goods is recoverable through the normal VAT return or, for non-established businesses, through the VAT refund procedure under Sec. 18(9) UStG or EU Directive 2008/9/EC.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrbescheinigung / ATLAS electronic export declaration and export confirmation)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (describing HS 7117 or 7616/8302/8309 hard enamel lapel pin merchandise)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note (or equivalent proof of physical transportation out of the EU)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank crediting evidence (equivalent to German foreign receipts documentation)
  • Export ledger and sales contracts or order documentation demonstrating third-country customer identity
  • If applicable, proof of delivery to the EU consignee and proof of export from the first EU member state of entry

Germany is progressively mandating electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions: from 1 January 2025, all German VAT-registered suppliers must be able to receive e-invoices in a structured format; from 1 January 2027, domestic B2B supplies must generally be invoiced electronically in a CEN-EN16931-compliant format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD), with EDI accepted until 1 January 2028. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system for ordinary VAT; VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and Intrastat declarations are transmitted electronically via ELSTER and official APIs. Fiscalized cash registers require a certified TSE (technical security element) for B2C sales, but no central invoice clearance API applies to standard manufacturing exports.

German tax authorities rigorously examine export evidence during VAT audits; where the exporter cannot produce a valid customs export declaration, transport documentation, or credible proof of cross-border delivery, the zero-rating is reversed and VAT is assessed at 19% from the date of supply. Late VAT carries interest under Sec. 233a AO, and the general assessment period is four years, extended to ten years in cases of tax evasion. False export documentation or misuse of customs procedures triggers penalties under Sec. 369-376 AO and Sec. 148-152 UStDV, including monetary fines and criminal sanctions of up to five years imprisonment, or up to ten years in especially serious cases, together with retrospective clawback of input VAT credits and recusal of the supplier's documentary evidence. Germany does not impose a separate statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export zero-rating; if a contractual or platform-driven 180-day inward remittance timeline is used and missed, the tax authorities may treat the counterparty or export as fictitious in audit, so written extension documentation is recommended.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is an IMF Article VIII member; the Euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. No central-bank allocation, no conversion queue, and no administrative authorization is required for trade settlement, profit repatriation, or capital-account flows. The only friction is documentary: a GmbH dividend distribution requires a shareholder appropriation resolution (Gesellschafterbeschluss), a tax balance sheet demonstrating distributable profit, and German dividend withholding tax at 26.375% (25% plus solidarity surcharge, where applicable) under Sec. 43 EStG, with treaty-reduced rates (e.g., 5% under the Germany-China DTT with a 10% qualifying holding; 5% under the Germany-US DTT with a 10% qualifying holding) accessible via Bundeszentralamt für Steuern exemption or refund certification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender, no repatriation deadline, no minimum local conversion ratio, and no restriction on retaining foreign currency offshore. The sole residual statutory constraint is statistical: under Sec. 67 AWV (threshold raised on 1 July 2020 from EUR 12,500 to EUR 50,000 per transaction), inbound and outbound cross-border payments above EUR 50,000 are automatically transmitted by German clearing banks to the Deutsche Bundesbank; the GmbH must be able to evidence the underlying commercial reason within 7 days on request. No surrender ratio applies to Etsy/Stripe USD receivables.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (monetary authority and cross-border payment statistics); Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin, prudential supervision of KWG-licensed credit institutions); Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) and Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) enforcing the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for operational treasury, Low-Medium for statutory share-capital escrow. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: A GmbH is exempt from independent statutory audit only if it qualifies as small under HGB §§267, 316: it must exceed no more than one of the three thresholds on two consecutive balance-sheet dates; the thresholds are EUR 6,000,000 balance-sheet total, EUR 12,000,000 annual net turnover, and 50 average employees. Medium and large GmbHs must appoint a certified auditor (Wirtschaftsprüfer) for annual accounts. Group audit consolidation is additionally required where a corporate group exceeds at least two of: EUR 24,000,000 group assets, EUR 48,000,000 group turnover, and 250 employees.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (15.825% combined); municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) approx. 14-17% results in aggregate effective rate of approx. 30%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge); reduced to 0% for qualifying EU parent companies under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive; treaty rates typically 5%-15% for non-EU residents

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border controlled transactions with related parties: EUR 500,000 for goods and EUR 100,000 for services; Master File required for group turnover exceeding EUR 100 million; Country-by-Country Report required for consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750 million

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) comparable to UAE; however, substance is enforced through tax-residency principles (statutory seat or place of management), anti-abuse rules under Section 42 AO, and EU ATAD/CBCR obligations. A GmbH lacking real substance (no premises, employees, or decision-making) may be disregarded, with transactions recharacterised or attributed to the place of effective management

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years, criminal fine, forfeiture of evaded amounts, 6% annual interest on arrears, late-payment penalties

Director personal liability: Managing director may become personally liable under Section 69 AO; personal assets subject to seizure; criminal court may impose a travel ban and revoke residence status
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Presumption of concealed income, tax adjustment at the tax office's discretion; penalty up to 5% of the adjusted amount (minimum EUR 5,000, maximum EUR 1,000,000)

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incomplete documentation face personal fines and possible civil liability for resulting corporate tax shortfalls; repeated misconduct may trigger disqualification proceedings
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the corporate structure for German tax purposes, recharacterisation of payments as hidden profit distributions, withholding tax at 26.375%, additional penalties and interest

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors may be jointly liable for withheld taxes, unpaid corporate tax, and trade tax; insolvency administrators can claw back value; personal travel bans may be ordered in serious cases
Anti-Money Laundering / KYC Failure

Statutory sanction: Fines under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG) up to EUR 1 million for enterprises, criminal prosecution under Section 261 StGB for money laundering, confiscation of profits, account freezing

Director personal liability: Managing directors can be held individually liable for omitted SAR filings; directors may be barred from acting as managers for up to 5 years, subjected to travel restrictions, and imprisoned for up to 10 years in aggravated laundering cases

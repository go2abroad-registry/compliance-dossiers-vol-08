Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Taxation of the Republic of Cyprus (Cyprus Tax Department, Ministry of Finance); official portal: 'Tax for All' (https://www.taxforall.gov.cy) and ARIADNE VAT e-filing portal (https://ariadne.mof.gov.cy); customs and VIES data exchange integrated through the EU Export Control System and Cyprus THESEAS single window.

E-government filing portal

For a domestic Cyprus private limited company (Ltd, ELF code HE77), the Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN: 8 digits plus one check character, format 88888888X) is allocated at provisional incorporation by the Registrar of Companies and confirmed by the Tax Department. A foreign entity not incorporated in Cyprus must file Form TIN 101 with the competent District Tax Office before commencing any taxable activity and must, where applicable, appoint a licensed fiscal representative. For VAT, Form VAT 101 must be submitted electronically via ARIADNE before the first taxable supply of goods or services in Cyprus; the compulsory domestic VAT registration threshold is EUR 15,600, but a non-established foreign supplier or vessel operator has a zero registration threshold and must register from the first taxable supply. A certified CYTA Trustgate digital signature is required for secure API/e-filing; the TIN must be quoted for customs export declarations, VIES activation, port clearances, immigration manifests, and maritime escrow wiring.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Cyprus incorporated private Ltd is a separate legal person and, when centrally managed and controlled in Cyprus, is tax resident and subject to corporate income tax at the flat rate of 12.5% on worldwide income. Non-resident entities without Cyprus management are taxed only on Cyprus-source income; shareholders pay no Cyprus income tax on dividends and generally no capital gains tax on disposals of shares. Offshore supply vessel chartering income may be brought within the alternative Cyprus Tonnage Tax regime on an entity-level deemed-profit basis if validly elected. There is no pass-through taxation in Cyprus for limited companies, no state/municipal income taxes, and no separate branch profits tax.

Available election forms

(1) Irrevocable election for Cyprus Tonnage Tax under the Merchant Shipping (Deemed Merchant Shipping Income) Law 61(I)/2010, replacing actual chartering profits with tonnage-based deemed profits taxed at rates from 0% up to approximately 4.5% depending on net tonnage and vessel operational days, available for eligible offshore platform supply vessels under the Cyprus flag or equivalent international registry; (2) IP Box regime under Section 24A of the Income Tax Law allowing an 80% notional deduction for qualifying intangible property income, producing an effective 2.5% headline rate for eligible marine technology patents; (3) Group Loss Relief election under Article 15 ITIC for losses surrendered among 75%-owned Cyprus-resident group companies; (4) branch election for a Cyprus branch of a foreign company to be taxed solely on Cyprus-source income under Section 34; (5) no US-style check-the-box election exists, but hybrid entity and hybrid transfer outcomes are neutralized under ATAD transposition provisions in the Income Tax Law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for supplies of goods and services by persons established in Cyprus; the intra-Community acquisition and distance-selling threshold for Cypriot SMEs is EUR 35,000; non-established foreign suppliers and offshore vessel charter operators are subject to a zero registration threshold and must register for Cyprus VAT from their first taxable supply in the territory of the Republic of Cyprus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cyprus grants 0% VAT on (a) exports of goods dispatched or transported outside the European Union where customs export formalities and documentary evidence are completed; (b) intra-EU B2B supplies of goods or services to a VAT-registered counterpart subject to reverse charge, requiring a valid customer VIES number and invoice wording; (c) international sea transportation and the chartering, hiring, or leasing of seagoing vessels, including offshore platform support vessels, where the vessel operates on voyages departing from or arriving at ports or offshore installations outside Cyprus or outside EU territorial waters, or where the vessel is put at the disposal of a non-EU charterer and the place-of-supply rule for B2B services places the supply outside the EU (outside scope rather than formally zero-rated); (d) loading, unloading, repair, bunkering, and supply services directly connected to international seagoing vessels. Pure coastal, intra-Cyprus, or intra-EU port-to-port offshore supply charters remain taxable at the Cypriot standard 19% rate. The supplier must substantiate the international character of the voyage and, for outside-scope services, the status and establishment of the charterer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic via the EU Export Control System / Single Administrative Document - SAD) evidencing the goods or vessel departure from EU customs territory
  • Validated VIES certificate of the customer status where intra-EU B2B dispatch or reverse charge is claimed
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Manifest (including the specific IMO vessel manifest) evidencing the offshore installation location or foreign destination outside Cyprus
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 cover message evidencing receipt of charter hire in EUR or foreign currency on the Cyprus escrowed bank account
  • Original Time Charter or Bareboat Charterparty identifying the vessel IMO number, voyage route, charterer legal entity, place of commencement, and foreign point of use

As of the determination date, Cyprus does not apply mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance invoice authentication. Electronic invoicing is voluntary in the private sector under the PEPPOL framework, while mandatory B2G e-invoicing (PEPPOL BIS 3.0 / BDI 1.1) applies to suppliers of the Cypriot central government pursuant to Law 35(I)/2016 transposing Directive 2014/55/EU. Tax invoices for maritime charter supplies must satisfy Article 43 of the VAT Law: sequential numbering, issue within 30 days, supplier and customer identification and VAT registration numbers, vessel/charter reference, consideration, and place-of-supply allocation. VAT returns and recapitulative statements are filed electronically through the ARIADNE portal using encrypted XML/API transmission with an X.509 digital certificate; the Tax Commissioner may require selective electronic access to invoicing records during audit, but there is no mandatory clearance API or government stamping requirement.

Cyprus VAT authorities may open retrospective audit inquiries into any zero-rated or outside-scope claim; under Article 133 of VAT Law 119(I)/2001 assessments may be raised within six years from the end of the month in which the relevant return was due. Where the zero-rating or outside-scope treatment is not substantiated by the export/charter documentation bundle, the supply is recharacterized as taxable in Cyprus at 19%, and the VAT is demanded together with statutory default interest (currently up to 9% per annum) and a civil penalty of up to 100% of the under-assessed tax. Fraudulent or knowingly incorrect export invoicing or charter documentation triggers criminal liability for tax evasion: fines of up to EUR 20,000 per offence (increased for serial or cross-border aggravated cases) and imprisonment of up to five years. Input VAT refunds previously granted on the export supply are clawed back with the same interest and penalties; the Tax Department may also suspend or revoke the taxpayer's VIES registration and impose a re-audit freeze of up to three years on subsequent zero-rated export claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a full Eurozone member state; the euro (EUR) is the only legal tender. Free movement of capital is guaranteed under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the national Exchange Control Law was abrogated upon EU accession (1 May 2004). Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully liberalized, with no CBC declaration, approval, or FX license required for euro-denominated movements to EU/EEA counterparties. Profit repatriation and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted; Cyprus imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Cyprus private limited company to non-residents, subject only to standard corporation tax compliance and VAT/TIC filing clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline applies in Cyprus. All export and charter-hire receipts may be retained in EUR, USD, or any permitted currency at the company's commercial discretion. Invoice settlement for vessel leasing may be denominated in USD or GBP; however, the CBC requires that any Cyprus-established credit institution report statistical data on cross-border transactions under the Balance of Payments framework, but imposes no conversion ratio or holding deadline.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου / Central Bank of Cyprus, CBC) acting within the Eurosystem and under the authority of the European Central Bank (ECB), exercising monetary policy, licensing, and macro-prudential supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM with legal constraints. Cyprus-licensed EMI (Electronic Money Institutions) operating under the Payment Services and Electronic Money Laws (Law L.206(I)/2018 transposing PSD2) and CBC e-money regulations may be used as secondary settlement rails. Offshore digital EMIs (UK, Singapore, or EU-licensed) are permitted as supplementary wallets for charter-hire collections, but are NOT viable as the primary escrow or operational account for maritime capital flows due to tiered banking counterparty restrictions, enhanced due diligence demands for ISIC 5222 corporate structures, and limitations on high-value MT103-based trade clearing. Any EMI used must be EEA-authorized and passportable into Cyprus.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Cyprus imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions by a Cyprus-resident company to non-resident shareholders. The 17% Special Defence Contribution is confined to Cyprus tax residents and has been abolished for corporate recipients, leaving a 0% effective outbound rate.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 9 months from the end of the tax year; calendar-year companies must file by 30 September of the following year. Provisional tax is settled in instalments during the year, with the final balance payable at filing.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 (Entity Identification) Legal name, Cyprus Tax Identification Code (TIC), Registrar of Companies (RoC) number, registered office address, tax-residency status, elected accounting year-end, and business activity code (ISIC 5222 / NAICS 483211). A non-resident entity with no Cyprus permanent establishment (PE) files no Cypriot return. A non-resident branch must first register the PE with the RoC and Tax Department, then file using the branch registration details and restrict the return to Cyprus-source and treaty-attributable income. Late filing: fixed administrative penalty of EUR 200, increasing where the return remains outstanding after formal notice; late payment: surcharge of 5% of the tax due plus statutory interest accruing monthly; under-declared tax attracts penalties up to 100% of the deficiency; deliberate falsification constitutes a criminal offence carrying fines and imprisonment (see fatal red flags).
Line 2 (Gross Charter Revenue) All gross receipts from offshore supply vessel leasing, including time-charter, voyage-charter, bareboat-hire and ancillary platform-support services. Non-resident PE filers report only Cyprus-source gross revenue and the share of international shipping revenue attributable to the Cypriot PE under the applicable Double Taxation Agreement (DTA) shipping article; revenue taxable solely in the other state is excluded. Late filing: fixed administrative penalty of EUR 200, increasing where the return remains outstanding after formal notice; late payment: surcharge of 5% of the tax due plus statutory interest accruing monthly; under-declared tax attracts penalties up to 100% of the deficiency; deliberate falsification constitutes a criminal offence carrying fines and imprisonment (see fatal red flags).
Line 3 (Operating Deductions) Deductible vessel operating expenditure: crew wages and social contributions, fuel, insurance, port and harbour fees, maintenance, repair, charter commissions and tax depreciation of the vessel. Deductions must be apportioned to the Cypriot PE under DTA Article 7 (business profits) using the functionally relevant asset, risk and personnel allocation; head-office costs are deductible only to the extent properly attributed to the PE. Late filing: fixed administrative penalty of EUR 200, increasing where the return remains outstanding after formal notice; late payment: surcharge of 5% of the tax due plus statutory interest accruing monthly; under-declared tax attracts penalties up to 100% of the deficiency; deliberate falsification constitutes a criminal offence carrying fines and imprisonment (see fatal red flags).
Line 4 (Adjustments and Incentives) Add-back of non-deductible expenditure; claims for participation exemption, notional interest deduction, 80% IP Box deduction, group relief and foreign tax credits. Attach the shareholder's Cyprus Tax Residence Certificate (TRC) and ultimate beneficial ownership declarations before claiming participation-exemption relief; PE claims require certification by a Cyprus-licensed auditor where statute mandates. Late filing: fixed administrative penalty of EUR 200, increasing where the return remains outstanding after formal notice; late payment: surcharge of 5% of the tax due plus statutory interest accruing monthly; under-declared tax attracts penalties up to 100% of the deficiency; deliberate falsification constitutes a criminal offence carrying fines and imprisonment (see fatal red flags).
Line 5 (Tax Computation) Computation of taxable income under Law 118(I)/2002, application of the 12.5% corporate rate, deduction of instalments already paid, and resulting balance due or refund. Non-resident PE balances are payable in EUR through TAXISnet; any overpayment is refunded after audit, subject to the statutory time bar. Late filing: fixed administrative penalty of EUR 200, increasing where the return remains outstanding after formal notice; late payment: surcharge of 5% of the tax due plus statutory interest accruing monthly; under-declared tax attracts penalties up to 100% of the deficiency; deliberate falsification constitutes a criminal offence carrying fines and imprisonment (see fatal red flags).
Line 6 (Distributions) Declaration of dividends or other distributions paid to shareholders during the relevant tax year, including distributions deemed under the special defence contribution rules. Non-resident shareholders are outside the scope of the 17% Special Defence Contribution; no Cyprus withholding tax is due and no additional SDC return is required for non-resident recipients, although the distribution amount must still be disclosed. Late filing: fixed administrative penalty of EUR 200, increasing where the return remains outstanding after formal notice; late payment: surcharge of 5% of the tax due plus statutory interest accruing monthly; under-declared tax attracts penalties up to 100% of the deficiency; deliberate falsification constitutes a criminal offence carrying fines and imprisonment (see fatal red flags).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital is EUR 25,000. At registration, at least 25% of each cash contribution and in aggregate at least EUR 12,500 must be fully paid in; in a one-man GmbH, the sole shareholder must furnish security (e.g., a bank guarantee or deposit account pledge) for any uncalled cash contribution. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt / Federal Central Tax Office, Bonn) for EU VAT identification numbers and cross-border administrative assistance; local Finanzamt (municipal fiscal office) for corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT filing, and payroll tax; Generalzolldirektion (Bonn) for EORI and customs/export clearance. Digital portals: ELSTER (www.elster.de) mandatory for tax filing and registration; BZSt online services (www.bzst.de); ATLAS customs system for exports.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Submit 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the competent Finanzamt within 4 weeks of commercial register entry, attaching notarized articles of association, Handelsregister excerpt, director appointments, and banking details; receives corporate Steuernummer (KES, local tax number). Step 2: Apply to BZSt (via ELSTER or Form) for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (EU VAT ID), mandatory for intra-Community supplies, exports, and imports; the Finanzamt usually forwards the application or the taxpayer files separately online. Step 3: Obtain EORI number from Generalzolldirektion for any export/import customs declaration. Foreign shareholders need no separate local TIN unless subject to German-source withholding tax; the GmbH itself is the statutory taxpayer. Representative filing: a German fiscal representative is permitted only for non-EU entrepreneurs, but the GmbH as a resident taxable entity files directly.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

GmbH is a Kapitalgesellschaft subject to entity-level Körperschaftsteuer at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (total 15.825%) and municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax, base rate 3.5% multiplied by municipal Hebesatz; combined effective burden approx. 28-32%). No US-style check-the-box; flow-through status is not electable at entity level. Available elections: (a) corporate and trade tax Organschaft (fiscal unity) requiring >50% voting rights, financial/economic integration, and a registered profit-pooling agreement; (b) VAT Organschaft under §2(2) UStG requiring legal/financial/organizational integration; (c) IFRS transition election for financial statements (HGB remain tax basis under §5(1) EStG); (d) depreciation and inventory elections under HGB/EStG; (e) application of anti-hybrid/MiDiD rules limiting deduction asymmetry; (f) waiver of small-business exemption under §19(2) UStG if applicable; (g) option to designate a German permanent establishment for trade tax allocation purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 taxable turnover in the preceding calendar year and EUR 50,000 projected current-year turnover (Kleinunternehmergrenze, §19 UStG). A GmbH engaged in manufacturing (ISIC 1079) will mandatorily register for VAT when either threshold is exceeded; additionally, mandatory registration applies from the first transaction irrespective of threshold for intra-EU acquisitions, exports, imports, or any reverse-charge-eligible supply. Voluntary VAT registration is available below the threshold and is economically advisable for mandatory export refund claims.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export delivery (Ausfuhrlieferung, §6 UStG) qualifies for 0% VAT when goods (packaged instant rice noodles, HS 1902) are physically transported to a non-EU destination by the supplier or a commissioned carrier; documentary proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) per §§8-10 UStDV is mandatory, including customs exit confirmation (MRN) or transport evidence for low-value shipments. Intra-Community supplies (§6a UStG) to EU customers are zero-rated with valid EU VAT ID and EC Sales List; export-related services (e.g., contract packaging for foreign principals) may be zero-rated under §7 UStG where the recipient is a non-EU business and services directly relate to movable goods. No VAT percentage applies on zero-rated exports, but the supplier retains input VAT refund rights.

  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement) or Air Waybill (Luftfrachtbrief)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank payment / SWIFT credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds)

  • Gelangensbestätigung (proof of arrival in third country) or customs-stamped transport confirmation for truck/rail exports

Mandatory B2B domestic e-invoicing: from 1 January 2025 all taxable persons must be able to receive structured e-invoices; issuance obligation phasing from 1 January 2027 (turnover > EUR 800,000 in 2026) and fully from 1 January 2028. Accepted formats: XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X (EN 16931) transmitted by email, portal, or EDI - no centralized government real-time clearance stamping or single API gateway for invoice approval exists today; Germany follows the decentralized EU ViDA model with future transaction-based digital reporting (planned post-2029). Invoices must comply with §14 UStG authenticity, integrity, and readability rules, be retained electronically for 10 years under GoBD. For exports, ATLAS electronic export clearance generates a MRN; no national digital stamp duty applies on commercial invoices. Customs declarations, cargo manifests, and exit confirmation are filed electronically via ATLAS/ECS submission.

Finanzamt Außenprüfung (tax audit) strictly verifies export evidence under §6 UStG and §§8-10 UStDV; missing, contradictory, or belated documentation triggers immediate VAT reassessment plus 6% annual interest (§233a AO) and 6% late-payment interest (§240 AO). Deliberate false zero-rating or forged export proof constitutes tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under §370 AO, punishable by up to 10 years imprisonment, personal liability of managing directors (Geschäftsführer) under §69 AO, retrospective clawback including Hinterziehungszinsen of 6% per annum (§235 AO), and disqualification from public contracts. Light-negligence misdeclarations incur fines under §§378-379 AO; systematic deficiencies in export portfolio lead to presumptive assessment (Hinzuschätzung) and full input VAT denial for the underlying export purchase invoices; the BZSt coordinates cross-border audits with EU authorities (EUROFISC) and automatic exchange of information intensifies scrutiny of export refund claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a regime of free current account convertibility in line with EU regulations and the AWG. Capital account transactions are subject to notification or approval thresholds (e.g., €1 million for portfolio investments). Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted provided AML/KYC and beneficial ownership verification are satisfied; no general capital controls apply to legitimate corporate operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds to authorized banks. Exporters retain foreign currency receipts directly. Statutory reporting to Bundesbank required for transactions exceeding €12,500 per shipment or €100,000 annually; tax authorities must be notified of export value and proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Bundesbank) as the primary foreign exchange authority, supported by the European Central Bank (ECB) for eurozone transactions under the Foreign Exchange Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz - AWG) and EU Payment Services Directive

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed by BaFin under the Payment Services Supervision Act offer limited cross-border viability for German corporates. Strict requirements include minimum €30,000 capital, full AML/KYC infrastructure, and prior BaFin authorization; most instant cross-border EMI services are restricted or unavailable without traditional correspondent banking relationships.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax (effective federal corporate tax burden: 15.825%), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). The combined burden depends on the municipality; it is commonly approximately 30%, not a single nationwide statutory rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 25% withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing a combined domestic rate of 26.375%. A reduced rate or exemption may apply under German domestic law, an applicable tax treaty, or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, subject to eligibility, beneficial ownership, anti-abuse, and procedural requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally, returns are due seven months after the end of the tax period. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser prepares the return, the statutory extended deadline is generally 14 months after year-end, subject to statutory exceptions, administrative extensions, and any special deadline applicable to the relevant tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local file documentation is generally required for cross-border related-party transactions where the aggregate consideration exceeds EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions in the relevant fiscal year. A master file is generally required where the multinational group’s consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million. Thresholds, related-party definitions, transaction aggregation, and documentation timing are governed by section 90 AO and related regulations; confirm the rules applicable to the specific tax year.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime. German tax authorities may nevertheless challenge arrangements lacking commercial substance under domestic anti-abuse rules, including section 42 AO, apply transfer-pricing and beneficial-ownership requirements, and deny treaty or EU directive benefits under applicable anti-abuse provisions. A GmbH should maintain evidence of genuine decision-making, personnel, premises, functions, assets, risks, and commercial rationale consistent with its reported income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 AO may be punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Unpaid tax, interest, and related surcharges remain payable. Negligent tax reduction under section 378 AO may result in an administrative fine of up to EUR 50,000.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal criminal or administrative liability for their own conduct and may be personally liable for tax deficiencies under section 69 AO where culpable breach of tax duties causes non-collection or underpayment. The GmbH's limited liability does not shield a director from personal liability for their own misconduct. Imprisonment is possible in criminal cases; travel restrictions are not an automatic statutory consequence.
Late or omitted tax returns and unpaid assessed tax

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharges, late-payment surcharges, interest, estimated assessments, enforcement measures, and administrative fines may apply under the AO. Intentional non-filing or concealment may escalate to tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are responsible for ensuring timely filing and payment. Personal liability may arise under section 69 AO for culpable breaches, including failure to remit taxes when due; criminal liability requires the relevant statutory elements. No automatic travel ban applies.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income, impose additional tax and interest, and apply documentation-related surcharges under section 162 AO and related provisions. Documentation failures may result in an estimated adjustment and statutory penalties; the amount depends on the failure and applicable statutory rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal tax-liability or criminal exposure where they knowingly authorize false reporting or culpably breach tax duties. A pricing adjustment alone does not automatically pierce the corporate veil or establish a crime.
Treaty abuse, conduit arrangements, and withholding-tax default

Statutory sanction: Relief may be denied or reclaimed; withholding tax, interest, and surcharges may be assessed. The GmbH may be liable as withholding agent under applicable German tax law. Intentional false claims may constitute tax evasion or another offense.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for culpable failure to withhold or remit tax and may face criminal liability for intentional fraudulent claims. Personal imprisonment requires criminal conviction; travel bans are not automatic.
Corporate insolvency and creditor prejudice

Statutory sanction: German insolvency law may require timely filing and permit recovery of prohibited payments, civil liability, and criminal sanctions for specified conduct. The applicable filing deadline and sanctions depend on the insolvency facts and the relevant provisions of the Insolvenzordnung and criminal law.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal liability for prohibited payments and failure to file promptly, and may face criminal penalties for insolvency-related offenses. This is distinct from ordinary shareholder limited liability.
Money laundering, sanctions, and prohibited trade

Statutory sanction: Penalties depend on the specific offense and applicable AML, sanctions, export-control, or criminal provisions and may include substantial fines, confiscation, business restrictions, and imprisonment. Food-manufacturing activity does not itself remove AML or sanctions obligations applicable to relevant transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible employees may be personally liable for their own knowing participation or culpable failures where a statutory duty applies. Corporate liability, fines, and confiscation may also apply. Travel bans are not an automatic consequence.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance (Greek: Tmima Forologias), with the electronic filing portal TaxisNet at https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy; VAT administration is operated through the ARIADNE e-portal at https://ariadne.mof.gov.cy; company incorporation is administered by the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (https://www.companies.gov.cy); customs control is exercised by the Cyprus Customs and Excise Department, Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

Cyprus private limited companies (HE77 / Ltd) automatically receive an 8-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon incorporation, transmitted ex officio by the Registrar of Companies to the Tax Department; no separate TIN application is required for domestic incorporations. Foreign entities establishing a Cyprus permanent establishment, branch, or other taxable presence must apply to the competent District Tax Office with a completed TIN application form, certified certificate of incorporation of the foreign parent, memorandum and articles of association, registered office certificate, power of attorney, identity evidence of directors and shareholders, and proof of foreign tax residence. VAT registration: a foreign hotpot restaurant operator or condiment supplier is mandatorily registrable for VAT through the ARIADNE portal (Form VAT 101) within 30 days of the date on which taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600 in any 12-month period, or from the date where there are reasonable grounds to expect that threshold to be exceeded; intra-EU distance sales attract mandatory registration once annual sales to Cypriot consumers exceed EUR 10,000.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Cyprus Ltd (HE77) is a taxable legal person subject to corporate income tax at the entity level at the flat 12.5% rate; no pass-through or transparent treatment is available for limited companies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Cyprus Ltd (HE77) is a taxable legal person subject to corporate income tax at the entity level at the flat 12.5% rate; no pass-through or transparent treatment is available for limited companies. Cyprus tax residence is determined by central management and control: resident companies are taxed on worldwide income, while non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. A Cyprus-incorporated overseas hotpot restaurant chain remains entity-level taxed in Cyprus unless central management and control is factually established outside Cyprus, in which case non-resident status may limit Cypriot taxation to Cyprus-source income only.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is fixed by corporate legal form and cannot be altered by taxpayer election. Available statutory elections and regimes relevant to a Cyprus hotpot restaurant group include: group relief election to surrender losses among 75%+ group companies; VAT group registration election treating group companies as a single taxable person; Notional Interest Deduction (NID) claim on new equity capital; intellectual-property box election granting up to 80% exemption on qualifying profits subject to EU nexus rules; tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping; deemed dividend distribution elections; and the mandatory hybrid mismatch rules adopted under EU ATAD (Law 35(I)/2021), which require disclosure and deny deductions for hybrid entity/arrangement outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover in Cyprus for mandatory VAT registration of foreign and domestic suppliers of taxable goods or services; a separate EUR 10,000 annual threshold applies to intra-EU distance sales of goods to non-taxable persons in Cyprus under the Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) regime. A non-established overseas restaurant operator may also be required to register for any taxable activity performed in Cyprus regardless of turnover where local acquisition, POS, and merchant acquiring rails are used in respect of domestic supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to exports of goods (including hotpot condiments and packaged food items) supplied from Cyprus to destinations outside the European Union, provided the goods physically leave the EU customs territory and the supplier retains transport and customs evidence. Zero-rating also applies to intra-EU dispatches to another EU member state where the customer's valid VAT identification number is stated on the invoice and verified in VIES; cross-border restaurant catering and condiment wholesale services supplied to a taxable person in another EU country are zero-rated under the reverse-charge place-of-supply rules, while restaurant services physically performed outside Cyprus for overseas customers fall outside the Cyprus VAT territorial scope. Domestic hotpot restaurant catering in Cyprus remains taxable at the reduced 9% VAT rate; only export and certain cross-border supplies qualify for 0%.

Cyprus has not yet implemented a general real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction-control (CTC) mandate for B2B or B2C invoices. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for business-to-government (B2G) transactions under Cyprus Law 111(I)/2023, which adopts the EU EN 16931 standard and is phased in for government suppliers. VAT returns must be filed electronically via the ARIADNE portal, which exposes REST APIs for machine-to-machine reporting and bulk submission. All VAT-registered operators, including overseas hotpot restaurant entities using local POS systems with merchant acquiring, must issue compliant VAT invoices and archived fiscal records, and retain B2G e-invoices in structured data format for a statutory period of six years.

Fraudulent or improperly substantiated export zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to retrospective clawback of VAT at 19%, interest at 9% per annum, and administrative penalties of up to double the under-assessed tax. Cyprus Customs and the Tax Department conduct joint post-clearance audits cross-checking SAD exit records, VIES data, bank inward remittance certificates, container manifests, and merchant acquiring/POS transaction flows. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days, submission of false remittance evidence, or absence of a valid customs auction file triggers rebuttal of the zero-rating presumption and reassessment for up to five years, extendable to ten years in cases of evasion or wilful default. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion carries imprisonment up to five years and fines up to EUR 50,000; the general anti-abuse provisions of the Cyprus VAT Law and ATAD anti-hybrid rules may additionally deny VAT refunds or input credit where artificial arrangements are detected.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus is a Eurozone member state (euro in circulation since 1 January 2008) and applies full capital-account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted; profit remittances, dividend distributions, and capital repatriation from a Cyprus Private Limited Company (HE77) to non-resident shareholders require no CBC exchange-control license or prior approval. The Exchange Control Law repeal of 1994 abolished all surrender and licensing machinery. Practical hurdles are limited to statistical Balance of Payments declaration via the reporting bank and EU restrictive-measures screening for third-country counterparties. The overseas hotpot restaurant operation may freely receive EUR, USD, or CNY; no convertibility quota or approval threshold applies to restaurant royalty, management fee, or dividend flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Cyprus. There is no statutory timeline for export proceeds repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to surrender foreign currency earnings to the Central Bank. Since Euro adoption, the domestic unit is EUR and enterprises may retain any foreign exchange receipts held in multi-currency accounts indefinitely. The only obligations are indirect: the settlement bank must file monthly Balance of Payments statistical returns with the CBC, and payments above EUR 10,000 must be reported through the standard transaction reason code framework.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Greek: Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου; transliteration: Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou; abbreviation: CBC), operating as the national supervisory authority within the European System of Central Banks and the Single Supervisory Mechanism. The Ministry of Finance retains no residual exchange control powers; all FX and banking regulation is exercised by the CBC under the Central Bank of Cyprus Law of 2002, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, restricted to secondary liquidity rails. EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passported into Cyprus under the Electronic Money Law 85(I)/2012 (as amended) and the Payment Services Law 83(I)/2012 may lawfully provide multi-currency operational accounts and FX conversion for the hotpot group. Legal constraints: an EMI cannot extend credit, cannot maintain client funds on an unsecured basis beyond the safeguarding requirements, and generally lacks direct Visa/Mastercard acquiring membership; therefore POS merchant acquiring and MCC 5812 settlement must be cleared through a Tier-1 commercial bank or a licensed acquiring bank. An EMI (e.g., Revolut Business or a Cyprus-licensed electronic money firm) is advisable only for supplier micro-payments and tax float, not as the primary compliance repository or clearing bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital injections from China/Hong Kong must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the Cyprus Capital Escrow Account, quoting ECB/CBC Balance of Payments purpose code S2011 (Direct Investment – Equity) in field 70 with reference to the shareholders' resolution date and Article 63 TFEU free-movement clause. The CBC does not require prior approval, but the receiving bank must file a BoP statistical declaration and retain the proof-of-funds pack for five years. Merchant acquiring revenues generated by the overseas hotpot POS terminals (Visa/Mastercard, MCC 5812) should flow by acquiring-bank settlement to the Cyprus Operational Account via SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated territories (purpose code SM 300 – Services), and via MT103 through CHIPS with pre-agreed FX conversion for USD-denominated territories. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions for Cyprus Local File purposes; Master File required where the consolidated group revenue reaches EUR 750 million under the OECD Action 13 standard. Documentation must be produced within 60 days of a Tax Department request.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has not enacted standalone Economic Substance Regulations, but substance requirements operate through tax residence management-and-control principles, beneficial ownership conditions for DTA relief, EU ATAD anti-abuse and CFC rules, and Cyprus's general anti-avoidance doctrine. A Cyprus company owning an overseas hotpot restaurant must maintain real decision-making, local staff or service providers, registered office, proper accounting records, commercial substance, intercompany agreements, and transfer pricing documentation; otherwise residence and treaty benefits can be challenged.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Recovery of underpaid tax, late-payment surcharge, daily statutory interest, and criminal prosecution under the Cyprus Criminal Code and Income Tax Law; conviction may result in a fine and imprisonment of up to five years, plus confiscation of the economic benefit of the evasion.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the company is used as a façade for the director's evasion. Directors may face personal civil restitution, disqualification, travel bans, asset-freezing orders, and custodial sentences.
Persistent Non-Filing and Failure to Pay

Statutory sanction: Automatic EUR 100 penalty per tax year, 5% late-payment surcharge on unpaid tax, daily statutory interest at 2.75% per annum, best-of-judgment assessment by the Tax Commissioner, and enforced collection by distraint, garnishee orders, and winding-up proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors who continue trading while the company is insolvent and owes taxes may incur personal liability for wrongful trading, be disqualified from acting as directors, and be subject to travel bans and asset restraint in enforcement proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Abuse / Profit Shifting Through Related-Party Transactions

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department will adjust the consideration to an arm's-length price, assess additional corporate tax at 12.5%, add late-payment interest and surcharge, impose transfer pricing documentation penalties, and deny treaty benefits where the arrangement lacks commercial substance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or sign false transfer pricing documentation may be personally liable for false accounting, fraudulent trading, and tax evasion; criminal penalties include restitution, disqualification, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax residence, withdrawal or refusal of the Cyprus Tax Residency Certificate, reallocation of income to the jurisdiction of effective management, application of Cyprus GAAR and EU ATAD CFC rules, additional tax, interest, and penalties, and loss of double tax treaty protection.

Director personal liability: A director who certifies false residence or substance faces criminal liability for false statements; courts may pierce the corporate veil and order directors to restore diverted funds, compensate creditors, and submit to anti-dissipation measures including travel bans and asset seizures.
Anti-Money Laundering / Structuring of Cash Proceeds

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law 188(I)/2007; money laundering is a felony carrying imprisonment of up to 14 years, substantial fines, confiscation of proceeds, and parallel tax evasion penalties for the undeclared income.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners involved in the laundering chain are personally liable; the corporate veil will not protect personal wrongdoing, and civil recovery, restraint orders, travel bans, and custodial sentences apply.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – central authority issuing VAT identification numbers; local Finanzämter are the competent local tax offices for income/corporate tax registration. Electronic filing portal: www.elster.de.

E-government filing portal

A foreign shareholder or director of a German GmbH must obtain a national tax number (Steuernummer) by submitting the standard registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the local Finanzamt via ELSTER or in paper form, typically within one month of commencing business. The GmbH then applies separately to BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using Form UStVA/USt 1 or via the BZSt online procedure. Non-resident managers may appoint a fiscal agent; foreign entities without a German permanent establishment must register for VAT only when supplying goods/services in Germany, using the simplified BZSt registration process.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; German corporations (GmbH) are subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at entity level. No pass-through classification is available for a GmbH under German tax law. — A foreign shareholder or director of a German GmbH must obtain a national tax number (Steuernummer) by submitting the standard registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the local Finanzamt via ELSTER or in paper form, typically within one month of commencing business. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; German corporations (GmbH) are subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at entity level. No pass-through classification is available for a GmbH under German tax law.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (i) Organschaft (corporate tax and VAT fiscal unity) allowing a German parent to consolidate a German subsidiary for VAT and corporation tax purposes; (ii) treatment of certain hybrid entities under §14 KStG subject to substance requirements; (iii) option under §4 Abs. 3 EStG for cash-basis profit determination for certain entities; (iv) election for VAT exemption/usufruct and small-business status under §19 UStG if statutory thresholds are met. Check-the-box classification is not recognised in Germany; hybrid mismatch election relief is restricted under the ATAD implementation (§4k EStG).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to a domestic German GmbH; any economic activity triggers VAT registration. Reduced small-business exemption under §19 UStG applies only if prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000. For EU distance sales of goods, an EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold (OSS scheme) applies to non-resident suppliers. For a German GmbH, mandatory VAT registration arises irrespective of turnover unless the small-business exception is validly claimed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under §6 UStG applies to supplies of goods delivered to a third-country territory (including customs export declaration), or to supplies to another EU member state with a valid EU VAT ID (import/export VAT exemption). For goods, the supplier must substantiate that the goods physically departed the EU within the statutory time limit. Offshore services are generally zero-rated when the recipient is a business established in a third country that is not registered for VAT in Germany, provided the place of supply is outside the EU under §§3a, 3f UStG; documentary proof of the recipient's business status and location is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / EDEN) with export accompanying document
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document evidencing departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice (Rechnung) referencing the VAT zero rate and export basis
  • Customs export certificate (Ausfuhrnachweis) – printed/generated by ATLAS customs system
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer proof showing payment received from the foreign buyer (used as corroborative evidence of export consideration)

Germany mandates structured electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) for B2B domestic transactions from 1 January 2025, using formats such as XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x, or EDI-based formats compliant with EN 16931. Invoices must be transmitted via compliant electronic channels; there is no national real-time clearance, stamping, or pre-clearance API. However, export invoices and invoices to non-EU customers are excluded from the e-invoicing mandate but must still be issued under ordinary VAT rules. No digital fiscal stamp or real-time clearance regime is currently applied at the national level.

The German tax authorities require export evidence to be compiled within the statutory time limit (generally by the 10th day of the month following the export invoice). If adequate proof of export is not produced within that period, the zero rating is retrospectively denied and VAT is assessed at 19%, plus late-filing penalties (Verspätungszuschlag) and interest of 0.5% per month. Fraudulent or knowingly incorrect export zero-rating claims trigger criminal tax evasion proceedings under §370 AO, fines up to the amount evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and charges for attempted evasion. Additionally, input VAT deductions may be clawed back and the responsible managing director can be held personally liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility on both current and capital account under Articles 63–66 TFEU and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG). Germany is a Eurozone member; no exchange control or prior licensing applies to cross-border wires, trade settlements, or profit repatriation. Dividend remittance from a GmbH is subject to 25% German withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%), reduced to 0% for qualifying EU corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) or under applicable double-tax treaties; royalty and interest outbound payments may be subject to treaty-based relief. The only administrative hurdle is the monthly statistical reporting obligation to the Bundesbank cast on all cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline applies to export proceeds in Germany. A German GmbH may freely retain USD, CNY, or any other currency offshore or in domestic multi-currency accounts. The statutory requirement is purely statistical: any receipt or payment from/to non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank via the AWV balance-of-payments reporting channel by the 7th day of the month following the payment month. Non-compliance is an administrative offence; clean reporting history materially de-risks future trade-finance onboarding.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (monetary authority, external statistics, and balance-of-payments reporting); prudential banking and AML supervision concurrently exercised by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz – KWG) and the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz – GwG).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocol for the EUR 25,000 statutory contribution: instruct the non-resident subscriber's bank to send a SWIFT MT103/INV to the GmbH's EUR IBAN at the domestic tier-1 bank, with the beneficiary field reading the GmbH's exact legal name and the payment narrative explicitly stating 'Stammeinlage/Geschäftsanteil No. X per notarized subscription agreement dated [date] — HRB [registry number]'. The remitter must be the shareholder named in the share register; third-party payments are unacceptable. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax itself, resulting in a 15.825% combined national rate; trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) applies at municipal Hebesatz-driven rates, typically 14-17%, producing an aggregate effective corporate burden of approximately 30-33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital yield tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, aggregating to 26.375% statutory leakage on all dividend distributions; no supplementary trade tax on the distributing GmbH.

Filing deadline is 31 July of the year following the fiscal year end; when the return is prepared by a licensed German tax advisor, the deadline is automatically extended to 31 July of the second subsequent year (e.g., 2024 return due 31 July 2025, or 31 July 2026 with advisor).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany imposes a Local File and Master File obligation for cross-border related-party transactions when the annual volume exceeds EUR 1,000,000 for transfer of goods, EUR 100,000 for provision of services per related party, or when the German entity's gross turnover exceeds EUR 50 million. Master File preparation is mandatory when the multinational group’s consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50 million and the German unit’s covered transactions fulfil the local thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) equivalent to offshore advisory regimes; however, substance is a hard legal prerequisite enforced through § 42 AO (general anti-abuse), § 10 AO (domicile and effective management criteria), § 7-14 AStG (OECD/ATAD-harmonised CFC rules), and treaty limitation-of-benefits clauses. A German GmbH that purports to book sales revenue through a zero-tax jurisdiction must demonstrate actual management decisions, operational staff, physical premises, and substantial economic functions in Germany; otherwise the entire income is re‑attributed to the GmbH and taxed at the full corporate rate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment of up to five years or a monetary penalty under § 370 AO plus automatic recovery of the evaded tax, back interest at 6% per annum, late payment surcharges, and extended assessment period (10 years for deliberate concealment).

Director personal liability: Geschäftsführer (managing directors) face personal civil liability under § 34 and § 35 AO for unpaid corporate tax; criminal courts can issue sequestration orders over personal assets, impose travel bans pending trial, and upon conviction the director may be banned from serving on any German company board (§ 6 StGB). The corporate veil is pierced if the director intentionally frustrates tax collection, making the director's personal estate attachable for the company's tax debt.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under § 42 AO and OECD BEPS principal purpose test; all income is re‑attributed to the German GmbH and taxed at the effective 30-33% corporate rate, plus late payment interest and penalty rates. Substantial underreporting can trigger criminal penalties up to five years' imprisonment. Non-compliance with beneficial ownership disclosures under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG) may result in fines of up to EUR 100,000.

Director personal liability: Under § 39 AO and § 30 GmbHG, directors may be personally liable for corporate debts if they intentionally deplete corporate assets for fraudulent purposes. Directors who knowingly set up the shell structure are jointly and severally liable for the full underpaid tax and penalties; automatic travel bans are obtainable under § 132a StPO; and disqualification from holding a director’s office for three years follows a criminal conviction.
AML / False Declaration and Transparency Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 150,000 under § 56 GwG for intentional breaches of anti-money laundering obligations; failure to register beneficial ownership can attract fines up to EUR 100,000; criminal prosecution for money laundering (up to three years’ imprisonment) if the directors know of the illicit origin of the funds; proceeds are subject to seizure and confiscation under § 73 StGB.

Director personal liability: The managing directors bear direct statutory liability for the GmbH’s AML compliance. Negligent breach creates strict civil liability for all consequential tax and regulatory damage; knowing participation in concealment of beneficial ownership can lead to imprisonment of up to five years, a travel ban under preparatory investigation, and the piercing of the corporate veil under § 823(2) BGB in conjunction with the GwG.

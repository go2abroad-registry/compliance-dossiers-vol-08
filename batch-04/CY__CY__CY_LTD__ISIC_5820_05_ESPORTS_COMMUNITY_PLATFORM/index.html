Competent corporate registry

Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver, Ministry of Energy, Commerce and Industry, Republic of Cyprus (Nicosia central registry and online e-company portal)

Governing statute

The Companies Law, Cap. 113, as amended (ss. 3-20 on incorporation; s. 6 requirements for the Memorandum of Association); the Companies (Amendment) Laws 2007-2023

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum and Articles of Association filed together with Form HE1 (Declaration of Compliance) and the statutory notices of registered office and of directors and secretary, resulting in the Certificate of Incorporation

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 38 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Cap. 113; a private limited company may incorporate with a single share of EUR 1.00. No escrow account, capital deposit, or up-front capital verification is required, and shares may be designated as fully or partly paid in the Memorandum.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Cyprus Fire Service for the registered office and any occupational premises, with inspection validity within the last 12 months; structural load and occupancy certification issued by a certified civil engineer under the Cyprus Buildings Law; and workplace clearance from the Department of Labour Inspection under the Factories Act Cap. 134 and the Safety and Health at Work Law L.89(I)/1996. For a remote-first digital team, these clearances are limited to the registered office and any small administrative suite in Cyprus.

Paid-in capital rule: None. No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Cap. 113; a private limited company may incorporate with a single share of EUR 1.00. No escrow account, capital deposit, or up-front capital verification is required, and shares may be designated as fully or partly paid in the Memorandum.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Ministry of Finance) — official portal: www.tax.gov.cy; electronic filings submitted through the Tax Department e-filing portal (Taxisnet/Ariadne).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity must first obtain a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN). Procedure: (1) incorporate a Cyprus Private Limited Company with the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Law, Cap. 113, or register a foreign branch under section 138; (2) file the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, registered office address, directors' identity documents, and bank account details; (3) where economic activity exists or is intended, apply for VAT registration by submitting Form T.C.1069 to the Tax Department, or use a registered tax representative for non-established businesses; (4) the Tax Department issues the TIN and VAT number within approximately 15 working days upon verification.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-incorporated entity must first obtain a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN). Procedure: (1) incorporate a Cyprus Private Limited Company with the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Law, Cap. 113, or register a foreign branch under section 138; (2) file the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, registered office address, directors' identity documents, and bank account details; (3) where economic activity exists or is intended, apply for VAT registration by submitting Form T.C.1069 to the Tax Department, or use a registered tax representative for non-established businesses; (4) the Tax Department issues the TIN and VAT number within approximately 15 working days upon verification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election is available. Cyprus applies statutory entity-level taxation at 12.5% corporate income tax rate. Available elections include: Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital; Group Relief election under section 25 of the Income Tax Law; and the IP Box 80% deduction election for qualifying intangible assets. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD apply and are disclosed through the annual tax return; no positive hybrid mismatch election is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 for taxable supplies of goods and services in any 12-month period; EUR 10,000 for distance sales of goods into Cyprus by non-established suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) applies to: (a) export of goods outside the European Union, evidenced by a customs export declaration and proof of dispatch; (b) intra-Community supplies to a VAT-registered customer in another EU Member State, conditional on both parties' VAT identification numbers and VIES listing; (c) certain international services, including services directly related to exports and transport services; and (d) digital esports services and tournament platform fees supplied to non-EU business recipients where the effective use and enjoyment of the service occurs outside the EU. All zero-rating claims require the supplier to hold documentary evidence of dispatch, delivery, and payment, as well as the commercial and contractual substance of the transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) accepted by Cyprus Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of transport or dispatch (e.g., carrier/Courier booking log, proof of delivery)
  • Customer's authorised order and contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt from abroad
  • Recipient's VAT identification number (for intra-Community supplies)

Cyprus has not yet implemented a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance regime for B2B/B2C VAT invoices. E-invoices may be issued voluntarily where authenticity of origin and integrity of content are assured in accordance with VAT Law. Mandatory electronic invoicing applies only to public procurement B2G contracts under EU Directive 2014/55/EU. VAT returns are filed electronically through the Tax Department e-filing portal; no API-based pre-clearance stamping or SAF-T reporting is currently in force. Standard VAT invoices remain required for all taxable supplies; simplified invoices apply for low-value transactions.

In the event of an audit, the Tax Department will verify the full export evidence chain. Where zero-rating is disallowed because the export is fictitious, lacks documentary support, or the consideration is not repatriated from abroad within 180 days, the VAT becomes immediately payable at 19% from the time of supply. Penalties include interest of 1.5% per month on the outstanding VAT, surcharges of up to 100% of the tax, and criminal fines up to EUR 50,000 or imprisonment for fraudulent evasion. Retrospective assessments generally extend three years from the relevant quarter; in cases of fraud or gross negligence the limitation period extends to six years (or ten years where VIES/fraud exceptions apply). The full VAT, penalties, interest, and clawback of any input VAT recovery are recoverable from the supplier and, where applicable, from directors or officers under Cypriot anti-fraud provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus has been a euro-area member since 1 January 2008; the euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Free movement of capital is absolute under Article 63 TFEU, and no exchange-control or capital-authorization regime applies to inward investment, profit repatriation, dividend distribution, loan repayment, or disposal proceeds. A Cyprus private limited company may remit dividends to non-resident shareholders without Central Bank of Cyprus approval; the only post-transaction obligation is the resident receiving/paying bank's monthly balance-of-payments (BoP) statistical declaration to the Central Bank under ECB BPM6 compilation rules. Dividend withholding tax is 0% for non-resident shareholders under the participation-exemption regime, and there is no repatriation tax, thin-cap barrier beyond normal arm's-length rules, or minimum holding-period for capital. Esports platform revenues (tournament entry fees, sponsorship, in-platform digital sales) are classified as services exports, not capital transactions, and are fully remittable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus has no statutory export-proceeds surrender or repatriation-conversion requirement. Service export receipts (e.g., global matchmaking subscriptions, tournament fee income, sponsor contributions) denominated in USD, GBP, or other foreign currency may be retained indefinitely in resident multicurrency corporate accounts without mandatory conversion into EUR. The sole duties are: (i) accounting treatment in accordance with IFRS — IAS 21 on foreign currency translation; (ii) BoP reporting by the Cyprus clearing bank of the transaction type per ECB Manual; and (iii) corporate tax declaration of FX gains/losses as ordinary taxable/deductible items at the 12.5% rate.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου) — the national monetary authority, EU banking prudential supervisor, and operator of the Republic's balance-of-payments statistical reporting system; euro-area monetary policy is delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium — EU-passported e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (e.g., multibank platforms headquartered in Lithuania, the Netherlands, and Ireland) can lawfully issue EUR IBANs and settlement wallets to the Cyprus entity under PSD2 passporting rights. However, viability is constrained by three legal-commercial realities: (i) major Cypriot commercial banks follow an active de-risking policy, refusing to open nostro/interbank accounts for EMI-correlated flows, which materially shrinks the EMI's practical payout corridor; (ii) EMI balances are not covered by the Cyprus Deposit Protection Fund (instead a segregated-trust protection under the EMI's home-state wallet rules applies); and (iii) the target payment rails (Stripe, PayPal) settle through EU-regulated financial institutions (Stripe Technology Europe Ltd, Dublin, and PayPal (Europe) S.à r.l. & Cie S.C.A., Luxembourg), which already act as de facto EMIs. Recommended architecture: a primary account at Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank for treasury and tournament prize payouts, plus one specialized EMI account (e.g., for multi-currency merchant settlement conversions) limited to auxiliary flows so that the core operational ledger stays on the licensed bank balance sheet.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Classification of esports tournament platform as an unlicensed 'betting or gaming' business; Cypriot compliance may flag prize pools, paid tournament entries, and player-to-player transfers as suspicious secondary gambling under the Betting and Gaming Law 106(I)/2018.

Root cause: Classification of esports tournament platform as an unlicensed 'betting or gaming' business; Cypriot compliance may flag prize pools, paid tournament entries, and player-to-player transfers as suspicious secondary gambling under the Betting and Gaming Law 106(I)/2018.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for shareholder loans and equity contributions: SWIFT MT103 from the parent/shareholder's own verified account to the Cyprus entity's operational account at Bank of Cyprus/Hellenic Bank (BIC BCYPCY2N / HEBACY2N). For EUR intra-EU injections, SEPA Credit Transfer (XML ISO 20022 pain.001) is sufficient and settles via TARGET2 within T+0 with no BoP approval; the commercial purpose must be indicated as 'M1 — Investment in equity capital' or, for shareholder loan, 'M3 — Credit extension from related enterprise' per the ECB BoP purpose-code manual. For USD injections, MT103 with Beneficiary Account (field 59), Originator to Beneficiary Information (field 72) referencing the Capital Subscription / Share Issue Resolution, and the Cyprus receiving bank's USD correspondent corridor (Bank of Cyprus USD via JPMorgan Chase NY; Hellenic Bank USD via BNY Mellon NYC) — settlement T+1/T+2. Verification packets retained: notarized board resolution for share issuance, stamped Form HE/F-3 filed with the Registrar, bank statement of origin of funds, and signed FX conversion order if the captial is immediately swapped into EUR. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual controlled-transaction value for Cyprus Local File; a Cyprus entity forming part of a multinational group with consolidated turnover above EUR 750,000,000 is required to maintain a Master File.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not impose a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) filing regime comparable to some other jurisdictions, but the Cyprus Tax Department applies OECD/BEPS substance-over-form principles and EU ATAD anti-abuse rules. To obtain a Cyprus tax residence certificate and treaty benefits, the company must demonstrate local management and control, board meetings held in Cyprus, a local registered office, employees, and operational decision-making in Cyprus.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Assessment of all unpaid tax plus a 10% surcharge and interest at 5.5% per annum; administrative fines up to EUR 50,000; imprisonment up to five years on criminal conviction for fraudulent tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or permit avoidance are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax deficiency, subject to criminal sanctions, travel bans, and disqualification from acting as a director of any Cyprus company.
Shell Company and Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of Cyprus tax residency or cancellation of the Certificate of Tax Residence; reattribution of management and control to the place of effective management; disallowance of IP Box benefits; strike-off under the Cyprus Companies Law; penalties up to EUR 20,000 for misuse of corporate form.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the resultant tax deficiencies, unlawful dividend claw-backs in an insolvency, and are exposed to disqualification orders and travel bans where the shell structure is found to be a deliberate sham.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Cyprus AML Law (L.188(I)/2007, as amended), fines up to EUR 500,000 or an aggregate amount equal to twice the value of the transaction, asset freezing orders, and imprisonment up to 15 years for money laundering offences. The Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) may also suspend or revoke any relevant licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally prosecutable for money laundering and facilitation offences; they may be subject to personal asset confiscation, travel bans, and imprisonment, irrespective of the corporate entity's liability.

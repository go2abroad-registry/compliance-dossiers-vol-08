Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Not applicable - no US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch election is available to a Cyprus Ltd; the entity is a per se corporation taxed at entity level at 12.5%. Cyprus-law elective mechanisms relevant to planning include: (i) Group Relief election for Cyprus tax-resident group companies (75% or greater shareholding); (ii) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity capital; (iii) 80% IP Box relief election for qualifying intangible-asset income; (iv) tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping activities; and (v) Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for intra-EU distance sales of goods.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 aggregate taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period for goods and services supplied in Cyprus; EUR 10,000 intra-EU distance-sales threshold for Union OSS; no threshold applies to non-established taxable persons supplying taxable goods or services in Cyprus

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT with input VAT credit) applies to: (i) export of goods physically transported outside the European Union by the supplier or on its behalf, evidenced by an accepted Customs export declaration (SAD/AES release); (ii) intra-EU supplies of goods to a customer holding a valid VAT identification number in another member state, reported through VIES and EC Sales Lists; and (iii) supplies of goods placed into customs warehousing or free zones for export. For oversized home-furniture e-commerce, the supplier must establish that the goods left EU territory and that commercial and payment documents correspond to the export; the zero rate is not available for internal EU sales or where proof of export is absent.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / AES electronic export declaration with customs release reference)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR road consignment note, or Air Waybill evidencing physical carriage of the furniture to a destination outside the European Union
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer or payment-order evidence showing receipt of export consideration on the company's bank account
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and exporter/delivery declaration corresponding to the shipped goods

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), API e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping regime is currently imposed on domestic B2B or B2C sales in Cyprus. VAT returns (Form VAT 101), EC Sales Lists, Intrastat declarations and OSS returns are filed electronically through the Ariadne tax portal. B2G e-invoicing to Cypriot public-sector bodies requires electronic submission via the national e-procurement and e-invoicing solution aligned with PEPPOL standards. Taxpayers must issue sequential commercial invoices, retain full digital accounting records and maintain ViDA-readiness; ViDA B2B e-invoicing and real-time reporting obligations have not yet been transposed into domestic Cyprus law.

Cyprus Tax Department audits export zero-rating by cross-referencing Ariadne VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, Intrastat records and Customs export data. Where the mandatory export documentation bundle is not secured and retained within the statutory time window, the zero rate is denied, output VAT is reassessed at 19% on the full invoice value, and previously recovered input VAT is clawed back with statutory interest. Administrative penalties and per-return and per-offense monetary penalties prescribed by VAT Law 94(I)/2000 apply; fraudulent or negligent misstatement may trigger additional penalties up to double the tax undercharged and criminal prosecution for VAT evasion with fines and/or imprisonment. Standard reassessment windows are five years, extending to ten years or no limit in cases of fraud; the Tax Department may also impose penal interest and institute distress and collection measures. Cyprus maintains no exchange-control repatriation deadline; however, artificial deferral of export consideration beyond approximately 180 days may attract transfer-pricing and substance scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Cyprus adopted the euro on 1 January 2008 and has repealed all foreign-exchange-control statutes; no residual licensing, prior-approval, surrender, repatriation or hedging-mandate regime exists for either current-account or capital-account transactions under domestic law or EU law (TFEU Articles 63-66 guarantee free movement of capital). Capital-account convertibility is unrestricted for inward equity injections, shareholder loans, dividend distributions, capital reductions and liquidation proceeds. Profit-remittance hurdles: (i) the distributability test of the Cyprus Companies Law, Cap. 113, s.62 requires audited available profits, a board resolution and a solvency declaration; (ii) dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to 0% Cyprus withholding tax under domestic law and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, while Cyprus-resident individual shareholders are exposed to a 17% Special Defence Contribution (Law 117(I)/2002); (iii) every cross-border flow of at least EUR 12,500 must be classified by the reporting bank under the CBC International Transactions Reporting System (I-TRS) for euro-area balance-of-payments statistics - a non-tariff administrative requirement, not a convertibility restriction; and (iv) the CBC may also require bank confirmation of the underlying commercial documentation such as invoice, contract or board resolution for I-TRS code verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Cyprus law imposes no mandatory surrender, no statutory conversion ratio (effective 0% mandatory conversion) and no repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The entity's e-commerce receivables settled by Stripe (EUR) and Affirm (USD) may be held offshore, retained in a Cyprus multi-currency account, or converted to EUR at the bank's prevailing market rate solely at the treasury's discretion. The only binding obligation is statistical: the CBC I-TRS declaration (transaction-level or aggregated settlement reporting depending on the reporting bank's internal threshold) and, for USD receipts above EUR 12,500 equivalent, the bank's mandatory purpose-of-payment classification. No Central Bank approval is required to open or maintain offshore receivables accounts.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou / KENTRIKI TRAPEZA TIS KYPROU), Nicosia - the sole monetary, banking-supervisory, prudential and foreign-exchange statutory authority in the Republic of Cyprus. The Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) regulates investment, fintech and EMI-licence holders but exercises no foreign-exchange-control powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: MODERATE-HIGH as a supplementary payment rail; LOW as a replacement for a CBC-licensed credit institution. EEA Electronic Money Institutions (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Payoneer, Neat) may passport into Cyprus under PSD2 and the Cyprus Payment Services and Electronic Money Laws (Law 31(I)/2018 and Law 81(I)/2018, as amended) and provide the entity with EUR/GBP/USD IBANs without a Cyprus physical branch - acceptable for PSP settlement collection and supplier disbursements. Legal constraints: (i) CBC-licensed banks may not rely on non-bank EMI customer-due-diligence files, so EMI history does not shorten bank onboarding; (ii) corporate tax payments via the JCC Smart e-payment portal, VIES VAT filings, and ISA 505 audit confirmation requests conventionally require a Cyprus bank account; (iii) Affirm's USD payout stream and Stripe's pooled EUR settlements must be reconciled in a Cyprus multi-currency account to meet CBC I-TRS statistical classification, as EMIs are not banking counterparties for I-TRS reporting; and (iv) management-and-control substance files prepared for the Cyprus Tax Department are materially strengthened by a Cyprus bank relationship. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: (1) Equity or share-premium injection - remit by SWIFT MT103 from the parent/shareholder's bank with CBC I-TRS purpose code 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital'; the receiving Cyprus bank files the I-TRS declaration and requires a board resolution allotting shares and an updated share register. (2) Shareholder loan - SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'Direct Investment - Intercompany Loans', supported by a signed loan agreement, board approval, and an amortisation/interest schedule compliant with Cyprus arm's-length and interest-deductibility rules (Income Tax Law s.33); the loan must not breach any thin-capitalisation threshold applied by the tax authorities. (3) EU-origin euro funds may arrive by SEPA Credit Transfer but still require the CBC statistical purpose declaration where the transfer exceeds EUR 12,500. (4) Verification - the bank will demand the underlying contract, invoice or resolution, and may apply a 1-2 business day hold pending I-TRS classification; foreign-currency injections are converted at the bank's spot rate and may trigger FX position disclosure confirming the commercial purpose. (5) All injections must be booked to the capital escrow or operational account in EUR; the CBC statistically distinguishes direct investment (10% or greater ownership) from portfolio investment, and the classification is recorded as of value date. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year accounting period ending 31 December, the IR1 must be submitted by 31 December of the following year (i.e., 12 months after the end of the tax year). For fiscal years ending on any other date, the return is due within 12 months of the accounting period end. The final settlement of corporate income tax is due 9 months after the relevant accounting period end (1 September for a 31 December year-end), and any late payment accrues interest at 8% per annum.

Deadline: · Frequency:

File and pay by 31 July of the current tax year; the first provisional instalment is due by 1 August and the second by 31 December of the same year. A revised estimate may be filed by 31 December where actual results are higher; downward revision is restricted once the August instalment is due.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by 30 June of the year following the tax year. The deemed distribution rule treats 70% of accounting profits after corporate tax as distributed dividends to shareholders; the charge is levied on Cyprus-resident shareholders only.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €750,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with related parties: triggering the preparation and retention of a Local File; multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least €750,000,000 must additionally prepare and retain a Master File. Transactions below the €750,000 threshold are excluded from full documentation but the taxpayer must complete the simplified related-party declaration embedded in Form IR1 and retain underlying contracts and benchmarking notes.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies the OECD BEPS substance standards, the EU ATAD anti-abuse rules and the Cyprus Tax Department's administrative substance scrutiny. A Cyprus e-commerce company selling oversized furniture must demonstrate genuine physical and operational substance in Cyprus: local management, resident directors, a leased or owned warehouse, payroll for logistics and customer-service staff, and Cypriot risk-management decisions (demand forecasting, insurance, supplier contracts). The IP Box benefit additionally requires the OECD nexus fraction. A passive letter-box company without such substance is vulnerable to re-characterisation, denial of tax-residency certificates and treaty benefits, and application of CFC-type attribution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Article 30 of the Cyprus Income Tax Law: a fine of up to twice the tax evaded and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years; administrative tax-geared penalty of 20% of the evaded tax; statutory interest at 8% per annum; the Tax Commissioner may reopen assessments for the prior six years (extended to ten years where fraud is proved).

Director personal liability: Where the company is a mere façade or trading with intent to defraud, the Cyprus court may lift the corporate veil; directors are prosecuted personally in criminal proceedings, may be ordered to pay civil restitution of the evaded tax, and can be issued with departure prohibition orders and asset-freezing orders under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law.
Shell Company and Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation of profits under general anti-avoidance rules; denial of Cypriot tax-residency status and of Tax Residence Certificates; application of the EU ATAD CFC rules to re-attribute passive income; reassessment at 12.5% plus a 10-20% false-declaration surcharge and 8% per annum interest; removal from any substance-compliant status used by payment processors.

Director personal liability: Under Companies Law Cap. 113 s. 305 (fraudulent trading), directors may be held personally accountable for the company's liabilities; personal Tax Commissioner collection certificates can be enforced directly against directors of sham entities; travel bans and disqualification orders from holding directorships apply.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Fixed administrative penalty of €10,000 for failure to maintain the mandatory TP documentation under the 2022 Cyprus Transfer Pricing Decree; TP adjustments to taxable profits; a 10% negligence surcharge rising to 20% for intentional concealment; 8% per annum interest on the re-assessed tax; possible 5% additional fine for inaccurate documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the TP policy declaration or knowingly submit false benchmarking data incur individual liability for the €10,000 documentation penalty; in severe cases the Tax Commissioner may issue personal collection orders; criminal liability arises where false documentation is filed in a judicial context.
Anti-Money Laundering and Payment Processor Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law N.188(I)/2007: criminal fines of up to €200,000 per contravention and/or imprisonment of officers for up to 5 years; the Cyprus Central Registry UBO requirements impose fines of up to €20,000 on the company and up to €10,000 on each defaulting officer; payment processors may additionally terminate merchant contracts and freeze settlement funds.

Director personal liability: Directors and the nominated money-laundering compliance officer face personal criminal prosecution, civil restitution of laundered funds, travel restrictions, and disqualification from corporate office; a director who knowingly approves a merchant account structure designed to evade processor screening is personally liable for resulting fines and processor claims.
Late Filing and Registry Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Accumulated penalties of €100 per return plus €25 per month of delay, tax-geared surcharges of 10-20%, 8% per annum interest; registry failure penalties of up to €500 and daily defaults; a company struck off the Registrar of Companies for persistent default loses its legal capacity and its Cypriot tax benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for debts incurred after the date of winding-up or striking-off; personal travel bans may be ordered where tax debts exceed €5,000 and the director is leaving Cyprus without settlement; directors of a struck-off company face joint civil liability for all corporate tax arrears.

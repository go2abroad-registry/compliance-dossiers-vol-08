Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Ministry of Finance); sole authority for corporate income tax, VAT and Special Defence Contribution; official e-filing portal 'Tax For All' (Ariadne) at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy/ and VIES at https://ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/vies/. Customs export declarations are lodged through the Cyprus Customs & Excise Department (EU Customs Union) via the EU Single Window and ICS2.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity with a taxable nexus in Cyprus must file Form TD2001 (or the electronic Ariadne application) to obtain a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN). Where the foreign entity makes taxable supplies connected with Cyprus but has no fixed establishment, VAT registration is mandatory from the first euro and is filed on Form TD2001-V; applicants established outside the EU/EEA must appoint a Cyprus-based tax representative approved by the Director of the Tax Department, whose mandate is submitted with the dossier. The registration pack includes certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, proof of foreign tax registration, audited financial statements or opening balance sheet, proof of intended Cyprus substance (registered office, Central Bank of Cyprus EMI licence evidence) and the tax representative's acceptance. TINs are issued on the spot; VAT numbers carry the prefix 'CY' plus eight digits and are validated on VIES before the permit to use the number and issue invoices is confirmed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus has no check-the-box classification; the private limited company is automatically taxed as an entity under the Income Tax Law N.118(I)/2002. Discretionary elections relevant to an EMI: (i) VAT grouping under the VAT Law 94(I)/2000 permitting a single aggregated registration for closely linked group entities; (ii) Notional Interest Deduction election on new equity capital (Form TD2001-NID); (iii) deemed-distribution election under the Special Defence Contribution rules for non-trading foreign dividends; (iv) ATAD hybrid mismatch alignment election (Directive 2016/1164, no grandfathering for legacy hybrids); (v) participation exemption election for qualifying dividend and capital-gain income; (vi) indirect tax election to treat core e-money issuance, payment processing and wallet top-up services as exempt financial supplies under Article 135(1)(d) of Directive 2006/112/EC with waiver of input VAT recovery, and the main-establishment election for place-of-supply determination of digital financial services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General obligatory registration threshold: EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for suppliers established in Cyprus. Non-established foreign suppliers have a zero threshold (mandatory registration from the first euro). Intra-EU distance sales of goods and telecommunications, broadcasting and electronic services benefit from the single EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual threshold; exports, reverse-charge B2B supplies and exempt financial supplies do not count toward the threshold. Reduced rates of 5% and 9% are limited to enumerated supplies; e-money issuance and payment services remain outside the reduced-rate framework.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical exports of goods to a destination outside the EU are zero-rated under the export provisions of the VAT Law provided the goods are removed from EU customs territory (the Tax Department accepts transport evidence issued within the customs-approved transport window, normally 90 days of supply) and the full export trail is retained. Offshore services, including software as a service, payment platform access, wallet management, IBAN issuance and FX conversion, are zero-rated under the cross-border place-of-supply rules when the customer belongs outside the EU or, for non-business customers, effective use and enjoyment lies outside the EU. Services supplied to EU counterparties are outside the zero-rating scope and follow reverse-charge or VAT exemption. The EMI may claim zero-rating only for services demonstrably supplied to non-EU merchants where the payment-fee consideration originates outside Cyprus; the claim is made on the periodic VAT return together with the evidence bundle and is subject to refund examination by the Tax Department.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX A) with movement reference number (MRN), customs office of exit stamp and EU exit confirmation
  • Bill of Lading (sea), Airway Bill (air) or CMR/CTD consignment note (road/rail) evidencing physical departure of goods from EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order and transport contract matching the customs value declared
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a Cyprus-licensed authorised bank evidencing the credit of export proceeds in foreign currency within the 180-day repatriation window
  • For offshore services: signed service agreement, certificate of tax residence of the non-EU service recipient, bank statements evidencing inbound non-resident remittances, and, where relevant, API/infrastructure logs demonstrating effective use and enjoyment of the digital wallet or IBAN services outside the EU

Cyprus does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, API-based transaction stamping or an SDI-type continuous transaction control system for domestic B2B/B2C supplies. E-invoicing is permitted where authenticity of origin, integrity of content and readability are assured, with invoices carrying the Directive 2006/112/EC mandatory fields and unique sequential numbering. A mandatory B2G e-invoicing framework (EU Directive 2014/55/EU, standard EN 16931) applies to invoices rendered to Cypriot public contracting authorities. Export and intra-EU movements are reported to customs through the EU Single Window and ICS2 using the SAD and MRN; EC Sales Lists (F-files) are e-filed through Ariadne/VIES by the 15th day after the calendar quarter. Cyprus has announced transposition of the EU ViDA digital reporting initiative once the final EU text is adopted, with expected staged go-live from 2030; until then no statutory e-invoice clearance checkpoint exists for the EMI's payment-services fee invoices.

The Tax Department applies the Kittel abuse-of-law jurisprudence (Joined Cases C-439/04 and C-440/04) to deny the 0% rate where the exporter knew or should have known of fraud in the chain. A false, inflated or artificially interposed export claim is classified as tax fraud under the penal provisions of the VAT Law, attracting an administrative addition of up to 100% of the VAT in dispute, statutory default interest on the clawed-back output tax, reversal of input VAT credits and exclusion from the simplified export-refund procedure. Directors and officers face criminal fines and imprisonment of up to three years; customs misdeclarations separately incur penalties under the Union Customs Code (Regulation (EU) 952/2013), withdrawal of Authorised Economic Operator status and publication of the taxpayer in the Tax Department's public register of defaulters. The ordinary assessment period is five years; in cases of fraud or intentional evasion no limitation period applies, and recovery is enforced by attachment, freezing orders and director personal-liability assessments.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully liberalised. Cyprus acceded to the EU on 1 May 2004, suspended the pre-existing Exchange Control Law (Cap.159), and adopted the euro on 1 January 2008. The capital account is open pursuant to Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union; dividend distribution, profit remittance, capital redemption and loan repayments are unrestricted and require no Central Bank of Cyprus permit. The temporary crisis-era restrictions imposed in 2013 were completely dismantled by 2016 through the restrictive-measures repeal framework. The only residual obligations are statistical: the CBC requires monthly balance-of-payments reporting and quarterly international transactions reporting for cross-border flows above EUR 12,500, which is a reporting mechanism, not an authorisation mechanism.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Cyprus imposes no statutory deadline for repatriation of export proceeds or merchant settlement receipts, and no mandatory conversion ratio into euro. A licensed EMI may retain 100% of its export or e-money loading collections in foreign currency (USD, GBP, CHF, PLN, etc.), subject to the prudential requirement to hold capital adequacy in euro and to mitigate material open currency positions under its Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP). This is a treasury risk policy obligation, not an exchange-surrender rule.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks. Its supervisory mandate over the EMI sector is exercised under the Payment Services and Electronic Money Law of 2018 (L.128(I)/2018) and the Central Bank of Cyprus Law (L.138(I)/2002). The European Central Bank is the monetary policy authority for the euro.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (conditional). A Cypriot licensed EMI may lawfully hold safeguarding balances only with a central bank or an EEA-licensed credit institution under Article 7 of the E-Money Directive, as transposed in L.128(I)/2018. Consequently, using a non-bank digital EMI or offshore IBAN-as-a-Service provider as the depositary for safeguarded e-money funds is not compliant unless that provider is itself an EEA credit institution. Foreign EMIs may be used only as non-depository collection aggregators for ancillary non-EUR corridors, and only if the outsourcing is notified to the Central Bank of Cyprus. Additional constraints include SEPA scheme rule-mandated direct or indirect participation requirements for IBAN issuance and enhanced due diligence by correspondent banks when layered virtual-IBAN flows are detected. The compliant treasury architecture therefore anchors safeguarding, operational and capital-escrow balances at Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank, with the foreign EMI limited to corridor collection where local bank presence is absent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are routed via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer where the remitter is in the EEA) from the shareholder's bank to the EMI's dedicated capital escrow account at Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank. The payment instruction must carry an accurate purpose narrative: 'EQUITY CAPITAL INCREASE - ISSUED SHARE CAPITAL AND SHARE PREMIUM' or, for shareholder loans, 'INTERCOMPANY LOAN - DIRECT INVESTMENT', accompanied by the ECB/CBC statistical classification for direct investment equity capital. The receiving bank will condition release of funds on the production of the shareholder resolution, the form HE30 allotment return filed at the Department of the Registrar of Companies, and the original SWIFT reference. No Central Bank of Cyprus prior authorisation is required; the transaction is captured automatically through the bank's balance-of-payments reporting. For non-EUR injections, funds clear via the bank's nostro network (Bank of Cyprus at Citibank N.A. New York or Deutsche Bank Frankfurt) and are converted to euro onshore at the ECB reference rate. Verification at final level requires a 12-month bank statement history of the shareholder, source-of-wealth declaration, and, where applicable, a legal opinion on the corporate authorisation of the remitter.EUR treasury settlement is executed through T2 real-time gross settlement via the EMI's commercial bank participant ID, with retail e-money payouts using SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for immediate wallet disbursements. Cross-border non-EUR trade settlements and supplier payments use SWIFT MT103 priority payment instructions through the correspondent network of Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank. For documentary trade settlements, the EMI utilises Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) and Standby Letters of Credit (MT760) issued through the bank's trade finance department. Card scheme clearing for the e-money business is settled via the sponsoring principal member (Visa and Mastercard) into the settlement account, followed by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA transfers from the segregated safeguarding account. Liquidity congestion is managed through a daily forecast, a prefunded euro buffer in the T2 account, a USD buffer in the nostro account, and standing instructions to convert excess FX balances at market to stay within the ICAAP open-currency-position limit.Outright EUR/USD and EUR/GBP vanilla forward contracts transacted with the treasury desks of Bank of Cyprus or Hellenic Bank, with maturity matched to projected wallet and trade settlement cashflowsEUR/USD foreign exchange swap agreements (near and far legs) deployed to cover temporary currency gaps in the liquidity bufferEuropean-style EUR/USD and EUR/GBP currency options purchased for downside protection, subject to approval within the EMI's ICAAP risk appetiteCross-currency interest rate swaps (EUR/USD) for hedging intercompany or shareholder loans with tenor beyond 12 months, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with CSA, and subject to EMIR clearing and margin obligations applicable to a financial counterpartySpot FX transactions on the onshore interbank market at ECB reference rates, used as daily microhedges for treasury value-at-risk managementNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible or available for euro pairs, which are fully convertible and settled onshore; NDFs may be considered only for exceptional non-euro exotic currency exposures through the bank's offshore back-to-back books and only with explicit Central Bank of Cyprus prudential approval

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions triggers the mandatory Cyprus Local File (and TP return / transfer pricing summary); a Master File is aligned with EU documentation practice for groups whose consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50 million; below threshold the taxpayer must still keep contemporaneous benchmarking evidence if transactions materially affect tax liability.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus applies economic substance by assessing central management and control; a licensed EMI must maintain real substance: Cyprus-registered office, resident executive directors, senior management employed locally, IT and AML compliance infrastructure operated in Cyprus, risk management meetings, and regulatory capital held in Cyprus. Absence of substance invalidates tax residence certificates, permits reallocation of profits to actual management jurisdiction, and triggers licence withdrawal under the Central Bank of Cyprus and CySEC enforcement powers.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under Cyprus Income Tax Law and Criminal Code: a fine up to EUR 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years; additional tax equal to the under-assessed tax may be imposed; late payment interest at 3.5% compounds.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors risk criminal prosecution; personal assets can be seized through civil restitution; travel bans and court-ordered surrender of passports are common in cross-border investigations.
Shell Company / Non-Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax Department can deny residency certificate, disregard entity for tax purposes, and charge tax at 12.5% on de facto owner plus penalties up to 10%; Central Bank/CySEC licence revocation and administrative fines per compliance contravention.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unlawful distributions; restriction orders, disqualification from acting as directors, and travel bans in enforcement.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to arm's length, additional 12.5% tax, interest, and penalties; failure to produce Local File can trigger EUR 750 fine and 10% surcharge; criminal false documentation charge if concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers liable for TP documentation accuracy; artificial arrangements causing insolvency expose directors to civil claims; joint investigations with EU tax authorities may result in travel restrictions.
AML Violations Linked to Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Cyprus AML Law: fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; imprisonment for responsible individuals; regulatory action by CySEC/CBC including revocation of EMI license.

Director personal liability: Directors/officers personally liable for non-compliance; assets can be frozen; travel bans pending criminal proceedings; violations remove limited liability shield.
Illegal Dividends and Customer Fund Misuse

Statutory sanction: Unlawful distribution amounts are recoverable from directors; tax authority treats distribution from non-taxable reserve as chargeable income; Central Bank penalties for safeguarding breach and licence revocation.

Director personal liability: Joint and several liability for unlawful distributions; court may order reinstatement of distributed funds; personal insolvency proceedings and travel bans in cross-border cases.

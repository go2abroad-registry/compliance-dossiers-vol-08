Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Department of Taxation, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Cyprus — Tax for All (Ariadne) digital portal: https://taxforAll.mof.gov.cy

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company (HE77) obtains its statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) at incorporation via the Department of Registrar of Companies and Intellectual Property; the same TIN is used for income tax and VAT. VAT registration is a separate procedure completed through Form TD1025 (Application for VAT Registration / Voluntary or Compulsory); non-resident companies making taxable supplies in Cyprus (other than distance sales below threshold) must register for VAT irrespective of local turnover and should appoint a local VAT representative where the EU VAT Refund Directive 2008/9/EC threshold applies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation — Cyprus tax-resident companies (incorporated in Cyprus are deemed resident) are subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide income, with territorial in-rem exemption on non-Cyprus permanent establishment income where the non-resident target is taxed at a nominal rate of at least 11.5%; the entity does not qualify for pass-through treatment under Cyprus general tax law and is not a deemed tax-transparent entity for Cyprus purposes.

        
Available election forms
Cyprus check-the-box analogue: (i) group loss relief election under Art. 19-21 of the Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002 with 87.5% common ownership; (ii) IP Box regime election (deduction equal to 80% of qualifying intangible-derived profit under Art. 9F); (iii) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity (Art. 9E); (iv) tonnage tax opt-in for qualifying shipping (Merchant Shipping Law); (v) tax transparency election for partnerships only, not for limited companies; (vi) dividend and capital gains anti-hybrid adjustments governed by EU ATAD 2 (DAC6 MDR reporting) affecting hybrid mismatch arrangements.

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating applies to: (i) export of goods physically leaving the EU (Art. 146 VAT Directive; Cyprus VAT Law Art. 14) with proof of dispatch within 90 days and receipt of export declaration; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to another EU taxable person holding a valid EU VAT ID within the IVS (VIES) — CY_LTD's cross-border video matching app qualifies for favourable treatment on B2B digital services through the reverse-charge mechanism; (iii) international transport services or services directly related to goods for export (including app-store advertising links to export transactions are excluded — pure data/ad services do not qualify). As the video-chat app sells B2C digital subscriptions via Apple IAP and Paymentwall, the underlying supplies are not export zero-rated but are 'electronically supplied services'; place of supply for B2C is the customer's country of establishment/residence, and the OSS shall be used to charge local VAT at destination; zero-rating is claimable only for intra-group technical hosting services where the recipient is a foreign business customer (reverse charge) with documentary evidence of the recipient's VAT ID and conclusion of contract.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Republic of Cyprus maintains no operative exchange-control or capital-authorisation regime. The euro is fully convertible on both the capital account and the current account, as guaranteed by Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital). Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are unencumbered: Cyprus imposes zero withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties, and there is no need for CBC pre-approval for equity injections, shareholder loans, dividend declarations, or profit repatriation. The only practical hurdle is the implementing banks balance-of-payments (BoP) statistical reporting obligation; any transfer exceeding EUR 12,500 must be tagged with the correct EU BPM6 purpose code on the wire or e-banking remittance instruction, and the bank will request a standard source-of-funds declaration for exceptional amounts. There is no distinction between resident and non-resident accounts for convertibility purposes.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules apply to a Cyprus resident company. The euro is the domestic currency; the entity receives EUR-denominated settlement flows from Apple IAP and Paymentwall merchant-of-record structures, and there is no statutory timeline for repatriation, no percentage-based conversion ratio, and no requirement to convert foreign-currency receipts through an authorised dealer within a fixed period. The residual compliance duty is the CBC statistical directive requiring banks to report all cross-border and high-value transactions (threshold EUR 12,500) with a valid BPM6 purpose code, which the treasury must pre-map against each payment stream.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou / Central Bank of Cyprus, CBC), acting as the national prudential supervisor and eurosystem National Central Bank; residual capital-control administration was historically shared with the Cypriot Ministry of Finance under the Suspension of Payments and Capital Controls Law of 2013 (L.87(I)/2013), but that emergency framework expired in full in 2015 and no successor exchange-control authority exists today.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Medium-High viability as a supplementary fast-settlement rail, but Low viability as the sole primary treasury account. CBC-licensed e-money institutions, such as locally licensed EMIs offering virtual IBANs, are legally viable for instant EUR disbursements, SEPA CT, and sub-ledger automation, and passported EU/EEA EMIs (e.g., UK- or EU-headquartered digital payment platforms) may operate in Cyprus through the payment-service passporting regime under the Cyprus Electronic Money Law (L.81(I)/2018). However, no EMI can hold capital-escrow evidence, issue documentary credits, grant bank comfort, or be used alone to substantiate the Cyprus substance required by the tax examiner. Non-EEA offshore digital EMIs that do not hold a passporting notice in Cyprus should not be used as the principal deposit corridor; they are acceptable only as inbound merchant-of-record collection rails (e.g., Paymentwall settling globally), while the treasury waterfall must always end at a Tier-1 Cypriot bank account to avoid an AML/sanctions integrity problem with the CBC and correspondent banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Compliant protocol: book the inbound contribution through SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the shareholder's own bank account, using the beneficiary IBAN of CY_LTD at the chosen Tier-1 bank and the remittance reference 'Direct Investment — Equity Capital (BPM6 F.1)'. Cyprus banks require the remitter to state the EU BPM6 purpose code even on intra-EU transfers; for an equity injection the accepted tag is the CBC BoP category 'FDI — Equity'. The bank will additionally request: (i) a notarised copy of the directors' resolution approving the share issue or capital increase; (ii) the share subscription agreement and updated register of members; (iii) source-of-funds detail for amounts above EUR 50,000; (iv) CRS/CIS declarations where the remitter is a tax-transparent entity; and (v) confirmation in the CBC anti-illicit-financing circular format that the transfer is not routed through a third-country or non-bank exchanger and is not sanction-tainted. Shareholder loans must be separately tagged as 'Shareholder Loan (BPM6 F.5)' and supported by a signed facility note with an arm's-length interest rate and repayment schedule.Primary clearing rail for recurrent operating disbursements (data-centre hosts, cloud capacity, moderation services, software subscriptions) is the single customer credit transfer via SWIFT MT103, or the SEPA-CT rail for EU beneficiaries, each carrying the matching ECB BPM6 service category 'Telecommunications, computer and information services'. For high-value hardware acquisitions, the rail escalates to a commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) coupled with airway bills, pro-forma invoices, and certificates of origin where the counterparty sits in a higher-risk jurisdiction. Intra-group treasury cash-pooling should use SWIFT CAMT (camt.053/camt.054) statement interfaces and documented inter-company loans evidenced by signed loan agreements. All outgoing wires above EUR 12,500 must be pre-tagged with the CBC-mandated transaction category or they will be suspended pending a narrative declaration; the treasury policy must therefore pre-map purpose codes for content moderation, software services, director reimbursements, tax prepayments, and dividends.Deliverable EUR/USD foreign-exchange forwards (plain vanilla, up to 12-month tenor) executed under an ISDA 2002 master agreement with a Tier-1 counterparty — permitted without CBC pre-authorisation as a hedge of the entity's USD-denominated Apple IAP revenue.EUR/USD FX swaps (swap-points transactions) for short-term liquidity conversion between EUR and USD treasury balances, settled on the banking-day or T+2 corridor.Cross-currency swaps (XCCY) and cross-currency interest-rate swaps for long-dated USD liability mirroring (e.g., financing or shareholder debt), subject to clearing capacity and uncleared-margin-rules (UMR) eligibility screening.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) — permitted only as an exception for non-convertible emerging-market settlement currencies (e.g., USD/CNH where a deliverable forward is impossible), and subject to bank counterparty approval, CBC UMR screening, and an explicit board-approved hedging policy; NDFs are not used for EUR/USD because the pair is fully convertible and deliverable in the Cypriot market.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High viability as a supplementary fast-settlement rail, but Low viability as the sole primary treasury account. CBC-licensed e-money institutions, such as locally licensed EMIs offering virtual IBANs, are legally viable for instant EUR disbursements, SEPA CT, and sub-ledger automation, and passported EU/EEA EMIs (e.g., UK- or EU-headquartered digital payment platforms) may operate in Cyprus through the payment-service passporting regime under the Cyprus Electronic Money Law (L.81(I)/2018). However, no EMI can hold capital-escrow evidence, issue documentary credits, grant bank comfort, or be used alone to substantiate the Cyprus substance required by the tax examiner. Non-EEA offshore digital EMIs that do not hold a passporting notice in Cyprus should not be used as the principal deposit corridor; they are acceptable only as inbound merchant-of-record collection rails (e.g., Paymentwall settling globally), while the treasury waterfall must always end at a Tier-1 Cypriot bank account to avoid an AML/sanctions integrity problem with the CBC and correspondent banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared when the aggregate value of controlled transactions with a related party exceeds EUR 250,000 annually. Master File must be prepared when the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds EUR 5,000,000 annually. CbC reporting applies to MNE groups whose consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

      
Economic substance scope: Cyprus has no codified Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, economic substance is a core legal requirement derived from the statutory 'central management and control' test for tax residence, the OECD TP Guidelines, and the EU ATAD/ATAD2 rules. For an ISIC 6311 video-match app operator, substance requires: (i) board meetings of the Cyprus directors held in Cyprus at regular frequencies; (ii) local employment with functional responsibility for commercial contracts, moderation, cybersecurity, and analytics; (iii) office premises and actual day-to-day operations; and (iv) qualified personnel dedicated to managing IP rights if the IP Box regime is claimed. The Cyprus Tax Department may deny tax residence or reclassify income when a shell profile is detected; apps that generate high revenue with minimal local activity are especially sensitive to source/situs challenges from Apple IAP and Paymentwall flows.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion – Fraudulent Under-reporting of Revenue
        
Statutory sanction: Additional assessment for the shortfall, statutory interest at 1% per month, and an administrative penalty of up to 100% of the deficient tax. On criminal conviction, fines of up to EUR 17,086 and imprisonment for up to five years; conviction may also lead to publication of the taxpayer's name.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or permit the false filings are personally liable under the Companies Law, Cap. 113 for fraudulent trading (civil restitution to the tax authority and creditors); courts may issue travel bans and asset-freeze orders. For egregious evasion involving cross-border receipts, an extradition route exists under Cyprus extradition treaties, and imprisonment is available as a term of sentencing.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: The Tax Commissioner may invoke the central management and control test to deny Cypriot tax residence on a retrospective basis, thereby exposing income to taxation in the actual place of management and to transfer pricing penalties. Under the Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD), the company may be disregarded or its payments recharacterized. The Registrar of Companies may strike the company off the register for non-filing or dissolution if the registered address fails to establish any business activity.

        
Director personal liability: The veil of incorporation is pierced where directors allow the company to be used as a mere conduit for tax avoidance: directors face joint and several restitution of the tax deficiency, disqualification orders, asset freezes, and travel restrictions. A travel ban may be imposed under the Cyprus Exchange Control and Court Practice where there is risk of dissipation of assets.

      

      
      

        AML / Counter-Financing of Terrorism – Inadequate KYC and Reporting
        
Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the AML Law (Law 188(I)/2007, as amended) of up to EUR 200,000 for each violation; for the legal person and responsible individuals, criminal conviction can impose fines of up to two times the value of the laundered assets plus imprisonment for up to five years. Failure to obtain a service-provider licence (where the business crosses into financial activities) triggers further enforcement under the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission framework; in this scope no statutory license is required, but the enhanced medium-risk AML profile demands written procedures.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and the appointed anti-money laundering compliance officer (where required) are personally liable for non-submission of STRs if they had knowledge or suspicion of money laundering. Financial sanctions and travel bans can be imposed by the Cyprus Courts under the Prevention and Suppression of Money-Laundering Activities Law; a director's personal bank accounts and assets are subject to freezing orders.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Non-compliance and Cross-border Margin Stripping
        
Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may adjust the taxable profit applying the arm's length principle under the Income Tax Law and the Transfer Pricing Rules of 2022, impose penalties on the additional assessment at up to 100% of the additional tax, and charge monthly default interest. Failure to produce TP documentation after a written demand triggers a fixed administrative fine of EUR 2,500 and a further daily fine of EUR 100, subject to a total of EUR 20,000.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who direct the intercompany margin policy are not automatically liable for the TP adjustment, but if they personally prepared or approved adjusted documentation with false facts, they are exposed to civil liability for fraudulent misrepresentation and criminal penalties for tax evasion. Where the lack of documentation results in a liquidation scenario, directors may be personally responsible for unpaid taxes under the fraudulent preference rule and for acts of reckless trading.

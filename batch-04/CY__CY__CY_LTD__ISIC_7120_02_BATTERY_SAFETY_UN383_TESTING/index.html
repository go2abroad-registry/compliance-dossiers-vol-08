Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a private limited liability company in Cyprus. The company must issue at least one share, but paid-up capital may be nil or any nominal amount. No escrow or deposit requirement applies at incorporation stage.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate under the Fire Protection and Combustion Installations Law 2020, issued by the Cyprus Fire Service; structural load inspection by the District Building Authority; and occupational health and safety clearance by the Department of Labour Inspection, Ministry of Labour, under the Safety and Health at Work Law 89(I)/1996.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a private limited liability company in Cyprus. The company must issue at least one share, but paid-up capital may be nil or any nominal amount. No escrow or deposit requirement applies at incorporation stage.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department, Ministry of Finance, Republic of Cyprus (Ariadne online portal: https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy/); VAT administration is carried out by the VAT Commissioner acting through the same Tax Department.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus Private Limited Company is incorporated by the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver and automatically obtains a corporate registration number (HE prefix) which constitutes the statutory Tax Identification Number. For VAT, the company must submit an electronic VAT registration application (form VAT-101) through the Ariadne portal once annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600, or earlier where intra-Community acquisitions or distance sales exceed EUR 10,000, or voluntarily where the company intends to make zero-rated exports. The Tax Department issues an 8-digit VAT number with a 'C' suffix (e.g., 12345678C) within approximately 10 business days; non-resident companies without a Cyprus fixed establishment must appoint a tax representative pursuant to the Cyprus VAT Law.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Cyprus Private Limited Company is incorporated by the Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver and automatically obtains a corporate registration number (HE prefix) which constitutes the statutory Tax Identification Number. For VAT, the company must submit an electronic VAT registration application (form VAT-101) through the Ariadne portal once annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600, or earlier where intra-Community acquisitions or distance sales exceed EUR 10,000, or voluntarily where the company intends to make zero-rated exports. The Tax Department issues an 8-digit VAT number with a 'C' suffix (e.g., 12345678C) within approximately 10 business days; non-resident companies without a Cyprus fixed establishment must appoint a tax representative pursuant to the Cyprus VAT Law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' passthrough election. Statutory elections relevant to a Cyprus Ltd include: (i) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity introduced after 1 January 2015; (ii) IP Box regime election for qualifying intangible assets (80% deemed deduction before capital allowances, giving an effective 2.5% tax rate); (iii) group relief election for transfer of losses within a 75% owned group; (iv) election to apply the tonnage tax regime (not applicable to testing laboratories); (v) VAT grouping election under section 11 of the VAT Law; and (vi) partial-exemption and margin scheme elections for VAT purposes. All elections are effected by filing the relevant forms with the Tax Department before statutory deadlines.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 (annual taxable turnover of supplies of goods and services made in Cyprus); EUR 10,000 (EU-wide threshold for intra-Community distance sales and cross-border B2C services under the One-Stop-Shop regime); voluntary registration is permitted for export-focused businesses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated (0%) treatment applies to: (a) goods physically exported to destinations outside the EU, provided the goods leave the EU customs territory within 90 days of dispatch and the supplier retains transport evidence; (b) intra-Community supplies where the customer's valid EU VAT number is obtained and entered in the VIES system; and (c) business-to-business services supplied to customers established outside the EU (including offshore UN38.3 battery testing and CE-RED laboratory services), for which the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction, making the supply outside the scope of Cyprus VAT but fully creditable for input VAT deduction as an effectively zero-rated export supply. The testing lab must obtain a written statement of customer business status and evidence of non-EU establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document, SAD) electronically lodged and cleared by the Cyprus Department of Customs and Excise
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical removal of goods from Cyprus
  • Commercial invoice and underlying export contract or purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank statement) evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-EU customer
  • Copy of the customer's VAT registration certificate or equivalent legal proof where relying on intra-Community zero-rating

Cyprus does not currently mandate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime like Italy or Saudi Arabia. However, all VAT-registered businesses must issue e-invoices in a format compliant with EU VAT Directive rules; invoices must be transmitted electronically for public-sector contracts under Directive 2014/55/EU (EN 16931 / PEPPOL). Mandatory periodic digital reporting obligations include: (i) monthly or quarterly VIES EC Sales Lists for intra-Community supplies; (ii) Intrastat declarations for movements of goods above annual thresholds; and (iii) annual SAF-T (Standard Audit File for Tax) XML filings to the Tax Department for medium and large taxpayers, with phased rollout. Invoices must be retained for at least 7 years.

The Cyprus Tax Department may launch retrospective audits over a 6-year period (extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or wilful default). Where export zero-rating is claimed without fiscal justification, the 0% rate is clawed back to 19% with interest at 1.2% per month from the invoice date, plus penalties. Fraudulent export claims or false documentation attract a civil penalty of up to twice the VAT evaded and, upon criminal conviction, fines up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years under sections 90-93 of the VAT Law. Additionally, the tax authorities may deny input VAT deductions in respect of the underlying export transactions and report the matter to the Cyprus Customs and financial-intelligence authorities for anti-money-laundering review.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Cyprus repealed its Exchange Control Law in 1994, and the temporary emergency liquidity restrictions imposed during the 2013 banking resolution have expired; no exchange-control permits, convertibility licences or prior-authorisation requirements apply to cross-border payments. Profit remittance for a private limited company is unrestricted: dividends may be paid to resident or non-resident shareholders after the statutory solvency test and corporate approvals, with no Cyprus withholding tax on dividends in respect of non-resident shareholders under the general Income Tax Law. The applicable constraint is not currency convertibility but compliance evidence: audited financial statements, the TD7 corporate income tax return, and AML-driven source-of-funds documentation required by the settlement bank. Free movement of capital under TFEU Articles 63-66 prevails, with exceptional safeguard measures available only in extraordinary euro-area circumstances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender regime exists in Cyprus. The Central Bank of Cyprus imposes no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation deadline and no requirement to sell export receipts into EUR. A Cyprus exporter may retain foreign-currency proceeds indefinitely in multicurrency accounts held with authorised credit institutions. The remaining obligations are statistical and prudential: accurate invoicing and customs declarations under EU customs law, economic-substance compliance under the Taxation of Companies Law (supported by the laboratory's physical premises, staff and equipment), and the Central Bank of Cyprus balance-of-payments statistical return covering external transactions of the entity.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (established under the Central Bank of Cyprus Law of 2002, as amended), operating within the Eurosystem and cooperating with the European Central Bank as the competent monetary authority, payment-systems overseer and foreign-exchange statistics compiler for the Republic of Cyprus.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility as a complementary disbursement and collection rail. Wise Business and Revolut Business are passported into Cyprus under PSD2 as EEA electronic-money institutions or EEA credit institutions, providing local IBANs in EUR, GBP and USD that are suitable for low-value UN38.3 vendor payments, overseas test-site invoices and freight-forwarder reimbursements. Legal constraints: (1) balances held with a pure EMI are safeguarded rather than deposit-insured and therefore fall outside the Cyprus Deposit Protection Scheme; (2) EMIs are not authorised to issue or settle commercial letters of credit, discount trade bills or act as escrow agents; (3) the passported provider must notify the Central Bank of Cyprus of its cross-border activity, and the Cypriot entity's tier-1 bank still applies standard AML treatment to incoming funds from the EMI above EUR 10,000; (4) EMI onboarding requires the same UBO documentation, permitted-use declarations and proof of the laboratory's ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation. The EMI rail is an overlay on, not a substitute for, a regulated commercial bank account.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The TD4 must be submitted within 15 months from the end of the tax year (normally the calendar year). For a FY ending 31 December, the filing due date is 31 March of the second following year (e.g. FY 2024 return due 31 March 2026). Provisional tax declarations and payments (Form TD3) are generally due in two equal instalments on 31 July and 31 December of the tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Cyprus Tax Department requires a Local File when the aggregate value of controlled transactions with associated enterprises exceeds EUR 750,000 in a tax year. The Master File is obligatory when the worldwide MNE group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds EUR 750 million. Country-by-Country reporting applies at the same EUR 750 million consolidated group revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not apply the offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) found in BVI/Cayman. Nevertheless, economic substance is decisive for corporate tax residency under the 'management and control' test (central administration, board meetings and decision-making in Cyprus). A Cyprus-incorporated shell company without real office, local staff or true testing operations is vulnerable to substance-over-form, treaty-shopping and ATAD anti-abuse rules; the tax authority can disregard the entity and reattribute income to its beneficial owners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Income Tax Law and the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, the Tax Department may impose civil penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded, plus late payment interest at 3.5% per annum. Criminal conviction for fraudulent tax evasion carries a fine and imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who instigate or knowingly permit the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax. Criminal courts may issue travel prohibitions, order confiscation of personal assets, and impose active prison sentences on the responsible directors.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: The Tax Commissioner may adjust the taxable income to reflect arm's length terms, assess additional tax, and apply interest. Failure to prepare or submit mandatory TP documentation attracts automatic delay penalties (generally EUR 100 to EUR 500) plus a potential penalty of up to 100% of the tax understated where non-compliance is aggravated.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately approve non-arm's-length pricing or deny document production can be held personally liable for the additional tax through civil claims for breach of fiduciary duty, and criminal sanctions may be sought for aiding tax evasion.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Abuses

Statutory sanction: Cyprus tax authorities and courts may disregard the artificial entity and assess its income in the hands of the beneficial owners. Denial of treaty benefits is likely under the principal purpose test (PPT). A liability equal to the unpaid tax, plus interest, and penalties for providing false information may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow shareholders risk joint liability for the tax advantage created by the sham structure, civil claims from creditors or tax authorities, and, in fraud scenarios, criminal prosecution with possible travel bans and imprisonment.
Anti-Money Laundering / Deficient CDD

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (L.188(I)/2007), administrative fines can reach 10% of annual turnover or up to EUR 1 million for severe breaches. Criminal liability for money laundering itself carries a maximum imprisonment of 14 years and an unlimited fine.

Director personal liability: The appointed AML Compliance Officer, senior management, and directors face personal criminal fines, imprisonment up to 14 years for laundering offences, travel bans, and disqualification from serving as directors.
Regulatory Fraud / Falsification of UN38.3 Test Reports

Statutory sanction: Cyprus criminal fraud provisions (Penal Code Cap. 154) impose imprisonment of up to five years and fines for obtaining money or inducing reliance by false certification. Regulatory authorities may revoke the laboratory's accreditation, suspend operations, and recall all reports; civil claims from affected clients and shippers may follow.

Director personal liability: Directors and the responsible testing engineers are personally liable for fraudulent misrepresentation and professional negligence. They may be sued for restitution, forced to compensate clients, banned from travelling pending criminal proceedings, and sentenced to prison if convicted.

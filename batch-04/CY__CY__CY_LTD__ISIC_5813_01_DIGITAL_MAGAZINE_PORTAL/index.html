Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), an integrated department of the Ministry of Finance responsible for both direct taxation and VAT; online filing and taxpayer services are administered through the ARIADNE integrated tax e-services portal (https://tax.mof.gov.cy). The VAT sub-unit is the VAT Service (Υπηρεσία Φ.Π.Α.) within the same department.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation at the Cyprus Department of Registrar of Companies, the Tax Department automatically issues an 8-digit Tax Identification Number (TIC/TIN) to the private limited company (HE77). The company or its authorized auditors then register for VAT through the ARIADNE portal using Form V1 within 30 days of the day on which taxable turnover exceeds EUR 15,600 in any rolling 12-month period, or of the first day a EUR 5,000 monthly threshold is exceeded in 3 consecutive months; digital publishers may file Form V1 voluntarily at any time. A non-Cyprus-resident company with no fixed establishment must appoint a Cyprus fiscal representative for VAT registration under Section 31 of the Cyprus Value Added Tax Law 246/1990. The VAT registration certificate is issued within 30 days after the application; the effective date of registration is fixed at the date the VAT liability arose. Upon registration the company also receives a VAT number (CY prefix followed by 9 alphanumeric characters).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Cyprus private limited liability company is a separate tax entity resident in Cyprus when managed and controlled in Cyprus; it pays corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide profits and no pass-through election exists. For non-resident ownership, a Cyprus-incorporated company that is managed and controlled in Cyprus is taxed on worldwide income; a Cyprus-incorporated but foreign-managed company is taxable only on Cyprus-source income. The digital-publishing activity is taxed at entity level outside any tax-exempt zone; the IP Box regime may reduce the effective taxation on qualifying intangible-derived income to as low as 2.5%.

Available election forms

Cyprus has no US-style check-the-box mechanism. Statutory elections relevant to the digital-publishing Ltd include: (i) EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) Union scheme election for B2C electronic services and intra-Community distance sales, replacing separate local registration in each destination member state; (ii) VAT-grouping election under the Sixth Schedule of Law 246/1990, allowing related Cyprus-established entities to be treated as one taxable person for VAT; (iii) election for the de minimis EU cross-border B2C digital-services threshold of EUR 10,000 (excluding VAT), below which the place of supply remains Cyprus; (iv) group loss-relief election under Sections 26-28 of Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002; (v) optional IP Box deduction under Section 26B of Law 118(I)/2002 for qualifying intangible assets (80% of qualifying profits deductible, effective rate circa 2.5%); and (vi) hybrid mismatch coordination under the ATAD implementation (Section 39A of Law 118(I)/2002) confirming that payments qualify under the substance safe harbor. OSS participation is elected quarterly through the ARIADNE portal using Form VAT 161A.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for Cyprus-established businesses; an accelerated EUR 5,000 per-month test applies over 3 consecutive months. For intra-EU digital B2C services and distance sales, the Union-wide registration trigger is EUR 10,000 (excluding VAT) per calendar year; above that threshold the supplier must register under the OSS Union scheme or separately in each destination member state. No threshold applies to non-established suppliers of cross-border digital services; they must register in the EU state of identification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the digital magazine portal export activities, zero-rating applies as follows: (i) exports of physical printed matter from Cyprus to non-EU destinations are zero-rated at 0% VAT if the exporter retains customs export evidence and removes the goods from the EU within 3 months; (ii) B2B digital services (advertising placements, content licensing) supplied to a business customer established outside the EU are outside the scope of Cyprus VAT under the place-of-supply rule, provided two independent documentary proofs of non-EU establishment are retained; (iii) B2B services to an EU business customer (e.g., Google Ireland Ltd) are taxable where that customer is established and are normally handled under the reverse-charge mechanism with the customer's VAT number recorded; (iv) B2C electronic magazine subscriptions to subscribers in non-EU countries are not subject to Cyprus VAT; subscriptions to EU consumers are subject to the destination member state's VAT through the OSS regime. Zero-rating is disallowed for services consumed in Cyprus; the burden of proving export status rests exclusively with the supplier.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EU Single Administrative Document) lodged via the Cypriot customs export system (THESEAS) — required only when physical publications are shipped; not applicable to pure digital service exports
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document evidencing removal from the EU within 3 months of supply
  • Commercial invoice, supply contract and written terms showing the service recipient and place of establishment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank credit statement evidencing the foreign-currency or EUR export consideration credited to the Cyprus bank account
  • Customer VAT identification number (for EU B2B customers) or two independent documents proving a non-EU business establishment (for 0% B2B export treatment)
  • OSS quarterly return (Form VAT 161A) and the OSS transaction log for B2C digital subscriptions to EU consumers

Cyprus has no mandatory B2B/B2C real-time electronic-invoicing clearance regime as of 2025, and no digital-stamping or real-time API clearance requirement; e-invoicing is permitted but not compulsory for domestic B2B transactions. For B2G transactions, Cypriot public-sector customers accept PEPPOL-compliant e-invoices under EU Directive 2014/55/EU. Statutory invoice-issuance deadlines are within 30 days of the date of supply for domestic supplies and within 15 days after the end of the month of supply for intra-EU supplies. Periodic VAT returns (Form VAT 8) are filed quarterly through the ARIADNE e-portal; OSS detailed records must be maintained for 10 years and submitted electronically. The Tax Department is assessing implementation of the pending EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) real-time e-invoicing obligation, but no national mandate is in force for FY2025.

Cyprus law does not prescribe a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; 180 days is the prudent internal benchmark adopted in bank credit-file reviews and transfer-pricing documentation for export invoicing cycles. In tax audits under Section 46 of Law 118(I)/2002 and the VAT Law, the Tax Department cross-references export declarations, THESEAS customs records, INTRASTAT listings, OSS submissions and the company's bank SWIFT inflows. Absence of export evidence within 3 months triggers automatic reclassification of supplies as domestic taxable supplies at 19% with interest. Fraudulent zero-rating claims attract: (i) VAT clawback of the full 19% output tax with interest at the statutory rate of 5.75% per annum plus a 10% late-payment surcharge; (ii) administrative penalties up to double the tax evaded under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law 4/1978; (iii) criminal liability with imprisonment of up to 3 years and fines of up to EUR 30,000 per false declaration; and (iv) where disguised schemes exceed EUR 100,000, money-laundering charges under Law 188(I)/2007 with extended confiscation powers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Euro is fully convertible under EU Treaty free-movement-of-capital rules (Article 63 TFEU); no exchange-control licence or approval is required for current-account payments, capital injections, dividends, loan repayments, royalties, or profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders. Cyprus imposes no discriminatory convertibility limits; the only practical hurdles are bank AML/KYC/onboarding documents and Cyprus statutory withholding taxes (for example 0 per cent for EU parent-subsidiary dividends and up to 10 per cent for royalty payments unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. Cyprus has no statutory timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export receipts. Digital subscription and advertising export proceeds may be held or used in EUR or foreign currency, subject only to the receiving bank's reporting of the transaction to the Central Bank of Cyprus for balance-of-payments statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou); exchange-control functions are abolished and the CBC acts as Eurosystem member and collector of balance-of-payments statistics through resident credit institutions. CySEC is relevant for regulated FX and payment-services activity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, subject to EEA licensing. Only electronic money institutions carrying a valid EEA PSD2 licence and passporting notification may lawfully open payment accounts for a Cyprus Ltd; offshore or non-EEA EMIs are not treated as bank accounts in Cyprus, may weaken tax/audit evidence, and many Cypriot banks will not accept them as proof of payment flows. EMIs should be used only as auxiliary settlement rails, not as a substitute for a Cyprus-licensed bank relationship.

0% — Cyprus imposes no withholding tax on outbound dividends, regardless of the shareholder's residency; dividends received by Cyprus resident companies are also exempt under the participation exemption regime.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

File within six months after the end of the accounting period; for calendar-year accounting periods, by 31 March of the following year. Final income tax payment is due on the same date. Provisional tax is due in two installments, on 31 July and 31 December of the tax year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First installment due by 31 July of the tax year; second installment by 31 December of the tax year. Provisional tax is required when estimated tax due exceeds €800.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 — Cyprus transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is mandatory where the aggregate value of related-party transactions exceeds EUR 750,000 per tax year; Master File and Country-by-Country Report obligations apply to multinational groups with consolidated turnover of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations like those in Gulf jurisdictions, but the substance doctrine operates through the management-and-control tax-residence test, the modified-nexus substantial-activity condition for the IP Box, and substance-based issuance of tax residency certificates. A Cyprus-resident digital magazine publisher must perform real strategic, editorial and commercial functions in Cyprus.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Law No. 118(I)/2002, Section 33)

Statutory sanction: On conviction, a fine of up to €15,000 and a term of imprisonment of up to 5 years, together with court costs, interest and the full tax arrears; the assessment window extends to 10 years in cases involving fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations, direct concealment, or benefit from diverted funds are exposed to personal criminal prosecution; courts may pierce the corporate veil and impose joint and several liability for the company's unpaid tax and penalties.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Tax Commissioner may reclassify management and control abroad, withdraw Cyprus tax residence, deny the IP Box 80% deduction and treaty benefits, and reassess tax plus 3.5% per annum interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally responsible where the company is a mere façade; travel bans and asset-freezing orders are available in collection proceedings, and professional service providers face regulatory disciplinary action.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may make a primary TP adjustment with additional tax and interest; administrative penalties for incomplete record-keeping apply, and the legal burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil liability to the company for neglect in supervising TP compliance; where an artificial arrangement was designed with director involvement, restitution and disqualification orders may follow.
AML / Money Laundering (Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law No. 188(I)/2007)

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of up to 14 years and fines up to €500,000; additionally, MOKAS (the Cyprus Unit for Combating Money Laundering) may issue asset confiscation orders and administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who fail to report suspicious transactions through internal STR channels are personally liable to criminal sanctions; MOKAS can impose travel bans, asset freezes, and disqualification pending investigation.
Failure to File / Late Payment Collection Regime

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of €200 plus €100 per month (capped at €1,200) and statutory interest at 3.5% per annum; the Tax Director may issue distress warrants, seize bank accounts, and order sequestration of company assets without prior court judgment.

Director personal liability: Persistent non-filing and diversion of corporate funds may lead to director disqualification, personal travel bans, and enforcement against personal assets where the corporate veil is pierced or where funds were misappropriated.

The subsequent lending activity will operate under Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 (ECSPR) but does not alter the standard incorporation route.

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required under Cap. 113; the Company may be formed with share capital fully subscribed through non-cash consideration and no escrow fund deposit is legally required. As a regulatory precondition, the CySEC licensing file will require own funds of at least EUR 25,000 (or one quarter of annual fixed overheads if higher) under Article 10(2) of Regulation (EU) 2020/1503, evidenced by a Cypriot bank account at authorization.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Cyprus Fire Service following the Civil Defense Laws 1996-2020 (suppression system, exit signage, and false-alarm response plan for the [Limassol] registered office); structural load certification by an approved engineer for the ICT/server room; town-planning office use permit verifying open-office occupation class; occupational health and safety clearance from the Department of Labour Inspection covering workplace risk assessment, VDU workstation ergonomics, DSE assessments, and first-aid provision under the Occupational Safety and Health Laws 2008-2022.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is required under Cap. 113; the Company may be formed with share capital fully subscribed through non-cash consideration and no escrow fund deposit is legally required. As a regulatory precondition, the CySEC licensing file will require own funds of at least EUR 25,000 (or one quarter of annual fixed overheads if higher) under Article 10(2) of Regulation (EU) 2020/1503, evidenced by a Cypriot bank account at authorization.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Ministry of Finance), accessed through the Cyprus Tax Portal at https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy; official informational site at https://www.mof.gov.cy/me/analitikon-ypiresion/tax-department.

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor either incorporates a Cyprus private limited company (HE number) with the Registrar of Companies (Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver) under Companies Law Cap.113, or registers a Cyprus branch of a foreign company. The entity then applies for tax registration through the Tax Portal using the statutory application for registration and obtains a Cyprus Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN/TIC). If taxable VAT supplies are anticipated, the same application includes a VAT registration request, or a separate Form TD101 is used for non-established taxable persons. A Cyprus tax representative may be appointed for non-resident VAT applicants.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Cyprus-resident private limited company is taxed as a separate corporate taxpayer on worldwide income at the 12.5% corporate income tax rate; qualified foreign PE profits, certain dividends, and capital gains on foreign assets are outside the tax base or exempt by statutory provisions. — A foreign investor either incorporates a Cyprus private limited company (HE number) with the Registrar of Companies (Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver) under Companies Law Cap.113, or registers a Cyprus branch of a foreign company. The entity then applies for tax registration through the Tax Portal using the statutory application for registration and obtains a Cyprus Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN/TIC). A Cyprus tax representative may be appointed for non-resident VAT applicants.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Cyprus-resident private limited company is taxed as a separate corporate taxpayer on worldwide income at the 12.5% corporate income tax rate; qualified foreign PE profits, certain dividends, and capital gains on foreign assets are outside the tax base or exempt by statutory provisions.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or elective hybrid mismatch classifications. Hybrid mismatch adjustments are mandatory under EU ATAD as implemented by Cyprus Law 33(I)/2019 and subsequent amendments. Available statutory elections include group loss relief surrender, notional interest deduction on new equity capital, and certain intra-group payment and transfer-pricing elections within arm's-length documentation requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 of taxable turnover in the preceding 12 months or expected taxable supplies within the next 30 days; no threshold applies to non-established foreign persons, who must register from the first taxable supply or transaction in Cyprus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating is claimable where the vendor exports goods outside the EU and retains proof of physical departure, including the customs export declaration, transport document, commercial invoice, and, where applicable, contract and payment terms. Intra-EU supplies to VAT-registered counterparties are zero-rated with a valid EU VAT number and movement evidence. Services: offshore services supplied to non-EU B2B recipients are generally outside the scope of Cyprus VAT rather than formally zero-rated, provided the place-of-supply rules place the service outside the EU and the supplier holds valid business evidence such as contract, recipient VAT or tax number, and actual use or benefit abroad. Financial lending and exempt financial services remain VAT-exempt under the VAT Law and cannot routinely be zero-rated unless supplied as an export-related intermediate service to a non-EU customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / electronic EXS) bearing acceptance by Cyprus Customs
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Cyprus has no mandatory real-time fiscal invoice clearance, API tax-stamping, or pre-authorisation of invoices as of the assessment date. VAT returns and e-invoices are filed through the Tax Portal; electronic invoicing may be used if authenticity and integrity are guaranteed, and PEPPOL-type e-invoicing is accepted for public-sector invoices. No digital stamping or real-time clearance gateway is currently in force.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict audit by the Cyprus Tax Department. Failure to substantiate outward shipment or misusing zero-rating can trigger retrospective VAT assessments, denial of refunds, and clawback of previously refunded input VAT. In fraud or misdeclaration cases, assessment periods extend up to 10 years, and interest is commonly charged at 2% per month or part thereof. Penalties may reach up to 100% of the tax understated, and criminal fines and imprisonment may apply for fraudulent evasion; customs and anti-money-laundering enforcement may also apply in parallel.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full euro-zone convertibility: Cyprus adopted the EUR on 1 January 2008; the euro and all G10 currencies are freely convertible on both capital and current accounts, and there is no exchange-control approval, surrender or repatriation requirement for P2P microlending proceeds. Article 63 TFEU and the Cypriot implementation of the EU Capital Movements Directive prohibit exchange restrictions between member states and vis-à-vis third countries except under EU sanctions/restrictive measures and AML/CFT due diligence under L.188(I)/2007 (as amended by 5AMLD and 6AMLD). Profit remittance is unrestricted; dividends paid to EU-resident corporate shareholders benefit from a 0% withholding rate under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (EU) 2011/96, while non-resident recipients are subject to a 17% domestic withholding tax, relievable under Cyprus double-tax treaties. Non-EUR investor funding (principally USD and GBP) converts freely at licensed bank desks, and no prior Central Bank permit is required for inbound equity, loan or investor funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to this platform: Cyprus imposes no export-proceeds surrender statute, no mandatory conversion ratio, no holding-period restriction and no repatriation timeline for foreign-currency receipts. The platform may retain USD/GBP investor funds and loan repayments in foreign-currency accounts indefinitely. Compliance is limited to balance-of-payments external-statistics reporting to the Central Bank of Cyprus: monthly MFA filings for cross-border transactions above EUR 12,500 and quarterly consolidation for operators beneath that threshold; failure to file attracts administrative penalties only, with no restriction on the underlying funds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου); the competent monetary and banking supervisory authority for credit institutions, with the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) serving as co-regulator for payment institutions, electronic money institutions and crowdfunding service providers under the European Crowdfunding Service Providers Regulation (EU) 2020/1503.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High viability: CySEC, as competent authority under the Electronic Money Law L.81(I)/2012 (as amended in 2018), authorises e-money institutions (EMIs) with EUR 350,000 minimum initial capital and statutory client-money segregation; a Cypriot-licensed or EBA-passported EU EMI provides an independent clearing rail for borrower disbursement and investor repayment waterfalls, reducing dependence on a single tier-1 bank. Legal constraints: client funds must be segregated from the EMI's own funds with a recognised prior-claim right; safeguarded funds must be deposited with a Central Bank of Cyprus-authorised credit institution; the platform itself must not take deposits or grant repayment guarantees, which would trigger the credit-institution licensing regime under the Business of Credit Institutions Law L.66(I)/1997. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK- or Asia-licensed fintechs) cannot serve as the primary disbursement rail for Cypriot or EU borrowers without a Cyprus or EU payment-services authorisation or passport; they are usable only as a treasury overlay.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The T.D.4 must be filed within 12 months after the end of the relevant fiscal year or accounting period; for a 31 December year-end, the filing deadline is 31 December of the immediately following calendar year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

T.D.3 is due by 31 July of the tax year; the first instalment is payable by 31 July and the second instalment by 31 December of the same tax year; newly incorporated companies must file T.D.3 within one month of incorporation, with a final deadline extension to 31 December of the following year where the tax year end differs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cyprus transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is mandatory where the aggregate gross value of controlled transactions with related parties exceeds EUR 750,000 per tax year; the TP summary schedule must be attached electronically to the T.D.4 via TAXISnet for all controlled transactions where documentation is required. Companies above the threshold must prepare and retain a benchmarking study and contemporaneous documentation for three years; a Master File aligned with OECD Chapter V is expected for multinational groups and may be demanded by the Cyprus Tax Department, while interest deductions on intra-group financing remain subject to the EU ATAD interest limitation rule of 30% EBITDA.

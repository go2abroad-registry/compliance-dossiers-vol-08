Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and certain cross-border tax matters; the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate tax registration. Tax registration and filings are generally handled through the ELSTER online tax portal.

E-government filing portal

After incorporation and commencement of business, submit the electronic Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung through ELSTER to the Finanzamt responsible for the company's registered office. The Finanzamt issues a tax number (Steuernummer) after reviewing the registration. Apply separately to the BZSt for a German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.), generally using the tax number or other requested company details. A foreign-established entity making taxable supplies in Germany must register with the competent German tax office before or upon commencing those supplies; the competent office depends on the foreign entity's place of establishment. Registration requirements may be affected by the reverse-charge mechanism, the Union One Stop Shop, or other applicable special schemes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is generally a separate taxpayer for corporate income tax and trade tax; Germany has no general US-style check-the-box election to treat a GmbH as a pass-through. Certain qualifying corporate groups may elect the German fiscal unity regime (Organschaft) if statutory financial, organizational, and economic integration requirements are met. German and EU anti-hybrid rules may deny deductions or require inclusion in specified mismatch cases; these rules are not elective entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exempts a business from VAT registration for taxable domestic supplies. The domestic small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) is generally available where prior-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions; it is an exemption regime, not a general registration threshold. For qualifying cross-border EU consumer supplies, the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold may be relevant to place-of-supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the EU may qualify as VAT-exempt with the right to deduct input VAT (zero-rated in commercial effect) where the statutory export-supply conditions are met and the supplier retains prescribed evidence that the goods left the EU. Intra-EU supplies are governed by separate exemption rules, including customer VAT-ID and transport-evidence requirements. Services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer is offshore: place-of-supply rules determine whether German VAT applies, and specific exemptions or reverse-charge rules may apply.

  • German export customs declaration and electronic exit confirmation or other legally accepted proof that the goods left the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, and relevant shipment
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the shipment, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, carrier confirmation, or equivalent delivery evidence
  • Purchase order, sales contract, and payment records supporting the transaction and customer identity
  • For intra-EU supplies, where applicable, customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required recapitulative statement records
  • Inward remittance certificate is not a general German statutory condition for export VAT exemption

Germany does not generally require real-time tax clearance, fiscal digital stamping, or transmission of every domestic invoice to the tax authority. Since 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for relevant domestic B2B transactions. Issuing obligations are being phased in: transitional rules generally permit paper or other electronic invoices through 2026, with extensions through 2027 for qualifying issuers; structured e-invoicing is generally required from 2028, subject to statutory exceptions and conditions. A compliant structured format must meet German and EU requirements; ordinary PDF is not a structured e-invoice. No general real-time API clearance mandate applies.

Germany imposes no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The supplier must substantiate the export exemption and retain records under German tax and accounting rules. If evidence is missing or the statutory conditions are not met, the exemption may be denied and German VAT assessed, with interest under applicable rules. Intentional or reckless tax evasion may trigger tax penalties or criminal prosecution; incorrect or late VAT returns may also result in statutory surcharges and other administrative consequences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has a fully liberalised cross-border payment and currency regime as part of the euro area. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are generally free from exchange-control approvals under EU free movement of capital. Profit remittances by a GmbH are permitted once annual accounts are approved and legal solvency tests are met; the practical hurdles are fiscal and documentary rather than exchange-control based: profit distribution taxes (Abgeltungsteuer 25% plus solidarity surcharge and trade tax), withholding tax proof according to the double-tax treaty, beneficial-owner declarations, AML/anti-corruption due diligence, and adherence to EU/UN sanctions and export controls. There is no requirement to convert EUR or any foreign currency through a domestic-authorised bank, save for statistical purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds and no minimum conversion ratio. Export receivables may be left in foreign currency and used freely outside Germany. The only compliance layer concerns statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border receipts over the statutory threshold (currently EUR 12,500), which must be declared in the monthly external-balance-of-payments statistics under the AWV; there is no imposed timeline for repatriation or conversion.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank), in conjunction with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action, operating under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditionally viable for low-value e-commerce/pre-order flows, but not as a primary treasury bank. An EU-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) with a BaFin passport may offer payment accounts, German IBANs through partner banks, SEPA and cross-border payment execution, merchant payouts and FX conversion under PSD2. It cannot take deposits, extend trade credit, issue documentary credits, or hold shareholder capital in escrow unless acting through a safeguarded account at a licensed bank. Offshore/non-EEA EMIs have no passporting right into Germany; using an offshore EMI would be a high legal/operational risk and would not satisfy corporate bank KYC expectations. If used at all, an EMI should be used only as a secondary collection rail managed by the German GmbH, with the core treasury and statutory capital maintained in a full-service credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or loan capital should be routed exclusively through the corporate bank account of the German GmbH. For euro-area transfers use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) with the GmbH’s German IBAN and BIC, specifying payment purpose 'Stammeinlage' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen'; for non-EEA currencies and third-country banks use SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary name, IBAN/account number, bank BIC and clearing details. Each wire must include an AWV statistical purpose code from the Deutsche Bundesbank’s current payment-statistics classification (typically the 'A02' family for direct investments/equity and 'A03' for loans), the reference to the notarised contribution resolution, and the transaction must be reconciled against the escrow account used to prove payment of the €25,000 minimum share capital.

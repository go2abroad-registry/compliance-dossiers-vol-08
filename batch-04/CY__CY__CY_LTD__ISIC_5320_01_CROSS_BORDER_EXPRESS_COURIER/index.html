Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (TPD/Cyprus Ministry of Finance) – official portal: https://tax.mof.gov.cy; VAT administered via the Cyprus VAT Service within the same Tax Department.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus private limited company is automatically issued a Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN/TAX I.D.) at incorporation. A non-resident foreign entity establishing a branch or permanent establishment must register with the local District Tax Office within 60 days of commencing operations, submitting certified charter documents, board resolutions, and audited opening financials. VAT registration is mandatory for non-established businesses making taxable supplies in Cyprus and is effected via Form VAT 101 to the VAT Service, resulting in a CY VAT number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. Cyprus-resident private limited companies are fully separate tax subjects paying corporation tax at 12.5% on worldwide income; territorial limitations apply only to foreign branches of Cypriot companies under controlled-foreign-company and double-tax-treaty provisions. No tax-exempt zones exist for express courier operations.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is recognized for Cyprus legal entities. Statute provides opt-in regimes: notional interest deduction (NID) on fresh equity, IP Box regime (5% deemed gross profit, effective 2.5%), tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping income, group relief between 75%+ related entities, and deemed-dividend distribution rules. EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance (ATAD) hybrid mismatch rules apply mandatorily; no entity-classification election is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 taxable turnover in any twelve-month period; non-established express-courier operators are required to register without any threshold if they make taxable supplies in Cyprus

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods transported to a destination outside the EU by or on behalf of the express courier, evidenced by a customs export declaration; international transport of goods to third countries and ancillary courier services connected directly to export are zero-rated. The claimant must hold complete proof of physical removal from the EU and a valid relationship between the supply and the export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic export declaration accepted by Cyprus Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch to a non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or commercial bank credit confirmation proving repatriation of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous control, or digital stamping requirement is imposed on B2B invoices in Cyprus. B2G e-invoicing is obligated through the PEPPOL network; VAT returns, VIES listings, and INTRASTAT filings must be submitted electronically via TAXISnet. The Tax Department is preparing a phased mandate for structured e-invoicing but has not introduced API-based real-time clearance as of this stage.

Upon audit, missing export proof triggers reassessment at 19% VAT, interest at 2% per month capitalised annually, administrative penalties up to 25% of the underdeclared tax, and up to 100% for wilful intent; criminal proceedings may impose fines and imprisonment up to five years. Zero-rated refunds are clawed back retroactively with all VAT reclaimed on denied export claims, including liability under EU anti-fraud and carousel-fraud provisions.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Cyprus maintains a fully liberalised capital-account and current-account regime in accordance with Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Directive 88/361/EEC. There are no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no prior approval requirements for remittance of profits, dividends, loan repayments, or intra-group current transactions. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to bank-level AML/KYC due diligence, proof of economic substance, tax compliance, and the Central Bank of Cyprus balance-of-payments reporting framework. Profit distributions to EU/EEA shareholders attract no Cyprus withholding tax, and non-EU shareholders are generally also exempt from withholding tax under Cyprus domestic tax law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate sales revenues into euro. Export receipts may be received in any freely convertible currency and credited to corporate foreign-currency or euro accounts. The only formal requirement is accurate recording for VAT purposes (zero-rated export of services under the Cyprus VAT Law) and the completion of transaction-level balance-of-payments reporting to the Central Bank of Cyprus through the settlement bank if the transaction value exceeds the applicable reporting threshold. No prior exchange-control licence is required.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou), acting as the EU national competent authority for banking supervision, balance-of-payments statistics, and foreign-exchange market oversight; the Cyprus Ministry of Finance retains policy competence for financial-sector legislation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary payment-clearing rail; Low as the sole or primary treasury settlement venue for a Cyprus-incorporated medium-risk international express courier. Cyprus-licensed or EEA-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may provide EUR and multi-currency accounts and faster payment functionalities, but they are not licensed credit institutions and are not appropriate for capital-escrow, high-value SWIFT settlement, or long-term treasury warehousing. Legal constraints require the EMI to hold an Electronic Money Institution licence under the Cyprus Electronic Money Law (N.82(I)/2012) or be passported under Directive 2009/110/EC, and to apply the full customer due diligence and transaction-monitoring standards of the Cyprus AML Law (N.188(I)/2007). Many EMIs reject or restrict medium-risk courier/freight-forwarding merchants, so a definitive corporate onboarding dossier and transaction-flow evidence are mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from the shareholder's account to the Cyprus company's designated capital-escrow or operational account at a Tier-1 Cypriot bank. Field 70 must contain a precise purpose description, for example 'SHARE CAPITAL INCREASE / SHARE PREMIUM', and the beneficiary and ordering-customer references must exactly match the company incorporation records. For euro-denominated shareholder cables, SEPA Credit Transfer may be used if the remitting institution is in a SEPA-participating country, but SWIFT MT103 remains preferred for audit trail and for non-EUR remittances. The file prepared for the receiving bank should include the capital-increase resolution, updated shareholder ledger and register, confirmation from the Cyprus Registrar of Companies and, where applicable, the balance-of-payments reporting data required by the Central Bank of Cyprus. Bank compliance will verify that the remitter name is identical to the registered shareholder and that no sanctions, PEP, or adverse-media hits arise.Clearing rails should be selected by currency, value date, and risk. For euro payments to EU sub-contractors, tax authorities, utilities, and customs agents, use SEPA Credit Transfer with structured creditor reference; for high-value urgent euro payments, use the Target2 high-value payment system. For non-EEA or non-euro trade settlements and sub-agent or consolidator payouts, use SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers with underlying invoice and airway-bill references in Field 70; internal house transfers may be executed by SWIFT MT202 COV. Where the courier operation requires trade-based guarantees or documentary settlement, commercial letters of credit issued through SWIFT MT700 and standby letters of credit through SWIFT MT760 are permissible with the bank's trade-services department. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Cyprus does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions to non-residents. Dividends paid by a Cyprus resident company to any non-resident shareholder are not subject to Cyprus income tax, SDC or WHT.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return is due within 12 months after the end of the tax year. For a company with a 31 December year-end, the filing deadline is 31 December of the following year. Provisional tax payments are due by 31 July (interim) and 31 December (final instalment) of the tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 - a Cyprus taxpayer is required to prepare and file transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) when the aggregate value of controlled transactions with related parties exceeds EUR 750,000 in a tax year. A circular from the Cyprus Tax Department applies proportionality and allows a simplified file for lower intercompany risk transactions.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not operate a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. However, Cyprus law incorporates the OECD BEPS standards, the EU Principal Purpose Test, and the Cyprus General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR). A Cyprus resident company must maintain real economic substance and demonstrable board activity in Cyprus; a mail-box company with no office, staff, or actual logistics functions may be re-characterised as non-Cypriot tax resident or subjected to treaty-abuse denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraud

Statutory sanction: Assessment of omitted tax plus statutory interest; administrative penalty of up to double the amount of tax evaded; criminal conviction on indictment liable to a fine of up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years; forfeiture of assets derived from the evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in, conniving at, or negligently permitting the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. Polish/cypriot courts may issue travel bans, asset freezing orders, and disqualification orders preventing the director from acting as an officer of any company for up to 5 years.
Artificial shell company / sham substance

Statutory sanction: Re-allocation of income under Cyprus transfer pricing rules; re-determination of tax residency to the place of effective management, with Cypriot tax assessed at 12.5% on worldwide income and denial of Treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test. The Registrar of Companies may order a striking-off for failing to maintain a registered office and file age statements.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for wrongful trading and for the payment of corporate taxes where they act in breach of the Companies Law (Cap. 113); their personal assets may be attached and their ability to act as directors in any EU jurisdiction may be restricted.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment increasing taxable profit; interest charged at the Cyprus statutory late-payment rate; documentation penalties if the TP file is not submitted; shortfall interest and double tax exposure where the foreign counterparty is not given a compensating adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false transfer pricing documentation are subject to administrative fines of up to EUR 5,000 individually and may be disqualified as director for up to 3 years; their acts may also be relied upon by the Tax Department to pierce the corporate veil.
AML and sanctions compliance failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (188(I)/2007), the company faces fines up to EUR 2 million or 10% of annual turnover for critical AML failures; accounts may be frozen; sanctions violations carry separate EU/UN penalties. On conviction the offence is punishable by imprisonment up to 14 years for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and criminally liable where they knew or ought to have known of money laundering activity; they may be imprisoned, personally fined, and subjected to travel bans and asset forfeiture. Executive officers cannot be indemnified by the company for criminal fines.
Failure to file and false tax declarations

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalties (EUR 100 plus EUR 50 per month) as described; false statement penalty up to EUR 25,000; interest on unpaid tax at 1% per month; the company may be struck off the Register and dissolution of the company prevents claim for corporate tax only after a final winding-up order.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly liable for the payment of tax and penalties where they allowed the company to trade while insolvent or where they failed to file statutory returns; they may be disqualified and barred from obtaining professional licences, and may be personally assessed in the course of liquidation.

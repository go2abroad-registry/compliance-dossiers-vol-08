Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Finanzamt (local German tax office) with jurisdiction over the GmbH's registered seat, responsible for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessments; Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) as the federal central tax office for issuance of the USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number), EC Sales List processing, and EU cross-border coordination; ELSTER electronic filing portal (https://www.elster.de) as the mandatory digital access point for registration and periodic VAT filings.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned German GmbH: (1) execute notarized incorporation and register the entity in the commercial register (Handelsregister); (2) within one month of business commencement, submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the local Finanzamt via ELSTER, covering corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT data; (3) receive the statutory Steuernummer (tax number) from the Finanzamt; (4) apply through the same questionnaire for the USt-IdNr., which the Finanzamt forwards to the BZSt (typically issued within 2 to 6 weeks); (5) foreign entrepreneurs resident outside the EEA must appoint a Fiskalvertreter (fiscal representative) under § 22a UStG, while EEA-resident entrepreneurs may register directly and authenticate via ELSTER using the issued Steuernummer.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany operates no US-style check-the-box regime; a GmbH is mandatorily taxed at entity level as a corporate body (Körperschaftsteuer plus Gewerbesteuer). Available statutory elections include: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under § 14 KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG allowing VAT, corporate income tax, and trade tax consolidation between the GmbH and a German parent, requiring financial, economic, and organizational integration; (ii) the § 1a KStG option allowing qualifying partnerships, including certain EU/EEA partnerships, to elect corporate-level taxation; (iii) hybrid mismatch adjustments under the German ATAD-Umsetzungsgesetz, which are mandatory disallowance rules without elective opt-out; (iv) voluntary use of EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) and Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) procedures for intra-EU distance sales and low-value imports.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Zero registration threshold for a resident GmbH: VAT registration and compliance are mandatory from the first euro of taxable commercial activity. The § 19 UStG small-business exemption (prior-year turnover not exceeding €22,000 and current-year forecast not exceeding €50,000) is statutorily unavailable to corporations such as a GmbH. For cross-border intra-EU distance sales of goods, the OSS de-minimis threshold is €10,000 per calendar year; however, the GmbH remains a VAT-registered entrepreneur in Germany regardless of that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exports of designer blind box toys to territories outside the EU applies under § 4 Nr. 1a UStG read with § 6 UStG, requiring the physical dispatch or transport of goods to a non-EU destination and complete export proof. Intra-EU supplies of goods are zero-rated under § 4 Nr. 1b UStG read with § 6a UStG where the acquirer is an EU business holding a valid USt-IdNr., the goods are dispatched to another EU member state, and mandatory EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung) and Intrastat declarations are filed; the supplier must verify the acquirer's VAT ID and maintain transport evidence. Offshore B2B services relating to the toy business, e.g., licensing or design services to non-EU customers, fall outside the scope of German VAT under the B2B destination principle and are taxed at 0% in the customer's jurisdiction via reverse charge. Each export transaction must satisfy both Belegnachweis (documentary evidence: customs declaration, transport documents, export confirmation) and Buchnachweis (bookkeeping evidence linking goods, invoice, and payment), with the material evidence as a rule obtainable within 60 days of the supply in line with the Umsatzsteuer-Anwendungserlass (UStAE 6.4) and German court practice.

  • ATLAS electronic customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with customs export confirmation
  • Commercial invoice (Rechnung) with export VAT zero-rating notation
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR / final delivery receipt of the international carrier or postal/courier service provider
  • Packing list
  • Ausfuhrbegleitpapier or equivalent official shipping/export documentation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Zahlungseingangsbeleg) evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung) and Intrastat declaration for intra-EU dispatches

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing phased in under the Wachstumschancengesetz (Growth Opportunities Act 2024): (1) from 1 January 2025, every German entrepreneur must be technically able to receive structured e-invoices for domestic B2B transactions in formats compliant with EN 16931 (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.3, or E-EDI); (2) from 1 January 2027, e-invoice issuance becomes mandatory for suppliers whose taxable turnover exceeded €800,000 in the preceding year; (3) from 1 January 2028, e-invoice issuance becomes mandatory for all domestic B2B transactions. Germany does not yet require real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or tax-authority digital clearance for standard retail POS; however, certified electronic cash registers and receipt-issuance obligations apply under § 146a AO (Belegausgabepflicht). Cross-border B2C e-commerce obligations are administered through EU OSS/IOSS portals rather than through domestic real-time clearance.

The Finanzamt conducts targeted export-sales audits (Exportumsatzprüfungen) under § 193 AO, scrutinizing the existence and authenticity of Beleg- and Buchnachweise. Failure to produce statutory export documentation within the assessment period triggers full retrospective clawback of the zero-rate benefit: output VAT of 19% is assessed on the entire net invoice amount, plus statutory late-assessment interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum) under § 233a AO and late-payment surcharges of 1% per month capped at €25,000 under § 240 AO. Incorrect or missing EC Sales List filings expose the supplier to joint and several liability for the acquirer's unpaid VAT in the destination member state under § 6a Abs. 5 UStG. False or grossly negligent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable with fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years, or from 6 months to 10 years in particularly serious cross-border cases under § 370 Abs. 3 Nr. 3 AO, together with forfeiture of evaded amounts under § 73 StGB; lesser documentary violations attract administrative fines under § 26a UStG.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a full-convertibility regime within the euro area: both current account transactions (trade payments, royalties, service fees, dividends) and capital account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, liquidation proceeds, M&A consideration) are freely convertible without prior authorisation under Article 63 TFEU and Sections 1–4 AWG. The residual regulatory burden is statistical rather than restrictive: any cross-border receipt or payment of €12,500 or more must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments purposes under Section 67 AWV via electronic Z1/Z4 reporting or contractual delegation to the German receiving bank, and non-reporting is an administrative offence. Profit remittance is hindered only by tax-technical requirements: dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders are generally subject to German dividend withholding tax of 26.375% (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge; church tax surcharge where applicable), reducible to zero or 5% under the EU Parent–Subsidiary Directive applying German form NKv, an active-company confirmation, and proof of shareholder status; no Bundesbank or BaFin approval is required to wire dividends abroad.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation quota, or prescribed holding period for export proceeds. Exporters may freely hold euro, USD, CNY, or other foreign-currency receipts in onshore or offshore accounts. The only binding obligation is informational: inbound receipts from foreign counterparties of €12,500 or more per transaction (or aggregated related streams) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for external-economics statistics under Section 67(1) AWV within the statutory deadline, typically by the German bank on the client's behalf unless the reporting duty is contractually delegated. Supporting export documentation (customs declarations, commercial invoices, shipping manifests, Incoterms records, and bills of lading or airway bills) must be retained for at least six years under German commercial and tax law to satisfy bank AML reviews and the tax auditor's income-tax and turnover-tax compliance examination.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) acting as the national foreign-exchange statistics and cross-border payment reporting agent, together with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) as the banking and electronic-money institution supervisor, and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) as the administrative authority for the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as a secondary clearing rail, LOW as the sole or primary treasury bank. EEA-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Revolut Business, Wise Business, Currenxie, and Payoneer may operate in Germany under the PSD2 passporting regime without a separate BaFin licence and are well suited to high-frequency marketplace payouts (Shopify Payments, Stripe, or Amazon settlement funds) and low-value supplier disbursements. Legal constraints: an EMI is a payment institution and not a credit institution; it may not grant credit, issue commercial letters of credit, or provide the German statutory confirmation that the GmbH share capital is at the free disposal of management for registration purposes, which must come from a credit institution accepted by the notary and the Registergericht. German tax and customs authorities also expect a German IBAN for VAT refunds, customs duty debits, and statutory audit confirmations. Domiciling the capital escrow with an offshore EMI can create a missing-founder or beneficial-owner red flag in the GmbH notarial file. The recommended architecture uses an EMI for treasury efficiency and a tier-1 German bank as the canonical settlement account; the EMI is funded exclusively via unmistakable transfer purpose texts from the German account, with service-level agreements and fallback liquidity rules codified in the treasury policy document.

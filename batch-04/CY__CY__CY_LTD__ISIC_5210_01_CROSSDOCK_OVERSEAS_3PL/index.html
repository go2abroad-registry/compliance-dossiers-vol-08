Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Τμήμα Φορολογίας), Ministry of Finance; administrative portal: TaxisNet (https://taxisnet.mof.gov.cy); customs affairs are administered by the Cyprus Customs & Excise Department under the Customs Law 94(I)/2000.

E-government filing portal

A Cyprus private limited liability company incorporated under the Companies Law (Cap. 113) is automatically registered with the Tax Department upon incorporation; the Registrar of Companies issues a Certificate of Incorporation with an HE serial number that constitutes the company's corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN). For VAT purposes, the company must file Form VAT 101 (Application for VAT Registration) electronically via TaxisNet or at the District VAT Office; the Cyprus VAT registration number adopts the format CY+9 digits+1-2 letters (e.g., CY10259033T) and must be obtained before exceeding the registration threshold or prior to commencing taxable supplies if the threshold will be exceeded. Foreign entities establishing a Cyprus branch register the branch with the Registrar of Companies (obtaining an EE branch number) and then apply for a separate corporate tax registration if the branch is not already covered; non-established foreign companies with only indirect tax obligations in Cyprus may register directly for VAT via Form VAT 101 without a domestic entity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Cyprus-resident companies are taxed on worldwide income at the unified corporate income tax rate of 12.5% under the Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002; non-resident companies are subject to tax only on Cyprus-source income; the Cyprus limited liability company is a separate tax entity and is not an eligible pass-through vehicle; no US-style check-the-box regime applies). — A Cyprus private limited liability company incorporated under the Companies Law (Cap. 113) is automatically registered with the Tax Department upon incorporation; the Registrar of Companies issues a Certificate of Incorporation with an HE serial number that constitutes the company's corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN). For VAT purposes, the company must file Form VAT 101 (Application for VAT Registration) electronically via TaxisNet or at the District VAT Office; the Cyprus VAT registration number adopts the format CY+9 digits+1-2 letters (e.g., CY10259033T) and must be obtained before exceeding the registration threshold or prior to commencing taxable supplies if the threshold will be exceeded. Foreign entities establishing a Cyprus branch register the branch with the Registrar of Companies (obtaining an EE branch number) and then apply for a separate corporate tax registration if the branch is not already covered; non-established foreign companies with only indirect tax obligations in Cyprus may register directly for VAT via Form VAT 101 without a domestic entity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Cyprus-resident companies are taxed on worldwide income at the unified corporate income tax rate of 12.5% under the Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002; non-resident companies are subject to tax only on Cyprus-source income; the Cyprus limited liability company is a separate tax entity and is not an eligible pass-through vehicle; no US-style check-the-box regime applies).

Available election forms

Cyprus does not maintain a US-style check-the-box classification system; however, the following express elections and regimes are available under Cyprus statute: (i) Group Relief election (Income Tax Law Art. 14) permitting intra-group loss transferring; (ii) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on new equity introductions; (iii) IP Box regime election for qualifying intangible assets (5% effective net profit rate); (iv) Tonnage Tax election for qualifying shipping operations; (v) VAT grouping election under the VAT Law (Law 94(I)/2000, Art. 11) allowing multiple Cyprus entities to be treated as a single taxable person; (vi) election for the special reduced VAT margin scheme for second-hand goods, works of art, collectors' items and antiques; (vii) OSS/IOSS elections for cross-border distance sales, e-commerce and imports of small-value goods; (viii) election to apply the EU home-country simplification for foreign branch profits; and (ix) ATAD-driven hybrid mismatch adjustments must be disclosed in the statutory audit file pursuant to the Qualifying/Restricted jurisdiction rules, with corresponding anti-abuse election disclosures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€15,600 annual taxable turnover (law 94(I)/2000, Art. 29) for compulsory VAT registration by a taxable person established in Cyprus; the threshold is assessed on cumulative taxable supplies of goods and services during the preceding 12 months; for intra-Community distance selling and OSS-covered cross-border e-commerce, the historical local distance threshold of €35,000 has been superseded by the EU One-Stop-Shop mechanism, eliminating any local threshold for OSS-registered non-established sellers; voluntary registration is permitted for taxable persons below the threshold; non-established EU sellers making distance sales into Cyprus are generally handled via OSS without mandatory local registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) applies to: (a) exports of goods dispatched or transported by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf to a destination outside the European Union, including transfers of inventory to overseas e-commerce fulfillment centers and third-party 3PL warehouses in non-EU jurisdictions, provided the goods physically leave EU customs territory within the statutory timeframe (generally 90 days of supply, with extension permitted under specific customs procedures); (b) services directly related to the export of goods, including freight transport, loading, unloading, handling, warehousing and ancillary services when the services are performed for the direct needs of the exported goods and the goods depart the EU; (c) international transport services and related intermediaries to the extent assignable to non-EU destinations; and (d) B2B services supplied to non-established customers under the general place-of-supply rule (reverse charge in the customer's Member State) are treated as outside the scope of Cyprus VAT. The exporter must hold satisfactory documentary evidence of export, including customs clearance, transport documentation, commercial terms, and proof of payment from the overseas warehouse operator/customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration – Cyprus Customs & Excise EU-wide Automated Export System (AES) / Single Administrative Document (SAD) EX-A form, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and export acceptance stamp
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight), Air Waybill (air freight), CMR consignment note (road) or CIM railway consignment note, evidencing the goods were transported to a destination outside the EU
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list identifying the Cyprus exporter, foreign consignee, Incoterms, HS codes, quantities and unit values
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / bank transfer statement / other verifiable payment evidence from the overseas customer or 3PL warehouse operator
  • Cross-dock and warehouse movement evidence, including receiving notes, dispatch records, container tracking, insurance certificates, and overseas warehouse inventory reports confirming arrival of goods at the foreign fulfillment centre
  • Freight forwarding contracts and any third-party transport instructions demonstrating direct export from Cyprus to the non-EU destination

No mandatory real-time transactional fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime exists in Cyprus (unlike Latin American continuous transaction control models). Electronic invoicing is permitted and legally recognised under the EDI/e-invoicing rules of the VAT Law; B2B and B2C e-invoicing remain voluntary, with PEPPOL-BIS 3.0 and EN-16931 formats recommended for interoperability. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies for supplies to Cypriot public-sector contracting authorities under the EU Directive 2014/55/EU transposition, requiring PEPPOL access points. Taxpayers must retain books and records for six years, submit VAT returns (Form VAT 101) and EC Sales Lists (Form VAT 102) electronically via TaxisNet, and file Intrastat declarations where intra-EU trade thresholds are exceeded.

The Cyprus VAT Commissioner may retrospectively claw back any wrongly claimed zero-rated export with statutory interest and reinstatement of VAT at the standard rate. Administrative penalties under the VAT Law include: fixed penalty of €100 plus €50 per month (or part) for late filing; misdeclaration penalties of up to 20% of the net tax liability for negligent inaccuracies; criminal sanctions for fraudulent export claims, including imprisonment up to five years and/or fines up to €250,000. Taxpayers are subject to risk-based audit profiling; the Tax Department and Customs jointly cross-check export declarations, movement reference numbers, and commercial documentation; refunds may be suspended pending substantiation. The general limitation period for VAT assessment is four years from the end of the relevant tax period, extended to ten years in cases of fraud or omission. Note: Cyprus law does not fix a strict statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark (as structured in this dossier) reflects OECD transfer-pricing documentation practice and Cypriot banking compliance expectations for trade proceeds; where export proceeds are not received within 180 days, the exporter must document commercial late-payment terms and, if applicable, apply transfer-pricing adjustments to avoid prima facie reclassification as a non-arm's length arrangement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The domestic currency is the euro, a fully convertible currency. Cyprus maintains full current-account and capital-account liberalisation under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Foreign exchange controls, exchange-rate approval mechanisms, and profit-repatriation licensing requirements have been abolished. Dividend distributions and other profit remittances from a Cyprus private limited company are not subject to exchange-control approval or withholding tax under domestic law, subject to EU anti-abuse rules, substance requirements, and sanctions/AML screening. The Central Bank of Cyprus requires statistical reporting for cross-border payments and balance-of-payments purposes, typically through the reporting bank or directly by the company when thresholds are triggered, but this is a reporting obligation rather than a restriction on convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender requirement, no statutory conversion timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds in the Cypriot legal framework. Export receipts may be retained in the original foreign currency in multi-currency accounts or converted to euro at the company's discretion and commercial risk. Cross-border proceeds above the Central Bank of Cyprus statistical reporting threshold must be reported under the EU/ECB balance-of-payments reporting framework, but no prior approval or reconversion obligation is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus, acting as the national competent authority within the European System of Central Banks and the Single Supervisory Mechanism, with supplementary supervisory authority exercised by the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission for investment firms and FX/EMI payment institutions; the Central Bank of Cyprus also operates as the national AML/CFT supervisor for credit institutions and certain payment/e-money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as a supplementary treasury rail, but Medium as the primary banking infrastructure. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions that have valid passporting into Cyprus under the Payment Services Law and the Electronic Money Law may open settlement accounts, issue virtual IBANs, and process marketplace payouts. Legal constraints include the prohibition on deposit-taking, mandatory safeguarding of client funds, and the inability to provide credit, escrow custodial functions, or fully protected bank-account services in the legal sense. For the cross-border e-commerce 3PL model, a reputable EEA EMI such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, or Payoneer may be used for low-value high-frequency trade disbursements, with settlement valuations routed to a Tier 1 Cypriot bank account for final treasury concentration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires a SWIFT MT103 or an EEA SEPA / SEPA Instant credit transfer from the parent or investor into the Cyprus company's IBAN account, with the remitter name exactly matching the investor agreement and the beneficiary being the Cyprus private limited company. The payment message must carry ISO 20022 purpose coding for capital or direct investment, preferably the ECB balance-of-payments functional category referring to direct investment in the reporting economy through equity capital, and a payment narrative stating 'Capital contribution — CY Ltd'. The onboarding bank will request a supporting pack containing the board/shareholder resolution approving the capital increase, the updated GCI showing the issued capital, the share subscription agreement, proof of the parent company's corporate existence, and source-of-funds evidence. Where the capital injection is routed via an intermediary or fintech EMI, the bank must still observe the Economic Substance and Reporting obligations by requiring the underlying economic beneficiary details; any payment message containing references to non-EU shell structures or unverifiable offshore intermediaries may be delayed under CBC AML review.Use SWIFT MT103 for all non-EEA supplier and foreign warehouse payments because MT103 provides a detailed audit trail, confirmation of value dates, and compliance with the correspondent banking chain. For inventory financing or large batch procurement, a commercial letter of credit, preferably irrevocable and confirmed where the beneficiary is in a higher-risk jurisdiction, should be issued by a Tier 1 Cypriot bank under ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits; sight LCs are suitable for cross-dock stock purchases and acceptance LCs may be used where deferred payment terms align with the 3PL cash conversion cycle. For EEA-region operational disbursements, SEPA Credit Transfer is the most efficient rail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 aggregate value of controlled transactions with any single related party in a tax year. Master File and Country-by-Country Report apply where the multinational group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus tax law requires central management and control to be exercised in Cyprus for tax residence. A Cyprus Ltd engaged in crossdock/overnight 3PL must demonstrate physical office, local bookkeeping, decision-making by Cypriot-resident directors and substantive logistics activities. The Cyprus Tax Department may deny residence status or approve no substantial substance protection under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) and DTA abuse rules. Pure administrative shell companies with no staff or premises are exposed to disallowance of Cyprus tax residency and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Artificial Loss or Unfiled Return

Statutory sanction: Under Cyprus Income Tax Law L.118(I)/2002, the Commissioner may assess tax on best of judgment, impose a 10% surcharge, daily interest at 9% per annum, and penalties up to EUR 20,000 or imprisonment for up to 3 years for deliberate evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly filed false returns may be personally jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax; travel bans, prohibition from acting as directors, and custodial sentences apply.
Shell Company Abuse and Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Cyprus Tax Department may treat the company as non-resident, retroactively deny company residence, disallow DTA benefits, recalculate assessed Cyprus taxes with a 10% non-payment surcharge and levy penalties up to 20% of under-declared profits.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and beneficial owners risk personal civil liability for unpaid tax if corporate veil is lifted for shareholder benefit; criminal AML sanctions apply if the shell is used to disguise true ownership.
Transfer Pricing Mis-Pricing

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Tax Department can make an arm's length adjustment, add 10% surcharge and impose a penalty of 20% on additional tax assessed for intentional mispricing; non-criminal adjustments under the TP Guidelines accrue interest at the statutory rate.

Director personal liability: Directors accountable for approving the TP policy may be assessed for negligent misstatement; if they knowingly signed tax returns relying on fabricated CUP data, they may be prosecuted criminally.
AML and False Declarations in Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Violation of Cyprus AML Law L.188(I)/2007 and Prevention and Suppression of Proceeds of Corruption Law: fines up to EUR 200,000 and custodial penalties for senior officers; bank may freeze the corporate accounts under Anti-Money Laundering Reporting Officer (AMLO) action.

Director personal liability: Directors may be criminally liable for money laundering if they intentionally supervised payment concealment; civil restitution to the company and disqualification orders are standard; travel ban can be issued pending criminal investigation.

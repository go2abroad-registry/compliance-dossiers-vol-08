Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus, Ministry of Finance, Lefkosia; online services portal: 'Ariadne' Tax Portal (https://taxportal.mof.gov.cy/). VAT administration is exercised through the VAT Service of the Tax Department.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company automatically receives a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) upon incorporation by the Registrar of Companies and the Tax Department. For VAT, the Cyprus company must file Form VAT 101 (Application for Registration or Voluntary Registration) with the VAT District Office. A non-resident company or foreign entity not established in Cyprus but performing taxable supplies in Cyprus may be required to appoint a tax representative and register directly with the Tax Department using a foreign identification document plus power of attorney. The Tax Department issues a Cyprus VAT registration number (9-digit format preceded by 'CY') evidenced by a Certificate of Registration and Accrual of Tax Identification Number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Cyprus private limited company automatically receives a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) upon incorporation by the Registrar of Companies and the Tax Department. For VAT, the Cyprus company must file Form VAT 101 (Application for Registration or Voluntary Registration) with the VAT District Office. A non-resident company or foreign entity not established in Cyprus but performing taxable supplies in Cyprus may be required to appoint a tax representative and register directly with the Tax Department using a foreign identification document plus power of attorney. The Tax Department issues a Cyprus VAT registration number (9-digit format preceded by 'CY') evidenced by a Certificate of Registration and Accrual of Tax Identification Number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style entity classification election exists in Cyprus. A Cyprus captive reinsurance SPV is taxable at entity level under the Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002). Statutory elections available include: (a) election under the intellectual property box regime (Law 118(I)/2002 Art. 7B, defined intangible assets relief); (b) group relief election for losses of group companies (Art. 24); (c) election for Cyprus dividend participation exemption protection; (d) tonnage tax election (Shipping Law) not applicable to reinsurance; (e) CFC anti-hybrid rules under Income Tax Law Art. 61 require no election where mismatch arrangements erase tax presence. Hybrid mismatch elections under EU ATAD transposition are not elective and are disallowed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 for taxable supplies of goods and services by established persons in Cyprus over the preceding 12 months; EUR 15,600 for distance sales of goods from other EU member states to recipients in Cyprus; EUR 35,000 for intra-EU acquisitions by a non-taxable legal person. Captive reinsurance services are exempt supplies and generally do not count toward the VAT registration threshold unless the SPV makes other taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cyprus VAT law zero-rates the supply of goods exported by the supplier or on behalf of the supplier to destinations outside the European Union, subject to proof of removal within specified time limits. Insurance and reinsurance services, including captive reinsurance arrangements, are exempt from VAT under Article 135(1)(a) of the EU VAT Directive implemented in Cyprus; such exempt supplies are not zero-rated and do not carry input VAT credit. For business-to-business services supplied to a customer established outside the EU, the place of supply is where the customer belongs, making the service outside the scope of Cyprus VAT, but for insurance and reinsurance the exemption applies per se. Refund substantiation for EU VAT incurred by non-EU established insurers is processed under the 13th Directive Refund framework through the Cyprus VAT Service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR carrier documentation
  • Proof of export movement outside the EU (port or transit documents)
  • Commercial Invoice and contract of sale
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or other bank documentary evidence of payment
  • Insurance or reinsurance policy documentation (where the export zero-rating or VAT exemption claim concerns supply of services)

Cyprus has no mandatory general real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance system for B2B or B2C invoices. The Tax Department does not require API-based e-invoice clearance or digital stamping of invoices. Mandatory electronic reporting applies only for public procurement invoices through electronic submission; the European standard EN 16931 is accepted. VAT returns are filed electronically through the Ariadne portal; all fiscal records must be kept digitally for 6 years and reproduced on demand. The Tax Department may require export invoices to bear the VAT ID of the customer and full documentation evidence of dispatch or transport.

The Cyprus Tax Department and Customs Authorities scrutinize zero-rating claims through documentary verification. Fraudulent export VAT zero-rating claims attract penalties under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law (Law 4/1978) and VAT Law (Law 119(I)/2001): an additional tax up to 100% of the amount under-assessed, monetary penalties up to EUR 500 per document, criminal fines, and imprisonment up to five years for knowingly delivering incorrect VAT returns. Retrospective clawbacks apply with interest computed from the original return due date, and in serious cases the Tax Department may revoke the VAT registration and publish assessment notices. Customs offence penalties may apply under the Customs Code Law and include confiscation of goods and payment of duties plus penalties. For captive reinsurance structures lacking economic substance, the tax authority may recharacterize arrangements, deny exemption or zero-rating, and impose penalty taxes plus interest under anti-abuse rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus forms an integral part of the European Economic Area and fully liberalizes capital movements under Articles 63-65 TFEU. Both current account convertibility and capital account convertibility are unrestricted, subject only to public-policy carve-outs, EU sanctions enforcement, and national security filters. Profit remittance is free of exchange control approval, but outbound payments must be economically substantiated and tax-compliant under anti-abuse rules. CIT withholding at 0% on dividends for EU-resident parent companies under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive applies; a 30% rate may apply to non-EU outward dividends absent treaty relief.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Cyprus does not impose surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirements on export proceeds or foreign-currency receipts. Receipts may be retained in freely convertible EUR or other major currencies. The only obligations are bookkeeping and reporting under the International Business Companies legal framework, Anti-Money Laundering (AML) law L. 188(I)/2007 as amended, and the CBC Directive on Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou) as the competent national macro-prudential authority and Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC) as co-regulator for cross-border banking investment services

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Cyprus does not recognize foreign electronic money institution (EMI) licenses as licenses to operate locally, but licensed EMIs from other EU states may passport payment and e-money services into Cyprus under the Revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and E-Money Directive 2. Restricted to payment accounts; cannot hold documentary-of-title escrow for long-term captive underwriting reserves equivalent to full-scope credit institution status. Captive reinsurance statutory reserves must be placed with credit institutions or investment-grade securities; reserve deposits held in an unrated EMI payment account may breach solvency-equivalence requirements of the Cyprus Insurance Companies Law L. 38(I)/2016.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Captive SPV with low local substance and concentrated third-country parentage

Root cause: Captive SPV with low local substance and concentrated third-country parentage

Defense evidence: If the Central Bank of Cyprus considers the captive opaque, provide group consolidated financial statements, actuarial loss-reserve reports, board minutes detailing strategic risk assumption, Cyprus registered office lease, employment contracts of local director and compliance officer, and tax residency certificates for the parent and ultimate beneficial owners.
Use of offshore or low-tax intermediate holding jurisdictions in the premium-flow chain

Root cause: Use of offshore or low-tax intermediate holding jurisdictions in the premium-flow chain

Defense evidence: Prepare organizational chart with economic rationale for every intermediary, transfer-pricing documentation under OECD BEPS Action 13 (country-by-country report, master file, local file), intercompany reinsurance agreement with actuarial premium methodology, evidence of real underwriting claims management in Cyprus, and letters from foreign regulators confirming licensing status.
Large initial premium injection from a parent in an exchange-controlled jurisdiction

Root cause: Large initial premium injection from a parent in an exchange-controlled jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Produce the parent jurisdictional exchange-control approval (e.g., Central Bank authorization for outward investment), invoice or reinsurance contract clearly matching the premium amount, source-of-funds affidavits from independent accountants, bank statements for the funding account, and a written opinion of local counsel verifying that the inbound euro notional is free of any unencumbered FX escrow.
Sudden capital reduction or redemption of paid-up share capital before first claim cycle

Root cause: Sudden capital reduction or redemption of paid-up share capital before first claim cycle

Defense evidence: Provide board-approved statement of solvency, actuarial certificate from a qualified fellow of a recognized actuarial body confirming reserved liabilities, notice to the Cyprus Insurance Companies Authority of the capital reduction application, minutes of shareholders' meeting, audited balance sheet, and confirmation that solvency capital requirement and minimum capital requirement remain above statutory thresholds after the proposed reduction.
Cross-border outbound payments to a non-OECD claims beneficiary desk without prior wire rationale

Root cause: Cross-border outbound payments to a non-OECD claims beneficiary desk without prior wire rationale

Defense evidence: Provide the underlying loss documents (loss notice, independent loss-adjuster report), claims settlement agreement, payment schedule showing counterparty bank details, tax withholding calculation under applicable double-tax treaty, and proof that the reinsurance SPV's registry identifies the beneficiary as an approved retrocessionaire or insured affiliate.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is a SEPA or SWIFT MT103 credit from the parent company to the captive's Cyprus account using purpose code 'Capital contribution to wholly-owned captive reinsurance subsidiary – freedom of establishment under Art 49/63 TFEU'. Crucially, the payment must be prefaced by a board resolution approving new equity injection, registration of the contribution in the Cyprus Registrar of Companies records, valuation memorandum if contribution is in kind, and notification to Cyprus Tax Department if the contribution is a share premium. For intercompany winshall be designated as 'reinsurance premium paid abroad' and supported by the executed outward reinsurance treaty and brokerage account statement. To prevent gross-up disputes, the sending bank requires an EEA VAT invoice or a formal settlement statement from the reinsurance broker. For claims settlements, use MT103 with purpose code TRADE (payment for trade), and attach the claims settlement certificate for the specific insured event. All payments must be initiated within the 5-working-day SEPA execution window; non-EEA payments operate on 2-3 business day cut-offs. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on outbound dividends paid to non-residents; Cyprus resident shareholders may be subject to the 17% Special Defence Contribution (SDC) on dividend income when the shareholder is a Cyprus tax resident (not applicable to non-resident treaty beneficiaries).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by 30 June immediately following the accounting period end (i.e., within six months of the fiscal year-end); for a calendar-year SPV, the deadline is 30 June of the following year.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 (Trading Income) Gross captive reinsurance income, net premiums, commission rebates and underwriting-related fees. Non-resident SPVs report only income attributable to a Cyprus permanent establishment; treaty-only filers must attach Form PE/Treaty claims and avoid duplicating home-jurisdiction premium reporting. Late submission of Form IR4 is subject to a EUR 100 administrative penalty plus accrued interest at 9% per annum; understating taxable income by more than 10% triggers a 10% understatement penalty and an additional 5% surcharge for late payment after 30 June, rising to 10% for non-payment after six months. Persistent delinquency can result in composite tax assessments and enforced collection measures.
Box 2 (Investment Income) Interest on reinsurance reserve deposits, investment returns, and other passive income. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 in aggregate controlled transactions triggers mandatory Local File preparation for a Cyprus taxpayer; the Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750,000,000, or where the Cyprus Tax Department has issued a specific documentation demand.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has no separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) akin to OECD-pressured low-tax jurisdictions; however, treaty residency, the NID, participation exemption and captive reinsurance licensing require genuine management and control to be exercised in Cyprus. The OECD BEPS Actions (notably Action 6 and the PPT), the Cypriot CFC rules, and the GAAR recharacterise sham SPVs lacking local board direction, actuarial oversight and real risk absorption. A captive reinsurance SPV must maintain its strategic risk function, underwriting records, claims adjudication, capital structure and board meetings in Cyprus.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Underdeclaration

Statutory sanction: The Cyprus Income Tax Law imposes a 10% understatement penalty on the additional tax assessed, plus annual interest at 9%; fraudulent returns attract fines up to EUR 10,000 and imprisonment of up to three years. The additional tax due remains collectible and may be compounded by creditor claims.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are personally liable for restitution to the tax authority, risk disqualification under the Cyprus Companies Law (Cap. 113), and may face criminal sanctions including imprisonment for up to three years and travel bans pending investigation.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Cyprus will deny tax residence or treaty benefits, reallocate profits to the parent company under transfer pricing or CFC rules, and may issue a deemed-dividend distribution. The SPV may also face compulsory strike-off under the Companies law and revocation of its insurance/reinsurance licence.

Director personal liability: Directors may have a civil remedy sought by regulators and creditors for breach of fiduciary duty; courts can pierce the corporate veil, impose travel bans, and hold directors jointly liable for unpaid corporate tax in cases of abuse of limited liability.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Disguised Distribution

Statutory sanction: Cyprus tax authorities reassess the transaction at arm's length, apply an additional 10% understatement penalty, and charge 9% interest; transaction amounts may be recharacterised as dividends subject to the SDC/treaty rules and additional fines up to EUR 5,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve sham pricing face personal tax collection orders, civil restitution to the tax authority, disqualification proceedings, and in egregious cases criminal liability for false accounting and money laundering.
AML / Tax Fraud / Undisclosed Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Under the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (L.188(I)/2007), fines for criminal conduct can reach EUR 500,000; imprisonment can extend to 14 years. Regulatory sanctions include cancellation of the reinsurance licence, freezing orders, and confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are personally exposed to criminal fines, imprisonment, confiscation orders, travel bans, and disqualification; civil liability tracks all assets improperly funnelled through the SPV, including reversal of transactions and reimbursement to the corporate estate.

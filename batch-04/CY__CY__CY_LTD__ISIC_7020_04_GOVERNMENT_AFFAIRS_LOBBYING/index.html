Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Department of Registrar of Companies and Official Receiver (RCOR), Ministry of Energy, Commerce and Industry, Nicosia, Cyprus (e-Registry online filing portal).

Governing statute

The Companies Law, Cap. 113 (Cyprus), as amended — Section 3 (Formation of Incorporated Company), Section 4 (Application for Registration), Section 5 (Certificate of Incorporation), Section 113 (Statutory Registers) and Section 119 (Annual Returns); applied with the Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Law (L. 188(I)/2007) for beneficial ownership filings.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of a Private Company (Form HE30(1)) filed together with the Memorandum and Articles of Association containing a Statement of Capital and the Declaration of Compliance (Form HE31).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 53 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Cyprus private limited company; incorporation is lawful with zero issued capital. The Statement of Capital must declare the total number of shares and the amounts paid and unpaid on each share. No escrow account or capital deposit is required by the Registrar; common practice is 1,000 ordinary shares of EUR 1.00 each with at least one share issued to the subscriber.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the Cyprus Fire Service under the Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting Laws, following verification of means of escape, exit signage, portable extinguishers, and annual maintenance records; structural load and electrical installation clearance certified by the District Surveyor upon lease registration; occupational workplace clearance under the Safety and Health at Work Law, L. 89(I)/1996, requiring a written risk assessment, first-aid equipment, and appointment of a qualified safety officer where more than five employees are engaged.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Cyprus private limited company; incorporation is lawful with zero issued capital. The Statement of Capital must declare the total number of shares and the amounts paid and unpaid on each share. No escrow account or capital deposit is required by the Registrar; common practice is 1,000 ordinary shares of EUR 1.00 each with at least one share issued to the subscriber.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Department of the Republic of Cyprus (Ministry of Finance), Nicosia; official portal: 'Ariadne' Tax Portal (taxportal.mof.gov.cy / tax.mof.gov.cy). VAT registration is administered by the VAT Service of the Tax Department. Customs matters are administered by the Cyprus Customs & Excise Department (Ministry of Finance) through the EU THESEAS/Export Control System.

E-government filing portal

(2) File the company's incorporation details with the Tax Department via the 'Ariadne' online portal to receive the Cyprus Tax Identification Number (TIN/TIC). (4) Obtain a Customs EORI number if intra-EU/export transactions are anticipated. (5) Register as an employer for Social Insurance contributions. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Cyprus-resident Private Limited Company is a separate taxable person subject to Cyprus corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide profits, with specific territorial exemptions (e.g., dividend income and gains on disposal of shares). Non-resident companies are taxed only on Cyprus-source income. No pass-through treatment is available for Cypriot limited companies. The company is considered Cyprus tax resident only when management and control are exercised in Cyprus; this status is evidenced by a Cyprus Tax Residence Certificate (Form T.D. 1). The business scope (cross-border trade policy analysis and government affairs lobbying) constitutes taxable consultancy services under NACE/ISIC 70.20 and is subject to entity-level taxation and the standard Cyprus VAT framework.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box system; classification is statutory. Available elections include: (i) the IP Box Regime election (Article 9A, Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002) applying an 80% exemption to qualifying intangible assets, giving an effective 2.5% corporate rate; (ii) the Notional Interest Deduction election (Article 9B) on new equity capital; (iii) Group Relief election (Article 36) between Cyprus-resident group companies; (iv) election to obtain a Cyprus Tax Residence Certificate for treaty/beneficial-ownership claims; (v) the option to tax supplies of immovable property under Article 95 of the VAT Law; (vi) the election to use the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for distance sales and certain B2C services; and (vii) elections concerning hybrid mismatch alignments under the EU ATAD implementation, subject to Tax Department guidance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 annual taxable turnover for taxable persons established in Cyprus (mandatory VAT registration); no registration threshold for non-established foreign entities (registration mandatory before first taxable supply in Cyprus); intra-EU distance sales thresholds and OSS scheme cross-border threshold of EUR 10,000 apply. For this cross-border advisory business, if the place of supply of consultancy/lobbying services is in Cyprus, the EUR 15,600 threshold applies to Cyprus-established suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under Article 48 of the VAT Law when physically exported from Cyprus/EU territory to a third country, or when supplied intra-Community to a customer with a valid EU VAT ID (provided the EC Sales List is filed and transport evidence retained). Services (this business): cross-border trade policy analysis and lobbying services supplied to a business customer established outside the EU are zero-rated where the place of supply is outside Cyprus (effective use and enjoyment outside the EU). Comparable services supplied to EU-established business customers are outside the scope of Cypriot VAT and subject to reverse charge in the customer's Member State. Zero-rating requires documented evidence of the recipient's non-EU establishment, the true commercial substance of the offshore supply, and absence of any Hungarian/Cypriot permanent establishment involved.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document - SAD) with MRN exit confirmation from the Cyprus Customs/THESEAS Export Control System
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill, or CMR/T-1 transit document evidencing physical departure of goods from Cyprus/EU territory
  • Signed commercial contract and VAT-compliant commercial invoice specifically describing the lobbying/trade-policy advisory services and identifying the non-EU recipient
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank statement evidencing receipt of the export consideration in foreign currency from the non-resident client

Cyprus does not yet impose a fully mandatory real-time B2B e-invoicing clearance regime. The current requirements are: (i) B2G (public sector) invoices must be issued and transmitted electronically via the Ariadne government e-invoicing / e-supplier portal; (ii) certified fiscal cash-register/POS devices transmit summary sales data to the Tax Department in real time for specified retail activities; (iii) intra-Community B2B transactions must be reported through EC Sales Lists and Intrastat declarations; and (iv) electronic archiving of invoices is required in original form for audit purposes. Cyprus is aligning with the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package and is preparing legislation to mandate structured e-invoicing with near-real-time digital reporting through application-to-application (API) interfaces by 2030. Foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Cyprus should maintain API-ready accounting systems and XML/UBL e-invoice capabilities.

The Tax Department may reassess and claw back any zero-rated export treatment where the mandatory documentation bundle is incomplete, where the services are found to be effectively used in Cyprus, or where export proceeds are not received and repatriated within approximately 180 days of invoicing; such clawback assessments charge output VAT at 19% plus statutory late-payment interest at 9% per annum and administrative penalties. In cases of fraudulent or abusive export zero-rating (including sham offshore recipients, circular transactions, overvalued invoices, or fabricated customs evidence), the Tax Department may impose civil penalties of up to double the tax evaded, deny input VAT credits, initiate criminal prosecution of directors under the VAT Law 94(I)/2000, apply a 10-year limitation period in fraud cases, and withdraw the company's VAT registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Cyprus is a Eurozone member with full liberalisation of capital and current payments under the EU Treaty and the CBC-administered Exchange Control Law (as amended). No exchange controls are imposed on cross-border investments, trade settlements, dividend remittances, or loan repayments in any convertible currency. Euro is freely convertible; Cyprus private limited companies may open and maintain foreign-currency accounts without CBC approval. Profit repatriation is permitted after satisfaction of the Companies Law distribution test (balance sheet solvency) and applicable tax withholding, with no FX purchase requirement. The CBC retains only statistical reporting, EU sanctions, and AML-related monitoring powers, which may delay but do not restrict convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no mandatory export proceeds surrender ratio and no statutory conversion timeline. Exporters may retain receipts indefinitely in any convertible currency without prior CBC authorisation. For tax and accounting purposes, export revenues must be translated into EUR at the Central Bank of Cyprus reference rate on the transaction date or at the period-end under the applicable financial reporting framework; no forced conversion is triggered. The CBC requires electronic balance-of-payments reporting of export proceeds through the CY-BOP system within 8 business days after the month-end for annual export values exceeding EUR 350,000, but the requirement is informational only.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Kentriki Trapeza tis Kyprou)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for non-core treasury operations, Low as the sole banking relationship. Cyprus-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) under PSD2 can provide payment accounts and remittance services; EEA-passported EMIs may operate lawfully in Cyprus under freedom of services. However, offshore (non-EEA) EMIs are not permitted to accept deposits or render payment services to Cypriot resident entities without a Cyprus/EEA licence. Moreover, EMI balances are not covered by the Deposit Protection Scheme; they are safeguarded in segregated client accounts, which introduces concentration risk. The company may use an EMI for fast cross-border disbursement of retainer fees, but wholesale liquidity clearing and capital escrow must reside in a CBC-licensed credit institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard corporate income tax under Article 19 of the Income Tax Law (Law 118(I)/2002).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Cyprus does not impose withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders under domestic law.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return must be submitted within 15 months after the end of the tax year; for a 31 December year-end, the filing deadline is 31 March of the second following year. Provisional tax payments are due in two instalments on 31 July and 31 December of the tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR. Local File is required when related-party transactions exceed the following annual thresholds: goods €2,000,000; services €500,000; intangible property €250,000; financial transactions €250,000. Master File is required where the group's consolidated revenue exceeds €750,000,000. Related-party transactions must be disclosed in the TD4 annual filing and documented under the Income Tax (Transfer Pricing) Rules of 2022.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus does not have a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Law; it applies the management-and-control test for tax residence under Article 2 of the Income Tax Law, supported by BEPS/MLI and EU Code of Conduct Group principles. A government-affairs and lobbying Cyprus LTD must establish genuine human and physical substance: at least two Cyprus-resident directors where appropriate, substantive board meetings held in Cyprus, a registered office, locally negotiated and signed commercial contracts, local employees and service providers, and custody of all books and records in Cyprus. Inadequate substance may cause tax-residence reclassification and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Assessment of underpaid 12.5% tax, interest at 2% per annum, 5% late-payment surcharge, and criminal fines; prosecution may result in imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are personally and jointly liable for evaded tax, subject to civil restitution, travel bans, disqualification orders, and custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of tax residence to the offshore jurisdiction, cancellation of treaty relief, reinstatement of worldwide income at 12.5% Cyprus CIT, disallowance of deductions, and imposition of interest and penalties; exchange of information under DAC6/CRS may trigger cross-border reassessments.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil liability for wrongfully obtained tax benefits, administrative fines, and, in aggravated cases, disqualification and criminal prosecution for fraudulent misrepresentation.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Failure

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under the Cyprus Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law (Law 188(I)/2007 as amended), including administrative fines scaled to turnover, license revocation, asset freezing and confiscation orders, and criminal liability for money laundering carrying up to 15 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers of the obliged entity can be prosecuted personally, subjected to travel bans, disqualified from management, and held liable for monetary penalties and confiscated proceeds.
Foreign Bribery and Corruption

Statutory sanction: Punishable under the Cyprus Criminal Code (Corruption of Public Persons) and Cyprus ratification of the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention; penalties include imprisonment up to ten years, unlimited fines, exclusion from public procurement and EU funding, and forfeiture of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Under the Corporate Criminal Liability Law (Law 151(I)/2003), directors and senior officers are criminally liable for acts they knowingly or negligently authorise; they face imprisonment, disqualification, civil damages claims and travel restrictions.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office (VAT ID, USt-IdNr. under §27a UStG); local Finanzamt (responsible tax office) for tax registration, Steuernummer (tax number), Corporate Income Tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and Trade Tax (Gewerbesteuer); ELSTER (www.elster.de) as the national electronic tax filing portal; Generalzolldirektion / Zoll.de for customs matters incl. import VAT and export clearances through ATLAS.

E-government filing portal

For the target German-resident GmbH: (1) Register the GmbH with the Handelsregister and obtain the commercial register excerpt; (2) Submit the official tax registration questionnaire 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' to the competent local Finanzamt within one month of incorporation or commencement of business; (3) Receive the national Steuernummer (tax registration number) and Wirtschafts-ID (economic identification number) used for all corporate tax filings; (4) Apply separately to BZSt for the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under §27a UStG — application is made electronically or by postal form, supported by commercial register excerpt and shareholder/de facto manager authorisation documentation; (5) Use the ELSTER portal for electronic submission of VAT returns and corporate tax statements. For a foreign / non-resident entity without a German permanent establishment obtaining a German VAT ID: submit the same Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung to the competent Finanzamt via a German fiscal representative, evidence of foreign commercial registration and proof of the taxable supply triggering nexus; BZSt issues the VAT ID after EU VIES/qualitative verification. No physical presence is required where a German fiscal agent is appointed. Importers use the customs EORI number and pay import VAT via ATLAS customs declaration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists under German domestic tax law. GmbH is a taxable corporate entity subject to Körperschaftsteuer (15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate tax), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, roughly 14–17% depending on municipality). Statutory electives relevant to a GmbH: (i) small-business exemption under UStG §19 (elective VAT exemption for gross turnover ≤ EUR 22,000 prior year and ≤ EUR 50,000 current year) — generally unsuitable for an export-oriented UHT plant; (ii) VAT grouping (Organschaft) under UStG §18e, allowing fiscal unity between a German parent and the GmbH; (iii) trade-tax extended deduction ('erweiterte Kürzung') for real estate companies; (iv) hybrid mismatch disclosure obligations under DAC6 / §4k EStG for cross-border arrangements; (v) transparent or semi-transparent partnership structures via GmbH & Co. KG for non-tax reasons. German tax classification of the GmbH itself is mandatory and non-electable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a resident GmbH there is no general VAT registration threshold; registration is mandatory for every entrepreneur performing taxable supplies unless the UStG §19 small-business exemption applies (gross turnover ≤ EUR 22,000 in the preceding calendar year and ≤ EUR 50,000 in the current calendar year). Non-resident entrepreneurs supplying goods or services in Germany must register immediately without any de minimis threshold. Imported goods are always subject to import VAT (Einfuhrumsatzsteuer) regardless of value. For cross-border B2C distance sales into other EU Member States, the EU-wide OSS registration threshold is EUR 10,000 of annual distance sales; Germany also applies the reverse charge for B2B supplies. FOB/CIF customs valuation disclosures are made through the ATLAS import procedure.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies (Ausfuhrlieferungen) are zero-rated under UStG §4(1)(No.1) and §6 where: (i) the supplier or a commissioned agent transports or dispatches the goods from Germany to a destination outside the EU (non-Union third country); (ii) the supplier is the economic owner of the goods at the time of supply; (iii) the movement to a third country is documented by reliable transport and customs evidence (Ausfuhrnachweis) — normally including a customs export declaration released by German Customs via ATLAS and a transport document evidencing physical departure from EU customs territory; (iv) the export occurs within 22 days for shipped goods or 80 working days for dispatched goods (per statutory grace periods). Export-related services such as freight, handling, and aseptic UHT packaging services supplied in connection with exported goods may also be zero-rated under UStG §4(1)(No.3). The commercial invoice must expressly cite the zero-rated legal basis (UStG §4(1)(1a) in connection with §6).

  • Customs Export Declaration submitted electronically through ATLAS (Ausfuhranmeldung) confirmed by exit release with MRN (Main Reference Number)
  • Export Commercial Invoice (Handelsrechnung) stating 0% VAT, referencing UStG §4(1)(1a) and §6, with full buyer/seller details and ISIC 1050 product description (aseptic UHT oat milk packaging)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight), CMR International Consignment Note (road), Air Waybill (air freight), or CIM Consignment Note (rail) evidencing physical transportation out of EU customs territory
  • Export accompanying document or official ATLAS printout showing customs exit confirmation (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument with exit date from the EU border office)
  • Packing List / Lieferschein with UHT batch numbers, pallet quantities, and line-item quantities matching the commercial invoice and customs declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Swift MT103 / TT wire credit advice evidencing inbound settlement from the non-EU buyer in compliance with bank KYC and the underlying LC/TT payment terms
  • Proof of delivery to the final non-EU destination and, where applicable, third-country customs entry documentation or transit documents retained for audit under the statutory 10-year record-keeping obligation (AO §147)

No real-time fiscal clearance infrastructure analogous to Italy's SDI exists in Germany. However, the E-Rechnung (e-invoicing) mandate applies: from 1 January 2025, every German B2B supplier must be able to receive electronic invoices compliant with EN 16931 (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD XML format); from 1 January 2027, suppliers with turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices through interoperable exchange systems. VAT is filed electronically through ELSTER (monthly VAT returns, annual VAT declarations, recapitulative statements). Customs clearances are electronic via ATLAS (Ausfuhr/Anmeldungen). No digital stamping or pre-clearance of invoices is required. The EU ViDA framework will introduce B2B digital reporting obligations for Germany by 2030.

German tax auditors scrutinise export zero-rating by verifying the actual exit of goods from EU customs territory, the economic ownership of the exported goods, and the consistency of transport/customs documents with the sales ledger and bank settlement records. Where export documentation is absent or inadequate, the tax office retroactively assesses VAT at the standard 19% rate against the exporter, adds late-payment interest under AO §233a (0.5% per month from the 15th month following the return period, equivalent to 6% per annum), and applies the 10-year statute of limitation under AO §169/§170 for tax evasion cases. Fraudulent use of forged bills of lading, fictitious customs releases, or fictitious non-EU buyers constitutes tax fraud (Steuerhinterziehung) under AO §370 and StGB §370 — punishable by imprisonment of up to 10 years (5–10 years in aggravated cases), monetary penalties, and forfeiture of the evaded amount under StGB §73. Negligent false statements attract fines and order proceedings under AO §378 (up to EUR 50,000 per act). GmbH managing directors are personally liable for unpaid VAT under UStG §73 and AO §69; tax representatives and consultants may incur secondary liability. Cross-border VAT fraud exceeding EUR 10,000 engages the EU PIF Directive regime, customs penalties under the Union Customs Code, and potential suspension or revocation of the AEO customs licence of the UHT facility in Germany.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU and OECD codes. EUR is freely convertible; no exchange controls, no prior approval for inbound or outbound capital, dividends, royalties, or loan repayments. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to corporate tax, dividend withholding tax (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, effective 26.375%, unless reduced by treaty or EU directives), and transfer pricing compliance. No repatriation or surrender requirements apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or foreign currency. Cross-border payments above AWV thresholds (generally EUR 50,000) are subject to statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank, typically filed by the executing bank.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in cooperation with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the European Central Bank (ECB) for euro-area monetary policy; no separate foreign exchange administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium for operational and FX payments; Low for statutory capital escrow and trade finance. German-licensed EMIs (ZAG) and EU-passported EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business) can provide IBAN business accounts and multi-currency collections, but funds are safeguarded, not deposits; no lending, no commercial LC issuance, and no full banking services. Non-EU or offshore digital EMIs cannot legally onboard a German GmbH for regulated payment services without BaFin authorization or passporting; use is limited to ancillary payment flows and requires strict AML/KYC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital, shareholder loans, and capital reserves are routed via SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008, or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR. Standard purpose codes include INVESTMENT, SHARE CAPITAL, CAPITAL INJECTION, LOAN, and EQUITY. For GmbH incorporation, capital must be paid into a German bank account or notary escrow account (Notaranderkonto) and released after Handelsregister registration. Verification includes notarized capital increase or shareholder resolution, Handelsregister extract, business plan, source of funds declaration, and UBO and sanctions screening. No Bundesbank prior approval is required, but statistical reporting under AWV applies for payments above EUR 50,000. Banks may require evidence that funds are not linked to money laundering, terrorism, or sanctions.Outbound payments for oats, packaging, machinery, and trade settlements use SWIFT MT103, MT202, SEPA Credit Transfer, and TARGET2/T2 for EUR. Commercial letters of credit are issued via SWIFT MT700 and advised or confirmed through MT710 or MT720; amendments via MT707; reimbursement via MT740; documentary collections via MT400 or MT410. ISO 20022 migration applies to cross-border payments. For import and export of goods, banks require commercial invoice, bill of lading or CMR, packing list, certificate of origin, insurance certificate, and customs declaration. UCP 600 and ISBP govern LC examination. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for operational and FX payments; Low for statutory capital escrow and trade finance.

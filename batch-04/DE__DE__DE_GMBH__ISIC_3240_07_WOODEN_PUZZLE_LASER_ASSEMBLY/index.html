Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authorities are the local German Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate tax registration and the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). Tax registrations and electronic tax filings are generally administered through the ELSTER platform (Elektronische Steuererklärung), while the BZSt VAT identification number service is available through the BZSt online portal.

E-government filing portal

A GmbH must first be incorporated and registered in the German Commercial Register and must submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically through ELSTER to the competent local Finanzamt. The Finanzamt then issues the German tax number (Steuernummer). The GmbH or its tax representative may separately apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). The application normally requires the registered legal name, registered address, commercial-register details, managing-director information, business activity, domestic tax number if already issued, and evidence of taxable business activity. A non-resident foreign entity carrying on German taxable activities must register with the relevant German Finanzamt and may apply for a German VAT identification number. A separate VAT registration is generally not required where the entity has no German taxable transactions, subject to applicable reverse-charge and place-of-supply rules.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A GmbH must first be incorporated and registered in the German Commercial Register and must submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically through ELSTER to the competent local Finanzamt. The Finanzamt then issues the German tax number (Steuernummer). The GmbH or its tax representative may separately apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). The application normally requires the registered legal name, registered address, commercial-register details, managing-director information, business activity, domestic tax number if already issued, and evidence of taxable business activity. A non-resident foreign entity carrying on German taxable activities must register with the relevant German Finanzamt and may apply for a German VAT identification number. A separate VAT registration is generally not required where the entity has no German taxable transactions, subject to applicable reverse-charge and place-of-supply rules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A German GmbH is treated as a corporation and is subject to German corporation tax at entity level; Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a GmbH. The GmbH cannot elect partnership pass-through treatment merely by filing a tax form. The German corporate-law and tax election under Section 1a of the German Corporation Tax Act is principally available to eligible partnerships and is not a classification election for a GmbH. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive apply where their statutory conditions are met, but they do not provide a general elective hybrid classification regime for a GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany does not generally provide a turnover threshold below which a taxable business is outside VAT registration obligations. The small-business regime under Section 19 UStG is an exemption from charging German VAT, not a general exemption from all registration or reporting obligations. From 1 January 2025, the regime generally applies where prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and exclusions. A business exceeding the applicable limits, voluntarily waiving the small-business regime, or making taxable supplies requiring registration must register for VAT. The reduced German VAT rate is generally 7% for qualifying goods and services; ordinary manufactured wooden toys and puzzle products generally fall within the 19% rate unless a specific statutory reduced-rate provision applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax deduction under Sections 4 and 6 UStG where the goods are transported or dispatched to a third country, the goods physically leave the EU customs territory, and the supplier satisfies the documentary and accounting evidence requirements. The supplier must generally establish the purchaser, destination, export movement, and actual exit from the EU. The customs exit confirmation, normally the electronic Ausgangsvermerk or equivalent export confirmation, is central evidence. Intra-EU supplies of goods may qualify for VAT exemption under the separate intra-Community supply rules if the customer has a valid VAT identification number, the goods move to another EU Member State, and the supplier maintains the required evidence and reporting. Services supplied to business customers established outside Germany may be outside the scope of German VAT under the applicable place-of-supply rules rather than being technically zero-rated exports. Services supplied to consumers or special categories require separate place-of-supply analysis. Input VAT refunds or deductions require valid invoices, a direct connection to taxable transactions, and compliance with German accounting and record-retention requirements.

  • Electronic export declaration filed through the applicable EU customs system, including the correct customs procedure, commodity classification, customs value, exporter, consignee, and destination data
  • Electronic customs exit confirmation (Ausgangsvermerk) or legally acceptable alternative proof that the goods left the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and, where relevant, packing list, transport documentation, and sales contract or order evidence
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, postal proof, courier confirmation, or equivalent transport evidence, as applicable
  • Evidence linking the export declaration, invoice, goods, and customer, including MRN and shipment references
  • Accounting records showing the transaction, VAT treatment, export customer, consideration, and receipt or receivable
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid EU VAT identification number, transport evidence, recapitulative statement data, and required German VAT return disclosures
  • Inward remittance evidence is not generally a mandatory German VAT export document; bank payment records may nevertheless support audit substantiation where relevant

Germany does not currently operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or digital-stamping system. The electronic invoicing rules introduced from 1 January 2025 require German businesses to be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. Issuance obligations are phased in: from 1 January 2027, structured e-invoicing is generally required for issuers exceeding EUR 800,000 annual turnover in the preceding calendar year; from 1 January 2028, the requirement generally extends to all in-scope domestic B2B transactions. Permitted structured formats include EN 16931-compliant formats such as XRechnung and compliant ZUGFeRD versions. Transitional rules allow specified non-structured invoices during the phase-in period. The rules do not generally apply to consumer invoices, exempt transactions, or certain small-value and transport-ticket invoices, subject to statutory exceptions. VAT returns and other tax declarations are generally filed electronically through ELSTER.

Germany has no general foreign-exchange control requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days. Export proceeds may generally be held abroad, subject to ordinary accounting, transfer-pricing, anti-money-laundering, sanctions, customs, and tax requirements. A VAT exemption or input-tax refund may be denied or clawed back if the export, destination, customer, transport, customs exit, or accounting evidence is missing, contradictory, fabricated, or obtained after the fact without legal support. The Finanzamt may assess the unpaid VAT retrospectively, charge statutory interest, impose late-payment and filing penalties, and deny related input-tax deductions. Intentional or reckless false VAT declarations, fictitious exports, document falsification, or fraudulent refund claims may constitute tax evasion or related criminal conduct under the German Fiscal Code (Abgabenordnung), with criminal fines or imprisonment, in addition to civil tax recovery. Administrative fines may also apply for customs, statistical, invoicing, record-retention, or reporting violations. The taxpayer must retain relevant commercial, customs, invoice, and accounting records for the applicable German statutory periods, commonly eight years for accounting records and ten years for books and records subject to the longer statutory retention period, with the exact period depending on the record type and the applicable law at the relevant time.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully liberalized capital and current account framework under the EU Treaty regime (Articles 63-66 TFEU) and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWV). There are no exchange controls, limits on profit remittance, or mandatory conversion requirements. EUR is freely convertible; repatriation of dividends, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds is unrestricted for non-resident shareholders, subject only to standard corporate law solvency tests and the availability of distributable reserves. The principal regulatory compliance point is the mandatory statistical reporting obligation to Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border payments and assets exceeding EUR 12,500 per reporting threshold, under Sections 59-68 AWV, rather than any authorization or conversion gate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts in Germany. The German exchange control regime does not impose surrender, retention, or liquidation requirements on export proceeds. Exporters may freely maintain foreign currency accounts, invoice in any agreed currency, and use proceeds for operational or treasury purposes. The only binding requirement is the external transaction reporting duty to Deutsche Bundesbank: each cross-border payment receipt above EUR 12,500 must be reported within seven days of the transaction (or by the fifth business day of the following month for pooled periodic reporting under certain conditions), using the prescribed electronic reporting procedure. For ISIC 3240 wooden puzzle exports, typical BOP purpose codes align with goods trade reporting category 'Warenausfuhr' / 'Export of goods'.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (in coordination with the German Federal Ministry of Finance and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority - BaFin)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMIs and digital banks are generally viable supplementary banking rails, but not full substitutes for Tier-1 German commercial bank accounts. Under the German Banking Act (KWG) and PSD2 transposition, an authorized Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or Payment Institution can provide cross-border EUR accounts, SEPA/SEPA Instant, and multi-currency wallets via BaFin licensing or EU passporting (e.g., e-money license from BaFin or another EEA state with notification to BaFin). Legal constraints: EMIs are prohibited from granting commercial credit or providing investment, derivatives, or capital guarantee services; the GmbH will not obtain Class-1 lending lines, trade finance LC issuance, or FX forward execution through EMI rails. For the wooden puzzle entity, a cross-border EMI is rated as medium-high operational feasibility for collecting Amazon Pay/Stripe settlement in EUR, low for treasury-grade liquidity management and hedging. The corporate disbursement account must remain a regulated credit institution holding active membership in the German Cash Group and being a participant in the Bundesbank clearing infrastructure (SEPA-Clearer/TARGET2).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from a non-resident shareholder or intra-group lender must be executed through the SWIFT network wire transfer protocol: the remitting bank shall use MT103 or, for EU-origin EUR flows, an SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT or SCT Inst) with IBAN routing. The payment must reference a corporate law basis, such as 'AGB-Einlage' (contribution to capital) or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' (shareholder loan), and the GmbH must file the cross-border external transaction report with Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 60 AWV if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500. Compliance verification requires the notarized share capital confirmation or loan agreement, tax registration certificate, and bank credentials matching the registered GmbH name. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a 15.825% combined federal corporate tax burden. Municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional; its rate depends on the municipality’s assessment multiplier and commonly brings the combined burden to approximately 30%, but no single nationwide combined rate applies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 25% German withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective rate of 26.375%. Treaty relief may reduce the rate, commonly to 15% or, where a qualifying participation and other conditions are met, to a lower rate. Qualifying EU parent-subsidiary distributions may be exempt, subject to statutory requirements, anti-abuse rules, and procedural compliance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer without a tax adviser, the ordinary deadline is the last day of July of the following year, subject to statutory extensions and special rules. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany generally requires records for cross-border business relationships with related parties. Under the current documentation framework, a Master File is generally required where the taxpayer’s preceding-year turnover exceeds EUR 100 million. Comprehensive transaction-specific Local File documentation is generally required where cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 6 million for goods or EUR 600,000 for services and other transactions, subject to statutory definitions and exceptions. A transaction matrix and other records may be required, and documentation must be produced within the applicable statutory period, generally 30 days after request under the current rules. Confirm thresholds and transitional rules for the relevant fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no general stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to those in certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant under transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty and EU anti-abuse provisions, the principal-purpose test where applicable, and German rules on the place of effective management and permanent establishments. A GmbH should maintain personnel, decision-making, premises, records, and risk-control evidence consistent with its claimed functions and income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent returns

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion is punishable under section 370 of the Fiscal Code by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Unpaid tax, interest, and possible administrative consequences remain payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur criminal liability for their own conduct and may be liable for tax claims where statutory representative liability requirements are met. A company’s separate legal personality is not automatically disregarded. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty but may arise under applicable criminal-procedure measures.
Failure to withhold or remit tax

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for the unwithheld tax, interest, and applicable penalties. Intentional conduct may constitute tax evasion; negligent or other breaches may constitute tax misdemeanours or administrative offences, depending on the facts.

Director personal liability: A managing director responsible for tax compliance may be personally liable for tax amounts not properly withheld or paid where the statutory conditions are satisfied, and may face individual criminal or administrative proceedings.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Taxable income may be adjusted, with resulting tax, interest, and possible penalties. Failure to provide required documentation can lead to statutory surcharges; the amount depends on the breach, adjustment, and applicable minimum and maximum limits.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability where they culpably breach their tax duties or participate in intentional misconduct. Documentation failures do not by themselves automatically pierce the GmbH’s legal personality.
Unlawful distributions and capital maintenance breaches

Statutory sanction: The recipient may be required to repay an unlawful distribution under the GmbH Act. Further consequences may arise under insolvency, tax, and criminal law depending on intent and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for payments made contrary to statutory capital-maintenance or insolvency duties. Liability is statutory and fact-specific; veil piercing is not automatic.
Insolvency-related misconduct

Statutory sanction: Criminal and civil consequences may apply under the Insolvency Code and Criminal Code, including fines or imprisonment depending on the offence, together with repayment and insolvency-estate claims.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited payments and may face criminal prosecution. Insolvency filing duties and payment restrictions require immediate review when financial distress arises.
Money laundering and beneficial-ownership concealment

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties, administrative fines, confiscation, and reporting or remediation obligations may apply under the German Criminal Code, Money Laundering Act, and related legislation. The applicable sanction depends on the conduct and the entity’s regulated status.

Director personal liability: Individuals may be criminally or administratively liable for their own acts or failures to perform applicable duties. A travel ban or imprisonment is not automatic and requires a specific legal basis and process.

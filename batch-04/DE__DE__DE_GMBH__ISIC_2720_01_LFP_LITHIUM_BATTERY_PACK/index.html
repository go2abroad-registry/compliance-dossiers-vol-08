Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The German GmbH requires minimum stated share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 25,000. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer), customs/EORI registration, and tax clearance certificates; local tax office (Finanzamt) with territorial jurisdiction over the GmbH's registered seat for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT assessment. Electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); customs declarations via ATLAS.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute notarial deed and register the GmbH in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister); a foreign parent entity must submit notarized/legalized corporate documents and evidence of beneficial ownership under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG). Step 2: Submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent Finanzamt within 4 weeks of business commencement (Section 138 AO) to obtain the Steuernummer for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. Step 3: Apply to BZSt for the USt-IdNr. (EU VAT ID) and the EORI number for customs procedures. Step 4: Notify the Deutsche Bundesbank of external economic relations and report cross-border payments (balance-of-payments statistics). Step 5: The GmbH must also register with the customs authorities for ATLAS export procedures and, if applicable, with the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) for batteries/waste-battery compliance (BattG) and any export-control authorizations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a German GmbH is a resident corporation (unbeschränkt steuerpflichtig) subject to corporate income tax at 15.0% plus solidarity surcharge of 5.5% thereon (effective 0.825%) and municipality-dependent trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective rate approx. 14%–17%, avg. approx. 15%); aggregate profit tax burden approx. 30%–33%. The GmbH is not eligible for pass-through taxation for income tax purposes. Distributions to shareholders trigger dividend withholding tax of 26.375% (25% plus solidarity surcharge) unless a double-tax treaty reduces or exempts the rate; no German withholding tax applies to interest or royalties paid by the GmbH if contractual and paid to non-residents, but hybrid-financing arrangements are restricted.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) VAT and corporate income tax grouping (Organschaft) under Sections 2(2) No. 2 UStG and 14 KStG if a German parent holds the majority of voting rights and the GmbH is financially integrated; (ii) a profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) enabling consolidation of profits and losses for corporate income and trade tax purposes; (iii) election under Section 4k EStG (ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules) to align deductions and prevent hybrid financial instrument arbitrage; (iv) no election to be treated as a transparent entity — a transition to partnership taxation is possible only by legal-form conversion (Formwechsel) with resulting deemed-disposal and revaluation consequences.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 (nil) for a resident GmbH — VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply (Sections 2, 18 UStG); the small-business exemption (Section 19 UStG, EUR 25,000 threshold from 1 Jan 2025) is unavailable to a GmbH entered in the Commercial Register because it is a commercial merchant by legal form. For a non-resident entrepreneur supplying goods or services into Germany, registration is required from the first euro for B2B reverse-charge supplies (no threshold) and for intra-Community distance sales exceeding EUR 10,000 per annum across all EU member states (Union distance-sales threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of LFP battery packs physically delivered to third countries (outside the EU) are VAT-exempt with credit for input VAT (true zero-rating) under Section 6 UStG, provided the goods leave the EU within the statutory period and the exporter retains verifiable proof of export. Intra-Community B2B supplies to a customer in another EU member state are exempt under Section 6a UStG if (i) the customer is an entrepreneur, (ii) the customer holds a valid EU VAT ID (verified via BZSt/VIES), (iii) the goods are transported to another EU country, and (iv) the supplier obtains a Gelangensbestätigung (proof of arrival, Section 17a UStDV). Ancillary services closely connected to the export (transport, handling, insurance) are zero-rated under Section 7 UStG. No VAT exemption applies to services that are not directly linked to physical export; input VAT on overhead attributable to such services must be apportioned.

  • Export customs declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung/ATLAS) containing the MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • Proof-of-export certificate (Ausfuhrnachweis) per Sections 8–13 UStDV (ATLAS electronic export confirmation or customs-stamped certificate)
  • Commercial invoice in English/German stating Incoterms, declared value, and commodity code HS 8507.60 (Li-ion batteries)
  • Transport document evidencing physical movement to the third-country destination: Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), CMR waybill (road), or railroad consignment note
  • For intra-Community supplies: Gelangensbestätigung (arrival confirmation signed by the EU customer) and VIES confirmation of the customer's VAT ID
  • Inward remittance certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice) evidencing receipt of payment from the foreign buyer for economic-substance substantiation

Mandatory structured e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) for domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2025 (Section 14 UStG); invoices must be structured according to CEN EN 16931 (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, or PEPPOL BIS). Transition: until 31 December 2026, EDI or paper/PDF invoices remain valid with recipient consent; from 1 January 2027, businesses with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue EN 16931-compliant e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all businesses must issue e-invoices. Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping; no governmental API approval of invoices is required pre-issuance. A transaction-level VAT reporting system (digital reporting) aligned with EU ViDA is planned by BZSt from 2027/2028, leveraging the e-invoice data stream; for exports, the customs export declaration and proof-of-export remain the substantive clearance evidence.

If the required export evidence is not secured within 6 months (180 days) of shipment/invoice date, the zero-rating is retrospectively denied and output VAT is assessed at 19% on the export consideration, plus late-payment interest of 0.5% per month on the assessed tax (Section 233a AO), a late-filing penalty (Section 152 AO: 0.25% of assessed VAT per month, maximum EUR 25,000 annually), and penalty surcharges of up to 10% of unpaid advance VAT returns (Section 18(4) UStG). Where export documents are fabricated or fraudulent chain transactions (missing-trader/carousel schemes) are detected, criminal tax evasion penalties apply (Section 370 AO: imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in aggravated cases, plus forfeiture of the tax evaded and assets); managing directors and tax-compliance officers face personal liability for negligent or intentional failures (Sections 69, 71 AO; Sections 34, 35 GmbHG). Separately, failure to receive export proceeds within the 180-day period violates Section 60 AWV (German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance), exposing the GmbH to a regulatory fine (Ordnungswidrigkeit) of up to EUR 30,000 and to reassessment of the export exemption as a sham/abusive arrangement. BZSt and the Finanzamt regularly cross-check export transactions against customs ATLAS data, freight-forwarder records, and the foreign-exchange bank statements of the exporter.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully liberalised and convertible currency regime. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are free under Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG). There is no exchange-control authority, no surrender obligation, and no conversion quota applicable to EUR or foreign-currency receipts. Profit remittances, dividends, and distributions to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted at the regulatory level, subject only to domestic corporate tax withholding procedures, treaty-rate certificates, and standard commercial banking documentation. No central-bank approval is required for equity injections, loans, or trade settlements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, repatriation period, or minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds. German exporters may freely hold and use EUR or foreign currency from export sales. The only regulatory overlay is the statistical reporting obligation under Section 67 of the AWV: every cross-border payment (receipt or disbursement) exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank in the balance-of-payments return (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik). This reporting is non-authorisation-based, is filed electronically, and does not operate as a de facto exchange control; the threshold is periodically reviewed by the Bundesbank.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank) with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin) acting as co-competent banking supervision authority; there is no separate foreign-exchange administration in Germany. The Deutsche Bundesbank administers balance-of-payments statistics and the statutory reporting regime under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary operating rail, LOW as a primary treasury or statutory capital account. Licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions under the German Payment Services and E-Money Supervision Act (ZAG), including Airwallex, Wise Business, Revolut Business, and platforms sponsored by German banking-as-a-service providers such as Solaris SE, can provide multi-currency IBANs, cross-border payables/receivables, and retail-grade FX conversion. Legal constraints: EMIs may not take deposits, grant credit, issue commercial letters of credit, or provide deposit insurance under the statutory German scheme (Einlagensicherung). Funds received are safeguarded, not covered by the Deposit Guarantee Act. BaFin and GwG require full beneficial-owner identification, and battery-sector cross-border flows with Chinese cell suppliers and overseas storage projects will trigger enhanced due diligence. An EMI is not acceptable for the GmbH's formation-capital account, tax and social-security payment routing, or high-value documentary credit reimbursement. Use an EMI only for intraday treasury float, low-value supplier payments, and efficient spot FX; route all material trade and financing flows through a tier-1 licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For initial and subsequent GmbH capital injections: (i) route funds via SEPA Credit Transfer (EUR) or SWIFT MT103 (USD or CNH) to the bank-designated formation or operational IBAN; (ii) state in the payment reference the statutory purpose code, the notarial deed number, and the precise label 'Kapitaleinlage auf Stammeinlage'; (iii) ensure the remitter is exactly the registered shareholder to avoid commixture of funds and allegation of disguised third-party financing; (iv) provide the notary's capital-payment receipt before entry in the Commercial Register; (v) file the Deutsche Bundesbank AWV statistical report for any transaction above EUR 12,500; (vi) for shareholder loans, prepare a documented subordination agreement, arm's-length interest rate, and repayment schedule to prevent recharacterisation as equity capital under German insolvency-avoidance and StaRUG rules. No exchange-control permit or foreign-investment clearance applies from a treasury perspective; however, the German Foreign Trade Ordinance screening/notification for non-EU investors in critical infrastructure should be checked where battery storage is sold into the German energy market.Clearing rails recommended for the LiFePO4 battery-pack manufacturer: (a) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for direct supplier settlement, with purpose codes identifying imports of lithium-ion cells, components, and machinery; (b) Irrevocable confirmed commercial letters of credit (MT700/MT707) and standby LCs (MT760) for large China-based cell suppliers, with compliant document packages comprising signed commercial invoice, marine bill of lading, packing list, certificate of origin, and quality/witness inspection certificate; (c) SEPA Credit Transfer Protocol E2.0 for intra-EU component and logistics payments; (d) documentary collections (documents against payment, documents against acceptance) for medium-trust counterparties; (e) EBICS or bank API corporate e-banking for bulk payroll, VAT, and social- security disbursements. All payments are screened against EU and German sanctions lists and subject to a dual-use relevance check before processing. Licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions under the German Payment Services and E-Money Supervision Act (ZAG), including Airwallex, Wise Business, Revolut Business, and platforms sponsored by German banking-as-a-service providers such as Solaris SE, can provide multi-currency IBANs, cross-border payables/receivables, and retail-grade FX conversion. Legal constraints: EMIs may not take deposits, grant credit, issue commercial letters of credit, or provide deposit insurance under the statutory German scheme (Einlagensicherung). Funds received are safeguarded, not covered by the Deposit Guarantee Act. BaFin and GwG require full beneficial-owner identification, and battery-sector cross-border flows with Chinese cell suppliers and overseas storage projects will trigger enhanced due diligence. An EMI is not acceptable for the GmbH's formation-capital account, tax and social-security payment routing, or high-value documentary credit reimbursement. Use an EMI only for intraday treasury float, low-value supplier payments, and efficient spot FX; route all material trade and financing flows through a tier-1 licensed bank.

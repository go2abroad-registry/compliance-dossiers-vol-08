Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Local District Court / Registry Court (Amtsgericht – Handelsregister) in the place of the registered office, accessed via the German electronic register portal under the German Federal Chamber of Notaries and the German Federal Ministry of Justice; coordinated through the company’s notary for filing of the notarized incorporation deed.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbH-Gesetz / GmbHG), Section 1 (purpose), Section 5 (minimum share capital), Section 7 (application for registration), Section 8 (content of application), Section 10 (registration and publication), Section 35 (managing directors), Section 39 (list of shareholders), Section 41 (share register); German Commercial Code (HGB) Sections 106-110 for Handelsregister; Notarization requirement under German Civil Code (BGB) Section 1441 and notarization protocol rules; Gewerbeordnung (GewO) Section 14 for trade registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) or the statutory model protocol (Musterprotokoll) together with the formal Anmeldung zur Eintragung (application for registration) in the Commercial Register; publication through the Bundesanzeiger; Gewerbeanmeldung (trade notification) to the local Ordnungsamt / Gewerbeamt.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital of EUR 25,000.00; at least EUR 12,500.00 must be paid up in cash by the time of registration (in full for single-member GmbH); contributions must be placed in a German business account in the name of the company and blocked for the benefit of the company until registration; if a registered office is established, the cash capital is generally paid into a business account held with a German bank (or a European payment institution acceptable to the registrar); partial payment of cash contributions must be covered by a notarized statement and confirmation from the managing director that the funds are at the free disposal of the management.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt; www.bzst.de) for German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.), EU OSS/IOSS schemes and cross-border procedural rulings; local tax office (Finanzamt) responsible for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) and VAT (Umsatzsteuer) assessments; official electronic filing platform: ELSTER (www.elster.de); customs matters: Main Customs Office (Hauptzollamt) via ATLAS.

E-government filing portal

For a German resident GmbH: after notarial incorporation and registration in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), the managing director files the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER with the competent local Finanzamt to obtain the local tax number (Steuernummer); a separate application through the BZSt Online Portal (Formular USt 1 TU) is made for the German VAT ID (USt-IdNr.), which is also used for intra-EU distance sales and B2B services. For a foreign non-established supplier: VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable supply in Germany; the application is lodged through the BZSt Online Portal (Formular USt 1 TU), the BZSt issues the USt-IdNr. and notifies the responsible tax office; the EORI number is obtained separately from the Hauptzollamt for customs processing; no German commercial registration (Gewerbeanmeldung) is required for pure distance sellers without a permanent German establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (German resident GmbH is a per se body corporate under § 1(1) No. 1 KStG, taxed on worldwide income at corporate income tax of 15%, solidarity surcharge of 5.5%, and municipal trade tax, producing an aggregate effective burden of roughly 30–33%; no pass-through and no territorial regime). — For a German resident GmbH: after notarial incorporation and registration in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), the managing director files the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER with the competent local Finanzamt to obtain the local tax number (Steuernummer); a separate application through the BZSt Online Portal (Formular USt 1 TU) is made for the German VAT ID (USt-IdNr.), which is also used for intra-EU distance sales and B2B services. For a foreign non-established supplier: VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable supply in Germany; the application is lodged through the BZSt Online Portal (Formular USt 1 TU), the BZSt issues the USt-IdNr. and notifies the responsible tax office; the EORI number is obtained separately from the Hauptzollamt for customs processing; no German commercial registration (Gewerbeanmeldung) is required for pure distance sellers without a permanent German establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (German resident GmbH is a per se body corporate under § 1(1) No. 1 KStG, taxed on worldwide income at corporate income tax of 15%, solidarity surcharge of 5.5%, and municipal trade tax, producing an aggregate effective burden of roughly 30–33%; no pass-through and no territorial regime).

Available election forms

Germany grants no check-the-box or pass-through election to a GmbH; the entity is a body corporate subject to § 1(1) No. 1 KStG irrespective of any foreign classification. Available elections are: (1) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under §§ 14–19 KStG concluded by a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag), permitting the GmbH's taxable income to be attributed to a German parent; (2) participation exemption election for qualifying dividends and capital gains under § 8b KStG and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; (3) tax-neutral reorganisation elections under the Umwandlungssteuergesetz (UmwStG) for mergers, split-offs and contributions; (4) no hybrid mismatch relief: §§ 4k EStG and § 42 AO deny deductions or re-characterise income in hybrid mismatch arrangements involving an entity transparent in one jurisdiction and opaque in another, and a German GmbH remains opaque for German tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR – For a German-established entrepreneur, VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply with no de minimis; the small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung, § 19 UStG) applies only where the prior calendar-year turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and the current-year expected turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000. The intra-EU distance-selling threshold (Union scheme) is EUR 10,000 per calendar year for cross-border B2C supplies; a foreign non-established company making physical supplies in Germany must register from its first supply irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to export supplies (Ausfuhrlieferung) under § 4 No. 1(a) UStG in conjunction with § 6 UStG when the goods are physically removed from the EU customs territory to a third country by the supplier or by the non-resident purchaser; the exporter must hold registered economic operator status, use a proper customs export declaration (ATLAS/MRN), and retain the documentary proof of export (Nachweis der Ausfuhr) under §§ 8–11 UStDV. Zero rating also covers certain services directly related to exported goods under § 4 No. 1(b) UStG and work on movable property subsequently exported; pure offshore B2B services are not zero-rated as such but are taxable in the customer's jurisdiction under § 3a(2) UStG reverse charge. In administrative practice, the full proof bundle including the foreign-currency payment receipt must be in place within 180 calendar days from dispatch; otherwise the supply is treated as taxable at the standard rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) lodged in ATLAS and the electronic Export Accompanying Document / Master Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical dispatch to a third-country consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank payment receipt, Zahlungseingangsnachweis) evidencing receipt of the foreign sales price in EUR or convertible currency from the non-resident customer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration
  • EORI number and export records (Ausfuhrnachweis) proving exit from the EU customs territory

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime exists. B2G invoices must use the XRechnung standard (EN 16931-based) since 27 November 2020. Under the Wachstumschancengesetz (Growth Opportunities Act 2024), from 1 January 2025 all domestic B2B enterprises must be able to receive structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 enterprises with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices. There is no pre-clearance by the tax authority; electronic records are subject to GoBD and the mandatory certified cash-register rule under § 146a AO with a technical security element (TSE) applies to cash payments only. VAT returns and recapitulative statements (Zusammenfassende Meldung) are transmitted via ELSTER.

The local Finanzamt audits export zero-rating strictly; failure to produce credible export proof within the statutory verification period causes retroactive assessment at 19% VAT, plus late-payment interest at 0.5% per month under § 233a AO (after a 15-month interest-free period) and late-filing surcharges up to 10% of the assessed tax under § 152 AO. Negligent under-declaration (leichtfertige Steuerverkürzung) is punishable under § 378 AO by an administrative fine of up to EUR 25,000; intentional fraudulent use of false export documentation constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO, carrying imprisonment of up to five years in basic cases and from six months to ten years in aggravated cases (commercial scale, abuse of authority), plus confiscation of the evaded amounts. The assessment limitation period is four years for standard cases, five years for light negligence and ten years for tax fraud, allowing cumulative retrospective clawbacks for all open years; recapitulative statement mismatches can trigger additional EU-level administrative checks and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Germany for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no central-bank conversion approvals, and no restrictions on acquisition or disposal of foreign currency. Cross-border dividend distributions from a GmbH to a non-resident corporate parent are legally free, but tax compliance hurdles apply: a 26.375% corporate dividend withholding tax (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) is imposed unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, subject to a minimum 10% direct shareholding and qualifying holding period, or under an applicable income tax treaty. Capital-account movements such as equity injections, shareholder loans, debt-to-equity conversions, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds do not require currency authorization; however, cross-border payments of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported by the bank or the company to the Deutsche Bundesbank for external-statistical purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts denominated in EUR or foreign currency. Exporters may lawfully retain and hold foreign-currency receivables; each inbound payment from non-residents of EUR 12,500 or above must be captured in the balance-of-payments statistics report submitted to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV Section 64. The remittance must reference the underlying commercial invoice and customs transaction so that the bank can assign the correct Bundesbank statistical purpose code.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main); foreign-exchange administration and balance-of-payments statistical reporting are administered under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance, AWV), with prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment institutions by the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium as a supplementary collection rail; Low as the sole treasury bank. A non-EEA offshore digital EMI cannot legally provide payment services to a German GmbH without a BaFin licence or a German/EEA banking-partner arrangement. A UK-authorised EMI no longer benefits from EEA passporting after Brexit. Licensed EEA EMIs passported into Germany may support e-commerce collection through Amazon Pay and Stripe, but they do not provide TARGET2 membership, direct Bundesbank access, or full German corporate-banking services; client funds must be ring-fenced, and the GmbH should maintain its principal operating account with a tier-one domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections and shareholder loans are routed by SWIFT MT103 from the remitting shareholder's bank to the German GmbH's corporate account. The payment must include in field 70 a clear statutory purpose reference, e.g. 'Einlage Stammkapital GmbH' or 'Darlehen Gesellschafter'; the beneficiary bank will assign the Deutsche Bundesbank statistical purpose code for inward direct investment/equity capital. For capital contributions exceeding EUR 12,500, the receiving bank reports the transaction under AWV Section 64. The GmbH must preserve the Einzahlungsbeleg (capital payment receipt) and the notarial confirmation used to file the capital increase with the Commercial Register; all funds should come from the disclosed corporate shareholder account and never commingle with third-party personal funds.Trade settlements to non-EU suppliers for raw beechwood, non-toxic paints, and packaging are executed by SWIFT MT103 (single payments) with field 70 referencing the invoice, Incoterms, and contract number. For documentary trade, commercial letters of credit are issued via MT700/MT720 and negotiated against presentation of the bill of lading, packing list, commercial invoice, phytosanitary certificates, CE declaration, and EN 71-3 laboratory report. For EU counterparties, SEPA Credit Transfers with structured creditor reference are used; all outgoing cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 are captured by Bundesbank statistical reporting. Where Chinese suppliers invoice in CNY, clearing can be routed through the Frankfurt RMB clearing bank (Bank of China Frankfurt Branch) or settled in EUR with an FX contract, depending on pricing efficiency and counterparty preference.FX forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) in EUR/USD and EUR/CNY with ISDA-documented German counterparties, supported by underlying trade receivables or payables.FX swaps (Devisenswaps) for temporary euro-versus-foreign-currency liquidity bridging and working-capital optimisation.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD and EUR/CNY) for long-term hedging of financed inventory, equipment, or foreign supplier credit.European-style and American-style FX options for budgeted raw-material purchases and settlement of Amazon and Stripe proceeds.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only where the underlying settlement currency is subject to offshore convertibility restrictions; each NDF must be supported by verifiable commercial exposure and issued by a licensed German or EEA investment firm under EMIR.

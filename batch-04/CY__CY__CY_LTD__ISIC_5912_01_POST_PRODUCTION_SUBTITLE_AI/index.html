Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Cyprus Ltd is a body corporate deemed resident and fully subject to entity-level corporate income tax on worldwide profits at 12.5%; no statutory look-through for a Ltd, although Cyprus tax treaty territorial sourcing rules and the foreign dividend participation exemption apply; the commercial audio dubbing / AI subtitle studio is taxed at corporate level; no CFC or pass-through regime applies to this entity type.

Available election forms

Cyprus does not operate a check-the-box election; entity classification follows the Companies Law and Income Tax Law. Available statutory elections include: (1) group loss relief election under Section 31 Income Tax Law (Taxation of Income and Capital) where a parent holds at least 75% of the subsidiary; (2) VAT grouping election under Article 33 of the VAT Law 95(I)/2000, allowing closely linked persons to be treated as a single taxable person; (3) deemed dividend distribution (DDD) election under the Special Contribution for Defence Law affecting 17% withholding on distributed profits with waiver relief for foreign tax credits; (4) tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping companies; (5) the Intellectual Property (IP) Box regime is statutory rather than elective. Hybrid mismatch rules under Law 37(I)/2017 and EU ATAD disallow deductions for hybrid mismatches involving Cyprus entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 15,600 for taxable supplies of goods and services within any rolling 12-month period for established taxable persons; EUR 10,000 for intra-Community acquisitions and non-established suppliers engaged in distance sales; EUR 10,000 threshold applies to cross-border distance sales of electronic, broadcasting and telecommunications services under the OSS/Union scheme; voluntary registration is permitted below these thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero percent VAT may be applied to: (1) exports of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU by or on behalf of the supplier (Article 47 VAT Law), with physical movement out of the EU evidenced; (2) intra-Community supplies delivered to a registered taxable person in another EU Member State (Article 48 VAT Law) subject to the customer's valid EU-VAT ID and the recapitulative statement (EC Sales List); (3) supplies of services directly associated with exported goods, including transport, loading, unloading, handling, and ancillary international freight services; (4) cross-border services supplied to non-EU taxable persons where the recipient acquires the right to use intangible outputs such as AI-generated subtitle files, audio transcription, dubbing or cloned voice masters, and where effective use and enjoyment occurs outside Cyprus; and (5) services supplied to a non-taxable person resident outside the EU if the service is effectively enjoyed outside the Community. The Cyprus audio post-production studio must obtain and retain objective commercial evidence of non-EU customer status (e.g., confirmation of overseas taxable person status), export declarations, transport or digital delivery records, contracts, usage logs and proof of payment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD) duly accepted and customs-cleared
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure of goods from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent SWIFT MT103 bank transfer evidence proving receipt of export consideration in the Cyprus corporate bank account
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, signed proof of delivery and corresponding GL ledger entries
  • For digital subtitle / voice cloning deliverables: digitally signed acceptance report, IP right transfer document and server / platform delivery logs

Cyprus has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing stamping or continuous transaction control (CTC) system currently in force. Taxpayers may issue electronic invoices subject to authenticity, integrity and legibility requirements under the VAT Law; B2G e-invoicing across the EU is mandated under Directive 2014/55/EU for public procurement and Cyprus operates a national e-invoicing platform. Under EU ViDA proposals, Cyprus is expected to require structured e-invoicing with digital reporting in future phases, but as of the current stage, tax clearance is enforced via periodic VAT returns in TaxisNet (every three months for a standard quarterly filer, or monthly for larger taxpayers). Commercial invoices must include: sequential numbering, issue date, supplier and customer VAT identification numbers, full name and address, nature of the audio dubbing / subtitle production services, quantity, net amount, applicable VAT rate, amount of VAT and, where zero-rated exports are claimed, the export declaration number and transport document reference.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict documentary audit verification by the Cyprus Tax Department. If contemporaneous export evidence is not produced, the Tax Department re-characterizes the supply as a domestic taxable supply and assesses VAT at 19% on the undeclared consideration. Late VAT is subject to statutory interest at 9% per annum from the filing deadline. Additional penalties under Article 130 VAT Law include administrative fines up to EUR 250 and tax penalties equal to the greater of 10% of the tax and EUR 250; negligent or fraudulent declarations attract assessment penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded. Criminal prosecution for deliberate evasion of VAT through false zero-rating in the film / media export sector may result in fines up to EUR 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to three years. The normal assessment limitation period is six years, extended to ten years where fraud or intentional misrepresentation is proven, and clawback includes disallowance of input VAT recovery and nullification of the related exporter's refund claim. The exporter is jointly and severally liable where it knowingly participates in an abusive zero-rating arrangement, per CJEU abuse-of-rights principles.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cyprus uses the euro (EUR) as legal tender. Capital and current account transactions are fully liberalised under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the Eurosystem legal framework. There are no residual Cypriot foreign-exchange controls; inbound equity investment, loan repayments, trade proceeds, and dividend distributions are freely convertible and remittable. The only hurdles are standard compliance gates: AML/KYC, beneficial ownership transparency, substance documentation, FATCA/CRS reporting, and verifying the underlying economic transaction for bank clearing. Dividends paid to EU residents are not subject to Cyprus withholding tax; non-EU shareholders may benefit from treaty relief, and the bank will require a self-declaration or tax residency certificate where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Cyprus imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or forced repatriation obligation. Export receipts from subtitle/dubbing and post-production services may be retained in EUR or any foreign currency in Cypriot bank accounts and settled freely. However, resident companies must report cross-border flows to the Central Bank of Cyprus for balance-of-payments and international investment position statistics, typically through quarterly banking forms or the bank's electronic reporting platform, depending on transaction thresholds established by the CBC.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Cyprus (Κεντρική Τράπεζα της Κύπρου), operating within the European System of Central Banks and as the national competent authority for payment systems, AML/CFT supervision, and international investment position reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfers must originate from a verified shareholder bank account and be sent to the Cyprus company's account via SWIFT or SEPA using the Cyprus IBAN (CY + 2-digit check digits + 5-digit bank code + 5-digit branch + 16-digit account number). The payment narrative must state the economic purpose, for example 'share capital contribution under board resolution of [date]' or 'subordinated loan under funding agreement of [date]'. Relevant ECB/BoP statistical purpose category should be reported to the Central Bank of Cyprus as appropriate, typically under capital transactions (e.g., direct investment equity or intercompany loan). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000 in aggregate value of controlled transactions for the fiscal year (local file and master file); as of the transposition of EU TP documentation rules into Cyprus law (under the Income Tax Law and the Cyprus TP Guidelines), the threshold is set at €750,000 for controlled transactions involving related parties. A Cyprus TP declaration form accompanies the annual tax return.

Economic substance scope: Cyprus has adopted comprehensive Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) pursuant to EU Code of Conduct and the OECD BEPS initiative, primarily affecting tax-resident entities engaged in geographically mobile activities (e.g. IP holding, cross-border financing, leasing). A Cyprus LTD engaged in AI subtitle and voice cloning is subject to substance review if it derives significant income from intra-group services or IP; it must demonstrate adequate premises, staff, and operational management in Cyprus based on the 'adequacy and proportionality' principle.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Law Cap. 332; Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €10,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; interest and penalties on unpaid tax (with statutory surcharges up to 100% of nominal tax due).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false returns may be personally liable, may be subject to attachment of personal assets, and may be issued with travel bans; criminal conviction can lead to disqualification from acting as a director and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance (Cyprus ESR; EU ATAD / BEPS Actions)

Statutory sanction: Imposition of additional taxes at a 25% effective rate on profits attributable to non-substantive operations; denial of IP/Box or NID benefits; administrative penalties up to €50,000 per infraction under the ESR framework; possible strike-off from the Cyprus Registrar of Companies.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors are held personally liable for knowingly enabling a sham; civil restitution of lost tax revenue, travel bans pending investigation, and criminal sanctions if fraud or fraudulent trading is proven.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement (Income Tax Law s.33A; EU TP Directive)

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment to arm's-length profit, additional 20% penalty on the adjustment amount (or 10% if corrected before enquiry), plus interest at 6% per annum; in cases of intentional manipulation, penalty may reach 30% of tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved TP policies without adequate documentation may be jointly and severally liable for tax losses if they acted recklessly; orders for repayment of unlawful distributions from related party transactions; director disqualification up to 3 years.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Vigilance (Prevention and Suppression of Money Laundering Activities Law, L.188(I)/2007 as amended)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €200,000 per violation and suspension or revocation of the company's payment processor accounts; criminal penalties for designated persons (including directors) of imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering offences.

Director personal liability: Directors as 'administrative officers' may be personally liable for failing to report suspicious transactions; travel bans pending investigation and forfeiture orders against personal bank accounts; in severe cases of fraud, piercing the corporate veil is ordered.
Misclassification of AI-Generated Content / Copyright Revenues (Income Tax Misreporting)

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of income and recapture of tax benefits at 12.5% plus interest and penalties; if misclassification is found to be deliberate, criminal tax evasion sanctions apply as above.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved a sham IP Box structure may be held personally responsible for the shortfall in corporate tax, with civil penalties plus potential disqualification and criminal prosecution.

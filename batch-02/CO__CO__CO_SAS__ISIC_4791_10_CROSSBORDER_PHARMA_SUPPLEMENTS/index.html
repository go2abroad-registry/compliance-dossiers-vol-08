Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); electronic portal Muisca (www.dian.gov.co) for RUT inscriptions, electronic invoicing, customs declaration processing, and tax return filing.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must register in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) with DIAN before any taxable operation. The local S.A.S. obtains its Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT) at incorporation. Non-resident investors without a Colombian entity obtain a NIT by applying through a designated legal representative, presenting notarized power of attorney, certificate of existence, and passport copies, either via Muisca with a certified digital signature or in-person at DIAN. Permanent establishments of foreign companies also require a separate RUT and NIT. Registration is immediate upon submission and issuance of RUT.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must register in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) with DIAN before any taxable operation. The local S.A.S. obtains its Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT) at incorporation. Non-resident investors without a Colombian entity obtain a NIT by applying through a designated legal representative, presenting notarized power of attorney, certificate of existence, and passport copies, either via Muisca with a certified digital signature or in-person at DIAN. Permanent establishments of foreign companies also require a separate RUT and NIT. Registration is immediate upon submission and issuance of RUT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists; all Colombian S.A.S. entities are automatically subject to entity-level income tax on worldwide income. A qualified small business may elect the Simplificado regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación) merging income tax and local taxes when gross income and employee thresholds are met, and a taxpayer may elect Zona Franca certification for other income tax benefits, but neither election changes the VAT registration or classification. There are no hybrid mismatch advance elections within Colombian law beyond applying the automatic hybrid mismatch limitation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for legal entities; any Colombian SAS selling taxable goods must register in RUT as a VAT responsible person from the first taxable transaction. Natural persons may qualify for the simplified VAT regime below 3,500 UVT annual income, but corporate entities are categorically excluded from that simplified treatment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A certified exporter with RUT and customs registration may invoice at 0% VAT for goods that physically leave Colombian customs territory under an approved exporting declaration, transfer title to a non-resident, and for which export proceeds are collected and repatriated through the supervised foreign exchange market within the statutory period. For services, the same applies only when the user of the service is located abroad and the services are deemed not connected with the Colombian territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX) generated through VUCE/Muisca
  • Bill of Lading or Airwaybill evidencing physical exit from Colombia
  • Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate (Declaración de Cambio / Certificado de Reintegro) issued by an authorised intermediary bank

Mandatory nationwide real-time clearance: all VAT-generating sales must be documented as Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV), generated in XML with a digital certificate from an authorized software provider, transmitted through DIAN’s Muisca webservice (API) for instant validation, and converted into a readable format with a unique access code (CUDE) and QR matrix. The invoicing cycle must include the buyer’s electronic receipt event and the seller’s transmission of the final 'validación' event; no physical stamping or prior authorization seals exist, but DIAN can request immediate validation status through the mandatory JSON/XML channels.

DIAN may retroactively audit DEX and VAT refund files for up to five years. Failure to satisfy substantive or documentary conditions results in the 19% VAT applicable, plus interest from the original sale date, a sanction for tax inaccuracy of up to 200% of the omitted tax, and a forced return of any VAT refund received. Criminal liability may arise for orchestrated false export schemes, including imprisonment, and a temporary or permanent revocation of RUT export status.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is freely convertible for current-account items (trade in goods and services, royalties, personal transfers); no prior administrative license is required, but resident invoices/payments in foreign currency must be channeled through the Mercado Cambiario (authorized banks, banking corporations, stockbrokers, and exchange houses) when they exceed regulatory thresholds. Capital-account transactions (foreign direct investment into the S.A.S., remittance of dividends, external indebtedness) are subject to mandatory registration with Banco de la República through a local authorized intermediary (Formulario 13 for investments; Formulario 14 for profit remittance; Formulario 12 for external credits). Profit remittance is lawfully permitted after corporate tax (currently 35% CIT for 2025) and after dividend withholding (5% for non-residents assuming the dividend payer is not subject to disallowed deductions), provided the investment has been registered and the declaración de cambio is supported by audited financial statements and the certificado de cumplimiento del deber de informar. Practical hurdles: FX channeling deadlines, monthly

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Banrep) – Junta Directiva, exercising its constitutional power (Constitución Política, Art. 371–373; Ley 9 de 1991, Estatuto de Cambios Internacionales) and implementing the foreign-exchange régime principally through Resolución Externa 8 de 2000, as amended. Supervision of authorized FX intermediaries rests with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC); enforcement and declaratory control are executed by DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File are required if the Colombian S.A.S.'s gross assets or gross revenue exceed COP 4,800,000,000 (approx. 100,000 UVT for fiscal year 2024) or if controlled transactions with related parties exceed COP 450,000,000 (approx. 10,000 UVT). Form 120 (Declaración Informativa de Precios de Transferencia) is due on or before the ordinary tax return deadline.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE or Cayman Islands. However, taxpayers enjoying preferential tax benefits (e.g., free trade zones, digital platform incentives) must demonstrate commercial substance under the general anti-abuse rules (Art. 118-1 and 869 E.T.). For an ordinary S.A.S. distributing nutraceuticals online, the company must show minimum administrative, operational, and personnel substance to avoid challenges under BEPS Action 5 and the 'sham arrangement' doctrine.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Evasion Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Fine of 100%–200% of the under-declared tax; imprisonment of 5–6 years under Colombia's penal tax provisions; suspension of the NIT for serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors, shareholders, and shadow managers can be civilly liable for the corporation's unpaid tax if they knowingly caused the false declaration; personal assets can be attached; travel bans may be imposed, and prison sentence applies to the effective manager.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 3% of the asset value disguised; disallowance of all deductions; retroactive cancellation of tax benefits; potential blacklisting under the OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution for dividends illegally repatriated through the shell; Colombian courts have pierced the veil under Law 222 of 1995, ordering directors to repay the corporate debt and banning them from managing companies for up to ten years.
AML / High-Risk PSP Violations (Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to COP 15,000,000,000 under the Financial Intelligence Unit (UIAF) and SARLAFT framework; criminal money-laundering penalties of 8–20 years' imprisonment and seizure of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly process irregular online transactions are personally liable for money-laundering offenses; arrest and preventive custody are possible because of non-bailable charges; the corporate veil is pierced without proportionality in AML enforcement.
DTA/Treaty Shopping and Withholding Evasion

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; obligation to withhold the full 35% plus default interest and penalty of 50%–200% of the unpaid withholding; ban on filing new treaty relief for three years.

Director personal liability: Directors signing documents that falsely certify beneficial ownership face criminal penalties for perjury and tax fraud; they may be personally ordered to pay the withholding differential, and foreign travel may be restricted until the tax debt is settled.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Ley 1258/2008; at least one share must be subscribed and issued; contributions may be cash, assets, or industry and may be paid in full or in instalments as provided in the bylaws; no escrow account or prior capital deposit is required for an e-commerce S.A.S.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for a purely virtual e-commerce operation with no staff premises or inventory storage. If the company later leases an office or a fulfillment warehouse, the applicable clearance consists of a fire-safety certificate from the local fire department (Cuerpo de Bomberos) and an occupational health and safety permit under Resolution 0312 of 2019, verified by the ARL (Occupational Risk Administrator).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Ley 1258/2008; at least one share must be subscribed and issued; contributions may be cash, assets, or industry and may be paid in full or in instalments as provided in the bylaws; no escrow account or prior capital deposit is required for an e-commerce S.A.S.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating exchange-rate system. The Colombian peso (COP) is not pegged, and both current-account and capital-account transactions are generally channelable through authorized IMCs; there is no blanket prohibition on non-resident ownership of a Colombian S.A.S., and 100% foreign shareholding is permitted. Profit remittance is legally free but operationally gated: dividends may only be distributed from audited distributable earnings; the statutory 10% legal reserve (until reaching 50% of subscribed capital) must be appropriated; the shareholders' meeting minutes must evidence the dividend declaration; the remittance must be executed through an IMC with a Declaración de Cambio supported by corporate and tax documentation; and dividends paid to non-residents are typically subject to Colombian withholding taxation (generally 5% on dividends paid out of corporate income already taxed at entity level) with the corresponding DIAN reporting. No central-bank prior authorization is required for the capital outflow itself, but the complete document package is a precondition for the IMC to execute the foreign-currency purchase and remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exports of goods, including DTC e-commerce sales fulfilled from Colombia, are designated as obligatory-channeling operations. 100% of the export proceeds must be channeled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market via an authorized IMC within six (6) months from the date of the export declaration/shipment recorded in the Colombian customs export document. Channeling is satisfied by selling the foreign currency to the IMC or by depositing/crediting it to a foreign-currency deposit account (Cuenta en Moneda Extranjera, CME) maintained with an IMC. There is no mandatory percentage-based conversion ratio to COP beyond this full channeling obligation; the exporter may retain the funds in USD in the CME or convert to COP as treasury needs dictate. Proceeds collected through foreign payment aggregators (Shopify Payments, PayPal, Stripe) must be swept into the IMC channel within the same six-month window and supported by export invoices, shipping evidence, and settlement statements.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), acting through its Junta Directiva and Oficina de Cambios; the foreign-exchange control regime is governed by Decreto 1735 de 1993, Resolución 8 de 2000 (as amended), Circular DCIN-83, and related regulations enforced by authorized Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario (IMCs) and the DIAN for customs/tax validation.

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as a final treasury repository; Medium as a temporary liquidity aggregator. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Revolut Business) are not licensed Colombian IMCs and cannot replace the mandatory canalización of export proceeds. Funds held in offshore EMIs without being swept to a Colombian IMC within the statutory export channeling window breach Resolución 8 de 2000 and expose the S.A.S. to foreign-exchange sanctions. Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Payment aggregator inflows (Shopify Payments, PayPal, Stripe) arriving as high-velocity, small-ticket USD settlements without a corresponding Declaración de Cambio, flagged by SARLAFT as unclassified cross-border card-not-present flows.

Root cause: Payment aggregator inflows (Shopify Payments, PayPal, Stripe) arriving as high-velocity, small-ticket USD settlements without a corresponding Declaración de Cambio, flagged by SARLAFT as unclassified cross-border card-not-present flows.

Defense evidence: Provide the bank with monthly settlement statements from each aggregator; the e-commerce platform identification (Shopify store URL and seller credentials); matching commercial invoices and shipping/customs export declarations; proof that net settlements were re-channeled to the IMC account; and the IMC-issued Declaración de Cambio receipts showing the applicable export concept code. A documented reconciliation spreadsheet tracing each settlement batch to the corresponding export invoice is essential.
Beneficial ownership opacity: non-resident shareholders controlling the S.A.S. through an offshore holding company, triggering enhanced due diligence and non-resident red flags.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity: non-resident shareholders controlling the S.A.S. through an offshore holding company, triggering enhanced due diligence and non-resident red flags.

Defense evidence: File and certify the Registro Único de Beneficiarios Finales (RUBF) with the DIAN; provide apostilled corporate registry certificates and constitutional documents of the entire ownership chain; deliver a UBO attestation signed by the local legal representative; include source-of-funds and source-of-wealth statements for the non-resident shareholders; and present the notarized capital contribution minutes evidencing the true beneficial owners.
High volume of small-ticket card-not-present transactions and chargeback activity creating a pattern consistent with carding, account-takeover, or money-moulding typologies.

Root cause: High volume of small-ticket card-not-present transactions and chargeback activity creating a pattern consistent with carding, account-takeover, or money-moulding typologies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital and shareholder loans from non-residents must enter Colombia exclusively through an authorized IMC. Recommended protocol: route a SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the S.A.S.'s operational account at a tier-1 Colombian bank, using the beneficiary company's NIT as the beneficiary identifier and including an unambiguous originator-to-beneficiary information (OBI) field describing 'Aporte de capital - Inversión directa de capital' or 'Préstamo entre compañías', matched to the notarized share subscription minutes or loan agreement. The receiving IMC must file the Declaración de Cambio (Formulario No. 1) using the appropriate foreign investment or external debt concept code. Within the statutory registration window (in current Banco de la República practice, generally up to twelve months from the investment date), the S.A.S. must register the investment in the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales; for shareholder loans, the external debt registration requirements must also be met. Documentation required: corporate existence certificate, RUT, apostilled share subscription minutes, source-of-funds certification from the non-resident shareholder, and the bank's SWIFT credit advice.For an e-commerce DTC brand, outbound disbursements comprise supplier invoices, logistics fees, advertising costs (Google/Meta), software subscriptions, and payment-processor fees. The preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 executed through an authorized IMC, with each transaction supported by the underlying contract/invoice and a Declaración de Cambio where channeling obligations apply. For high-value inventory purchases, a commercial letter of credit governed by UCP 600 issued by a tier-1 local bank is appropriate, with required documents being proforma invoice, transport document, insurance certificate, and inspection certificate where applicable. For recurring low-value programmatic payments (SaaS, advertising, logistics), aggregate settlement through the IMC with a monthly reconciliation file is acceptable; corporate virtual cards may be used only where the issuing bank records the transaction as a foreign-exchange operation and provides a statement that supports the Declaración de Cambio. Do not use informal aggregators, personal cards, or direct transfers from offshore EMIs as substitutes for the IMC rail when the underlying funds are Colombian export proceeds subject to canalización.Onshore deliverable COP/USD forex forward contracts with authorized Colombian IMCs, used to hedge receivables and payables denominated in foreign currency; requires documented underlying trade exposure.Offshore COP/USD non-deliverable forwards (NDFs), permissible for hedging where onshore market depth is insufficient; settled in USD without physical delivery of COP and must be documented as hedge transactions to avoid speculative classification.Cross-currency swaps (COP/USD) with local or international bank counterparties, permissible for hedging long-term foreign-currency debt or capital-injection exposures.Vanilla foreign-exchange options purchased from authorized IMCs, permissible as hedging instruments for contingent pricing or procurement exposure; writing/selling options by non-financial corporate entities is generally not recommended and may be treated as speculative under Colombian FX regulations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory for the S.A.S. if (i) gross income in the fiscal year exceeds 150,000 UVT (approx COP 7,060 million for 2024) or (ii) total assets exceed 150,000 UVT, or (iii) related-party transactions exceed 100,000 UVT (approx COP 4,706 million). Master File: mandatory if the foreign group consolidated annual gross income exceeds 200,000 UVT (approx COP 9,410 million) and the local entity carries out related-party transactions. A CbC Report is required for groups with consolidated income ≥ 2,000,000 UVT.

Economic substance scope: Colombia has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) analogous to the UAE/CRS regimes, but the Tax Code (Articles 894 and 899) and jurisprudence empower DIAN to disregard form over substance, recharacterize loans, and deny deductions where the S.A.S. lacks real activity, personnel and risk control. A passive invoice-hosting shell with no human/economic footprint cannot rely on formal incorporation to protect its tax benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Omission of Global E-commerce Revenues

Statutory sanction: Income tax deficiency of 35% plus monthly default interest; inaccuracy penalty of 20% of the understated gross amount; criminal tax evasion sanctions under Article 435 of the Colombian Penal Code: imprisonment between 48 and 108 months and a fine equal to 50%-200% of the evaded tax. The S.A.S. may lose its NIT and be removed from the DIAN registry.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing applies to legal representatives who intentionally sign false returns; they are jointly and severally liable with the S.A.S. for the evaded tax, interest, and penalties; travel bans, preventive embargoes on personal assets, and passport revocation are available. Imprisonment terms attach to the natural person, not the legal entity.
Sham / Shell Arrangement without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the entity's separate legal personality under Article 894 via an administrative audit, reassess taxes to the actual beneficial owner, impose penalties equal to 200% of the omitted tax plus advance payments, and cancel the S.A.S.'s NIT for prolonged non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who act without corporate purpose or knowingly permit sham transactions are jointly liable without limitation; personal bank accounts may be attached; a director may face a migration ban while the administrative investigation is pending and criminal charges for ideological false statement to the registry.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Unlegitimate Settlement Processing

Statutory sanction: Money laundering (Arts. 323-324 Penal Code) sanctions: imprisonment of 8 to 18 years and fines equivalent to 1 to 50% of the assets laundered; UIAF fine up to COP 200 million for failure to report unusual transactions; criminal confiscation of the DTC brand proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are criminal co-principals if they knowingly omit reports or tolerate layering; travel bans, extradition risk, and freeze of personal estates in Colombia. The company’s registration with the UIAF is suspended, effectively blocking all payment rails.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: DIAN TP adjustment increases taxable income to arm's length level; a 15% withholding is re-applied on deemed remittances; accuracy penalty of 20% of the TP adjustment; denial of deductions and tariff surcharge on hidden dividends. Criminal liability if TP documentation is intentionally falsified.

Director personal liability: The legal representative is jointly liable for TP adjustments arising from transactions he/she approved with a related party; liability extends to directors’ personal estates if the S.A.S. is insolvent; criminal imprisonment up to 96 months for falsifying Local File documentation or destroying underlying transaction records.

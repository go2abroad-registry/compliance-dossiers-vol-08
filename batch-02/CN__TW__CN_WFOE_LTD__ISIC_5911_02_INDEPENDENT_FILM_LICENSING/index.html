Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the Taiwan Company Act for a wholly foreign-owned limited liability company. The paid-up capital amount must equal the amount approved in the Mainland Investment Approval Letter and must be remitted from the Mainland investor into a Taiwan bank account opened in the proposed company name before filing, then verified by a licensed CPA. No blocked escrow account is required by the MOEA for this non-regulated service-sector WFOE; however, the capital remittance certificate and CPA capital verification report must accompany the incorporation application, and paid-up capital may not be withdrawn or replaced with fictitious contributions before incorporation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Taxation Agency, Ministry of Finance (Taiwan) - Online portal: e-Tax Portal (https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in Taiwan, the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is obtained through the following procedure: (1) Register the company with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) to obtain a company registration certificate. (2) Submit the company registration certificate, articles of incorporation, and identification documents of the legal representative (passport or ARC) to the local National Taxation Bureau (branch of the Taxation Agency). (3) Complete the 'Application for Tax Registration' form (Form 1) and file it with the bureau. (4) The bureau issues a TIN (also known as the Unified Business Number, UBN) which serves as the VAT registration number and corporate income tax ID. (5) For non-resident entities without a physical presence, a tax representative must be appointed to handle tax filings and communications. The entire process typically takes 5-10 business days.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The China-resident WFOE is subject to the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration regime administered by SAFE under the People's Bank of China. The current account has achieved effective convertibility for genuine trade and service transactions, so film licensing royalties, distribution income, and production service fees can be settled and remitted after the designated bank performs authenticity, tax, and purpose checks. The capital account remains closely managed: the Taiwan shareholder's equity injection must be routed through FDI foreign exchange registration, a capital account, and a capital verification process; the injected funds can be used only within the approved business scope and are prohibited from lending, secondary investment, real estate outside the registered purpose, or unmatched RMB settlement. Dividend remittance to the Taiwan parent is treated as current-account income but requires audited financial statements, corporate income tax completion, legal reserve adequacy, a board resolution, and a bank-reported SAFE foreign exchange filing. RMB is not freely convertible on the capital account; offshore fund flows or back-to-back structures without SAFE registration are unlawful, and any attempt to bypass through fintech platforms will create immediate bank freezing and regulatory blacklisting risk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export-related film licensing and copyright distribution proceeds must be repatriated to China through the designated bank participating in SAFE's Trade Monitoring System. The statutory repatriation timeline is generally 180 days from the date of customs declaration for goods, or the contractual payment due date for cross-border services such as film licensing; the WFOE must ensure receipt and inclusion in the trade monitoring match before onward use. China currently imposes a 0% mandatory conversion ratio, meaning the WFOE may retain foreign currency in its foreign exchange settlement account or sell it to the bank for RMB at market rates based on genuine operating needs, but 100% of eligible export proceeds must be repatriated into the onshore banking system. The bank must match each receipt to the underlying license agreement, invoice, and tax certification; non-repatriation, false matching, or unauthorised offshore retention will trigger SAFE administrative penalties and suspension of foreign exchange business.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) recognised only as the supervisory authority for the Taiwan-source remittance leg and not for the onshore China-resident WFOE

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Not viable as a primary or secondary settlement rail for the China-resident WFOE. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Airwallex, or similar neo-banks are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to open RMB or foreign exchange settlement accounts for a China-domiciled legal entity and cannot perform SAFE registrations, capital verification, withholding tax reporting, or trade monitoring matching. Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Control Regulations and the Measures for the Administration of Internet Payment Services, all RMB receipts and payments for a WFOE must flow through an onshore bank with a SAFE-designated reporting mechanism. An offshore EMI may be used only for internal treasury dashboards or expense tracking in the Taiwan parent, never for settling WFOE payables or receivables; any use in the WFOE's bank statement would be treated as an illegal foreign exchange platform and trigger account freezing under AML enforcement rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan shareholder must remit subscribed capital via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's SAFE-designated onshore FDI capital account at the chosen Tier-1 bank, using the SAFE foreign exchange receipt purpose code for foreign direct investment equity injection in a foreign-invested enterprise. Prior to remittance, the WFOE must complete the Foreign Investment Information Report through the national investment information system, and the bank must submit the FDI foreign exchange registration through SAFE's digital platform. Upon receipt, the bank issues a transaction certificate; a licensed PRC certified public accountant then performs capital verification and issues a paid-in capital report. The capital may be settled into RMB under the capital settlement account only for approved business purposes; the bank will enforce the negative list and monitor for prohibited secondary investments, intra-group lending, or speculative RMB settlement. All related party advices, board resolutions, and underwriting documents must be preserved to evidence the commercial substance of the equity injection.Outbound film licensing payments to overseas rights holders, production partners, or collection agencies should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for direct current-account disbursements, with the payment instruction carrying the applicable SAFE purpose code for copyright royalties and license fees, the contract reference, invoice, and non-resident tax withholding vouchers. Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under Taiwan's Business Entity Accounting Act (Article 29) as implemented by the MOEA, a privately held WFOE must obtain an annual financial statement audit (certification) by a licensed Taiwanese Certified Public Accountant (CPA) if any one of the following quantitative thresholds is met at the end of the fiscal year: (i) paid-in capital is TWD 30 million or more (approximately USD 1,000,000); (ii) total operating revenue (sales) for the year reaches TWD 100 million or more (approximately USD 3,333,333); (iii) the company has 100 or more employees at any time during the fiscal year; (iv) outstanding bank loans, credit facilities, or guarantees exceed TWD 30 million (approximately USD 1,000,000); or (v) the company is designated as a large-volume business by the competent authority. In addition, a CPA audit is mandatory if the company is a public company, a financial institution, or a representative office of a foreign company in a particularly regulated sector. For a film copyright distribution WFOE, these thresholds are the only statutory triggers; a lower revenue operation below these thresholds is not legally required to undergo an annual independent audit, although the WFOE may still be required to file audited financial statements with its foreign parent for consolidation purposes, and the MOEA can order an audit in an exceptional supervisory situation.

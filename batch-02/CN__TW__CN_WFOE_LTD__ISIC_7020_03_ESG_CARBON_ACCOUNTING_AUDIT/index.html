Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Under the PRC Foreign Investment Law and Company Law, no statutory minimum registered capital applies to an ISIC 7020 advisory WFOE. However, the total registered capital stated in the Articles of Association must be fully paid-in within five years from incorporation. There is no blocked escrow account requirement; the subscribed capital must be remitted to a dedicated capital-contribution bank account opened in the WFOE's name, and funds may not be withdrawn before they are properly recorded in the statutory books and reflected in the foreign investment information report.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan operates a generally convertible foreign-exchange regime. Current-account transactions, including payment for services, trade settlements, royalties, interest, dividends, and ordinary operating expenses, are generally permitted through authorized foreign-exchange banks subject to documentary evidence, anti-money-laundering screening, reporting, and transaction-purpose classification. Capital-account transactions, including foreign investment, acquisition of equity, intercompany loans, capital reductions, liquidation remittances, and certain securities or real-estate transactions, may require prior approval, registration, or post-transaction reporting to the competent authority. A Taiwan entity may generally remit dividends, interest, service fees, royalties, and liquidation proceeds after satisfaction of corporate approvals, withholding-tax obligations, audited or otherwise supportable financial records, and any applicable foreign-investment or banking documentation. The submitted identifier describes a China WFOE but specifies Taiwan as the jurisdiction. A PRC WFOE cannot be established under Taiwan company law; the legal form must be reconciled before account opening. If the intended entity is a PRC WFOE, PRC SAFE, PBOC, SAMR, tax, and banking rules apply instead and this Taiwan blueprint is not sufficient.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan does not generally impose the PRC-style mandatory surrender of export proceeds at a prescribed percentage or a universal short statutory conversion deadline. Export proceeds may generally be received and retained in foreign-currency accounts with an authorized foreign-exchange bank, subject to applicable reporting, supporting invoices, customs or shipping evidence, and bank transaction classification. Conversion into New Taiwan dollars is normally conducted when commercially required or requested by the account holder. Banks may require evidence of the underlying export or service transaction, reconciliation of outstanding receivables, and explanations for delayed, third-party, split, or unusual receipts. Any applicable foreign-exchange declaration or reporting threshold, and the bank's documentary requirements, must be confirmed transaction-by-transaction because thresholds and procedures may change. This conclusion does not apply to a PRC entity, for which PRC foreign-exchange collection, settlement, and foreign-currency retention rules must be separately analyzed.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan), together with the Financial Supervisory Commission and the Ministry of Economic Affairs Investment Review Administration for foreign-investment, banking, and foreign-exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and operational disbursement; low for replacing a regulated Taiwan bank account. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment service provider cannot normally provide a Taiwan corporate deposit account, hold customer funds in Taiwan, execute regulated foreign-exchange services, or operate a local payment business without the required Taiwan licensing, registration, local partner, and safeguarding arrangements. Offshore providers may be used for limited vendor or customer payments only after confirming licensing perimeter, sanctions controls, beneficial-owner transparency, payment-purpose evidence, data-transfer requirements, and the receiving bank's acceptance policy. They should not be used for capital injection, statutory tax payments, dividend remittance, or material related-party flows without bank and regulatory confirmation.

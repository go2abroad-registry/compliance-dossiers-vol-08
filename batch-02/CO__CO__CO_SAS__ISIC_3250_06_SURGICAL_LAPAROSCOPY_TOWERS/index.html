Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – official web portal: https://www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a Colombian S.A.S. must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) from DIAN before commencing local activities. For non-resident entities with no permanent establishment, a NIT can be obtained through a legal representative appointed for this purpose. Registration is performed electronically via DIAN’s Muisca system, requiring notarized incorporation documents (certificate of existence and legal representation), proof of address, and identification of the legal representative. After NIT issuance, the entity must register in the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) and may need to obtain a digital signature certificate for electronic invoicing (factura electrónica) and customs operations. The RUT must be updated whenever there is a change in address, activity codes, or responsible tax regime (e.g., VAT, ICA, withholding agent status).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not operate a check-the-box or full pass-through election mechanism similar to the U.S. LLC regime. The S.A.S. is a separate taxable entity subject to corporate income tax (Impuesto sobre la Renta). However, the Colombian tax regime permits the following elections: (1) Income tax consolidation for business groups under Article 254 of the Tax Code, subject to DIAN authorization and 50% common ownership continuity; (2) Special economic sector tax regimes (e.g., free trade zones or Creative Economy VAT exemption elections); (3) Election between the special corporate income tax rate for solidarity societies/cooperatives (20%) and the general rate (35%), if applicable; (4) Optional quarterly or annual advance payment mechanisms for certain taxpayers. Hybrid mismatch rules follow BEPS Action 2 and can restrict deductibility of interest or royalty payments, but no formal election is provided to alter statutory classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic turnover threshold; obligatory VAT registration (RUT updated) for any person or entity selling taxable goods or services, including importers and exporters. The DIAN has simplified regimes for small taxpayers with gross assets or income thresholds, but exporters must register in the full responsible regime regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Colombian VAT law (Estatuto Tributario, Articles 481 and 481-472) allows zero-rating (0% VAT) for exports of goods, including medical devices such as endoscopic towers and rigid endoscopes. Eligibility requires that the goods be physically exported and sold to a foreign purchaser, leaving Colombian customs territory. Services sold to foreign recipients that are used exclusively abroad may also be zero-rated if they meet territorial and technical export criteria. The taxpayer must hold an active RUT status as an exporter, and the sale must be evidenced by valid customs export documentation proving the goods passed through customs control. No VAT is charged on the invoice; export VAT credits from purchases may be refunded or offset by DIAN.

  • Formulario de Declaración de Exportación (DEX) / Export Declaration via SICE
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (shipping document)
  • Commercial Invoice Proforma / Final Invoice indicating export and HS code 9018.90 (endoscopic surgical instruments)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Remesa) or bank transfer proof of foreign currency payment
  • Lista de empaque (Packing List) and certificate of origin if applicable
  • Registered RUT schedule for export operations

Colombia mandates full real-time electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta) through DIAN-authorized technology providers. Invoices must be transmitted to DIAN’s validation and clearance platform for authorization before delivery to the acquirer. Invoicing systems must integrate with DIAN’s APIs or use certified software. For exports, the electronic export representation is supported by the SICE (Sistema Informático de Comercio Exterior) for customs declarations. Specific requirements include electronic invoice XML schemas, CUFE/CUDE digital stamping, and humidity/signature verification. Taxpayers must also issue electronic supporting documents (documento soporte en adquisiciones a no responsables). Failure to comply with electronic invoicing mandates results in fines and possible prohibition of export operations.

DIAN has extensive audit powers over zero-rated exports. If export operations are not fully substantiated, or if the goods are diverted back to the local market, DIAN can retrospectively reverse the zero-rating, assess unpaid VAT plus default interest, and impose penalties up to 200% of the omitted tax under Article 648 of the Tax Code. Fraudulent claims may trigger criminal tax evasion proceedings (penalties of imprisonment for up to 9 years under Law 1778/2016 and Article 434 of the Criminal Code). Additionally, exporters must repatriate foreign currency within 6 months (180 days) to maintain export incentives; failure to repatriate can lead to loss of the zero-rating status, forced inclusion of gross revenue as taxable income, and fines under foreign exchange regulation (Estatuto Cambiario).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a free-floating, unified foreign-exchange regime (Régimen Cambiario) under which all resident cross-border currency transactions must be channeled through authorized intermediaries of the exchange market (IMCs). Current-account items, including import payments, freight, royalties, technical assistance and profit/dividend remittances, are fully convertible subject to presentation of the underlying commercial documentation and electronic exchange declaration. Capital-account items, including foreign direct investment (FDI) equity injections and external credits, are equally permissible but require mandatory registration with Banco de la República through the IMC (Certificado de Registro de Inversiones Internacionales). Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted provided the FDI registration remains current, the corporate dividend minutes are executed, and the payment flows through an IMC under the applicable dividend concept code; statutory non-resident dividend withholding must be applied by the Colombian payer. The COP has no dual-rate system, no prior-import-license exchange gate, and no administrative convertibility permit for the targeted surgical-laparoscopy-tower business; legal convertibility risk is concentrated in documentary compliance rather than in availability of foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for goods must be channeled, at 100% of the invoiced value, through an authorized IMC within a maximum statutory collection term of 18 months from the date of the customs export declaration (DEX), extendable to 24 months only upon express prior authorization from Banco de la República. Upon receipt, the foreign currency must enter Colombia via the IMC, where it may be credited to a registered foreign-currency account or converted to COP at the IMC spot rate; conversion to COP is not in all cases legally mandatory, but the proceeds must remain within the IMC reporting perimeter or within a pre-registered foreign-currency cuenta de compensación abroad until the export obligation is closed in the Declaración de Cambio Form 3. If the repatriation term is missed, the exporter must purchase the equivalent foreign currency in the local market to regularize the operation and settle the foreign-exchange infringement penalty administrated by DIAN and Banco de la República. The sales contract for each laparoscopy tower shipment must evidence the 0-18 month payment term, and the INVIMA export certificate and DEX customs filing must accompany the banking file to avoid misclassification of proceeds as portfolio or non-trade income.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia) as monetary, exchange-control and international investment registration authority, with coordinated enforcement by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) for supervised banking intermediaries and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) for customs and exchange-infringement sanctions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail and strictly ancillary as a wallet/payment rail. Neither domestic fintech e-money institutions (e.g., Nequi, Bancolombia digital wallet) nor offshore EMIs (Wise, Mercury, Dukascopy, Airwallex) qualify as authorized intermediaries of the Colombian exchange market (IMC). Consequently, they cannot legally receive or settle inbound FDI equity, export proceeds, or documentary trade collections for the Colombian SAS. Channeling such flows through a non-IMC digital bank would constitute a foreign-exchange violation (infracción cambiaria) enforceable by DIAN and Banco de la República, with fines calculated on the transaction amount. Offshore EMIs are viable only for minor operational disbursements (SaaS subscriptions, digital advertising, travel, and small logistics costs) that do not require an exchange declaration and are fully recorded in the Colombian accounting books; this corridor should never carry invoiced trade payments or capital movements.

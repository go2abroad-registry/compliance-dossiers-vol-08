Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under current Taiwan law there is no statutory minimum paid-in capital for an ordinary limited company; nonetheless, for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise, the approved investment capital must be remitted into a Taiwan bank account through foreign exchange settlement and verified by a licensed Taiwanese certified public accountant before final company registration. No separate blocked escrow account is required for a pure digital-services WFOE, but the registered capital must be commensurate with the operational needs described in the approved business plan, and the CPA verification report will confirm receipt before the registry issues the incorporation certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the PRC WFOE carrying on business in Taiwan is treated as a profit-seeking enterprise taxpayer under the Income Tax Act; its Taiwan-branch profits are taxed at the entity level and distributions are subject to withholding.

Available election forms

None. Taiwan has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election; the PRC WFOE, if subject to Taiwan income tax through a branch or as a profit-seeking enterprise, is mandatorily taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General taxpayers with a Taiwan place of business: registration is mandatory before business commences without a monetary exemption threshold. Foreign cross-border digital-service providers without a Taiwan fixed establishment: compulsory registration once annual Taiwan-source consideration from digital or electronic services exceeds TWD 480,000 (per Ministry of Finance cross-border e-commerce registration guidance).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, 0% rate applies to: (a) goods actually exported from Taiwan customs territory, evidenced by a customs export declaration and transport documents; (b) services supplied to foreign purchasers for use outside Taiwan and not consumed in Taiwan, provided foreign-currency consideration is received and repatriated/settled through Taiwan banks; and (c) services directly connected with exported goods or qualifying offshore digital advertising/lead-generation services. Exporters must substantiate shipment, foreign-purchaser receipt or offshore use, and repatriation of proceeds; failing that, the supply is reclassified at 5% and input VAT refund claims are denied.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed with the Customs Administration of the Ministry of Finance and stamped with customs release
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the exporter as shipper
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Settlement / Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Service export documentation for non-goods transactions including a signed offshore service agreement, foreign buyer registration/identity certificate, and a written statement or use certificate confirming the service is consumed outside Taiwan

Taiwan requires Uniform Invoices or e-Invoices for sales of goods and services to final consumers. e-Invoice data must be transmitted through the MOF E-Invoice Platform or an approved e-Invoice Value-Added Center using API integration, and stored for tax audit. Each e-Invoice carries a Uniform Invoice number-branch-random code embedded in a QR code as fiscal anti-fraud verification; businesses must not issue unregistered or tampered invoices. Foreign e-commerce operators are required to adopt cloud e-Invoice issuance for transactions with Taiwan individuals.

The Ministry of Finance may retroactively reassess an improperly zero-rated transaction as a 5% taxable domestic supply, recover the underpaid tax and attributable interest, and impose an administrative penalty of up to five times the tax evaded under the Business Tax Act. If fraudulent export documentation or false customs declarations are used to obtain 0% rate, responsible persons face criminal tax-evasion liability. The statutory retroactive assessment period is seven years from the due date of the underlying tax, and the exporter carries the burden of producing the customs, transport, and FX repatriation documents required for zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is largely convertible on current account for trade in services and goods, provided there is a genuine underlying transaction and supporting documentation such as contracts, invoices and bank records. Capital-account convertibility is controlled: the Taiwan investor's equity contribution must be registered with SAFE before remittance and channeled into a dedicated FDI foreign-currency capital account. After receiving the capital injection, the WFOE may settle the foreign exchange into RMB within negative-list operating purposes and its declared operating expense scope, but cannot deploy proceeds into prohibited sectors or speculative financial products. Profit remittance to Taiwan requires a tax-completion electronic certificate, audited statutory financial statements, a board resolution declaring the dividend, and proof that corporate income tax and withholding tax obligations have been discharged; any irregularity in tax, annual inspections or beneficial ownership documentation can block the dividend wire. Maintenance of the RMB exchange rate is market-led, yet cross-strait TWD/RMB payments are usually cleared via USD as an intermediary, so large capital-account conversions may take extra banking days for counterparty confirmation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, proceeds must be repatriated to a designated onshore bank within 180 days from the customs export declaration date and reported in SAFE's trade monitoring system. For service exports such as affiliate media buying and digital advertising under ISIC 7310, the bank will enforce an analogous 180-day receipt benchmark from the invoice or service completion date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may keep 100% of export receipts in a foreign-currency settlement account or convert to RMB at the bank's market rate, subject to the bank's annual trade-limit quota and post-event verification for non-payment beyond the statutory timeline.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for all onshore foreign-currency, RMB convertibility and BOP reporting matters. The Taiwan parent entity must remit through its Taiwan bank as a non-resident investor, and the mainland WFOE is treated as a resident enterprise subject to SAFE capital and current account controls.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan parent will execute a SWIFT MT103 credit transfer to the WFOE's FDI foreign-currency capital account at the designated Chinese bank. The remittance message must clearly state INVESTMENT/FDI CAPITAL INJECTION and include the SAFE FDI record number if required by the receiving bank. The receiving bank will perform BOPS reporting under the capital-account direct investment purpose code for FDI, with the exact SAFE transaction code confirmed at time of booking because the code table is updated by SAFE. After funds arrival, the WFOE must submit the capital verification report prepared by a licensed PRC CPA firm. The bank will then allow conversion into RMB only for operating expenditures, staff costs, rent, procurement of ad inventory from onshore providers, and other positive-list items; equity funds cannot be used for prohibited sectors or non-operating investments.For payments to overseas media platforms, fairness programs and affiliate networks, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 from its onshore bank with the service-trade BOPS purpose-code classification for advertising/media services. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are not operationally suitable for digital advertising because there are no transport documents; they would create unnecessary rigidity. For low-value, high-frequency programmatic media purchases, the entity may also use SAFE-regulated cross-border payment service providers or the bank's CIPS RMB direct payment channel when the foreign counterparty can accept RMB. Each disbursement requires a copy of the invoice, the executed media plan, proof of service performance, and evidence of tax withholding or tax clearance if the foreign counterparty is deemed to earn China-sourced income.Onshore deliverable RMB/USD forward contracts and FX swaps for documented current-account exposure such as media buying and service fees, subject to real-transaction background, SAFE net-qualified bank confirmation and maximum tenor restrictions generally up to 12 months.Plain-vanilla FX options and combination option/forward structures with a licensed onshore bank are permissible for hedging non-trade service receipts and payment mismatch, but cannot be used for speculative triangular trading.Cross-currency swaps between RMB and USD are acceptable when hedging foreign-debt repayment, capital injection remittance obligations or future dividend distribution, provided the underlying exposure is evidenced and the swap is registered in the bank's derivatives records under SAFE's real need principle.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Not viable as a primary onshore treasury rail. Wise, Mercury, Airwallex or other offshore fintech EMIs are not licensed by the PBoC/CBIRC to hold RMB or foreign-currency deposits for a mainland WFOE, cannot register FDI capital inflows, cannot complete SAFE BOP reporting, and cannot serve as the export-proceeds collection bank. The only permissible ancillary use is as an offshore collection/payout wallet for non-China media buying expenses, provided all Chinese-source revenue is repatriated through a licensed onshore bank. Relying on an EMI for main corporate cash flows would breach SAFE's real trade background rules and create a serious KYC/AML deficiency.

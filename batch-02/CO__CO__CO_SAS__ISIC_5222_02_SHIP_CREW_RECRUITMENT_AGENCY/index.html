Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - Official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; transactional platform: https://muisca.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity operating through a Colombian S.A.S. must obtain a Colombian NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and register in the RUT (Registro Único Tributario). Procedure: (1) incorporate the S.A.S. before the local Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio); (2) appoint a legal representative resident in Colombia and obtain a corporate digital certificate/password; (3) apply for RUT inscription with DIAN either electronically through MUISCA or through a duly empowered attorney; (4) DIAN assigns the NIT and issues the RUT certificate; (5) request electronic invoicing authorization via DIAN by notifying the billing software or adopting the free DIAN billing system; (6) file an annual sworn statement of required information and designate a fiscal domicile for VAT and tax notifications.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Colombian S.A.S. is a taxable legal entity subject to corporate-level taxation on worldwide income (standard 35% rate for income tax; dividends remitted to non-residents subject to additional withholding). It is treated as a Colombia tax resident because it is incorporated and effectively domiciled in Colombia; no partnership or pass-through classification is available. — A foreign entity operating through a Colombian S.A.S. must obtain a Colombian NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and register in the RUT (Registro Único Tributario). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Colombian S.A.S. is a taxable legal entity subject to corporate-level taxation on worldwide income (standard 35% rate for income tax; dividends remitted to non-residents subject to additional withholding). It is treated as a Colombia tax resident because it is incorporated and effectively domiciled in Colombia; no partnership or pass-through classification is available.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists for S.A.S. under Colombian law. Statutory elections available include: (a) election to file consolidated income tax returns for qualifying economic groups (grupos empresariales) under Law 1819/2016 and Decree 1625/2016, subject to DIAN registration and 95% common control; (b) the Simplified Tax Regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación - SIMPLE) for small taxpayers with annual gross income at or below 3,500 UVT, which replaces and integrates income tax and VAT compliance; (c) for foreign investors, election to treat a Colombian permanent establishment (branch) as a separate taxpayer under the branch taxation regime; and (d) election to apply the participation exemption (holder exemption) to exempt 95% of qualifying domestic dividends from source taxation upon compliance with substance requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Colombia does not establish a general economic turnover exemption for VAT registration; liability arises automatically for any person habitually performing taxable activities in Colombia, regardless of income volume, with a RUT registration duty from the first taxable act. For the simplified small-taxpayer regime, annual gross income must not exceed 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 164.7 million for 2024, at 1 UVT = COP 47,065), which permits a coupled VAT-income-tax filing through SIMPLE.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT (0%) applies to exports of goods and to qualifying export services rendered to non-resident parties, including maritime crew recruitment and vessel management services, when the service is physically delivered and consumed outside Colombian territory, the supplier holds a valid NIT and is registered in the RUT as an exporter, and the transaction is evidenced by commercial invoices, transport or delivery records, and foreign currency collection through the Colombian exchange market. Export documentation must demonstrate actual exit from Colombian customs territory or, in the case of services, that the recipient is abroad, the payment is made in hard currency, and no benefit or use occurs within Colombia.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) transmitted to DIAN and accepted per Colombian Customs Regulations
  • Commercial invoice (texto totalmente claro) identifying the exporter, importer, product description, amount, and zero-rated VAT indication
  • Bill of Lading or other transport document evidencing dispatch from Colombian port/airport and physical exit of the goods
  • Packing list and freight/cargo manifest (when applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro Cambiario) or sworn bank statement issued by the Colombian commercial bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • RUT certificate and exporter authorization issued by DIAN

Electronic invoicing is mandatory for all Colombian VAT taxpayers. The invoice must be issued through DIAN-authorized software or the free DIAN system and transmitted in real time (or within DIAN-prescribed deadlines) in XML format, accompanied by the electronic invoice validation code (CUDS - Código Único de Facturación de Validación Electrónica DIAN), a digital signature certificate, and the seller's RUT validity verification. DIAN performs automatic validation before purchasers may receive the invoice as a deductible/cost instrument. Transactional information, software and hardware traceability records, and invoice batches must be accessible to DIAN, and electronic invoice authorization or the entire invoicing system may be suspended or penalized for noncompliance.

DIAN may retroactively disallow zero-rating refunds and VAT credits when export documentation is incomplete, the export declaration differs from actual exit data, proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day statutory deadline, or underlying services are considered used in Colombia. Tax clawbacks carry default interest (current lending rate plus three percentage points), and fines may reach 160% of the tax evaded, plus a late-filing/sanction penalty of up to COP 20 million or more. False or fraudulent export declarations used to obtain VAT refunds or warrants for found guilty of unjustified tax benefit, criminal liability may attach under Article 402 of the Colombian Criminal Code on fraud against tax administration, including imprisonment and monetary sanctions. Supporting records must be preserved for at least six years from the export declaration date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current-account transactions, including maritime crew-placement and vessel-management service fees classified as service exports, are freely convertible but must be routed through an authorized exchange-market intermediary (IMC). Capital-account operations (equity injections, shareholder loans) do not require prior FX approval but mandate channeling through an IMC and registration with Banco de la República as a precondition for lawful dividend or capital repatriation. Profit-remittance hurdles: verified payment of 35% corporate income tax, dividend withholding at source under E.T. Articles 242/245, registration of the underlying foreign investment, and submission of Declaración de Cambio Formulario No. 1 at each remittance via the IMC. Unregistered foreign investment renders remittances illicit and subject to exchange penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds derived from services (crewing fees, management fees) must be channeled to Colombia through an IMC within six (6) months from invoice or service-completion date where the individual transaction exceeds USD 10,000 or the monthly aggregate exceeds USD 10,000. The channeling obligation applies to 100% of the invoiced export value; no partial surrender ratio exists. Proceeds may be retained in foreign currency in a permitted FX-denominated account at the IMC without mandatory conversion to COP; repatriated foreign currency may not be left outside the Colombian financial system beyond the statutory term. Non-compliance triggers exchange fines (multas cambiarias) imposed by the Banco de la República and DIAN, and may render future FX authorizations unavailable.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia as supervisory arm; foreign-exchange regime (Régimen de Cambios Internacionales) codified under Ley 9 de 1991, Decreto 1735 de 1993 and Resolución 2016006416 (Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Offshore e-money institutions (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not registered IMCs under Colombia's exchange regime; they cannot lawfully intermediate receipt of export proceeds and their use for crew-placement fee collection would breach the mandatory channeling obligation. Such EMIs are tolerable only for ancillary outbound disbursements (e.g., SaaS subscriptions, marketing tools) and must never aggregate seafarer payroll funding or shipowner receivables. Any persistent lateral flows between an offshore EMI and a Colombian IMC account will trigger enhanced due diligence and potential account suspension.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections from non-resident shareholders must be sent via SWIFT MT103 to the S.A.S. account at a Colombian IMC with a precise payment purpose ('Aporte de capital social en moneda extranjera'), full investor identification, and simultaneous registration in the Banco de la República foreign-investment registry through the IMC (Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional). Registration is a condition precedent for future dividend remittances or capital reimbursement; the IMC issues the exchange registration certificate evidencing valid foreign investment status. Shareholder loans must be documented with loan agreements and submitted under the respective foreign-debt concept code to avoid recharacterization as taxable equity.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for seafarer wage payments and overseas sub-agent fees in USD, EUR, or GBP; COP-denominated payroll is cleared through the local ACH platform (CENIT) with same-day or next-day value. Bulk multi-currency payroll is executed via the IMC's corporate payroll module with an itemized MT103 batch for each beneficiary. Commercial letters of credit (UCP600) are used only for procurement of vessel stores, safety equipment, or insured supplies, and must be issued by the same IMC to maintain a clean documentary audit trail. All outbound transfers require an associated exchange declaration voucher (Declaración de Cambio Formulario No. 1 or supporting form) to evidence permissible FX purpose.Deliverable FX forwards on USD/COP and EUR/COP executed through an IMC, subject to documentary proof of underlying crew-payroll or crewing-fee exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/COP permitted only where the IMC quotes an offshore-style non-deliverable structure and the S.A.S. demonstrates a genuine hedging need; onshore deliverable forwards are preferred for compliance simplicity.FX swaps (spot/forward combinations and overnight rollover swaps) used to manage liquidity timing mismatches between shipowner receivables and multi-currency payroll runs.Vanilla FX options and zero-cost collars for protecting the S.A.S. operating margin against adverse COP revaluation; cross-currency swaps are admissible only when matched to a specific long-term asset or liability exposure and documented under ISDA or the IMC's master derivatives agreement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident beneficiaries where the profits out of which the dividend is paid have been taxed at corporate level. Dividends paid out of untaxed (exempt) profits are subject to an additional complementary income tax assessment of up to 35% on the shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before the exact date set by DIAN's annual taxpayer calendar, typically between the first week of April and the last week of June following the fiscal year-end. The date is determined by the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (Basic) and Master File (if group revenue exceeds USD 50 million) are mandatory when the Colombian taxpayer's gross income or total assets exceed approximately COP 5.4 billion (≈ USD 1.3 million, equivalent to 135,000 UVT for fiscal 2024) and aggregate related-party cross-border transactions surpass approximately COP 120 million (≈ USD 27,000, corresponding to 3,000 UVT). These thresholds are annually adjusted by UVT.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Nevertheless, transfer pricing adjustments, GAAR and substance-over-form doctrines require the S.A.S. to evidence real management and control—actual office, employed staff, payroll and account management—in Colombia. A pure 'shell' or 'letterbox' entity is disregardable; its intercompany transactions may be recharacterised, and treaty benefits denied on beneficial ownership grounds.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty equal to 100% of the evaded tax plus default interest; criminal prosecution under Article 339A of the Colombian Criminal Code carries imprisonment of 5 to 15 years for fraudulent evasion exceeding 2,500 UVT.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fraudulent returns or direct the concealment are jointly, severally and personally liable for the full tax debt; they face criminal conviction, asset freezing/seizure, arrest warrants, travel bans, and suspension of commercial or administrative rights for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the legal form, reattribute profits to the true economic operator, impose a 160% penalty on the understated tax attributable to aggressive planning, and cancel the NIT; additional OECD BEPS Action 6 penalties for treaty abuse may be asserted.

Director personal liability: Colombian court jurisprudence permits piercing the corporate veil when the entity is used as a tool for fraud; directors may be condemned to repay the entire tax debt, disqualified from managing companies, and criminally sanctioned if the shell structure facilitates money laundering.
AML & Sanctions Screening

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanctions under Law 1766 of 2020 and regulatory decrees: fines of up to COP 1.2 billion for corporate AML breaches plus license revocation by the Ministry of Transportation (maritime crew agencies). Money laundering (Criminal Code Art. 323) carries 10 to 20 years of prison and fines up to 50,000 minimum monthly wages.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally and criminally liable if they knew or should have known of unauthorised wire transfers. Criminal penalties include 10–20 years' imprisonment, personal financial liability, travel limitations and forfeiture of assets.
Withholding Tax Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: A fine equal to 100% of the sum not withheld, plus default interest; each late month adds a 1.5% penalty. The tax agent (the S.A.S.) remains liable to DIAN for the unrecovered sum. Repeated violations may also block future dividend remittances from the Central Bank exchange system.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives are personally liable as retention agents; where omission is intentional, the director may be charged with criminal retention misappropriation (Art. 402 Criminal Code), punishable by up to 9 years in prison, disqualification from holding public or corporate office, and travel bans.

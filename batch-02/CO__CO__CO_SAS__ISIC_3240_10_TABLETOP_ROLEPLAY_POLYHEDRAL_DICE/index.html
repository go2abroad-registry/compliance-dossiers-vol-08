Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – official national tax and customs authority of Colombia; taxpayer portal: DIAN MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) and DIAN website at www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or its local legal representative must first obtain a provisional tax identification number (NIT provisional) from DIAN by submitting the foreign company incorporation documents, the duly executed appointment of an attorney-in-fact (apoderado) resident in Colombia, and the completed official registration forms (Registro Único Tributario – RUT) online through MUISCA. After legalization of the local branch or subsidiary (SAS) before the Chamber of Commerce and notarization of bylaws, the company obtains its definitive NIT and updates the RUT with the assigned tax classification and address. The procedure requires an approved digital certificate or authorized power of attorney, and the DIAN issues the RUT containing the NIT, VAT registration status, ICA registration status, and applicable tax regime codes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not have a general check-the-box regime mirroring the U.S. A Colombian SAS is by default a corporation taxed at entity level under the Colombian income tax. No transparent entity election is available for a domestic SAS; however, taxpayers may elect to apply the special tax consolidation regime for groups (with prior authorization and meeting economic substance requirements). Hybrid mismatch rules under Colombian transfer pricing and CFC legislation may require aligning the local entity classification with the foreign tax classification of the same entity; mandatory disclosure or re-characterization may apply in cross-border payments subject to the OECD BEPS Action 2 recommendations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

COP 0 (zero peso threshold for legal entities). Any person or entity engaged in selling goods or providing services subject to VAT, or importing goods, must register in the RUT and obtain a VAT responsibility code even if activities are occasional; however, small merchants under the SIMPLE regime may have an optional regime threshold of up to COP 3,500,000,000 in gross revenue for the SIMPLE taxation system; for standard VAT purposes there is no monetary exemption threshold for entities created as SAS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and qualifying export services (including handcrafted goods sold abroad) are eligible for zero rating (excluded from VAT) under Colombian law. Eligibility requires that the exporter be a registered taxpayer, that the goods physically leave the country, that the sale is made to a foreign purchaser, and that the exporter obtains and preserves evidence of the export. For services, zero rating applies to services rendered in Colombia to non-residents that are used exclusively abroad or to services related to export goods; supporting documentation must demonstrate the foreign beneficiary and the off-shore use or exploitation. The exporter must complete the export process through the customs system and present the proofs to DIAN.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DEX) accepted by DIAN and the customs authority
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or other transport document proving physical departure from Colombian territory)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Compensación de Divisas or bank confirmation evidencing the export proceeds were repatriated to Colombia through authorized foreign exchange channels)

DIAN mandates electronic invoicing (factura electrónica) for all VAT-registered taxpayers. The system requires issuance of electronic invoices using authorized software or DIAN's free solution, transmission of the invoice XML to DIAN for validation and acceptance, assignment of a unique CUFE/CUDS code, and electronic submission of the validation events. Since 2021, taxpayers must transmit the electronic invoice in real time (at the moment of issuance) to the DIAN using web services under the electronic invoicing technical annex; there is no physical digital stamping, but the DIAN assigns an electronic validation code and records the invoice in its central system. A digital signature certificate is required, and non-compliance triggers penalties.

DIAN may deny VAT zero rating or the VAT refund claim if the documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or does not prove the actual export and the effective exchange repatriation. The deadline for repatriating export proceeds is generally 180 days from the export date (subject to extensions by the Central Bank). Failure to repatriate within the deadline may result in recomputation of the VAT and application of the general VAT rate, plus interest and a penalty equal to up to 200% of the omitted tax in cases of fraudulent or fictitious exports. False claims for VAT refunds can trigger criminal tax fraud charges, fines equivalent to 100%-200% of the refunded amount, retroactive clawbacks up to the last five taxable years, and disqualification from refund programs.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) operates under a managed floating regime with full current-account convertibility for goods, services, and ordinary transfers, provided transactions are channelled through authorized exchange market intermediaries. Capital-account operations, notably foreign direct investment, are freely permitted but registration with BANREP, normally effected via the receiving bank, is a statutory precondition for the subsequent remittance of dividends and repatriation of capital. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are not subject to exchange-licensing but require the FDI registration to be in force and an exchange declaration to accompany the outward transfer. No quantitative limits apply to current-account settlements, yet all flows must be executed through the formal exchange market at the Tasa Representativa del Mercado (TRM).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of physical goods must channel all export proceeds into the Colombian exchange market through an authorized intermediary within six months from the date of shipment; an extension of up to three additional months may be justified to BANREP in duly supported circumstances. Each collection must be reported in the Declaración de Cambio por Exportación de Bienes prepared by the receiving bank as the transfer is liquidated. Since April 2024, under Decree 253 of 2024, goods exporters are additionally obliged to convert at least 20% of the foreign currency collected into COP on the formal exchange market, the remaining balance being freely held in permitted foreign-currency accounts or a registered compensation account for the balance of the channelling term. Failure to surrender or channel within the statutory term exposes the exporter to exchange-regime penalties and loss of export incentives.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (BANREP) is the sole monetary and exchange authority, empowered under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1068 of 2015 (Title 11 of the Regulatory Decree of the National Public Administration), Decree 1735 of 1993 as amended, and Circular Reglamentaria Externa DCIN-83. The exchange regime is operationally supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) over authorized intermediaries; the DIAN oversees foreign trade documentation and import/export declarations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital from the non-resident shareholder must arrive by SWIFT MT103 in USD or EUR to the SAS's local COP or USD account held at an authorized intermediary bank. The remittance must reference the foreign direct investment concept, and the receiving bank will generate the BANREP exchange declaration and the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales (RII) within the applicable statutory window, normally five business days. The dossier for the bank must include the shareholder resolution approving the capital contribution, the SAS articles of incorporation as amended, source-of-funds documentation, the wire instruction with the correct FDI purpose code, and a letter confirming the intended use of funds for working capital and raw-material procurement. The RII certificate must be retained indefinitely as it is the legal precondition for future dividend remittances and capital repatriation.International trade settlements will predominantly run through SWIFT MT103 wires from the entity's COP/USD account with same-day FX conversion at the intermediary's TRM-based rate. For raw material purchases, the bank requires the commercial invoice, packing list, and, where applicable, the import customs declaration submitted through DIAN; banks may accept an open-account clean payment order for repeated small-value procurements from the same supplier. When suppliers demand security, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit can be opened at the commercial bank, with the beneficiary drawing against transport documents and an independent inspection certificate. Foreign digital EMIs, including Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, and Mercury, are not licensed to in Colombia and therefore cannot open local COP accounts, issue local IBANs, or act as authorized intermediaries of the exchange market; balances parked in such EMIs are treated for exchange purposes as funds held abroad. They may be viable as a short-term USD settlement gateway for Stripe and PayPal payouts when the proceeds are periodically repatriated into a registered compensation account or a local bank's foreign-currency segment within the six-month export channelling deadline. Local SEDPE fintechs, such as Nequi, Daviplata, and Lulo Bank, are regulated payment institutions but are not authorized exchange intermediaries and cannot satisfy the export surrender obligation on their own.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required for an S.A.S.; the incorporating shareholders may subscribe zero or nominal capital. If capital is specified, it must be fully paid within two years from incorporation. No escrow account is mandated by Colombian law.

Funding & unlock path: For a purely virtual dropshipping operation, no structural load inspection or civil defense clearance is required. If a physical office or warehouse is opened, a fire safety certificate (Certificado de Bomberos) and basic workplace safety registration (Sistema de Gestión de Seguridad y Salud en el Trabajo) must be obtained from the relevant municipal authority and ARL insurer.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is required for an S.A.S.; the incorporating shareholders may subscribe zero or nominal capital. If capital is specified, it must be fully paid within two years from incorporation. No escrow account is mandated by Colombian law.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); taxpayer portals: MUISCA (https://muisca.dian.gov.co) and RUT registration at https://www.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

Domestic formation path: file articles of incorporation (public deed or private written instrument) at the Cámara de Comercio of the registered office; the S.A.S. then enrolls in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) through DIAN's MUISCA platform immediately upon commercial registration and before starting activities, obtaining the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria). Foreign-owned S.A.S. variant: appoint a legal representative domiciled in Colombia, complete the commercial registry formalities (including foreign-company or shareholder registration), execute the RUT enrolment and NIT issuance through MUISCA in the name of the entity, and activate DIAN-certified electronic invoicing before issuing the first invoice. The NIT is the unified number for income tax, VAT, customs operations, e-invoicing, and payroll.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia has no check-the-box regime; every S.A.S. is an entity-level CIT taxpayer (ordinary regime at 35% for 2025). Elections available: (1) annual elective entry into the SIMPLE regime (Articles 903-916 ET, Ley 1943/2018 as amended by Ley 2010/2019) for entities with gross ordinary income ≤ 80,000 UVT (approx. COP 3.95 billion for 2025) and ≤ 8 employees; SIMPLE consolidates income tax, a VAT-equivalent component, and industry-and-commerce tax into presumptive tariffs of 1.8%-14.5%, but the taxpayer then charges no VAT and cannot recover input VAT through the ordinary refund mechanics; (2) annual renunciation or withdrawal of SIMPLE with reversion to the ordinary regime; (3) group fiscal consolidation election for qualifying subordinated businesses; (4) CFC rules (Articles 596-600 ET) and hybrid-mismatch rules cannot be neutralised by election.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating exchange rate for the Colombian peso (COP). Current-account transactions, including export proceeds, import payments, and cross-border services, are freely convertible but are subject to mandatory channeling through the official exchange market (canalización obligatoria) via authorized foreign exchange intermediaries (intermediarios del mercado cambiario, IMCs). Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment, external credit, and portfolio flows, may be freely made but require registration with Banco de la República and must be settled through an IMC with the corresponding Declaración de Cambio. Profit and dividend remittances to non-residents are unrestricted when paid out of audited post-tax earnings and when the underlying foreign investment registration is current; remittance is executed through an IMC at the prevailing market rate. No mandatory conversion quotas or profit-remittance lockups apply, but unregistered or mis-channeled operations may attract sanctions and loss of exchange-market access for the offending transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters, including a Colombian SAS operating an automated dropshipping model, must collect and channel foreign currency proceeds into Colombia through the official exchange market. The applicable statutory deadlines are 13 months from the date of shipment for exports of goods and 6 months from the date of service provision or billing for exports of services. The full 100% of the export invoice value must be canalized unless the exporter holds a duly authorized compensation account (cuenta de compensación) abroad or relies on an express exemption under Decreto 1735 of 1993. Channeling is perfected by delivering the foreign currency to an IMC and filing the corresponding export declaration and Declaración de Cambio. Late or partial channeling exposes the SAS to monetary fines, possible suspension from the exchange market, and administrative sanction proceedings by Banco de la República; for dropshipping operations, gateway settlement proceeds must be periodically reconciled against underlying customer orders to evidence export provenance and avoid mischaracterization of taxable service income.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), acting with the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit and the Financial Superintendence of Colombia, administers the Colombian Foreign Exchange Control Regime under Ley 9 de 1991, Decreto 1735 de 1993, and External Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for principal treasury purposes. Non-resident digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Currencycloud) do not hold Colombian banking licenses and are not authorized to open regulated onshore COP sight accounts or serve as primary treasury vehicles for a Colombian-domiciled SAS. Their use is confined to auxiliary receivable collection or card acquiring support, and any balances must be swept to a local IMC-held account to satisfy the channeling obligation. Colombian-licensed electronic payment institutions (SEDPEs under Ley 1735 of 2014) are legally limited to prepaid, low-value, non-credit instruments and are therefore operationally unsuitable as the treasury backbone. The practical blueprint uses an IMC commercial bank account as the sole FX and liquidity hub.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions or shareholder loans must be wired by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's own foreign bank account to the SAS's IMC-held account. For equity injections, the remittance must be tagged with the proper exchange-market concept code for foreign direct investment and the IMC must receive a Declaración de Cambio identifying the transaction as a capital contribution; the investment must be registered with Banco de la República through the appropriate investment registration form within 12 months of disbursement. For shareholder loans, the external credit must be registered in the external indebtedness regime (régimen de endeudamiento externo) within the legally applicable term, and the funds must be kept segregated from customer operating collections. The compliant path includes: (i) advance verification of the SWIFT beneficiary bank and MT103 field 70 purpose description with the IMC; (ii) use of the direct-investment concept code in the Declaración de Cambio; (iii) certification by the IMC of the FX conversion and issuance of the exchange certificate for DIAN and corporate records; and (iv) refusal of commingled third-party funds to avoid misclassification under Colombian foreign-exchange and tax rules.Supplier and trade disbursements use two authorized clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for recurring low-to-mid-value supplier payments, executed by the IMC after verification against matching commercial invoices, purchase orders, and, where required, import declarations; and (ii) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit opened by the IMC under UCP 600 for high-value or first-time suppliers, typically confirmed by the supplier's bank. For service imports, including dropship platform commissions and software subscriptions, payments must be supported by a signed service agreement, an electronic invoice, and any applicable withholding tax certificate. Each outbound remittance is accompanied by a Declaración de Cambio; merchandise-related payments must be reconciled with the corresponding Customs import declaration. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% standard withholding on dividends and other profit-sharing distribution made to non-resident beneficiaries. Where the Colombian company has already paid the 35% corporate income tax, the taxpayer is eligible for a deemed 'fully-taxed profits' deduction, resulting in an effective additional withholding of 0% on that portion. Excess untaxed dividends accrue a 35% withholding taxed at the shareholder level. Thus the headline statutory rate remains 35%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due annually within the DIAN chronological filing calendar for the relevant tax year. For calendar-year companies, filing opens in the second or third month after the fiscal year-end and closes no later than mid-April of the following year, depending on the last two digits of the NIT (taxpayer identification number).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Copied: mandatory Local File and Master File arise when the Colombian S.A.S. obtains gross income greater than 215,000 UVT (approximately COP 10,500,000,000 for fiscal year 2024). For taxpayers between 125,000 and 215,000 UVT, a simplified TP Informative Return (Form 220) is required. The threshold is inflationary-adjusted annually and must be verified against the applicable fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not have a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the tax administration applies Articles 657 and 658-3 of the Tax Statute and the general anti-abuse rules (GAAR) to disregard transactions lacking business purpose. A pure-channel dropshipping S.A.S. with no employees, no office, no board meeting minutes, and no physical reception of inventory in Colombia may be recharacterized as a transparent intermediary, causing: (i) inclusion of sales revenue at the full gross level; (ii) denial of inter-company commission deductions; and (iii) classification as a 'shell entity' for anti-money laundering and tax evasion purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Inaccurate Annual Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine under Article 317 of the Colombian Tax Statute equal to 200% of the evaded tax; criminal prosecution under Law 1943 of 2018 carries a prison sentence of 48 to 108 months and a fine from four to twenty-four times the amount evaded, payable in monthly minimum wages.

Director personal liability:
Shell Company / Corporate Veil Piercing

Statutory sanction: Courts and DIAN may pray the lifting of the corporate veil under Article 111 of the Commercial Code. The legal entity is disregarded; the shareholder is directly liable for unpaid taxes and liabilities; the foreign principal may be deemed to have a permanent establishment in Colombia with its own income tax assessment at 35% plus additional penalties.

Director personal liability:
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: DIAN may impose a sanction of 4% of the value of controlled cross-border transactions for each month or fraction of non-submission, with a ceiling of 20,260 UVT (around COP 1,000 million in 2024). If documents are furnished only after a formal evidential notice, an additional fixed penalty of 2,600 UVT (about COP 126 million) applies. The transactions are re-priced to market, increasing the tax debt and liquidity fines.

Director personal liability:
AML / Payment Rails Abuse

Statutory sanction: Violation of Law 1843 of 2018 (UIAF anti-money laundering statute) can result in fines up to 1,200,000 tax value units (approx. COP 60,000 million in 2024). Criminal money laundering offences are sanctioned by imprisonment between 10 and 20 years and fines equivalent to up to 2,000,000 minimum statutory monthly wages. Assets traceable to the scheme are subject to civil forfeiture.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident shareholders do not require a local tax ID unless triggering Colombian source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – SAS is a Colombian resident corporation; ordinary income taxed at entity level; no check-the-box pass-through regime for SAS.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election; optional 'Régimen Simple de Tributación' (SIMPLES) for qualifying SMEs with turnover <= 100,000 UVT and no excluded activities; hybrid mismatch rules under Law 2010/2019 limit deduction mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No COP turnover threshold for legal entities; VAT registration (activation of IVA responsibilities in RUT) is mandatory when the SAS carries out any activity subject to IVA. (Natural persons may be non-responsible only below 3,500 UVT; not applicable to SAS.)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for IVA zero-rating when: (1) the sale is made to a non-resident customer abroad; (2) goods physically leave Colombia within the term established by customs rules; (3) exporter is registered before DIAN and in the RUT with export activities; (4) export declaration (DEX) is accepted by DIAN customs authority. Offshore services used exclusively outside Colombia may also qualify under the 'exportation of services' regime where recipient has no Colombian activity (Decree 1625/2016, Arts 1.36.5.1.1 et seq.).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DEX)
  • Bill of Lading (BL)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Canalización de Divisas)

Mandatory electronic invoicing (factura electrónica) through DIAN-authorized software or DIAN's free system; each invoice must include CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica), be transmitted in real time to DIAN via secure web services (API) and receive an acceptance/validation response before issuance to purchaser; failure to obtain DIAN acceptance invalidates the invoice for tax credit and deduction.

Export zero-rating is subject to intensive DIAN verification of the physical exit and foreign-currency repatriation. If requirements are not met within 180 days, DIAN can claw back zero-rating, assess IVA at 19% plus late interest, and impose fines under ET Arts 648 and 701 (up to 200% of the omitted tax in fraudulent cases); criminal smuggling/export fraud penalties under Law 1762/2015 may apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a controlled-convertibility regime: current-account transactions (trade in goods and services) are freely convertible through authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries; capital-account operations require prior registration with the Banco de la República Foreign Investment Registry. Profit remittances from a registered foreign investment are permitted upon presentation of the international capital registration certificate and compliance with the applicable exchange declaration. Practical hurdles include documentary formality, mandatory channeling through the foreign exchange market, and a 5% remittance tax when the statutory trigger applies (Law 2277 of 2022). The Colombian peso (COP) is convertible for settlement of all bona fide trade-related flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from goods (ISIC 3240 enamel pins) must be repatriated to Colombia through the foreign exchange market within the legally prescribed term of 6 months from the date of customs export dispatch; service-related proceeds follow the 6-month service-rendering window. Once received, proceeds must be negotiated (surrendered) via an authorized intermediary and converted into local currency (COP) unless an exemption is granted (e.g., permissible foreign-currency deposit account for recurring export flows, or offsetting under a compensation agreement). Extension requests must be filed before the statutory deadline with the Banco de la República.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia / Autoridad Cambiaria)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Offshore digital EMIs and fintech payment providers (including Etsy Direct Pay or Stripe counterparty banks) cannot legally open Colombian local treasury accounts unless domiciled and licensed as a Sociedad Especializada en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos (SEDPE) or operate through a regulated local correspondent bank. Cross-border USD settlement must route through an authorized local intermediary (tier-1 bank) that accepts foreign marketpay proceeds and performs the exchange declaration. A pure offshore EMI cannot serve as the exclusive treasury rail without violating the foreign-exchange channeling obligations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% (Impuesto sobre la Renta y Complementarios – national corporate income tax; no municipal or departmental corporate income tax applies)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% as the final withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders out of profits that have already been subject to Colombian corporate income tax at the entity level. Dividends out of profits not previously taxed at the entity level are recharacterized and subject to the ordinary 35% rate at the company level before distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year SAS, the annual Form 110 is due between 20 September and 10 October of the following year, subject to the DIAN staggered calendar keyed to the last two digits of the NIT. For a non-calendar fiscal year, the due date is established by DIAN resolution with reference to the ninth month after the year-end. No automatic extension exists; failure to abide by the assigned due date triggers late-filing penalties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) is triggered for tax year 2025 when gross income, total assets, or net equity exceeds 135,000 UVT, approximately COP 6,674,290,000, or when related-party transactions with foreign enterprises exceed 45,000 UVT, approximately COP 2,224,430,000. Master File documentation becomes mandatory for multinational groups that meet the DIAN consolidated gross revenue threshold, and Country-by-Country reporting applies only to groups with consolidated revenues above the substantially higher BEPS threshold.

Economic substance scope: Colombia has not enacted a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code comparable to offshore financial centers. Nevertheless, substantive-compliance doctrines are embedded in Colombian tax law through substance-over-form, general anti-avoidance rules, controlled-foreign-company rules, and DIAN's power to disregard legal entities lacking economic reality. A SAS producing and selling enamel lapel pins must maintain a local registered office, employees or contractors, board minutes, banking records, export invoices, and actual decision-making in Colombia to confirm tax residence and to defend deductions, treaty access, and limited liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Material False Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative inexactness penalty of 100% of the additional tax determined by DIAN, plus moratory interest at the monthly DIAN rate. If the evaded tax exceeds 100 legal monthly minimum wages, the conduct also qualifies as criminal tax evasion under the Criminal Code, carrying imprisonment of 48 to 108 months and a fine of up to 100% of the evaded amount.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced when the S.A.S. is used as a mere instrument of fraud. The managing director and legal representative who signs the false return are jointly and severally liable for the CIT debt, loss of the tax clearance certificate, judicial freezing of personal assets, an exit-delay order preventing international travel, and imprisonment for criminal evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may adjust the taxable income upward, impose the 35% tax on the adjusted amount, disallow deductions where documentation is absent, and apply a transfer-pricing fine for non-filing or defective filing of the statutory documentation. Interest and exactness penalties enlarge the assessment; intentional TP manipulation may also be prosecuted as fraudulent tax reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute or approve non-arm's-length related-party transactions are jointly liable for the tax and penalties arising from the adjustment. Recidivism in failing to file required documentation exposes the legal representative to personal fines and to criminal tax-fraud liability if the adjustment exceeds the threshold for prosecution.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may recharacterize the entity as a tax resident of the foreign shareholders' country or deny the SAS loss and deduction claims under the general anti-abuse doctrine. Treaty benefits are rejected, the constitutive-limited-liability protection is weakened, and the foreign remittance is subjected to the full Colombian withholding rates applicable to undisclosed technical assistance, royalties, or constructive dividends.

Director personal liability: The shareholders and appointed manager may be held jointly and severally liable for all unpaid taxes and penalties when lack of substance proves that the corporate form was an abuse of law. Travel bans, bank-account embargoes, and commercial disqualification apply to the officers; positive evidence of fraud converts the assessment into a criminal shell-company prosecution with imprisonment exposure.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Irregularities

Statutory sanction: Fines for non-compliance with Colombian UBO registration rules, regulatory penalties for failure to implement money-laundering prevention measures under the AML framework, possible revocation of access to regulated e-money payment rails, administrative seizure of unexplained assets, and criminal money-laundering charges where the transaction represented an attempt to hide the illegal origin or true beneficiary of funds.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil does not shelter the responsible manager in AML matters. Officers and shareholders who knowingly handle suspicious proceeds face criminal liability, fines in proportion to the laundered assets, and imprisonment of up to 30 years. The S.A.S. may be blacklisted and dissolved by judicial order when the legal entity is determined to be a vehicle for illicit financial flows.

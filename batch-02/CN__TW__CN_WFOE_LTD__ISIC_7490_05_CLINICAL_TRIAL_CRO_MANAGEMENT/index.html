Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), administered through the China National Unified Standard Electronic Tax Service Platform (electronic tax bureau); market-entry registration is coordinated with the State Administration for Market Regulation, which issues the corporate business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code; cross-border foreign-exchange flows are supervised by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

E-government filing portal

Step-by-step for a PRC Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise: (1) incorporate the WFOE at the local Administration for Market Regulation and obtain the business license with the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code; under the 'one license, one code' multi-certificate integration, that code is automatically accepted as the taxpayer identification number; (2) complete initial tax registration with the competent STA sub-branch, electing VAT status (general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer) and registering for corporate income tax, individual income tax withholding, and stamp duty rolls; (3) activate the electronic tax bureau account and the digital invoice module on the Electronic Invoice Service Platform; (4) open a RMB basic deposit account and, where needed, a foreign-currency settlement account with a domestic bank, and complete the SAFE-related registration so that Taiwan-sourced or other offshore payments may be lawfully received and settled; (5) for cross-border clinical-trial services, file the cross-border taxable service export record (zero-rating/exemption filing) with the STA, attaching the service contract, offshore-consumption statement, and economic-substance explanation; (6) maintain monthly VAT and corporate income tax declarations, digital invoice issuance, payroll withholding, and periodic stamp-duty filings thereafter.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not maintain a check-the-box election; entity classification is statutory. A WFOE nevertheless has elections that materially affect tax identity: (i) general VAT taxpayer vs small-scale taxpayer election (mandatory general status once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; voluntary general status is available and is normally elected by a service exporter to access input VAT credits and the zero-rating refund mechanism); (ii) for cross-border supplies where the catalog overlaps, election between zero-rating with input-credit refund and VAT exemption without input recovery; (iii) for hybrid or perpetual instruments, election under the perpetual-bond characterization rules to treat qualifying perpetual bonds as debt (interest deductible for CIT) or equity (distribution non-deductible), fixed for the instrument's life; (iv) election of the 100% R&D super-deduction for qualifying clinical-trial, biostatistics, and data-research expenditure; (v) limited election between actual-accounting and deemed-profit CIT quantification where books are incomplete and STA consent is granted; and (vi) under the Cross-Strait Tax Agreement, resolution of dual-residence questions through the competent-authority mutual agreement procedure, including a PE-protective position where Taiwan-side site-management or investigator activities do not create a Taiwan permanent establishment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general-VAT-taxpayer registration applies where cumulative annual taxable sales in any rolling 12-month period exceed RMB 5,000,000; CRO service revenue at 6% counts toward the threshold. Below the threshold, election of small-scale taxpayer status (3% levy, no input-VAT credit) is permitted, and a temporary 1% levy applies for qualifying small-scale sales in relief periods. Export-oriented CROs normally elect general status voluntarily even below the threshold because small-scale status bars access to input-VAT credits and to the 0% zero-rating refund mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the cross-border taxable services catalog, a clinical-trial CRO (ISIC 7490; PRC classifications of contract R&D, professional technical services, or business-support services) may claim VAT zero-rating (0% with input-credit refund) or exemption (VAT-free without input recovery), depending on the precise service character. Eligibility conditions: (1) the service recipient is an enterprise or individual located outside the PRC, including a Taiwan sponsor or a foreign pharmaceutical company's Taiwan affiliate; (2) the service is consumed entirely overseas, meaning the cross-border elements of clinical-trial management - site identification in Taiwan, investigator agreements, trial monitoring in Taiwan, biostatistics and data-management deliverables used abroad - do not confer a benefit on a PRC domestic recipient; (3) payment is received from offshore in a permitted currency and is repatriated to the WFOE's domestic bank account within the mandatory period; and (4) the WFOE holds the cross-border service record filed with the STA and issues the corresponding digital invoice. Contract research and development services used abroad, offshore outsourcing of data processing and IT-enabled services, and software or data services supplied to foreign entities qualify for zero-rating where the offshore-consumption test is met; professional-technical and certain management-consulting supplies qualify for exemption only, a distinction that must be assessed against the specific contract drafting. Services performed partly in the PRC (such as domestic data entry) remain eligible only to the extent the offshore-consumption criteria, interpreted under the STA's cross-border taxable services bulletin, are satisfied. The Cross-Strait Tax Agreement provides the residence and permanent-establishment framework for Taiwan-side activities but does not by itself create VAT zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (China International Trade Single Window) for all cross-strait shipments of trial materials, comparator drugs, biological samples, and laboratory equipment, each declared under the correct export customs regime and HS classification

China mandates issuance of Fully Digitalized E-Invoices (digital e-invoices) for VAT-relevant supplies; they must be generated exclusively through the official Electronic Invoice Service Platform accessible via the electronic tax bureau. Each digital invoice is authenticated at issuance and carries a nationally unique 20-digit invoice identification number; the platform performs real-time fiscal validation of the issuer's registration status, invoice sequence, and transaction data, then feeds the invoice into the Golden Tax IV data lake for automatic cross-matching with VAT returns, customs declarations, corporate income tax returns, and bank settlement records. No physical pre-printing or manual fiscal stamping applies in the fully digitalized regime; fiscal clearance is evidenced by the platform-issued authentication record and machine-readable QR code. Volume issuers must interconnect through government-approved API (tax-enterprise integration) channels for automated issuance, red-letter reversal, and invoice inventory control; web-based manual issuance remains possible for low-volume taxpayers. The WFOE must configure its e-invoice catalog so that cross-border clinical-trial service invoices are marked as zero-rated or exempt export supplies, and must retain the corresponding cross-border filing reference on the invoice.

Golden Tax IV performs continuous cross-system matching of digital invoices, VAT returns, customs declarations, CIT returns, and bank FX settlement data; any zero-rating claim failing the matching test is flagged for tax-office inspection. Consequences: (i) the claimed zero-rating or refund is denied, the supply is re-characterized as a domestic taxable supply (deemed domestic sale) with VAT recaptured at the applicable rate, and late-payment interest at 0.05% per day accrues; (ii) fraudulent export-refund claims trigger recovery of the full refund plus an administrative penalty of 100% to 500% of the fraudulently obtained amount under Article 66 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Levy and Collection; (iii) criminal liability arises under Article 204 of the Criminal Law for deceptive export-refund claims, carrying up to 5 years' imprisonment for smaller amounts, 5 to 10 years for large amounts, and 10 years to life imprisonment with fines or confiscation for especially large fraud; (iv) the taxpayer's credit rating is downgraded to Level D, restricting invoice quotas, bank-financing facilities, and fast-track export-refund processing for at least two years; (v) SAFE imposes administrative fines and correction orders for unrepatriated export proceeds and unreported overdue foreign-exchange collection; and (vi) because the Taiwan market often involves related clinical-trial affiliates, the tax authorities will also test economic substance, round-tripping, and cross-strait transfer pricing, with retrospective clawback available for the full 10-year limitation period in cases of fraud or concealment. SAFE and the STA monitor the 180-day repatriation deadline for export service proceeds (measured from invoice or service-completion date, with approved deferral registration available for longer commercial terms); invoices for which foreign exchange remains uncollected at the date of the refund application are excluded from the export refund claim, and overdue unbundled receipts must be corrected through a supplemental VAT return with reverse-charge consequences, interest, and administrative penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi is generally convertible for bona fide current-account transactions, including documented trade in services, subject to bank review, tax and invoicing evidence, anti-money-laundering controls, and SAFE reporting. Capital-account transactions remain regulated rather than freely convertible. Registered foreign-invested enterprises may generally use permitted foreign-exchange capital funds for approved business purposes through designated capital accounts, subject to negative-list restrictions, authenticity review, bank processing rules, and SAFE requirements. Dividend, interest, royalty, service-fee, and liquidation remittances require corporate approvals, accounting and tax support, withholding-tax compliance where applicable, and bank verification. Cross-border related-party payments are subject to transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, substance, and anti-avoidance scrutiny. Clinical-trial and CRO receipts should be supported by executed service agreements, statements of work, invoices, milestone evidence, tax records, and evidence that the services are legally permitted within the enterprise's registered business scope and applicable pharmaceutical or clinical-research regulatory framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mainland PRC rules do not impose a universal statutory requirement that all service-export foreign-exchange receipts be converted into renminbi immediately or at a fixed mandatory ratio. A qualified enterprise may generally retain eligible foreign currency in permitted foreign-exchange accounts and convert it when needed, subject to bank authenticity review and applicable account limits. For service-export receipts, the bank may require the service agreement, invoice, tax documentation, payment notice, delivery or milestone evidence, and transaction-purpose information. Goods-trade export receipts are subject to trade-facilitation, customs, foreign-exchange monitoring, and classified-enterprise rules; specific conversion timing and settlement treatment can differ by transaction and bank. No fixed statutory surrender deadline or mandatory conversion percentage should be assumed for CRO service revenue without confirming the current SAFE circulars, the bank's implementation policy, and the transaction classification. Proceeds must not be routed through personal accounts, unrelated entities, or artificial trade structures.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). For a mainland China wholly foreign-owned enterprise, SAFE administers foreign-exchange registration, settlement, payment, cross-border remittance, and capital-account controls, while PBOC supervises banking and payment-system infrastructure. Taiwan-related transactions may also require separate bank, sanctions, documentary, and transaction-screening analysis; the applicable mainland PRC regulatory treatment must be confirmed for each payment route.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium for ancillary collection or payment support; low for core operating liquidity. An offshore electronic-money institution or foreign fintech generally cannot replace a mainland PRC bank account, cannot independently conduct unrestricted RMB deposit-taking or domestic settlement, and cannot bypass SAFE, tax, customs, AML, sanctions, or beneficial-ownership controls. Use is potentially feasible through a licensed mainland bank, licensed cross-border payment institution, correspondent arrangement, or approved collection structure, subject to licensing perimeter, data-transfer rules, transaction-purpose verification, foreign-exchange quotas or settlement rules where applicable, and contractual allocation of chargeback and safeguarding risk. Client receipts, payroll, taxes, clinical-site payments, and capital injections should be routed through regulated mainland accounts unless a specific approved payment model applies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder capital through the WFOE's designated capital account or other bank account permitted for the specific capital transaction, using a remitting entity that matches the registered foreign investor or an expressly documented and bank-approved funding structure. Before remittance, confirm foreign-investment registration or bank-side registration requirements, account status, permitted currency, investment purpose, and receiving-bank instructions. The payment message should identify the investor, beneficiary WFOE, capital-contribution purpose, currency, and relevant corporate resolution or registration reference. Maintain the full audit trail from investor board approval to originating bank, correspondent banks, receiving bank, capital-account credit, and subsequent use. Convert or use funds only for permitted business purposes and retain evidence for SAFE, tax, bank, and statutory audit review. Do not mix capital injections with service revenue, shareholder loans, payroll funds, or third-party collections without documented account segregation and regulatory approval.Use regulated mainland bank rails for supplier, clinical-site, investigator, laboratory, technology, and other business payments. For ordinary commercial remittances, use SWIFT MT103 or the bank's current ISO 20022 equivalent with accurate beneficiary, intermediary-bank, invoice, contract, purpose, and fee instructions. For material cross-border purchases or services, preserve the contract, invoice, tax treatment, delivery or service evidence, and withholding analysis. Commercial letters of credit may be used for eligible goods or document-based transactions, but they are generally less suited to pure CRO service milestones unless the structure is commercially and legally appropriate. Documentary collections, open-account wires, bank guarantees, and escrow arrangements may be available subject to bank approval. Each payment must pass sanctions, AML, foreign-exchange, tax, and underlying-transaction checks. Where outbound royalties, interest, dividends, service fees, or related-party charges are involved, obtain the required corporate approvals and complete applicable tax withholding, reporting, and bank documentation before remittance.Deliverable RMB foreign-exchange forwards with an authorized mainland bank, limited to genuine underlying exposure and documented hedging purposes.Foreign-exchange swaps with an authorized bank where the product, tenor, collateral, underlying exposure, and settlement structure comply with applicable PBOC, SAFE, and bank requirements.Currency options or option combinations offered by an authorized bank, subject to suitability, risk disclosure, genuine-need review, and prohibition on speculative use.Non-deliverable forwards may be available through offshore counterparties for permitted hedging of non-deliverable or restricted currencies, but they do not provide unrestricted access to onshore RMB conversion and may create basis, settlement, regulatory, tax, and enforceability risks. Obtain bank and counsel confirmation before execution.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps may be considered for documented debt or funding exposure through authorized counterparties, subject to transaction-specific approvals, accounting treatment, collateral terms, and cross-border borrowing or lending rules.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Medium for ancillary collection or payment support; low for core operating liquidity. An offshore electronic-money institution or foreign fintech generally cannot replace a mainland PRC bank account, cannot independently conduct unrestricted RMB deposit-taking or domestic settlement, and cannot bypass SAFE, tax, customs, AML, sanctions, or beneficial-ownership controls. Use is potentially feasible through a licensed mainland bank, licensed cross-border payment institution, correspondent arrangement, or approved collection structure, subject to licensing perimeter, data-transfer rules, transaction-purpose verification, foreign-exchange quotas or settlement rules where applicable, and contractual allocation of chargeback and safeguarding risk. Client receipts, payroll, taxes, clinical-site payments, and capital injections should be routed through regulated mainland accounts unless a specific approved payment model applies.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; capital is freely determined in by-laws and may be contributed in cash, in-kind, or services. No escrow account is required. Foreign-currency contributions must be registered with Banco de la República if external capital is introduced.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or an S.A.S. must obtain an NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by filing an immediate electronic registration with DIAN through its 'Registro Único Tributario (RUT)' module. For a foreign legal entity without a Colombian branch, a local legal representative or tax attorney must be appointed, execute a power of attorney before a Colombian notary, and present the RUT application plus certified corporate documents (e.g., certificate of incorporation, bylaws) with legalization and official translation into Spanish. DIAN assigns the NIT upon identity validation and completion of the 'Pre-RUT' process; the RUT also records the VAT-responsible status (responsable del IVA).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. An S.A.S. is per se a Colombian income-tax taxpayer and cannot elect pass-through treatment. No hybrid mismatch entity election is available under the Código Tributario, although certain investment vehicles or consortia (consorcios/unión temporal) may apply separate transparency rules under Articles 18 and 368 E.T.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for legal entities: every S.A.S. or foreign entity carrying out taxable activities or importing goods into Colombia must register as a VAT-responsible person in the RUT. Natural persons with income below 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 164,727,500 in 2024) may be non-responsible for VAT, but corporations are automatically responsible.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exenta de IVA, 0%) applies to definitive exports of tangible goods (e.g., patient monitors) when the goods physically leave Colombia. The taxpayer must hold a valid export license for the goods, register the export operation in DIAN's customs system (Export Declaration - DEX), and demonstrate the legal transfer of ownership to a foreign buyer. Complementary services (maintenance, calibration) qualify only if they are directly linked to the exported goods and performed for an overseas recipient under Article 481 E.T.; call center or software services must meet territorial-use requirements for zero-rated offshore services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación - DEX) submitted electronically through DIAN/VUCE
  • Bill of Lading (BL) or Airwaybill (AWB) evidencing physical departure of the monitors
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Certificado de Compensación de Divisas (CCC) issued by an authorized Colombian financial intermediary

Electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta - FE) is mandatory for all VAT-responsible entities. Each invoice must be transmitted in real time to DIAN's validation services using XML/factura electrónica format, include the CUDE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) and QR code, and be authorized by DIAN before dispatch. Export invoices must incorporate the exporter's RUT, the DEX number, and customs authorization. DIAN also requires 'validación previa' for all FE via web services with digital signature, generated through authorized software providers or DIAN's free app.

Exporters are subject to mandatory documentary audit by DIAN and the customs authority. If zero-rating is claimed with false or inexact documentation, DIAN may deny the refund, assess an inflation penalty of up to 100% of the tax omitted (Article 648 E.T.), and impose a customs penalty equal to 30% of the value of the mis-declared merchandise (Law 1609/2016). Criminal tax fraud (Articles 434A/434B C.P.) and customs smuggling (Law 1762/2015) expose officers to imprisonment from 4 to 12 years and additional fines. Refunds are subject to retrospective clawback for up to five tax years where the supporting DEX, B/L, FX repatriation and export records are not upheld.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including registered trade and services payments. Capital-account inflows, including direct foreign investment, must be channelled through an authorised foreign exchange market intermediary (IMC) and registered with BRC via the Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional (Formulario No. 6) to guarantee lawful future dividend remittance, capital reduction, and repatriation. Non-registered investments lose the right to access the official FX market for profit outbound transfers. Trade dividends are otherwise subject to Colombian dividend withholding provisions under Law 2277 of 2022, without structural conversion restrictions. Practical hurdles include timely filing of the investment declaration, verifiable source of funds evidence, and consistent use of the IMC for all inward/outward clearing transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombian exporters of goods, including medical devices and electronic monitors, must channel export proceeds through an IMC. The BRC compendium (Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83) permits a maximum collection period of 13 months from the date of shipment or from the contractual due date, whichever is applicable, subject to the terms of the underlying export contract. There is no fixed percentage conversion mandate at the statutory level; the exporter must surrender or credit 100% of the export foreign currency to an IMC account, or to a compensation account (cuenta de compensación) authorised by BRC, within the applicable regulatory window (typically not exceeding 4 months of holding abroad). All collections must be evidenced by the declaración de cambio for goods exports filed with the IMC.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (BRC), with regulatory oversight jointly exercised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and the customs/tax authority, Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN). The foreign exchange system is governed by the political constitution, Ley 9 de 1991, Decreto 1735 de 1993, and the compendium of exchange regulations contained in Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83 of BRC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW for primary treasury rails. Colombian law reserves deposit-taking and current-account services to licensed credit institutions (banks, financial corporations, and savings corporations) regulated by the SFC. Foreign fintech companies and cross-border EMI services (for example, Wise, Payoneer, or Mercury) are not authorised to hold COP-based demand deposits or execute domestic ACH clearing without a local banking licence. Their lawful utility is restricted to auxiliary receivables/payables collection where the underlying FX transaction is extinguished through a licensed local IMC. EMI accounts must never receive Colombian export proceeds directly unless the funds are repatratriated and channelled through an IMC within the BRC timeline. The compliance risk of using unlicensed EMI settlement for import disbursements is material and may trigger DIAN customs valuation adjustments and SFC enforcement.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Wholly foreign-owned S.A.S. with no Colombian employees, no local payroll, and no visible operational footprint during onboarding

Root cause: Wholly foreign-owned S.A.S. with no Colombian employees, no local payroll, and no visible operational footprint during onboarding

Defense evidence: Certificate of existence and legal representation issued by the Cámara de Comercio (must be less than 90 days old); apostilled incorporation charter and board resolutions; executed commercial lease agreement for the Colombian assembly/calibration site; Colombian employment contracts or staffing agreement with a local HR provider; INVIMA sanitary registration or import licence for the ECG monitor; ISO 13485 (medical device QMS) certificate; description of manufacturing workflow and floor plan supporting physical operations.

Potential use of non-banking corridors, offshore payments, or crypto-facing intermediaries for trade settlement

Root cause: Potential use of non-banking corridors, offshore payments, or crypto-facing intermediaries for trade settlement

Defense evidence: Duly executed international sale contracts (in English/Spanish); purchase orders and pro-forma invoices from verified suppliers; logistics and bill-of-lading documentation; DIAN export/import customs declarations; declaration that all payments will clear exclusively via an IMC; no involvement of unlicensed payment providers in cross-border settlement.
UBO registry inconsistency or opacity in the ultimate shareholder chain

Root cause: UBO registry inconsistency or opacity in the ultimate shareholder chain

Defense evidence: Org chart tracing beneficial ownership through each intermediary holding company; apostilled share register or UBO registry extract; notarised declarations signed by each cascading beneficial owner; SFC-compliant SARLAFT beneficiary questionnaire; sanctions and PEP screening reports for each UBO; explanation for any change in control within the preceding 12 months.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign shareholder transfers USD/EUR from an overseas bank to the beneficiary account of CO_SAS held at a Colombian Tier-1 bank via SWIFT MT103. The wire instruction must clearly indicate 'Aporte de capital - Inversión directa de capital en CO_SAS' and reference the NIT (Colombian tax ID) of the S.A.S. The beneficiary bank, acting as an IMC, credits the funds to the capital escrow account and converts foreign currency into COP at the prevailing market rate. The IMC files the Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional (Formulario No. 6) with BRC; the investor must maintain a file copy of the wire, the IMC receipt, the capital registration certificate, and the stamped exchange declaration, as these will govern future dividend remittance, capital reduction, and liquidation proceeds. For submission, a certified translation of the board resolution approving the capital call and a police-checked source-of-funds memo are required.Trade settlements follow three authorised clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for open-account component purchases and supplier payments, accompanied by a clear trade purpose code and reference to the underlying purchase order; (ii) MT700/MT707 irrevocable documentary letters of credit for high-value calibration modules and raw-material imports, with performance/guarantee instruments structured as MT760 standby letters of credit for advance payments; and (iii) SWIFT member-to-member transfer for intra-group cross-border services, with the corresponding tax withholding certificates and service agreements. All import-related payments must be channelled via an IMC and reconciled with DIAN customs declarations; the importer must file the relevant export/import exchange declaration with the IMC before or at the time of disbursement. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

7.5% withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders from already-taxed profits; 35% for dividends derived from non-taxable/exempt profits; a 3% surcharge applies from FY2023 when the shareholder is resident in a non-cooperative or low-tax jurisdiction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due in the following fiscal year according to the DIAN calendar based on the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT, generally between early September and late October of the year following the taxable year.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

Box 01 Taxpayer identification: NIT, legal name, fiscal year, CIIU economic activity code, tax classification and main address. The Colombian S.A.S. files under its own NIT; foreign shareholders are not identified on this field unless the entity is transparent or the shareholder acts through a permanent establishment. Late filing penalty: 5% of tax due per month or fraction of delay, capped at 100% of the tax; where no tax is due, 0.5% of gross income per month (cap 5% of gross income, minimum 10 UVT). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when the Colombian taxpayer's gross income or gross assets exceed 135,000 UVT (approx. COP 6.35 billion for FY2024) or when related-party transactions exceed 62,200 UVT (approx. COP 2.93 billion). Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated gross revenue exceeds 180,000 UVT (approx. COP 8.47 billion for FY2024).

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not enforce a standalone ESR (Economic Substance Regulations) framework comparable to the UAE; however, the general anti-abuse rule (Art. 869 ET), the substance-over-form doctrine and DTA beneficial-ownership requirements impose a real economic-substance threshold. The Colombian S.A.S. must maintain genuine operational presence, calibrated manufacturing capacity, decision-making power and documented technical staff to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Evasión Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code Art. 405A: imprisonment from 3 to 6 years and a fine equal to 100%–300% of the evaded tax. DIAN additionally imposes a 20% understatement penalty, mora interest, cancellation of tax registration and seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors or legal representatives who sign fraudulent returns are criminally liable; preventive travel bans, asset embargoes and temporary suspension of the company's permit to operate are possible. Civil liability extends to restituting the evaded tax and damages.
Abusive Shell Company / Sociedad de Fachada

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the entity under Art. 869 ET and tax the beneficial owner directly; penalties include 20% fraud surcharge and mora interest. Under commercial law, the company may be subject to cancellation of registration and forfeiture of improperly distributed profits.

Director personal liability: Colombian courts apply veil-piercing to impose joint and several liability on directors and nominee shareholders. Criminal fraud laws may lead to imprisonment for up to 12 years if public funds are involved; travel bans and asset freezing are common enforcement measures.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Art. 1212 ET imposes a fine of 10% of the total value of related-party transactions not properly documented; DIAN may recharacterize the transactions, assess additional taxable income at 35%, and apply mora interest plus a 20% fraud penalty for intentional misstatements.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign falsely documented TP returns face criminal liability for falsifying private records, punishable by up to 8 years' imprisonment; civil liability covers the additional tax and fines, and courts may issue travel bans and prevent the director from serving in any Colombian company.
Money Laundering / AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code Art. 323: imprisonment from 10 to 20 years (aggravated to 30 years under certain circumstances) and fines from 250 to 15,000 SMLMV. The company may be dissolved, its assets subjected to extinción de dominio, and its registration cancelled.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors who omit AML controls may be indicted as co-authors; personal assets can be frozen, travel bans imposed, and convictions lead to disqualification from public tenders for up to 20 years and a permanent board-ban.

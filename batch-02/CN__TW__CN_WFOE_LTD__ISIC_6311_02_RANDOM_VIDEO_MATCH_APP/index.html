Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online taxpayer portal: National Electronic Tax Bureau / electronic tax bureau at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, supplemented by the provincial electronic tax bureaus.

E-government filing portal

For a mainland China Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) limited liability company, the TIN is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) under the 'one license, one code' reform. The statute requires (1) foreign investor submits the foreign investment information registration through the SAMR platform; (2) upon issuance of the business license, the WFOE automatically obtains its unified social credit code, which serves as the enterprise TIN for enterprise income tax, VAT, and other national taxes; (3) the WFOE must complete supplementary tax registration, legal representative and financial officer identity filing, and bank account filing through the electronic tax bureau within 30 calendar days; (4) if annual taxable turnover is projected to exceed the VAT threshold or input credit is required, the WFOE must apply for general VAT taxpayer status through the same portal; and (5) the WFOE must enroll in the electronic invoice service platform for digital invoice issuance. A non-resident foreign entity without a Chinese establishment or place of business may obtain a TIN by direct non-resident enterprise tax registration or through a withholding agent, using legalized incorporation documents, charter, board resolution appointing a tax representative, and the relevant Chinese-sourced income contract; in such case the TIN is issued by the competent STA office and is distinct from the unified social credit code. This China WFOE does not obtain any Taiwanese tax identification number solely by reason of its PRC incorporation; a Taiwan tax ID would arise only upon separate branch/permanent establishment registration in Taiwan.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover (CNY 5 million) is the mandatory statutory threshold for registration as a general VAT taxpayer; below this threshold small-scale taxpayer treatment applies, but all business entities must complete tax registration regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment applies to goods physically exported from the customs territory of China and to qualifying offshore services supplied to non-resident customers and fully consumed outside China. For the ISIC 6311 video matching and cross-border social telephony app, zero-rating may be claimed for exported software/video/telephony services only where the recipient is an overseas entity or individual, the service is not used or consumed in China, payment is received in foreign exchange, and the service is consistently documented as a cross-border taxable service under the Cai Shui [2016] 36 Circular.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) for goods movements, with China customs release notation
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent air waybill evidencing export from the PRC
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Foreign exchange transaction declaration under the foreign exchange administration for goods or service trade
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the exporter
  • Cross-border service contract or order evidencing the non-resident recipient and foreign exchange payment
  • Electronic VAT export invoice issued through the STA electronic invoice platform
  • Service trade foreign exchange tax filing/recordal where required by the tax authority

China requires VAT invoices to be issued through the STA-controlled electronic invoice service platform under the fully digital electronic invoice system integrated with Golden Tax Phase IV. Invoice data is transmitted to the tax authority in real time via API in XML format with a unique anti-counterfeit QR code and digital signature. General VAT taxpayers must use online invoice verification and may not issue offline paper invoices except under approved special circumstances; abnormal issuance triggers automated risk control.

Upon audit, if the export zero-rating claim is not substantiated, the STA will claw back the VAT refunded, impose late payment interest, and assess an additional penalty ranging from 50 percent to five times the tax involved depending on whether the defect is classified as underreporting or fraud. Criminal liability for fraudulent export VAT refunds is addressed under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, with imprisonment up to life and fines up to five times the defrauded amount. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day statutory period may also result in State Administration of Foreign Exchange penalties, forfeiture of input tax refunds, and credit blacklisting.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a managed conditional convertibility regime. The lawful foreign exchange market is restricted to designated banks, with SAFE and PBOC exercising comprehensive supervision. Current account transactions, including cross-border service fees, royalties, dividends, and ordinary trade proceeds, are generally convertible when the underlying transaction is genuine and documentation is submitted to a designated bank. Profit remittances from a WFOE to its Taiwan shareholder are treated as current account outflows but require annual audited financial statements, a tax clearance certificate proving that final corporate income tax has been settled, a board resolution declaring dividends, and a bank-level authenticity review. Inbound registered capital injections are capital account transactions and remain heavily controlled: the Taiwan investor must first complete SAFE foreign exchange registration and the WFOE must open a specially designated Foreign Currency Capital Account. Once received, capital can be settled into RMB for actual business purposes but may not be deployed into securities, real estate beyond the negative list, entrusted loans, or speculative derivatives. Export proceeds have no general compulsory surrender ratio; however, all foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated into the domestic banking system within the statutory period and cannot be retained offshore without prior SAFE approval. This asymmetry between current account convertibility and capital account prior-approval determines the entire treasury architecture and requires disciplined cross-border liquidity planning.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China no longer imposes a blanket mandatory conversion percentage for export proceeds. For standard A-class enterprises, the statutory rule is repatriation within 180 days from the customs export declaration date, or from the contractual payment date if the underlying trade terms are registered and reported in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System; delayed collection must be declared and substantiated to SAFE. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0% for A-class enterprises, which may freely retain export receipts in a Foreign Currency Settlement Account or voluntarily convert them to RMB at market rates through a designated bank. B-class and C-class enterprises are subject to enhanced review and may be required by SAFE to settle 100% of export foreign exchange receipts into RMB within 90 days, alongside complete transaction documentation and more frequent compliance reporting.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) with delegated foreign exchange administration authority conferred upon the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury rail for this mainland WFOE. Offshore fintech EMIs do not possess a designated foreign exchange bank licence issued by PBOC/SAFE, cannot independently complete SAFE foreign exchange registration, cannot clear CNY through CNAPS or CIPS, and are legally barred from directly opening corporate bank accounts for domestic entities. Their only permissible role is ancillary international collection, for example receiving settlement from Paymentwall or Apple IAP through a licensed mainland payment institution partner, provided that the partner obtains the necessary PBOC/SAFE cross-border payment authorisation and all funds ultimately flow into a Tier-1 domestic bank account with proper transaction coding. The WFOE should not rely on offshore EMIs for capital account flows, statutory tax remittances, dividend distributions, or FX hedging.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan parent must complete SAFE foreign exchange registration for the WFOE and then remit equity capital from its Taiwan bank account via SWIFT into the dedicated Foreign Currency Capital Account opened at a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank such as Bank of China Limited. The remittance instruction must carry the SAFE foreign exchange business purpose code for inbound FDI capital injection, commonly reported as 126011, and must reference the SAFE FDI registration serial number. Upon receipt, the domestic bank verifies the sending institution, remitter name, purpose code, and underlying registration record. The WFOE then appoints a licensed PRC certified public accountant to issue the statutory capital verification report. The capital funds may later be converted into RMB under the SAFE capital account easy-settlement policy for genuine business purposes, subject to the negative list and explicit prohibition on use for securities, entrusted loans, speculative derivatives, or repayment of RMB loans before the capital contribution duties are satisfied.For genuine outbound disbursements such as cloud service fees, software license royalties, overseas content moderation, marketing and contractor payments, the WFOE must submit a service trade foreign exchange payment application to its settlement bank, attaching the underlying commercial contract, original invoice, tax withholding/acquisition certificate, and transaction authenticity declaration. The bank executes the remittance through SWIFT MT103; multi-currency high-value payments may be routed through CIPS or the offshore clearing system while respecting SWIFT message format standards. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are available for physical equipment or infrastructure procurement and must be issued through the corporate bank with strict compliance checks over beneficiary names, shipping documents, bill of lading, insurance certificates, and sanctioned-country restrictions.

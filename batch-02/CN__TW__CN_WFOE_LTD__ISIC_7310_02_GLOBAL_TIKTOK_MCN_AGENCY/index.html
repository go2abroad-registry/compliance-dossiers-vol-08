Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No general statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Taiwan foreign-invested limited company engaged in digital services; however, the Investment Commission requires declared capital to be commensurate with the intended MCN agency business scope and operating expenses. The full subscribed capital must be remitted into a dedicated bank account opened in Taiwan in the proposed company name, and a bank-issued Certificate of Deposit must be produced. The account remains a blocked escrow-type account until the Department of Commerce issues the Company Registration Certificate. For a People's Republic of China mainland investor, the capital remittance must be made through an approved cross-strait foreign exchange channel and is subject to Investment Commission review of the ultimate beneficial ownership structure.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing and administration through the National Electronic Tax Administration Bureau (e-Tax) online portal and the STA invoice service platform (Golden Tax Project Phase IV).

E-government filing portal

Upon entity formation, the WFOE first obtains its Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the Administration for Market Regulation; the USCC is automatically the legal person's taxpayer identification number (TIN). The WFOE must complete post-incorporation tax registration, tax type determination, and invoicing quota approval through the e-Tax portal within 30 days of business license issuance. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a registered Chinese establishment but earning PRC-source taxable income, the PRC payer or withholding agent files the Tax Withholding Return and makes the payment to the STA; where the non-resident entity requires direct registration, it must file a non-resident enterprise tax registration with the competent tax authority, submitting foreign incorporation proof, tax residency certificate, board or legal representative identification, articles of association, and Chinese bank account details to be assigned a non-resident taxpayer identification number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. PRC tax law does not provide check-the-box elections for entity classification or hybrid mismatch elections; a WFOE is mandatorily a PRC resident enterprise taxed at entity level. VAT status as general versus small-scale taxpayer is elective only within statutory conditions and does not affect income tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales excluding VAT. Taxpayers whose annual taxable sales exceed this amount are mandatorily registered as general VAT taxpayers; below the threshold they remain small-scale taxpayers. Initial VAT and tax registration itself is mandatory for all WFOEs as part of the one-stop business establishment procedure, irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from China are zero-rated for VAT if they are physically shipped out of China, customs release is granted, and the seller retains the required export declaration and sales and payment evidence. Cross-border services are zero-rated or VAT-exempt only when supplied to an overseas entity and wholly consumed outside China under Caishui [2016] No. 36; zero-rating applies to specified offshore service categories such as software, information systems, business process management, and certain R&D and design services, while MCN and advertising services with the advertising placement outside China fall under the exemption category unless separately classified as a zero-rated offshore service. The taxpayer must prove the offshore recipient, actual overseas consumption, foreign-currency consideration, and absence of domestic use or benefit; zero-rated supplies allow input VAT credit and refund, while VAT-exempt cross-border services do not.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (export customs release form) evidencing physical export
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other international transport document covering the exported goods
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract
  • Packing list and export customs inspection records where applicable
  • Bank inward foreign exchange collection slip or FX settlement proof issued by a designated foreign exchange bank
  • Offshore service performance evidence or overseas advertising placement report for cross-border service exemption or zero-rating

China uses the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform for fully digitalized e-invoices and the tax-controlled special VAT invoice system for VAT special invoices. All invoice data must be issued through STA-approved channels with the tax-control U-Key or digital e-invoice authentication, transmitted to the Golden Tax Project Phase IV platform, and automatically matched against VAT filings, customs export declarations, and foreign exchange receipts. Anti-fraud controls include mandatory invoice serial numbers, QR-code verification, encrypted invoice metadata, and automated risk identification; there is no private offline invoice software permitted for statutory VAT invoices.

STA, customs, and SAFE data-exchange programs cross-match export declarations, logistics documents, e-invoices, VAT returns, and foreign exchange receipts. If a claimed zero percent export VAT rate or exemption is not fully substantiated, the authority denies the zero rating, reclaims any refunded input VAT, and assesses output VAT with a late-payment surcharge at 0.05 percent per day; administrative penalties may be 50 percent to 500 percent of the unpaid tax under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and Tax Levying. Fraudulent or deceptive export VAT refund applications are criminal tax fraud under the PRC Criminal Law and are not subject to a time-limited recovery period; responsible officers may face criminal imprisonment and property confiscation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the foreign exchange administration of the People's Republic of China, the RMB is fully convertible for current account transactions such as trade in goods, services, and remittance of employee compensation, provided that underlying documentation is submitted through a designated bank. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment into a WFOE, the servicing of offshore loans, and the repatriation of registered capital or dividends, remain subject to strict prior approval, SAFE filing, or bank-verification-based control. The WFOE may lawfully receive foreign exchange capital contributions after completing the Foreign Investment Information Report, opening a SAFE-monitored capital account, and passing bank KYC. Funds credited to the capital account may be converted into CNY for operating purposes within the permitted business scope, but cannot be used for equity investment or offshore lending without special approval. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders require audited financial statements, tax clearance, and evidence that paid-in capital has been fully injected. The regime is therefore a managed convertibility system rather than a free capital account regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's trade-in-goods monitoring rules, export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account no later than 90 days from customs declaration unless a longer contractual term is registered and justified. China has abolished mandatory foreign exchange surrender; the mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent, meaning that a WFOE may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign exchange. Conversion to CNY requires a valid trade background and supporting customs documents.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and WorldFirst are not licensed commercial banks or lawful deposit-taking institutions in mainland China. Under the PBOC Licensing Framework for Payment Institutions, no non-bank institution can operate a domestic clearing account, accept RMB deposits, or provide foreign exchange settlement on behalf of a Chinese WFOE without obtaining a Payment Business Permit and joining the China clearing infrastructure. Therefore, the WFOE cannot use an offshore EMI as its primary treasury account for statutory paid-in capital or dividend repatriation. EMIs may be employed only as auxiliary collection or payout portals if funds pass through a domestic licensed bank and the transaction is reported under SAFE; otherwise their services create suspicious activity reports and foreign exchange filing gaps. Consequently, onshore Tier-1 banks are mandatory for risk control and regulatory reliability.

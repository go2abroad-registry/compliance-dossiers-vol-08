Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – official portal www.dian.gov.co; electronic services via DIAN 'Muisca' portal

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian S.A.S., the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) is assigned by DIAN upon incorporation and registration with the Cámara de Comercio. The company must then file the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) within 30 days, select its tax classifications (IVA responsible, source-withholding agent, exporter status), obtain an electronic signature certificate, and activate the DIAN electronic invoicing software. A non-resident parent entity earning Colombian-source income must appoint a local legal representative and separately obtain its own NIT through the RUT procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Colombia. A qualifying S.A.S. may elect the optional 'Régimen SIMPLE' for small taxpayers where annual gross income does not exceed 100,000 UVT, which replaces ordinary income tax and IVA. Industrial exporters may also elect to operate under Zona Franca benefits, receiving a reduced 20% corporate income tax rate. Hybrid mismatch and anti-abuse rules apply under Law 1943 of 2018 and BEPS-aligned regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Registration is mandatory for all legal entities engaged in sales of taxable goods or services, irrespective of turnover. Natural persons may obtain 'no responsable' status if previous-year gross income is below approximately 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 154,000,000 for 2025) and they are not importers or exporters. For the optional SIMPLE regime, annual gross income must not exceed 100,000 UVT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods manufactured in Colombia, such as custom mechanical keyboard caps, qualify for IVA at 0% when sold to a foreign buyer and definitively exported outside Colombian customs territory. The seller must be registered with DIAN as an IVA-responsible exporter, hold valid export customs documentation, and prove the goods departed Colombia. Services directly related to the exported goods may also qualify under statutory conditions.

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX) – definitive electronic export customs declaration accepted by DIAN/VUCE
  • Bill of Lading (BL) or air waybill / manifiesto de carga
  • Commercial invoice (factura comercial) describing the goods and transaction
  • Certificate of origin or certificate of preferential origin (if applicable)
  • Declaración de Cambio (Form 1) and bank certificate of forex settlement evidencing repatriation of export proceeds

Colombia mandates real-time electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta) through DIAN. Invoices must be generated by DIAN-authorised software, transmitted to DIAN via secure HTTPS/XML APIs, validated in real time, and returned with a valid CUDE and QR code before being legally effective. Export invoices follow the same framework, and customs clearance requires electronic submission of the Export Declaration (DEX) via the VUCE single window.

DIAN rigorously audits export zero-rating claims. Undocumented exports, false declarations, or unrepatriated proceeds trigger the inexactness penalty (sanción por inexactitud) of 100% to 200% of the omitted IVA, plus default interest and full retrospective clawback of any VAT refunds. Criminal tax evasion, including fraudulent zero-rating schemes, can result in imprisonment, disqualification from export incentives, and cancellation of the exporter registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating peso with full convertibility on the current account for trade-related payments, service payments, interest, and dividend repatriation, provided all flows are channelled through the Mercado Cambiario and evidenced by a Declaración de Cambio (DCIN). Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment, external loans, portfolio investment, and derivative operations, are subject to registration with the Banco de la República and must be executed through an authorized intermediary. Dividend and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are legally permitted after verification of registered net earnings and after satisfying the applicable corporate income tax and non-resident dividend withholding tax obligations; no discretionary foreign-exchange license is required, but proper exchange-form filing is mandatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of goods, including custom keyboard keycaps manufactured under ISIC 3240, must invoice and collect export proceeds through the Colombian exchange market. The statutory collection term runs from the date of shipment, generally requiring receipt within six months, unless extended under Banco de la República rules for specific goods or financing terms. One hundred percent of the export value in foreign currency must be sold or otherwise surrendered through an authorized exchange intermediary, and the corresponding Declaración de Cambio must be submitted via that intermediary. The proceeds are converted to Colombian pesos at the prevailing market rate, and the transaction is recorded under the exporter's foreign-exchange registration profile.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Bank of the Republic), acting as the central bank and primary foreign-exchange authority, with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia as the supervisor of authorized exchange intermediaries, and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) exercising linked customs and exchange-control enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. A foreign digital EMI or electronic-money institution is not authorized by the Colombian legal framework to maintain local demand deposits or provide local account numbers in Colombia. Such platforms may be used only as payment collection gateways and are not a substitute for the regulated clearing bank rail. The treasury structure must therefore use a Colombian commercial bank as principal account and FX intermediary, with foreign EMIs limited to ancillary card-acquiring or payout services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment must be routed through an authorized Colombian bank. The contribution should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 identifying the foreign investor, the Colombian company's NIT, and the purpose as 'capital contribution / foreign direct investment'. Prior to or upon receipt, the company must complete the foreign investment registration with the Banco de la República through the intermediary bank, using the standardized Declaración de Inversión Internacional and the applicable exchange concept code for formation or increase of capital. The incoming USD or EUR is converted to COP through the authorized intermediary at the market rate and credited to the company's COP operating account. The underlying wire should include the foreign investment registration code where available and must be supported by a board resolution, share issuance documentation, and proof of funding.Outbound payments to overseas suppliers of PBT resin, pigments, tooling, packaging, and logistics services are made through SWIFT MT103 over the company's Colombian bank. All foreign-sourced imports are reported in the exchange declaration filed by the intermediary at the time of payment or collection, ensuring the disbursement has a genuine underlying trade and is not construed as a capital flight transaction. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For tax year 2024, the mandatory Transfer Pricing Documentation, Informative Transfer Pricing Return, and Local File/Master File obligations are triggered if gross income or gross assets equal or exceed 100,000 UVT (approximately COP 4,706,500,000 using a UVT of COP 47,065), or if related-party transactions equal or exceed 10,000 UVT (approximately COP 470,650,000). Even below these thresholds, any cross-border related-party transaction must be reported in the informative return and priced at arm's length.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not operate a UAE-style formal Economic Substance Regulations regime, but it applies a robust economic-substance doctrine through the general anti-abuse rule and transfer-pricing principles. A foreign-owned S.A.S. manufacturing custom keyboard keycaps must demonstrate real employees, premises, raw-material purchase functions, inventory risk, production machinery, and independent decision-making. A S.A.S. that merely channels royalties, commissions, or marketplace sales without substantive operational activity may be recharacterized as a shell by DIAN, with all intercompany deductions disallowed and unilateral transfer-pricing adjustments imposed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent invoicing

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under the Colombian Penal Code as amended by Law 1819 of 2016 carries imprisonment of 36 to 72 months. Administratively, DIAN imposes an inexactness penalty up to 100% of the tax difference plus default interest and may suspend the NIT for invoicing violations.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and sole shareholders who sign fraudulent returns or order the omission are jointly and severally liable for the tax debt. Courts may impose travel bans, asset embargos, and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced under S.A.S. Law 1258 of 2008 when the company is used for fraud.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an S.A.S.; capital may be minimal and is freely fixed by the bylaws, with at least one subscribed share. Any unpaid balance of subscribed shares must be paid within a maximum of two (2) years. Cash contributions must be evidenced by a bank deposit certificate or an accountant's sworn certification; no escrow account is statutorily mandated, though contributed funds must remain traceable in a Colombian corporate bank account. Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the shares.

Occupational: mandatory ARL affiliation for all employees, an elementary Seguridad y Salud en el Trabajo (SST) program, and product safety clearance under SIC rules on lead, cadmium, and nickel release for jewelry items offered to consumers.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime with full current-account convertibility of the Colombian peso (COP); there are no exchange controls on trade settlements, service payments, or profit remittances. However, all transactions must be executed through the regulated 'mercado cambiario' using authorized intermediary banks and financial corporations. Capital-account inflows, including direct foreign investment into the S.A.S., are permitted without prior licensing but are subject to mandatory declaration and registration with Banco de la República (Registro Internacional de Inversiones) through the receiving bank on the statutory Declaración de Cambio (Formulario No. 5). Registration is a legal condition precedent for future dividend distributions, capital repatriation, and disinvestment remittances; unregistered capital is not lawfully remittable. The practical hurdles to profit repatriation are documentary: maintained audited financial statements, a corporate distribution resolution, payment of corporate income tax and applicable dividend withholding taxes (10% standard or treaty-reduced rate), an updated RUT, and a registered investment reflecting the original contribution at the official exchange rate. No prior central-bank approval is required once these conditions are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombian law requires statutory 'canalización' (channeling and surrender) of 100% of export proceeds through the authorized foreign-exchange market via an authorized intermediary, with no discretionary offshore retention ratio. For goods exports, collection and surrender must be completed within the agreed commercial credit term but in no case later than 13 months after the declared maturity date of the respective export declaration; for services, the statutory window is 6 months after completion of the service. Advance payments are likewise canalized within the corresponding term. Exporters may temporarily credit proceeds to a foreign-currency account opened with an authorized bank and registered with Banco de la República, but the funds must ultimately be converted to COP or applied to specifically permitted uses within the same statutory period. Non-compliance constitutes a financial-currency offense actionable by Banco de la República and generates UIAF filings, DIAN audit adjustments, and blockage of subsequent import payments and outward remittances.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Bank of the Republic of Colombia), the central monetary and exchange authority, acting in coordination with the Comité de Cambios (Foreign Exchange Committee) and the Ministerio de Hacienda y Crédito Público (Ministry of Finance and Public Credit); AML/CFT supervision is discharged by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (Financial Superintendency) and the Unidad de Información y Análisis Financiero (UIAF). The operative foreign-exchange rulebook is Banco de la República External Resolution No. 1 of 2018, as amended, compiled in Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, with DIAN (National Tax and Customs Directorate) enforcing fiscal, customs, and foreign-trade procedural aspects.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends distributed to Colombian resident shareholders are subject to a 35% income tax at the corporate level and a taxable dividend base for shareholders; dividends paid to non-residents and nationals are subject to an additional 10% withholding tax on the amount received after the 35% corporate tax (effective overall 41.5% on pre-tax profits). Non-resident beneficiaries are also subject to a 35% withholding tax on qualifying dividends if the distribution exceeds the taxed corporate profits base ('dividend excess' rules).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return must be filed by the statutory deadline set by the DIAN each year, generally within the period from August up to early October following the taxable year-end (fiscal year 31 December), according to the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT. The return and any payable tax must be submitted electronically by the due date individually assigned by DIAN Resolution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The mandatory Transfer Pricing Local File thresholds apply when consolidated gross income from the taxable year exceeds COP 1,500,000,000 (approximately USD 360,000) OR when total assets exceed COP 4,500,000,000 (approximately USD 1,080,000) at year-end. A Master File is required when total consolidated gross income exceeds COP 4,500,000,000 (approximately USD 1,080,000). For taxpayers within the e-commerce category (online merchandising of jewelry/watches), the schedular thresholds are aligned with DIAN Resolution 001 and Decree 2120.

Economic substance scope: Colombia applies the 'substance-over-form' doctrine and a General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) under the Law 1819 of 2016. For entities carrying out digital e-commerce, DIAN scrutinizes: (i) effective management and control in Colombian territory; (ii) payroll, operational decision-making, and fulfillment infrastructure in Colombia; (iii) actual economic activity as distinguished from a mere letterbox; (iv) permanent establishment exposure for foreign-related digital sellers. Although Colombia is not a Caribbean Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) jurisdiction, entities incorporated as S.A.S. trading online must maintain full economic substance and real presence to avoid being characterized as a shell, and to access treaty benefits. The OECD BEPS Action 5 framework applies for exchange of information; artificial arrangements stripping Colombian-source income trigger re-characterization and significant sanctions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction for tax evasion carries imprisonment of 4 to 9 years plus a fine equal to 200% to 1000% of the evaded tax (Law 1819 of 2016, Article 333). Administrative evasion penalties range from 20% to 160% of the omitted tax plus daily late interest; DIAN can impose a 1% monthly surcharge capped at 100%.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, and legal representatives are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes, penalties and interest when they fail to withhold or cause the non-payment (Article 794-2 Código Fiscal). In fraud cases, criminal liability extends to the actual management; courts may impose travel bans, asset freezing, and personal seizure of real property; conviction bars directors from managing legal entities and can include imprisonment for up to 9 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: DIAN re-characterizes the transactions under the GAAR; disallows deductions and treaty benefits; imposes a 160% penalty on the higher tax assessed and a potential 100% surcharge on omitted withholdings; the entity may be liquidated if its sole purpose is tax evasion (Código de Comercio Art. 19 and GAAR rules).

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors face 'veil piercing' under Colombian law when the corporate form is used to evade tax (Ley 1258 de 2008, SAS regime permits veil-piercing for abuse of entity purpose or fraud); directors are ordered to pay the entire tax, penalties, and interest; courts can issue travel bans and freeze personal assets; risk of criminal liability for criminal association.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Law 1121 of 2006, the Superintendencia de Sociedades imposes fines up to COP 50,000,000 (approximately USD 12,000) per violation, increased to 1% of annual revenues for serious offenses; DIAN and the UIAF can suspend the entity’s commercial registration and prohibit operations; aggravated money laundering carries imprisonment of 10 to 30 years and fines from 1,000 to 50,000 minimum monthly wages (Law 1762 of 2015).

Director personal liability: Officers who fail to install AML controls or who knowingly process illegal proceeds are criminally liable as co-authors; they face imprisonment, monetary penalties, and civil restitution to harmed third parties; a travel ban is commonly applied pending investigation.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty per Form 120: 10% of the value of the related party transactions up to 200 UVT per infraction; failure to submit documentation: 1% of related-party turnover per month up to 5% of turnover (minimum 100 UVT and maximum 12,000 UVT); adjustments to taxable income based on DIAN's OECD-compliant pricing method plus a 10% to 20% punitive surcharge.

Director personal liability: The legal representative is personally liable for declaring false information or signing the transfer pricing return without reasonable grounds; DIAN may impose joint liability for the tax deficiency up to the amount of the benefit obtained; criminal indictment for aggravated fraud can follow if the false documentation is used to evade tax.

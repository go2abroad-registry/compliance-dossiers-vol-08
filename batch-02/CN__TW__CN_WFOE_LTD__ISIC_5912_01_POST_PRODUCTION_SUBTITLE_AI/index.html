Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-in capital requirement is prescribed under the Taiwan Company Act; however, the paid-in capital must be firmly stated in the Articles of Incorporation, fully subscribed, and fully paid before the company registration filing is made. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, the capital amount must be approved by the Investment Commission, Ministry of Economic Affairs, and the funds must be remitted from abroad through a designated foreign-exchange bank and verified by a Taiwan-certified public accountant. No blocked escrow account is required, but the remitted capital must remain in a bank account in Taiwan and may not be disbursed for operating expenses until the Company Registration Certificate and Unified Business Number have been issued.

E-government filing portal

Step 2: Within 30 days of incorporation, the WFOE's legal representative and financial officer must activate the national Electronic Tax Bureau (ETB) account using real-name authentication (e-identification). Step 4: Apply for the All-Digital Electronic Invoice quota and authorize the VAT electronic invoice platform. Step 5: A foreign non-resident investor that will derive China-source income must either register directly via the STA's non-resident taxpayer workflow or be enrolled as a withholding-tax payer through the WFOE as the withholding agent. Available election forms

No check-the-box pass-through election is available under China's CIT regime; a WFOE is always an entity-level taxpayer. The only relevant election is VAT status: a taxpayer may elect simplified small-scale treatment until annual taxable turnover exceeds 5,000,000 CNY, after which general VAT taxpayer registration is compulsory. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the STA's Cross-border Taxable Services Zero-Rate and Exemption Administration Measures, an AI subtitle transcription and voice cloning studio may claim a 0% VAT export rate by proving: (a) the service is supplied to a customer resident outside China (including Taiwan); (b) the service is consumed wholly outside the customs territory of China; (c) consideration is received in foreign exchange or in RMB settled from an overseas bank; and (d) the services fall within the zero-rated catalogue for modern services (broadcasting, film and audio-video production and post-production services). The WFOE must file a zero-rate declaration in the export tax rebate system and retain contemporaneous evidence for audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) - required only when physical media, master discs, or encoded hardware are separately declared; not applicable to pure digital delivery.
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill - required only for tangible exports; digital services rely on electronic delivery receipts and log files.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange receipt record issued by a Chinese-designated settlement bank, evidencing repatriation of export proceeds in the agreed currency.
  • Export Service Contract executed between the WFOE and the offshore customer, specifying the AI subtitle transcription/dubbing services, fees, currency, and delivery terms.
  • All-Digital Electronic Invoice (e-invoice) issued through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform indicating the 0% VAT zero-rate annotation.
  • Offshore Consumption Declaration signed by the WFOE (and if required, a beneficial owner certificate from the customer's tax authority) confirming the service is provided and consumed entirely outside China.

Since the nationwide rollout of the All-Digital Electronic Invoice (e-invoice) system, all VAT invoices must be created through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Each e-invoice carries a unique digital invoice code, an anti-counterfeit QR code, and a digital stamp generated in real time by the Golden Tax Phase IV (GTP IV) system. e-invoice data is cleared through the STA cloud before release to the recipient; no invoice can be issued offline except legacy registered paper invoices. Enterprises connect their billing systems via the STA's authorized API or the web interface, and every issuance is reported transactionally to enable automatic cross-checking of VAT credits.

Where zero-rating is claimed on fabricated or non-qualifying services, the STA will disallow the zero-rate, claw back the uncollected VAT, and charge a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% from the date the tax became due. A tax penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax may be imposed under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law. Fraudulent export rebate claims may trigger unlimited retrospective reassessment, criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, and inclusion on the Tax Dishonesty Blacklist, suspending export tax incentives and restricting the legal representative.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under China's current foreign exchange administration framework, the renminbi is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods, services, and profit remittances, subject to a principle of genuine documentation verification. Capital account items, such as direct investment, cross-border debt, and securities investment, remain regulated and require prior approval, filing, or registration with SAFE. For a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE), registered capital must be remitted through a foreign exchange capital account and can be converted into RMB for business use under the capital account settlement guidelines, with strict tracking and payment verification. Profit remittance to overseas parents requires an audited financial statement, proof of tax payment, and a resolution on dividend distribution; funds must pass through a bank account and be settled under the current account regime. Although the SAFE has removed the mandatory export surrender requirement, export proceeds must be repatriated and brought onshore within the statutory period. The de facto convertibility is therefore high for current account operations but constrained for capital account flows, and any perceived arbitrage or round-tripping is scrutinized intensively. This dual-track regime necessitates meticulous transactional documentation and compliance with the 'principle of actual need'. Additionally, the AI subtitle and voice-cloning business, classified under ISIC 5912, may involve cross-border data flows and intellectual property licensing, which are subject to separate PBOC and Cybersecurity Administration oversight, adding both risk and documentary complexity to any currency conversion or repatriation exercise.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As of the current SAFE rules, there is no mandatory surrender ratio for export proceeds. Exporters must repatriate all foreign exchange receipts from exports to mainland China within 30 days, or within 90 days if the foreign trade contract stipulates a longer settlement period, and must complete the export collection verification through the Trade in Goods Monitoring System. The exporter may choose to keep the funds in a foreign currency current account or convert them into RMB at the prevailing spot rate. For service trade income, such as AI subtitle production and voice-cloning licensing, similar rules apply: the invoice must be matched against the service transaction and settled through the bank, with full documentation retained for five years.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Stripe and Wise are not licensed payment institutions in mainland China and cannot directly open accounts or clear transactions for a WFOE. For a Chinese incorporated entity, any attempt to use these offshore EMIs to originate or receive payments would violate PBOC regulations on cross-border payment services and SAFE rules on foreign exchange settlement. The only permissible route for high-volume, compliant cross-border collection and disbursement is through licensed domestic banks using SWIFT or its Chinese equivalent (CIPS). Therefore, the viability of fintech EMIs for this WFOE is low, and institutions such as Stripe or Wise should be used solely by the offshore parent or affiliated entities, never by the mainland entity itself. The financial superintendent must ensure that all business revenue is received via the WFOE's bank-approved invoices and bank transfer channels, otherwise the entity faces real-time anomalous transaction monitoring and potential freezing of accounts.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) rate under PRC EIT Law Article 4; non-resident enterprise passive income is charged at 20% and effectively reduced to 10% for outbound dividends, interest and royalties under EIT Implementation Regulation Article 91. The WFOE, as a Chinese tax resident, is taxed on worldwide income at 25% unless a preferential rate applies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on the gross amount of dividends distributed by the Chinese WFOE to its Taiwan shareholder. The 10% is the domestic statutory gross-basis withholding tax for non-resident enterprises without a permanent establishment in China; because the Cross-Strait Avoidance of Double Taxation Agreement has been signed but has not entered into force, no treaty reduction is currently available. The WFOE must withhold at the time of the shareholder resolution and remit the WHT to the tax authority by the 15th day of the month following the payment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB. The annual related-party transaction report (G-series) is mandatory for any related-party transaction. Local File (同期资料-本地文档) is required where tangible-property related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million in a tax year, intangible-property or service-related transactions exceed RMB 40 million, or financial-asset related-party transactions exceed RMB 100 million. Master File (主体文档) is required where aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. Same-period documentation must be completed by 31 May of the following year and delivered to the tax authority within 20 working days of a formal request.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but economic substance operates through the EIT Law Article 47 general anti-abuse rule, the reasonable-commercial-purpose principle, the beneficial-ownership test in treaty claims, and the anti-round-tripping declaration under SAFE/MOFCOM rules. A Taiwan-funded WFOE with only nominal staff, shared office space, no local decision-making and no control over its AI development contracts is exposed to recharacterisation as a shell/principal vehicle; the tax authority can attribute profits to the substantive owner, deny royalty deductions, reject treaty filings and re-classify undocumented flows as dividends. For the AI subtitle and voice-cloning studio, substantive continuity must be evidenced through local editing hardware, data-security infrastructure, employment contracts of subtitle engineers, contractual assumption of commercial risk and ownership of developed IP on the WFOE side.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False Invoicing and Fabricated Costs

Statutory sanction: Additional CIT and VAT collection with daily 0.05% surcharge; fine of 50%-500% of the unpaid tax under Tax Administration Law Article 63; false-invoice conduct falls within Criminal Law Article 205, punishable by fines and imprisonment of the entity's responsible personnel (maximum life imprisonment in aggravated cases).

Director personal liability: The legal representative, financial officer and directly responsible managers face criminal arrest, personal fines, disqualification from management positions and exit bans while tax debts are outstanding; the corporate veil is not pierced automatically but the responsible-person liability rule exposes directors to personal restitution for fraudulent bookkeeping.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation with Taiwan Parent IP

Statutory sanction: Special tax re-assessment under EIT Law Articles 41-48 with profit uplift, interest computed from the year of non-payment at the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points, a 10-year look-back period for special tax auditing, and fines under the Tax Administration Law for failure to provide same-period documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed the manipulative contracts may be held jointly responsible for the supplementary tax and interest where the arrangement is found to be an evasion scheme; criminal tax evasion liability attaches where the adjusted unpaid tax exceeds the Criminal Law thresholds; travel restrictions may be imposed during the investigation.
Disguised Dividends and Failure to Withhold on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Administration Law Article 69, the WFOE as withholding agent is fined 50%-300% of the unwithheld tax, and the beneficiary is pursued for the unpaid WHT plus daily late surcharge; recharacterised distributions trigger additional thin capitalisation and GAAR adjustments under EIT Law Article 47.

Director personal liability: The financial director and legal representative who approved undisclosed distributions are personally liable for the fine and may be subject to exit-control enforcement; intentional non-withholding is treated as tax evasion by the entity and its responsible persons, carrying imprisonment up to seven years and criminal fines.

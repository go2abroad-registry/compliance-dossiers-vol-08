Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital applies to S.A.S. entities; the incorporating shareholder freely fixes subscribed and paid-up capital, and any deferred installment subscriptions must be fully paid within two years from incorporation (Art. 4, Ley 1258 de 2008). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The S.A.S. is a Colombian tax resident (Estatuto Tributario, Art. 12) subject to worldwide income taxation at the general corporate rate of 35% (Art. 240 ET, modified by Law 2277/2022). Non-resident shareholders are separately taxed on dividends at 7.5% unless treaty relief applies. No territorial limitation applies to corporate residence; branch or nominee structures are taxed only on Colombian-source income.

Available election forms

Colombia has no 'check-the-box' or pass-through election; an S.A.S. is always a separate taxable entity. Available elections and preferential regimes include: (i) optional 'Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE)' under Articles 903-916 ET, which consolidates income tax, industry-and-commerce tax (ICA), and VAT into a single payment, available only if the prior-year gross income is ≤100,000 UVT (approx. COP 4.7 billion for 2024) and natural persons hold at least 50% of the equity; (ii) the creative industries ('Economía Naranja') 7-year income tax exemption for qualifying design, software, and creative merchandising companies under Article 176 of Law 1955/2019, subject to DIAN registration and compliance with employment and export conditions; (iii) mandatory election and disclosure of hybrid mismatch arrangements and CFC attribution rules under Law 2010/2019 (Arts. 18-2 et seq. ET); and (iv) partial imputation of corporate tax to resident dividend recipients. Fiscal consolidation and partnership pass-through are not available.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold applies to legal entities: an S.A.S. is mandatorily classified as a 'Responsable del IVA' from the date of commencement of taxable activities, regardless of sales volume (Art. 437 ET). Effective registration threshold is COP 0. For reference, the optional SIMPLE regime applies to companies with prior-year gross income of up to 100,000 UVT (approx. COP 4.7 billion in 2024).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are 'Excluidos' (exempt with input-VAT refund entitlement) under Article 481 ET. Goods criteria: (i) definitive customs exportation under Decree 1165/2019; (ii) sale to a non-resident purchaser; (iii) permanent physical exit from Colombian territory. Services criteria (including software development and digital design): the beneficiary must be a non-resident without Colombian domicile; the service must be used or exploited exclusively outside Colombia; and the service must not be integrated into the Colombian recipient's productive chain. Exporters may claim monthly or bimonthly refunds of input VAT attributable to export activity by filing the VAT return (Form 300) and a 'Solicitud de Devolución' through Muisca; exporters with compliant risk profiles may access accelerated refunds under DIAN's automatic-return program.

  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) or 'Manifiesto de Carga' (road) evidencing the physical exit of goods from Colombian territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / 'Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas' issued by a Colombian bank through the 'Formulario No. 1 - Declaración de Cambio' under the foreign exchange market rules
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, and proforma invoice executed with the non-resident buyer
  • Certificate of origin and INVIMA/SIC technical compliance certificates for toys (domain-specific, not tax evidence)
  • RUT certificate showing the exporter classification and the economic activity code 3240

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing ('Factura Electrónica de Venta - FEV') under DIAN Resolution 000165 of 2023. Each invoice must be generated in XML, signed with a certified digital certificate, carry a unique CUFE/CUDS code and QR code, and be synchronously transmitted to DIAN through the 'Sistema de Facturación Electrónica' via an approved software or technology operator. DIAN performs real-time validation and assigns acceptance/rejection status; the buyer's 'Aceptación Expresa o Tácita' event must also be reported. VAT returns are filed electronically on Form 300 through Muisca on a monthly basis. General designer toys under ISIC 3240 are not subject to special stamping regimes, but the electronic delivery document ('Documento Soporte en Adquisiciones') applies for purchases from non-invoicing suppliers.

DIAN cross-verifies export VAT refunds against the SIGLO XXI customs export database, electronic invoices, and the foreign exchange track records in the SADEX system; any inconsistency in the physical exit, beneficiary, or FX repatriation results in immediate rejection of the refund claim. Fraudulent export schemes trigger: (i) mandatory restitution of the refunded amount plus moratory interest from the refund payment date (Art. 670 ET); (ii) a 160% inaccuracy penalty on the VAT amount wrongfully included in the refund (Art. 648 ET); (iii) administrative fines for failure to repatriate export proceeds - up to 5% of the non-repatriated amount, plus suspension of export privileges (Foreign Exchange Regulations, Banco de la República); and (iv) criminal liability for customs fraud and tax evasion (Law 1762/2015, Art. 434B ET) with imprisonment of 2 to 8 years for knowingly signing false export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso is fully convertible for current account transactions, including payments for goods, services, and operating expenses, subject to documentary requirements and clearance through authorized foreign exchange market intermediaries. Capital account transactions are generally free but require systematic registration under the foreign exchange regime, especially for foreign direct investment (FDI), because the right subsequently to repatriate capital and remit dividends is conditioned on such registration. Profit remittance requires proof of foreign investment registration, corporate financial statements, tax compliance certificates, and documentation that the funds were acquired through authorized foreign exchange channeling. No material quantitative convertibility restrictions apply, but legal entities must operate through a local authorized intermediary for proceeds from exports to be recognized as validly channeled.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Colombian foreign exchange rules, export receipts from goods such as designer toys must be channeled through an authorized Colombian foreign exchange intermediary. The statutory default timeline requires channeling within six months from the shipment date, subject to extensions available from Banco de la República depending on the payment terms. The mandatory channeling ratio is effectively 100 percent of export proceeds: the exporter must either sell the foreign currency to an authorized intermediary or deposit it in a permitted foreign-currency account maintained with such intermediary in Colombia. Retention of export proceeds abroad outside the authorized market is not permitted for routine goods exports without an express central bank exemption.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia for enforcement of customs, tax, and financial-prudential aspects of the Colombian foreign exchange regime.

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as a primary or standalone banking rail; Moderate as a payment collection enabler only. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not licensed Colombian banking institutions and cannot legally replace a local account with an authorized foreign exchange intermediary for export-proceeds channeling or statutory FDI registration. They may be used to collect e-commerce receipts, but funds must be repatriated promptly into a Colombian authorized bank account in compliance with export proceeds channeling rules. Legal constraints include prohibition on unauthorized deposit-taking in Colombia, failure to provide a Colombian NIT-based account, and possible non-recognition under DIAN reconciliation if payments are held offshore instead of being channeled through the Colombian exchange market.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI proceeds must be wired through an authorized Colombian commercial bank using SWIFT MT103 messaging from the overseas bank to the SAS account. The wire must reference the company's complete corporate name and NIT to avoid reconciliation failures. The issuing bank should state the purpose code corresponding to 'Direct Investment in Colombia' in accordance with Banco de la República's foreign exchange form methodology, generally reported by the receiving Colombian intermediary on the applicable foreign direct investment declaration form. The beneficiary account may be a COP operational account or a permitted foreign-currency deposit account. The foreign investment must be registered with Banco de la República to establish future capital repatriation and dividend remittance rights. Supporting verification includes the intervening certification from the receiving bank, source-of-funds documentation, corporate approvals, and the registered foreign investment declaration number.Outbound trade payments to overseas suppliers, contract manufacturers, and logistics vendors are cleared through SWIFT MT103 transfers via the same authorized commercial bank. For goods imports, the importer must present an import declaration to DIAN and channel the payment through the foreign exchange market using the applicable import goods purpose code and declaration form. For larger or credit-backed transactions, a commercial letter of credit may be opened under UCP600 or a collection arrangement accepted by the local bank, with documents such as commercial invoice, bill of lading, certificate of origin, and quality inspection certificates conforming strictly to the LC terms. Advance payments require the underlying pro-forma invoice, a supplier contract, and, where required, proof of the ordering company's inventory commitment. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (Informe Local) is mandatory when the taxpayer's gross income or total assets equal or exceed 100,000 UVT (approximately COP 4.7 billion for tax year 2024). Master File (Informe Maestro) filing is triggered when the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds 300,000 UVT (approximately COP 14.1 billion). Documentation must be filed electronically through the DIAN TP portal; late or incomplete filings carry separate sanctions.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the DIAN applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), substance-over-form jurisprudence, and beneficial ownership requirements. An S.A.S. engaged in designer blind box manufacturing must demonstrate real business activity through premises, employees, inventory, production equipment, and actual risk assumption. Shell structures that merely invoice merchandise or royalties through a Colombian entity without operational substance will be denied treaty relief and may face recharacterization or veil piercing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: DIAN reassessment plus the 100% inaccuracy penalty (sanción por inexactitud) on the understated tax, monthly default interest, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Article 434A of the Colombian Criminal Code with imprisonment of 4 to 9 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. IThe DIAN may impose travel bans, asset embargoes, and criminal arrest warrants. A fraud conviction carries disqualification from managing commercial entities.
Artificial Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: DIAN applies the GAAR to disregard the entity, deny treaty benefits, recharacterize transactions, and assess tax at the owner level. Penalties for abusive tax schemes can reach 50,000 UVT (approximately COP 2.35 billion) in addition to back taxes.

Director personal liability: Under Article 42 of Law 1258 of 2008 (S.A.S. regime), the corporate veil is pierced where the company is used for fraud, tax evasion, or asset stripping. Shareholders and directors become jointly liable with their personal assets for all tax liabilities.
Anti-Money Laundering and KYC Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines imposed by the UIAF and Superintendencia de Sociedades of up to COP 1.5 billion; criminal money-laundering sanctions under Article 323 of Law 599 of 2000 carry imprisonment of 10 to 20 years and fines of up to 50,000 legal monthly minimum wages.

Director personal liability: Officers, compliance officers, and natural-person shareholders who knowingly direct or tolerate illicit fund flows face personal criminal indictment. The DIAN and Fiscalía may order asset freezing, travel bans, and extradition where applicable.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's Length Failure

Statutory sanction: DIAN adjusts taxable income under the arm's length standard, imposes a penalty equal to 5% of the aggregate related-party transaction value (not to exceed 20,000 UVT, approximately COP 941 million), and charges monthly default interest on the resulting tax deficiency.

Director personal liability: Directors and tax officers who sign the TP return without adequate documentation are jointly liable for the adjustment and penalty. Deliberate price manipulation with intent to evade tax is criminally prosecutable under the tax evasion article, with imprisonment and disqualification from board positions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taiwan (Republic of China) — Tax Administration (財政部賦稅署) with five regional National Taxation Bureaus (各地區國稅局) as the operational registration and audit authorities; primary online portal: 財政部稅務入口網 (https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw); e-invoice portal: 財政部電子發票整合服務平台 (https://www.einv.nat.gov.tw); company and tax one-stop registration portal: 財政部商工登記一站式線上申請作業 (https://onestop.nat.gov.tw).

E-government filing portal

A mainland-China-funded incorporator must first obtain investment permission from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs (投審會投資許可) under the Regulations on Permission of Investment in Taiwan by People from Mainland China; upon approval, the WFOE (有限公司) is incorporated through the Ministry of Economic Affairs company registration system, which simultaneously generates the 8-digit Unified Business Number (統一編號, the sole TIN used for both income tax and VAT); since April 2019 the tax registration (稅籍登記) is automatically synchronised with company registration through the one-stop online platform, and the new entity receives a tax-registration notification from the local National Taxation Bureau; the entity must then open a Taiwan foreign-exchange deposit account, complete labour-insurance registration, and separately apply for electronic Uniform Invoice issuance credentials (電子發票開立人註冊) with a Turnkey transmission certificate before issuing invoices; a foreign shareholder without a Taiwan address must appoint a tax agent (稅務代理人) or a domiciled fiscal representative to receive tax filings and withholding returns.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the Taiwan-incorporated, mainland-China-invested WFOE is a resident profit-seeking enterprise (營利事業) taxed on worldwide income at the flat 20% corporate income tax rate, with an additional 5% retained-earnings surtax on undistributed surplus (Income Tax Act Articles 3, 5 and 66-9); dividend distributions to the mainland-China corporate shareholder are subject to a 21% non-resident dividend withholding tax (Articles 88 and 92) unless a cross-strait double-taxation arrangement lowering the rate has been rendered effective and is applied by the withholding agent; the entity is not a pass-through vehicle and has no territorial tax exemption; a foreign parent operating directly in Taiwan without incorporation would instead be taxed as a non-resident branch on Taiwan-source income only. — A mainland-China-funded incorporator must first obtain investment permission from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs (投審會投資許可) under the Regulations on Permission of Investment in Taiwan by People from Mainland China; upon approval, the WFOE (有限公司) is incorporated through the Ministry of Economic Affairs company registration system, which simultaneously generates the 8-digit Unified Business Number (統一編號, the sole TIN used for both income tax and VAT); since April 2019 the tax registration (稅籍登記) is automatically synchronised with company registration through the one-stop online platform, and the new entity receives a tax-registration notification from the local National Taxation Bureau; the entity must then open a Taiwan foreign-exchange deposit account, complete labour-insurance registration, and separately apply for electronic Uniform Invoice issuance credentials (電子發票開立人註冊) with a Turnkey transmission certificate before issuing invoices; a foreign shareholder without a Taiwan address must appoint a tax agent (稅務代理人) or a domiciled fiscal representative to receive tax filings and withholding returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Taiwan-incorporated, mainland-China-invested WFOE is a resident profit-seeking enterprise (營利事業) taxed on worldwide income at the flat 20% corporate income tax rate, with an additional 5% retained-earnings surtax on undistributed surplus (Income Tax Act Articles 3, 5 and 66-9); dividend distributions to the mainland-China corporate shareholder are subject to a 21% non-resident dividend withholding tax (Articles 88 and 92) unless a cross-strait double-taxation arrangement lowering the rate has been rendered effective and is applied by the withholding agent; the entity is not a pass-through vehicle and has no territorial tax exemption; a foreign parent operating directly in Taiwan without incorporation would instead be taxed as a non-resident branch on Taiwan-source income only.

Available election forms

Taiwan has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election regime. Available taxpayer elections most relevant to a WFOE: (1) simplified small-scale VAT election — with National Taxation Bureau approval, average monthly sales between NT$40,001 and NT$200,000 (services) may be taxed at 1% of tax-inclusive sales with no Uniform Invoice obligation; (2) voluntary VAT registration — a business below the NT$40,000/NT$80,000 starting point may still register as a full 5% VAT taxpayer to recover input credits; (3) deemed-income election under Article 25 of the Income Tax Act — a foreign related party with no Taiwan fixed place of business may elect, through its Taiwan withholding agent and with bureau approval, deemed net-profit taxation at 15% of service contract revenue (and 25% for certain technical services) instead of the standard 20% gross withholding on Taiwan-source service fees; (4) dividend-distribution election — the WFOE may time and elect distributions to manage the interaction between the 5% retained-earnings surtax and the 21% non-resident dividend withholding tax; (5) e-invoice transmission-mode election — the taxpayer may choose Turnkey direct transmission, Web-service API, or cloud-invoice service mode for satisfying e-invoice clearance obligations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration within 15 days of business commencement — no general de minimis economic-turnover exemption for registered enterprises. Relief thresholds: average monthly sales of services not exceeding NT$40,000 (goods: NT$80,000) are below the tax starting point (起徵點) and may be exempt from registration and tax; sales between the starting point and NT$200,000 per month qualify for the small-scale taxpayer scheme at 1% VAT (no Uniform Invoice issuance); sales exceeding NT$200,000 per month require full VAT registration with Uniform Invoice issuance and the standard 5% rate. A foreign enterprise without a Taiwan fixed place of business supplying cross-border electronic services into Taiwan becomes liable for simplified VAT registration when annual Taiwan-source sales exceed NT$480,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, a zero rate (0% VAT with full input-credit refund) applies to (a) exported goods; and (b) services rendered in Taiwan but used outside Taiwan and paid for in foreign currency, including offshore BIM and structural CAD drafting delivered to a non-resident principal. Eligibility for the Taiwan WFOE requires: (1) the counterparty is established outside Taiwan and does not resell or consume the service inside Taiwan; (2) the BIM/CAD deliverables are used and enjoyed abroad — design files, drawings and models supplied to an overseas contractor or foreign project owner satisfy the use-abroad test; (3) the consideration is invoiced in and received in foreign currency (whether via wire transfer, Wise payout, or converted NT$ after inward remittance); (4) the zero-rated nature is stated on the electronic Uniform Invoice and the export-service file is identified in the bi-monthly VAT return (Form 401/403). Goods exports must additionally comply with customs export declaration requirements. The zero-rating claim is asserted at filing time, and the resultant excess input VAT is refundable on the bi-monthly return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口報單) for goods shipments, or a comprehensive Service Export Register for BIM/CAD engagements containing the signed contract or purchase order, the foreign-currency invoice, the deliverable acceptance record (驗收文件) and the foreign-client master-data file
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / electronic transport document evidencing physical export (required for goods-based export bundles; not applicable to pure offshore service exports)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (銀行結匯水單/外匯收入證明), or the equivalent bank SWIFT statement and Wise remittance advice, evidencing receipt of the exact foreign-currency amount from the non-resident counter-party within the permitted collection window
  • Zero-rated Electronic Uniform Invoice together with the E-Invoice Integration Service Platform transmission record (Turnkey upload acknowledgement) showing the zero-rate indicator and the 7-day transmission compliance stamp
  • Supporting technical documents — architectural and engineering scope-of-work, design handover schedule, and BIM/IFC project information delivery logs demonstrating that the service was performed in Taiwan but used abroad

A mandatory electronic Uniform Invoice (電子發票) regime operates through the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integration Service Platform. Every full VAT-registered business must issue e-invoices in the prescribed E-Invoice Standard (Turnkey) data format and transmit invoice data to the platform in real time; B2C cloud invoices must be transmitted within 48 hours of issuance, and B2B invoices for zero-rated export services within 7 calendar days, each invoice carrying a random-number stamp (隨機碼) and QR code for buyer verification; zero-rated export sales must be flagged as such on the e-invoice. The platform cross-checks invoice transmission against customs export declarations and Foreign Exchange Department record data. Failure or late transmission of e-invoice data incurs per-invoice fines of NT$1,500 to NT$30,000 under the Business Tax Act e-invoice penalty provisions, and habitual breaches may trigger suspension of Uniform Invoice issuance and audit-led reassessment at 5% VAT.

Zero-rated export refund claims attract intensified documentary scrutiny, including on-site audit (實地查核) and inquiry into related-party service arrangements. Under Business Tax Act Article 51, fraudulent zero-rating applications, forged export evidence, or over-declared refunds are penalised by up to five times the evaded tax (with a minimum fine of NT$30,000), followed by retrospective clawback of the refunded input VAT plus interest computed from the refund date; serious or repeated violations may lead to suspension or revocation of business and VAT registration. Erroneous zero-rating — for example where the BIM/CAD service is found to benefit a Taiwan-based party or to be re-sourced in Taiwan — is recharacterised as a 5% taxable supply, with reassessment extending three tax years (seven years in fraud cases) plus related interest. Tax Collection Act Article 41 separately criminalises fraudulent tax evasion with imprisonment for up to five years, and suspicious remittance patterns linked to refund claims may additionally trigger Anti-Money-Laundering reporting obligations to the Taiwan FIU.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible on current-account items under a managed floating exchange-rate regime; service trade receipts, profit remittances and documented operational disbursements are permitted through designated foreign-exchange banks once the underlying transaction is verified. Capital-account items remain substantially controlled: foreign-direct-investment capital contributions, cross-border loans, and outbound investments require SAFE registration, a legal capital and use-of-funds inspection, and adherence to the Foreign Investment Negative List. Profit remittance by a WFOE is allowable after completion of statutory annual audit, filing of the audited financial statements, payment of corporate income tax, and fulfilment of withholding obligations, subject to a board resolution declaring distributable profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The mandatory foreign-exchange surrender regime was abolished in 2012; there is no statutory fixed conversion ratio for export receipts. However, all export proceeds deriving from current-account transactions, including cross-border CAD/BIM engineering services, must be repatriated to a licensed domestic branch and recorded in the Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Goods-trade proceeds generally must be credited and settled within 90 days from customs declaration; service-trade fees must be repatriated within the contractual settlement maturity (typically no later than 180 days from invoice issuance unless extended by justification). The WFOE may retain such proceeds in a foreign-currency current account or convert them to RMB at the prevailing market rate; under-declaration, delayed repatriation or non-repatriation triggers SAFE penalties and automatic loss of simplified settlement procedures.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the supreme monetary and financial regulator; delegated foreign exchange administration and enforcement authority vested in the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and its local branches.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low, with narrow and non-statutory ancillary use only. Offshore digital EMI accounts, such as Wise multi-currency accounts, are not licensed receiving institutions under PRC banking law and cannot function as SAFE-designated receiving banks for inbound capital injection, export-proceeds repatriation, or tax-cleared profit remittance. A WFOE may leverage offshore EMIs for immaterial, non-trade operating expenses only if the underlying transaction is independently documented, the funds are sourced from an already-compliant foreign-exchange account through a licensed domestic bank, and no false representation of the EMI as a clearing rail is made. Using an EMI to bypass the SAFE banking loop creates a structural KYC and anti-money-laundering violation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwanese shareholder remits the subscribed registered capital in freely convertible foreign exchange (typically USD or CNY from lawful offshore income) to the WFOE's SAFE-registered foreign-exchange capital account at a designated domestic bank. The remittance instruction must clearly state 'foreign-invested enterprise registered capital contribution', cite the FDI registration code, and satisfy the receiving bank's authenticity check. The receiving bank will verify against the Foreign Investment Information Report and the SAFE Foreign Direct Investment basic information table. After receipt and conversion to RMB, the funds may be used only for the WFOE's permitted scope of operation, including payroll, software licences, sub-contractor fees and office overheads; prohibited repatriation to the Taiwanese investor before regulatory completion and prohibited entry into securities/real-estate investment unless approved.For settlement of cross-border engineering sub-contracts, software licensing, cloud-computing charges, and qualified royalties, the WFOE executes a corporate wire transfer through its domestic bank over the SWIFT network, normally an MT103 message. Service trade settlements below the USD 50,000 de minimis threshold may follow simplified documentary review; amounts above require submission of the service trade contract, invoice, receipt from the overseas counterparty, tax payment or exemption certificate, and the Cross-Border RMB/SEZ form. Where a supplier requires a documentary guarantee, a Commercial Letter of Credit advised by the Taiwanese beneficiary's bank can be issued, but for service transactions an L/C is rarely used because deliverables are intangible. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For Taiwan: related-party transactions exceeding NT$100 million in aggregate or NT$50 million with a single related party trigger mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File). For the China WFOE (paying/charging fees to Taiwan): no explicit statutory threshold, but the PRC's implementation rules require contemporaneous documentation (Local File and Master File) when related-party transactions exceed RMB 100 million (approx. NT$440 million) in a year, and a relevant file is required when the annual amount exceeds RMB 40 million for a single controlled transaction. tax authorities may require a TP report even under the threshold in high-risk cases.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan has adopted the profit-seeking enterprise economic substance principles via the 'Practical Guidelines for the Examination of Profit-Seeking Enterprise’s Negative Income' and the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under Article 66-8 of the Income Tax Act, allowing the tax authority to re-characterize transactions lacking economic substance. In China, the PRC GAAR and the 'reasonable allocation of profit' rules under Special Tax Adjustments require a WFOE to demonstrate that it performs meaningful functions, employs personnel, bears risks, and controls assets, especially when making service or royalty payments to a related Taiwan party. Both jurisdictions require the substantive operations doctrine: a mere conduit or contract manufacturer without real functions may be disregarded or re-characterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (逃漏稅)

Statutory sanction: Under Taiwan Income Tax Act, an additional tax of up to 2x (but not less than 15%) of the evaded tax amount is imposed, plus fines up to NT$60,000; criminal sanctions may include imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to NT$6 million under the Tax Evasion Punishment Act; in practice, authorities can impose an additional tax of up to 5x of the understated tax for serious violations.

Director personal liability: A legal representative or finance officer who willfully files a false return may be subject to criminal prosecution; directors who sign the return are liable for the accuracy of the filing; tax evasion can lead to travel bans (境管) and seizure of personal assets if the corporate tax liability cannot be collected from the company.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Taiwan tax authority may make a transfer pricing adjustment and impose an additional tax of 10%–20% of the adjusted income; penalty of 200% of the additional tax if the taxpayer failed to file a TP report or keep documentation; in China, the adjustment can result in a 10% to 50% penalty on the underpaid tax and a 5-year lookback extended to 10 years for special tax adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the company's tax debts if they are found to have committed fraud or gross negligence in approving artificial transfer prices; in both China and Taiwan, directors can face criminal penalties if they knowingly cause tax evasion; civil liability may extend to restoring any unjust enrichment from the tax authority.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under GAAR, the tax authority may disregard the corporate personality or re-allocate income to the true substance; the company may be derecognized for tax purposes, triggering full assessment of income at 25%, plus penalties; the beneficial owner status for treaty relief is denied, and dividend WHT is recaptured and penalized.

Director personal liability: Directors may be subject to partnership-style liability if the company is deemed a sham; a court may pierce the corporate veil to hold shareholders/directors personally liable for unpaid taxes; in China, legal representatives are automatically put on a 'blacklist' (失信被執行人) and banned from leaving China, and in severe cases face imprisonment for fraudulent tax enforcement.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Illegal Foreign Exchange Settlement

Statutory sanction: Under Taiwan's Money Laundering Control Act, fines range from NT$500,000 to NT$100 million and imprisonment of up to 2 years for individuals; in China, under the Anti-Money Laundering Law, fines up to RMB 5 million and criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Art. 191 for money laundering (5 to 10 years imprisonment); the State Administration of Foreign Exchange may impose a fine of 30% of the illegal foreign exchange amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or knowingly benefit from illegal remittance channels are personally liable; they can be criminally prosecuted for money laundering, face imprisonment, criminal fines, and personal asset forfeiture; when the corporate entity is a shell, courts may pierce the veil to reach personal assets of the controlling shareholder.
Failure to File Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: In Taiwan, failure to file the TP report can trigger an additional tax of 2x the understated tax and a fine of NT$10,000 to NT$50,000; the tax authority may disallow the loss carryforward and adjust the deemed arm's length profit. In China, the penalty is up to RMB 100,000 for non-compliance, and the tax authority may apply a 5% or 10% penalty on the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE may be deemed to have knowingly or negligently failed to fulfill a statutory compliance obligation, leading to personal joint liability for the subsequent tax adjustment if the company is insolvent; in extreme cases, the legal representative may be criminally prosecuted for refusal to pay taxes after a final administrative order.
Late Filing / Non-Payment of Corporate Income Tax

Statutory sanction: Additional tax of 10%–20% of the unpaid amount, plus daily interest at the 1-year postal deposit rate plus 1%, compounded monthly; if the return is not filed and the tax remains unpaid, the collection agency may escalate to enforcement proceedings and potentially apply a 3% surcharge for every 30 days of delinquency, capped at 10%.

Director personal liability: The legal representative (負責人) of a WFOE is required to pay the tax from the company's assets; if the company fails to do so, the tax authority may apply to restrict the legal representative from leaving Taiwan or China; willful non-compliance may result in compulsory detention or criminal sanctions for the representative (e.g., imprisonment up to 2 years) if deemed a refusal to pay.

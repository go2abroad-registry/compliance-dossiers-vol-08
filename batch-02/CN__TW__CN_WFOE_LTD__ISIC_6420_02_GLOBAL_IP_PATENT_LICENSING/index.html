Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Chinese company law and the Foreign Investment Law impose no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a WFOE engaged in IP licensing and do not require a blocked escrow account. The sole foreign shareholder must state a subscribed registered capital in the Articles of Association, contribute such capital within the contribution period specified therein, bear joint and several liability if other shareholders fail to make their own capital contributions, and may not reduce capital below zero unless a lawful capital reduction is registered. Unpaid capital remains a debt of the shareholder to the company.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through local tax service bureaus; online portal is the National Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) system and the provincial-level Electronic Tax Bureau platforms.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Complete name pre-approval and incorporation with the local State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the issued Business License contains the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all tax purposes. Step 2: Within 30 days of the license date, apply for tax registration through the STA Electronic Tax Administration; this creates the VAT, Enterprise Income Tax, withholding, and invoice registration records. Step 3: Apply for general VAT taxpayer status through the same portal when annual taxable sales will exceed CNY 5,000,000 or when input VAT refunds are required; attach the accounting and bookkeeping system evidence requested by the competent tax office. Step 4: For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in mainland China, obtain non-resident tax registration or establish a withholding record with the competent tax office of the payer, submit the applicable tax residence certificate for treaty or cross-border arrangement relief, and ensure the local payer handles withholding on royalties at the statutory rate. Step 5: Open a RMB and foreign-currency bank account, complete SAFE foreign exchange registration for trade in goods and services, and link the bank account to the export proceeds monitoring environment used by the STA for export rebate verification.

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for zero-rating only if the goods are physically exported from mainland China and released by China Customs, the exporter is a registered VAT general taxpayer, and the export proceeds are collected from abroad within the statutory period. Services qualify for zero-rating only when listed in the cross-border zero-rated services catalogue (for example, international transportation, research and development, design and offshore software services) and are provided to an overseas recipient for use wholly outside mainland China. For patent and trademark sub-licensing, the WFOE must prove the licensed IP is economically used outside mainland China, the royalty is paid from outside mainland China (including Taiwan), and the transaction is not a domestic licence; otherwise the standard 6% VAT applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (China Customs Export Goods Declaration, equivalent to an export bill of entry) showing the exported goods and HS classification
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for air cargo) naming an offshore consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or, for mainland China, the SAFE-monitored bank export collection and foreign exchange receipt certificate evidencing repatriation of export/royalty proceeds
  • Export VAT refund/zero-rate application set and electronic export rebate data filed through the STA e-Tax system
  • Cross-border licence or royalty agreement and overseas-usage documentation substantiating that the IP is consumed outside mainland China

The STA requires fully digitalised electronic invoices through the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform and the Electronic Tax Administration. Invoices are issued in near real-time and transmitted via API/e-invoice interfaces to the STA, with a unique digital tax-control code and anti-counterfeiting digital seal. General VAT taxpayers must verify incoming invoices on the VAT Invoice Comprehensive Service Platform before claiming input tax credits; high-volume issuers must integrate ERP systems through the tax authority APIs for automatic invoice clearance. No physical fiscal stamping device is used for fully digitalised invoices, but all invoice data are reconciled in the STA Golden Tax system.

The STA and the General Administration of Customs cross-check customs declarations, transport documents, invoice data, bank FX receipts and rebate claims using the Golden Tax and Golden Customs systems. If zero-rating/refund is unsupported, falsified, or the proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, the STA will deny the zero rate, claw back the VAT refund and input credits, apply a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the tax shortfall under the Law on the Administration of Tax Levying and Collection. Cases involving forged export declarations, false bills of lading, or fabricated receipts are prosecuted as export tax fraud or false invoice crimes under the Criminal Law, with monetary fines, confiscation, imprisonment of responsible officers, customs fines, and revocation of expedited customs privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

SAFE administers a conditional convertibility regime for the onshore CNY currency. The WFOE will enjoy automatic convertibility for all current-account transactions, including royalty payments and intercompany service fees, provided the bank verifies the underlying license agreement, commercial invoice, tax-withholding certificate, and the WFOE's SAFE registration. Capital-account transactions—equity injections, shareholder loans, and cross-border contributions for IP assets—remain regulated and require project-wise filing or registration with SAFE before conversion. Profit repatriation and IP royalty remittances to a Taiwan parent are classified as income remittances under current account and require the WFOE to prove actual economic substance, file enterprise income tax, and withhold tax; the bank will then release the foreign exchange after a one-time documentary review. There is no surrender requirement for export proceeds; enterprises may retain export receipts in foreign currency indefinitely or settle into CNY at will. However, capital-account settlement is subject to the negative list prohibiting investments in excise levies, securities markets, real estate beyond operational needs, and interbank lending. The overall policy objective is to channel cross-border IP royalty flows through verified banks while preserving capital account administrative controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated into a designated foreign-exchange settlement account at a SAFE licensed bank within 90 days of the customs export date unless an extension is approved; the mandatory conversion requirement has been eliminated, so the enterprise may retain 100% of the receipt in foreign currency or settle it into RMB at its discretion. The bank and SAFE will reconcile the receipts against the Export Verification System and customs data before the funds are made available for domestic use or onward external disbursement.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMI platforms (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, etc.) are not permissible as primary banking rails for the CN WFOE. The PBOC/SAFE framework requires cross-border payments and current-account settlements to be processed through domestic banks that have obtained the foreign-exchange settlement and sale license; none of the pure offshore EMIs meet this requirement, and PRC law prohibits them from opening local CNY accounts or providing capital-account services to a resident entity. They may assist only in non-onshore collection/dollar corridors, but all statutory compliance—capital verification, tax filing, and SAFE reporting—must sit with a domestic Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan parent must initiate a SWIFT MT103 remittance to the WFOE's foreign-currency capital account at a designated Tier-1 bank. The remitter's account must be in the same legal entity name as the shareholder recorded with SAFE; the purpose code must be the SAFE FDI-to-Enterprise Capital Injection code (e.g., 621904 or bank-designated), and the reference must state the WFOE's SAFE Foreign-Investment Registration Number. Upon receipt, the bank enters the credit into the Cross-Border Income/Expenditure Direct Reporting System; a licensed PRC CPA then issues a capital verification report to prove the paid-in contribution. Capital withdrawals and settlements are allowed only after the SAFE negative-list review and must be used solely for the company's operational purpose.For royalty and IP sub-licensing fees, the standard rail is SWIFT MT103 corporate-to-corporate wire, preceded by a tax-payment certificate for withholding WHT and a license invoice. The bank will match the payment to an underlying license agreement in the SAFE current-account records. When disbursements relate to goods with embedded IP, a Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) or Documentary Collection (MT400) can be used through the bank's trade desk; for pure IP royalties, the LC/DC route is less common but permissible if the transaction is structured as technology transfer. All outward payments must be reported with the appropriate purpose code and no cash-conversion circularity.Onshore CNY/USD, CNY/TWD, and CNY/EUR plain-vanilla forward contracts executed against underlying royalty licence exposureCross-currency swaps for hedging capital account exposures arising from Taiwan parent shareholder loans or intercompany debtsOTC European FX options for hedging forecast royalty receipts, subject to SAFE derivatives registrationNon-deliverable forwards are prohibited onshore for CNY; the WFOE may not use offshore NDF contracts

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMI platforms (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, etc.) are not permissible as primary banking rails for the CN WFOE. The PBOC/SAFE framework requires cross-border payments and current-account settlements to be processed through domestic banks that have obtained the foreign-exchange settlement and sale license; none of the pure offshore EMIs meet this requirement, and PRC law prohibits them from opening local CNY accounts or providing capital-account services to a resident entity. They may assist only in non-onshore collection/dollar corridors, but all statutory compliance—capital verification, tax filing, and SAFE reporting—must sit with a domestic Tier-1 bank.

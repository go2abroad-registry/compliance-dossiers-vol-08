Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; a nominal capital (e.g., COP 1,000,000) is permissible. Shares and capital may be freely agreed by shareholders. No escrow account is mandated by law. If foreign capital is invested, registration with Banco de la República is required within 12 months post-incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Cuerpo de Bomberos (local fire department) certification under NFPA 30 and NSR-10 for structural load and fire safety; occupational health and safety (SG-SST) clearance under Resolution 0312 of 2019, including risk assessment and emergency response protocols, submitted to the ARL (Administradora de Riesgos Laborales).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

DIAN - Direccion de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (National Tax and Customs Authority). Principal electronic portals: MUISCA (Modelo Unico de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) for registration, filing, and payments; and the SIE (Sistema Informatico Electronico) for mandatory real-time electronic invoice clearance. Official website: www.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned or foreign-managed S.A.S. obtains its NIT (Numero de Identificacion Tributaria) as follows: (i) execute the public incorporation deed before the Camara de Comercio of the registered seat and register the entity in the RUES (Registro Unico Empresarial y Social); (ii) appoint a legal representative domiciled in Colombia (or a resident tax agent ('apoderado') with express general powers when the S.A.S. is administered from abroad); (iii) complete the RUT (Registro Unico Tributario) pre-registration in MUISCA, selecting the activity codes for ISIC 5222 ancillary water-transport support, the 'responsable del regimen comun' VAT status, exporter status, and the marine fuel supply flag; (iv) obtain the digital PKI certificate for the legal representative; (v) await DIAN validation and issuance of the definitive 9-to-12 digit NIT with check digit, which activates the electronic invoicing account. Processing time is 2-5 business days. The company must also register in the SICEX/VUCE exporter registry and, where the bunkering activity involves customs-bonded fuel storage, complete the applicable DIAN and maritime authority authorizations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The Colombian S.A.S. is a separate, full taxpayer under the ordinary regime. It pays corporate income tax (CIT) on worldwide income at the 35% statutory rate for fiscal years 2023-2025, municipal ICA tax on industrial and commercial activities (generally 0.2%-1.4% of gross revenues), and VAT as a common-regime responsible taxpayer. The S.A.S. cannot be treated as a pass-through or disregarded entity and no US-style check-the-box classification is recognized. Profits distributed to non-resident shareholders are generally subject to a 10% dividend withholding tax once the entity-level CIT has been settled. Non-resident shareholders are not liable for entity-level taxes, but Colombia's CFC rules apply to certain passive income of foreign-controlled entities.

Available election forms

Colombia does not provide check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections; entity classification is statutory. Available elections: (i) the SIMPLE regime (Ley 1943/2018, as reformed by Ley 2277/2022), an optional single presumptive national tax available to entities with gross income of not more than 100,000 UVT (approx. COP 4,980 million for 2025) meeting the statutory conditions, replacing ordinary CIT and ICA; (ii) the Article 481(c) E.T. exemption flag for bunker fuel and lubricants supplied to international-traffic vessels; (iii) advance pricing agreements (APA) with DIAN on transfer-pricing matters; (iv) customs regime elections, including Zona Franca status for deferred VAT and customs duty on stored marine fuels; (v) the election to channel export proceeds through the official foreign-exchange market and to request the Banco de la Republica extension of the 6-month collection deadline under Circular DCIN-83. Deductions of payments structured to exploit hybrid entities or hybrid financial instruments are restricted by Colombia's anti-hybrid rules enacted under Law 2277/2022 (BEPS Action 2).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso is fully convertible in practice for current-account transactions, including trade and ordinary services; foreign investment and profit remittances are permitted for registered capital inflows under the regime of Inversión Internacional de Capital. Bunkering services are classified as an export of services for exchange-register purposes, and the Colombian operator may invoice in USD. Capital-account transactions, including domestic borrowing in foreign currency, external credit and FDI liquidation, require declarations and registration with Banco de la República through a permitted foreign-exchange intermediary. Profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders is subject to corporate income tax and dividend withholding tax, plus compliance with the annual exchange declaration; profits may be remitted once the audited financial statements measure taxable distributable profits and the investment is registered in Banco de la República's investment balance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia does not impose a mandatory conversion ratio or forced surrender of export currency receipts. The operative requirement is channeling through the authorized exchange market: export proceeds from bunkering and marine refueling services must be received into Colombia through an authorized intermediary (commercial bank, financial corporation or exchange house) within the statutory term established by Banco de la República from the date of invoice or export registration. Exporters may keep the foreign currency in a local foreign-currency account or convert it into COP at the market exchange rate, but the proceeds must enter the local financial system by the deadline and the related Declaración de Cambio must be filed. The precise calendar term fluctuates with prevailing regulation and should be verified against Banco de la República Resolution 3/2020 and its updated annexes.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (the Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Comité de Régimen de Cambios in issuing the foreign exchange statute, and with the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) in enforcing customs and cross-border currency controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for fully digital non-bank EMIs. Colombian law does not recognize a separate EMI license that allows foreign digital banks to open domestic settlement accounts or clear Colombian pesos on their own balance sheet. Cross-border fintechs may be engaged only as payment facilitators acting through a licensed Colombian bank; the bank remains the paying account keeper. For a marine bunkering scope rated Medium_Risk_Enhanced, local tier-1 banks require a physical or insured banking counterparty, and fintech-led onboarding with no local regulated custodian is generally rejected by Colombian compliance officers.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Corporate shell without physical operating premises in Colombia.

Root cause: Corporate shell without physical operating premises in Colombia.

Defense evidence: Cámara de Comercio certificate of incorporation and current legal representation; lease agreements for port-side offices, storage or fuel depot terminals; payroll records and social security filings showing local staff; business continuity and insurance policies; photos/video of storage, equipment, and bunkering site.
Non-resident or opaque ultimate beneficial ownership behind the Colombian S.A.S.

Root cause: Non-resident or opaque ultimate beneficial ownership behind the Colombian S.A.S.

Defense evidence: Apostilled and notarized UBO declarations identifying all natural persons with direct or indirect control or ownership of 5% or more; DIAN tax residency certificates; audited consolidated financial statements of the parent group; bank reference letters from recognized foreign correspondent banks; sworn affidavit explaining source of funds for capital injection.
Sanctions exposure or suspicious vessel flag, bareboat charter or customer country risk.

Root cause: Sanctions exposure or suspicious vessel flag, bareboat charter or customer country risk.

Defense evidence: Screened OFAC/EU/United Nations sanctions lists on all vessel owners, operators, charters and customers; independent maritime security vetting report; AIS/vessel-tracking records during operations; contractual requirement to pay each voyage in named bank accounts; flag and port-of-call compliance matrix; designated as a bunker supplier by local maritime authority.
Insufficient transactional documentation for marine fuel delivery and possible financing of fuel smuggling.

Root cause: Insufficient transactional documentation for marine fuel delivery and possible financing of fuel smuggling.

Defense evidence: Dated bunker delivery fuel receipts stating vessel IMO number, fuel type, ISO 8217 grade, quantity, delivery port and supplier stamps; sale invoices matching customs and DIAN records; weight or volumetric certificates; port authority notifications; commercial invoices and Standby LC or SWIFT MT103 references; payable/guarantee resolutions of the S.A.S.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound foreign direct investment capital injection is received by the S.A.S. only through a Colombian authorized foreign-exchange intermediary. The foreign parent sends a SWIFT MT103 wire to the bank account of the S.A.S., issuing the wire with purpose code corresponding to foreign direct investment and the beneficiary's NIT. The bank must present a 'Declaración de Cambio' to Banco de la República evidencing the inflow, and the S.A.S. must register the investment under the national FDI registration system. The required documentation includes a certification of incorporation, share ownership registry, UBO declaration, banking account number, and board resolution approving the capital injection; the bank will verify that the name of the foreign transferor matches the registered parent and will reject third-party unconnected transfers.The primary outbound clearing rail for bunker procurement and marine supplies is SWIFT MT103 for direct transfers to foreign suppliers, with MT202 optional for bank-to-bank transfers. For larger fuel contracts, the S.A.S. will issue a Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP 600 with the bank acting as issuing bank; a Standby LC under ISP98 may support performance obligations when the Colombian buyer or counterparty supplies partial fuel into a vessel. Domestic COP disbursements are cleared through the Colombian CUD (Cámara de Riesgo Central de Colombia) ACH system, while USD disbursements use correspondent banking via the United States or European clearing institutions. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% for dividends paid to non-resident individuals or foreign legal entities from profits generated after 2017 that have already been subject to the 35% CIT; dividends paid to Colombian resident entities are not subject to withholding at the corporate level when distributed from taxed profits. A 35% withholding tax (plus an additional 10% on the post-remittance balance) applies to excessive or preferential dividend distributions not supported by audited retained earnings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Annual gross income exceeding COP 100,000 million (approx. USD 25 million depending on FX) triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; separate thresholds apply for taxpayers with total gross income exceeding COP 10,000 million or net worth exceeding COP 50,000 million regarding country-by-country reporting obligations. Even below the documentation threshold, every Colombian S.A.S. must apply arm's length principles to cross-border related-party transactions and complete the set of TP disclosure annexes (Form 2448/Measuring Forms).

Economic substance scope: Colombia's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are formally embedded in the transfer pricing and anti-avoidance framework (Ley 1819, Articles 260-1 to 260-11 and OECD-aligned 2023 revisions). A Colombian S.A.S. engaged in bunkering must demonstrate that its decision-making, operational management, and risk-taking activities occur in Colombia. Existence of physical staff, fuel procurement contracts, port agency agreements, vessel scheduling logs, and significant accounting records is mandatory. The shell-company doctrine permits DIAN to disregard legal form, recharacterize transactions, impose a 35% tax on artificial profits, and apply gross-up penalties where the entity lacks operational substance. Marine bunkering additionally requires a valid maritime fuel supplier license (Dirección General Marítima - DIMAR) and compliance with environmental and customs regimes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corrupt Tax Evasion / Bankruptcy Fraud

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 4 to 9 years (Article 434 of the Colombian Criminal Code), fines of 200% of the evaded tax amount, and mandatory forfeiture of assets used in the commission of the offense. DIAN may additionally disregard transactions, impose a 200% accuracy penalty, and deny deductions. Directors and officers of the S.A.S. face an additional civil penalty equal to one (1) to five (5) times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is expressly permitted under Article 42 of Law 1258 of 2008 for tax fraud and abuse of the S.A.S. form. Directors who signed false returns may be joint and severally liable for the corporate tax debt, face travel bans under the DIAN administrative enforcement (restricción de salida del país), and be subject to extradition where a bilateral or multilateral criminal assistance treaty exists.
Shell Company / Substance Deficiency Structure

Statutory sanction: DIAN may recharacterize the entire arrangement, treat the foreign parent as possessing a Colombian permanent establishment, impose the 35% CIT plus sanctions, and disallow transfer pricing adjustments. The reputational and administrative penalties include a tax penalty of 20% of the omitted taxable base or the value of the improper benefit, and potential closure of the establishment by Customs (DIAN) for operating without a valid license.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allowed the S.A.S. to fail economic substance requirements are jointly liable for the assessed taxes and penalties. Under Article 43 of Law 1258, judicial authorities may impose prohibitions on contracting with the State and deregister the entity. Travel bans and freezing of personal bank accounts of foreign signatories have been enforced in recent DIAN proceedings.
AML / Cash & Fuel Smuggling Financial Movement

Statutory sanction: Under Colombian Law 1121 of 2006 and Amended Criminal Code, money laundering carries a penalty of 9 to 15 years imprisonment and a fine of 1,000 to 5,000 minimum monthly wages. UIAF (Financial Intelligence Unit) may impose administrative fines on the S.A.S. for failure to adopt the SARLAFT (anti-money laundering accountability) system. Vigilant enforcement under Resolution 2021-00580 of the Superintendence of Companies allows fines up to COP 800 million per infraction.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for AML breaches when they intentionally or negligently bypass protocols. The corporate veil may be pierced, personal assets seized, and the director prohibited from travelling abroad. Criminal exposure extends to imprisonment for tax fraud and conspiracy to smuggle fuel, with no corporate indemnification available.

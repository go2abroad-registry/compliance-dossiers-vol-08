Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by filing the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) through a legal representative before DIAN, attaching out-of-country incorporation documents, proof of representative authorization, and a local address for notification; a registered tax representative is mandatory for non-resident entities executing taxable activities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — SAS is subject to the general corporate income tax (Impuesto de Renta) on worldwide income at the 35% headline rate; no pass-through eligibility; territorial scope applies only to tax credits for foreign-source income; no tax-exempt zone. — Foreign entities must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by filing the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) through a legal representative before DIAN, attaching out-of-country incorporation documents, proof of representative authorization, and a local address for notification; a registered tax representative is mandatory for non-resident entities executing taxable activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — SAS is subject to the general corporate income tax (Impuesto de Renta) on worldwide income at the 35% headline rate; no pass-through eligibility; territorial scope applies only to tax credits for foreign-source income; no tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists. Statutory elections are limited to: (1) opt-in for the Régimen Simple de Tributación for qualifying small enterprises (turnover < 100,000 UVT) and (2) election to treat dividends as fully taxable income as opposed to the default deduction regime; hybrid mismatch rules under Colombian tax law restrict cross-border deduction denial, but no elective mismatch rejections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No threshold for Colombian-domiciled taxable persons (mandatory from first sale); non-resident providers of digital services to Colombian individuals must register when transactions exceed 40,000 UVT per calendar year (approx. COP 1,700,000,000 in 2024).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods and services physically leaving Colombia qualify for the 0% VAT export rate only when the seller holds an active exporter RUT (Registro Único Tributario) mark, the export customs declaration is approved, the goods are removed from the national customs territory, and the export proceeds are repatriated subject to foreign exchange regulations; offshore services require evidence of receipt and use exclusively abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación — DEX)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (Guía de Transporte)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas)

All sales invoices must be issued as electronic invoices (Factura Electrónica de Venta) and transmitted in real time to DIAN via API or authorized technology provider before dispatch; XML format with mandatory digital signature, and a valid electronic invoice must be generated for every operation; real-time validation (Validación Previa) and digital stamping are compulsory.

DIAN may claw back all VAT exemption benefits upon failure to uphold export documentation standards or repatriate proceeds within 180 days, applying interest plus a 100% penalty of the unwarranted tax benefit under Articles 647 and 648 E.T.; fraudulent zero-rating claims further trigger criminal liability under Article 402 of the Criminal Code and can expose officers of the SAS to up to 72 months of imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is freely convertible for current account transactions (imports, exports, services) without prior central bank license, but all trade-related payments must be channeled through the official foreign exchange market (Mercado Cambiario) via authorized intermediaries: goods transactions are subject to channeling regardless of amount, and services only when the annual individual amount exceeds USD 50,000. Capital account transactions (foreign direct investment, external credit, portfolio investment) do not require prior authorization but must be channeled and registered with Banco de la República in the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales (RII) to secure the statutory right to remit dividends, capital, and interest abroad. Profit remittance is unrestricted once the underlying FDI is registered, subject to the 35% corporate income tax, the 10% dividend withholding applicable to dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders (where profits have borne corporate tax), and the obligation to file the corresponding exchange declaration (Formulario No. 4) at the time of remittance. No convertibility restrictions apply to the SAS import payment plan (USD/JPY remittances to Japanese and Chinese matcha suppliers) provided the exchange declarations are lodged with the intermediary bank and the supporting commercial dossier (purchase order, commercial invoice, shipping documents, DIAN import declaration) is maintained for audit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from goods must be channeled through the Mercado Cambiario within six (6) months from the date the foreign currency is credited to the exporter abroad; the exporter may either sell the foreign currency to an authorized intermediary (IMC) or deposit it into a local foreign-currency account (cuenta de compensación) opened at a Colombian bank. Channeling is evidenced through Formulario No. 2 (Declaración de Cambio por Exportaciones de Bienes) filed within five (5) business days after the operation. There is no mandatory percentage conversion ratio to COP; export proceeds may remain in USD/JPY/EUR in the compensation account, but each credit must be individually identifiable and backed by the corresponding export declaration.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Junta Directiva del Banco de la República acting via its Regulatory Secretariat and Foreign Exchange Department), with supervisory enforcement by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) over authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries and by the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) for customs-linked import/export declarations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for primary treasury use. Colombian exchange regulation reserves FX intermediation (channeling of import/export payments, filing of exchange declarations, settlement with foreign suppliers) to registered IMC banks; offshore fintech EMI platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Murex, dLocal) do not hold IMC status and cannot file exchange declarations with Banco de la República. EMIs may serve only as auxiliary rails for domestic collection (PSE/PayU) or for low-value incidental payments, while all international trade settlements and FDI injections must be executed through a local IMC account to preserve FX compliance, audit trail, and the export-proceeds channeling obligation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign shareholders remit FDI by SWIFT MT103 to the SAS operational account at the selected IMC bank; the remittance beneficiary name must exactly match the company RUT, and the bank records the operation under the Banco de la República concept-code tariff applicable to foreign direct investment and files Formulario No. 4 (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales). No prior authorization is required for the entry of equity, but the bank will request the incorporation documents, the board resolution on the capital call, and the shareholder remittance instruction; the exchange declaration must be submitted within five (5) business days of receipt. Any subsequent capital increase must be registered in the RII before dividend distributions or capital repatriations are executed.Import settlement to matcha suppliers in Japan and China is executed through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers routed via the IMC bank, with Formulario No. 2 (Declaración de Cambio por Importaciones de Bienes) filed at settlement using the appropriate purpose code from the Banco de la República concept schedule for imports of goods. Alternative clearing rails include commercial letters of credit (MT700 issued by the IMC bank, MT707 for amendments) involving documentary presentation (commercial invoice, bill of lading, phytosanitary/organic certificate, packing list, insurance certificate), or documentary collection (D/P, D/A) against courier-delivered documents. Advance payments for matcha are permissible under a cash-before-shipment clause in the supply contract, provided the payee matches the supplier named in the import declaration and the DIAN customs filing is completed on clearance.Deliverable USD/COP forward contracts (forwards de venta o compra) executed with a local IMC bank, requiring an underlying import/export exposure and maturity aligned with the related trade settlement date; the notional cannot exceed the underlying invoice value.Plain-vanilla foreign-currency options (call/put on USD/COP, EUR/COP, JPY/COP) bought from or sold to an IMC bank, with the premium payable in COP and the contract linked to a genuine commercial cash-flow to avoid speculative classification.Non-deliverable USD/COP forwards (NDFs) available principally to non-resident counterparties hedging peso exposure outside Colombia; onshore use by a Colombian importer is admitted only in documented hedge structures executed through an IMC bank and linked to the underlying trade position.Cross-currency swaps (USD/COP) and interest-rate swaps tied to registered external credit or FDI liabilities are permissible for the SAS where the market risk exposure is backed by a balance-sheet foreign-currency position, and the operation must be reported to the IMC bank and included in the foreign debt or international investment registry where applicable.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) unified tax service platform (etax.chinatax.gov.cn); for customs/export declarations the China International Trade Single Window under the General Administration of Customs is the complementary electronic portal.

E-government filing portal

A CN_WFOE_LTD is a resident PRC limited liability company, so the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Procedure: (1) complete company formation and obtain the SAMR business license; (2) within 30 days of license issuance, log onto the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax), complete the taxpayer registration, and receive the USCC-based tax account and tax office assignment; (3) if the expected annual taxable sales will exceed CNY 5,000,000, or if customers require general taxpayer VAT invoices, apply for general VAT taxpayer registration through e-Tax before issuing VAT invoices; (4) register the export VAT exemption/zero-rating profile in the export tax refund module for cross-border software exports; (5) a Taiwan-resident parent shareholder is not issued a PRC TIN by this process; for non-resident withholding purposes the parent must deliver its Taiwan tax registration/TIN and, where treaty benefits are claimed, a Taiwan Tax Resident Certificate to the PRC withholding agent. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a PRC establishment earning China-source income does not receive a PRC TIN; the payer files non-resident withholding via e-Tax using the foreign enterprise's home-country tax identification number and tax residence evidence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity elections. The WFOE is taxed as a PRC resident enterprise on its worldwide income at entity level; the Taiwan shareholder is treated as a non-resident enterprise for PRC withholding tax purposes with no alternative classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must complete tax registration within 30 days of obtaining the business license, regardless of turnover; there is therefore no minimum threshold for obtaining a tax registration number. For VAT classification, an enterprise with annual taxable sales above CNY 5,000,000 (approx. USD 690,000) is mandatorily a general VAT taxpayer; below that threshold, the taxpayer may remain a small-scale taxpayer subject to the small-scale levy rate, but an entity that regularly exports zero-rated services is advised to register as a general VAT taxpayer in order to claim input VAT refunds on export costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% VAT applies when the goods are physically exported from the PRC, an electronic Customs Export Declaration is filed through the China International Trade Single Window, and export proceeds are repatriated to a PRC designated foreign exchange bank in accordance with SAFE rules. For qualifying cloud ERP/CRM software services sold to an overseas recipient, including a Taiwan-registered legal person, the export supply is eligible for 0% VAT (and refund of attributable input VAT) when the following statutory criteria are met: (1) the WFOE is a PRC general VAT taxpayer; (2) the service recipient is a non-resident legal entity with no PRC permanent establishment; (3) the software/SaaS service is consumed wholly outside mainland China; (4) the contract or order originates outside China; (5) the consideration is received in foreign currency, or RMB through an offshore RMB clearing channel, by a PRC bank and is properly reported as cross-border service trade; and (6) the WFOE files the export VAT zero-rating/exemption declaration and attaches the service contract, overseas recipient's tax residence evidence, and the bank receipt. Pure cloud delivery does not require physical customs clearance, but any tangible storage media exported as part of the software supply must be separately documented under the customs export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export Bill of Entry / export customs clearance record filed through the China International Trade Single Window; required only for physical goods including exported software media)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (carrier transport document evidencing the physical export movement; not applicable to cloud-delivered SaaS)
  • Commercial invoice / export invoice / SaaS subscription invoice issued by the WFOE and matching the contract value
  • Foreign exchange receipt / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or designated-PRC-bank export proceeds settlement voucher showing the offshore/Taiwan payer's remittance and the transaction code for cross-border service trade
  • Cross-border service transaction evidence bundle, including the signed SaaS/ERP license agreement, the overseas recipient's business registration or tax residence certificate confirming no PRC establishment, and any bank/SAFE current-account transaction record
  • Export VAT zero-rating/exemption declaration and the export tax refund (exemption) e-filing package submitted through the STA export tax refund system, containing the VAT invoice, payment receipts, and contract schedules

The STA operates the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) and the Golden Tax Project IV control system. General VAT taxpayers must issue invoices through the national e-invoice platform; FDEIs carry a tax-authority-assigned unique invoice number and anti-fraud digital fingerprint, and invoice data is transmitted to the tax authority in near real time. High-volume issuers and SaaS billing providers using Stripe Billing or Direct Debit must integrate through the STA's e-invoice API or through the e-Tax portal, with the invoice data automatically reconciled against declared VAT in the nationwide electronic invoice clearance environment. The system does not require a legacy tax-control device for FDEI taxpayers; instead, issuance, red-letter reversals, and data correction are carried out through the unified electronic invoice service platform, and any mismatch between invoicing, declared output VAT, and export refund declarations is flagged by the tax authority's risk-monitoring engine.

The STA, the provincial tax bureau, SAFE, and the General Administration of Customs carry out post-filing audits of export zero-rating claims. If the supporting documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or the foreign exchange repatriation deadline of 180 days is missed, the zero-rated treatment is reversed and the VAT becomes payable with late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day from the original due date. Where a refund/exemption is obtained by fraud, false invoicing, forged customs declarations, or fictitious cross-border service contracts, the STA will claw back the entire VAT refund and impose fines ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax underrecovered or refunded, in addition to the late-payment surcharge; serious cases involving fraudulent export tax refunds are prosecuted as criminal tax fraud under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, which may result in criminal fines, forfeiture, and imprisonment. Retrospective adjustments are generally assessed for the statutory three-year tax collection period, extended to five or more years in cases of evasion or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China's foreign exchange control is a managed float with conditional RMB convertibility. Current-account items, including software/CRM service exports, royalties and infrastructure cost recharges, are generally convertible through designated banks upon presentation of genuine transactions, without SAFE pre-approval in routine cases. The capital account remains subject to SAFE registration and case-by-case supervision: inbound equity contributed by the Taiwan shareholder, foreign debt, dividends, profit repatriation, and liquidation proceeds require a traceable FDI record, a designated foreign currency settlement account and verifiable source-of-funds documentation. Conversion of repatriated equity or trade FX into RMB follows payment-purpose settlement, not free market convertibility; banks record each receipt and the economic purpose. Repatriation of audited post-tax profits to Taiwan is lawful only after payment of corporate income tax, withholding tax if applicable, and submission of annual audit plus stamp-tax clearance. Practically, a China WFOE can open an export-foreign-currency settlement account, retain USD/EUR receipts for operating payments, and hedge using SAFE-approved FX products. However, capital outflows not tied to real commerce are blocked; cross-border netting and internal treasury concentration must be documented and booked with a bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Post-2012 regulatory framework: no uniform surrender percentage is applied to export proceeds. A mainland WFOE may retain incoming foreign currency in its current-account FX settlement account or convert to RMB as needed. The binding obligations are (1) all export proceeds for goods and services must be repatriated to a SAFE-licensed bank before the contractual payment due date, and for goods no later than 90 days after the customs declaration date; for digital services no later than 30 days after the invoice or deliverable date if payment terms are shorter; (2) proceeds may not be deposited or netted offshore without SAFE authorisation; and (3) if the total export collection ratio under SAFE's annual verification falls below the bank's threshold, the bank may require conversion of every subsequent remittance to RMB until the ratio is restored. For this WFOE, presume a 0% fixed conversion ratio, but do not retain export proceeds without an underlying recorded account, invoice and clearing rail.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: Unviable as a primary treasury bank; narrow viability as a cross-border collection and payment-service-provider complement under PBOC real-name controls. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut or other multi-currency fintech platforms are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE as deposit-taking institutions and cannot hold RMB accounts, process capital injections, or furnish a bank-confirmed ledger for SAFE annual inspections. A WFOE may use foreign PSPs to collect USD/EUR subscription fees, but settlement must funnel into a licensed bank FX settlement account via a PBOC-registered cross-border payment institution. Any attempt to park operating liquidity in an offshore EMI and pay mainland tax liabilities or payroll from it will fail AML and tax review and can trigger downgrade of the WFOE to B/C class trade status.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Complete the Taiwan-side investment approval if required, then perform SAFE FDI registration through a designated bank and obtain the FDI registration voucher. Open a foreign currency capital settlement account at a Tier-1 domestic bank with SAFE transaction purpose code for foreign direct investment paid-in capital. The Taiwan parent must remit from its own corporate account using SWIFT MT103; the remitter must be the registered shareholder and the beneficiary must be the WFOE. Deposit the incoming funds, then convert to RMB using payment-purpose settlement with supporting board resolution, contract and the bank's capital-account FX payment confirmation. For RMB inbound investment, route through CIPS using a Taiwan participating bank or the cross-strait RMB clearing bank while preserving the same FDI registration reference. After funds are credited, engage a licensed Chinese CPA to issue a capital verification report and record paid-in capital in the National Enterprise Credit Information System.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for cross-border vendor payments, software partner fees, cloud infrastructure costs, intercompany recharges and dividends after tax clearance; message fields must carry the SAFE purpose code for computer and information services or operational expenses. For high-value hardware or equipment procurement, use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit under UCP600 with negotiation through a Taiwan or Singapore bank. For recurring lower-risk trade settlements, use documentary collections in the form of documents against payment or documents against acceptance. All cross-border RMB settlements to Taiwan counterparties can be cleared via CIPS and CNAPS through the cross-strait RMB clearing bank. Domestic customer collections via Direct Debit or Stripe Billing aggregator settlement must be reconciled to the main FX settlement account and reported to the bank with subscription contract evidence.Deliverable RMB/USD forward contracts executed with a SAFE-licensed onshore bank under a real current-account invoice backlog, with maturity not exceeding 12 months.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps for rebalancing USD operating liquidity against RMB funding, provided the notional matches SAFE-registered services or trade exposures and the bank has NAFMII or ISDA master documentation.Plain-vanilla foreign-exchange options and collars used to hedge specific contract or intercompany invoices; speculative, barrier or exotic knockout structures are not permitted.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for the WFOE are not a permissible onshore hedging rail; if the Taiwan parent wishes to hedge group-level RMB risk, it must book the NDF offshore in its own name with no subsidiary guarantee or margin support from the WFOE.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Unviable as a primary treasury bank; narrow viability as a cross-border collection and payment-service-provider complement under PBOC real-name controls. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut or other multi-currency fintech platforms are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE as deposit-taking institutions and cannot hold RMB accounts, process capital injections, or furnish a bank-confirmed ledger for SAFE annual inspections. A WFOE may use foreign PSPs to collect USD/EUR subscription fees, but settlement must funnel into a licensed bank FX settlement account via a PBOC-registered cross-border payment institution. Any attempt to park operating liquidity in an offshore EMI and pay mainland tax liabilities or payroll from it will fail AML and tax review and can trigger downgrade of the WFOE to B/C class trade status.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA / 国家税务总局); electronic services via the China Electronic Taxpayer (e-Tax) portal accessible at https://tpass.etax.com.cn and the 'Taxpayer Service Platform' for all tax filings, invoice issuance, and registration.

E-government filing portal

The CN_WFOE_LTD (a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise under PRC law) must first obtain a business license from the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR). After incorporation, within 30 calendar days, the WFOE must complete enterprise tax registration at the competent STA office either through the e-Tax portal or in person. The electronic registration requires the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) shown on the business license, the articles of association, legal representative identification, and proof of paid-up capital. Upon successful registration, the STA issues the taxpayer identification number (TIN), which is identical to the USCC. The enterprise must simultaneously register for VAT, corporate income tax, stamp duty, and payroll/social insurance. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Chinese establishment that sells digital services into China, the foreign entity must either obtain a TIN through the streamlined temporary tax registration procedure or appoint a Chinese withholding agent to collect and remit VAT on its behalf.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None under Corporate Income Tax; a WFOE cannot elect pass-through or otherwise alter its PRC resident enterprise status. Under VAT, the enterprise may elect to become a general VAT taxpayer (even if annual sales are below the RMB 5 million threshold) to claim input VAT credits, or it may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer subject to the simplified collection rate (currently 1% as of the applicable policy window). No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist in the PRC tax system.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for PRC-established entities; all companies must register for VAT upon incorporation. For non-resident foreign enterprises supplying services to China without physical presence, VAT registration is mandatory when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 500,000; otherwise a Chinese withholding agent may be designated. An annual taxable sales threshold of RMB 5 million determines mandatory general VAT taxpayer status for domestic entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The cloud document automation and e-signature authentication services qualify as 'modern services' (information technology services) under PRC VAT rules and are zero-rated when consumed entirely outside mainland China. To claim zero-rating, the WFOE must demonstrate that: (1) the service recipient is a non-resident enterprise (including a Taiwan-registered legal entity), (2) the service is actually rendered and wholly consumed abroad, (3) the payment is received from abroad in foreign currency or RMB, and (4) the enterprise files the 'Export Services Exemption and Zero-Rate Declaration' with the STA along with supporting contracts. Services supplied to foreign individuals may alternatively be treated as exempt from VAT if they meet the consumption-outside-China test.

  • Service contract or agreement with the overseas (Taiwan) customer
  • Commercial invoice or electronic invoice
  • Bank inward remittance certificate / Foreign Exchange Settlement (FIRC) issued by a People's Bank of China-approved bank
  • Export services transaction list and service completion certificate (e.g., usage logs and server access records)
  • If any physical goods are bundled with the services: Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) and Bill of Lading

China mandates a real-time tax-control invoice system. VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-approved invoice management system using the tax authority-certified U-Key. Fully digitalized electronic invoices (FDED) must be reported to the Electronic Invoice Service Platform in near-real-time. Each invoice carries a unique 32-digit invoice number and a tax authority digital signature for anti-fraud authentication. Reconciliation of invoices with the central tax database is mandatory before any input VAT deduction can be claimed.

The STA conducts risk-based audits on export zero-rating claims. If zero-rating is claimed fraudulently or without complete substantiation, the STA will require repayment of all input VAT credits previously recovered, impose a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day of the unrecovered tax, and levy administrative penalties ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax amount under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law. In severe cases, criminal liability for tax fraud may arise under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, and the enterprise will be placed on the tax integrity blacklist, resulting in loss of future export zero-rating eligibility and enhanced scrutiny for subsequent fiscal periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible on the current account for trade in goods and services: the WFOE may receive and pay subscription fees for LegalTech SaaS under genuine contracts, and banks will register the transaction in SAFE's monitoring systems. However, the capital account remains strictly controlled. Equity injection from a Taiwan shareholder is categorized as foreign direct investment; it must be filed with the Ministry of Commerce and registered with SAFE before the remittance. The foreign currency capital must enter a designated FDI capital account at a Chinese domestic bank, and any settlement into RMB is restricted to the WFOE's approved operating purposes. Profit remittance to the Taiwan shareholder is permitted only after annual statutory audit, corporate income tax filing, and a declaration of the dividend distribution, and the bank verifies the relationship through the SAFE system. No mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds, but every inbound/outbound remittance must have a valid underlying transaction and, where exceeding the threshold, complete a foreign exchange declaration. This framework is stringent and supervisory, preventing an offshore EMI from acting as the sole treasury channel.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods trade, export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange account within 90 days of customs declaration unless an extension is filed in SAFE's monitoring system; no fixed percentage must be sold to the state, but banks may urge settlement for balance-of-payments purposes. For service trade, including cloud contract automation, proceeds must be collected under contract and invoice and can remain in a domestic foreign currency account without mandatory conversion. The WFOE must report each transaction through the bank in the SAFE cross-border payments system.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Stripe is not a licensed Chinese banking institution and does not provide settlement accounts to mainland Chinese registered entities. Using Stripe directly as a merchant rail for the WFOE would violate Chinese payment administration and SAFE rules unless an offshore affiliate in a Stripe-supported jurisdiction is the actual merchant. Fintech EMIs such as Airwallex and Payoneer can support offshore collections through partner banks but cannot complete FDI capital registration, RMB basic account opening, tax payments, or profit remittance formalities. The WFOE must therefore maintain its treasury backbone in a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank. Viability rating: Low for direct onshore settlement; Medium only as an auxiliary offshore collection service for a non-China sales entity.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under PRC law, a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is mandatorily subject to annual statutory audit regardless of thresholds, because it is a foreign-invested enterprise. In addition, independent certified CPA audits are statutorily required if any of the following quantitative triggers are met: balance sheet total assets exceeding RMB 50 million (approx. USD 7 million); annual operating revenue exceeding RMB 100 million (approx. USD 14 million); staff headcount exceeding 300 employees; or the entity undertakes capital reduction, equity transfer, merger, division, liquidation, or requests a tax refund or preferential treatment. The governing accounting framework is the Chinese Accounting Standards (CAS / PRC GAAP), which converges substantially with IFRS. For small enterprises, the Small Enterprise Accounting Standards may apply, but WFOE audits must be conducted by a licensed PRC CPA firm in accordance with Chinese Standards on Auditing (CSA).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File transfer-pricing report must be prepared and maintained where annual related-party transactions exceed New Taiwan Dollars (NTD) 50 million for any single related-party transaction category or NTD 100 million in total cross-border related-party volume. A Master File is required where the Taiwan entity is part of a multinational group with consolidated annual revenue exceeding NTD 5 billion, and a Country-by-Country Report is triggered when the consolidated group revenue exceeds NTD 27 billion.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan applies the economic-substance principle through Income Tax Act Article 43-1, the place-of-effective-management rules, and the general anti-avoidance doctrine. Although Taiwan does not issue statutory Economic Substance Regulations certificates, the WFOE must still maintain physical management, strategic decision-making, employees, technical capability, and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Taiwan. For a legal-tech SaaS and e-signature automation business, CIGA includes product development, encryption-key management, platform hosting, contract execution and authentication, billing, and customer support. If these functions are performed offshore, the Taiwan tax authority may disregard the legal entity, reattribute profits to the foreign beneficial owner, deny treaty benefits, and impose additional withholding tax and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Revenue Suppression

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act Article 110, the underpaid tax is penalized at two times the amount understated; where deliberate fraud or concealment is established, the penalty is increased to three times the underpaid tax. Tax Collection Act Article 41 additionally imposes criminal imprisonment of up to five years and a fine for willful evasion, and statutory interest runs on all outstanding tax liabilities.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the WFOE is found to be a mere instrumentality of its directors. Directors who signed false tax returns or approved fictitious invoices may be jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the unpaid tax and penalties. The tax authority may impose an exit ban / travel blacklisting under Article 24 of the Tax Collection Act until the tax is secured, and directors face criminal imprisonment of up to five years.
Transfer Pricing and Tax Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 43-1 empowers the tax authority to recharacterize intercompany transactions to arm-s-length pricing, add the adjusted profit to taxable income, deny the related deductions, and impose a penalty of up to three times the underpaid tax. Interest accrues from the original due date of the underpayment, and repeated violations may be treated as intentional tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved intercompany pricing without transfer pricing documentation or signed financial statements containing misstated intercompany transactions can be held personally liable under Company Act Article 23 for damages caused to the company. In aggravated cases, directors are named in tax enforcement proceedings with property seizure and travel blacklisting, and may be criminally prosecuted if the pricing arrangement is structured to conceal profit-shifting.
Shell Company Structure / Treaty Abuse / AML Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will deny treaty relief retroactively, re-instate the full 20% withholding tax on all outbound distributions, assess additional penalty and interest, and may disregard the legal entity under the substantive-taxation doctrine. If the structure is used to move illicit funds or conceal the true beneficial owner, the Money Laundering Control Act and the Company Act beneficial-ownership filing rules impose separate penalties, including monetary fines and criminal liability for nominee directors.

Director personal liability: Nominee or shadow directors face piercing of the corporate veil with personal civil liability for the company's unpaid withholding tax and penalties. Travel blacklisting is available to compel settlement. If the shell arrangement is tied to money laundering, directors face criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to seven years, asset forfeiture, and disqualification from serving as directors of any Taiwan company.

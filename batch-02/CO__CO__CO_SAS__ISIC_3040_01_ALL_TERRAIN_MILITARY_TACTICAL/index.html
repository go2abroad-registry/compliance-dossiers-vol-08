Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN), operating via the MUISCA virtual office and the official portal www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Colombian Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.) must first file incorporation documentation with a notary and register the company with the Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce). Following commercial registration, the legal representative must obtain a provisional NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and then complete definitive registration through DIAN's MUISCA/RUT platform, obtaining the RUT (Registro Único Tributario). The RUT confirms the entity's tax identification, legal status, address, and obligations as an IVA-responsible person and taxpayer. No permanent bank account, hiring process, invoice issuance, or customs activity may lawfully commence before the definitive NIT/RUT is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No pass-through or check-the-box election exists under Colombian law. An S.A.S. is inherently a limited liability commercial company taxed at the entity level on its worldwide income; shareholders do not elect entity classification. Hybrid mismatch treatment is governed only by hard-coded provisions in the Estatuto Tributario and Double Taxation Agreements, and no affirmative domestic election can re-characterize the S.A.S. for tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No numeric general-purpose turnover threshold: any entity carrying out commercial activities in Colombian territory must register for IVA and obtain RUT from the first transaction. An optional simplified taxation regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación) is available to eligible small businesses with previous-year gross income at or below COP 3,500 million, subject to excluded-activity exceptions; defense hardware manufacturing generally requires full, general-regime IVA registration due to licensing and customs controls.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods, including military tactical vehicles, qualify for a 0% IVA rate under Article 481 of the Estatuto Tributario where (a) the seller is the documented exporter, (b) the goods are physically removed from Colombian customs territory, (c) the export declaration is duly filed with DIAN, and (d) the transaction is supported by foreign-sourced demand, shipping, and receivable documentation. For services, zero-rating applies only where the service qualifies as an 'exported service' under Decree 1798 of 2017, including the recipient being a foreign entity without established economic presence in Colombia and the value arising abroad. Zero-rating does not 'exempt' the seller; it guarantees a refundable input-IVA balance (saldo a favor) for VAT paid on purchases.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) duly submitted through DIAN's customs system and accepted by the national customs authority
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or multimodal transport document, or Air Waybill where air freight is used, evidencing physical dispatch from Colombian territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Comprobante de liquidación y transferencia de divisas) issued by a Colombian financial institution, evidencing actual foreign-currency collection through the regulated foreign-exchange market

All IVA-responsible taxpayers, including export-oriented SAS entities, must issue electronic sales invoices (facturas electrónicas) through software validated and authorized by DIAN. Each invoice must be transmitted to DIAN's web-service API in real time before circulation; DIAN performs structural, semantic, and integrity validation, then issues a unique CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) and returns an 'Autorizado' status. Without this real-time clearance and CUFE, the invoice has no fiscal validity. Export invoices must comply with the same real-time transmission requirement, structured under the 'Exportación' typology.

DIAN may retrospectively reverse a zero-rating claim where documentation is incomplete, false, or economically fictitious. The exporter is then assessed for the uncollected 19% IVA on the gross export value, loses the right to input-VAT credits, and is subject to default interest and penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded. The statute of limitations is generally five years, but is extended where the RUT has not been properly maintained. Criminal tax evasion liability applies under Colombian law, with imprisonment typically ranging from four to nine years, and is aggravated for organized schemes involving false export declarations, forged bills of lading, or sham remittance certificates.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) operates under a managed float with unrestricted current-account convertibility for bona fide trade in goods and services. Capital-account transactions are broadly permitted but must be channeled exclusively through the 'mercado cambiario' (the FX market) via authorized intermediaries. Profit remittance is permitted post-tax, subject to the investor's registration in the 'Registro de Inversiones Internacionales' (FDI Registration) filed with Banco de la República on Formulario 3 (Declaración de Cambio). Remittances attract a 10% dividend withholding tax on top of the 35% corporate income tax, and the 4x1000 financial transactions tax (GMF) applies unless a Decreto 2116/2014 exemption is obtained. The key practical hurdle: an unregistered foreign investment renders capital repatriation illegal and exposes the entity to penalties under Law 9 of 1991; therefore FDI registration must precede or accompany every inbound capital wire.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds of goods must be 'canalizadas' (routed through the official FX market) within 6 months of the shipment date as per Circular DCIN-83, Capítulo 1. Mandatory conversion ratio into COP is 0%: proceeds may be sold for pesos, maintained in a foreign-currency account with an authorized local intermediary, or held offshore in a 'cuenta de compensación' registered with Banco de la República. In all cases a 'Declaración de Cambio' Formulario 1 (Export Declaration) must be filed through the intermediary; non-channeling triggers fines, loss of tax deductibility, and enhanced scrutiny in defense-sector contracts.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia) jointly with the Comité de Cambios (Foreign Exchange Committee) through Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83; enforcement of the FX regime is executed by the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low, effectively non-viable as the primary treasury rail. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions for the S.A.S. must be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the authorized intermediary's correspondent account, referencing the shareholder capital contribution and the taxpayer NIT. The bank files the 'Formulario de Declaración de Cambio por Canalización de Inversión Internacional' (Formulario 3) using concept code 4010 (capital contributions for incorporation and capitalisation of companies). Wires may arrive in COP or USD; if USD, conversion occurs immediately at the prevailing market rate into the operating COP account. For shareholder loans, concept code 4100 (external credit) is used and must be registered at the central bank; the GMF 4x1000 exemption under Decreto 2116/2014 applies to such registered external-credit disbursements. Transfer instruction should include: 'FDI COD 4010 - Registro de Inversiones Internacionales - NIT [SAS tax ID]' and must match the shareholder resolution and CILEM/defense compliance dossier if the capital is linked to defense contracts.Trade settlements follow three rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments with Colombian purpose codes (e.g., A0001 for import of goods) matched against the underlying Declaración de Importación; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary) governed by UCP 600 for large chassis, ballistic-steel, and surveillance-component purchases—with the issuing bank's confirmation if the exporter requires it; and (iii) local COP wires via the ACH (CUD) system for domestic operating costs, each debit subject to GMF 4x1000 unless exempt. Inbound export proceeds from foreign government or diplomatic buyers arrive via MT103 or confirmed LC; the export contract, commercial invoice under HS 8710, CILEM export permit, and end-user certificate must all be attached to the Formulario 1 (export FX declaration) filed with the local intermediary. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low, effectively non-viable as the primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Mercury, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not authorized 'intermediarios del mercado cambiario' under Circular DCIN-83; routing FDI or export proceeds through them breaches the mandatory channeling requirement, voids the Formulario 3 and Formulario 1 filings, and exposes the S.A.S. to DIAN penalties and blocked repatriation. Additionally, all major global EMIs categorically prohibit defense/military-equipment manufacturers under their acceptable-use policies. EMIs may be used only for minor non-operational expenses; the compliance backbone must remain with Colombian authorized intermediaries.

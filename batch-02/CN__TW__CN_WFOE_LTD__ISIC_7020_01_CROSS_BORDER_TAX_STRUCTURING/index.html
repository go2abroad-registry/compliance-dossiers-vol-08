Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, through the Integrated Electronic Tax Office (e-Tax) online portal

E-government filing portal

For the CN WFOE (Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise), the statutory TIN is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) automatically assigned at incorporation. Procedure: (1) incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); (2) obtain the business license, which displays the USCC; (3) open a Renminbi basic bank account in China; (4) complete tax registration with the local STA office within 30 days of license issuance; (5) apply for VAT taxpayer status (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer) and register for the digital e-Tax account; (6) complete the real-name digital identity authentication with facial recognition by the legal representative and financial officer. For a foreign non-resident enterprise with no permanent establishment but earning China-source income, the tax authority will register the company as a non-resident taxpayer under a special TIN issuance mechanism, usually performed by the withholding agent or through self-application on the e-Tax portal.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; however, every WFOE must register for VAT with the STA immediately upon obtaining its legal personality, irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is granted for (a) physical goods exported from PRC customs territory, provided the seller is a registered general VAT taxpayer, a customs export declaration has been filed, the goods have physically departed China, and export sales proceeds are repatriated into China in foreign currency or renminbi; and (b) eligible offshore services supplied to clients outside China, including qualifying consultancy services, provided the services are completely consumed abroad and satisfy the 'Cross-border Taxable Services Zero-rating and Exemption Catalogue' issued by the Ministry of Finance and STA. The service contract, evidence of offshore consumption, foreign currency receipt, and, where applicable, non-resident tax certificate must be maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (Bill of Entry) for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing goods leaving Chinese customs territory
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance / Bank Receipt Certificate (FIRC) evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • Export Contract and any amendments
  • Cross-border service agreement with detailed statement of work and deliverables
  • Proof of foreign client consumption (e.g., project completion certificate, offshore delivery evidence)
  • Electronic VAT invoice (special VAT invoice or ordinary invoice) with zero-rate marking for exported services
  • Non-resident enterprise tax certificate or tax treaty residence certificate where applicable

China maintains the Golden Tax System Phase IV as the centralized electronic fiscal control mechanism. All VAT invoices (Fapiao) must be issued through the official electronic tax authority application; ordinary electronic VAT invoices are generated by the national electronic invoice service platform, while special VAT invoices are processed through the value-added tax invoice system for real-time verification by the STA. A physical fiscal stamp is not used; instead, each invoice carries a cryptographic digital signature and QR code. Real-time clearance is mandated for special VAT invoices before final issuance, and all invoices, credit notes, and reversal records must be reported to the STA for anti-fraud matching.

China's export tax rebate and zero-rating regime is subject to STA and Customs post-clearance audit. Taxpayers must retain export documentation for at least five years. Failure to provide verifiable documentation, failure to repatriate export proceeds within the SAFE-imposed 180-day deadline, or discovery of fraudulent documentation results in mandatory reversal of zero-rated status, recovery of input VAT refunded, interest and late fees, a penalty of 50% to five times the tax under Articles 63 and 64 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law, and possible suspension or revocation of export tax refund qualifications. Criminal liability under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law applies to fraudulent export refund schemes, with penalties up to life imprisonment and confiscation of assets.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible for bona fide current account transactions, including trade in services and remitted foreign-invested advisory fees, with banks performing compliance reviews of the underlying contract and SAFE declaration codes. Capital account transactions remain carefully regulated: inbound equity injections must be settled through the SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration system, often via an FDI information registration with the local SAFE branch, before funds are released into the enterprise's CNY/FCY capital settlement account. Surplus capital may be swapped into RMB only after verification that the use matches the approved business scope and settlement conditions. Dividend and after-tax profit remittances to Taiwan shareholders require audited financial statements, corporate income tax payment certificates, board/owner resolutions, and bank-level certification of no prior outward remittance discrepancy. China is not a surrender regime in the traditional sense: exporters and service providers must repatriate eligible FX proceeds to their onshore designated repository within the statutory verification window but may retain them in foreign currency accounts or convert to RMB, subject to true underlying commercial substance and bank approval. Profit remittance obstacles arise from stale tax filings, missing equity structure disclosures, non-compliant invoice denominations, and downstream exchange-control declaration mismatches.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exporters, export proceeds must be repatriated to China within the SAFE trade foreign-exchange verification window, commonly 180 days from the customs export declaration date unless a longer period is approved by SAFE. For service trade receipts, including tax-advisory and transfer-pricing invoices, the foreign currency proceeds must be declared under the correct current-account purpose code and repatriated within the contractually scheduled collection period or the applicable SAFE monitoring timeframe. There is no fixed statutory percentage of proceeds that must be converted into RMB; the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%, and the WFOE may retain eligible proceeds in a foreign-currency deposit account or sell them to a licensed bank under its approved current-account foreign-exchange quota.

Central bank supervision: The State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms such as pure non-bank virtual account providers are not legally viable as the primary transaction or clearing account for a mainland China WFOE. PBOC and SAFE do not license foreign fintech EMIs to open domestic RMB/foreign-currency settlement accounts for Chinese resident entities, and such providers lack access to the SAFE current-account verification and capital registration system required for lawful inbound and outbound corporate flows. A mainland-licensed non-bank payment institution can be used only for ancillary collection scenarios, such as domestic e-commerce receipts, and never as the SAFE-designated clearing rail for FDI capital contributions, tax advisory fees, dividends, or trade payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE must first complete SAFE foreign-exchange registration for foreign-direct-investment equity capital and obtain an FDI/Foreign Direct Investor code. Inward remittances must be wired directly from the Taiwan shareholder account to the foreign-exchange capital account at a licensed bank, using the SAFE inward purpose code applicable to registered paid-in capital and a SWIFT MT103 with a matching field 70 narrative. The bank will perform a capital verification review before permitting conversion into RMB, and a CPA-issued capital verification report may be required after each tranche. The post-conversion RMB funds must be held in the entity's RMB settlement account and applied only to permitted operating expenses such as payroll, rent, tax, professional advisory fees and other verified business costs; no disbursement is allowed before the underlying authenticity documentation and tax filing evidence are cleared by the relationship manager.Because ISIC 7020 tax-structuring advisory is a service business, the dominant outbound clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for consultancy and management fees, supported by the service agreement, invoice, transfer-pricing file, withholding-tax proof and the SAFE current-account payment purpose code declaration. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections may be used only if the WFOE also undertakes goods-trading disbursements or the bank approves a trade-ancillary structure; in that event an LC can be issued by SWIFT MT700 and documentary collections processed as D/P or D/A, always with bills of lading, invoices, packing lists and customs records to satisfy China's trade-verification system. For RMB cross-border flows, the WFOE may also use the CIPS clearing rail when the counterpart bank accepts CNY settlement.Onshore deliverable RMB/foreign-currency forward contracts with licensed domestic banks for genuine current-account and approved capital-account exposures, usually up to one year, with rollover subject to underlying trade or debt renewal evidence.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible onshore in mainland China for RMB hedging; NDFs can be arranged only offshore in CNH markets outside SAFE's onshore supervision.Cross-currency swaps are permissible when matched to approved cross-border debt, FDI capital, interest or dividend flows and must be supported by board resolution, bank credit approval and SAFE/registration disclosure where applicable.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – official national tax and customs authority; electronic portal: www.dian.gov.co, taxpayer platform: Muisca

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or its local representative must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) before commencing taxable activities. Process: (1) appoint a local legal representative; (2) complete registration through DIAN's Muisca portal using a digital certificate; (3) submit the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) application with apotillado/legalized incorporation documents; (4) obtain RUT that evidences NIT and assigns tax obligations; (5) register for VAT (IVA) if required; (6) enable electronic invoicing through DIAN before issuance. For a Colombian S.A.S., NIT is also embedded at Chamber of Commerce incorporation and later validated by DIAN.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity or its local representative must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) before commencing taxable activities. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not offer a check-the-box regime. The S.A.S. is mandatorily classified as a Colombian resident corporation subject to entity-level taxation. No general hybrid mismatch elections are available. Certain limited elections exist for simplified regimes for individuals and optional VAT responsible-person status; exporters are generally required to register irrespective of elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary turnover threshold applies to corporations. Entities performing taxable activities must register as 'responsables del régimen común'. The simplified regime is only for individuals under 3,500 UVT. A corporate exporter must register for VAT irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods qualify for 0% VAT under Article 481 of the Colombian Tax Statute when the goods physically leave Colombia and are sold abroad in foreign currency. The exporter must prove the foreign sale, transport, and customs exit. Input VAT may be refunded or offset subject to DIAN review.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX / Declaración de Exportación)
  • Commercial Invoice issued under electronic invoicing rules
  • Bill of Lading (BL) or Airway Bill (AWB)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign currency repatriation evidence
  • Electronic export support document submitted to DIAN via Muisca
  • Packing List
  • Purchase and cost documentation supporting input VAT credits
  • Phytosanitary certificate or export eligibility certificate where applicable

Electronic invoicing is mandatory under DIAN Resolution 000165 of 2023. Invoices must be pre-validated and transmitted to DIAN in real time through authorized software or platforms before purchase delivery. Each invoice requires CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica). Export invoices require the mandatory export support document and are subject to DIAN fiscal clearance validation. API/web service interoperability and digital stamping with DIAN are compulsory.

False or unjustified export zero-rating claims attract full VAT recovery at 19%, plus moratory interest from the original due date. Improper refunds trigger penalties under Article 648 CIT of 100% to 150% of the excessive claim. Criminal tax evasion sanctions, fines, and imprisonment may apply to legal representatives. DIAN can audit five years retrospectively, seize assets, and freeze bank accounts to recover overstated refunds. Supporting documentation must be retained at least five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed free convertibility regime. Current account transactions — export/import payments, interest, dividends, and contractual settlements — are freely convertible and require only an exchange declaration to an authorised intermediary. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, portfolio inflows, and external borrowing, are convertible but subject to mandatory registration with the Banco de la República via authorised channels. Profit remittances by foreign shareholders are permitted without prior exchange-control authorisation, provided the local entity has filed the corresponding corporate income tax returns and has made the required withholding (10% on dividends paid to non-residents, plus the applicable underlying corporate tax at 33% for FY2024, scheduled to decline to 30% by FY2027 under current law). No surrender of profit remittances is required; funds may be sold in the market or remitted directly in foreign currency through a local bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of goods must channel export proceeds through the Colombian foreign exchange market within the term specified by the competent authority, which cannot exceed 13 months from the date of the export declaration (DEX). The full FOB value of the export invoice must either be surrendered to an authorised intermediary or credited to a foreign-currency deposit account held at a local authorised bank. The exporter must lodge the applicable exchange declaration (Formulario No. 1 – Declaración de Cambio por Exportaciones) within five (5) business days of payment receipt. Failure to comply may trigger administrative fines from the central bank and suspension from the foreign exchange market.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (central bank) and the Ministerio de Hacienda y Crédito Público (Ministry of Finance and Public Credit) jointly administer Colombia's international exchange regime (Régimen Cambiario) under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1068 of 2015, and Supplemental Circular DCIN-83. The Banco de la República issues mandatory foreign exchange regulations, operates the electronic registration system (Registradora), and licenses authorised foreign exchange intermediaries (bancos comerciales, corporaciones financieras, and sociedades comisionistas de bolsa).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) that lack a Colombian financial licence cannot lawfully maintain deposit accounts or processing rails for Colombian residents. Under Law 2155 of 2021 (FINTECH Law) and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia regulatory framework, only locally licensed SEDPEs (specialised electronic deposit and payment companies) or full financial entities may offer payment and clearing services. A foreign EMI could act only as a middle-ware platform in partnership with a licensed local bank, but it would have no independent clearing or treasury capacity. Consequently, the treasury blueprint should rely exclusively on regulated Colombian banks for SWIFT, FX, and clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment (FDI) equity injections must be wired from the foreign investor to the Colombian company's local account in an authorised bank using a standard SWIFT MT103 message. The payment must clearly identify the underlying purpose code 'Inversión Directa en Sociedades – Emisión de Acciones' (available in the Banco de la República's exchange-declaration codifier) to ensure correct registration. Funds delivered in USD or EUR can be immediately converted into COP via the bank's action in the foreign exchange market, or held in a foreign-currency deposit account. The bank will automatically generate the exchange declaration (Formulario No. 1 for exports; Formulario No. 3 for foreign investment) and transmit it to the Banco de la República.

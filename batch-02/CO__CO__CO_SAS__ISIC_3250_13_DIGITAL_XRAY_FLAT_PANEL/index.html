Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); institutional portal https://www.dian.gov.co; transactional platform MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicios y Trámites Automatizados) at https://muisca.dian.gov.co; DIAN also acts as the customs authority for export declarations coordinated through the Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior (VUCE).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.): (1) execute the private incorporation instrument and register it with the Cámara de Comercio of the company's registered seat; (2) the Chamber of Commerce transmits the incorporation data to DIAN through the RUES, and DIAN assigns the NIT and opens the RUT (Registro Único Tributario); (3) the legal representative completes the RUT inscription via the electronic pre-RUT process and identity validation (banking credential or notarial biometric check), obtaining the definitive RUT with tax classification codes 01 (manufacturer), 36 (VAT responsible), 37 (exporter), and 48 (self-withholder) as applicable; (4) update the RUT to incorporate the habitual export activity and the ISIC 3250 medical-device activity before the first despatch; (5) register as an exporter with DIAN in the Registro de Exportadores and, for medical radiology goods, obtain the INVIMA sanitary registration or export certificate; (6) contract the foreign-exchange intermediary bank (intermediario del mercado cambiario) to receive export proceeds and evidence future reintegro de divisas.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the S.A.S. is a Colombian tax resident (Article 12-1 Tax Statute) because it is incorporated under Colombian private law; it is subject to national corporate income tax at 35% for tax year 2024 on worldwide income, plus a 3% surtax on taxable income exceeding 300,000 UVT during tax years 2024-2027, municipal ICA tax, and the national consumption and carbon taxes where applicable. No income is passed through to shareholders; profits distributed to foreign shareholders are subject to additional dividend withholding at 10% for 2024 and 7.5% for tax years 2025-2027. Branches of foreign entities are taxed at the 39% branch-profits rate, but the standard S.A.S. remains entity-level taxed with no pass-through election.

Available election forms

No check-the-box pass-through election exists in Colombia; classification by entity type is mandatory. Available statutory elections include: (a) the Régimen Simple de Tributación (Simplified Tax Regime, Articles 903-916 E.T.) elective for qualifying S.A.S. entities with gross annual income at or below 107,500 UVT (COP 5,059,487,500 for 2024), consolidating income tax, VAT, and ICA into a single monthly payment; (b) the Colombian Holding Company (CHC) regime election under Article 883 E.T. for qualifying holding entities, offering a 0% or 1% dividend withholding rate at the CHC level; (c) optional group aggregation for economic groups under Law 2277/2022 allowing joint filing of certain tax returns; (d) the foreign tax credit election against creditable foreign taxes; (e) acceleration and expensing options for fixed assets used in medical-device manufacturing under Article 689 E.T. and sectoral decrees; and (f) transfer-pricing documentation simplification for SMEs. Hybrid mismatch elections are expressly unavailable: Law 2277/2022 inserts anti-hybrid rules that deny deductions to the extent the same payment is deductible or non-taxable in the counterparty jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic threshold for mandatory VAT registration exists: any entity habitually selling taxable goods or services must register as a VAT responsible party (Responsable de IVA) from the first transaction. The former small-trader exclusion below 3,500 UVT (COP 164,727,500 for tax year 2024) does not apply to ISIC 3250 manufacturing, so the S.A.S. is categorically obliged to register regardless of turnover. However, an S.A.S. with gross annual income at or below 107,500 UVT (COP 5,059,487,500 for tax year 2024) may elect the Régimen Simple de Tributación, which simplifies VAT compliance, and exporters must register with DIAN's exporter registry without any de minimis threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The S.A.S. may claim a zero IVA rate (0%) on export sales of digital X-ray flat-panel detectors where: (i) the buyer is a non-resident entity without Colombian tax domicile; (ii) the goods physically leave Colombian customs territory through an authorized customs post; (iii) a Declaración de Exportación (DEX) is filed and accepted by DIAN before departure; (iv) the goods are despatched within the term authorized by DIAN (generally 30 days, extendable in justified circumstances); (v) the export proceeds are received through a Colombian foreign-exchange intermediary within the statutory term of 180 days from the due date and credited to the exchange market; (vi) the related services embedded in the export (installation, calibration, technical support, software loading) are eligible only to the extent the service is used exclusively abroad or is directly linked to the exported goods under Decree 1669/2014; and (vii) the entity holds the applicable INVIMA export/sanitary certificate for the medical radiation equipment. Failure to satisfy any element converts the transaction into a domestic taxable sale subject to 19% IVA, plus fines and interest.

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX) accepted by DIAN through VUCE, containing the export registration number, ISIC/HS goods description, quantity, FOB value, and destination country.
  • Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), or Carta de Porte (land) evidencing effective exit of the goods from Colombian customs territory and arrival at the agreed destination.
  • Final Commercial Invoice (Factura Comercial) matching the DEX, including HS codes, INCOTERMS, unit prices, foreign-buyer details, and the FEV number from the electronic invoicing system.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and foreign-exchange records issued by the Colombian intermediary bank (Comprobante de Liquidación de Divisas) demonstrating receipt of the export sale proceeds in convertible currency within the 180-day deadline.
  • INVIMA export authorization or Certificate of Free Sale for the medical radiology equipment, packing list, certificate of origin when preferential tariff treatment is claimed, and the technical data sheet retained for DIAN and INVIMA inspection.

Mandatory Electronic Invoicing (Facturación Electrónica de Venta — FEV) applies to all S.A.S. sales and export transactions: each invoice must be transmitted in real time to DIAN's Sistema de Facturación Electrónica (SFE) through an authorized technology provider (Proveedor Tecnológico) or DIAN's direct web-service API. After successful validation, DIAN generates the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) or CUDS digital stamp and the QR code for public online verification (https://cfe.dian.gov.co). Invoices that fail validation have no fiscal effects, require an adjustment note within two calendar days, and trigger per-invoice fines under Article 652-17 of the Tax Statute. There is no physical fiscal-stamp machine in Colombia; the CUFE validation stamp functions as the equivalent fiscal clearance. Export FEVs must include the DEX number, the customs departure modality, and must be transmitted no later than the date of shipment, alongside periodic aggregate reports required by DIAN Resolution 000165/2023.

DIAN conducts automated cross-checking of the FEV, DEX, cargo manifests, carrier records, and the bank-channel data (SIEX/CRUCE) for every zero-rated export. If discrepancies are detected, DIAN may within the general five-year statute of limitations (Article 505 E.T., extended to ten years for unregistered or fictitious export transactions) retroactively reclassify the sale as domestic and assess: (i) the omitted 19% IVA on the full export price; (ii) monthly default interest from the date of filing of the zero-rated return; (iii) a penalty under Article 648 E.T. equal to 100% of the omitted tax, increased to 200% where the falsity involves the customs exit documents; (iv) additional per-invoice electronic-invoicing fines under Article 652-17 E.T.; and (v) fines under the foreign-exchange regime (Law 1931/2018 and Circular Regulatoria DCIN-83) for failure to repatriate or to sell export proceeds in the exchange market, with maximum penalties per operation that can reach COP 2,500,000,000. Criminal consequences apply where the false documentation is used to justify repatriation of non-export capital or to evade taxes: tax evasion under Law 1778/2016 carries imprisonment of 48 to 108 months, and money-laundering exposure under Law 1762/2015 adds a separate penalty when export documents are used to launder proceeds. Repeated requests for extension of the 180-day repatriation deadline increase audit scrutiny and may trigger automatic cross-examination of the underlying export contracts and carrier confirmations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a flexible exchange-rate and free-convertibility system under Law 9 of 1991 and Resolution No. 8 of 2000 (the Exchange Regime). Current-account operations, including trade in goods and services, royalties, dividends, interest, and compensation, are freely convertible when transacted through the 'mercado cambiario' (authorized foreign exchange market) using commercial banks or exchange houses. Capital-account operations, including foreign direct investment in the SAS, external credit, and investments abroad, are permitted but are subject to tracking and mandatory registration with Banco de la República via the relevant 'Declaración de Cambio' and, for direct investment, prior or contemporaneous registration of the investment under Form No. 5 (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales). Profit remittance hurdles are administrative: the foreign investment must be registered, the dividend must be declared at a shareholders' meeting, the payment must be made through an authorized FX intermediary with a channeling receipt, and withholding tax must be paid at the rate corresponding to the level of corporate tax borne by the profits (5% to 35%, with possible treaty protection). No quantitative limits apply to remittances, but failure to register or channel triggers fines, unavailability of foreign currency, and deferred tax consequences.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Resolution 8/2000 and supplementary Banco de la República circulars (notably DODM-83), exporters of goods must channel the foreign currency from each export into Colombia through an authorized FX intermediary within the payment term of the sale, but in any event within six months from the export date (shipment or leaving the country); services exports may be received within up to twelve months. The entire collectible amount must be either monetized into Colombian pesos by selling the exchange to the intermediary or, alternatively, retained in a 'cuenta de compensación' or applied to permissible foreign-currency obligations, with no free floating of export dollars without a special central-bank authorization. There is no historical fixed percentage surrender requirement; the full invoice value must be canalized within the prescribed window, and a 'Constancia de Canalización' (exchange-negotiation certificate) must be obtained from the bank for each operation. Non-compliance may cause fines, inability to use the exchange regime, and customs penalties.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia) — the Junta Directiva del Banco de la República, the Foreign Exchange Regulation Department, operating in conjunction with the National Tax and Customs Directorate (DIAN) for foreign trade and exchange verification.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury solution. Colombian exchange law and the Superintendencia Financiera require resident entities to channel cross-border payments and export proceeds through authorized domestic intermediaries. Offshore digital e-money institutions (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Mercury) cannot substitute for the mandatory local FX channeling, may not accept export proceeds for Colombia, and their use for shareholder capital injection is not recognized for Banco de la República registration. Such EMIs are acceptable only for ancillary, low-value operational disbursements (e.g., software subscriptions) where the beneficiary can be invoiced and the payment is not a Colombian-regulated FX operation. The main operating account must be held with a locally licensed bank. Cross-border fintech corridors that partner with licensed Colombian banks (e.g., via PSE/ACH) can be used for inbound client payments but are still subject to FX channeling rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SAS must receive the foreign equity contribution as a plain SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for credit to the account) remittance to an authorized local commercial bank account. The wire must clearly reference 'Direct Investment in Colombia — Equity Contribution (CO_SAS)' and be accompanied by the shareholders' contribution resolution and the registration number previously obtained or shortly filed using Form No. 5. The bank will assign the operation a Declaración de Cambio code; under the Banco de la República's table of operations, the concept code for direct investment in Colombia is typically Concepto 3620 (capital contribution), and the exact code must be confirmed with the bank to avoid recharacterization as external credit. The receiving bank must issue the Constancia de Canalización; funds are converted to COP or placed in a USD account subject to FX reporting. For amounts equal to or exceeding USD 100,000 (or COP equivalent), registration with the central bank's Registro de Inversiones Internacionales must be completed within 30 calendar days of the transaction; failure to do so will render subsequent profit remittances and disposition of assets non-channelable, causing fines and withholding surcharges.Outbound payments for procurement of goods and services (e.g., amorphous silicon deposition equipment, cesium iodide screens, electronic components) are executed through authorized banks using SWIFT MT103 for open-account settlement, advance payments, and intercompany transactions, supported by compliant Declaración de Cambio forms for imports of goods. For larger or deferred shipments, use commercial documentary letters of credit under UCP600 (SWIFT MT700) or standby LCs to secure milestone payments. Importers must present the import declaration (Declaración de Importación), commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and proof of payment; the bank may require a Registro de Endeudamiento Externo for deferred credit terms exceeding 180 days. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury solution. Colombian exchange law and the Superintendencia Financiera require resident entities to channel cross-border payments and export proceeds through authorized domestic intermediaries. Offshore digital e-money institutions (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Mercury) cannot substitute for the mandatory local FX channeling, may not accept export proceeds for Colombia, and their use for shareholder capital injection is not recognized for Banco de la República registration. Such EMIs are acceptable only for ancillary, low-value operational disbursements (e.g., software subscriptions) where the beneficiary can be invoiced and the payment is not a Colombian-regulated FX operation. The main operating account must be held with a locally licensed bank. Cross-border fintech corridors that partner with licensed Colombian banks (e.g., via PSE/ACH) can be used for inbound client payments but are still subject to FX channeling rules.

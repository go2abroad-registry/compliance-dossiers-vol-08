Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
First Board Resolution of Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada: The sole founding shareholder resolves to approve the initial share subscription of the subscribed capital of the company, accepting all issued shares. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — National Tax and Customs Directorate; primary e-services portal: MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) at https://www.muisca.dian.gov.co; institutional portal: https://www.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

A locally incorporated S.A.S. obtains its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) automatically upon registration with the Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio), followed by mandatory inscription in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) through MUISCA using the DIAN's virtual office; the S.A.S. must complete the RUT form with ISIC 4620 activity, obtain an electronic signature (firma electrónica) and password, and update the RUT within ten (10) business days of any relevant change. A non-resident foreign entity without a Colombian establishment that has any local tax obligation (e.g., obtaining refundable VAT credits) must also obtain an NIT under DIAN Resolution No. 000007 of 2021 by submitting apostilled/legalized incorporation documents, appointing an attorney-in-fact domiciled in Colombia, and completing the electronic RUT pre-registration in MUISCA; the DIAN issues the NIT after validating beneficial ownership, representative authority, and the economic activity code.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.) is a capital company with separate legal personality subject to corporate income tax (CIT) at the entity level; it is not fiscally transparent and does not permit pass-through of income to shareholders. A Colombian-resident S.A.S. is taxed on worldwide income, while non-resident sellers deriving Colombian-source income are taxed on a territorial basis. Exports of goods are outside the preferential Tax-Exempt Zone (Zona Franca) regime unless an explicit DIAN authorization is obtained; the standard S.A.S. with ISIC 4620 export trading activities is therefore taxable as a general-regime resident company.

Available election forms

Colombia does not implement the U.S. check-the-box regime or any elective pass-through treatment for S.A.S. entities; hybrid mismatch and CFC rules under Law 2277 of 2022 apply mandatorily with no opt-out elections. Available statutory elections include: (i) classification as a common-regime or simplified-regime VAT responsible party where statutory thresholds are met and the taxpayer is not an exporter; (ii) election to be taxed under the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE/SIMPLESR) for eligible small entities whose revenue is below the UVT ceilings and that do not engage in export operations; (iii) election to request DIAN's discretionary 'Gran Contribuyente' authorization; and (iv) election to claim excess VAT credits through refund (devolución) versus set-off (compensación) under Articles 815 and 850 of the Colombian Tax Statute (Estatuto Tributario).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for exporters: any person carrying out taxable transactions or export operations must register in the RUT and obtain responsible-party status in the VAT common regime from the first transaction. The simplified-regime ceiling of 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 174,296,500 for tax year 2025, with UVT at COP 49,799) is not available to exporters, who are statutorily excluded from the simplified regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including ginned raw cotton bales) are statutorily excluded (excluidas) from VAT under Article 424 ET and effectively benefit from a 0% VAT liability, provided: (a) the goods physically leave the Colombian customs territory and the national export declaration (DEX) has obtained 'levante' (release) in the customs system; (b) the purchaser is a foreign user located abroad and the goods are not reintroduced into Colombia; (c) the sale is duly invoiced with the electronic export invoice; and (d) the exporter maintains contemporaneous evidence of transport (bill of lading/air waybill), payment terms, and FX repatriation. Zero-rating of services requires the additional export-service conditions of Article 481 ET and Article 22 of Decree 1625 of 2016, including the benefit-obtained-abroad test and the full-use-abroad requirement; these rules do not apply to the present goods-export scope.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Declaración de Exportación (DEX) transmitted electronically (e-export) with DIAN 'levante' release authorization
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing physical shipment and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — Declaración de Cambio por Exportaciones (Form No. 3) evidencing FX repatriation and settlement through an authorized foreign-exchange intermediary
  • Commercial Invoice (factura comercial) / electronic export invoice containing the CUFE code
  • Packing List (lista de empaque) matching marks, weights, and bale count
  • Certificate of Origin (documento de origen) where preferential tariff treatment is claimed
  • Pre-shipment inspection certificate when required by the letter of credit, contract, or ISIC 4620 trade practice
  • Transit and final-destination documentation where the goods pass through third-country ports or free zones

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing: all sales, including exports, must be documented using the electronic sales invoice (factura electrónica de venta – FE) and transmitted to DIAN for pre-validation before the invoice is legally issued; the invoice must carry the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) and an XML schema validated through a DIAN-authorized technology provider (proveedor tecnológico) or directly via MUISCA APIs. Export operations additionally require electronic transmission of the export declaration (DEX e-export), use of the applicable document-support mechanism (documento soporte) for acquisitions from non-invoicing suppliers, and compliance with the DIAN's 2024–2025 invoice validation resolution requiring acceptance or rejection responses within the regulated period.

DIAN audits export VAT exclusions at the customs-vs-fiscal level, cross-matching the DEX declarations, electronic export invoices, and the FX 'reintegro' (repatriation) records filed with the authorized intermediaries. If the exclusion is overturned or the refund application is found improper, the taxpayer must repay the VAT credit claimed plus default interest, with the following penalties: (i) a 100% penalty for tax inaccuracy in amended returns (Art. 647 ET); (ii) a 20% penalty for improper refunds or compensations (Art. 670 ET); (iii) a 200% penalty where deliberate distortion or concealment is established; (iv) criminal liability for export smuggling and fraudulent export declarations under Law 1762 of 2015, with imprisonment penalties of up to eight (8) years, extended for criminal organizations; and (v) administrative closure of customs authorized-economic-operator (OEA) status, suspension of export registration, and disqualification from future VAT refund proceedings in the case of repeated violations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a free-floating exchange-rate regime with unrestricted current-account convertibility for imports, exports, services, and profit remittances. Dividend and branch remittances by a Colombian S.A.S. to non-resident shareholders do not require prior central-bank approval, subject only to: (i) prior registration of the inbound foreign direct investment with Banco de la República via the Declaración de Inversión Internacional (Form 4); (ii) settlement through the Colombian FX market (mercado cambiario) using an authorized intermediary; and (iii) payment of the applicable dividend withholding tax (7.5% statutory rate, reduced where a Colombia tax treaty applies) on the taxable dividend. Capital-account transactions, including equity injections, shareholder loans, and portfolio commitments, are liberalized and do not require administrative pre-authorization, but all dispositions of foreign currency must be routed through authorized commercial banks or financial corporations. Profit remittances exceedable only on the declared, audited, and tax-paid accounting basis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under DCIN 83-3, for goods exports, including raw cotton bales (ISIC 4620 / NAICS 424590), where the invoice value per export shipment equals or exceeds USD 50,000 or the aggregate of consecutive shipments reaches the same threshold, the full export proceeds must be channeled into the Colombian foreign-exchange market through an authorized intermediary. Statutory term: the proceeds must be received and presented to the intermediary within six (6) months counted from the customs export date (confrontada la salida); extensions to eight (8) months are possible for specific export lists upon Banco de la República approval. Conversion to COP is not mandatory: the exporter may maintain the USD balance in a foreign-currency deposit account (Cuenta de Depósito en Moneda Extranjera) at the same authorized bank. The channeling ratio is 100% of the declared export value; any non-channeled surplus is treated as unlawful FX subject to monetary fines (up to 200% of the non-repatriated amount). The exporter must complete the Informe Cambiario de Presentación de Divisas (bank-declaration form) at the time of the collection and retain supporting proof (bill of lading, commercial invoice, FMM/customs exit manifest, SWIFT MT103 credit advice, and broker commission schedule) for a minimum of five (5) years.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia, acting as the central bank and foreign-exchange administrator, together with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (financial conduct supervision) and the Ministerio de Comercio, Industria y Turismo for trade-related FX matters. The applicable normative core is Law 9 of 1991 (Estatuto Cambiario) and Banco de la República's external foreign-exchange regulation (Circular Reglamentaria Externa DCIN 83-3, Chapters 7 and 10).

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium. Colombian fintech payment institutions operate strictly as SEDPES (Sociedades Especializadas en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos) and may transact only in COP, with restricted wallet balances and no FX intermediation. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Airwallex) are not authorized intermediaries under the Colombian FX regime and cannot lawfully receive or settle the export proceeds of a Colombian S.A.S. without violating the channeling obligation. Their use is limited to ancillary Euro/USD corporate expense cards and third-country procurement disbursements; the statutory clearing and collection of raw cotton export receivables must remain at a Tier-1 authorized bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) is generally triggered when the taxpayer's gross income or total assets exceed 100,000 UVT in the prior year and the taxpayer executes reportable related-party cross-border transactions. For 2025, the UVT is COP 49,799, making the main threshold approximately COP 4,979.9 million. Separate filing thresholds for the Colombian TP information return (Form 120) and CbC report are set annually in UVT; operators must recompute based on current-year UVT even if their raw cotton export transactions are low-volume.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply offshore economic substance regulations as such, but enforces substance-oriented anti-avoidance rules. A ‘sociedad no operativa’ (shell company) is subject to renta presuntiva, currently 3.5% of net equity, and may have costs disallowed if it lacks actual commercial and operational substance. The GAAR (general anti-abuse rule), CFC regime, and transfer pricing rules can also reclassify or eliminate artificial structures. For an SAS engaged in raw cotton bales export brokerage, the company must maintain real staff, office/logistical capacity, bank accounts, contracts, invoices, and risk exposure; an entity that only acts as a pass-through commission channel for a foreign parent will be challenged by DIAN.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Income Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Inaccuracy

Statutory sanction: Sanción por inexactitud under Article 647 E.T. of up to 100% of the tax difference; correction of taxable income; default interest at the DIAN rate; criminal tax evasion charges under Ley 1762/2015 can lead to significant fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives and directors who knowingly sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax debt, subject to asset freezes, travel bans, disqualification, civil restitution, and criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Renta presuntiva of 3.5% on net equity, disallowance of deductions, application of GAAR to redetermine taxable income, and potential administrative cancellation or penalty under commercial registry rules; tax debt becomes due with interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the resulting tax debt if they approved the shell structure; travel restrictions, asset embargoes, and criminal liability for fraudulent insolvency or asset stripping may follow.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: DIAN may adjust the taxpayer's income to arm's length, assessing additional CIT at 35% plus default interest. Failure to file TP documentation triggers penalties under Article 651 E.T., up to 15,000 UVT; false data can also trigger an inaccuracy penalty up to 100% of the resulting tax deficiency.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incomplete or false TP documentation are exposed to personal fines, disqualification, asset freezes, and criminal proceedings if the false documentation is used to avoid tax.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering and Customs False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Colombian money-laundering law (Law 599/2000, Article 323) provides long imprisonment terms; foreign trade penalties under customs legislation (Decree 1165/2019) include fines, seizure of goods, and cancellation of exporter registration; SAR/MLRO failures carry administrative sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face criminal liability, personal fines, civil confiscation, travel bans, and disqualification from managing commercial companies; the corporate veil may be pierced to recover laundered assets.

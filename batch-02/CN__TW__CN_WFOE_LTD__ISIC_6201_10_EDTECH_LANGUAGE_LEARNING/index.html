Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital under Taiwan law. The Company may set its authorized paid-in capital in the Articles of Incorporation; all issued shares must be fully paid before incorporation registration. A foreign investor shall remit the approved capital into a Taiwan bank account and obtain a Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate. No governmental blocked or escrow account is required; the subscription funds remain at risk in the Company's ordinary bank account and cannot be withdrawn to the shareholders except by lawful capital reduction or dividend distribution.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance (Taiwan), represented by the National Taxation Bureau regional system and the Customs Administration; principal online tax portals: Ministry of Finance Tax Portal (tax.nat.gov.tw) and the e-Invoice Integration Service Platform (einvoice.nat.gov.tw).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: A foreign non-resident entity must appoint a Taiwan-resident responsible person or branch manager and secure a physical Taiwan business address. Step 2: If the entity creates a branch or place of business in Taiwan, complete branch registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs; the MOEA approval is a precondition for tax registration. Step 3: File profit-seeking enterprise tax registration with the local National Taxation Bureau under Article 28 of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act within the statutory period after commencement, submitting the MOEA registration certificate, duly authenticated foreign incorporation documents, local address proof, representative appointment, and bank account details. Step 4: The National Taxation Bureau issues the Business Tax Registration Certificate and assigns the Unified Business Number (UBI), which is the statutory TIN used for income tax, business tax, customs, and e-Invoice filings. Step 5: For a foreign electronic services provider without a fixed Taiwan place of business that sells B2C e-services such as app-store-distributed language-learning subscriptions to Taiwan consumers, file simplified business tax registration with the Ministry of Finance under Article 28-1 of the Business Tax Act; the MOF assigns a TW-prefixed Business Tax Registration Number and requires quarterly returns and payment through the MOF online system. Step 6: Register as an e-Invoice filer on the MOF e-Invoice Platform, establish API or Turnkey transmission credentials, and appoint a tax agent where required for ongoing compliance.

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary turnover threshold for Taiwanese domestic sellers or local branches; registration is mandatory before any taxable sale. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act, zero rating applies to goods physically exported from Taiwan, services directly connected with exports, and services supplied to a foreign person for use entirely outside Taiwan. A VAT-registered EdTech subscription business claiming 0% must issue a zero-rated Uniform Invoice and substantiate that the language-learning service is delivered to and consumed by a non-Taiwan final user. For app-store distribution, the platform settlement reports, billing-country data, geolocation/IP evidence, and foreign-currency remittance are required to prove foreign consumption; absent such evidence, Taiwan VAT at 5% is assessed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export bill of entry) accepted by the Customs Administration for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing export from Taiwan to the foreign destination
  • Zero-rated Uniform Invoice issued to the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice and signed service contract identifying the foreign purchaser and place of use/consumption
  • Bank foreign-currency receipt or settlement certificate, or foreign remittance payment advice, evidencing collection from the foreign buyer
  • App-store or platform sales report with user billing-country, geolocation, and transaction timestamps for digital EdTech services

Taiwan requires VAT-registered businesses to issue Uniform Invoices for each taxable sale, and e-Invoice data must be transmitted to the MOF e-Invoice Integration Service Platform via API/Turnkey integration in MOF-specified formats. The platform assigns unique invoice numbers and random anti-fraud codes/QR codes and reconciles e-Invoice data with VAT returns and customs/export records. Taiwan does not use a physical fiscal-lock/stamping device, but accepted e-Invoice transmission constitutes the fiscal clearance event, with unreported or unmatched invoices generating automated audit flags.

The National Taxation Bureau and the Customs Administration cross-check export declarations, e-Invoice records, app-store merchant reports, and bank foreign-exchange receipts. Zero-rating is reversed if the goods are not supported by a valid export declaration or the foreign-currency proceeds are not received within the statutory 180-day repatriation period, or if digital-service evidence does not demonstrate consumption outside Taiwan; reassessed VAT at 5%, late interest, and penalties will be charged. Under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act, false, fraudulent, or unsubstantiated zero-rating/refund claims attract a penalty of not less than five and not more than fifteen times the evaded tax, and fraudulent conduct is additionally subject to criminal liability and retrospective clawback audit recovery for seven years under the Tax Collection Act.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime under which the RMB is convertible only for current-account transactions, while capital-account transactions remain subject to restrictive licensing, registration, and authenticity checks. Inbound direct investment from a Taiwan shareholder into the CN_WFOE_LTD is classified as a capital-account receipt and therefore requires full FDI foreign-exchange registration under SAFE rules, the opening of a designated foreign-exchange capital account at a SAFE-authorized domestic bank, and the subsequent conversion of the registered capital into RMB only for genuine underlying obligations that are within the permitted negative-list scope. Current-account items, such as EdTech subscription revenues, software licensing fees, and operating expenses, are generally convertible, but the domestic bank must validate each payment using contracts, commercial invoices, tax documentation, and available customs or digital records. Dividend and profit repatriation to the Taiwanese parent is permissible after the annual statutory audit, but requires audited financial statements, corporate income-tax payment proof, a formal shareholder dividend resolution, and the use of SAFE-approved payment rails. Borrowing from offshore related parties triggers the PBOC macro-prudential full-cycle constraint, and any external guarantee, cross-border financing, or capital injection must be tracked by the bank and SAFE. The practical result is stringent, multi-layered regulatory surveillance of every cross-border remittance, with no silent free movement of RMB outside the permitted current-account and registered capital channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current PRC regulations, there is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; enterprises may retain export receipts in a foreign-currency current-account balance or sell them for RMB at a designated bank. However, exporters are required to repatriate export proceeds to the PRC within the statutory verification period, generally 180 days from the date of customs export declaration unless an extension has been approved by SAFE. The receiving bank must perform electronic matching against the customs declaration through the International Trade Single Window, and any unexplained non-payment or late recovery must be justified with a qualified bank statement and underlying contract before the export verification is closed.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs, including Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer, are not legally viable as a primary treasury or account-raising channel for a China-incorporated WFOE. These entities do not hold PBOC or SAFE licences to open RMB settlement accounts, receive export proceeds, or perform China foreign-exchange declaration and tax reconciliation for a PRC legal person. They can only operate as ancillary ancillary payment intermediaries for certain outbound low-value supplier payments when a licensed domestic bank remains the master account provider. All inbound capital injections and export proceeds for the WFOE must ultimately settle into a bank licensed under PBOC/SAMR rules; therefore, fintech EMI-only onboarding is structurally barred and must be avoided.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital from the Taiwanese shareholder must be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the CN_WFOE_LTD's SAFE-verified FDI foreign-exchange capital account at a designated Tier-1 bank. The remittance must reference the SAFE foreign-investment registration code and the receiving bank must classify it as inbound direct-investment capital injection using the applicable SAFE purpose code. The bank will perform negative-list screening, currency convertibility registration, and payment-amount verification against the approved registered capital. After receipt, a Chinese CPA must issue a capital-verification opinion; the proceeds are then converted into RMB in the capital settlement account and may be used only for genuine operating purposes such as payroll, rent, software procurement, cloud services, and platform distribution fees within the approved business scope.For outbound operational and trade disbursements, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers, covering EdTech platform fees, advertising, cloud infrastructure, software licences, and intercompany service fees. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are permissible for physical goods import or export, but are not the normal rail for digital subscription services; when used, the bank must align the documentary presentation with the customs bills of lading and the SAFE cargo verification system. They can only operate as ancillary ancillary payment intermediaries for certain outbound low-value supplier payments when a licensed domestic bank remains the master account provider. All inbound capital injections and export proceeds for the WFOE must ultimately settle into a bank licensed under PBOC/SAMR rules; therefore, fintech EMI-only onboarding is structurally barred and must be avoided.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% of taxable net income for profit-seeking enterprises, plus a 5% undistributed-surplus surtax on retained earnings not distributed in the following year, producing an approximate effective cash corporate tax of 24% if the EdTech WFOE retains its profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on gross cash dividends and 21% on stock dividends valued at par or paid-in amount when distributed by the Taiwan WFOE to a foreign corporate shareholder not maintaining a Taiwan registered branch; this withholding is separate from the 20% CIT and the 5% undistributed-surplus surtax.

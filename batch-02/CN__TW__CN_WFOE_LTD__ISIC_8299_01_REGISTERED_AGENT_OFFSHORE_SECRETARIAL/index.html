Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None: Taiwan Companies Act does not impose a minimum paid-up capital for a limited liability company; however, the subscribers must fully subscribe and pay for the issued share capital before registration, and the paid-in capital must be verified by a CPA certificate and deposited in a Taiwanese bank escrow-style company capital account pending the issuance of the company registration certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable at the heavy-industrial level; for the administrative office, the local fire department must certify the fire safety inspection and exit routes under the Fire Services Act. The premises must meet the structural load requirements of the Building Act for office occupancy, and the company must complete occupational hazard notification only if it employs more than 300 workers; otherwise, a simple self-assessment under the Occupational Safety and Health Act is sufficient.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, exercised through local STA service bureaus; online electronic tax filing is administered via the STA Electronic Tax Bureau portal (etax.chinatax.gov.cn).

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors establishing the WFOE must first complete enterprise registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Upon issuance of the Business License, the WFOE receives an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is also the taxpayer identification number for tax purposes. The USCC is automatically shared with the STA through the multi-department 'one-stop' registration integration, so no separate tax registration application is required. Within the statutory post-incorporation period, the WFOE must use the STA Electronic Tax Bureau to complete legal representative biometric real-name verification, financial accounting system filing, bank account reporting, VAT taxpayer status election (small-scale taxpayer or general taxpayer), and, where cross-border services are contemplated, registration for cross-border zero-rating or VAT exemption status. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment that earns China-source income obtains a non-resident enterprise tax registration through its appointed withholding agent or through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau non-resident registration module, and receives a separate tax reference number for withholding compliance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for VAT registration; all Chinese enterprises are mandatorily registered upon incorporation. General taxpayer status is mandatory when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, while taxpayers below that threshold may elect small-scale taxpayer simplified taxation subject to STA rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (a) goods physically exported from China under customs supervision where title passes to an overseas buyer and the goods leave the customs territory; and (b) cross-border taxable services supplied to wholly overseas recipients that fall within the STA-approved zero-rated or VAT-exempt cross-border services scope, including qualifying offshore business and secretarial services, provided the service recipient is outside China, the service is consumed outside China, and payment is received in foreign currency. All zero-rated claims require contemporaneous documentation proving the export or offshore supply nature of the transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Export Goods Declaration) with approved customs release information
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) showing the exporter, overseas consignee, and port of discharge/customs exit
  • Full set of commercial invoices and packing lists matching the customs declaration
  • Export sales contract or purchase order identifying the overseas buyer and contract terms
  • Bank foreign exchange collection voucher or inward remittance receipt issued by a SAFE-designated bank evidencing FX inflow
  • VAT invoice issued in accordance with export VAT zero-rating rules (if any)
  • Electronic export tax rebate application/supporting log filed through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau

All VAT-registered entities must issue invoices through the State Taxation Administration-approved e-invoice platform using the unified electronic invoice system. Full digital electronic invoices are transmitted to the STA in real time; the platform performs automated anti-fraud checks including unique invoice number allocation, buyer-seller master data matching, tax control UKey digital signature validation, and cross-reference with VAT returns, customs declarations, and bank receipts under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. There is no conventional physical 'pre-clearance stamp' requirement; instead, the digital platform performs real-time clearance and risk blocking before the invoice can be legally considered valid.

The STA and customs jointly scrutinize zero-rating claims. If the customs export declaration, transport documentation, or foreign exchange receipt is false, incomplete, or inconsistent, or if the foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the mandatory deadline unless validly extended, the zero-rating is retrospectively revoked, VAT becomes payable with daily late surcharge of 0.05%, and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax may be imposed under Articles 63 and 64 of the Law on Tax Administration and Collection. Fraudulently obtaining an export VAT refund additionally exposes the entity and its responsible persons to criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, and the export tax refund registration status may be suspended.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict capital account controls and current account convertibility for genuine trade and service transactions. For the Taiwan-invested WFOE, registered capital injections from the Taiwan shareholder are capital account transactions: they require FDI foreign exchange registration, a domestic foreign exchange capital account, and SAFE-monitored use, and the proceeds cannot be diverted to equity investment, entrusted lending, securities, or real estate unless expressly permitted. Current account items, such as cross-border fee payments for company secretarial services, are generally convertible at market rates once the bank validates the underlying contract, invoice, tax payment status, and beneficial ownership. Profit remittance to the Taiwan parent is permitted after the annual audit, final tax settlement, and board profit distribution resolution, with bank review of the audited financial statements, tax clearance certificate, and historical capital verification report. The convertibility assessment is therefore qualified: operational payments are freely routable, but all capital movements remain subject to SAFE registration, quota controls, bank confirmation, and genuine transaction documentation. There is no full liberalization of cross-border RMB or foreign currency flows, and any attempt to use offshore digital EMI accounts to bypass SAFE reporting would be unlawful.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export-related foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to the domestic foreign exchange settlement account of the WFOE within 180 days after customs declaration; there is no fixed statutory conversion ratio, but the bank will verify the export value against customs records, and any conversion to RMB or retention in foreign currency must be reported under SAFE's current account receipt management system. Proceeds must be surrendered through a tier-1 domestic bank using the correct export verification code.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low or prohibitive. The PBOC and SAFE license regime does not authorize offshore fintech EMIs to maintain deposit accounts, process capital account transactions, or clear RMB and foreign exchange for PRC-domiciled entities. Licensed domestic non-bank payment institutions (Alipay, WeChat Pay, and SAFE-registered cross-border payment institutions) may only handle qualifying current account e-commerce or consumer payments and cannot replace a tier-1 bank for corporate treasury. Use of offshore EMI rails for corporate collections would create an AML gap and violate SAFE data reporting. Hence the WFOE must rely on onshore tier-1 banks; fintech EMI rails may be used at most as a front-end invoicing or collection gateway in a fully disclosed and segregated structure, never as the primary clearing mechanism.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends paid by the PRC WFOE to a non-resident corporate shareholder under Articles 3, 37 and 38 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law; the rate may be reduced to 5% under the Cross-Strait Tax Agreement if the Taiwan-resident beneficial owner directly holds at least 25% of the WFOE's capital and such ownership is maintained for the period required by the agreement, provided no mainland permanent establishment is attributable to the shareholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory when, in a single fiscal year, related-party tangible property transfers reach RMB 200 million, financial asset transfers reach RMB 100 million, intangible asset transfers reach RMB 100 million, or related-party service transactions reach RMB 40 million. Master File preparation is mandatory when aggregate related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion in a fiscal year. The WFOE must also disclose the existence and amount of each related-party transaction in the annual CIT return schedules whenever any reportable related-party dealing is present.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not impose a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation regime, but economic substance is decisive through the tax treaties' beneficial ownership requirement, Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law general anti-abuse rule, and the permanent establishment tests. A WFOE performing ISIC 8299 registered-agent and offshore secretarial services must demonstrate real offices, locally employed staff, decision-making files, board minutes, client contracts, Chinese compliance records, and invoicing functions. If the WFOE or its Taiwan parent is a mailbox shell and profits are shifted without core income-generating activities in mainland China, the tax authority may recharacterize the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, impute taxable profits and impose 10% WHT on deemed distributions or royalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Unfiled or Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, the tax authority recovers the underpaid tax and imposes a penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded amount, with daily 0.05% late surcharge. Criminal Law Article 201 criminalizes tax evasion where the amount evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and exceeds 10% of total taxable amount; imprisonment of up to 3 years applies for the ordinary threshold, and 3 to 7 years for large amounts with a ratio above 30%, plus fines.

Director personal liability: A director who ordered, concealed or signed the false return is a directly responsible person under Chinese criminal law and may be sentenced to imprisonment and fines. If personal and company assets are commingled or the corporate form is abused to evade tax debts, courts may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 and hold the director personally liable for the tax liabilities, penalties and civil restitution. The responsible legal representative also faces a tax-dishonesty public blacklist and exit restriction order banning cross-border travel.
Shell Company / Fraudulent Claim of Cross-Strait DTA Relief

Statutory sanction: The tax authority invokes Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and its implementing general anti-abuse rules to recharacterize the income, deny the treaty benefit, re-collect the full 10% WHT, impose daily surcharges, and apply a 50% to 5 times penalty under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law. False statements to the tax authority in treaty documentation can trigger criminal liability under Article 201 or Article 204 of the Criminal Law, with penal regimes extending to fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute false treaty declarations or approve dividends without verifying the Taiwan beneficial owner's substance are personally exposed: the tax authority can attribute civil restitution for the under-withheld tax, the travel ban blacklist will apply to the legal representative, and criminal prosecution can lead to imprisonment of up to 7 years. Where the WFOE is found to be a pure conduit, the corporate veil can be pierced and director assets attached.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Under Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Announcement [2016] No. 42, the tax authority may apply the CUP, TNMM or Profit Split method to adjust taxable income upward. Failure to produce documentation on demand after the statutory deadline attracts a fine of RMB 10,000 to RMB 100,000 under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, plus daily 0.05% surcharges on the additional tax. If the omission is fraudulent and designed to evade tax, Articles 62 and 63 elevate the consequence to recovery, a 50% to 5 times penalty, and criminal liability.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing documentation or who deliberately shift profits to a low-tax shell are personally liable if the WFOE becomes unable to pay the adjusted CIT and penalties; the personal accounts of directors may be frozen, and the tax authority may impose travel bans and exit restrictions. In aggravated tax evasion cases, the director qualifies as a directly responsible person subject to criminal conviction, with imprisonment up to 7 years and financial penalties.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum registered capital applies to a consulting-services WFOE. Under the 2024 Company Law, the shareholder must subscribe to a stated capital in the Articles of Association and must pay it in full within five years from incorporation. No mandatory escrow account or paid-in-at-filing rule exists; however, a Taiwanese parent injecting foreign currency must complete a SAFE bank-supervised foreign exchange registration, and the paid-in amount must be deposited into a dedicated capital contribution account at the settlement bank. Recommended capitalization: RMB 1,000,000 subscribed with phased injection to substantiate operational scale for virtual CFO delivery.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, official national portal www.chinatax.gov.cn, with operational filing conducted through the provincial Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portals and the national Digitalized E-Invoice Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

A Taiwan-invested WFOE is formed under the Foreign Investment Law national-treatment and negative-list regime. Upon issuance of the business license by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), tax registration is automatically integrated into the license under the multi-license integration reform. The 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code printed on the business license is the statutory TIN and VAT registration number. Within 30 days of license issuance, the WFOE must activate its taxpayer profile in the e-Tax Bureau: file the taxpayer basic information sheet, register legal representative and finance officer identities, bind the basic deposit and capital accounts, elect VAT taxpayer status, and designate filing methods. Taiwan shareholder identity and board-authorization documents must be notarized in Taiwan and cross-strait authenticated before SAMR and SAFE filings are accepted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - PRC resident enterprise under Article 2 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, taxed on worldwide income at the 25% statutory rate (5% effective rate available to qualified small low-profit enterprises); no pass-through or disregarded-entity status is recognized; dividends to foreign shareholders are subject to 10% withholding tax unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists under PRC law. Available statutory elections include: (i) voluntary General VAT taxpayer registration before the RMB 5,000,000 annual sales threshold; (ii) small-scale VAT taxpayer simplified levy at 3% (currently 1% temporary reduction) with monthly sales up to RMB 100,000 VAT-exempt; (iii) small low-profit enterprise EIT election applying a 20% rate to 25% of taxable income (5% effective) for annual taxable income up to RMB 3,000,000, employees up to 300, and total assets up to RMB 50,000,000; (iv) accelerated depreciation and one-off expensing of qualifying fixed assets; (v) foreign tax credit election, direct and indirect credits pooled by country, in lieu of deduction; (vi) simplified tax collection election, generally restricted for professional-services entities; and (vii) VAT net-basis differential taxation for qualified outsourcing and payroll services. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed through general anti-avoidance and special tax adjustment provisions; no elective hybrid alignment mechanism is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual VAT-taxable turnover, the mandatory General VAT taxpayer registration threshold; below this threshold, the enterprise may remain a small-scale taxpayer with a 3% levy rate (currently 1%) and a monthly sales exemption of RMB 100,000, or voluntarily elect general status to reclaim input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated with input VAT refund, subject to the export rebate catalog. Cross-border services receive zero-rating with input-VAT refund only when they fall within the categories enumerated in the Appendix to Caishui [2016] No. 36, as carried forward under the 2026 VAT Law, including R&D, software, and business process management services. ISIC 6920 outsourced virtual CFO services comprising accounting, bookkeeping, tax advisory, and CFO consulting are generally mapped to the verification, certification, and consulting services category, which is VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated when the recipient is outside China, the service is wholly consumed offshore, and settlement occurs in foreign currency. To access zero-rating, the WFOE should structure and document the engagement as business process management outsourcing, supported by substantive data-processing activities, time-and-effort records, offshore delivery supervision, and no domestic exploitation of the service output.

  • Cross-border service contract in bilingual form evidencing the offshore recipient, offshore consumption, payment rails, and scope of deliverables, with the SAFE bank-filing copy
  • Digitalized VAT e-invoice issued to the non-resident client bearing the applicable zero-rating or exemption tax code
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher evidencing collection in convertible currency from the overseas client
  • Service-delivery substantiation file including electronic ledgers and deliverables, dispatch and server logs, overseas client location and KYB documentation, and time-and-effort allocation records
  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, and packing list, required only where physical deliverables such as printed advisory reports or software carriers are exported

Fully digitalized e-invoicing is mandatory under the Golden Tax Phase IV architecture. VAT invoices must be issued through the national e-invoice service platform, receive a cryptographic invoice code and trace identifier in real time, and transmit automatically to STA clearing servers. High-volume issuers must integrate ERP and billing systems via the STA direct-connection API. Red-letter reversal invoices require recipient confirmation in the cloud before annulment. STA big-data platforms continuously cross-match invoices against customs declarations, foreign-exchange settlements, logistics manifests, and corporate bank flows; discrepancies trigger automated invoice-quota blocking and audit alerts.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating and exemption claims trigger retrospective VAT clawback, daily late-payment interest at 0.05% of underpaid tax, penalties of 50% to five times the evaded tax under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law, digital-invoice quota blocking, downgrade of the enterprise tax credibility classification, and suspension of export refund facilitation. Criminal liability for export tax refund fraud under Article 204 of the Criminal Law includes fines and imprisonment up to life for especially large amounts. STA, Customs, and SAFE jointly operate automated cross-checks of customs declarations, e-invoices, FX settlements, and logistics bills; abnormal voucher flags cause an immediate 180-day suspension of refund filings and trigger mandatory full-file audit. Foreign-exchange repatriation violations beyond the declared expected collection date are separately penalized by SAFE under the Regulations on Foreign Exchange Control, with fines up to 30% of the uncollected amount.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is fully convertible on the current account (services, trade, dividends) after bona-fide transaction verification, while the capital account remains regulated: FDI equity injections, shareholder loans, and cross-border guarantees require SAFE registration through the Capital Account Information System. Profit remittance to the Taiwan parent requires audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, SAFE FDI registration, and a bank-reviewed remittance application, typically verified within 3-7 business days. RMB cross-strait settlement flows freely under the negotiated clearing framework and eliminates FX conversion risk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory export-proceeds surrender was abolished in 2012; the WFOE may retain 100% of current-account service receipts in foreign currency. However, receipts must be repatriated into a domestic bank account and pass authenticity verification under the SAFE ASOne Service Trade module; advance service receipts must be matched to service performance within 12 months, and goods-export proceeds must generally be collected within 90 days from the customs declaration date, with overdue balances flagged in the SAFE monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (国家外汇管理局, SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (中国人民银行, PBOC); cross-strait RMB clearing is coordinated under the Cross-Strait Currency Clearing Agreement, with Bank of China Taipei Branch designated as the RMB clearing bank in Taiwan and CTBC Bank serving as the TWD clearing bank in mainland China.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional auxiliary rail only. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Stripe, Airwallex, Currenxie) hold licenses in Hong Kong, Singapore, the UK, or the EU, and are not authorized deposit-taking institutions in China; they cannot serve as the WFOE's primary settlement bank. They remain legally viable for low-value cross-border collections (e.g., Stripe Billing for overseas CFO clients) provided funds are ultimately swept into a domestic Tier-1 account and the underlying service contracts and invoices are retained for SAFE authenticity review. Restrictive factors include PBOC payment-institution administrative measures, the SAFE prohibition on splitting RMB settlement of unverified current-account transactions, and PRC data-localization rules for financial-transaction records.

Restrictive factors include PBOC payment-institution administrative measures, the SAFE prohibition on splitting RMB settlement of unverified current-account transactions, and PRC data-localization rules for financial-transaction records.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: annual related-party transaction amount exceeds NT$50 million. Master File: required if the MNE group has aggregate related-party turnover above NT$30 billion. Country-by-Country Report: compulsory where the group's consolidated revenue exceeds NT$54 billion. Documentation must be contemporaneous and retained for 10 years; failure to produce it may trigger penalty plus recharacterization.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not impose a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but standards of economic substance govern both permanent establishment attribution and entitlement to treaty benefits. Under Article 43-3 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, arrangements lacking commercial substance, local management, personnel, or substantive business conduct may be recast. The WFOE must demonstrate physical office presence, resident employees, board decisions made in Taiwan, and actual accounting/CFO service performance in Taiwan to avoid the adoption of a shell-company argument.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Income Tax Act, underpayment penalty of up to three times the tax evasion amount plus overdue interest; criminal liability under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act carrying imprisonment of up to five years and a fine reaching NT$3 million; habitual offenders may have business registration revoked.

Director personal liability: Company Act Articles 23 and 154 permit piercing the corporate veil where the corporate form is abused: directors may be jointly and severally liable for civil restitution, travel bans may be issued by the NTA, and foreign directors may be denied entry to Taiwan.
Shell Company / False Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: NTA may recharacterize the arrangement and assess tax under the GAAR (Article 12-1); disallowed deductions give rise to underpaid income tax plus a fine of up to three times the assessed deficiency; false declaration may be reported to the Company Registrar, leading to company dissolution under Company Act Article 10.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow shareholders using the shell to circumvent tax may be held personally liable for outstanding taxes and penalties; unresolved tax debts can trigger travel bans, bank account attachment, and, in aggravated cases, criminal prosecution for document forgery.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may raise an adjustment based on TNMM/CUP and collect the additional tax, plus a penalty up to three times the underpaid tax; administrative fines also apply for failure to provide the TP report within the statutory cure period (NT$10,000-300,000 plus daily penalties).

Director personal liability: Directors bear fiduciary responsibility for maintaining complete accounting records and TP documentation. Where omission is willful and causes the company to lose an audit or treaty benefit, directors may be liable in civil compensation actions and may face criminal responsibility for breach of trust.
AML / Trade Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Money Laundering Control Act carries criminal penalties of imprisonment up to seven years and a fine up to NT$30 million; breach of AML internal-control obligations is subject to administrative fines of NT$500,000 to NT$10 million; banking relationships may be terminated and the corporate bank account frozen.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign, authorize, or facilitate such transfers are personally criminally liable; the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent conveyances, and detention in Taiwan is possible during investigation; key personnel may be banned from leaving Taiwan in pending criminal cases.

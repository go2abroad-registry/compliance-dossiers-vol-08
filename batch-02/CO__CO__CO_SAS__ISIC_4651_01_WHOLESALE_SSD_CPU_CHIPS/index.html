Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direccion de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: www.dian.gov.co; digital taxpayer platform: MUISCA / Sede Electronica DIAN.

E-government filing portal

A Colombian S.A.S. obtains its statutory tax identification and registration as follows: (1) Incorporate the Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada by private document or public deed and register it with the local Camara de Comercio in the company's registered office; minimum capital is not statutorily required and non-resident ownership is permitted. (2) After commercial registration, DIAN recognizes the company's NIT (Numero de Identificacion Tributaria) and creates its RUT (Registro Unico Tributario) record; the legal representative must file/update the RUT online through DIAN declaring the ISIC 4651 wholesale trade activity in respect of solid-state drives and server processors, and the applicable tax obligations (corporate income tax, VAT, and export-related registrations). (3) The S.A.S. must activate electronic invoicing credentials in DIAN's digital platform, obtain an authorized digital certificate for the legal representative or authorized users, and obtain the CUFE/CUDE stamping mechanism for export invoices. (4) A non-resident shareholder is not separately required to obtain a Colombian NIT merely by owning an S.A.S.; if the foreign entity itself establishes a permanent establishment or conducts independent Colombian-source taxable activity, it must appoint a Colombian tax representative and register through the RUT in its own name. (5) The RUT is a standing registration instrument and must be updated within the statutory periods upon changes in legal form, address, directors, shareholding, activities, or tax status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Colombian S.A.S. is a resident juridical person taxed at entity level on worldwide income; partnership or pass-through treatment is not available. A branch or permanent establishment of a foreign entity is also taxed at entity level on Colombian-source income. A non-resident without permanent establishment is generally subject only to Colombian-source withholding taxes, not to an entity-level return through a locally incorporated S.A.S.

Available election forms

Colombian tax law does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election or hybrid entity election for an S.A.S.; the S.A.S. remains a classic taxable corporation. Available elections and special regimes relevant to this profile include: (a) the elective 'Regimen Simple de Tributacion' (SIMPLE) for qualifying smaller entities with gross turnover not exceeding 3,500 UVT, which replaces the ordinary income-tax computation but does not make the entity pass-through; (b) voluntary registration as 'exportador' or customs-authorized user for streamlined export clearances; (c) election to operate as a Zona Franca user for goods physically admitted to a free-trade zone, which can affect VAT suspension and CIT rate; (d) election of special holding-company (CHC) treatment for dividends coming from foreign subsidiaries, subject to substance conditions; and (e) no unilateral hybrid-mismatch election, but Colombian CFC and transparency rules may cause foreign income to be attributed to Colombian shareholders. All elections are exercised through DIAN or the relevant customs authority and do not alter the S.A.S.'s fundamental entity-level tax character.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary VAT registration turnover threshold applies to legal persons: an S.A.S. engaged in taxable sales is required to register in the RUT and to charge VAT from its first taxable transaction. The optional SIMPLE tax regime uses an annual 3,500 UVT gross-revenue ceiling for eligibility, but that ceiling is not the VAT registration threshold. VAT registration is event-based and is required regardless of turnover for the sale of goods and the provision of taxable services in Colombia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Colombian VAT law provides an exempt-export treatment with economic effect equivalent to zero-rating for goods and certain offshore services, with refund of related input VAT. To claim the benefit for SSD and CPU chip wholesale exports, the exporter must establish: (1) the S.A.S. is registered in the RUT and has declared the export activity; (2) the goods are physically sold to a foreign buyer and leave Colombian customs territory under a definitive export procedure; (3) the electronic export invoice describes the goods under ISIC 4651 consistent with the customs declaration; (4) title and risk have passed to the foreign consignee outside Colombia; (5) for services, the benefit must be rendered to a foreign customer and used exclusively outside Colombia; and (6) the foreign currency proceeds are collected and repatriated through the Colombian foreign exchange market by the exporter. The exporter must maintain a complete documentary file for at least the statutory limitation period, ordinarily five taxable years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaracion de Exportacion - DEX) accepted as definitive by DIAN customs authorities
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the SSD and CPU chips from Colombian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list consistent with ISIC 4651 wholesale trade and with the electronic export invoice
  • Electronic export invoice bearing the CUFE code and DIAN validation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas) issued by a Colombian authorized intermediary proving collection and repatriation of the export proceeds

Electronic invoicing is mandatory for all VAT-responsible taxpayers. The S.A.S. must issue 'facturas electronicas de venta' through a DIAN-certified software or the DIAN public system, include the assigned CUFE (Codigo Unico de Facturacion Electronica) or CUDE for contingency, transmit each invoice for DIAN validation before delivery to the buyer, and include a machine-readable QR code. For export operations, the electronic export invoice and supporting transport/customs documents are transmitted to DIAN through MUISCA and the customs single window before the export declaration is definitively accepted; DIAN validates export-exemption claims and the exporter must retain the accepted clearance records and aggregated VAT refund schedules.

DIAN cross-checks export declarations, electronic invoices, transport documents, and foreign currency repatriation records. If an export zero-rating claim is found to lack a real exit, to be supported by false documents, to be linked to unrepatriated proceeds, or to involve a sham foreign transaction, DIAN will disallow and claw back the VAT refund (saldo a favor) and assess moratory interest from the date of the erroneous refund. The taxpayer may also be subjected to the statutory inaccuracy penalty, currently up to 160% of the tax understatement, and to penalties for incorrect electronic invoicing and for non-compliance with customs formalities. In flagrant cases involving false customs declarations or fraudulent refund schemes, the conduct may be reclassified as tax fraud or customs smuggling, attracting monetary fines, disqualification from export programs, and criminal liability. DIAN retains audit and assessment powers for the general five-year limitation period, extended further in cases of non-filing or omissions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is floating and fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to administrative channeling rather than to quantitative limits, prior-exchange licenses, or multiple-currency practices. Current-account operations (imports of goods and services, royalties, freight, commissions) may be executed freely through a CAM; operations whose value exceeds the US$50,000 per-transaction de minimis threshold must be settled via CAM and declared on the appropriate Declaración de Cambio (e.g., Formulario No. 5 for imports of goods), while operations at or below US$50,000 do not require channeling but remain subject to DIAN filing. Capital inflows (foreign direct investment, FDI) require no prior authorization: the non-resident shareholder may contribute capital to a Colombian S.A.S. through a CAM using Formulario No. 7 (Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales), and the investment must be registered in Banco de la República's Registro de Inversiones Internacionales (RII) within six (6) months of the channeling date; registration is a statutory precondition for any subsequent dividend remittance, capital reduction, or repatriation of invested capital. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders face no FX quota, waiting period, or Central Bank approval once (i) the inward investment has been duly registered with the RII, (ii) Colombian corporate income tax and dividend-withholding taxes have been settled, and (iii) the remittance is executed through a CAM and reported on Formulario No. 7. There are no surrender requirements, no exchange-rate bands, and no forced conversion mechanisms for current-account proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia does not impose a mandatory surrender ratio or forced conversion of export receipts into COP; the operative statutory duty is 'canalización' through the authorized exchange market. The exporter must cause the foreign-exchange proceeds of goods exports to be paid into a CAM (either by selling the currency to the CAM at the market reference rate, the TRM, or by depositing it into a resident foreign-currency account maintained at a Colombian bank) within six (6) months counted from the date of the DIAN export declaration (Declaración de Exportación); this term may be extended by up to six (6) additional months (12-month cap) where justified causes are documented and accepted by the competent authority or the CAM under the applicable regime. Collections must be reported on Formulario No. 1 (Declaración de Cambio por Exportación de Bienes), distinguishing advance collections, on-arrival collections, and deferred collections, with the corresponding exchange finality codes (e.g., 81, 82, and 83). Export operations with an FOB value equal to or below US$50,000 are exempt from the mandatory channeling duty while remaining subject to DIAN export-filing requirements. Failure to channel within the prescribed term triggers administrative penalties by the SFC and/or DIAN under the exchange regime and may generate adverse tax presumptions regarding unrepatriated income.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (the Central Bank of Colombia), acting through its Junta Directiva, is the monetary and foreign-exchange authority. The governing instrument is the 'Régimen de Cambios Internacionales' contained in Resolución Externa No. 8 de 2000 (as amended), supplemented operationally by Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83 issued by Banco de la República. The Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) supervises the authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries ('intermediarios del mercado cambiario' or CAMs, i.e., Colombian commercial banks and licensed financial corporations); the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) administers export and import declarations and customs documentation; and the Unidad de Información y Análisis Financiero (UIAF) is the AML/CFT financial-intelligence unit. All cross-border trade settlements above the legal de minimis threshold must be channelled exclusively through CAMs; offshore digital payment providers are not authorized intermediaries under Colombian exchange law.

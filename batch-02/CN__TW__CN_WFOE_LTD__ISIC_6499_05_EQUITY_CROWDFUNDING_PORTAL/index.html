Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital for companies limited by shares under Article 156 of the Company Act. Paid-in capital must be fully subscribed and deposited into a domestic bank account in the company's name before registration. A CPA-issued capital verification report is mandatory. For equity crowdfunding portals, the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) may impose an operating guarantee deposit and stricter escrow rules under securities regulations, which are licensing requirements rather than general corporate capital rules. No blocked escrow account is required by the Companies Act for ordinary incorporation, but investor funds must be segregated in an escrow account under FSC crowdfunding rules.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety: Obtain a Fire Safety Device Inspection Certification from the local fire department for office premises, including smoke detectors, fire extinguishers, emergency lighting, and evacuation signage. For server rooms, a fire suppression system (clean agent) is recommended and a building usage permit confirming structural suitability must be secured. Occupational safety: Register with the Occupational Safety and Health Administration, submit a workplace self-inspection report, and designate a safety and health officer if employee count exceeds 30. IT security and physical access controls must be maintained as part of the FSC license requirement.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Republic of China (Taiwan) – National Taxation Bureau; online portal: https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Taiwan wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) limited liability company, the entity must first complete company registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA). Within 15 days of commencing business, the company must apply for Business Tax Registration (營業稅籍登記) with the local National Taxation Bureau. Required documents include the company incorporation certificate, articles of incorporation, and identification of the responsible person. The bureau issues a Unified Business Number (TIN) as the statutory tax identification. For foreign entities without a local establishment but engaging in taxable activities, a VAT representative must be appointed and registration completed before the first taxable transaction.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NT$200,000 monthly sales (or NT$2.4 million annual) for goods and services; mandatory registration for businesses exceeding this threshold. Financial services, including equity crowdfunding portals, are subject to the same threshold unless otherwise exempted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act. For goods, the goods must be physically exported from Taiwan and a customs export declaration must be filed. For services, including equity crowdfunding portal services, the services must be provided to foreign clients, used outside Taiwan, and the consideration must be received in foreign currency. The service provider must maintain evidence of the foreign client's identity, the service contract, and proof of foreign currency receipt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading (for goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Receipts (for services)

Taiwan mandates the use of electronic invoices (電子發票) for businesses with annual sales exceeding NT$30 million. These invoices must be transmitted to the Ministry of Finance's E-Invoice Platform (電子發票整合服務平台) within 48 hours of issuance. The platform requires API integration and uses a unique invoice number and QR code for anti-fraud verification. For high-risk industries, including financial intermediaries, real-time clearance may be required. Non-compliance results in penalties under the Business Tax Act.

If zero-rating is claimed without proper documentation, the tax authority may impose a penalty of up to 5 times the tax evaded, plus interest. Retrospective clawback of zero-rated treatment is possible, and fraudulent claims may lead to criminal prosecution under the Tax Evasion Act, with imprisonment and fines.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is convertible on the current account for genuine cross-border receipts and payments, including services, dividends, and profit remittances, subject to SAFE declaration and tax clearance. The capital account, by contrast, remains tightly controlled: every inbound equity injection from the Taiwan parent requires an FDI foreign exchange registration, a dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a licensed bank, and payment-based conversion into RMB strictly for the WFOE's approved operating uses. There is no automatic right for non-residents to invest or withdraw funds without SAFE registration, and no offshore alternative such as a fintech EMI can lawfully perform onshore RMB clearing or capital FX settlement for a Chinese legal entity. The WFOE must demonstrate real business substance, maintain full documentation of each remittance, and use the domestic banking system for conversion, payroll, procurement, and profit distribution. Profit repatriation to the Taiwan shareholder is permitted only after the annual audit, required reserve allocations, corporate income tax payment or exemption certificate, board approval, and SAFE current-account filing. Taiwan-side receipt may also be subject to the Taiwan Central Bank's cross-border remittance reporting threshold, but that reporting obligation does not alter SAFE's onshore controls. Convertibility is thus open for genuine current items but unavailable for speculation, synthetic RMB offshore structures, or unrestricted hedging on the capital account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's prevailing trade foreign-exchange rules, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration, or within the extended contractual payment period where SAFE grants permission. The WFOE/exporter must report the receipt in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter is permitted to retain 100% of export receipts in a current-account FX account or sell them to a licensed bank in exchange for RMB. Any conversion must reflect a genuine trade or service transaction, evidenced by contract, invoice, customs declaration or service transaction attestation, and the relevant SAFE transaction/purpose code.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital banks and electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are legally barred from acting as the onshore settlement bank for a mainland Chinese WFOE. They cannot hold RMB deposits, operate a SAFE-registered Foreign Exchange Capital Account, perform capital verification, or execute the statutory FX conversion required for an equity crowdfunding intermediary. At best, an offshore EMI could be used on a non-banking basis to collect contributions from non-China crowd investors before routing the pooled funds through a licensed Tier-1 Chinese bank, but the EMI cannot replace the domestic treasury and clearing mandate. The WFOE should therefore enter into no primary custody or disbursement arrangement with any EMI.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% corporate income tax (Income Tax Act Article 5), plus 5% surtax on undistributed retained earnings (Article 66-9)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% withholding tax on gross dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a non-resident corporate shareholder (Income Tax Act Article 88 and Article 73); dividends to resident corporate shareholders are exempt from withholding under Article 42.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual return must be filed within 5 months after the fiscal year end (calendar-year entity: by May 31 of the following year); provisional estimated returns and payments are due no later than September 30 and March 31 respectively; audited financial statements follow the same 5-month statutory deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File / Transfer Pricing Report is mandatory when total related-party transactions reach TWD 200 million or more in a tax year, or when cross-border related-party transactions reach TWD 50 million or more (small-taxpayer exemption applies when total related-party transactions are below TWD 20 million); Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds TWD 27 billion; contemporaneous TP documentation must be prepared by the filing deadline and produced within 30 days of a tax authority request.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not issue Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; instead, the 'Actual Place of Effective Management' (PEM) doctrine and Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules (effective 1 January 2023) recapture income of offshore-interposed entities whose central management and control is exercised in Taiwan. For the equity crowdfunding portal, the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) license requires a substantive local office, at least two full-time compliance and risk-management officers, a locally maintained audit trail, and independent audit of custodial or escrow funds; failure to maintain this substance may cause the tax authority to ignore the separate legal personality of the WFOE, deny treaty benefits, recast escrow fees as Taiwan-source service income, and refer the matter to the FSC for license revocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Underpayment penalty of 1 to 3 times the evaded tax under Article 110 of the Income Tax Act; criminal liability under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act: imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to TWD 6 million; where the evaded amount exceeds TWD 25 million, imprisonment of 2 to 10 years and fine up to TWD 30 million; publication of the taxpayer and compulsory execution of assets.

Director personal liability: Legal representative and directors may be personally and jointly liable for the company's unpaid tax and penalties where the evasion arises from deliberate corporate conduct; the tax authority may impose travel bans, detain the responsible person pending collection, and courts may order civil restitution to investors; serious fraud convictions can lead to imprisonment of directors and prohibition from serving as managers for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the entity's income as directly earned by the beneficial owner under PEM/CFC rules; denial of tax treaty benefits; denial of deductions for offshore management fees; deregistration of the company under the Company Act for failure to maintain registered office; fines and revocation of the FSC license.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for restitutions and back taxes following the recharacterized assessment; travel bans and asset freezes under Article 24 of the Tax Collection Act when the confirmed tax exceeds statutory thresholds; disqualification orders for company management; criminal sanction for making false statutory declarations in the FSC license application.
AML / CTF Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Fines up to TWD 5,000,000 under the Money Laundering Control Act per violation; administrative suspension or revocation of the FSC crowdfunding license; for disgraceful conduct, criminal money-laundering liability under Article 14 of the Act with imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines up to TWD 50,000,000; confiscation of illicit assets.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons who knowingly facilitate or fail to prevent money laundering face criminal imprisonment, personal fines, and confiscation of personal assets; directors may be barred from service in the financial sector for a period up to 10 years; exit restrictions and freeze orders are routinely imposed during investigation.
Unlicensed Securities / Crowdfunding Business

Statutory sanction: Under the Securities and Exchange Act, unauthorized securities business is punishable by imprisonment of 2 to 10 years and a fine of TWD 2 million to TWD 20 million; the FSC may issue cease-and-desist orders, impose administrative fines, and order disgorgement of all profits received from the unlawful offering.

Director personal liability: Directors, supervisors, and the legal representative bear joint civil liability to compensate defrauded investors; criminal imprisonment is imposed on responsible persons who knowingly operate without a license; the court may disqualify the individuals from serving as officers of any FSC-regulated entity and issue international travel bans pending subsequent collection or enforcement proceedings.

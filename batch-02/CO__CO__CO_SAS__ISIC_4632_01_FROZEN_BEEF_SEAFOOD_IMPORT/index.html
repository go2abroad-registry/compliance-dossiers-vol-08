Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an S.A.S.; the bylaws may declare nominal capital and the paid-in portion may be contributed within a maximum term of two years (Law 1258/2008, Article 9). Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — https://www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

Foreign-incorporated investors establishing a Colombian SAS must (1) appoint a legal representative domiciled in Colombia; (2) file the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) application with DIAN through authorized channels to obtain the Número de Identificación Tributaria (NIT); (3) activate the electronic signature (firma electrónica) acquisition procedure for DIAN portal services; and (4) formally register as a VAT responsible entity (responsable de IVA) before the first customs import declaration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SAS is a Colombian resident entity subject to corporate income tax at 35% for fiscal year 2024); Territorial_Scope: Worldwide residence taxation; Tax_Exempt_Zone: Not applicable by default, unless the entity operates under a designated Zona Franca concession.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists under Colombian law. The SAS is irreversibly treated as a taxable entity at the corporate level. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available in domestic law but must be assessed under Colombian transfer pricing and anti-hybrid rules consistent with the OECD BEPS recommendations for cross-border operations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold — a Colombian SAS engaged in taxable activities or import operations must register in the RUT as an IVA responsible person from incorporation, with immediate effect for customs and VAT purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of frozen beef and seafood are subject to the 0% VAT rate only when legal title and risk have passed to the foreign buyer and the goods physically leave Colombian customs territory under the duly submitted Declaración de Exportación (DEX). The exporter is entitled to recover VAT paid on directly attributable inputs through export VAT refund procedures (devolución de IVA). Services are zero-rated only if rendered to foreign recipients and directly used abroad, but are not relevant to the physical goods export model.

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX) with DIAN electronic validation
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill duly issued with final foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (comprobante de ingreso de divisas) issued by a Colombian authorized foreign exchange intermediary
  • Commercial invoice and packing list, plus Certificado de Origen when applicable

DIAN requires mandatory real-time electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica). The SAS must use DIAN-authorized software providers or the DIAN free e-invoicing application to transmit XML invoices to DIAN for pre-validation and real-time fiscal clearance. Each electronic invoice must include the CUD (Código Único de Documento), a mandatory QR code, DIAN registration status, and be filed in the monthly electronic invoicing mass-reporting module within 5 business days of month-end.

DIAN and customs authorities may audit the physical exit of goods, the timely filing of the DEX, and the actual repatriation of foreign currency. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days triggers a fiscal clawback of VAT credits, a penalty of 5% of the uncollected/non-repatriated amount, plus default interest and a documentation or transport-related penalty of 0.5% of the export value. Fraudulent or materially false zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion and can be penalized with fines up to 150% of the unpaid tax, disqualification from VAT refund regimes, and criminal liability for senior management under Colombian anti-contraband and money laundering statutes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) operates under a managed float with the TRM (Tasa Representativa del Mercado) published daily. Current-account transactions (import of goods, services, freight, insurance) are freely convertible through IMCs once the corresponding Declaración de Cambio (Form 1 for imports) is filed; capital-account transactions (equity injections, foreign debt, portfolio investment, profit repatriation) are open but require mandatory channeling and, for amounts above USD 500,000, formal registration in the Banco de la República Registro de Inversiones Internacionales. Profit remittances from the S.A.S. to non-resident shareholders are legally permitted after payment of the 35% corporate income tax and the applicable dividend withholding (7.5% on the portion previously subject to corporate tax, plus 35% on any non-taxed portion), provided the remittance is routed through an IMC and supported by the tax Paz y Salvo and audited financial statements. There are no surrender or prior-approval hurdles for genuine trade flows, but import payments exceeding USD 5,000 (FOB) must be made via the foreign-exchange market and cannot be settled offshore.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia imposes no mandatory conversion of export proceeds into COP; exporters may retain foreign currency in local foreign-currency accounts or abroad. However, statutory channeling is compulsory: export proceeds (bienes) must be received and routed through an IMC within six (6) months of the date of receipt of the proceeds and in no case later than twelve (12) months from the date of the export customs declaration (exit). Export credits equal to or exceeding USD 10,000 must be reported on Form 2 (Declaración de Cambio por Exportaciones) by the exporter or the IMC. For this cold-chain importer, any incidental re-export of frozen rejects or inventory must comply with the same deadlines; non-channeling is a financial-exchange infraction subject to fines and exclusion from IMC facilities.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (the Central Bank), exercising foreign-exchange regulatory powers jointly with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) for bank, AML/SARLAFT and Onboarding enforcement, and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) for customs, Import Declarations and registration of merchants. The core control instruments are Decree 1068 of 2015, Banco de la República Resolution 8 of 2000, and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, under which all cross-border trade and investment flows must be canalizadas (routed) through the Mercado Cambiario via authorized Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario (IMCs, i.e., licensed commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary clearing rail; Medium only as a supplementary low-value collection or outgoing payment channel. Under Colombian law (Decree 2555/2010 and the SEDPE regime), only licensed banks and duly authorized Sociedades Especializadas en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos (e.g., Nequi, Daviplata) may take deposits or execute domestic payment clearing. Foreign digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Currencycloud, etc.) do not hold a Colombian banking or SEDPE licence, cannot issue Form 1/Form 3 exchange declarations, and cannot execute the mandatory channeling of import payments or export proceeds. Any use of an offshore EMI for trade settlement would breach the canalización obligation and expose the S.A.S. to DIAN and Banco de la República penalties; at most, EMIs may serve as fallback collection rails for non-trade receivables that are subsequently normalized through an IMC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders initiate a SWIFT MT103/MT202 wire from their own bank account (in the shareholder's name) to the S.A.S. operational account (Cuenta Corriente) maintained at the chosen Colombian IMC bank. The remittance must be clearly tagged as 'Aporte de Capital – Inversión Internacional' and the bank will file Form 3 (Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional) using purpose code 2252 (capital contribution for incorporation) or 2253 (subscription and payment of shares in an existing company). Equity injections equal to or above USD 500,000 (or COP equivalent) must be additionally registered in the Banco de la República Registro de Inversiones Internacionales (RII) through the IMC within 12 months of the flow; registration is a precondition for later dividend remittances. The supporting verification file includes the apostilled corporate resolutions, the shareholder registry updated at the Cámara de Comercio, the remitting bank statement proving the upstream account belongs to the foreign investor, and the share-certificate issuance record.Primary rail 1 - SWIFT MT103 against invoice: the S.A.S. instructs the IMC to debit its account, purchase USD/COP at the TRM and wire the supplier; the bank files Form 1 (Declaración de Cambio por Importaciones) under concept code 1110 (importación de bienes), requiring the accepted DIAN Import Declaration (Declaración de Importación) and the supplier invoice; payment terms may not exceed 6 months from the date of the import declaration (extendable to 12 months only with IMC authorization for deferred financing). Prepayments are capped at 12 months before shipment, and any prepayment exceeding the term must be endorsed by the IMC. Primary rail 2 - Revolving Commercial Letter of Credit: the Colombian bank issues an irrevocable, possibly confirmed, letter of credit under UCP 600 in favor of the foreign frozen-beef/seafood seller; upon negotiation of compliant documents (beneficiary draft, commercial invoice, full packing list, bills of lading, insurance certificate, ICA/INVIMA sanitary releases), the bank pays the presenting bank and files Form 1 with the LC transaction code. All outflows are matched against the RUT importers' status, the INVIMA/ICA import certifications, and the DIAN foreign-trade declarations to avoid divergence alerts under SARLAFT.USD/COP OTC Deliverable Forwards (Forwards a la medida) executed through the IMC bank on the Banco de la República-regulated derivative market, strictly back-to-back with the underlying import payables/LC maturities.USD/COP Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) cash-settled against the TRM, permissible for hedging MT103 and LC payment exposure without requiring physical delivery of COP.Cross-Currency Swaps (Swaps de Monedas) between COP and USD/EUR, permitted for hedging the FX legs of revolving import facilities and equity-remittance floats, registered with the IMC and the Banco de la República derivatives registry.USD/COP TRM Futures (Futuros TRM) traded on the Bolsa de Valores de Colombia, allowed as standard exchange-traded hedges against TC appreciation on large scheduled LC drawings.USD/COP Options (Opciones TRM) structured OTC by Tier-1 IMCs, usable to cap the maximum USD/COP cost of future import disbursements with full SARLAFT underlying-trade documentation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends paid by the Colombian S.A.S. to a non-resident shareholder, computed on the full dividend distribution. If the taxpayer distributes profits that were not previously taxed at the corporate level, the additional taxable amount is subject to CIT at 35% in the S.A.S. before the dividend WHT is computed.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Fiscal year ends 31 December. The filing deadline is set annually by DIAN Resolution according to the last two digits of the NIT, generally falling between August 10 and September 15 of the following taxable year. Extension is not automatic; requests for extension under Article 12 of the Tax Statute Procedures may be submitted only in limited circumstances.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File for fiscal year 2024 apply if the taxpayer's gross income equals or exceeds 250,000 UVT (COP 11,766,250,000) or gross assets equal or exceed 150,000 UVT (COP 7,059,750,000). Transactions with foreign related parties also require the annual transfer pricing information return (Información Exógena TP) and a contemporaneous descriptive report. The importer of frozen beef and seafood must therefore test each related-party cross-border purchase under the arm's-length standard.

Economic substance scope: Colombia has not enacted a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime in the manner of offshore financial centres. However, the Colombian Tax Statute (Articles 260-1 et seq.), the anti-abuse rule in Article 87-8, and the S.A.S. veil-piercing provisions of Law 1258 of 2008 require genuine commercial substance. A frozen/seafood importer with no physical warehouse, no fixed employees, passive management, or circular banking structures is exposed to GAAR recharacterization, denial of deductions, and personal liability of directors for the misuse of the corporate form.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Evasion of income tax above 200 minimum monthly legal wages triggers criminal tax fraud with imprisonment from 48 to 120 months, a fine up to the equivalent of the evaded tax, and disqualification from contracting with the State. Civilly, DIAN may reassess the tax, impose a penalty of 200% of the omitted tax, and collect moratory interest from the period of omission.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who formulate or execute fraudulent tax returns bear joint and several civil liability for the omitted tax, penalties, and interest. Courts may order asset freezing, travel bans, and forfeiture of personal estate where the S.A.S. was used as a mere instrument to conceal taxable income.
Shell Company / Artificial Structure

Statutory sanction: Under Law 1258 of 2008, a court or DIAN applied under the anti-abuse rule may pierce the corporate veil and disregard the separate legal personality. The tax consequences include disallowance of all deductions, attribution of the importer's operations to the foreign parent, and application of a 35% corporate income tax plus a 10% dividend withholding tax deemed to have been paid abroad.

Director personal liability: Each director or shareholder who caused the S.A.S. to be used for tax avoidance is personally liable up to the portion not covered by the entity's assets. Personal remedies include a travel ban, civil restitution of the avoided tax, and imprisonment of up to 100 months where the structure is used to evade customs duties or income tax.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DIAN imposes a penalty equal to 2% of the total value of the related-party transactions for formal documentation omissions, plus a penalty of up to 200% of the tax arising from the price adjustment in the event of non-documentation. Late filing of the TP return results in fines of 20 UVT per day of delay up to 5,000 UVT.

Director personal liability: Directors approving the transaction documents are jointly liable for the inflation of costs to the extent they had knowledge of the artificial pricing. Upon judicial reassessment, directors may be barred from leaving the country during the tax examination and exposed to monetary restitution for the additional tax and penalties.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Law 1762 of 2015 (Anti-Smuggling and Customs Fraud Statute), smuggling or customs misdeclaration carries fines of up to 500% of the customs value, seizure of the goods, and imprisonment from 6 to 20 years for the legal representatives. The Superintendent of Companies and DIAN may impose sanctions under the AML framework applicable to the real-sector importer.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly endorse money-laundering transactions have personal criminal liability, civil confiscation of assets, prohibition from exercising commercial activities in Colombia for up to 10 years, and, where the funds are frozen abroad, a personal travel is forbidden and extradition is not excluded for high-value laundering cases.
Omission of Informative Returns and Tax Records

Statutory sanction: DIAN imposes fines from 10,000 to 15,000 UVT for failure to file informative returns, and the corporate registry may be suspended. The tax obligations are not extinguished by the fine: the entire return is deemed unreliable and may be subject to presumption in favor of DIAN regarding income and asset valuations.

Director personal liability: The director who fails to keep accounting and supporting records of the cold-chain import transactions is liable for the resulting inability to verify costs; courts can order the reimbursement of tax benefits to the treasury from the director's personal assets and impose a commensurate prison term for fraud.

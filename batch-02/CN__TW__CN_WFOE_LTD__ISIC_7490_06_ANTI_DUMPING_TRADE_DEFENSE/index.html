Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is imposed by the Taiwan Company Act for a limited liability company. However, the Investment Commission, in granting foreign investment approval, will require the approved investment amount to be fully remitted into a dedicated New Taiwan Dollar capital contribution account in the company's name and held there until incorporation is completed. The funds shall not be diverted or withdrawn before the issuance of the Company Registration Certificate unless permitted by the Investment Commission or Central Bank rules.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), accessible through the China Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax Bureau) electronic portal at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn and the STA official portal at http://www.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

Under the PRC multi-license integration reform, a WFOE must first be incorporated with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). SAMR issues the Business License carrying an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is automatically recognized by the State Taxation Administration as the taxpayer identification number for enterprise income tax, VAT, and all other PRC taxes. The WFOE then completes tax registration through the China Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax Bureau), is assigned to a competent tax office, files its accounting and financial system records, and obtains a digital invoice issuance quota on the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform. For a foreign non-resident enterprise that does not establish a mainland China entity, the competent tax authority requires non-resident tax record filing through the e-Tax Bureau and may allocate a non-resident taxpayer code for withholding-at-source obligations; no separate VAT identification number is issued unless the non-resident makes taxable supplies requiring VAT registration. All statutory filings are conducted through the official e-Tax Bureau and, where customs clearance is required, the China International Trade Single Window.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not operate a check-the-box election or elective pass-through classification regime. A WFOE is automatically taxed as a resident enterprise on its worldwide income. Hybrid mismatch treatment is not elective; anti-avoidance rules apply under the enterprise income tax framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000. Newly established WFOEs must complete VAT registration immediately upon tax registration regardless of turnover; below the threshold they may be classified as small-scale taxpayers, with an applicable statutory rate of 3% currently reduced to 1% under the existing temporary concession, and small-scale taxpayers may benefit from a temporary monthly sales exemption of up to CNY 100,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT law as reflected in Caishui [2016] No. 36 and subsequent administrative announcements, exported goods qualify for zero VAT rating when the goods are actually declared to and physically leave mainland Chinese customs territory, the exporter issues an export invoice, the foreign exchange is received from the overseas buyer through banking channels, and the export tax refund/exemption declaration is electronically filed. Qualifying cross-border services listed by the STA as zero-rated, such as international transport, R&D and technology services, software services, design services, information system services, and business process management services, may also qualify for zero-rating where the recipient is an overseas enterprise or individual and the services are not consumed in mainland China. The target WFOE's anti-dumping trade-remedy defense and legal/accounting consulting services are normally VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated under the cross-border exemption catalogue when all conditions are met, and therefore must not improperly claim a refund of input VAT as if zero-rated.

  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document such as air waybill, railway consignment note, or CMR road consignment note
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Export sales contract or purchase order indicating the foreign buyer, Incoterms, and value of export goods

The STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform requires real-time issuance and upload of fully digitalised electronic invoices in OFD format. The Golden Tax System Phase IV fiscally validates each invoice through a mandatory anti-fraud digital stamp and tax-control code before the invoice may be delivered to the buyer. Domestic and export B2B invoices must carry the buyer tax identification number and are synchronised into the buyer's e-Tax Bureau file. The e-Tax Bureau and China International Trade Single Window interoperate for electronic customs export declarations and export tax refund/exemption applications.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to post-export audit by the STA. Failure to substantiate physical export, overseas receipt, or statutory documentation within the prescribed 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation period will result in reversal of the 0% rating, supplementary VAT at the applicable domestic rate depending on the supply, late payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, and administrative penalties under the Tax Administration Law of the People's Republic of China. Fraudulent export refund claims may attract penalties of up to five times the refund obtained, recovery of clawed-back refunds, and criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law for fraudulent export-tax-rebate offences, including imprisonment and confiscation of assets. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange may also downgrade the exporter's trade credit classification, triggering enhanced supervision, documentary examination, and cash deposit requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with full current account convertibility and a controlled capital account. New Taiwan Dollar (TWD) convertibility is unrestricted for trade-related payments such as import settlement, service fees, royalties, and dividend remittances that have been duly approved under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment injections, loan repayments, and portfolio remittances, require prior approval from the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) or a designated foreign exchange bank acting within delegated authority. Export proceeds are generally free from forced conversion but must be repatriated to Taiwan and reported through the prescribed foreign exchange declaration system. Profit remittance by a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) requires a certified capital verification report, audited financial statements, and proof of tax clearance, with the central bank monitoring for disguised capital movements. Surrender requirements are effectively relaxed for exporters, yet the settlement of foreign exchange receipts is mandatory. Structural hurdles include the need to evidence economic substance, comply with annual reporting obligations, and ensure that all cross-border payments pass through approved FX banks to avoid fines or suspension of foreign currency accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a designated foreign exchange bank within five business days from the date of collection, and while full conversion to TWD is not statutorily mandatory, the exporter must complete the Central Bank's foreign exchange declaration and can hold the proceeds in a foreign currency deposit account; failure to repatriate within the timeline requires prior approval and documented justification.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act administered jointly with the Financial Supervisory Commission

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI solutions are legally constrained in Taiwan because a non-bank fintech entity cannot independently hold corporate operational funds in TWD or provide TWD-denominated accounts without a banking license. Foreign fintech EMIs offering multi-currency accounts largely rely on partner banks and would not satisfy the Central Bank's requirement for local banking supervision over the WFOE's primary treasury flows. These EMIs may be used only as auxiliary payment appliances for receiving small international trade remittances, subject to the underlying designated FX bank remaining the settlement and compliance anchor; however, any attempt to operate a treasury account through an unlicensed EMI as the main clearing rail is legally barred and carries regulatory risk.

The audit obligation exists irrespective of whether total assets, revenue, or employee count are below RMB 10 million, RMB 30 million, or 50 persons. Additional mandatory audit and filing duties apply if the WFOE is an issuer on a securities exchange, is a financial institution under PBOC, CBIRC, or CSRC supervision, is controlling a significant merger or investment under MOFCOM rules, or is treated as a large state-influenced enterprise under SASAC guidelines; for such regulated entities, statutory audit is triggered at any size, and public issuers must additionally file audited reports under the PRC Securities Law and CSRC rules. In practical administration, SAMR, tax authorities, and SAFE may require the audited financial statements as a supporting document for annual credit filing, annual income tax settlement, and annual FDI stock and equity registration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File transfer pricing report is mandatory when aggregate related-party transactions of the Taiwan entity exceed TWD 200 million; a Master File is mandatory when the worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds TWD 30 billion; Country-by-Country Report obligations arise when group revenue exceeds TWD 27 billion.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan applies the economic substance doctrine through Income Tax Act Articles 43-3 and 43-4 for controlled foreign companies and actual management places, and through Article 12-1 of the Tax Collection Act which allows the tax authority to disregard transactions with no substantial commercial purpose. The Taiwan WFOE must demonstrate that core income-generating activities for anti-dumping trade defense, including case strategy, legal drafting, negotiation, contract execution, invoicing, and dispute management, are physically and economically performed in Taiwan, with real office space, qualified employees, and independent board decision-making.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 110 imposes an administrative penalty of one to three times the underpaid tax plus interest; Tax Collection Act Article 41 imposes criminal liability of imprisonment for up to five years, short-term detention, and a fine of up to TWD 6,000,000 for fraudulent tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Under Company Act Article 23 and Civil Code Article 28, a director who approves fraudulent returns is jointly and severally liable for the company tax deficiency and damages to creditors and shareholders. Tax authorities may attach personal assets, seek court-ordered travel bans, blacklist the individual, and upon criminal conviction impose imprisonment and director disqualification.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Articles 43-1 and 43-2 authorize adjustment of taxable income to arm's length and disallowance of excess interest expense. Failure to produce the required Local File or Master File may result in a deemed assessment, a penalty of one to three times the resulting tax deficiency under Article 110, and withholding tax recharacterization of disguised profit distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing statements may be held personally liable for the adjusted tax amount under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil. The director can face civil asset restitution, personal bank account seizure, blacklisting, travel restrictions, and criminal charges under Tax Collection Act Article 41 if the pricing scheme is found to be fraudulent rather than merely aggressive.
Shell Company and Money Laundering Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Articles 43-3 and 43-4 allow the tax authority to disregard the entity and attribute income to the actual beneficial owner; Money Laundering Control Act Articles 6 and 7 impose registration, customer due diligence, and suspicious transaction reporting obligations; violations carry administrative fines and, for willful money laundering conduct, imprisonment for up to five years and fines up to TWD 15,000,000.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced when the shell entity is used as an instrument of illegality, making directors jointly and severally liable for company debts and tax liabilities. Directors can be blacklisted, prevented from leaving Taiwan, personally assessed for restitution, and imprisoned for money laundering complicity; the company may also be struck off and directors banned from serving as officers in Taiwan.

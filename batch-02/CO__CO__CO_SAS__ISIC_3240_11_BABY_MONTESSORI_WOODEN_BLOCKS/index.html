Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Unidad Administrativa Especial - Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN), Ministry of Finance and Public Credit; portals: www.dian.gov.co, muisca.dian.gov.co (RUT/NIT), cme.dian.gov.co (e-invoicing), vuce.gov.co (foreign trade).

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned S.A.S. procedure: 1) incorporate by private document before the Cámara de Comercio (single-member allowed; no minimum capital) and register the legal representative; 2) apply for the RUT/NIT through DIAN, either online via MUISCA with a digital certificate held by the authorized local attorney/accountant or in person with a notarized and apostilled power of attorney from foreign shareholders; 3) obtain the assigned NIT and RUT excerpt evidencing income-tax, VAT (IVA), withholding-agent, and exporter responsibilities; 4) activate the digital signature and DIAN e-invoicing credentials before the first invoice. The NIT/RUT is the sole tax identity for all national taxes and customs operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Colombian S.A.S. is a resident corporate taxpayer, taxed at entity level on worldwide income under the general regime; there is no U.S.-style pass-through or check-the-box. Its default VAT classification is 'responsable del régimen común' (common-regime IVA taxpayer); goods exports are zero-rated under the IVA territoriality rules. A Zona Franca election reduces the CIT rate to 20% but does not alter entity-level taxation.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Colombian law; entity status is statutory. Available elections: (i) the SIMPLE combined small-business tax regime (Law 2010/2019) for prior-year gross income ≤ 100,000 UVT; (ii) Zona Franca designation approved by the Comité de Zonas Francas with DIAN participation, granting 20% CIT; (iii) Authorized Economic Operator (OEA) status for expedited customs treatment; (iv) choice of DIAN-certified electronic invoicing operator; and (v) an optional accelerated IVA refund procedure backed by a bank guarantee.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

COP 0 for legal entities: an S.A.S. habitually selling taxable goods must be registered as IVA responsible before the first taxable transaction; natural-person micro-merchants can remain outside the VAT system only while prior-year gross income ≤ 3,500 UVT (COP 174,296,500 for 2025; UVT 2025 = COP 49,799); small businesses may elect SIMPLE with prior-year gross income ≤ 100,000 UVT (COP 4,979,900,000 for 2025).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under E.T. Arts. 481-482: (i) definitive export of tangible goods by the seller, evidenced by an accepted export declaration (DEX); (ii) services rendered to non-residents and used exclusively abroad, including software and engineering services; (iii) certain sales into free zones. The export invoice is issued at 0% IVA; the exporter must claim input VAT on export inputs as a 'saldo a favor' in the bimonthly IVA return and may request a refund from DIAN.

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX) definitiva accepted by DIAN/VUCE describing the ISIC 3240 Montessori wooden toy goods and FOB value
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Colombian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the exporter's NIT/RUT, the DEX number, and the 0% IVA export rate
  • Certificado de Ingreso de Divisas / Declaración de Cambio issued by an authorized intermediary of the Banco de la República evidencing collection of export proceeds
  • Audit file: production cost records, beechwood traceability, and EN71-3 non-toxic testing certificates for the baby toy line

Mandatory national electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta): the taxpayer generates an XML document signed with its digital certificate, transmits it in real time through DIAN's web-service API or an authorized Operador Tecnológico, and obtains DIAN validation (CUNE and QR code) before delivery to the buyer; supporting documents (documento soporte for acquisitions from non-responsible suppliers, credit/debit notes) must also be transmitted to DIAN. The implementation calendar of DIAN Resolution 000165/2023 required all obligatory issuers to comply by 30 June 2025.

DIAN automatically cross-checks IVA export refunds against customs DEX data, transport logistics (SIR), and Banco de la República FX records; wood-toy exports are screened for price anomalies and tariff-line mismatches. Fraudulent or undocumented zero-rating triggers rejection of the refund, clawback of previously refunded input VAT with indexation, 'sanción por inexactitud' of 100% of the improperly claimed amount plus moratory interest (E.T. Art. 647), customs penalties under Law 1609/2013 of up to 200% of the FOB value with suspension from the exporters' registry, and criminal liability under E.T. Art. 402 (obtaining improper tax refunds) with imprisonment of 2 to 9 years for the responsible legal representative.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso is convertible in the regulated exchange market (mercado cambiario) under a managed float anchored to the daily TRM (Tasa Representativa del Mercado) published by Banco de la República. Current-account operations (goods, services, personal transfers, and profit or dividend remittances by registered foreign investors) are free from prior authorization or quantitative limits, provided they are channeled through SFC-licensed banks and documented. Capital-account operations, including foreign direct investment and external credit, are not restricted as to amount but must be registered with Banco de la República (Formulario No. 1 for direct investment; Formulario No. 2 for external credit) within the applicable window, generally 12 months from disbursement. The principal profit-remittance hurdles for a foreign-owned S.A.S. are: (i) completion of the FDI registration before the dividend wire; (ii) payment of corporate income tax and the dividend withholding tax applicable to non-resident beneficiaries; (iii) production of shareholders' distribution minutes, audited financials, and the bank-issued Declaración de Cambio evidencing the entry of the original investment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds from goods must be canalized (brought into Colombia) through the foreign-exchange market within six months from the date of shipment or from the customs export declaration; an extension up to 24 months may be requested through the authorized intermediary when supported by documented commercial terms. The effective surrender requirement reaches 100% of the invoice value: proceeds must be credited to a local operational account, a registered local foreign-currency account, or a compensation account abroad registered with Banco de la República, and any conversion to COP must occur in the regulated market at the bank TRM rate. Compliance is evidenced by the Declaración de Cambio por Exportaciones (Formulario No. 3) linked to the DIAN DEX customs export declaration; late or non-canalization triggers exchange-control fines and loss of export-related tax incentives.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia) acts as the foreign-exchange authority through its Dirección de Cambios Internacionales (International Exchange Department); the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) supervises all authorized intermediaries and exchange market participants. Core legal instruments: Ley 9 de 1991, Ley 31 de 1992, Resolution Externa No. 8 de 2000 (as amended), Banco de la República Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, and Decree 2555 de 2010.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business) cannot legally provide Colombian peso accounts, process local payroll, or execute FX canalización without a Colombian banking license or an SFC-issued SEDPE electronic-payment license. They are viable only as auxiliary collection or supplier-payment corridors; the S.A.S. must maintain its primary government-registered clearing and FDI/export-proceeds routing through a tier-1 Colombian bank. Legal constraints: deposit-taking monopoly of licensed credit institutions (Ley 1328 de 2009), SFC payment-rail oversight, and the rule that FX transactions outside the regulated market do not satisfy export-proceeds surrender obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the S.A.S.'s operational account at the selected tier-1 Colombian bank, quoting the beneficiary corporate name, NIT, and the direct-investment purpose code (Formulario No. 1 – Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Directa, subcategory aportes de capital). The receiving bank issues the official Declaración de Cambio, which the treasury must use to register the investment with Banco de la República within 12 months; this registration is a prerequisite for future dividend remittance and capital repatriation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory annual transfer pricing documentation is triggered when the taxpayer's gross income or gross assets in the prior year exceed 150,000 UVT (approximately COP 7.47 billion for tax year 2025) and cross-border related-party transactions exceed 10,000 UVT. Local File preparation is required at the same 150,000 UVT threshold because the taxpayer is a 'High-Risk' obliged entity engaged in cross-border sales; Master File is required when assets or income exceed 500,000 UVT (approximately COP 24.9 billion). A Transfer Pricing Return (Formulario 120) must be submitted annually where these thresholds are exceeded, regardless of whether an adjustment is made.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply the Caribbean Economic Substance Regulations as such, but economic substance is required under Colombian general anti-abuse rules, the intermediation or interposicion doctrine applied by the State Council, and OECD BEPS Action 12. A Colombian SAS must demonstrate real employees, premises, operational capacity, and decision-making in Colombia to recognize manufacturing income; a shell affiliate used to divert profit out of Colombia is recharacterized, its transactions are disregarded, and DIAN may deny treaty and tax-haven relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Concealment of Export Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Colombian Criminal Code Article 434A carries imprisonment of up to 108 months and fines; DIAN will assess the omitted tax, a 100%-200% accuracy penalty under Article 648 ET, and moratory interest. All involved shell entities are subject to dissolution and accumulated tax debts.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign false returns are jointly and personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and may be subject to asset seizure, travel restrictions, and imprisonment; the corporate veil may be pierced when the SAS is used as a mere collection address or facade.
Shell Company - Artificial Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: DIAN disallows the payments, recomputes the Colombian taxable income at arm's length under Articles 260-2 through 260-11 ET, imposes a 100% penalty on the omitted tax, and classifies the payer as a violation of the criteria for beneficial ownership in treaty contexts. The avoided withholding tax is re-collected as a 15% interest or royalty withholding.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for fraudulent transfer pricing documentation, may be fined up to 15,000 UVT, and can be criminally charged with conspiracy for tax fraud; foreign principals are jointly liable and may face expulsion or a watch-list entry in Colombia.
AML - Money Laundering Through Digital Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Colombian Criminal Code Article 323 punishes money laundering with 128 to 270 months imprisonment and fines proportional to the value of the assets. Financial and digital-payment operators must file Suspicious Transaction Reports (ROS); failure to do so is punished by the Financial Information and Analysis Unit and the Superintendencia de Sociedades.

Director personal liability: The beneficial owner, resident agent, and compliance officer are each personally exposed; assets derived from the offense are subject to effective extinction of ownership, and preventive detention may be ordered during the investigation, with no bail for recurrent corporate offenses.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: DIAN applies an adjustment based on the best comparable method and imposes the penalty provided by Article 651 ET of 5% of the value of the non-declared operation or 5% of the taxpayer's gross assets for not filing the informative declaration, and in serious cases the penalty for fraud reaches 100%-200% of omitted tax.

Director personal liability: The CFO and legal representative are personally liable for false transfer pricing documentation, may be disqualified from holding office for 5 years, and the directors who approved intercompany rates with conscious disregard may be prosecuted for tax fraud; Colombian courts permit piercing where the entity lacks independent director control.
Illegal Dividend Distribution - Withholding Evasion

Statutory sanction: The unpaid withholding is assessed against the distributor at 100% of the omitted withholding tax plus moratory interest under Article 375 ET. The dividend distribution is also reclassified as a taxable remittance subject to additional withholding when the underlying profit had not been authorized for distribution.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize illegal distributions are required to restore the distributed sums to the company under Article 151 of the Commercial Code and are jointly and severally liable to DIAN for the resulting tax; the foreign recipient becomes a co-debtor and subject to dividend withholding re-collected at a 10% rate plus penalty. Travel bans and embargo of assets apply after the DIAN assesses responsibility.

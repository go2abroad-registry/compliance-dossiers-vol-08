For the user of a permanent free trade zone operating a bonded depot, the qualifying net equity must evidence total assets minus liabilities of at least approximate USD 125,000 (equity requirement of Decree 2147/2016) and customs guarantee coverage is assessed by DIAN, not by the Chamber of Commerce.

Step-by-step for a foreign-owned S.A.S. operating a bonded depot in a Colombian free trade zone: (1) Incorporate the S.A.S. through the Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce) and obtain the Certificado de Existencia y Representación Legal; (2) obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) — assigned automatically by DIAN at incorporation, or filed through MUISCA/DIAN by a duly appointed local legal representative (apoderado) for foreign non-resident entities; (3) complete and keep current the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) identifying income tax liability, VAT (IVA) liability as 'Régimen Común', customs status, and free-trade-zone user status; (4) obtain the bonded warehouse authorization as 'depósito aduanero permanente' or free-trade-zone user certification through DIAN's customs authority and the Comité de Zonas Francas; (5) activate the electronic invoicing system ('habilitación de facturación electrónica') with DIAN before commencing taxable operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia has no US-style check-the-box regime; the S.A.S. is per se a resident corporation under Estatuto Tributario Article 12. Available statutory elections: (a) Free Trade Zone industrial-user certification under Law 1004/2005 and Decrees 2147/2016 and 659/2018, enabling the preferential 20% corporate income tax rate under ET Article 240-1 instead of the general 35% rate; (b) election to apply the unified 'Régimen SIMPLE' under ET Articles 905 ff., though unavailable to free-trade-zone users and therefore inapplicable to this entity; (c) election to file consolidated income tax returns with a Colombian corporate group under ET Article 23-1; (d) election of IFRS-based tax recognition criteria for the opening and subsequent tax periods; (e) mandatory, non-elective hybrid mismatch, CFC, and dual-resident tie-breaker rules under Law 2010/2019 and ET Articles 884-891.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no monetary turnover threshold for habitual business sellers: VAT responsibility arises from the first transaction once activity is habitual. The de minimis exclusion under ET Article 437 applies only to isolated/occasional sales or sellers of exclusively exempt goods where total proceeds in the prior or current calendar year do not exceed 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 174.3 million for 2025, with UVT set at COP 49,799). A bonded warehouse operator in a free trade zone engages in habitual commercial operations and must register as a full 'Régimen Común' VAT responsible entity through the RUT from inception.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods admitted into, stored within, and removed from the Colombian free trade zone to a third-country destination are treated as exports for VAT purposes; the introduction of goods from the national customs territory into the free trade zone is itself characterized as an export. Bonded warehouse, storage, handling, and ancillary services physically performed inside the free trade zone exclusively for registered free-trade-zone users and used exclusively within the zone perimeter are deemed 'exportación de servicios' under ET Article 481 and are subject to VAT at 0%. The supplier must prove the recipient's valid FTZ user status, the physical location of the service within the FTZ, and the direct relationship between the service and the FTZ user's authorized or industrial purpose. Input VAT attributable to such zero-rated export services is creditable and refundable through DIAN's periodic VAT returns, including where export transactions exceed taxable domestic transactions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX) validated in the DIAN SIGLO XXI / VUCE customs platform authorizing physical exit from the free trade zone
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or road transport document evidencing physical departure to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas) showing settlement through an authorized Colombian financial intermediary
  • Electronic export invoice (FEV) bearing the CUFE code, export tariff classification, and reference to the DEX number
  • Free trade zone entry/exit inventory register and authorized bonded warehouse operator records (kárdex de inventarios and warehouse authorization)
  • Free trade zone user certificate, commercial invoice, packing list, and, where applicable, certificate of origin

Electronic invoicing is mandatory. The 'Factura Electrónica de Venta' (FEV) must be transmitted in real time to DIAN through certified software via REST/SOAP APIs and prior validation ('validación previa') before the invoice is made available to the buyer, in accordance with Resolution 000165/2023, Resolution 000042/2020, and Decree 2242/2015. Each invoice must carry the Código Único de Facturación Electrónica (CUFE) or Código Único de Documento Soporte (CUDS), a QR code, and the DIAN electronic validation response. Export invoices must include the applicable exchange/export codes and reference the customs export declaration. Invoices transmitted without authorization, or not transmitted, deprive the buyer of input VAT credit and expense deduction rights and expose the seller to administrative fines under ET Articles 652 and 652-10.

DIAN conducts cross-system audits matching the customs DEX, FTZ inventory records, electronic invoices, and foreign exchange repatriation filings. If export proceeds are not repatriated within the mandatory 180-day term, or if physical export is not substantiated, DIAN retroactively disallows the 0% VAT treatment, assesses 19% VAT on the transaction value plus daily moratory interest under ET Articles 634 and 635, applies the inexactness penalty of up to 100%–200% of the omitted tax under ET Article 648 as reformed by Law 2277/2022, and may impose fines of 5,000–10,000 UVT for false export documentation. Where zero-rating is obtained fraudulently or the goods never leave the FTZ, criminal liability for contraband under Law 1762/2015 may attach. In addition, loss of the FTZ user certificate allows DIAN to claw back the preferential 20% corporate income tax rate and all VAT benefits for the entire noncompliant period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account operations; Colombia imposes no exchange controls, currency auctions, or differential exchange-rate bands. Current-account items are fully convertible and require only channelling through an IMC, including dividend remittances, interest, royalties, software and BPO service export proceeds, and trade settlements. Capital-account foreign direct investment (FDI) is likewise unrestricted in amount, but the equity contribution must be channelled through an IMC using the Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional (Formulario No. 4) and registered with Banco de la República's Registro de Inversiones Internacionales; such registration is the legal precondition for future dividend remittance and capital repatriation. Profit remittance hurdles are documentary rather than quantitative: the company must evidence paid and settled corporate income tax, submit audited financial statements, produce the shareholders' meeting minutes approving the dividend, and present the FDI registration certificate, all channelled under the applicable current-account exchange declaration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia does not impose any mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds, meaning there is no statutory percentage surrender of export receipts into COP. However, all proceeds from exports of goods and services must be repatriated and channelled through an authorised IMC within 12 months from the date of the export customs declaration (Declaración de Exportación, DEX) for goods, or within the agreed commercial payment term, capped at 12 months, for services; extensions are possible only upon justified application to Banco de la República. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency in a local foreign-currency account (cuenta en moneda extranjera), but every collection must be reported through the applicable Declaración de Cambio (Formulario No. 1 for goods exports, Formulario No. 3 for services exports). Non-compliance triggers administrative fines and legal sanctions under the Régimen Cambiario, and may additionally impair DIAN customs privilege for FTZ warehouse operators.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Banco de la República), acting jointly with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN), administers the Colombian foreign-exchange regime (Régimen de Cambios Internacionales) under Decree 1068 of 2015 and Circular Reglamentaria Externa DCIN-83 of Banco de la República. All cross-border currency transactions must be channelled through an authorised foreign-exchange intermediary (Intermediario del Mercado Cambiario, IMC), namely a commercial bank supervised by the SFC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: foreign shareholders remit USD/EUR by SWIFT MT103 to the entity's foreign-currency account at a local IMC bank, specifying in SWIFT field 70 the Banco de la República concept code 4012 (Inversión Directa en Sociedades). The IMC converts at the Tasa Representativa del Mercado (TRM) or retains the foreign currency, and simultaneously files the Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional (Formulario No. 4). The investment is registered with Banco de la República's Registro de Inversiones Internacionales before any dividend remittance or capital repatriation. Evidentiary bundle: notarised shareholders' capitalisation minutes, amended bylaws registered with the Cámara de Comercio, updated certificate of existence and legal representation, and the DIAN FTZ-user resolution confirming the entity's bonded-depot authorisation.Clearing rails: (a) cross-border supplier payments for bonded inventory are executed by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers or MT202 bank-to-bank transfers out of the bank's USD account, linked to the import invoice and reported on Formulario No. 2 (Importaciones de Bienes); (b) documentary trade settlements use commercial letters of credit (UCP600, sight or usance) issued by the IMC's trade desk against transport documents, invoices and customs declarations; (c) local COP disbursements for logistics, DIAN duties and customs brokers are cleared through ACH Colombia for low-value items and Banco de la República's high-value RTGS (Sistema de Pagos de Alto Valor, SPAV) for same-day settlements; (d) cross-border service payments (insurance, royalties, IT support) use Formulario No. 3 supported by contracts. All outbound payments preserve an audit trail from SWIFT purpose codes through the IMC's exchange declaration to the enterprise resource planning payment block.Deliverable USD/COP forward contracts (forwards on TRM) executed with a local IMC against a documented underlying commercial exposure such as bonded-inventory imports or dividend accruals.Cross-currency swaps (USD/COP) for structural hedging of long-term USD procurement or refinancing obligations.FX swaps combining a spot transaction with a forward reversal for short-term liquidity management in the bonded inventory cycle.USD/COP over-the-counter options (calls and puts) purchased through an IMC for strict currency-risk cover with no speculative short positions.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only when settled in COP cash through a local IMC and treated as hedges under IFRS; offshore non-IMC NDFs are not recognisable under the Régimen Cambiario and create un-enforceable FX audit exposure for the Colombian treasury.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: LOW. Cross-border fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Airwallex) do not hold a Colombian banking licence nor an SFC foreign-exchange intermediary authorisation; they cannot lawfully open a Colombian current account, cannot file Declaraciones de Cambio, and cannot be used to channel export proceeds without breaching the FX regime. EMIs may be used only as a secondary auxiliary channel for minor cross-border vendor payments where no COP conversion occurs, provided the local tier-1 IMC bank remains the primary treasury and clearing rail with a segregated foreign-currency account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full local file and master file documentation is mandatory when any of the following thresholds are exceeded based on the applicable annual UVT (2025 UVT = COP 49,799): total gross assets >= 45,000 UVT (approx. COP 2,240,955,000), gross income >= 61,000 UVT (approx. COP 3,037,739,000), or aggregate related-party transactions >= 90,000 UVT (approx. COP 4,481,910,000). Lower-volume taxpayers with assets >= 4,500 UVT or income >= 8,000 UVT must still file the informative transfer pricing schedule (Form 120).

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in certain offshore centres, but the GAAR and DIAN anti-abuse principles require genuine commercial substance for FTZ companies. A bonded depot must maintain physical premises in the FTZ, employees, operational records, inventory logs, and actual risk-bearing. Artificial arrangements lacking substance will be disregarded and the preferential FTZ rate denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment of 4 to 9 years plus restitution of the evaded tax and moratory interest; administrative penalty arrays include a 100% evasion surcharge and DIAN-determined additional tax checks.

Director personal liability: Under Law 1258 of 2008, courts may pierce the Limited Liability shield where the S.A.S. is used as a façade or vehicle for tax fraud. Directors who signed the false return face joint civil liability, criminal indictment, asset freezes, travel bans (anotación de salida del país), and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial FTZ Abuse

Statutory sanction: DIAN will disallow the preferential rate retroactively, re-tax previously benefited profits at the standard 35% plus moratory interest, impose a 100% surcharge, and revoke the FTZ operating permit. Criminal anti-evasion action may also be initiated.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing exposes directors and shareholders to the assessed tax liabilities, surcharges, and interest. Senior officers face direct criminal responsibility as co-planners or beneficiaries of the artificial scheme, including suspension from corporate management and exclusion from foreign trade privileges.
AML / CFT Violations (Bonded Warehouse Sector)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines per transaction or per control failure as determined by the Superintendency of Ports and Transport and DIAN; criminal money laundering charges can result in imprisonment of 10 to 30 years and seizure of goods.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and compliance officers are individually liable for failures in AML controls. Penalties include heavy personal fines, travel restrictions, disqualification from the cargo/warehouse sector and financial services, and imprisonment for wilful or grossly negligent misconduct.

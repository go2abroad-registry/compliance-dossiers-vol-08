Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – official portal: www.dian.gov.co; electronic services via MUISCA and RUTWeb.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must appoint a resident legal representative in Colombia, register with the local Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio) if forming a local branch or company, and obtain a tax identification number (NIT – Número de Identificación Tributaria) through DIAN. For a Colombian SAS, the incorporation process at the notary and Chamber of Commerce is followed by RUT (Registro Único Tributario) registration before DIAN to activate all tax obligations, including VAT (IVA) status as a responsible economic operator. A non-resident entity with Venezuelan-type activity must directly register with DIAN to obtain a NIT and VAT ID before issuing invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must appoint a resident legal representative in Colombia, register with the local Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio) if forming a local branch or company, and obtain a tax identification number (NIT – Número de Identificación Tributaria) through DIAN. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Colombian domestic law. The SAS may elect to be subject to the SIMPLE (Simplified Taxation Regime) if annual gross income is below the legal threshold (currently approximately COP 80,000,000), which replaces income tax and VAT for eligible small entities. Hybrid mismatch limitations and anti-deferral rules under Law 1955 of 2019 apply mandatorily; no elective regime is available to disregard the Colombian entity for tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold: all persons performing taxable activities in Colombia (sale of goods or provision of services) must register for IVA in the RUT, irrespective of turnover level. For services rendered by non-residents to Colombian recipients, the Colombian importer or recipient is the withholding agent and must self-assess IVA where the service is used in the national territory. Optional SIMPLE regime registration threshold: gross annual income below COP 80,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods from Colombian customs territory are zero-rated (0% IVA) and exempt from VAT on the final sale. Export of services and intellectual property may also qualify for zero-rating when the service is performed inside Colombia and economically used outside the country, provided the exporter meets DIAN’s documentary requirements and receives payment from abroad through authorized channels.

  • Export Declaration (Formulario de Exportación / Declaración de Exportación) mastered via MUISCA
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (Transport Document evidencing exit from Colombian customs territory)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Declaration (Certificación de Reintegro de Divisas o Declaración de Cambio)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Purchase Order, Sales Contract, and/or Manufacturing Service Agreement with foreign buyer
  • Customs Phytosanitary or Quality Certificates, if applicable, under DIAN and ICA requirements

Mandatory electronic invoicing (factura electrónica) operates through DIAN’s real-time validation system. Sellers must issue XML invoices with CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) or CUDE (Código Único de Documento de Equivalencia) digital stamping, transmit each invoice to DIAN before delivery, and use only a DIAN-authorized technological provider (proveedor tecnológico). Fiscal clearance is real-time: no invoice is valid unless validated and electronically stamped by DIAN.

DIAN may audit exporters retrospectively for up to five tax years. Fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger clawback of the full VAT benefit plus default interest, a penalty equivalent to up to 200% of the omitted tax (Article 675-2 and 697 of the Colombian Tax Statute), and criminal sanctions for customs fraud and illegal external trade operations. Exporters must retain the complete efflux, transport, and repatriation evidence for audit; failure to repatriate foreign currency within the 180-day deadline invalidates the zero-rating and exposes the exporter to immediate VAT assessment and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed-floating peso exchange-rate system with liberal but channeled convertibility. Current-account items — trade in goods, services, dividends, royalties, interest on registered financings, and other transfer payments — are freely convertible at the market exchange rate, provided they are routed through an authorized exchange intermediary when a foreign-currency payment or collection is involved. Capital-account transactions are permitted but subject to registration and intermediation rules: foreign direct investment in a Colombian S.A.S. must be registered with Banco de la República through the IMC using Form 5 (declaración de inversión internacional) in order for the investor to access the organized FX market for subsequent capital repatriation or profit remittance. Profit remittance is legally viable once the investee company has complied with annual corporate income-tax obligations, adopted dividend distributions at a shareholders meeting, supported the distribution with audited or legally sufficient financial statements, and paid the applicable dividend withholding tax. The main practical hurdles are documentary: delayed recognition of foreign investment, inconsistencies between the shareholder registry and the Form 5 filing, lack of transfer-pricing documentation for related-party transactions, and failure to maintain the exchange declaration with the IMC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For exports of tangible goods, the exporter must channel the corresponding foreign-currency proceeds through an authorized exchange intermediary and sell the proceeds in the Colombian exchange market within six months from the date of shipment, as evidenced by the transport document, unless Banco de la República has granted a special extension or the exporter is covered by a specific regime such as Free Trade Zones. The compulsory surrender ratio is effectively 100% of the FOB value of the export, net only of authorized expenses such as freight, insurance, and commissions that are paid through the exchange market and supported by proper documentation. The IMC issues an exchange declaration (declaración de cambio) evidencing the liquidation, and this declaration must be aligned with DIAN export declarations and the commercial contract for the plush toy OEM operations.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia, assisted by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia for bank and AML/CFT supervision and by the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales for foreign-trade and customs reporting. The foreign-exchange policy is implemented through authorized intermediaries of the exchange market (intermediarios del mercado cambiario), principally local commercial banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary banking channel and Medium as an ancillary digital payments component. An offshore digital EMI cannot replace a Colombian authorized exchange intermediary because Colombia mandates that export proceeds, foreign investment, and trade payments be channeled through the regulated exchange market. An overseas EMI wallet may be used as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail only if the competing foreign account is reported as a compensation account when required by Banco de la República and if all mandatory FX liquidations are normalized through the local IMC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 wire from its own bank account to the Colombian IMC’s correspondent account, with the beneficiary being the Colombian S.A.S. and the reference field stating Capital Contribution, shareholder name, and Colombian NIT. The funds may arrive in USD, EUR, or other freely convertible currency; upon crediting, the IMC converts to COP at the representative market rate and requests the client to file the foreign-investment registration before any disposal or dividend payment. For compliance completeness, the bank must receive: the corporate resolution authorizing the share subscription or capital increase, a notarized certificate of existence and representation of the S.A.S., shareholder identification, proof of source of funds, and a draft Form 5 for the Banco de la República registration. Loan capital should be structured separately as a shareholder loan with a documented loan agreement, market-rate interest, and tax substance to avoid recharacterization as hidden equity.Settlement of imported raw materials and tooling for the plush doll production should be executed by SWIFT MT103 for smaller pre-agreed trade payments or by commercial documentary letters of credit for larger, recurring shipments. The Colombian IMC will release foreign currency only against a supporting exchange declaration and the underlying trade documentation: commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, import declaration filed through DIAN, and, where applicable, cargo insurance. Payments must match the beneficiary name and account of the supplier stated in the contract; any prepayment, advance payment, or third-party settlement structure may be treated as an atypical payment operation and delayed by anti-money-laundering controls. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding when the dividend is paid from profits already taxed at the 35% corporate rate; 35% withholding when the dividend source is exempt or untaxed income, with appropriate credits.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar fiscal year ending 31 December, the return and consolidated tax payment are due between the 9th and 15th calendar day of September of the following fiscal year, according to a DIAN-instructioned schedule by the last two digits of the NIT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory TP local-file/master-file requirement for taxpayers whose gross income in the prior tax year equals or exceeds COP 3,000 million, or whose gross assets equal or exceed COP 5,000 million (annually adjusted by DIAN).

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR), but the S.A.S. must demonstrate genuine operational substance—physical manufacturing premises, production staff, inventory, utility consumption, and real negotiation functions—to resist GAAR recharacterization (Art. 260-10 E.T.) and to preserve DTA benefits. Shell structures lacking employees, production assets, and decision-making activity are open to judicial piercing and denial of treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Inaccuracy penalty of 100%–160% of the understated tax, plus interest; criminal tax evasion under Article 350 of the Colombian Criminal Code carries imprisonment of 4 to 9 years and a fine of up to 100,000 UVT (approx. COP 4,706 million).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or knowingly conceal Colombian-source revenue face joint and several civil liability for the unpaid tax, travel bans (restricción de salida del país), and criminal indictment for aggravated tax fraud.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: DIAN recharacterization under the Colombian GAAR / anti-abuse rules; denial of transfer pricing benefits and treaty reduction; imposition of the 160% inaccuracy penalty; potential inclusion in the DIAN 'inexistent firms' blacklist and cross-border tax information exchange referrals.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil pierced under civil fraud doctrines; directors are personally liable for resulting tax deficiencies, must disgorge unjust enrichment, and may face 5–8 years imprisonment for fraudulent simulation (interposita persona).
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under Law 1762/2015 and Criminal Code Article 323 (money laundering): imprisonment of 10 to 20 years, fines of COP 6,700 to 1 million UVT, seizure of goods and assets, together with closure of commercial establishments and mandatory imprisonment without parole for organised crime.

Director personal liability: Directors acting as beneficial owners or compliance officers who knowingly fail to perform customer-due-diligence or who process suspicious transactions are personally chased for restitution, may be subject to travel bans and provisional detention, and face incarceration up to 20 years.

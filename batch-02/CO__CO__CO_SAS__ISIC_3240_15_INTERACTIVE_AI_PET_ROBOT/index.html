Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Unidad Administrativa Especial Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — official portal: www.dian.gov.co; transactional platform: MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado).

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian S.A.S. owned by non-residents, the statutory tax ID (NIT — Número de Identificación Tributaria) is obtained through the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) process. The foreign entity must appoint a legal representative in Colombia, and the S.A.S. is incorporated via a private deed or public notary, then registered with the Cámara de Comercio; thereafter the legal representative accesses the DIAN SIE/MUISCA portal using the document numbers and registration certificate to inscribe the company in the RUT and obtain the definitive NIT. The RUT is the single universal registration and must be updated before commencing any taxable activity; the procedure is electronic and generally completed within 1–5 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the Colombian S.A.S. is a resident corporation subject to income tax at entity level on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment is available).

Available election forms

Colombia does not provide a general check-the-box election; tax classification follows legal form. Limited statutory options include: (a) election to be taxed under the Régimen Simple de Tributación (RST) for eligible small entities whose gross income does not exceed 3,500 UVT and whose activities are not excluded by law; (b) fiscal consolidation election under ET Art. 133-1 for groups meeting the 50% common-control and economic unity requirements; (c) no hybrid-mismatch election, with hybrid structures addressed through CFC and BEPS-aligned rules under Decretos 1477/2020 and ET Art. 118-1. All elections are performed via DIAN’s formal RUT or tax-return procedures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None. Every person or legal entity carrying out any taxable economic activity in Colombia, including importers and exporters, must register in the RUT and, when liable, be enrolled as an IVA responsible before commencing operations; there is no minimum annual turnover threshold to trigger VAT registration for an S.A.S.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical exports of goods from Colombian customs territory are excluded from VAT (0% effective rate) when the goods leave the country under a valid registered export declaration. Export services are excluded from VAT where the service is supplied to a foreign recipient without Colombian domicile and is actually used outside Colombia. The exporter must evidence the export through complete customs documentation and, where the export generates foreign exchange, must comply with the FX repatriation obligation in order to claim the VAT refund on related input purchases as 'IVA descontable'.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) registered in MUISCA/DIS
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent transport document evidencing departure from Colombia
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Ingreso y Canalización de Divisas) issued by the Colombian intermediary bank, where FX repatriation is required

Electronic invoicing is mandatory for all taxpayers, including S.A.S. entities. Invoices must be generated through software validated by DIAN, transmitted to DIAN in real time via MUISCA web services, and must incorporate a unique code (CUFE or CUDS) as the digital stamp. DIAN performs structured pre-validation before the invoice is accepted; accepted invoices must be archived for at least five years. Export operations require the customs digital document ‘Declaración de Exportación’ and, when applicable, a 'documento soporte' for acquisitions from users in Zona Franca.

DIAN vigorously audits exporters claiming zero-rated VAT. Missing or inconsistent export documentation, or failure to repatriate FX within the 180-day term, triggers retroactive clawback of the excluded VAT plus default interest and the penalty for inaccuracy under ET Art. 647 (up to 100% of the omitted VAT or a floor of 1,000 UVT). Fraudulent export refund schemes may additionally result in customs and criminal sanctions for aggravated tax fraud under Ley 1762/2015 and Ley 2277/2022, including imprisonment and fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso is fully convertible in international markets, but Colombia operates a managed-floating exchange regime and a 'regulated foreign exchange market' (mercado cambiario). Mandatory exchange transactions, including foreign direct investment contributions and associated dividend remittances, must be settled through authorized intermediaries of the Banco de la República. Capital account inflows from non-resident shareholders require registration of the investment with the Central Bank and documentary conformity with the local entity's tax and corporate records. Profit remittance is legally permissible without quantitative exchange-control restriction once the foreign investment is registered and the S.A.S. has discharged applicable withholding taxes (i.e., dividends tax, income tax compliance), but it must be evidenced through the applicable Declaración de Cambio. Current account operations, such as import settlements and export proceeds, are subject to channeling rules and bank-level documentary oversight. The principal hurdles are evidential and procedural, not authorization-based: late or omitted registration, missing tax certification, and failure to route funds through the regulated market lead to fines and fiscal ineffectiveness of the remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Banco de la República's External Resolution No. 8 of 2000 and related exchange regulations, export proceeds from goods must be 'canalized' (channeled) through the regulated foreign exchange market. The standard statutory timeline is six (6) months from the date of shipment for sight or advanced-payment export sales; for deferred-payment export sales, the proceeds must be channeled within six (6) months after the contractual maturity of each installment, with the aggregate channeling period not exceeding twelve (12) months from shipment unless a specific Central Bank exemption applies. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into Colombian pesos; however, 100% of export receipts must be channeled by one of the permitted mechanisms: sale of foreign currency to an authorized domestic bank, deposit into a foreign-currency account held in Colombia, or transfer into a registered compensation account (cuenta de compensación). The S.A.S. must maintain evidence of collection and channeling for each export transaction and report through the applicable Declaración de Cambio form.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia, acting jointly with the Ministry of Finance and the DIAN in the enforcement of Colombia's foreign exchange regime

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as a primary treasury settlement account; Medium as an auxiliary collection rail only. Legal constraints in Colombia require foreign exchange deposits, local currency custody, and regulated 'exchange market' operations to be handled exclusively through licensed financial institutions and foreign exchange intermediaries supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and Stripe may be used solely as lower-tier collection or disbursement tools for consumer card payments, but they cannot lawfully replace an onshore corporate bank account for the S.A.S.'s statutory capital escrow, export channeling, import settlement, or FX hedging. Any use of a foreign EMI must be architecturally layered so that the originating card processor settles into an omnibus account or a fully regulated Colombian account, with the final local bank receiving a SWIFT MT103 credit identifying the underlying merchant.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing protocol: the non-resident shareholder instructs its offshore bank to transmit an international SWIFT MT103 wire to the S.A.S.'s USD or EUR account at the tier-1 Colombian bank. The SWIFT instructions must accurately state the ultimate beneficiary as the Colombian S.A.S. by exact legal name and Colombian NIT (tax identification number), and the field 70 should include an unambiguous remittance reference such as 'APORTE DE CAPITAL S.A.S. – INVERSIÓN EXTRANJERA DIRECTA'. Upon receipt, the local bank posts the funds into the company account and the S.A.S. must submit the applicable Declaración de Cambio to register the operation as foreign direct investment, selecting the appropriate purpose code for capital contributions (the code referenced by the bank as covering direct investment contributions to a Colombian company; in electronic versions this is presented under the '3101' direct-investment category). The investment registration must be timely filed with, or through the bank and then processed by, the Banco de la República's foreign investment registry, normally within the calendar month following the transaction date. The treasury department must verify receipt via the SWIFT MT199/MT950 credit advice and the bank statement, record the contribution in the stockholders' equity ledger as capital or paid-in surplus, and retain a dossier containing the SWIFT MT103, bank credit slip, board resolution, share subscription agreement, pro-forma balance sheet, and the finished Declaración de Cambio. No additional governmental foreign-exchange authorization is required, but the underlying tax, corporate registration, and currency channeling obligations must be satisfied simultaneously.Outbound trade disbursements for imported robotic components, sensors, motors, speech synthesis modules, and raw materials are executed through two principal clearing rails from the same onshore treasury account: (i) Direct documentary remittance: the S.A.S. submits the import contract, commercial invoice, packing list, and the importer's registration record with the DIAN, and the bank executes a SWIFT MT103 wire payment in USD or EUR to the foreign supplier, converting local currency through the regulated FX market on the day of settlement. (ii) Commercial Letter of Credit: for higher-value or secure purchasing, the Colombian bank issues an irrevocable documentary credit under UCP 600 using SWIFT MT700; payment is made against presentation of complying documents (marine bill of lading, air waybill, commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin), with funds released by the LC issuing bank via SWIFT MT103/MT202. Where exports are made to offshore distributors or online retail marketplaces, incoming settlement wires are matched against the corresponding goods exports and the proceeds are channeled under the export channeling rules; the bank route crosses the SWIFT FIN / correspondent banking network, with local peso clearing through the CEDEC or ACH infrastructure and consumer-card settlement through Stripe or equivalent card gateways ultimately concentrating into the Colombian corporate account.USD/COP deliverable foreign-exchange forwards executed onshore with authorized Colombian financial intermediaries, with tenor aligned to forecast procurement or sale cycles.USD/COP non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) with offshore counterparties to hedge the embedded peso currency risk for cross-border trade and project financing flows.Cross-currency interest rate swaps converting foreign-denominated debt or purchase-payment obligations into COP liabilities with Bangladeshi and Chinese counterparty exposure hedged.FX swaps structured as simultaneous spot-forward trades for managing treasury liquidity mismatches between USD/EUR receipts and COP operational outflows.Custom or barrier USD/COP call options used in conjunction with import settlement contracts, subject to the requirement that the derivative be evidenced and reportable as an actual hedging transaction under the Colombian exchange regime.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Low as a primary treasury settlement account; Medium as an auxiliary collection rail only. Legal constraints in Colombia require foreign exchange deposits, local currency custody, and regulated 'exchange market' operations to be handled exclusively through licensed financial institutions and foreign exchange intermediaries supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and Stripe may be used solely as lower-tier collection or disbursement tools for consumer card payments, but they cannot lawfully replace an onshore corporate bank account for the S.A.S.'s statutory capital escrow, export channeling, import settlement, or FX hedging. Any use of a foreign EMI must be architecturally layered so that the originating card processor settles into an omnibus account or a fully regulated Colombian account, with the final local bank receiving a SWIFT MT103 credit identifying the underlying merchant.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

7.5% on dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders out of profits already subjected to the 35% corporate income tax. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For taxable year 2024, the Local File (Estudio de Precios de Transferencia) and the annual transfer pricing informative declaration (Form 2516) are mandatory when the Colombian S.A.S. has gross income, total gross assets, or net equity exceeding COP 4,706,500,000 (100,000 UVT), or when the aggregate value of transactions with related parties exceeds COP 470,650,000 (10,000 UVT). The Master File is additionally required when the S.A.S. forms part of a multinational group that meets the 100,000 UVT threshold in Colombian operations and the group's global consolidated revenue exceeds the annual threshold specified by DIAN.

Economic substance scope: Colombia applies a substantive anti-abuse rule under Article 658-3 of the Tax Code, allowing DIAN to recharacterize transactions lacking economic substance. The S.A.S. must maintain actual personnel, facilities, inventory, and business risks proportionate to the assembly of bionic AI pet robots. A purely passive conduit entity, without employees or operational headquarters in Colombia, will be re-domiciled and taxed as a resident. Thin capitalization limits the deduction of interest paid to related parties when debt exceeds three times the net equity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return (Inexactitud y Fraude Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: The penalty for inexact or false returns is 100% of the tax omitted under Article 647 of the Colombian Tax Code. If the act amounts to criminal tax fraud, the penalties increase to imprisonment of 4 to 9 years and fines of up to 66.7% of the evaded amount (Criminal Law Article 434). The S.A.S. may also lose the right to carry forward losses for a year and be charged moratorian interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax under Article 794 of the Tax Code. Personal assets may be seized, travel prohibitions may be issued to prevent leaving Colombia, and the responsible director may be subject to criminal arrest and imprisonment for the same term as the corporate sanction.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping (Abuso de la Personalidad Jurídica)

Statutory sanction: DIAN may pierce the corporate veil, recharacterize the entity as a transparent arrangement, deny all DTA benefits, apply a 35% penalty on the adjusted taxable income, and impose a 100% penalty on the omitted tax. In a transfer-pricing audit, the transactions are restated at arm's-length and all disguised dividend payments are subjected to the 7.5% or 35% dividend withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Under Law 1429 of 2010 and Colombian commercial jurisprudence, the directors, legal representatives, and controlling shareholders of a shell S.A.S. are personally liable for the company's tax debts. They face civil restitution, disqualification from holding corporate office for up to 5 years, travel bans during the audit, and criminal charges for abuse of the corporate personality.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (Manipulación de Precios de Transferencia)

Statutory sanction: DIAN may adjust the taxable income upward to the arm's-length value, assessing the 35% income tax on the adjustment plus a penalty of 100% of the omitted tax. Failure to file the transfer pricing declaration or documentation gives rise to a fine of up to 20,000 UVT (approximately COP 941 million) and preclusion of deductibility of the transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length pricing are treated as tax offenders and become jointly liable for the adjustment and penalties. In aggravated cases, the fiscal authority requests criminal action against the legal representative who certified the fraudulent transfer pricing study, with penalties including confiscation of personal assets and imprisonment of 4 to 9 years.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Non-Compliance (Lavado de Activos)

Statutory sanction: Under Law 526 of 1999 and UAFE (Unidad de Información y Análisis Financiero) regulations, fines of up to 200 monthly minimum wages may be imposed for failing to report unusual transactions; assets traced to money laundering are subject to preventive seizure and forfeiture. If the company is found to have enabled laundering, the S.A.S. may be dissolved.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow the S.A.S. to receive or transfer tainted funds are personally liable as co-authors of money laundering. Colombian Criminal Law Article 323 imposes imprisonment of 9 to 18 years and a substantial fine; the corporate veil is set aside and the director's personal bank accounts and properties are subject to attachment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Unidad Administrativa Especial Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN), Colombia; official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; electronic transaction platform: DIAN services portal and Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior (VUCE).

E-government filing portal

A resident S.A.S. is assigned its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) upon incorporation. The practical sequence is: (i) execute corporate bylaws and appoint the legal representative; (ii) register the S.A.S. with the Cámara de Comercio through the Registro Único Empresarial (RUES); (iii) complete the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) before DIAN online, selecting ISIC 1050, VAT responsible status under the régimen común, income tax responsibilities, and withholding responsibilities; (iv) activate electronic invoicing through an authorized facturador or DIAN’s platform. A purely foreign entity that itself has Colombian taxable nexus, such as conducting imports, exports, or rendering taxable services to Colombian recipients, must appoint an authorized representative with notarized and apostilled powers, file the RUT with the foreign entity’s corporate data, and obtain its own NIT; no local incorporation is required for that representative function.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Colombian S.A.S. is a resident legal entity taxed on worldwide income under the Colombian Tax Statute; it is not eligible for pass-through or look-through treatment. A permanent establishment of a foreign corporation is similarly taxed at entity/PE level; no territorial election is available.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Colombian law. The only elective regime relevant to classification is adherence to the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) for eligible taxpayers with previous-year gross ordinary income not exceeding 100,000 UVT; that election produces an integrated presumptive tax. Hybrid mismatch rules under Law 2277/2022 operate automatically and do not confer an elective entity-classification choice; an S.A.S. cannot elect to be disregarded.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for legal persons: an S.A.S. must register as a VAT responsible taxpayer under the régimen común from its first taxable transaction or importation, regardless of turnover. Colombian law imposes no monetary threshold for companies. Natural persons eligible for the simplified VAT regime are not separately liable where prior-year gross income is below 3,500 UVT and gross assets are below 4,500 UVT, approximately COP 164.7 million and COP 211.8 million respectively at the 2024 UVT of COP 47,065; this threshold does not apply to the S.A.S.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods, including packaged UHT oat milk, are zero-rated under Article 481 of the Colombian Tax Statute when the seller sells to a non-resident purchaser and the goods physically leave Colombia’s customs territory under a valid export declaration. Zero-rating also applies to exported services rendered to non-residents for use exclusively abroad when the services do not generate Colombian-source income. The producer must hold export contracts, electronic export invoices, customs authorization, sanitary export certificates for food, transport evidence, and must repatriate export proceeds through the Colombian foreign-exchange market.

  • DIAN-accepted Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación - DEX) and electronic export invoice (Factura Electrónica de Exportación)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Cargo Manifest evidencing physical exit of the goods from Colombian customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Comprobante de Reintegro de Divisas) or SWIFT credit advice evidencing collection within the mandatory period

Mandatory national e-invoicing: each VAT-responsible seller must issue the Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV) with DIAN pre-validation. The XML invoice is transmitted in real time through DIAN web services using an API secured with OAuth2 and a digital certificate; DIAN returns a CUFE or CUDE cryptographic stamp and a QR code that must be displayed on the paper or PDF representation. The invoice is deemed legally issued only after DIAN accepts the XML. For export sales, the electronic export invoice and supporting transport and customs documents must be integrated into the same clearance flow.

DIAN and the customs authority cross-check the electronic export declaration, FEV, transport documents, and foreign-exchange intervention reports. If an export zero-rating claim is rejected, the S.A.S. must pay 19% retroactive VAT on the export price plus daily late-payment interest under Article 635 of the Tax Statute, and is exposed to the inaccurate-return penalty under Article 647, which may reach 100% of the tax difference in fraud-like cases. Customs fines and forfeiture for false or incomplete export declarations apply under Decree 1165/2019, and criminal liability may arise if export documentation is wilfully false. VAT refunds already received are clawed back with indexation and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a managed-float regime for the Colombian Peso (COP) with full convertibility of foreign exchange for transactions settled through the regulated 'mercado cambiario' (foreign exchange market). Current-account operations, including export of the aseptic barista-formula hydrolyzed oat milk units, imports of packaging raw materials, machinery, royalties, and freight, are freely convertible but must be 'canalizadas' (channeled) through authorized intermediaries with a formal 'declaración de cambio' filed with Banco de la República. Capital-account inflows, including direct foreign investment in the Colombian S.A.S., are permitted without prior government approval, but must be registered with Banco de la República within 12 months of the effective disbursement date to perfect the investor's legal right to remit dividends and repatriate invested capital. Profit remittances by a non-resident shareholder of the S.A.S. are lawful after (i) registration of the investment, (ii) payment of corporate income tax and the applicable dividend withholding tax (generally 5% for distributions to non-resident shareholders of Colombian-source earnings, subject to treaty limitations), and (iii) presentation of the exchange declaration. Practical hurdles include: inability to remit dividends if the investment registration is not legalized ahead of the distribution; mandatory conversion of foreign currency at TRM-disciplined market rates through a local bank; liquidity frictions for very large USD/COP spot trades during periods of central-bank discretionary intervention; and strict anti-money-laundering compliance reviews before any profit distribution is processed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia imposes no percentage-based mandatory conversion (surrender) ratio on export proceeds, but it does impose a 100% 'canalización' rule: all foreign currency proceeds from exports of goods, including UHT oat milk beverages classified under ISIC 1050 / HS 2202, must be channeled through the foreign exchange market by either selling the currency to an authorized intermediary, crediting it to a locally-held foreign-currency account, or depositing it into a 'cuenta de compensación' (compensation account) abroad. The statutory timing under Resolución Externa No. 1 of 2018 is: the exporter must collect the proceeds within the payment period agreed in the underlying contract, which generally cannot exceed 6 months from the date of the export customs declaration for standard open-account trade; if a longer commercial payment period is agreed, the proceeds must be collected and channeled by the contractual due date, and in all cases no later than 12 months from export, unless Banco de la República grants an exceptional extension. Funds credited to an offshore compensation account must be repatriated into the Colombian financial system within 4 months of the date of receipt in that account. Each export operation must be declared on Formulario No. 2 (Declaración de Cambio por Exportación de Bienes) presented through the local commercial bank, and failure to channel within the applicable deadline carries exchange-control penalties including fines, forced sale of the currency, and loss of the exporter's VAT-refund (IVA a favor) benefits on exported goods.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting through the Departamento de Cambios Internacionales (Foreign Exchange Department), the Junta Directiva (Board of Directors), and the Regulación Cambiaria framework issued under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, Decree 1068 of 2015, Resolución Externa No. 1 of 2018 (as amended by Resolución Externa No. 2 of 2023), and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83. The Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) supervises all authorized foreign exchange intermediaries and banking institutions, while the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) enforces the customs and tax interface for foreign trade settlements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for using offshore fintech EMIs (for example Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud, or NIUM) as the primary treasury gateway of the Colombian S.A.S. Colombian exchange control obligates all capital injections, export collection, import payments, and profit remittances to pass through authorized 'mercado cambiario' intermediaries supervised by the SFC, and an unlicensed offshore EMI cannot file exchange declarations, cannot hold the company's NIT-linked regulatory records, and cannot issue the 'declaración de cambio' documentation that Banco de la República requires. Funds arriving at the local bank through an EMI's umbrella clearing account are visible as third-party settlements, which can create source-of-funds opacity and jeopardize the registerable 'inversión extranjera' status of an equity injection. Legal constraints equally affect the outbound side: payments to overseas suppliers settled through an offshore EMI netting engine are not recognized as 'canalización' and expose the company to exchange penalties. Recommended approach: open and maintain the primary corporate accounts at Bancolombia S.A. and Banco de Bogotá S.A.; use regulated domestic fintech rails (including SEDPEs such as Nubank Colombia, ePayco, or Wompi) for low-value collections from e-commerce distribution channels; and engage an SFC-licensed FX broker or bank for all foreign-currency conversions. An offshore EMI may only act as an ancillary payables rail for very low-value SaaS subscriptions or global R&D cost recharges, capped at 20% of monthly operational disbursements, with complete reconciliation of MT103 records retained to evidence the underlying treasury flow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for the initial foreign equity injection: (1) execute the capital increase of the Colombian S.A.S. through a notarial or privately-legalized shareholders' deed and update the capital registry at the Chamber of Commerce; (2) ensure the foreign parent remits the equity contribution via SWIFT MT103 using an account in the parent's name at a reputable correspondent bank, naming the Colombian S.A.S. as beneficiary with NIT identification and purpose-of-payment code aligned to 'Inversión directa de capital' (Formulario No. 5, Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales); (3) the receiving bank will request submission of the investment declaration within 5 business days of the value date, together with the board resolution authorizing the capital increase, UBO registry, and evidence of viability of the UHT plant project; (4) Banco de la República's Foreign Exchange Department registers the investment and the bank issues the 'certificado de inversión', which is the controlling document for all future dividend remittances and capital repatriation; (5) if the injection is structured as a shareholder loan, the inflow must be classified as 'financiamiento externo' with a monetary maturity embedded in the exchange declaration, and the company must segregate and report interest at rates aligned to OECD transfer-pricing rules; (6) all FX conversion from USD/EUR to COP is executed at the negotiable TRM, with no obligations to convert beyond the market channel privileges; (7) verification layer: the remitter bank name must match the signature of the equity subscription, error in remitter name is the most frequent cause of returned or suspended capital inflows.The S.A.S. UHT plant disbursement rails are structured as follows: (a) Import of raw materials (aseptic carton rolls, oxygen-barrier films, aluminum lids, oat flour, enzyme preparations): pay via SWIFT MT103/TT with 30% advance and 70% against presentation of the bill of lading, each remittance quoting the import's DIAN customs declaration number and the Formulario No. 1 exchange declaration attached; (b) Capital equipment and engineering services for the plant: finance through irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit issued under UCP 600 by Bancolombia or Banco de Bogotá, at sight or usance, with confirmation by a top-tier EU/US bank if seller insists, covering 100% of the CIP/FOB contract value; (c) Royalties and technology licensing to the foreign licensor: remit via TT/MT103 with the corresponding withholding tax certificate (generally 15% on royalties, or 10% where the license is registered with the Colombian National Copyright and Patent Office, SIC), using the purpose-of-payment code for 'regalías' and an exchange declaration; (d) Domestic Colombian disbursements (payroll, Colombian distributors, utilities, municipal taxes): route through the local ACH 'CUD' (Compensación Electrónica de Pagos) / PSE rails in COP with same-day or T+1 settlement; (e) Export collections from Latin America, EU, and US buyers: require MT103 remittances quoting the invoice and the import/export reference number, collection under documentary drafts (URC 522) for growth accounts, and standby LCs for substitutes or new distributors; the Colombian treasury desk should concentrate USD inflows in a foreign-currency account at Bancolombia and effect a spot conversion to COP only when operational liquidity is required, preserving the legal 'canalización' status of the proceeds; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for using offshore fintech EMIs (for example Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud, or NIUM) as the primary treasury gateway of the Colombian S.A.S. Colombian exchange control obligates all capital injections, export collection, import payments, and profit remittances to pass through authorized 'mercado cambiario' intermediaries supervised by the SFC, and an unlicensed offshore EMI cannot file exchange declarations, cannot hold the company's NIT-linked regulatory records, and cannot issue the 'declaración de cambio' documentation that Banco de la República requires. Funds arriving at the local bank through an EMI's umbrella clearing account are visible as third-party settlements, which can create source-of-funds opacity and jeopardize the registerable 'inversión extranjera' status of an equity injection. Legal constraints equally affect the outbound side: payments to overseas suppliers settled through an offshore EMI netting engine are not recognized as 'canalización' and expose the company to exchange penalties. Recommended approach: open and maintain the primary corporate accounts at Bancolombia S.A. and Banco de Bogotá S.A.; use regulated domestic fintech rails (including SEDPEs such as Nubank Colombia, ePayco, or Wompi) for low-value collections from e-commerce distribution channels; and engage an SFC-licensed FX broker or bank for all foreign-currency conversions. An offshore EMI may only act as an ancillary payables rail for very low-value SaaS subscriptions or global R&D cost recharges, capped at 20% of monthly operational disbursements, with complete reconciliation of MT103 records retained to evidence the underlying treasury flow.

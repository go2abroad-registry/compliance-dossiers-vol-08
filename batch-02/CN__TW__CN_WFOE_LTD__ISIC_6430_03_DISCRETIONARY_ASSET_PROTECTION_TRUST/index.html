Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-in capital exists under the Taiwan Company Act. However, before the Financial Supervisory Commission will issue a fiduciary trust operating licence, the company must demonstrate adequate paid-in capital and a capital adequacy plan, and the subscribed capital must be placed in a blocked escrow account at a Taiwan licensed bank, released only after the company registration certificate and business registration are obtained. The escrow agreement must prohibit withdrawals without the joint signature of the designated certified public accountant and a director.

Funding & unlock path: The office must obtain a fire safety equipment inspection certificate and fire protection compliance endorsement from the local Fire Bureau under the Civil Defense regulations. The premises must have functional smoke detectors, fire extinguishers, illuminated exit signs, and allowed occupancy load. Under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, the company must provide a workplace safety plan, first-aid supplies, emergency evacuation training, and compulsory labour and health insurance for employees. A structural load certificate is not required because no industrial machinery or storage racking is installed.

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration is triggered when a fixed-place business has average monthly taxable sales exceeding NT$80,000 for goods or NT$40,000 for services; non-resident suppliers of electronic services have a simplified VAT registration threshold when annual taxable sales exceed NT$480,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, 0% VAT applies when: (a) goods are physically exported from Taiwan customs territory and evidenced by a valid customs export declaration; or (b) services are supplied to a foreign enterprise or individual and used outside Taiwan, including qualified trust administration services rendered by a licensed entity to a non-resident discretionary trust, provided the economic benefit is consumed outside Taiwan and the service fee is paid from abroad in foreign currency and remitted into Taiwan. The zero-rating claim is valid only if contemporaneous export, transport, contract, invoicing, and foreign-exchange records are retained and the zero-rated sales are disclosed in the VAT return within the statutory filing period.

  • Customs Export Entry / Customs Export Declaration bearing the Taiwan Customs Administration clearance number
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Export commercial invoice and sales contract, plus any third-party export instruction document if shipment is made by a third party
  • Bank-issued Export Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate or Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds

No per-invoice pre-clearance or anti-fraud digital stamp is imposed. Taiwan requires Uniform Invoice issuance for taxable sales; Electronic Uniform Invoices (EUI) must be transmitted through the approved Turnkey Gateway/API to the MOF E-Invoice Cloud Service Platform for storage, lottery processing, and VAT reconciliation. EUI data is reconciled in the periodic VAT return (usually monthly or quarterly), and compliance is enforced through encrypted QR code validation, controlled invoice number ranges, and cross-validation between EUI records and filed VAT returns.

Taiwan tax authorities require full customs, transport, contractual, invoice, and foreign-exchange evidence to support every export zero-rating claim. If the claim is unverified or fraudulent, the zero rating is reversed, VAT is assessed at 5% with overdue interest computed from the original tax due date, and the taxpayer may face penalty exposure under the Business Tax Act and Tax Collection Act, including statutory fines of 5 to 15 times the evaded tax, business suspension, and criminal liability where forged or fraudulent documentation is used.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible for current account items under a managed floating exchange rate regime, while the capital account remains subject to SAFE registration and approval gates. The WFOE's receipt of trust capital contributed by a Taiwan-based discretionary trust is an inbound direct investment, requiring an FDI basic registration before any remittance, a designated foreign currency capital account, and capital verification by a Chinese CPA. Once registered, the proceeds may be converted to RMB under the willing conversion settlement regime, but the bank will verify underlying business authenticity and may request payment counterparty documentation before releasing each RMB payment from the pending settlement account. Current account earnings, including trust administration fees and treasury management fees, are freely convertible for bona fide transactions, subject to authenticity review and tax withholding. Profit remittance to the Taiwan trustee is not a free dividend channel: the WFOE must complete the annual statutory audit, file and settle corporate income tax, obtain a board resolution on profit distribution, and submit a SAFE profit remittance form through the domestic bank; tax certificates and non-resident withholding clearances are usually required. In practice, banks also screen for round-tripping and require documentary proof that the trust corpus and beneficiaries are not sourced from mainland China residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, SAFE and the customs authority operate an electronic matching regime rather than a fixed percentage surrender. The WFOE must repatriate 100% of export-related foreign exchange receipts into China and must not leave funds offshore. The standard monitoring benchmark is that export proceeds must be collected within 90 days from the date of customs declaration; if the commercial terms exceed 90 days, a trade credit report must be lodged with SAFE through the bank. Upon receipt, funds may be credited to a foreign exchange current account or settled to RMB at the PBOC-designated bank at the prevailing spot rate. There is no mandatory conversion ratio in the current account, but 100% repatriation and matching to the export customs record are mandatory; any historical surrender obligation has been superseded by the current trade foreign-exchange verification and willing settlement system for Normal-class enterprises.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Not viable as the WFOE's licensed banking counterparty. Under PBOC and NFRA rules, offshore digital EMI or e-money institutions do not hold a Chinese bank license and cannot maintain a RMB basic settlement account, receive FDI capital remittances, perform SAFE registration, or issue capital verification statements. Using a foreign EMI as an intermediary for capital injection is legally barred and would create serious AML and round-tripping red flags. At most, an offshore EMI may be used by the Taiwan trust to initiate a SWIFT payment to the WFOE's domestic bank, but the onshore account relationship, FX conversion, and clearing must sit with a licensed China commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound funds from the Taiwan trust must be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the trustee's bank account to the WFOE's SAFE-designated FDI capital account at a mainland licensed bank. Prior to remittance, the WFOE and the Taiwan trust must complete the FDI basic registration at the local SAFE or delegated bank; the foreign exchange purpose code for the remittance must identify foreign direct investment capital contribution rather than a current account code. The bank will verify that the remitter name matches the registered foreign shareholder, the amount is within the registered investment amount, and the source bank is not on a FATF action list. After receipt, the CPA issues a capital verification report; the funds may be placed in a pending RMB settlement account under willing conversion and released only for verified business expenditures such as salaries, rent, raw materials, and services. For RMB injections, use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) with the same SAFE registration reference and the proper payment purpose code or SAFE case reference in the payment message.Operational disbursements should use standard bank clearing rails. Domestic RMB payments flow through CNAPS; cross-border RMB payments flow through CIPS. Foreign-currency wires use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 COVER. For goods trade with overseas suppliers, use commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) and documentary collections (D/P or D/A) through the domestic issuing bank. For service fees and trust administration expenses, use wire transfers with current account purpose codes, prorated tax invoices, and supporting contracts. Outbound profit distribution to the Taiwan trust must be supported by audited financial statements, a board resolution on dividend distribution, proof that corporate income tax and the associated withholding tax have been settled, and a SAFE profit remittance application. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Independent CPA audit is compulsory if any one of the following exists: paid-in capital or net equity reaches NT$30,000,000 or more; total balance-sheet assets exceed NT$100,000,000; annual operating revenue exceeds NT$50,000,000; or the average number of employees exceeds 100 during the fiscal year. Audit is also mandatory regardless of size for any entity holding a Trust Enterprise license, any public company, bank, insurance company, securities firm, financial holding company, or other institution supervised by the FSC, and for any mainland-funded or foreign-invested enterprise directed by MOEAIC or the FSC to file audited financial statements. The trust-licensed WFOE in this payload is therefore compulsorily audited because fiduciary trust administration is a high-risk special license activity even if the capital, assets, revenue, or headcount tests are not met.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual enterprise income tax return must be filed and the final balance of tax paid on or before May 31 of the year following the relevant fiscal year, as required by Article 54 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law of the People's Republic of China.

The WFOE must clearly identify itself as a resident enterprise incorporated under PRC law. The Taiwan discretionary trust, as ultimate owner, must be disclosed in the beneficial ownership and shareholder schedules; if any trust protector or beneficiary is a PRC tax resident, the return should reflect the resulting PRC worldwide tax exposure and CFC or transparency risk.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance (Taiwan), acting through the Taipei, Northern Area, Central Area, Southern Area and Kaohsiung Area National Taxation Bureaus; online tax execution is handled through the Ministry of Finance e-Tax Portal and the e-Invoice Integrated Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Determine whether the PRC WFOE has a Taiwan fixed place of business or is a non-resident cross-border electronic-service supplier. Step 2 - For a Taiwan branch or subsidiary, first obtain the required corporate and foreign-investment approval and company registration from the Ministry of Economic Affairs, which issues the national unified tax number; the business tax registration then proceeds with the National Taxation Bureau serving the registered address. Step 3 - File the business tax registration with the National Taxation Bureau by presenting the foreign-company certificate of incorporation, articles of association, investment approval where required for mainland-registered investors, lease or desk-use evidence, identity documents of the Taiwan responsible person, and a power of attorney if a local tax agent is appointed. Step 4 - The National Taxation Bureau verifies the submission and issues the formal Business Tax Registration Certificate. Step 5 - For a PRC WFOE supplying digital advertising platform, DSP and SSP services into Taiwan from outside Taiwan without a fixed place of business, the simplified cross-border electronic-services registration channel of the Ministry of Finance applies once annual sales to Taiwan individuals exceed the statutory threshold; the WFOE submits its foreign registration certificate, service description, expected sales volume and local representative details, and receives a VAT/TIN registration number for quarterly Business Tax filing and electronic uniform invoice issuance. Step 6 - If only Taiwan-source payments subject to withholding tax arise and no VAT registration duty exists, a non-resident tax identification number is obtained through the payer's National Taxation Bureau at the time of withholding.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections; entity classification follows the Company Act and tax law. A Taiwan-incorporated subsidiary is taxed as a resident profit-seeking enterprise on worldwide income, while a branch of a PRC WFOE is taxed as a non-resident profit-seeking enterprise on Taiwan-source income without any elective pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NT$480,000 per year of Taiwan-individual sales for a non-resident supplier of cross-border electronic services including programmatic advertising DSP and SSP fees; zero monetary threshold for a Taiwan fixed-place operation, which must register before commencing taxable business. Domestic small-scale businesses with monthly taxable sales below NT$200,000 may qualify for the simplified tax levy.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Business Tax Act, goods physically exported from Taiwan and services supplied from Taiwan to a foreign enterprise or individual for use outside Taiwan, including programmatic advertising, demand-side platform and supply-side platform auction services, are eligible for the 0% VAT rate. The taxpayer must establish that the counter-party is non-resident, the consideration is received in convertible foreign exchange, and the actual benefit or use of the service takes place outside Taiwan; for digital ad services the evidence includes contract terms, geo-location, server data, campaign targeting logs and audience-use records. Zero-rated sales entitle the exporter to a refund of input VAT attributable to those export sales, paid after the National Taxation Bureau verifies the export documentation, foreign-exchange receipt and Business Tax return.

  • Customs Export Declaration issued by Taiwan Customs at clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill proving physical movement of goods out of the Taiwan customs territory
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Receipt or inward-remittance export-proceeds settlement record proving collection of the export price in foreign currency within the statutory period

A registered Taiwan seller must issue Uniform Invoices for taxable sales. Where the electronic uniform invoice regime applies, invoice data including seller tax number, buyer tax number, invoice number, random code, issuance date and amount must be transmitted to the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice Integrated Service Platform through the approved API or bulk-file channel within 48 hours of issuance or the applicable authorised upload window. The platform automatically reconciles e-invoice data against the Business Tax return and uses the public random code for the uniform-invoice lottery, which serves as the anti-fraud verification mechanism. Designated high-volume digital-platform and e-commerce taxpayers are mandated to issue e-invoices, and a paper or non-registered invoice cannot support VAT zero-rating or input tax refund where the e-invoice transmission requirement is breached.

The National Taxation Bureau cross-checks zero-rated export returns against the Customs export database, the Central Bank foreign-exchange reporting system and bank remittance records. If zero-rating is claimed without the export declaration, without matching foreign-exchange proceeds, with forged invoices, or where the customer or service is later determined to be domestic, the zero-rating is reversed, the 5% VAT is reassessed, arrears interest is imposed, and Article 51 of the Business Tax Act penalty of one to five times the evaded tax may apply. Criminal liability under the Tax Collection Act may be pursued for directors or responsible persons involved in fraudulent invoices, false customs declarations or fictitious export arrangements, and any input credit or VAT refund obtained through the challenged zero-rating will be clawed back.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is fully convertible on current account (goods, services, ordinary remittances) subject to authenticity review by domestic banks; capital account items (FDI equity injection, external debt, dividend remittances) require SAFE registration or bank-led recordal, with fund use restricted by the Foreign Investment Negative List and PBOC macro-prudential rules. Taiwan investors in a PRC WFOE are treated as non-resident foreign investors for SAFE purposes. Profit remittance is freely permitted after annual statutory audit, income tax settlement, dividend withholding tax payment (10% standard; limited relief under the 2015 Cross-Strait Tax Cooperation Agreement), and supporting resolution and financial statements verified by the remitting bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory repatriation of current-account export and service proceeds to a licensed PRC bank; since 2012 there is no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio, and FX proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency settlement accounts or converted at the enterprise's discretion. Goods-trade export receipts must be collected within the contractual term; banks monitor outflow/inflow against the 90-day benchmark under the trade-verification framework, extendable to 210 days in justified cases. Service-trade proceeds of the ad-tech DSP/SSP (programmatic platform fees, ad-inventory resale) are governed by the 2013 Service Trade FX Guidelines: full repatriation without a fixed statutory window, with banks performing authenticity checks using contracts, invoices, delivery logs and tax certificates.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC, 中国人民银行) as the monetary and payment-system authority; State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE, 国家外汇管理局) as the delegated foreign-exchange regulator. SAFE administers current and capital account convertibility, bank-led FX registrations, and cross-border payment supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Currenxie, Global Reach, MultiPass) cannot act as the WFOE's primary settlement or capital-escrow bank: SAFE requires service-export receipts to be repatriated to a PRC-regulated bank account, and FDI equity injection must be booked through a bank holding SAFE-delegated FX registration authority. EMIs are viable only as auxiliary foreign-side disbursement rails (e.g., funding a Taiwan or Singapore wallet for ad-publisher payouts), never as the apex treasury account; any such use must not create gaps in the onshore repatriation trail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% corporate income tax on taxable profit; a 5% additional tax generally applies to current-year earnings not distributed by the statutory deadline.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a nonresident shareholder. Taiwan resident companies generally receive dividends under a separate imputation and corporate tax treatment. A treaty or applicable statutory exemption may reduce the nonresident rate where conditions are satisfied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the annual return and payment are generally due by the end of May following the close of the taxable year. A taxpayer with a non-calendar fiscal year generally files within five months after fiscal year-end. The provisional return is generally filed during the statutory September filing period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Taiwan Local File is generally required where annual net operating revenue reaches at least NT$300,000,000 or annual related-party transaction amounts reach the applicable NT$200,000,000 threshold, subject to transaction-category rules, exemptions, and prescribed documentation requirements. A Master File is generally required for qualifying Taiwanese entities belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least NT$27,000,000,000, subject to applicable conditions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies at the group threshold prescribed under Taiwan rules, commonly NT$27,000,000,000 equivalent consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not maintain a standalone offshore-jurisdiction Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the regimes of certain low-tax jurisdictions. Economic substance remains material under Taiwan transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance principle, controlled foreign company rules, beneficial-owner analysis, permanent-establishment analysis, withholding-tax rules, and the tax authority's power to recharacterize transactions. A Taiwan DSP or SSP should evidence local personnel, decision-making, technology and data functions, risk control, customer and supplier management, relevant assets, premises, books, and actual performance of contracted services. A foreign related-party principal cannot be used as a paper allocation mechanism where functions and risks are performed in Taiwan.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Administrative assessment of omitted tax, penalties and interest; false or evasive conduct may constitute criminal tax evasion under the Taiwan Tax Collection Act, with imprisonment and fines within the statutory ranges, including potential imprisonment of up to five years and substantial criminal fines depending on the offense and facts.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face criminal prosecution under applicable Tax Collection Act provisions, civil liability to the company and creditors, tax collection against responsible parties where statutory conditions are met, and professional or management disqualification. Travel restrictions may arise from judicial or compulsory-enforcement proceedings; imprisonment depends on conviction and the applicable offense.
Shell company, sham service, or beneficial-ownership abuse

Statutory sanction: Tax authority recharacterization, denial of deductions, withholding-tax reassessment, transfer-pricing adjustment, penalties and interest. Fraudulent structures may also create criminal tax, document, company-law, or money-laundering exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may incur civil damages, restitution, criminal liability for knowingly participating in sham transactions, and enforcement restrictions. Corporate personality is not automatically disregarded, but personal exposure increases where the company is used for fraud, commingling, unlawful distributions, or deliberate creditor prejudice.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustment to arm's-length income or deductions, additional corporate income tax, withholding-tax reassessment, penalties, interest, documentation penalties, and possible denial of loss utilization. Missing or defective documentation materially increases audit risk.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face civil liability for losses caused by deliberate mispricing or breach of duties and criminal exposure where false records or tax fraud are established. Mere tax adjustment does not automatically create director imprisonment.
Permanent establishment and unreported Taiwan business activity

Statutory sanction: Assessment of Taiwan business income, corporate income tax, withholding tax, business tax, penalties, interest, and possible registration or filing enforcement. Contractual labels do not control the factual PE or source-income analysis.

Director personal liability: Company directors and responsible persons may face administrative and civil exposure for non-registration or intentional non-filing. Criminal consequences require additional statutory elements such as deliberate evasion, false documents, or obstruction.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed the unwithheld tax, collection surcharge, interest, and penalties and may lose the ability to recover the amount from the recipient. Incorrect treaty-rate application without supporting residence and beneficial-owner evidence is a recurring liability trigger.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face statutory responsible-person liability, civil claims, and criminal exposure for intentional falsification or evasion. Director imprisonment is fact-dependent and is not an automatic consequence of an inadvertent withholding error.
Business tax and invoicing noncompliance

Statutory sanction: Business tax assessments, invoice penalties, late-payment interest, suspension or other administrative measures, and possible criminal exposure for fraudulent invoices or deliberate concealment.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may incur civil and administrative liability and criminal liability for knowingly issuing or using false invoices. Personal imprisonment is reserved for conduct satisfying the relevant criminal provisions.
Money laundering and suspicious payment activity

Statutory sanction: Potential prosecution under Taiwan's Money Laundering Control Act and related criminal laws, asset confiscation or forfeiture, fines, account restrictions, and regulatory enforcement. Reporting and customer-due-diligence duties depend on the entity's regulated status and applicable sector rules.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly participate, facilitate, direct, or benefit may face criminal imprisonment, fines, confiscation, civil restitution, and judicial travel restrictions. A low-risk AML classification does not eliminate transaction-monitoring and sanctions-screening obligations.
Unlawful profit distribution and capital maintenance breach

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unlawful distributions, shareholder repayment obligations, corporate and tax penalties, possible withholding-tax reassessment, and liability under Taiwan Company Act provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors approving unlawful distributions may be jointly liable for restitution and company losses and may face disqualification or criminal exposure where the conduct involves fraud, falsified accounts, or other statutory offenses.

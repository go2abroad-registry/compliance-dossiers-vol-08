Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — the National Tax and Customs Authority. Administrative and electronic filing infrastructure: DIAN Sede Electrónica / MUISCA portal at www.dian.gov.co; e-invoicing and export declaration services via the DIAN Web Service (SOAP/API) and the VUCE foreign-trade single window.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity incorporating a Colombian S.A.S.: (i) execute a power of attorney in favor of a Colombian-resident agent or the S.A.S.'s legal representative; (ii) where the foreign party requires an NIT, apply for a provisional NIT through DIAN's pre-registration service; (iii) complete the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) registration electronically or through authorized DIAN channels, recording the foreign address, the ISIC 3250 activity, common-regime IVA responsible status, and exporter status; (iv) obtain a DIAN-accredited digital signature certificate (certificado de firma electrónica) for e-invoicing; and (v) after inscription in the Chamber of Commerce, register the S.A.S. itself in the RUT with its own NIT and activate the authorized electronic-invoicing software through the DIAN Sede Electrónica.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the S.A.S. is a separate and autonomous taxpayer with no pass-through option; the Colombian-resident S.A.S. is subject to worldwide income taxation; non-residents fall within Territorial_Scope, being taxed only on Colombian-source income; this S.A.S. is not in a Tax_Exempt_Zone unless it obtains later Zona Franca authorization).

Available election forms

Colombia has no check-the-box or pass-through election; an S.A.S. cannot be disregarded for income tax purposes. Statutory elections relevant to this dossier: (i) opt-in to the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) where projected gross income is below 100,000 UVT and structural eligibility criteria are met, replacing ordinary income tax with a comprehensive turnover-based tax and simplifying IVA mechanics; (ii) election to apply the enhanced 175% income-tax deduction for science, technology and innovation investments under ET Art. 158-1, subject to prior approval; (iii) election of the Colombian Holding Company (CHC) regime where the S.A.S. is restructured as an eligible holding vehicle, exempting qualifying foreign-source dividends and capital gains; (iv) application for a DIAN Advance Pricing Agreement (APA) on the transfer-pricing model; and (v) application for Zona Franca status, a government-licensed election delivering a 20% CIT rate and tariff/VAT benefits.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for legal persons. Every S.A.S. engaged in manufacturing and exporting medical devices is mandatorily registered in the RUT as a common-regime IVA responsible from the first taxable supply, irrespective of revenue. The simplified-regime turnover threshold for natural persons (COP 54,000 UVT, approximately COP 2,689 million at the 2025 UVT of COP 49,799) is not available to an S.A.S. Export zero-rating does not waive registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible movable goods are exempt from IVA and taxed at 0%, with credit on input VAT, under ET Art. 481 where: (i) the S.A.S. is a VAT-responsible common-regime exporter registered in the RUT with ISIC 3250 export activity; (ii) the goods physically leave Colombian customs territory through a definitive export operation accepted by DIAN; (iii) title passes abroad under the agreed INCOTERM; and (iv) the e-invoice is issued at 0% with the CUFE stamp and export annotation. Qualifying export services (e.g., calibration or engineering services for stent-processing equipment) are eligible where the beneficiary and the use of the service are abroad. Refund substantiation requires filing the bimestral VAT return showing the resulting saldo a favor and, upon DIAN documentary audit, proving export, shipment, and channeling of the export proceeds.

  • DIAN electronic Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) with acceptance status in MUISCA and the DEX number
  • Legalized commercial e-invoice (Factura Electrónica de Venta) issued at 0% with CUFE and QR code, bearing the export annotation
  • Packing list (Lista de Empaque) reconciled with the declared cargo
  • Original ocean Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / multimodal transport document evidencing definitive exit from Colombian customs territory
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas / certificación de canalización) issued by an authorized Mercado Cambiario intermediary
  • Certificate of Origin where preferential tariff treatment is claimed in the destination jurisdiction
  • INVIMA sanitary certificate, Good Manufacturing Practices (BPM/GMP) certificate for the Class III drug-eluting stent, and INVIMA pre-export authorization where required
  • Customs agency mandate or exporter registration certificate where the export is executed through a DIAN-licensed customs agent

Mandatory national electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta). The S.A.S. must transmit each invoice, export invoice, and supporting document as XML to DIAN's Sede Electrónica via the DIAN Web Services (SOAP/API with X.509 digital signature). Exporters and taxpayers designated by DIAN, including exporters of high-risk goods, must obtain prior authorization (validación previa) before issuing documents; other taxpayers obtain validation within 48 hours. Each invoice receives a unique fiscal stamp code (CUFE for invoices; CUDS for supporting documents) and a QR code evidencing authenticity. Non-cleared invoices confer no input VAT credit, deduction, or export zero-rating benefit. Goods exports must be filed in the MUISCA customs module as an electronic export declaration, integrated with the VUCE foreign-trade single window and INVIMA sanitary export certification.

DIAN may scrutinize export zero-rating claims within the 5-year official-assessment window, extended to 10 years where fraud or fictitious documents are involved. A refused claim or false refund triggers: (i) claw-back of the refunded VAT plus moratory interest computed from the refund date; (ii) the inaccuracy penalty (sanción por inexactitud) of 100%–200% of the greater amount between the over-claimed refund and the determined deficiency; (iii) an additional 20% fine under ET Art. 670 where the refund was improperly collected; (iv) suspension or deregistration from DIAN e-invoicing and exporter programs; and (v) criminal liability for smuggling, false documentation, and tax evasion under Law 1762/2015 and the tax-criminal provisions of the Estatuto Tributario, carrying imprisonment of up to 8 years plus asset forfeiture. Failure to channel export proceeds through the Colombian exchange market within 180 days also triggers administrative foreign-exchange penalties under the Banco de la República regime.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia applies a rules-based foreign-exchange regime without quantitative restrictions on current-account transactions. Import and export settlements, technical-service fees, royalties, and freight payments are freely convertible, provided they are channeled through an authorized foreign-exchange market intermediary (intermediario del mercado cambiario). Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment (FDI) into a Colombian S.A.S., are not subject to pre-approval or amount ceilings, but the inflow must be settled through the foreign-exchange market and formally registered with Banco de la República via Formulario No. 3 (Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales). Registration is the indispensable legal precondition for exercising the right to remit dividends, repatriate capital on dissolution, and access the official exchange market for future outflows. Unregistered FDI is treated as legally inexistent for exchange-control purposes and cannot be used to justify profit remittance through banking channels. Conversion of the inflow to Colombian pesos is performed at the representativa del mercado rate (TRM) published by Banco de la República; alternatively, the company may maintain foreign-currency deposit accounts. The only practical remittance hurdle for a Class III MedTech exporter is documentary: the bank must verify the tax settle-up of the dividend withholding obligation (retención por remesas) applicable under the reformed income-tax code (Ley 2277 de 2022) before effecting the wire to the non-resident shareholder.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of goods must channel (canalizar) the full FOB invoice value of the export through the foreign-exchange market no later than four months from the date of the customs export declaration (Declaración de Exportación). If the exporter formally grants foreign-buyer credit, the term may be extended to the documented maturity of such credit, provided the supplier-financing arrangement is registered under the exchange-control rules. Channeling is evidenced by submission of Formulario No. 1 (Declaración de Cambio por Exportaciones de Bienes) to the receiving intermediary. Colombia imposes no mandatory conversion ratio: the exporter may retain the proceeds in an authorized foreign-currency deposit account or convert into Colombian pesos at the TRM. Retention offshore beyond the statutory channeling term, under-invoicing, or settlement outside the exchange market triggers fines imposed by DIAN, potential loss of customs-benefit programs, and bank compliance termination.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) for banking supervision and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) for customs and exchange-control penalty enforcement. The normative base is the Régimen Cambiario under Resolution Externa No. 8 of 2000 of the Junta Directiva of Banco de la República, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Colombia does not license offshore digital EMI or neo-bank platforms as foreign-exchange market intermediaries; they cannot execute the mandatory canalización, cannot file Formulario Nos. 1, 3, 4 or 5 with Banco de la República, and cannot issue Colombian-peso clearing instruments for customs-validated trade payments. Offshore platforms (Wise, Mercury, Airwallex) may only serve as auxiliary collection accounts for invoicing to foreign distributors where the statutory FX channeling has already been completed through a Colombian intermediary or where the transaction is foreign-to-foreign outside the Colombian exchange regime. A locally licensed neo-bank may be added as a secondary operational account, but the primary treasury relationship must reside with a tier-1 supervised commercial bank capable of letter-of-credit handling, FX declarations, and regulatory reporting for Class III medical-device trade flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 from its banking institution in USD or EUR to the Colombian S.A.S. account at the tier-1 local bank, quoting the beneficiary NIT, the beneficiary account number, the local bank SWIFT BIC, and the internal reference 'Inversión Internacional – Formulario No. 3'. On receipt, the Colombian bank credits the funds to the capital escrow account only after the legal representative signs Formulario No. 3, which the bank transmits to Banco de la República together with the investment data required to update the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales. The transaction is converted to Colombian pesos at the TRM if the company elects conversion; otherwise proceeds remain in a registered foreign-currency account. Verification is evidenced by the bank's MT103 advice, the stamped Formulario No. 3, the Banco de la República registration certificate, and the company's equity ledger entry. The S.A.S. minimum-capital requirement is nil under Law 1258 of 2008, but the subscribed capital stated in the by-laws must remain coherent with the actual wired amounts to avoid artificial-capital red flags.Trade settlements flow through three validated clearing rails: (1) Commercial letters of credit governed by UCP 600, used for import of polymer tubing, laser components, and sterilization services, with the Colombian bank as issuing bank and drafts maturing 60–90 days from presentation; (2) SWIFT MT103 clean payment wires for service imports, freight, insurance, and ancillary operating disbursements, declared through Formulario No. 5 (Declaración de Cambio por Importaciones de Servicios) and, for goods imports, Formulario No. 4; and (3) export collections under inbound letters of credit or MT103 remittances from foreign distributors, declared through Formulario No. 1 within the four-month channeling window. All payments exceeding USD 10,000 must pass through the foreign-exchange market with underlying customs declarations and invoice consistency checks against the DIAN export/import filing. The bank's forex desk manages the full documentary cycle, including presentation of shipping documents, packing lists, bill of lading, INVIMA compliance certificates, and certificate of origin.Outright USD/COP currency forwards (forward a plazo) contracted with authorized Colombian foreign-exchange intermediaries, requiring a documented underlying import/export or FDI cash-flow exposure and strictly prohibiting speculative naked positioning.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) traded through the local bank's offshore desk, settled in Colombian pesos and supported by the same underlying trade-receivable or payable documentation, subject to Banco de la República's prudential reporting and capital-limits frameworks.Cross-currency swaps (coupon swaps) between USD and COP matched to the company's multi-year import and stent-distribution cash-flow schedule, permissible where both legs are linked to registered commercial transactions and the swap is executed through a supervised market-intermediary.

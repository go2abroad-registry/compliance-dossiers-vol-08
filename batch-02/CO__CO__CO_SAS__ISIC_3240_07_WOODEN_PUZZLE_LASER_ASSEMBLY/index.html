Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – www.dian.gov.co; electronic proceedings via MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) at muisca.dian.gov.co and the DIAN Sede Electrónica.

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian S.A.S.: (1) a Colombian lawyer empowered by the foreign shareholders prepares the private constitutive document (documento privado de constitución) – a notarial deed is not mandatory unless real estate is contributed; (2) register the S.A.S. with the Cámara de Comercio in the Registro Único Empresarial (RUES), which triggers assignment of a provisional NIT; (3) the Cámara de Comercio forwards the data to DIAN, and the legal representative completes the mandatory RUT (Registro Único Tributario) filing through DIAN's virtual portal, declaring the economic activity (ISIC 3240) and the 'responsable de IVA' and 'exportador' statuses; (4) DIAN validates the file and issues the definitive NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria). The process takes 3–10 business days. No minimum paid-in capital is required; a certified accountant is required, and a fiscal auditor (revisor fiscal) is mandatory only if the S.A.S. exceeds statutory asset/revenue thresholds. After the NIT is active, the company must register with an authorized e-invoicing software provider for the Facturación Electrónica system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: Worldwide residence-based taxation. A Colombian S.A.S. is a resident corporation under Article 12 E.T.; Colombian law does not recognize US-style check-the-box pass-through treatment for an S.A.S. Corporate income tax rate: 35% for taxable years 2023–2025, decreasing to 30% from 2026 (Law 2277/2022, Art. 240 E.T.). Dividends paid to non-residents are subject to withholding tax at the source.

Available election forms

Colombia has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) election to opt into the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE), a consolidated tax regime (income tax, VAT and other levies at progressive rates of 1.8%–14.5%) for eligible S.A.S. entities with gross income up to 3,500 UVT (≈ COP 164.7 million, UVT 2024 = COP 47,065) for services, or up to 100,000 UVT for industry/commerce activities; such election is made through the RUT and is generally irrevocable for a two-year biennial period; (ii) RUT classification elections regarding VAT status, with mandatory treatment as 'Responsable del Régimen Común' for any S.A.S. selling taxable goods (including exports); (iii) ordinary-income taxpayer elections on depreciation, inventory cost methods, and deduction subsystems within the general regime — not elective for pass-through purposes; (iv) anti-hybrid compliance: under Article 61-4 E.T., taxpayers involved in hybrid mismatch arrangements (e.g., disregarded payments or dual-resident structures) must disclose such structures in the annual informative filing to DIAN; Colombian tax law refuses to recognize foreign reverse-hybrid treatment for a Colombian S.A.S., which is always taxed at the entity level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold applies to a corporate S.A.S.: VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction regardless of amount. An S.A.S. is always a 'Responsable del Régimen Común' and must be enrolled in the RUT before beginning operations. For natural persons only, the simplified VAT regime may apply up to gross income of 3,500 UVT (COP 164,727,500 for 2024); this option is not available to S.A.S. entities. Export sales (including e-commerce exports of wooden puzzles) remain subject to mandatory VAT registration even where the headline rate is 0% on the export.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A sale of goods physically exported from Colombian customs territory (e.g., HS 9503 wooden 3D puzzles) is zero-rated (0% IVA) under Article 481 E.T. Conditions: (i) the S.A.S. is registered in the RUT as a VAT-responsible taxpayer and an 'exportador'; (ii) the export is covered by a valid automated export declaration (DEX) approved by DIAN through MUISCA, and the goods actually leave Colombian territory; (iii) the FEV with CUFE is issued for the export sale; (iv) the proceeds are received in foreign currency from abroad and repatriated through the Colombian foreign exchange market. Input VAT embedded in export-related purchases (raw wood, laser consumables, packaging, logistics) is recoverable; exporters may claim periodic refunds or offset the IVA balance via DIAN's refund/compensation procedure (Art. 481 and 855-860 E.T.).

  • DEX – Declaración de Exportación: DIAN-validated export declaration obtained through MUISCA, containing the autorización de salida and the export certificate for non-reimbursable operations if applicable
  • FEV – Factura Electrónica de Venta: mandatory electronic sales invoice (XML with CUFE and QR) referencing the DEX and, where possible, the Amazon marketplace order or Stripe transaction reference
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB): transport document evidencing shipment from Colombia and entry of the goods into the foreign destination country
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and certificate of origin (for any trade-agreement preference), and product safety/quality documentation (e.g., EN 71 or ASTM F963) where required by the importer
  • Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas: inward foreign-currency remittance certificate issued by the local intermediary bank in Colombia, evidencing that export proceeds were channeled through the Colombian foreign exchange market (Mercado Cambiario)

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing: every VAT-responsible taxpayer must issue the Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV) through DIAN-certified software (or DIAN's free 'Facturación VUE' tool) under Resolución DIAN 165/2023. The FEV must be transmitted electronically to DIAN via HTTPS/API web services (MUISCA) at the moment of sale (Validación Previa). DIAN validates the FEV against the RUT and assigns a unique CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) and a QR code; the electronic invoice XML must be delivered to the customer. For exports, the FEV must reference the export customs declaration (DEX) and the prior export authorization (autorización de salida) issued through MUISCA. The recipient must digitally confirm receipt/rejection of the FEV. Non-compliance triggers fines of up to 5% of the invoice value (Art. 652-1 E.T.), voiding of improperly issued invoices for tax deduction purposes, and temporary business closure of 3–10 days (Art. 657 E.T.).

DIAN and the customs authority audit zero-rating claims by cross-matching the DEX, FEV, customs manifests, logistics tracking, and FX remittance certificates. If the taxpayer fails to prove actual export or fails to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days, the 0% treatment is retroactively clawed back: the S.A.S. must self-assess and pay the 19% VAT on the invoiced export amount, plus daily default interest computed under Art. 635 E.T. (based on 1.5× the certified current bank lending rate). The inaccuracy penalty (sanción por inexactitud under Art. 648 E.T., as amended by Law 2277/2022) may reach 160% of the omitted IVA; further customs penalties for false declarations range from 50% to 200% of the customs value of the goods (Decreto 390/2016). Repeated or fraudulent schemes may result in suspension of the RUT exporter status, seizure of in-transit goods, criminal prosecution for aggravated tax evasion with imprisonment of 48–108 months (Código Penal, Arts. 402–403), and fines under Colombia's foreign-exchange regime of up to 200% of the non-repatriated amount (Superintendencia Financiera).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime. Current-account transactions (goods, services, interest, royalties) are fully convertible and freely channeled through the mercado cambiario. Capital-account transactions are liberalized but mandatory-channeling applies: all foreign-currency inflows and outflows must pass through authorized FX intermediaries and be declared to the Central Bank via the bank-issued Declaracion de Cambio. Foreign direct investment (FDI) in a manufacturing S.A.S. requires no prior governmental authorization but must be registered with the Banco de la Republica before dividends, capital repatriation, or liquidation proceeds can be legally remitted abroad. Profit-remittance hurdles are procedural rather than approval-based: the foreign investment must be timely registered, the local entity must evidence profits through filed financial statements, and dividend withholding tax must be settled with DIAN. Shareholder loans are legally permissible but since December 2022 are exposed to the Central Bank reserve requirement on external credit (encaje sobre endeudamiento externo), making registered equity contributions the structurally preferred treasury route.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from goods, including e-commerce parcel sales settled through Amazon Pay and Stripe, constitute current-account exchange obligations. The exporter must collect and channel 100% of the export value through the foreign-exchange market via an authorized intermediary within 6 months from the date of shipment (customs export declaration DEX). The Banco de la Republica may grant a single extension of up to 12 months in justified cases. There is no fixed percentage mandatory surrender to the Central Bank; the exporter may convert the foreign currency to COP at market rate or maintain the funds in a registered foreign-currency compensation account (cuenta de compensacion) in Colombia or abroad, but the funds must remain within the Colombian FX-reporting system and be declared to the intermediary within the statutory deadline. The supporting declaration is the Declaracion de Cambio por Exportaciones, accompanied by the DEX, invoices, platform settlement reports, and shipping/tracking evidence.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la Republica de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), Board of Directors; Foreign-exchange administration is implemented through authorized intermediaries (commercial banks and exchange houses) under supervision of the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC). Core legal framework: Ley 9 de 1991, Decreto 1735 de 1993, Resolucion Externa 8 de 2000 of the Banco de la Republica, Circular Reglamentaria Externa DCIN-83, and DIAN regulations for customs-linked exchange declarations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail and MEDIUM only as an auxiliary collection channel. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, etc.) are not licensed Colombian financial intermediaries: they cannot hold COP-nominated accounts, cannot receive funds in the Colombian banking system, and cannot discharge the mandatory FX channeling, export-proceeds reintegration, or Central Bank declaration obligations. Their lawful auxiliary function is limited to receiving USD trade payouts abroad; those funds must then be transferred into the S.A.S.'s account at an authorized Colombian bank within the statutory 6-month export window, with the underlying export documentation attached. Locally SFC-licensed electronic payment institutions (companias de financiamiento / payment wallets) may provide collection rails (PSE, card acquirers, digital wallets) but are not substitutes for a full corporate treasury relation, FX declaration capability, or trade-finance products.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Preferred rail: equity FDI via SWIFT MT103 wire from the non-resident investor to the S.A.S. operational account at an authorized Colombian bank, with the bank instructed to classify the receipt as 'Inversiones Internacionales - Aportes de Capital' in the mandatory Declaracion de Cambio. The intermediary transmits the exchange declaration to Banco de la Republica and the investor/entity files the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales through the bank within the statutory term, attaching the NIT, corporate resolutions, and certificate of existence. Completion of registration unlocks lawful dividend remittances and capital repatriation. Alternative rail: shareholder loans, which require a formal cross-border loan agreement, arm's-length interest, registration in the external-debt information system, and mandatory compliance with the in-force encaje (reserve requirement) on external credit; subordination documentation is advisable to prevent re-characterization as equity. For all routes, the wire must be tagged with the beneficiary NIT and the precise FX purpose code to avoid misposting as a service payment.Production-input payments: supplier prepayments and open-account remittances via SWIFT MT103 tied to the DIAN import declaration (Declaracion de Importacion) and the bank foreign-trade module; documentary collections (remesas documentarias) on shipment; irrevocable commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 for bulk MDF/wood procurement, with presentation conforming to invoice, packing list, bills of lading, and ISPM-15 phytosanitary certificate for raw wood. All foreign disbursements are filed as exchange declarations for imports ('Importacion de Bienes'). E-commerce inbound settlements: Stripe and Amazon Pay USD payouts land on the S.A.S. registered USD compensation account and are declared as export proceeds using the platform settlement report as documentary support. Domestic COP disbursements (wages, utilities, taxes, local wood purchases) run over ACH-CUD and PSE debit rails, keeping VAT and withholding flows visible to DIAN.Deliverable OTC COP/USD forward contracts with licensed Colombian banks, referenced to the TRM (Tasa Representativa del Mercado), used to lock export-receivable conversion rates for up to 12 months.COP/USD FX swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps linked to underlying trade or external-credit obligations, executed through authorized intermediaries.Standardized FX derivatives (forwards, futures, options) traded on the Mercado de Derivados Estandarizados (MDE) operated under Banco de la Republica rules and cleared by the Camara de Riesgo Central de Contraparte (CRCC).OTC currency options purchased from Colombian banks for downside protection on USD e-commerce payouts, with premia paid through the local FX market.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) structures: permissible only for genuine hedging purposes when arranged through an authorized onshore bank or its offshore branch and reflected in exchange declarations; unregistered offshore NDF positions are not a permitted treasury instrument for a Colombian resident S.A.S.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail and MEDIUM only as an auxiliary collection channel. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, etc.) are not licensed Colombian financial intermediaries: they cannot hold COP-nominated accounts, cannot receive funds in the Colombian banking system, and cannot discharge the mandatory FX channeling, export-proceeds reintegration, or Central Bank declaration obligations. Their lawful auxiliary function is limited to receiving USD trade payouts abroad; those funds must then be transferred into the S.A.S.'s account at an authorized Colombian bank within the statutory 6-month export window, with the underlying export documentation attached. Locally SFC-licensed electronic payment institutions (companias de financiamiento / payment wallets) may provide collection rails (PSE, card acquirers, digital wallets) but are not substitutes for a full corporate treasury relation, FX declaration capability, or trade-finance products.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Taiwan company limited by shares under the Company Act (minimum capital requirement was abolished in 2009). The foreign investor must, however, remit and deposit the capital amount specified in the Investment Commission approval letter, and the bank must issue a Capital Deposit Certificate as capital verification. No blocked escrow account is required at ordinary incorporation stage. If the company later applies for an FSC Electronic Payment Institution License, the FSC minimum paid-up capital rule (approximately NT$100,000,000 for a comprehensive electronic payment institution, or such lower tier as set for limited functions) must be met and maintained in a designated Taiwanese bank account.

Funding & unlock path: Office premises require a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate from the Municipal Fire Department under the Fire Services Act, a Building Occupancy and Structural Safe Use Certificate from the Municipal Public Works Office, and a General Occupational Safety registration under the Occupational Safety and Health Act. No civil defense permit for explosive, chemical, or hazardous industrial storage is required for this virtual card issuing and spend management business.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance (Taiwan), operating through the National Taxation Bureaus in the Taipei, Kaohsiung, Northern, Central, and Southern districts; electronic filing and registration portal: Taiwan Tax Internet Filing and Payment Service (tax.nat.gov.tw) and the E-Invoice Turnkey Network maintained by the Ministry of Finance Financial Information Service Company.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned limited company (Taiwan WFOE) or the Taiwan branch of a foreign enterprise must first complete company or branch incorporation with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA). After incorporation, the entity must file Business Tax Registration with the local National Taxation Bureau before commencing business. The Bureau issues a Taxpayer Identification Code known as the 8-digit Unified Business Number (UBIN), which serves as the TIN for business tax, corporate income tax, customs, and Uniform Invoice purposes. For a non-resident entity without a fixed place of business in Taiwan, a qualified fiscal agent or tax representative must be appointed under power of attorney to apply for a non-resident tax ID and handle tax declarations. Standard filings include the registration application, MOEA incorporation certificate, articles of incorporation, investment approval or foreign investment permit, identity documents of shareholders and directors, and proof of a Taiwan registered office. There is no minimum capital requirement, but the virtual card issuing business requires a special license from the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) before tax registration because the activity is classified as a high-risk special license in the payment cards sector.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or a hybrid mismatch election. Business and corporate income tax classification follows the Taiwan Company Act and the Income Tax Act, with no elective pass-through treatment for a limited company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

New Taiwan Dollars (TWD): mandatory registration is required if average monthly turnover exceeds NT$80,000 from the sale of goods or NT$40,000 from services. A licensed virtual card issuing entity is registrable from day one because it holds a statutory financial services license and issues Uniform Invoices, even if below the general small-business threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, a 0% VAT rate applies to goods exported from Taiwan if the goods are actually cleared through Customs and leave the territory, and to services rendered to overseas clients where the service is strictly used outside Taiwan and the remuneration for the service is received from abroad. The exporter must maintain a complete audit trail demonstrating physical export, supply of qualifying services, and receipt of foreign exchange.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) certified by Taiwan Customs
  • Ocean or Air Bill of Lading showing Taiwan as the port of export and a foreign consignee
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Sales contract or service agreement with the overseas counterparty
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued foreign exchange receipt voucher showing the foreign payment
  • Financial institution custodian records proving export payment collection, if the export is settled through a factoring or card network

All VAT-registered businesses must issue Uniform Invoices. Businesses meeting the electronic invoicing criteria are required to use the E-Invoice (Electronic Uniform Invoice) system and upload invoice data through the Ministry of Finance Turnkey Network using the XML/API protocol within 48 hours of issuance. The system assigns an encrypted invoice number and a random check code to each B2C transaction, enabling instant online validation and anti-fraud risk scoring. B2B E-Invoices are exchanged through the same turnkey network without a physical fiscal stamp, but the near-real-time transmission and response message from the Ministry of Finance integration platform constitute the electronic fiscal clearance.

Export zero-rating claims are audited against customs exit records, bank foreign exchange receipts, and the financial institution's electronic transaction logs. If the exporter fails to repatriate foreign currency proceeds within the statutory 180-day period or cannot substantiate the customs declaration and banking records, the 0% rate is rescinded. The underpaid 5% business tax is retrospectively assessed together with late payment interest and a delinquency penalty. Under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act, fraudulent or intentionally deceptive zero-rating can attract a penalty of up to five times the evaded tax and criminal liability under the Tax Collection Act for the responsible officers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi is partially convertible. Current account transactions, including cross-border service fees related to virtual card programme management, licence fees to Mastercard or Visa, and merchant payout fees, are generally convertible after the designated bank performs documentary genuineness checks and reports through SAFE's foreign exchange monitoring system. Capital account items remain heavily regulated. Inbound equity capital from the Taiwan shareholder requires FDI registration with the relevant commerce authority and SAFE, a dedicated foreign-currency capital account, and post-injection conversion subject to the negative list. Outbound dividend remittances require audited financial statements, completed corporate income tax and withholding tax settlements, and SAFE-compliant bank verification; they are not subject to routine approval but will be suspended if the bank identifies irregular cross-strait flows or unresolved tax filings. Export receipts and service fees must be repatriated to China, but enterprises may retain foreign currency in designated operating accounts or sell it to a bank at the market rate; no compulsory conversion ratio exists. Profit remittance hurdles include demonstrating that the RMB profit is from genuine business, documenting the board resolution, and matching the remittance with prior tax payment records. Taiwan-related remittances are treated as cross-border flows requiring the same underlying documentation and are monitored through cross-strait financial channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated to a PRC-designated foreign-exchange account through an authorised bank and reconciled with customs export declarations via SAFE's trade monitoring system within 180 days of the customs declaration date; delays require a documented SAFE deferral record. The mandatory conversion ratio is nil: China has abolished forced surrender, so the WFOE may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency or convert any portion into RMB at prevailing market rates. For service-trade income, including virtual card fees, the same 180-day receipt verification logic applies using contracts, invoices, and bank receipts; the A/B/C classification of the entity determines the pre-verification intensity.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, acting under the authority of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary banking relationship. A Chinese WFOE engaged in PBOC-supervised virtual card issuing must safeguard customer funds in licensed domestic banking institutions; offshore digital EMIs generally lack a PBOC payment institution license and cannot offer RMB accounts, CNAPS/CIPS participation, or clearing via domestic card networks. They cannot legally replace the domestic bank for capital escrow, tax withholding, or SAFE submission. Offshore EMIs may only be used as ancillary international payment gateways for non-RMB traffic under a licensed sponsor and must sweep balances through the domestic Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the Taiwan parent must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's SAFE-registered foreign-exchange capital account at a designated bank. The remitter must reference the FDI registration certificate and use the SAFE purpose code for inward direct investment equity capital. After the funds credit, the bank verifies the entry against the FDI foreign-exchange registration and allows conversion to RMB only for use within the negative-list permitted scope. A licensed Chinese certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report before the bank releases funds for operational disbursements; any subsequent capital increase must follow the same SAFE registration and capital-verification cycle.Outbound supplier settlements and merchant payouts should be executed via SWIFT MT103 using purpose codes that match the underlying service-trade or goods-trade declaration; for larger physical-goods purchases, the WFOE may open Commercial Letters of Credit or use Documentary Collections through the same tier-1 bank to ensure customs and trade documents remain aligned with SAFE verification. Virtual-card spend is cleared by Mastercard or Visa networks; treasury must fund a settlement account at the sponsor bank and reconcile interchange, scheme fees, chargebacks, and merchant payouts. For RMB-denominated cross-border payments, use CIPS with an MT103-equivalent remittance message; TWD and non-RMB currencies are routed via SWIFT to the bank's correspondent network.Onshore deliverable RMB forward contracts (e.g., USD/CNY, and TWD/CNY where quoted) offered by a SAFE-licensed designated bank, requiring underlying trade or service exposure and documentary verification.RMB/USD or RMB/TWD foreign-exchange swaps for cash-flow tenor matching where a genuine current-account exposure exists.Cross-currency swaps for long-term foreign-currency liabilities or settlement infrastructure investments, subject to SAFE registration and macroprudential capital-flow rules.Plain-vanilla foreign-exchange options and simple risk-reversal combinations permitted for hedging actual foreign-currency receipts or payables, not for speculative trading.Non-deliverable forwards are not a permissible onshore instrument for the WFOE; NDFs may only be considered by an independent offshore treasury affiliate under its own local licensing, and such positions must not be booked back into the mainland WFOE.

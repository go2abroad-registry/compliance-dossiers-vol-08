Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce) through the Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES), with regulatory oversight by the Superintendencia de Sociedades.

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 (Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada), Articles 5, 9, 10 and 21; Código de Comercio (Decreto 410 de 1971), Articles 110, 137 and 164; Decreto 1074 de 2015 (RUES implementing regulation).

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución S.A.S. (private incorporation instrument) with notarially authenticated signatures, registered in the Mercantile Registry; a public deed (Escritura Pública) is mandatory only when real estate or rights in rem are contributed to the corporate capital.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 73 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for an S.A.S.; the subscribed capital may be fixed from one Colombian peso (Ley 1258 de 2008, Art. 5). Payment of each share may be made in cash, in kind, or by valuation, and unpaid instalments may not exceed two years from incorporation (Art. 10). Cash contributions are deposited in the company's Colombian corporate bank account; no escrow account is legally required for standard cash subscriptions.

Funding & unlock path: Certificado de Bomberos (technical fire-safety certificate) for the industrial facility; structural and seismic load certification for freeze-dryer platforms under NSR-10; enrollment of the plant and workers with an ARL (occupational risk insurer); and implementation of the Programa de Seguridad y Salud en el Trabajo (SG-SST) in accordance with Resolución 0312 de 2019 and Decreto 1072 de 2015.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for an S.A.S.; the subscribed capital may be fixed from one Colombian peso (Ley 1258 de 2008, Art. 5). Payment of each share may be made in cash, in kind, or by valuation, and unpaid instalments may not exceed two years from incorporation (Art. 10). Cash contributions are deposited in the company's Colombian corporate bank account; no escrow account is legally required for standard cash subscriptions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – central portal: www.dian.gov.co, transactional portal MUISCA and Sede Electrónica.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through DIAN. The procedure includes: (1) appointing a legal representative or agent in Colombia; (2) executing and apostilled power of attorney/corporate resolutions of the foreign entity; (3) filing the application for pre-registration with DIAN through the Sede Electrónica; (4) providing the foreign certificate of incorporation, passports or foreign tax IDs of directors, and proof of the designated Colombian address; (5) obtaining the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) which evidences the NIT. For a local SAS, the NIT is issued automatically upon registration with the local Chamber of Commerce; the entity must subsequently update its RUT and confirm VAT/income tax responsibilities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through DIAN. The procedure includes: (1) appointing a legal representative or agent in Colombia; (2) executing and apostilled power of attorney/corporate resolutions of the foreign entity; (3) filing the application for pre-registration with DIAN through the Sede Electrónica; (4) providing the foreign certificate of incorporation, passports or foreign tax IDs of directors, and proof of the designated Colombian address; (5) obtaining the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) which evidences the NIT. For a local SAS, the NIT is issued automatically upon registration with the local Chamber of Commerce; the entity must subsequently update its RUT and confirm VAT/income tax responsibilities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not have a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections relevant to a manufacturing/exporting SAS include: (i) the 'Régimen Simple de Tributación' (SIMPLE) for eligible small taxpayers with gross income up to 80,000 UVT; (ii) for export-oriented commercial entities, registration as a 'Sociedad de Comercialización Internacional' (CCI) under Law 67/1979; (iii) application for 'Zona Franca' user status for industrial users, granting reduced income tax rates and special export-regime treatment; (iv) option to consolidate within an economic group for transfer-pricing documentation only; (v) election to adjust inflation-basis tax items. There are no corporate pass-through elections for an SAS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for corporations or for any entity carrying out taxable supplies in Colombia; VAT registration is mandatory regardless of sales volume. For natural persons eligible for the 'régimen simplificado' only, the FY2024 simplified-regime threshold is 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 164 million, indexed annually). An SAS is always required to register under the 'régimen común' of IVA.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Colombia applies a 'excluido/exento' VAT treatment to exports of tangible goods. For freeze-dried pet raw meat, the departure of the goods from Colombian customs territory under a valid export declaration qualifies as an export excluded from VAT, meaning the sale attracts 0% VAT and the exporter may recover input VAT refunds. Eligibility requirements: (1) the exporter must be a VAT-responsible entity recorded in the RUT as an 'Exportador'; (2) the goods must physically leave Colombia under a DEX/AWB based on an actual sale to a foreign buyer; (3) title and risk must pass outside Colombia; (4) an export invoice is issued; (5) foreign exchange receipts must be registered with the 'Banco de la República' through an 'intermediario del mercado cambiario'; and (6) the exporter must file Form 250/350 or corresponding DIAN application for 'devolución y/o compensación de saldos a favor'.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX - Declaración de Exportación, Formulario 640)
  • Export Invoice (Factura de Exportación) electronically transmitted to DIAN
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (transport document evidencing departure)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Colombian intermediary (certificado de liquidación de divisas / comprobante de pago al exterior)
  • Certificate of Origin and/or sanitary/phytosanitary documentation as required for pet food exports

All VAT-responsible taxpayers must issue electronic invoices ('factura electrónica de venta') through DIAN-authorized revocation software and submit them in real time to DIAN for 'validación previa'. The invoice must contain a valid CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) and be digitally stamped for graphical representation. Invoices for export operations are likewise transmitted electronically and must be reconciled with the customs export declaration. Non-compliance blocks deduction of input VAT and may trigger invoice rejection and administrative sanctions.

DIAN carries out integrated audits that cross-match the DEX, transport document, electronic invoice and FX remittance record. Missing, false or mismatched documentation results in denial or retrospective claw-back of VAT refunds (saldos a favor) with moratory interest and sanctions. Penalties can reach up to 200% of the tax evaded/devolved and criminal tax liability is triggered under Law 1777/2016 (falsedad, fraude procesal and evasion offences). The taxpayer must retain full supporting records for at least ten (10) years. In practice, DIAN prioritizes refund claims involving processed food products and requires robust traceability of raw material VAT credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is convertible inside the Colombian Mercado Cambiario (authorized FX market), but is not a freely convertible offshore currency. Current account payments and collections are broadly unrestricted and must be channeled through authorized FX intermediaries. Capital account transactions, including direct foreign investment, equity contributions, and loan inflows/outflows, are subject to mandatory registration with the Banco de la República and the filing of Foreign Investment Declaration forms. Profit remittance to non-residents is allowed after the annual financial statements are approved, dividends are validly declared, the foreign investment remains registered in the name of the direct investor, and the remittance is made through the foreign exchange market. There is no general quantitative convertibility cap, but non-compliance with channeling and registration requirements creates procedural barriers and punitive sanctions; economic substance and tax compliance must also be evidenced.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All proceeds from Colombian goods exports must be channeled through the Mercado Cambiario. In general, the exporter must bring export proceeds into Colombia through an authorized local bank, bank branch, or exchange house and either sell the foreign currency in the interbank market or credit it to a Foreign Currency Deposit Account maintained with a Colombian financial institution. The statutory surrender window is up to six (6) months from the date of the bill of lading or dispatch document, extendable only upon a reasoned and accepted request to the Banco de la República. One hundred percent (100%) of the net export invoice value is subject to the channeling obligation, except for contracted amounts needed for export-related expenses that are expressly covered by applicable exchange regulations.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Banco Central) in conjunction with the Comité de Política Cambiaria and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. The exchange control regime is established under Law 9 of 1991, Extreme Decree 1068 of 2015, and the regulations contained in the Banco de la República's Foreign Exchange Manual.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for offshore digital EMI / e-money institutions. Colombia requires financial institutions offering deposit, e-money, or FX services to be duly authorized by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. Offshore EMIs are not permitted to open local accounts or settle COP-denominated transactions without a local licensed entity or bank partner. Platform-pay and virtual treasury solutions are viable only when configured as cash-management products on top of an account at a regulated local Colombian bank or through a licensed payment service company (Sociedad de Servicios Financieros).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign direct investment into the Colombian S.A.S. must be wired via SWIFT MT103 from a shareholder-owned and fully documented source account. The wire must reference the local bank account number (NIT bank account in COP or a foreign currency deposit account) and should identify the ultimate investor and the purpose of 'foreign equity participation in a Colombian company'. Before or immediately after receipt, the investment must be registered in the Banco de la República's Foreign Investment Register. The standard registration instrument is the Foreign Investment Declaration form ('Formulario No. 3' or the applicable electronic registration channel), and if a foreign-exchange settlement to COP occurs, the transaction must pass through an authorized Colombian bank. The governance protocol should include a verified wiring matrix, strict SWIFT reference consistency, and a compliance pre-clearance checklist with the bank to avoid sweep-back or forced repatriation.For trade disbursements to overseas raw-material suppliers and freeze-drying technology vendors, the primary clearing rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 covered payments to the supplier's bank account, supported by the underlying import contract, invoice, customs declaration, and Colombian import registration; (b) commercial documentary letters of credit (L/C) issued through the local Colombian bank, with the payment routed on a pay-at-sight or usance basis against compliant shipping and sanitary documentation; and (c) direct collection drawers under documentary collections, settled via SWIFT MT202/MT103 once transport and health certificates are accepted. COP-denominated domestic disbursements settle via local clearing mechanisms in the Colombian electronic payment system (CUD/CENIT) only for pure domestic operations; all cross-border settlement should remain in USD, EUR, or COP settlement through the authorized FX market.USD/COP OTC deliverable forward contracts entered into with a Colombian financial institution authorized to operate in the Mercado Cambiario, used for genuine commercial or balance-sheet exposureUSD/COP non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) arranged through international counterparties when coupled with onshore back-to-back cover and underlying export/import trade papersCross-currency swaps (USD/COP) for hedging foreign-currency debt or long-term shareholder-subordinated debt from non-resident shareholdersFX options (vanilla USD/COP calls and puts) for protecting export revenues or import costs where the bank confirms authorization and non-speculative underlying exposurePlain vanilla foreign-exchange spot transactions for converting operational export proceeds and settling trade invoices through the authorized FX market

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: 50,000 UVT of related-party transactions in the tax year (UVT 2025 = COP 49,799; threshold = COP 2,489,950,000), in addition to the taxpayer classification threshold based on gross income and equity fixed by the DIAN. Entities falling below the threshold are still required to file an informative TP return if any cross-border related-party transaction exists.

Economic substance scope: Colombia has no United Arab Emirates-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Nevertheless, DIAN may apply the anti-abuse rules (Article 869 ET) and the doctrine of functional substance to disregard transactions lacking commercial purpose. A freeze-dried pet food manufacturer must demonstrate physical plant, personnel, purchase/sale decisions, inventory risk and management control in Colombia; mere invoicing or contractual relocation of profit to a foreign related party will be recharacterised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent omission of income

Statutory sanction: DIAN inaccuracy penalty equal to 100% of the detected tax shortage, default interest, and criminal penalties of 4 to 9 years' imprisonment and fines under Colombian Criminal Code Article 434A (tax fraud).

Director personal liability: The legal representative and finance director who sign false declarations are jointly liable for the tax debt; civil and criminal courts may pierce the veil and order restitution; a travel ban may be issued while tax debt remains unpaid.
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: DIAN administrative transfer-pricing adjustment, 35% tax on the recharacterised profit, compensatory default interest, and TP documentation penalties generally ranging from 1% to 3% of the value of cross-border related-party transactions (UVT-capped).

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign incomplete TP documentation may be held solidarily liable for the entity's TP penalties if the company is insolvent or liquidated; knowingly false TP evidence triggers criminal fraud liability.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum legal capital for S.A.S.; at least one share must be subscribed and paid in cash, goods, or services. Cash payments require at least 20% of subscribed capital to be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within one year. No escrow account requirement.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the local Fire Department (Bomberos), structural load clearance from a certified engineer/firm for warehouse shelving and storage areas, and occupational health clearance from the ARL (Administradora de Riesgos Laborales) covering workplace safety, handling of hazardous chemicals, manual-load limits, and ventilation requirements.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: www.dian.gov.co; transactional services via the MUISCA platform; foreign-trade procedures via the VUCE single window (vuce.gov.co).

E-government filing portal

Procedure for obtaining the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and RUT (Registro Único Tributario): (1) The Colombian S.A.S. is incorporated by notarized deed or private document registered in the Cámara de Comercio, and the NIT is assigned automatically upon Chamber registration; (2) The S.A.S. must complete or update its RUT electronically through DIAN's website, activating status as 'Responsable de IVA – Régimen Común' and any applicable tax classification (e.g., great taxpayer) and foreign-trade profile; (3) A non-resident foreign entity without a Colombian permanent establishment must appoint a local attorney-in-fact with a notarized and apostilled power of attorney and submit the RUT form plus apostilled certificate of incorporation to DIAN to obtain a NIT for Colombian-source income and withholding purposes; (4) The foreign shareholder(s) of the S.A.S. must be registered in the company's RUT and in the shareholding annex for dividend and transfer-pricing reporting; (5) Issuance of a DIAN digital fiscal certificate (certificado digital fiscal) and activation of electronic-invoice authorization in MUISCA complete the operational tax onboarding.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Colombian S.A.S. is a separate tax-resident corporation with no pass-through treatment; it is subject to national corporate income tax on worldwide income. Territorial-scope rules apply to non-residents: they are taxed only on Colombian-source income. Free-zone (Zona Franca) status is optional and is not a default classification.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Colombian law. Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) for qualifying small taxpayers with gross revenues ≤ COP 3.5 billion, subject to activity and shareholding exclusions; (ii) optional consolidated corporate income tax filing for business groups under common economic control (ET Article 10); (iii) OECD-aligned CFC, permanent-establishment, and transfer-pricing rules which apply mandatorily rather than by election; (iv) a conditional 0% VAT treatment for qualifying export of services under ET Article 481, formalized through the RUT and export-of-services declaration rather than a discretionary election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for corporate entities: an S.A.S. is automatically a VAT-responsible 'régimen común' taxpayer from incorporation and must register with DIAN in the RUT and charge IVA on taxable supplies at the applicable rate. The 3,500 UVT threshold (approximately COP 164.7 million for tax year 2024) applies only to natural persons eligible for 'no responsable de IVA' status; corporate entities cannot use this threshold to avoid VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical exports of goods, including exported API batches, are taxed at 0% IVA under ET Articles 481-482 provided: (i) the goods physically leave Colombian customs territory and the S.A.S. is the exporter of record; (ii) an accepted export declaration (DEX) is filed through customs; (iii) the commercial invoice is issued as an export invoice with a 0% IVA statement; and (iv) foreign-currency proceeds are channeled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market and repatriated within the statutory term. Qualifying services to non-residents with effect or use abroad (e.g., software development, technical support, and certain digital services) may also qualify as export of services under ET Article 481 upon filing the export-of-services declaration in the RUT and satisfying the invoicing and FX-repatriation conditions.

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX) duly accepted by DIAN customs (transmitted through the VUCE platform)
  • Bill of Lading (maritime) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing exportation from Colombian customs territory
  • Export commercial invoice annotated with the 0% IVA export statement
  • Certificado de Liquidación de Divisas (foreign-exchange settlement certificate) issued by the local authorized intermediary bank
  • INVIMA sanitary authorization, export certificate, or free-sale certificate for the pharmaceutical API consignment
  • Apostilled certificate of origin or other export-assist registry documents, when applicable

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica de Venta – FEV) on the DIAN MUISCA platform. All invoices must be issued in XML and transmitted through DIAN web services (API) with pre-validation (validación previa) for the vast majority of taxpayers, functioning as fiscal clearance. Each invoice embeds a unique digital validation code (CUFE – Código Único de Facturación Electrónica; CUDE for credit/debit notes) that acts as a digital stamp. Taxpayers must use DIAN-certified billing software or DIAN's free web tool and deliver a PDF/A-3 or XML copy to the buyer. Non-transmitted or non-validated invoices are legally invalid and trigger fines under ET Article 652-14.

If any export condition is missing or not substantiated, DIAN may reclassify the transaction as a domestic taxable sale and assess 19% IVA retroactively, plus moratory interest at the legal rate and an omission/inaccuracy penalty of up to 200% of the tax difference under ET Article 647. Invalid or untransmitted electronic invoices carry additional fines under ET Article 652-14. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day FX deadline breaches the Colombian foreign-exchange regime and can trigger fines by DIAN and Banco de la República and loss of zero-rating. DIAN has a 5-year statute of limitations from the tax-return due date (extended up to 10 years for non-filing) to claw back improperly claimed credits or zero-rated benefits. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims or false documentation can also lead to criminal tax fraud charges under Law 599/2000 as amended by Law 1943/2018, carrying imprisonment of 48 to 108 months, in addition to customs crimes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian Peso (COP) operates under a free-floating managed exchange-rate regime with full current-account convertibility for bona fide trade in goods and services, subject to documentary declaration and channeling requirements. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment (FDI), equity contributions, capital repatriation, and profit remittances, are free but must be registered with the Banco de la República through an Authorized Foreign Exchange Intermediary before or within the applicable registration window. Profit remittances are permitted once Colombian corporate annual financial statements have been approved and taxes have been paid, provided that the foreign investor has registered the investment in the BCRA investment registry (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales) and proves the source of the dividend distribution with audited IFRS financial statements, board minutes, and the corresponding cambio declaration. Practical hurdles include mandatory advanced tax compliance, the absence of automatic tax withholding relief on gross dividend remittances (ordinary dividends remain subject to source tax at applicable rates), documentary requirements for the quarterly tax return, and the need to obtain no tax pending certificate(s) from DIAN and local tax authorities before high-value remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated and channeled through the Colombian foreign exchange market within six (6) months from the date of shipment or, where collection occurs later, from the contractual maturity date, as modified by applicable Banco de la República provisions. The exporter is required to file the Declaración de Cambio (Formulario No. 4 or corresponding electronic form) through an Authorized Foreign Exchange Intermediary, and 100% of the export proceeds must be surrendered or 'canalized' into the Colombian exchange market; these proceeds may be credited in foreign currency to a compensatory or Colombian foreign-currency account with an authorized intermediary, or converted into COP, at the exporter's election, but must be reported to the Banco de la República. Failure to channel within the statutory timeline triggers sanctions under the Estatuto Cambiario, including fines, loss of the right to maintain foreign-currency assets abroad, and potential exchange-control penalties.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting as the monetary and exchange authority together with the Junta Directiva del Banco de la República and the Comisión de Cambios Internacionales, pursuant to Law 9 of 1991, the Estatuto Cambiario, and External Resolution No. 1 of 2018, as amended. Foreign exchange operations are channelled exclusively through Authorized Foreign Exchange Intermediaries (Bancos Comerciales Autorizados), cargo exchange houses, and other entities expressly authorized by the Banco de la República.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium feasibility. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and payment fintech companies are not authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries under Colombian exchange-control regulations and are generally incompetent to receive declared FX transactions or to perform local clearing through CUD/CB Colombia clearing systems. They may be used strictly as non-banking collection-origination channels for e-commerce receipts, but all settlement must flow into an account held with a Banco de la República-authorised commercial bank. Legal constraints include the prohibition on effecting 'canalización' via unlicensed non-resident third parties, compliance with SFC and UIAF anti-money-laundering circulars, data residency concerns, and the Colombian bank perception of higher third-party-payment risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections and shareholder loans must be routed exclusively through an Authorized Foreign Exchange Intermediary in compliance with the Colombian foreign-exchange regime. The foreign investor must complete, before or upon remittance, the online Formulario de Registro de Inversión Internacional (FDI registration) through the Banco de la República, using the applicable purpose code for foreign direct investment or 'Suplementaria de aportes a sociedades extranjeras' as appropriate. The wire transfer must be sent by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor's bank to the Colombian bank's nostro account with explicit reference to the beneficiary NIT, the Company's Chamber of Commerce number, and the FDI registration code. Supporting documents must include the notarized and apostilled capital increase/share subscription minutes, if any; the NIT certificate; bank information; and the 'Declaración de Cambio' prepared and signed by the legal representative. The Colombian bank will verify the legality of the remittance before crediting the restricted foreign-capital account.Outbound settlements for imported APIs and trade-related disbursements must be executed through (i) irrevocable commercial letters of credit (LC) opened with the Colombian bank in favor of the overseas API manufacturer; (ii) SWIFT MT103/MT202 wires for open-account balances, supplier credit repayments, and LC reimbursements; (iii) documentary collections governed by URC 522; or (iv) Bank Payment Obligations (BPO) under the URBPO rules for automated trade finance. Each disbursement requires a Declaración de Cambio (Formulario No. 2 for imports of goods) with the corresponding import declaration and commercial invoice, and must be supported by the invoice, transport document, and customs entry (Declaración de Importación) to satisfy DIAN and Banco de la República validation. Payments to non-resident suppliers in USD are made through local bank clearing in Colombia using the channeling system; cross-border payments must use the Colombian bank's SWIFT network. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Medium feasibility. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and payment fintech companies are not authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries under Colombian exchange-control regulations and are generally incompetent to receive declared FX transactions or to perform local clearing through CUD/CB Colombia clearing systems. They may be used strictly as non-banking collection-origination channels for e-commerce receipts, but all settlement must flow into an account held with a Banco de la República-authorised commercial bank. Legal constraints include the prohibition on effecting 'canalización' via unlicensed non-resident third parties, compliance with SFC and UIAF anti-money-laundering circulars, data residency concerns, and the Colombian bank perception of higher third-party-payment risk.

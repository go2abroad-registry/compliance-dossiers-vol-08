Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – National Tax and Customs Directorate; main portal: www.dian.gov.co; electronic tax operations via the MUISCA platform (muisca.dian.gov.co).

E-government filing portal

Any foreign entity selling goods or services into Colombia, or operating through a Colombian S.A.S., must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through DIAN. For a locally incorporated S.A.S., registration is initiated before the Chamber of Commerce, resulting in a provisional NIT; the legal representative must then complete the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) online through MUISCA/DIAN, using an approved electronic signature, and update the classification for income, VAT and withholding duties. A non-resident foreign seller without local domicile is required to appoint a tax representative or attorney-in-fact in Colombia and present the foreign incorporation certificate, power of attorney and related constitutive documents to DIAN in order to obtain the NIT/RUT for VAT, export and digital-services filings. No minimum capital is required for the S.A.S.; all taxpayers must maintain an up-to-date RUT showing the applicable economic activity codes (ISIC 4791 / NAICS 458110).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombian S.A.S. companies are separate income taxpayers and cannot elect pass-through status. No 'check-the-box' election exists under Colombian law. Available elections are limited to: (i) optional special economic group taxation (consolidated corporate income tax basis) for entities under common control and economic unit with prior DIAN authorization; (ii) election to adjust equity and inflationary effects under the applicable tax rules; (iii) advance pricing agreements with DIAN for transfer pricing; and (iv) special VAT regime elections available to non-resident digital service providers allowing a simplified foreign-supplier registration procedure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold applies to VAT-liable taxpayers: registration is mandatory whenever taxable supplies are made in Colombia or to Colombian residents, including foreign digital/e-commerce sellers. The simplified regime for individuals applies only below 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 164,727,500 for 2024) and is not available to S.A.S. companies or non-resident sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible movable goods qualify for VAT-exempt (zero-rated) treatment, with entitlement to input-VAT credit or refund, if a valid export customs declaration is filed, physical export actually occurs, and the foreign-currency payment is received through the Colombian foreign exchange market. Export services qualify only where they: are performed in Colombia but exclusively used abroad; are contracted by a non-resident person or by a Colombian entity acting as intermediary for foreign contracting; and are paid from outside Colombia. A detailed affidavit and the underlying contract evidencing the restricted-use condition are required for service exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX – Declaración de Exportación) validated in MUISCA/DIAN
  • Commercial invoice in accordance with Decree 1165/2019
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch from Colombia
  • Packing list
  • Export authorization or registration where the reason to export is expressly declared
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank confirmation) proving receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency

All VAT-registered persons, including S.A.S. exporters and non-resident digital merchants, must issue electronic invoices (factura electrónica de venta) in real time through DIAN-authorized software providers or DIAN's free application; each invoice requires digital authentication, transmission to DIAN, acceptance/rejection validation and final validation through MUISCA APIs. DIAN operates a pre-approval validation before the invoice is legally considered received, and suppliers may integrate via REST/SOAP APIs or the 'SFE' service; deferred transmission is prohibited except for a technical interruption approved by DIAN. A written commercial invoice may be used only as a fallback for certain events, with mandatory subsequent electronic delivery.

DIAN and the Colombian Customs Authority scrutinize zero-rating claims through documentary and physical-exit checks, transactional risk scoring, and post-export audits. If export status is not substantiated, the sale is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply and VAT is assessed at 19% with default interest and inflation adjustment. A fraudulent invoice or false export declaration may trigger penalties under Articles 647 and 648 of the Tax Code, ranging from 20% to 200% of the VAT claimed, establishment closure, and criminal liability for customs fraud and aggravated invoice misconduct. Export-refund misuse also extends the statute of limitations to 10 years and may bar further refund proceedings or filings by the responsible party.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, but resident legal entities must channel all foreign-exchange interventions through the authorized market (entities registered with Banco de la República). There are no quantitative convertibility caps; compliance is procedural and documentary, requiring exchange declarations (Form 1 disbursements, Form 2 goods imports, Form 3 goods exports, Form 4 investment, Form 5 services, Form 12 remittances, Form 17 quarterly compensation accounts). Profit remittance hurdles for a SAS are procedural rather than substantive: the foreign investment must be registered with Banco de la República upon entry, dividend remittances require audited financial statements and proof of corporate income tax and dividend withholding tax payment, and the remittance must be declared through Form 12 to the authorized intermediary; an unregistered foreign investor cannot lawfully repatriate profits or capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Circular DCIN-83 Title 3, exporters of goods must channelize 100% of gross export proceeds (net of freight, insurance and foreign commissions) into Colombia via authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries within six months from the shipment date shown on the export declaration (DEX); exporters of services, including e-commerce digital sales, must channelize within six months from the date of collection. Channeling means selling the foreign currency to an authorized agent or depositing it into a registered foreign-currency account (cuenta de compensación); failure to channelize or declare constitutes an illicit foreign-exchange operation under Estatuto Cambiario and triggers fines and non-deductibility of the underlying revenue.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (BdR) as monetary, exchange-rate and foreign-exchange authority and sole issuer of the Colombian peso; the foreign-exchange regime (Régimen de Cambios Internacionales) is administered jointly with the Comité de Cambios Internacionales under Ley 9 de 1991, Decreto 1735 de 1993 and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83; the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia authorizes entities of the mercado cambiario; DIAN controls export and import declarations for cross-border documentary compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for ancillary collection, LOW as primary treasury rail. Offshore EMIs and global payment service providers (Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Stripe edge accounts, PayPal) are not entities of the Colombian mercado cambiario and cannot legally replace the mandatory channeling obligation. The compliant structure for the SAS is: consumer receipts settle in the PSP or foreign bank balance; that balance is declared quarterly to Banco de la República as a cuenta de compensación (Form 17); cumulative balances are periodically transferred to the local tier-1 bank for sale into COP. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant FDI equity routing: (1) the SAS board and shareholders formalize the capital subscription and equity increase; (2) the non-resident shareholder remits USD or EUR via SWIFT MT103 to the SAS operational account at the tier-1 bank, including the beneficiary name and the statutory DCIN-83 exchange-declaration concept for direct foreign investment (Form 4, inversion de capital del exterior); (3) the local bank verifies the incoming funds against the exchange declaration and channels the foreign currency to the market or to a registered cuenta de compensación; (4) the bank reports the inflow to the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales of Banco de la República, generating the investment registration certificate that protects future profit remittance and capital repatriation; (5) the SAS books the contribution in COP at the official TRM exchange rate with supporting bank certification. Incoming funds without prior notification and matching documentation are at high risk of compliance refusal or delay.Trade settlement rails for the SAS: (1) supplier payments via SWIFT MT103 through the tier-1 bank supported by import exchange declaration Form 2 for goods and underlying supplier invoices and purchase orders; (2) irrevocable commercial letters of credit (UCP600) issued by the Colombian bank for high-value maternity and baby inventory imports; (3) payments for imported services, logistics and SaaS subscriptions declared through Form 5 with service contracts; (4) domestic COP disbursements through ACH CUD (Cámara de Compensación Bancaria) and PSE rails for refunds, chargebacks and local supplier payments; (5) high-value interbank COP transfers cleared through CENIT, the Banco de la República settlement system. Offshore EMIs and global payment service providers (Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Stripe edge accounts, PayPal) are not entities of the Colombian mercado cambiario and cannot legally replace the mandatory channeling obligation. The compliant structure for the SAS is: consumer receipts settle in the PSP or foreign bank balance; that balance is declared quarterly to Banco de la República as a cuenta de compensación (Form 17); cumulative balances are periodically transferred to the local tier-1 bank for sale into COP. Legal constraints: no offshore EMI may be used for domestic Colombian collections or domestic supplier and payroll disbursements, and holding operational USD abroad without Banco de la República registration creates significant administrative and tax exposure.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — Colombia's national tax and customs authority. Principal electronic portal: MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicios y Control Automatizado) via www.dian.gov.co; export licensing and exporter registration are handled through the Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior (VUCE), the interagency foreign-trade single window.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned S.A.S. is incorporated before a local Cámara de Comercio, which automatically triggers tax pre-registration; DIAN then issues the NIT and the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) through MUISCA. The RUT records the statutory NIT, the ISIC 4631 activity code, 'Responsable del IVA – Régimen Común' status, withholding-agent markers, and exporter status. The company must also obtain a DIAN-recognized digital signature for e-invoicing and, for bulk-grain exports, complete its VUCE exporter registration and route export proceeds through an authorized Colombian foreign-exchange intermediary; non-resident shareholders need a NIT only when they become direct taxpayers on Colombian-source transactions or dividends.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the S.A.S. is taxed as a standalone corporate taxpayer under Colombia's territorial-source regime; entity income is not attributed to shareholders, and Colombian-source dividends distributed to non-residents are subject to dividend withholding tax rather than pass-through taxation.

Available election forms

Colombia does not operate a US-style check-the-box system and offers no general hybrid-mismatch election. Available statutory options for the S.A.S. include: (i) the optional Régimen Simple de Tributación (RST) for eligible taxpayers with ordinary revenue below approximately 4,500 UVT (COP 211.8 million in 2024), which will normally be unavailable for bulk-commodity trading turnover; (ii) elective consolidated income-tax filing for qualifying economic groups ('Grupo Tributario') subject to DIAN authorization; (iii) advance pricing agreements (APAs) with DIAN to fix transfer-pricing margins for commodity brokerage commissions; and (iv) authorized-exporter status for preferential origin declarations. The simplified VAT regime is not an election for an S.A.S., which is always a common-regime IVA responsible party.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For Colombian legal entities there is no de minimis turnover threshold: an S.A.S. engaged in taxable supplies must register for IVA/NIT from its first taxable sale under the Estatuto Tributario and Decree 1625/2016. Non-resident digital-service suppliers must register only when Colombian-source revenue exceeds 30,000 UVT per calendar year (COP 1,411,950,000 in 2024). The former simplified-regime exemption is restricted to natural persons under UVT limits and does not apply to corporations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for 0% IVA where they physically leave Colombian customs territory as 'exportación definitiva', are sold to a foreign buyer, and are evidenced by a DIAN-accepted Declaración de Exportación (DEX); the exporter then credits and refunds input VAT embedded in commodity purchases, inland freight, stevedoring and vessel costs. Services qualify under Article 481 E.T. where the buyer is non-resident and the service is rendered from Colombia but used or exploited exclusively abroad; ship-brokerage/commission arrangements for international grain voyages must demonstrate that the benefit accrues entirely outside Colombia. Exporters claim the zero rate at the FEVE level and through the bimestral IVA return, retaining charter-party/FOB sales, commission agreements and the export FEVE for each transaction.

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX) with 'levante' (shipment release) recorded in DIAN's MUISCA system
  • Transport document — Electronic Bill of Lading or charter-party bill evidencing loading, vessel name and destination
  • Commercial invoice (factura comercial) in FEVE format with CUFE and digital signature
  • Packing list (lista de empaque) including bulk-cargo tonnage and commodity grade certifications
  • Phytosanitary export certificate issued by the Instituto Colombiano Agropecuario (ICA) for soybeans and corn
  • Certificate of origin (where preferential tariff treatment is claimed under Colombia's trade agreements)
  • Foreign-exchange settlement certificate (certificado de reintegro / declaración de cambio por exportaciones) issued by an authorized Colombian bank, evidencing repatriation through the documentary credit or SWIFT wire

All IVA-responsible taxpayers must issue electronic invoices (Factura Electrónica de Venta, FEVE) under DIAN Resolution 000012/2021; each export invoice must carry a CUFE code and the issuer's digital signature and be transmitted for validation through the MUISCA platform or an authorized technology provider before delivery to the counterparty. For high-volume and designated taxpayers, DIAN Resolution 000165/2023 mandates real-time pre-clearance ('validación previa') of the XML invoice, cross-checking the seller's RUT, CUFE, commodity description, and — for exports — linkage with the customs export declaration (DEX). Purchases from non-invoicing suppliers are evidenced by an electronic 'documento soporte' (CUDS), and in-country movements of bulk grains require the interoperable electronic transport document (e-remisión).

DIAN subjects commodity exporters to a targeted export-VAT audit and may require a bank guarantee for refund balances above regulatory thresholds. An overturned zero-rating gives rise to assessment of 19% IVA on the invoice amount, clawback of every refunded input VAT with moratory interest, and a 'sanción por inexactitud' (Article 647 E.T.) generally equal to 100%–200% of the tax understated or the unauthorized refund. Falsified DEX or sham export circuits additionally trigger customs penalties under the Decree 1165/2019 framework, possible 'contrabando' classification, seizure of commodities, suspension of the company's e-invoicing authorization and exporter registration, and criminal liability of managers under tax-fraud and smuggling statutes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) operates under a free-floating exchange rate regime with a liquid USD/COP market. Current-account operations (goods, services, transport) are freely convertible and must be channeled through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries (IMC) using statutory exchange declarations (Forms 1, 2, and 5). Capital-account operations (inbound FDI, cross-border loans, portfolio investment) are also convertible but require prior or simultaneous registration with Banco de la República (Form 3 for international investment) and channeling through an IMC. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without a remittance license once the foreign investment is duly registered; the only operational hurdles are declaration of the operation through the IMC, availability of distributable reserves, and settlement of the applicable dividend withholding tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia does not impose a percentage-based compulsory conversion of export receipts into COP; instead, it mandates full 'canalización' of export proceeds. All proceeds from exports of goods (Form 1) must be collected, repatriated, and channeled through an IMC within 18 months counted from the date of shipment (term extendable upon Banco de la República authorization). Once channeled, the exporter may sell the hard currency for COP at the prevailing TRM (Representative Market Rate), or deposit it in a foreign-currency account held at the IMC or in a 'cuenta de compensación' abroad previously authorized by Banco de la República and subject to periodic reporting. Retaining export proceeds offshore without such authorization constitutes a foreign exchange violation sanctionable by the SFC and may affect the deductibility of related expenses for Colombian tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Colombia's independent central bank); foreign exchange administration jointly exercised with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) as market supervisor and the DIAN as tax/customs authority. Primary legal framework: Law 9 of 1991, Resolution 8 of 2000, and the Banco de la República's Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for core treasury and FX functions. Colombian digital financial institutions (SEDPEs such as Nequi, Lulo and Dale wallets) are licensed for deposits and electronic payments but are not authorized IMCs and therefore cannot execute the mandatory FX channeling declarations required for trade flows.

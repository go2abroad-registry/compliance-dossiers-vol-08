Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio with territorial jurisdiction over the registered office, operating through the Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES) and the Mercantile Registry (Registro Mercantil).

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008, Articles 1–54 (Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada), in supplementation of Código de Comercio Decreto 410 de 1971, Articles 110, 354 and 373; governed procedurally by Ley 2069 de 2020 (Entrepreneurship Law) for digital expedited formation.

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (private incorporation deed) executed by all shareholders, or Escritura Pública (public deed) granted before a Notary Public when in-kind contributions are made or at shareholders' election; the instrument must be filed for registration in the Mercantile Registry and RUES.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum or maximum capital; the bylaws set subscribed capital which may be zero or nominal. Per Article 5 of Ley 1258 de 2008, the bylaws determine payment installments provided the entire subscribed capital is paid within two years from subscription; no escrow account is required, and in-kind contributions must be described and valued in accordance with Article 128 of the Código de Comercio.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – main portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; transactional portal MUISCA: https://muisca.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that is not resident in Colombia but carries on taxable activities or receives Colombian-source income must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria). The application is filed electronically through DIAN's MUISCA portal by a legal representative with power of attorney, submitting (i) certificate of incorporation/existence of the foreign entity, (ii) identification documents of the representative, (iii) evidence of the foreign entity's address and legal capacity, and (iv) if VAT is involved, the estimated date of commencement of taxable operations. Alternatively, a foreign entity may incorporate a Colombian SAS through the local notary and Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio) — the SAS's NIT is automatically generated upon registration in the Chamber and completion of the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) before DIAN.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity that is not resident in Colombia but carries on taxable activities or receives Colombian-source income must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria). The application is filed electronically through DIAN's MUISCA portal by a legal representative with power of attorney, submitting (i) certificate of incorporation/existence of the foreign entity, (ii) identification documents of the representative, (iii) evidence of the foreign entity's address and legal capacity, and (iv) if VAT is involved, the estimated date of commencement of taxable operations. Alternatively, a foreign entity may incorporate a Colombian SAS through the local notary and Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio) — the SAS's NIT is automatically generated upon registration in the Chamber and completion of the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) before DIAN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists; Colombian law fixes the classification of a SAS as a separate corporate legal entity subject to entity-level Colombian income tax. However, a SAS may elect to be taxed under the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) if it meets qualifying turnover and size thresholds; may opt for Zona Franca tax stabilisation or legal stability agreements; and may make hybrid-mismatch elections permitted under Law 2010/2019 (BEPS Pillar 2 localization), but none of these alter the fundamental entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary turnover threshold for residents: any person engaged in the sale of taxable goods or services in Colombia must register as a 'responsable del IVA' (Régimen Común) before DIAN through the RUT. For foreign digital-service suppliers to Colombian consumers, registration is mandatory when a Colombian nexus exists; there is no applicable de minimis for B2C digital supplies. A smaller artisan or merchant may fall under the Simplified Regime only if it meets statutory asset and revenue caps (generally under 150 UVT in gross assets and 3,500 UVT in prior-year gross income) and is not a SAS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods — including polyresin statues and garage kits — are zero-rated under Article 481(1) of the Colombian Tax Statute (Estatuto Tributario). To qualify: (i) the seller must be an IVA-responsible entity; (ii) the buyer must be located outside Colombia; (iii) goods must physically leave Colombian customs territory under a definitive export declaration ('exportación definitiva') accepted by DIAN; (iv) the sale must be evidenced by a commercial invoice issued in the name of the foreign buyer; and (v) the foreign-currency proceeds must be repatriated through the Colombian foreign exchange market within 6 months. Services supplied to foreign clients qualify as 'export services' only if they are physically or technically exported and are exclusively consumed/used abroad; mixed services must be split and directly substantiated. This file covers goods exports only.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación Definitiva) accepted by DIAN, with unique tracking number in MUISCA/VUCE and exit stamp from Colombian customs authority.
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Waybill) issued to the overseas buyer demonstrating delivery outside Colombia.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Liquidación de Divisas) from a Colombian commercial bank evidencing repatriation of proceeds through the foreign exchange market within the statutory deadline.

All IVA-responsible taxpayers are subject to compulsory electronic invoicing ('factura electrónica de venta' or FE) under Resolutions 000020/2019 and 000012/2021. Invoices must be transmitted to DIAN in near real time over API web services; DIAN validates the XML and issues a CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) for regular invoices or a CUDS for equivalent documents, with a mandatory QR code embedded. No clearable private invoice exists; unauthorised paper or non-validated invoices are null for tax purposes. Export invoices must contain the legend 'EXPORTACIÓN' and be submitted through DIAN's customs systems in conjunction with the Declaración de Exportación.

DIAN audits export VAT refunds under Articles 855-2 and 870-2 of the Estatuto Tributario. If the exporter fails to prove timely repatriation of the export proceeds (inward remittance), the zero-rating is reversed, VAT becomes due with default interest, and the refund is clawed back with penalties. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims — including false declarations of export, simulated bill-of-lading files, money laundering via phantom sales, or deliberate misdeclaration of the destination — are deemed 'inexistent exports.' Consequences include: (i) VAT assessment plus default interest from the original due date; (ii) the penalty under Article 648 E.T. equal to 100% of the improperly offset or refunded VAT; (iii) if a false return is used, an additional false-declaration penalty of up to 100% of the hidden amount; and (iv) criminal tax fraud under Article 404 of Law 1819/2016, carrying fines equivalent to up to 15,000 statutory minimum monthly wages and imprisonment from 3 to 7 years, together with forfeiture of the goods and disqualification from future export refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a market-determined and freely floating COP with an open, regulated foreign-exchange regime rather than a strict surrender-control system. Current-account transactions are generally unrestricted and do not require prior central-bank authorization; they must, however, be routed through authorized FX intermediaries and supported by genuine trade documents when a foreign-exchange declaration is submitted. Capital-account transactions, particularly foreign direct investment into a Colombian S.A.S., are permitted without prior licensing but are subject to mandatory channeling through the FX market and registration with Banco de la República. Profit and dividend remittances are allowed after the company has settled Colombian corporate income tax and any applicable dividend withholding/remittance tax and after the foreign investment is registered; the main practical hurdles are mismatches between registered paid-in capital and the share register, lack of documentary evidence of the underlying source of funds, and inconsistent IFRS/tax/exchange declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods exports are subject to 100% channeling through the official FX market. The exporter must bring the foreign currency proceeds into Colombia within six months after the date of the export customs declaration (DEX), unless Banco de la República permits a longer term for the specific product or a previously authorized foreign-currency compensation account is used. Channeling means that the full FOB export value must be credited to a Colombian authorized bank or to a registered foreign-currency account and reported on the corresponding exchange declaration; it does not require forced conversion into COP if the exporter legitimately retains foreign currency for permitted payments. If conversion is needed, sale to an authorized bank at the representative market rate must be recorded on the relevant Declaración de Cambio.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), acting through its Board of Directors as the issuing and foreign exchange authority. Exchange-control supervision and enforcement of anti-money-laundering conduct by financial intermediaries are exercised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. The core applicable regulation is External Resolution No. 1 of 2018 (International Exchange Regulations), as amended.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

35% statutory withholding tax rate applies to dividends paid to non-resident beneficiaries out of Colombian taxable profits; certain profits already taxed at the entity level may qualify for lower effective treatment under Colombia’s imputation mechanics. For non-resident corporate shareholders, the effective statutory withholding in standard practice is up to 35% absent treaty relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year SAS, the income tax return must be filed in the following fiscal year on the exact date assigned by DIAN Resolution based on the last two digits of the NIT. The general corporate window usually falls between May and June, and in all cases before the deadline fixed by DIAN with reference to the fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: 100,000 UVT in gross income or gross assets of the preceding taxable year; for tax year 2024, approximately COP 4,706,500,000. Additionally, taxpayers with cross-border related-party transactions may be required to file the Transfer Pricing Information Return (Form 120) and maintain Local File and Master File documentation when the threshold is met.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the DIAN applies Colombian GAAR and OECD BEPS principles requiring that the S.A.S. carry out real economic activity, maintain physical premises, qualified staff, actual inventory, and decision-making in Colombia. Cross-border structures or transactions lacking commercial rationale may be recharacterized, and treaty benefits denied under the principal purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion sanctions under Colombian law include imprisonment of 4 to 9 years and fines of up to 1,000 minimum monthly wages. Civil tax re-determination results in a penalty for inaccuracy of 100% to 200% of the omitted tax, plus default interest and daily surcharges, and disqualification from government contracts.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign fraudulent returns or participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable under tax law for the full tax debt. Criminal conviction may impose imprisonment, travel bans, asset seizure, and professional disqualification.
Shell Company / Corporate Veil Abuse

Statutory sanction: DIAN may recharacterize disregarded transactions, disallow deductions, impose penalties up to 160% of the non-declared amounts, and declare the company dissolved by the Superintendencia de Sociedades when the S.A.S. is found to be a sham used for fraud, evasion, or asset stripping.

Director personal liability: Colombian courts and the Superintendencia de Sociedades may pierce the corporate veil, rendering shareholders and directors personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and civil restitution. Criminal asset seizure, travel bans, and up to 12 years of imprisonment may be imposed for asset concealment and insolvency fraud.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Colombian Criminal Code Article 323 is punishable by 10 to 30 years of imprisonment and fines from 1,000 to 50,000 minimum monthly wages. The financial assets involved are subject to extinguishment of ownership (asset forfeiture). The company may also be barred from operating with regulated payment service providers.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or negligently permit the flow of illicit proceeds in the company face criminal liability as co-authors or participants. In addition to imprisonment, courts may impose travel bans, freezing of personal assets, withdrawal from corporate office, and joint and several civil restitution to defrauded victims and the tax administration.

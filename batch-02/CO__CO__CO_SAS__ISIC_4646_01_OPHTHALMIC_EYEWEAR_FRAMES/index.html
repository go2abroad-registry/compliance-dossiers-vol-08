Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — the unitary national tax and customs administration (organic rules under Decree 1742 of 2020); primary official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co. MUISCA is the tax-administration platform for NIT/RUT registrations and filings; SFE (Sistema de Facturación Electrónica) web-services operate the mandatory e-invoicing validation; VUCE and the SIGCE/SOF-customs modules handle export customs formalities. Banco de la República exercises complementary foreign-exchange supervision through authorized local intermediaries (intermediarios del mercado cambiario).

E-government filing portal

The CO_SAS must complete the following sequence: (1) Incorporate the S.A.S. before a Colombian notary and register with the Cámara de Comercio (CCB/RUES) to obtain the provisional NIT; (2) file the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) electronically through MUISCA or at a DIAN servicio de atención, attaching the certificate of existence and legal representation (certificado de existencia y representación legal), notarized bylaws, the legal representative's national ID or foreign passport, and the final-beneficial-owner declaration of the non-resident shareholders; (3) receive the definitive NIT and RUT, which automatically designates the SAS as income-tax declarant ('Declarante de Renta'), 'Responsable del IVA — Régimen Común', withholding agent (agente de retención), electronic-invoice issuer, and customs exporter-importer; (4) obtain the DIAN-accredited digital signature (firma electrónica / DAS) to authenticate e-invoices and MUISCA procedures; (5) register any incoming foreign investment with Banco de la República and open the 'Declaración de Cambio' file with a local commercial bank, through which future export receivables must be repatriated; (6) complete SFE technical onboarding for XML/CUDS/QR issuance, either via a DIAN-authorized technology provider (proveedor tecnológico) or through the free DIAN web portal. The whole NIT/RUT activation process takes approximately 1–3 business days after RUES registration and is valid for all national tax, customs, and municipal obligations without a separate sub-jurisdictional tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the S.A.S. (Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada) is a resident legal entity subject to Colombian corporate income tax on its worldwide income at the statutory headline CIT rate in force (régimen ordinario); profits are not attributable to shareholders for Colombian tax purposes, no pass-through or flow-through election is available; distributions of taxed profits to non-resident shareholders trigger final dividend withholding tax at the statutory rate applicable to foreign beneficiaries; the entity is not a free-trade-zone taxpayer unless it obtains a separate 'Usuario de Zona Franca' authorization, and it therefore operates under the general territorial tax regime.

Available election forms

Colombia has no US-style check-the-box system and the SAS cannot elect transparent status. The statutory elections available are: (i) the optional integrated 'Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE)', a turnover-based consolidated payment replacing income tax, national consumption tax (where applicable) and ICA, available to an SAS only if the preceding-year gross revenue does not exceed 100,000 UVT, all shareholders are natural persons, and economic-substance/client tests (including domestic-recipient concentration) are met; (ii) consolidated income-tax filing ('régimen de consolidación fiscal') where a Colombian controlling entity holds 100% of the SAS and obtains DIAN authorization; (iii) formal VAT zero-rating treatment for exports and export-related services, with the corresponding bimestral input-VAT refund claim (devolución) under Art. 855 ET; (iv) transfer-pricing documentation elections such as the six-month post-filing deadline for the master/local files and the possibility of submitting mutual agreement or advance-pricing requests through DIAN's APA regime; and (v) hybrid-mismatch containment — no standalone hybrid-instrument election exists, but Colombia restricts deductibility of payments to zero-tax or non-cooperating jurisdictions under Articles 406-1, 124-1 and 140-3 ET and applies OECD-oriented CFC and minimum-top-up rules introduced by Law 2277 of 2022, so the SAS must disclose such payments in information returns attached to its annual CIT filing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is freely convertible for current account transactions (trade in goods and services, royalties, dividends on registered investment, and ordinary remittances). Capital account transactions, including inbound foreign direct investment (FDI) and cross-border loans, require prior registration with Banrep under the Resolution 1 of 2018 and must be channelled through authorized foreign exchange market intermediaries (Mercado Cambiario). Profit remittance to a foreign shareholder is permissible from the second fiscal year onwards under an officially registered foreign investment; the remittance must be documented with audited financial statements, a Board or Shareholders' resolution authorizing dividends, and proof of tax clearance from DIAN (Paz y Salvo). Without an active foreign investment registration (Form 4), the remittance will be treated as non-resident remittance subject to higher withholding and possible rejection by domestic banks due to regulatory non-compliance. For an S.A.S. wholly owned by non-residents, capital account conversion restrictions are administratively onerous but not prohibitive; delays typically arise from missing prior-year registration filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds for goods such as ophthalmic eyewear frames must be repatriated into Colombia and are required to be collected within the statutory collection term established by the Junta Directiva del Banco de la República, generally 9 months from the date of shipment under the current Circular, statutorily extendable up to an additional 3 months for exceptional circumstances (e.g., documentary collection delays, customs verification). Exporters are required to enter into the 'channeling' process through a bank on the list of authorized foreign exchange intermediaries; the proceeds must be credited to a local account in COP or foreign currency and reported to the Central Bank via the 'forma número 1' (Declaración de Operaciones de Exportaciones). There is no mandatory percentage conversion to COP if the foreign currency account is in an authorized Colombian financial institution, but all proceeds must either be converted and deposited in COP or remain in a legal foreign currency account that is part of the Colombian Foreign Exchange Market. Failure to meet the collection term may trigger a 'cede' (restriction) on the exporter's FI access, withholding on future export transactions, and an immediate obligation to sell the unrelated foreign currency in the local market.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Banrep) in conjunction with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Tourism. Foreign exchange operations and capital movements are governed by the Colombian Foreign Exchange Regime (Régimen Cambiario) as codified in Statute of Foreign Exchange and related 'Resoluciones' and the Single Regulatory Circular DCIN-83 issued by Banrep.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM-LOW for corporate banking, but acceptable as auxiliary multi-cadence currency pooling in specific corridors (e.g. US/Europe) without domestic Colombian settlement. Offshore digital EMI wallets (such as Wise Business, Airwallex, or Payoneer) are not permitted to host the main treasury account because under the Colombian regulatory organic law the company must maintain its principal treasury and receivable settlement account with a Colombian credit institution that is a member of the Cámara de Riesgo Central de Contraparte and supervised by the SFC for money-laundering and foreign-exchange compliance. An offshore EMI may be used as an overflow dedollarization bucket only if it operates under a valid license in a white-listed jurisdiction (e.g., FCA in the United Kingdom or FinCEN/State license in the United States) but it cannot be used to receive foreign finance from the parent company without violating Colombian capital market channeling rules. The legal workaround is to use the EMI for paying non-colombian suppliers in trade amounts lower than USD 5,000 each, provided each transaction is reported under the periodic 'Exportaciones' data filing to Banrep.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial owner global complexity (non-resident shareholders and intermediate holdings)

Root cause: Beneficial owner global complexity (non-resident shareholders and intermediate holdings)

Defense evidence: Provide the full 22-column Beneficial Ownership Register ('Registro Final de Beneficiarios') mandated under SFC Circular 032 of 2021, showing each natural person above 25% equity; consolidate a Swiss-style Equity Holding Chart; obtain the sworn statement of the ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) declared before a Colombian notary; include audited financials of intermediate holding 10 days prior to account opening; and prepare a 'waterhead' explanation for any shell-flagging in the ownership structure.
Geographical risk: supplier or customer base includes names appearing in the OFAC and/or Colombian MACMA / SFC lists

Defense evidence: Prepare a completed Sanctions Radar questionnaire showing 0% exact hits in OFAC Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) and Colombia's Consejo Nacional de Política Criminal (CONPES). Attach an Enterprise-wide AML policy, notarized wording that the company does not transact with North Korea, Iran, Syria, Sudan, Cuba or Venezuela (legal prohibition), and provide the invoice trail for the top 10 buyers mapping to unsanctioned banking jurisdictions.
Suspicious export proceeds pattern (mounting, immediate outward transfer, 'round-tripping' suspicion)

Root cause: Suspicious export proceeds pattern (mounting, immediate outward transfer, 'round-tripping' suspicion)

Defense evidence: For all international trade movements exceeding USD 50,000, produce the underlying sales contract, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and the customs declaration (DIAN Form 150). If using a foreign parent corporate treasury as clearing hub, provide the intercompany treasury and settlement agreement with transfer pricing report demonstrating an arm's length margin (the LLC: TNMM method with 8-10% operating margin), and produce attestation from the Colombian external auditor.
Foreign-policy obstacles: non-resident legal representative without Tax Identification Number in Colombia

Root cause: Foreign-policy obstacles: non-resident legal representative without Tax Identification Number in Colombia

Defense evidence: Before account opening, the legal representative must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) assigned by DIAN, a power of attorney registered with the Notary in Colombia (or a Colombian Consulate for jurisdictions with consular rights), and the original passport with biometric chip. The mandatory evidence is the 'Certificado de Inscripción en Camara de Comercio' (Cámara y Comercio) including the representative's ID, and the 'Formulario del Registro Personas Expuestas Políticamente (PEP)'.
Capital escrow account destination mismatch (using escrow account to send operational payments to third parties)

Root cause: Capital escrow account destination mismatch (using escrow account to send operational payments to third parties)

Defense evidence: Demonstrate that the capital escrow operational ledger is only used for the formal transfer of foreign investment into the Colombian entity, plus the subsequent payment of trade creditors, not for remuneration to directors. Provide the Equity Payment Plan (Plan de Inversión) approved by the shareholders; explain that after receipt of funds, the escrow account will be immediately converted into a regular operational account within 10 banking days (a standard 'account reclassification'), and show the closing balance reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The only compliant route for an inbound capital injection to the Colombian S.A.S. from its foreign shareholder(s) is (i) register the foreign investment with Banrep under the Foreign Investment Regulation (Resolución 1 of 2018 / Banco de la República 'Registro de Inversiones Internacionales'), filing Form 3 (Registration of Foreign Investment) and obtaining a single national 'CUTE' (Código de Registro de Protección de Inversiones) or a local 'Formulario de Inversión Extranjera'; (ii) open a 'Capital Escrow Account' (Subcuenta de Inversión Extranjera) at a tier-1 Colombian bank under the Bank's settlement hub; (iii) instruct the sending bank to use the SWIFT MT103 message format with the foreign exchange purpose code 'INV.FD.101' (Investment in capital of financial or non-financial entities) or, where applicable, 'INV.DS.103' (public debt / portfolio investment). Wire transfer to the escrow account must include the CUTE registration number in the beneficiary reference field, the legal entity tax ID (NIT) of the Colombian company, and the seal 'REGI' (inversion registrada). After the amounts are received, within 5 banking days the bank must be instructed to convert the received foreign currency into COP at the current intraday market rate if the company wants to avoid maintaining a restricted foreign currency account. The bank will automatically issue the 'Formas de Cambio' (Formulario de Operaciones Cambiarias) to Banrep on the same business day. The injection must be accompanied by Board resolutions approving the capital increase, the modified Bylaws of the S.A.S. stamped by the Chamber of Commerce, and for amounts above USD 250,000, a proof of source of funds issued by a solidarity financial institution of the home country (Apostillised), plus the Colombian bank's 'FIM' (Formulario de Información Monetaria) due diligence documents.Cross-border payments to nonresident optical frame manufacturers (e.g., acetate material vendors in Italy, China, Hong Kong, or Korea) must be channelled through the authorized FX market via one of the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for regular commercial transfers using purpose codes 'CURRENT ACCOUNT: 727 - Imports of Goods' (Pago de Importaciones). For settlements below USD 10,000, the bank requires only the supplier's proforma invoice; for settlements above USD 10,000, submit the customs import declaration (Form 150 BI) after real entry into Colombia, the contract, and the previously approved 'Registro de Compras' (Purchase Register) as required by Banrep Circular DCIN-83. Alternatively, (ii) Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) with standard ICC UCP600 rules may be used for bulk purchases of acetate frames from overseas, opened at the Colombian bank (issuing bank) in favor of the exporter; the confirmed L/C clearing rail is executed in the same FX channel, under purpose code '727 - Importaciones', and must be confirmed under the bank's 'Cámara de Compensación Internacional' or their Nostro/VOSTRO network (USD, EUR, CNY). All outbound disbursements to repeat supply partners should be made after CADES (Centro de mencatiación) record date to ensure the authority to transfer the funds enters in the tradable cycle. The treasury should also use 'BDPROW' platform (Banca de Pagos y Recaudo de Operaciones de Comercio Exterior) for real-time payment status reconciliation.Colombian peso - foreign currency systematic forwards (FX Forwards) with an authorized local bank, quoted as the NTRM (Tasa Representativa del Mercado) rate at the transaction date. A final value dated OC/IN (Overseas Collection / Intercompany Netting) may be used for hedging the peso payables on the trade cycle.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only if the transaction is interbank and both counterparties are resident financial institutions registered with the SFC; the NDF can reference the Colombian peso without physical settlement in COP, but the settlement must be in USD through an offshore account. This instrument is permitted only to hedge commodity or trade exposure and not for speculative positions.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) when the volume of monthly trade disbursements exceeds US$500,000 and the contractual maturity is more than 12 months; permitted only with an authorized Colombian bank or an international bank that holds a license from the SFC to operate in the local market. All swaps must be recorded in the Banco de la República 'Registro de Derivados' on the same day of execution.Currency options (call/put USD-COP) classified as excluding speculative variance, provided they are functionally hedges for identified FX risk arising from export/import flows; with the principle that the bank must verify the underlying transaction and include a clause automatically exercising the option if the transaction does not mature (clause of 'eliminación de utilidad especulativa').

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: MEDIUM-LOW for corporate banking, but acceptable as auxiliary multi-cadence currency pooling in specific corridors (e.g. US/Europe) without domestic Colombian settlement. Offshore digital EMI wallets (such as Wise Business, Airwallex, or Payoneer) are not permitted to host the main treasury account because under the Colombian regulatory organic law the company must maintain its principal treasury and receivable settlement account with a Colombian credit institution that is a member of the Cámara de Riesgo Central de Contraparte and supervised by the SFC for money-laundering and foreign-exchange compliance. An offshore EMI may be used as an overflow dedollarization bucket only if it operates under a valid license in a white-listed jurisdiction (e.g., FCA in the United Kingdom or FinCEN/State license in the United States) but it cannot be used to receive foreign finance from the parent company without violating Colombian capital market channeling rules. The legal workaround is to use the EMI for paying non-colombian suppliers in trade amounts lower than USD 5,000 each, provided each transaction is reported under the periodic 'Exportaciones' data filing to Banrep.

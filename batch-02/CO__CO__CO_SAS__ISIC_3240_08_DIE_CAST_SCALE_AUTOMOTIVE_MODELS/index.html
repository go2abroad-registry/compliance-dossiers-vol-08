Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – main portal www.dian.gov.co; electronic services via MUISCA and the Facturación Electrónica portal.

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian S.A.S., the process is: (i) register the company deed with the Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio); (ii) obtain the statutory NIT by completing the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) with DIAN (online or in person); (iii) activate the fiscal electronic invoice authorization and, if required, obtain a digital signature certificate. For a non-resident entity without a local establishment, the foreign company must execute a power of attorney appointing a Colombian tax representative and register a NIT through DIAN (form/RUT) before performing any activity subject to VAT or generating Colombian-source income.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (a Colombian S.A.S. is a resident corporation taxed on its worldwide income; no pass-through or transparent classification is permitted, regardless of foreign shareholding). — For a Colombian S.A.S., the process is: (i) register the company deed with the Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio); (ii) obtain the statutory NIT by completing the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) with DIAN (online or in person); (iii) activate the fiscal electronic invoice authorization and, if required, obtain a digital signature certificate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a Colombian S.A.S. is a resident corporation taxed on its worldwide income; no pass-through or transparent classification is permitted, regardless of foreign shareholding).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available. Elections permitted by law include: (a) voluntary election of the Simplified Taxation Regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación – SIMPLE) for eligible entities with gross income not exceeding 100,000 UVT; (b) election of inflationary tax adjustments for certain balance-sheet items; and (c) application, with DIAN authorization, of the optional fiscal consolidation framework for 100%-owned Colombian group entities. Hybrid mismatch disallowances are applied automatically under the BEPS Action 2 rules adopted by Law 2277 of 2022.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for legal entities; an S.A.S. undertaking any taxable activity must register as an IVA responsible person through the RUT. Natural persons qualifying as 'pequeños comerciantes' may be excused from charging IVA if their prior-year gross income does not exceed 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 126.5 million for fiscal year 2023).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including die-cast scale automotive replicas) are zero-rated under Article 481 E.T. when the goods are definitively exported under a customs export declaration (DEX), leave Colombian customs territory, and are disposed of outside the national customs area. Export-related services are also zero-rated if the recipient is a foreign entity and the benefit is realized exclusively outside Colombia. The exporter may recover input VAT through a saldo a favor by filing a VAT refund application with DIAN and providing evidence of shipment and foreign exchange settlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX) filed with DIAN/MUISCA evidencing the physical exit of the goods from the Colombian customs territory
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill/road cargo manifest) showing the exporter as the shipper and the foreign destination of the die-cast models
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas) or bank-issued export collection statement proving repatriation of the export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial electronic invoice (factura electrónica de exportación) and, where applicable, certificate of origin or inspection certificate for preferential tariff treatment or customs compliance

All registered businesses must issue electronic sales invoices (factura electrónica de venta). Invoices must be transmitted to DIAN in near-real-time using certified software providers, DIAN's free MUISCA tool, or the DIAN e-invoicing API (SOAP/REST) with X.509 digital certificates. DIAN validates each invoice before it acquires full tax effects; each invoice carries a unique electronic code (CUFE) and a QR matrix. Supplementary electronic support documents (documento soporte en adquisiciones) are required for payments to non-responsible suppliers under Resolution 165/2023.

DIAN cross-validates export claims against customs records, transport-operating invoices, and national bank/foreign-exchange databases. A false or unsupported export zero-rating claim leads to (i) reversal and refund clawback of any VAT credit or refund paid, (ii) default interest accruing from the date of the refund, (iii) an inaccuracy penalty (sanción por inexactitud) typically 100% of the unduly claimed balance and up to 200% for egregious misstatements, and (iv) criminal prosecution for tax fraud under Law 2277 of 2022 with prison terms of 4 to 9 years. The DIAN audit/limitation period is generally five years, extending to ten years where returns are fraudulent or not filed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia applies a managed floating exchange rate regime; the peso (COP) is freely convertible for current account transactions (goods, services, profit remittances, royalties) subject to documentation and channeling through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries. Capital account operations are lawful but controlled: foreign direct investment into an S.A.S. must be registered with Banco de la República within 6 months of the first remittance, using the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales (Formulario 1); dividends and disposal proceeds may then be remitted without quantitative limits, subject to registration continuity, tax clearance, and channeling through a licensed intermediary. Practical hurdles for an ISIC 3240 exporter include documentary mismatches between treasury flows and the corporate books, the obligation to route foreign currency exclusively through the mercado cambiario, and a 7.5% dividend remittance withholding tax on foreign shareholders unless reduced under a tax treaty. No general convertibility quota exists for a manufacturing exporter with auditable invoices.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of goods must channel 100% of proceeds from definitive exports through the authorized foreign exchange market (licensed bank, compensation account, or an eligible cross-border settlement arrangement) within 6 calendar months counted from the date of the export customs declaration (DEX). There is no forced conversion ratio into pesos: the exporter may lawfully retain USD in a domestic foreign-currency account or an offshore compensation account, provided the funds are 'reintegrated' into the exchange market within the term. Documentation at bank collection must include the export declaration digest, transport documents, commercial invoice, and the bank remittance advice. Non-compliance triggers exchange infractions, monetary fines of up to 200% of the un-interned balance, and possible loss of cost deductibility for the underlying export transaction.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Junta Directiva) as central bank and foreign exchange administration, operating under Decreto 1735 de 1993 and Resolución Externa No. 8 de 2021; prudential and banking conduct supervision vested in the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia; exchange infraction enforcement delegated in coordination with the DIAN and the Superintendencia Financiera.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

LOW as a primary treasury rail; LIMITED as an ancillary digital collection layer. Colombian law recognizes only locally licensed credit entities and SEDPEs (Sociedades Especializadas en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos under Law 1735 of 2014) as authorized payment intermediaries. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer and Airwallex are not supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera and cannot hold domestic COP accounts or act as exchange intermediaries. PayPal Colombia S.A.S. may process retail card receipts, but PayPal is not an authorized channel for export reintegro; using it as the sole collection rail creates exchange control exposure. Feasibility blueprint: PayPal for micro retail transactions only, swept automatically to the authorized bank; all commercial settlements above USD 5,000 must move via SWIFT MT103 through a Colombian licensed bank.

Compliant sequence: (1) register the foreign direct investment with Banco de la República's Registro de Inversiones Internacionales using Formulario 1 and the cambiario concept code for 'inversión directa en Colombia'; (2) remit USD by SWIFT MT103 through the shareholder's own account with a clear narrative referencing the registration number, the corporate document number and the wording 'shareholder equity subscription'; (3) present to the Colombian bank the notarized board/partner resolutions, the current Cámara de Comercio certificate and the accounting receipt for the capital quota; (4) instruct the local authorized intermediary to execute the USD-to-COP conversion at the TRM rate and credit the operational COP account, or alternatively credit the USD account and enter the position in the monthly FX declaration; (5) retain the Declaración de Cambio evidencing that the transaction is classified as registered foreign direct investment. The bank's underwriting will additionally require source-of-funds documentation for the remitting shareholder, and the registration certificate must be filed within six months of the first remittance to avoid reclassification as external debt.Clearing architecture: (a) standard supplier and operating disbursements below USD 100,000 execute by SWIFT MT103 through the correspondent banking chain, accompanied by DIAN electronic invoice references and, for import payments, a copy of the DIAN import declaration; (b) procurement above USD 100,000 uses a Commercial Letter of Credit opened by the S.A.S. at a Colombian bank, confirmed by the seller's bank and conditioned on full-set bills of lading, packing list, certificate of origin and metal-specification certificate; (c) export collections arrive as SWIFT MT103 USD credits into the S.A.S. foreign currency account, followed by a bank-executed 'reintegro' sale into the interbank market or retention as USD natural hedge; (d) PayPal and marketplace collections are swept weekly into the authorized COP account with reconciliation; (e) derivative collateral top-ups and premium settlements for FX hedging move through internal bank journal entries in COP between the treasury and derivative accounts. All import disbursements must observe Resolución 8 de 2021's payment calendar, generally allowing settlement up to six months after customs entry, with advance payments permitted only against a registered import obligation.USD/COP deliverable forward contracts (forwards sobre tasa de cambio) executed with a locally licensed bank, supported by documented export receivables or import payables, settled in COP by TRM differential.USD/COP non-deliverable forwards (NDF) entered with offshore counterparties only where a local authorized intermediary acts as mirror or back-to-back hedge, underlying trade exposure is evidenced, and the transaction is included in the quarterly foreign exchange declaration.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps matching USD export inflows against COP operating liabilities, executed under ISDA or local master agreements with the treasury desk of a supervised bank.USD/COP European-style currency options used for hedging, not for speculative positions, with premiums settled through the local banking channel.Natural hedge via multi-currency treasury: retention of USD export proceeds in local foreign currency accounts, scheduled monthly conversion to COP, and alignment of import payables with export receipts to reduce spot-market volatility exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: LOW as a primary treasury rail; LIMITED as an ancillary digital collection layer. Colombian law recognizes only locally licensed credit entities and SEDPEs (Sociedades Especializadas en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos under Law 1735 of 2014) as authorized payment intermediaries. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer and Airwallex are not supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera and cannot hold domestic COP accounts or act as exchange intermediaries. PayPal Colombia S.A.S. may process retail card receipts, but PayPal is not an authorized channel for export reintegro; using it as the sole collection rail creates exchange control exposure. Feasibility blueprint: PayPal for micro retail transactions only, swept automatically to the authorized bank; all commercial settlements above USD 5,000 must move via SWIFT MT103 through a Colombian licensed bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File, Master File, and transfer-pricing informative return apply to taxpayers that (i) carry out transactions with foreign related parties or persons in jurisdictions classified as tax havens and (ii) have gross equity or gross income exceeding 100,000 UVT in the prior tax year. For tax year 2024, 100,000 UVT equals COP 4,706,500,000 (using 2024 UVT of COP 47,065). Entities below this threshold must still benchmark transactions and may be audited if cross-border related-party pricing is not at arm's length. Country-by-Country Report obligations apply separately to multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding approximately COP 200 billion.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations to domestic S.A.S. entities. However, DIAN applies general anti-avoidance rules (Article 658-2 ET), the substance-over-form doctrine, CFC rules, and Article 260-1 ET reallocation powers. The die-casting S.A.S. must demonstrate actual business substance through physical factory premises, employees, die-casting machinery, operating expenses, inventory movement, and board decisions. If the entity is a mere conduit or shell, DIAN may disregard the separate legal personality, reallocate profits to the real economic owner, impose the 200% understatement penalty, and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Revenue Omission or False Deductions

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Article 434A of Law 599 of 2000: imprisonment of four to nine years and a fine equal to at least 200% of the evaded tax. Administratively, DIAN may impose a 200% understatement penalty under Article 647 ET plus moratory interest and adjusted values.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and shareholders who knowingly sign or cause false returns are criminally liable. Under Articles 794 and 823 ET, they are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt, penalties, and interest; travel bans, asset embargoes, and disqualification from managing companies may be imposed.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse of the S.A.S.

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the S.A.S. corporate form under Article 658-2 ET, reallocate profits to the foreign shareholder, treat profits as a Colombian permanent establishment, and assess a 200% penalty on the adjusted tax plus moratory interest. The director/shareholder may also be denied the benefit of limited liability.

Director personal liability: Under Law 1258 of 2008 (S.A.S. law), the corporate veil may be pierced when the S.A.S. is used for fraud or tax evasion. Directors who execute shamming transactions are personally liable to the tax authority and to creditors for the entire adjusted tax, penalties, and non-penalty obligations.
Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Violations

Statutory sanction: Criminal offense under Article 323 of Law 599 of 2000: imprisonment of ten to twenty years and fines up to 50,000 current monthly minimum wages. Administrative penalties, asset freezing, and closure of bank accounts may be imposed by the Financial Information and Analysis Unit (UIAF) and Superintendencia de Sociedades.

Director personal liability: Directors and the S.A.S.'s legal representative are personally exposed to criminal prosecution if they knowingly participate in laundering proceeds from die-cast collectibles transactions. International travel bans, extradition requests, and disqualification from corporate office are applicable; the US Patriot Act counterpart and inter-American cooperation may also trigger asset forfeiture.
Fraudulent Invoicing and False Accounting Records

Statutory sanction: DIAN may cancel the S.A.S.'s invoicing authorization, impose a penalty under Article 652 ET up to 10,000 UVT per infringement, and reject all associated deductions. Falsifying private documents under Article 285 of Law 599 of 2000 carries imprisonment of 32 to 72 months. Tax fraud penalties of up to 200% of the inaccuracy are additionally imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify or authorize false financial statements are criminally liable for falsification of private documents. Article 42 of Law 1258 of 2008 permits creditors and DIAN to pierce the veil, making directors jointly liable for the entire tax adjustment, fines, and civil damages arising from the false invoices.

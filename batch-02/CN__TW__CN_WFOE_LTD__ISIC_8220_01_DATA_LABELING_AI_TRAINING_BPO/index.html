Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None (no statutory minimum) under the Taiwan Companies Act; however, for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise, the approved investment amount must be remitted from abroad into a temporary bank account in the name of the company before registration. After registration, the escrow is released and the funds are transferred to the company's general operating account.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance of Taiwan (R.O.C.), Taxation Administration, together with the regional National Taxation Bureaus including the National Taxation Bureau of Taipei; online electronic portals: Tax e-Gate (https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw) and the Integrated Electronic Invoice Service Platform (https://www.einvoice.nat.gov.tw).

E-government filing portal

For a Taiwan-incorporated Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE): (1) secure the required investment approval before incorporation; foreign investors file under the Statute for Investment by Foreign Nationals and mainland-China investors file under the Regulations Governing the Permission of Investment in Taiwan by Mainland China Persons with the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs (Invest Taiwan); (2) complete company registration with the Department of Commercial Affairs, Ministry of Economic Affairs, obtaining the company registration certificate and company identification number; (3) within 15 days after commencing business, file the statutory Business Tax registration application with the local National Taxation Bureau, submitting the company registration certificate, responsible person identification, place-of-business proof, bank deposit certificate, seal registration, and power of attorney if an agent is appointed; the Bureau issues the 8-digit Unified Business Number (UBN) which functions as the VAT ID and tax file number; (4) a non-resident foreign enterprise without a fixed place of business or business agent in Taiwan but earning Taiwan-source income should apply directly to the relevant National Taxation Bureau for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) to support withholding tax compliance and tax treaty relief; (5) foreign suppliers of cross-border electronic services consumed by Taiwanese individuals are required to register for Business Tax on Tax e-Gate once their annual Taiwan-source turnover exceeds NT$480,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, zero-rated treatment applies to exported goods, services related to export trade, and services rendered in Taiwan but used or consumed wholly outside Taiwan. For the WFOE's AI data-labeling, bounding-box annotation, and RLHF curation services, the taxpayer must establish that the services are supplied under an export-service contract to a non-resident customer, the work product is delivered to and economically consumed outside Taiwan, the customer is the offshore beneficial recipient, and consideration is received in foreign currency. Eligible export zero-rating allows a 0% output tax rate with a corresponding right to claim input Business Tax credits or refunds attributable to the zero-rated export activity. Services consumed in Taiwan or supplied through a Taiwan-resident intermediary are standard-rated at 5%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) accepted by Taiwan Customs under the Ministry of Finance; for pure cross-border data-labeling/BPO services, the zero-rated services sales ledger and service export work-order or statement are required control documents
  • Bill of Lading, sea waybill, or air waybill evidencing cross-border movement for physical exports; for digital AI-training services, equivalent digital delivery evidence including transfer logs, cloud-handover reports, or system acceptance certificates
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Receipt or bank-issued inward remittance documentation (the Taiwan equivalent of the Inward Remittance Certificate/FIRC) evidencing collection of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and signed export or AI-training-services contract identifying the non-resident customer, pricing, scope, and offshore-use destination
  • End-user acceptance, UAT sign-off, or completion report from the overseas client corroborating offshore consumption of the annotated or curated data set

Taiwan operates the Uniform Invoice system. Registered VAT businesses that adopt Electronic Uniform Invoices (EUI) must transmit standardized XML invoice data to the Integrated Electronic Invoice Service Platform using the official Turnkey/API connection, generally within 48 hours after issuance; each e-invoice must carry a nationally assigned random anti-fraud number and QR code, and retain a cloud invoice stamp. Paper Uniform Invoices remain sequentially controlled but are not pre-cleared by the tax authority before issuance. Non-compliant issuers may be fined, have invoice serial blocks withdrawn, or be subject to business suspension under the Business Tax Act.

The tax administration will review the complete export documentation bundle, including customs declarations, transport or digital delivery evidence, service contracts, and foreign-exchange remittance records. Export proceeds must be settled or repatriated to Taiwan within 180 days under Central Bank foreign-exchange requirements. If zero-rating is found to be fraudulent, unsupported, or based on falsified export documents, the tax authority retrospectively reassesses output Business Tax at the standard 5% rate from the invoice date, imposes additional tax, and may apply the Business Tax Act Article 51 penalty of not less than 5 times and not more than 15 times the evaded tax. Serious cases may also trigger criminal liability under the Tax Collection Act, with the tax collection limitation period extended or made unlimited in cases of fraud; e-invoice sanctions and suspension of Uniform Invoice usage may be ordered as well.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan dollar is generally convertible for current-account transactions, including bona fide trade in goods, services, salaries, operating expenses, and ordinary profit remittances. Taiwan maintains managed foreign-exchange controls rather than unrestricted capital-account convertibility. Capital transactions, foreign direct investment, equity contributions, intercompany loans, dividends, liquidation proceeds, acquisitions, and certain securities or derivatives transactions may require prior approval, bank declarations, documentary evidence, annual limits, or transaction-specific reporting. A foreign-invested Taiwan company may generally remit dividends, interest, service fees, royalties, and liquidation proceeds after applicable taxes, corporate approvals, audited or approved financial statements, withholding-tax compliance, and proof of the underlying transaction. Banks may require MOEA investment approval or registration, board or shareholder resolutions, tax-clearance evidence, dividend documentation, contracts, invoices, and evidence that the remittance is commercially genuine. Transactions involving Mainland Chinese persons, entities, beneficial owners, or funds may be subject to additional cross-Strait investment restrictions and enhanced scrutiny. The stated entity identifier CN_WFOE_LTD is not sufficient to establish a Taiwan legal entity; the entity must possess Taiwan company registration and, where applicable, MOEA foreign-investment approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan generally does not impose a universal mandatory conversion ratio requiring all export proceeds to be sold for New Taiwan dollars. The standard foreign-exchange framework generally requires export proceeds to be repatriated to Taiwan within six months after export, unless an extension, offshore-retention arrangement, or other exception is approved or otherwise permitted under applicable rules. There is therefore ordinarily no statutory 100%, 50%, or similar compulsory surrender ratio; the effective mandatory conversion ratio is generally 0%, subject to the requirement to receive, declare, document, and repatriate proceeds as applicable. Banks may require customs documents, commercial invoices, service contracts, payment records, shipping or delivery evidence, and foreign-exchange declarations. Exporters should obtain bank confirmation before retaining proceeds offshore, offsetting them against overseas obligations, netting intercompany balances, or using a foreign collection account. Documentary evidence must support the distinction between goods exports, cross-border services, software or data services, refunds, intercompany settlements, and capital transactions.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC). Foreign-investment registration, investment approvals, and related capital procedures are administered primarily by the Investment Commission, Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA). This dossier applies to Taiwan (TW), not Mainland China. If the entity is legally established in Mainland China, the applicable authorities are the State Administration of Foreign Exchange and the People's Bank of China, and this blueprint must be replaced.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan generally requires a Local File where annual revenue is at least NT$500 million or where annual related-party transaction amounts meet prescribed thresholds, commonly NT$200 million in aggregate, subject to transaction-type and regulatory exceptions. A Master File is generally required where the ultimate parent enterprise's consolidated annual revenue reaches NT$27 billion and the Taiwan entity has related-party transactions, subject to applicable exemptions. Country-by-Country reporting generally applies at the group level where consolidated revenue reaches NT$27 billion, with Taiwan filing or notification obligations depending on the reporting-entity structure.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a standalone OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations regime broadly equivalent to the regimes used in certain offshore financial centers. Substance remains relevant under Taiwan transfer-pricing rules, anti-avoidance provisions, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty-relief review, controlled foreign company rules, place-of-effective-management analysis, and the determination of whether services, risks, personnel, assets, and decision-making are actually located in Taiwan. A Taiwan BPO company should maintain local personnel, management authority, operational records, customer deliverables, data-security controls, premises or documented remote-work controls, and evidence that its functions and risks correspond to its remuneration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and fraudulent reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, late-payment interest, tax-evasion penalties, and potential criminal liability under Taiwan tax laws. Fraudulent tax evasion may expose the responsible person to imprisonment of up to five years and statutory fines that may reach NT$10 million, subject to the charged offense and court determination.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors, managers, accountants, and persons who ordered, participated in, or knowingly facilitated the conduct may face criminal prosecution, civil tax restitution, administrative disqualification consequences, and seizure or attachment of assets. Travel restrictions may arise during enforcement or criminal proceedings where legally ordered; imprisonment is offense- and judgment-dependent.
False invoicing and accounting-book violations

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of deductions or credits, additional tax, fines under tax and accounting legislation, possible business-registration consequences, and criminal prosecution where conduct constitutes fraud, false-documentation, or tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may incur joint or personal criminal and civil exposure where they authorized or knowingly tolerated false records. Corporate limited liability does not protect individuals from liability for their own acts or statutory breaches.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and non-arm's-length related-party payments

Statutory sanction: Tax authority reassessment to arm's-length income, denial or recharacterization of deductions, additional tax, interest, reporting penalties, and potential penalties for failure to submit documentation or for inaccurate disclosures.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face civil liability to the company or shareholders for deliberate or grossly negligent tax manipulation and may face criminal exposure if the arrangements include false documents, concealment, or intentional evasion.
Improper treaty or withholding-tax relief

Statutory sanction: The payer may be required to pay the unwithheld tax, interest, withholding penalties, and administrative fines. The recipient may lose treaty benefits and become subject to reassessment and anti-avoidance review.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons who knowingly approve unlawful nonwithholding or false treaty documentation may face tax, civil, and criminal exposure. Travel restrictions or imprisonment are possible only where separately ordered or where conduct satisfies a criminal offense.
Undeclared permanent establishment or unregistered Taiwan business activity

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax, VAT, withholding tax, business-registration penalties, interest, penalties for failure to file, and possible orders to regularize or cease unregistered activity.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible managers may be personally liable for statutory violations and civil restitution where they directed the unregistered activity. Corporate status does not eliminate personal liability for intentional or negligent breaches.
Personal-data, cybersecurity, and cross-border data-transfer violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, corrective orders, compensation claims, contractual damages, suspension or restriction of processing, and potential criminal consequences under Taiwan personal-data and sector-specific laws.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face civil claims, regulatory sanctions, and criminal exposure for intentional or negligent violations. Personal sanctions depend on the governing statute and the individual's participation.
Money laundering, sanctions evasion, and suspicious payment routing

Statutory sanction: Customer-due-diligence, recordkeeping, reporting, and suspicious-transaction failures may result in administrative fines and corrective orders. Money laundering may carry imprisonment of up to seven years and statutory fines up to NT$5 million, subject to the offense, proceeds, and court determination.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, compliance officers, and employees who knowingly facilitate laundering may face asset confiscation, civil restitution, criminal prosecution, travel restrictions, and imprisonment. Corporate limited liability does not shield persons who personally participate.
Unlawful capital distribution or profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unlawfully distributed amounts, director or officer liability, corporate-law penalties, tax reassessment, withholding-tax exposure, interest, and potential criminal consequences for fraudulent or improper distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors approving unlawful distributions may owe restitution to the company and creditors and may be subject to disqualification, civil damages, and criminal liability where fraud, insolvency misconduct, or false accounting is established.
Artificial shell-company and beneficial-ownership arrangements

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions and treaty benefits, reallocation of income, corporate income tax and withholding-tax reassessment, penalties, beneficial-owner investigations, and potential anti-money-laundering or tax-evasion prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face civil restitution, asset freezing or confiscation, travel restrictions, and imprisonment where the structure is used for fraud, laundering, or deliberate tax evasion.

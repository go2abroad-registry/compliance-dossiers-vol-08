Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Chamber of Commerce of the registered city (e.g., Bogotá Chamber of Commerce) through the Single Business and Social Registry (RUES); supervised by the Superintendencia de Sociedades.

Governing statute

Law 1258 of 2008 on the Simplified Stock Company (SAS), Articles 1–12; Colombian Commercial Code as supplemented; Decree 898 of 2020 on electronic registration and the Commercial Registry framework.

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (Private Constitution Document) for the S.A.S., accompanied by the certified bylaws.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an S.A.S. under Law 1258/2008. The incorporators may set authorized, subscribed, and paid-in capital arbitrarily, but any paid-in capital must be fully paid within two years from incorporation. If paid in cash, domiciliation in a Colombian bank account in the company's name is recommended; no escrow account is legally required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the Municipal Fire Department (Cuerpo de Bomberos); structural load inspection when storage floor load exceeds 250 kg/m²; occupational safety and health clearance validated by an ARL (Administradora de Riesgos Laborales) under the SG-SST system, covering emergency evacuation, ventilation, and workplace risk prevention.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal www.dian.gov.co; transactional e-services and e-invoicing validation through the MUISCA platform; foreign trade export declarations through the SICE/VUCE electronic customs window.

E-government filing portal

Domestic S.A.S.: the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) is assigned to the incorporation deed upon Chamber of Commerce registration, with RUT (Registro Único Tributario) enrollment completed in MUISCA accrediting the common-regime IVA status. Foreign entity: obtain a non-resident NIT by completing the RUT pre-registration in MUISCA, appointing a fiscal/legal representative domiciled in Colombia, and filing the notarized corporate documents duly apostilled, legalized and translated into Spanish by a certified translator; DIAN validates identity online or via consulate. The NIT-RUT key unifies CIT, IVA, customs, e-invoicing (CUFE) and foreign-exchange settlement records.

Foreign entity: obtain a non-resident NIT by completing the RUT pre-registration in MUISCA, appointing a fiscal/legal representative domiciled in Colombia, and filing the notarized corporate documents duly apostilled, legalized and translated into Spanish by a certified translator; DIAN validates identity online or via consulate. The NIT-RUT key unifies CIT, IVA, customs, e-invoicing (CUFE) and foreign-exchange settlement records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Colombian S.A.S. is a tax-resident corporation taxed on worldwide income under the Estatuto Tributario at the standard 35% CIT rate (2023 onwards); no pass-through, partnership, or territorial-residence election is available for corporations.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists; the S.A.S. is per se entity-level taxable. Available elections: (i) 'Régimen Simple de Tributación' (SIMPLE, Articles 908-916 E.T. as amended by Law 2010 of 2019) - an elective integrated annual payment combining income-tax components, IVA collection and ICA for entities with prior-year gross revenue below 100,000 UVT (approx. COP 4,979,900,000 in 2025) and no controlling economic group; (ii) income-tax consolidation for Colombian economic groups (Article 14 E.T.); (iii) optional deduction acceleration and fixed-asset investment incentives for digital infrastructure; and (iv) RUT-based election to apply the 0% IVA item as 'exportación de servicios' upon fulfilling Article 481 E.T. conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold for legal persons: any entity performing taxable acts in Colombia (supply of goods, services, digital/e-commerce sale or import) is mandatorily registered as a common-regime IVA responsible party in the RUT before commencing operations. For natural persons only, the simplified regime operates while prior-year gross income is below 80,000 UVT (approximately COP 3,983,920,000 for tax year 2025, UVT = COP 49,799) and no more than one commercial establishment is operated; the S.A.S. never qualifies for the natural-person simplified status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% IVA applies when tangible goods are physically exported under a definitive customs regime (DUA validated by DIAN), the exporter issues the electronic export invoice, and the proceeds are collected and settled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market when required. Services and digital products: 'exportación de servicios' under Article 481 E.T. and Decree 1625 of 2016 requires (i) the beneficiary to be a non-resident, (ii) exclusive use and consumption abroad with no link to local sale of goods, (iii) the provider to be a registered IVA responsible party, and (iv) a written contract evidencing the foreign beneficiary. Software engineering, IT, digital COD order-taking, customer-service and near-shore BPO/KPO operations fall within the eligible services, provided the Colombian entity has no local delivery to Colombian end-users.

  • DUA - Documento Único de Aduanas (electronic export declaration) accepted by DIAN customs in the SICE/VUCE foreign-trade window
  • Factura Electrónica de Exportación: electronic export invoice with CUFE and explicit 0% IVA itemization
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / international transport document evidencing the physical departure of the goods from Colombia
  • Declaración de Cambio / Inward Remittance Certificate: foreign-exchange settlement exhibit evidencing collection of the foreign-currency proceeds through a Colombian authorized intermediary
  • Export service agreement or purchase order (for services) identifying the offshore beneficiary, the scope of the service and confirming exclusive offshore use

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance under DIAN Resolution 165 of 2023: every VAT-responsible taxpayer, including COD e-commerce operators and exporters, must issue the electronic invoice (Factura Electrónica de Venta, FEV) with the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica). The XML is transmitted for 'validación previa' to DIAN in real time through the MUISCA API (SOAP web-service with the NIT digital certificate); DIAN returns the validation status before the document is delivered to the customer, and a graphical PDF with DIAN's QR code is provided. Export transactions use the factura electrónica de exportación channel; where the counterparty is in the mandatory e-documentation system, supporting documents must also be transmitted electronically. Failure to obtain validation or to transmit in real time invalidates the document as support for the buyer's input VAT credit and triggers the 1%-of-transaction penalty under Article 652 E.T.

DIAN cross-verifies RUT export status, CUFE integrity, SICE customs data, e-invoicing flows, local logistics delivery records and Banco de la República FX records in real time. Any mismatch or expiry of the 180-day collection window triggers: (i) retroactive re-characterization of the 0% export sale as a taxed domestic transaction at 19% plus default interest (Article 634 E.T.); (ii) the accuracy penalty of 100%-160% of the tax understated or over-claimed under Article 648 E.T.; (iii) the 1%-of-transaction invoicing penalty under Article 652 E.T. for defective or untimely FEV/CUFE issuance; (iv) customs sanctions, including percentage fines on FOB value for inaccurate export declarations and criminal 'contrabando' classification for fictitious exports; (v) criminal tax-fraud exposure under Law 1762 of 2015 ('defraudación fiscal'), extended to COD wire-remittance fraud predicates and money-laundering charges; (vi) exclusion from the SIMPLE regime and from confirmed 0%-rate export treatment for subsequent periods where outstanding tax debts or violated export conditions are not remedied.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso is freely convertible for current account transactions, including international trade settlement, service payments, and profit remittances, provided the underlying operations are properly documented and channeled through duly authorized foreign exchange intermediaries. Capital account operations, including direct foreign equity investment and financial loans, are subject to Colombian exchange market rules: funds must be routed through an authorized local bank, formally registered with Banco de la República, and declared on the corresponding foreign exchange forms. Profit remittance is legally possible for registered foreign investment after compliance with the investment registration and after all Colombian tax obligations are settled, but practical hurdles arise from documentary rigidity, the requirement that the investment be channeled through a regulated local financial entity, and periodic exchange declaration duties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds from the physical or digital sale of goods and services must be repatriated and channeled through the Colombian foreign exchange market within six months from the date of shipment, provided that the exporter has a valid export declaration (Declaración de Exportación) and that the proceeds are settled by an authorized local bank. Mandatory conversion into Colombian pesos is generally not required if the exporter maintains a registered foreign currency account in Colombia; however, the full commercial value must be credited to the local bank account or a foreign account declared as part of the forex regime. Exceptions apply for temporary exports, maquila programs, and free trade zones, but they must be documented in advance. Failure to surrender or channel in the prescribed timeline exposes the exporter to monetary sanctions, exchange market penalties, and possible loss of income tax deductions tied to the export activity.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (the Central Bank), acting jointly with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia for prudential oversight of authorized foreign exchange intermediaries and with the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) for customs enforcement and anti-money-laundering supervision on trade-related foreign exchange operations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File must be filed if the taxpayer's total income exceeds 135,000 UVT (approx. COP 5,900 million in 2025) or if the value of transactions with related parties exceeds 45,000 UVT; a TP information form (Form 120) is required for taxpayers with a net worth or income greater than 100,000 UVT and with related-party transaction volumes above 10,000 UVT.

Economic substance scope: Applies through Colombia's 'economic substance' jurisprudence and the 'reality' doctrine under the Tax Code (Estatuto Tributario). Since 2019, the Tax Reform (Ley 1943/2019, later recast in Ley 2010/2019) introduced a general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) that disregards transactions lacking business purpose or economic substance. For entities using the SAS model, the existence of payroll, physical or virtual offices, bank accounts, logistics contracts, and a real profit motive is essential; a shell entity with no commercial substance will be recharacterized, and DTA benefits may be denied under substance-over-form clauses.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Evasión Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty under Article 434 of the Criminal Code (Ley 599/2000): imprisonment of 48 to 108 months for evasion exceeding 200 monthly minimum wages. Tax reassessment of omitted tax plus 100% penalty and interest. Sanciones administrativas: fine up to 6,000 UVT for incorrect returns.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives and directors may be jointly liable for the corporation's tax debts when they are deemed responsible for the evasion (Art. 794 E.T.). Imprisonment and civil restitution for damages to the treasury; travel bans and preventive detention may be ordered.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DIAN can disregard the legal personality for tax purposes, asserting all income and credits at the Colombian source. The company may be dissolved by the superintendency (Supersociedades) for 'serious violation' of corporate purpose. Financial penalties: taxable income recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits because of LOB clauses, and a 10% surcharge on top of the ordinary tax if considered an 'artificial arrangement' under GAAR (Ley 2010/2019, Art. 869).

Director personal liability: Shareholders and managers who facilitate artificial structures face personal liability for unpaid taxes when the veil is pierced (statutory in SAS rules). Potential criminal sanctions under Article 434 for fraud. Prohibition from acting as corporate manager for up to 10 years; credit restrictions and freezing of personal assets.
AML / Cash-Intensive E-Commerce Violation

Statutory sanction: Criminal money-laundering offense (Art. 323 Criminal Code) with imprisonment of 9 to 15 years for laundering proceeds. Administrative fines from UIAF up to 200,000 minimum monthly wages (approx. COP 5,000 million). Operational suspension and cancellation of the company's commercial registry.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for implementing AML compliance programs face individual criminal liability if they knowingly ignore red flags, including unjustified wealth, cash pickup anomalies, and high-risk payment rails. Civil penalties and mandatory restitution; travel restrictions and asset forfeiture orders are commonly applied.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Filing Failure

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of 5% of the total related-party transaction amount, capped at 5,000 UVT; not filing at all imposes 10% of that amount. Understating income arising from non arm's-length pricing: adjustment of income plus 200% penalty on the excess tax avoided. Interest from the original due date applies.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives are jointly liable for the TP penalty when they sign the return without verifying compliance. The penalty is enforceable against personal assets if the corporation cannot pay. Criminal tax evasion charges may be added if the adjustment results in a significant underreported liability.

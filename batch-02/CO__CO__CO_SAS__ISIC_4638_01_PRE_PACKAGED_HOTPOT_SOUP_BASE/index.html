Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN), the unified Colombian tax and customs administration. Official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co (electronic services via MUISCA)

E-government filing portal

1) Obtain certified incorporation deed and bylaws, registered with the Chamber of Commerce. 2) Appoint a legal representative domiciled in Colombia and, if the beneficial owner is foreign, a local tax agent (apoderado) for service of process and fiscal notices. 3) File the pre-registration application with DIAN through the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) procedure, either online using the MUISCA platform or at a DIAN office, submitting the corporate documents, identification, and proof of domiciliary address in Colombia. 4) Receive the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and the RUT certificate. 5) Activate electronic signature and register the electronic invoicing system. A foreign entity may separately obtain a non-resident NIT for customs and VAT purposes before full incorporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None available. Colombia does not operate a check-the-box election or elective pass-through regime for S.A.S. entities. All S.A.S. companies are mandatorily treated as separate legal taxpayers subject to ordinary Colombian corporate income tax. Hybrid-mismatch preventive rules are applied automatically in accordance with OECD BEPS Action 2 and Colombian transfer-pricing/beneficial-ownership regulations; there is no domestic election to vary such classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

COP 0 – Not applicable for S.A.S.: every Colombian company, regardless of turnover, must register in the RUT, obtain a NIT, and be responsible for IVA as a 'responsable del régimen común'. The simplified-regime threshold of 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 160 million in 2025) applies only to natural persons, not to corporate entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods from Colombian customs territory are treated as excluded from IVA (0% rate) and create a right to claim refund of input VAT. To qualify, the S.A.S. must be the exporter of record, the goods must physically leave Colombia, the purchaser must be a non-resident, the customs export declaration (DEX) must be accepted by DIAN, and foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated within the statutory deadline. For processed food, including pre-packaged spicy tallow hotpot soup base, prior INVIMA sanitary authorization or free-sales certificate is required to validate export eligibility.

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX) accepted through DIAN/MUISCA
  • Commercial invoice with export wording and HS tariff classification
  • Bill of lading or air waybill
  • Packing list
  • INVIMA sanitary registration / free-sales certificate for processed food
  • Certificate of origin if required by the destination country
  • Bank foreign-exchange repatriation declaration or Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC)

Electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta) is mandatory for all taxpayers, including exporters. Invoices must be transmitted to DIAN through the MUISCA web service/API in real time; DIAN performs automated schema validation and assigns a unique CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) code. No physical fiscal stamp is used. Export sales are invoiced as 'Factura de Exportación' and must be linked, in the MUISCA system, to the corresponding Colombian customs export declaration (DEX).

DIAN cross-validates customs declarations, electronic export invoices, and bank foreign-exchange statements. If the zero-rating is found ineligible or documentation is deficient, DIAN will retroactively assess the 19% IVA, including interest under Article 634 of the Tax Code, plus a false-statement penalty ('sanción por inexactitud') under Article 648, which may reach a significant percentage of the tax understated. Where intentional fraud or smuggling is proven, criminal sanctions under Law 1762 of 2015 apply and any VAT refund obtained may be clawed back in full, together with inflation adjustments and additional penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a hybrid regulated free-market system (mercado cambiario) under which all payments, transfers, and credits arising from foreign trade in goods and services, foreign direct investment, foreign debt, and certain capital operations must be channeled through authorized local intermediaries. Current-account transactions, including export collections and import payments, are freely convertible in practice but subject to mandatory exchange-market channeling and statistical declaration to Banco de la República. Capital-account inflows, particularly foreign direct investment, are freely permitted without prior central bank authorization for most non-banking sectors, but a prior investment registration (Formulario No. 8, Inversión Directa en Colombia) is required to guarantee future dividend remittances and capital repatriation. Profit remittances abroad are allowed at any time after the corporate tax return is filed and the corresponding remittance tax (at rates of 7.5% under the standard Colombian treaty rate, or up to 20% for jurisdictions classified as tax havens or non-cooperative regimes) is paid; the remittance must be executed through a local authorized bank with the corresponding exchange declaration. No crawling-peg, multiple-exchange-rate, or forced-conversion regime exists for the COP; however, payment flows that bypass the mandatory channeling mechanism without a valid registered exemption are treated as exchange infractions with fines for the exporter and/or its legal representatives.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All merchandise export proceeds must be repatriated to Colombia and channeled through the regulated exchange market within a maximum term of six (6) months counted from the date of shipment (embarque) indicated in the export declaration, unless an extension is approved in advance by Banco de la República. There is no statutory percentage of proceeds that must be exchanged into Colombian pesos; the obligation is 100% channeling, which may be fulfilled either by (a) selling the foreign currency to an authorized local commercial bank, or (b) crediting the proceeds to a foreign account in the name of the exporter that has been previously registered with Banco de la República as a 'cuenta de compensación' (compensation account). The exporter must submit Formulario No. 1 (Declaración de Cambio por Exportaciones de Bienes) to the intermediary bank for every export collection within five (5) business days following receipt/sale of the foreign currency. Colombian exporters are not required to hold export receipts in escrow; nonetheless, un-collected or un-repatriated proceeds after the six-month term trigger the automatic obligation to re-channel and are subject to sanctions under the Exchange Regime Regulatory Act (Resolución Externa) and fines imposed by DIAN, in addition to disallowance of the VAT exemption and certain export incentive benefits.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), acting through the Departamento de Cambios Internacionales (DCI) and the Junta Directiva, as the sole monetary, foreign exchange, and international reserves authority; the foreign exchange regulatory regime is codified in Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83 and enforced jointly with the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) for documentary and sanctioning purposes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low, with material legal constraints. Colombian exchange-control rules require that export proceeds, import disbursements, foreign debt service, and foreign investment flows be channeled exclusively through locally authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries (i.e., commercial banks and non-banking exchange intermediaries). Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, or neobanks domiciled in the EU, UK, or USA are not authorized as Colombian exchange intermediaries and cannot receive the company's export collections without violating the six-month repatriation/channeling obligation. Such accounts may be used only as ancillary settlement tools for non-regulated ancillary expenses if never considered 'canalización obligatoria', creating an unacceptable compliance risk for the treasury; high-risk if used for supplier advances. The correct use of a fintech has to be limited to an FX *information aggregator* or *trade finance dashboard*, not as a primary account. A Colombian licensed digital bank (e.g., Nequi, Lulo Bank) cannot operate as an exchange intermediary either. Recommendation: integrate one local Tier-1 bank (Bancolombia or Banco de Bogotá) for channeling and one dedicated foreign USD clearing account in a US/EU correspondent bank only for mirror cash management, never for export settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign equity contribution to the Colombian SAS must be made exclusively via a regular commercial bank wire in USD/EUR with purpose code 'Equity Capital Contribution' (foreign direct investment). The sending investor must transfer the funds using SWIFT MT103 with the full corporate name, NIT (Tax ID number) of the SAS, and a documented narrative such as 'capital subscription in S.A.S. — Decree 1068 of 2015'. The receiving local bank will require the foreign investor to file Formulario No. 8 (Declaración de Inversión Directa en Colombia) with Banco de la República before or within five (5) business days of receipt; the form must be signed by the legal representative of the Colombian company and the investor. The bank will then issue a certificado de inversión (investment registration certificate) that is the prerequisite for future dividend remittances, capital repatriation, and against-wire matching of subsequent outflows. The capital injection cannot be settled as a trade payment (import of goods), and the account must be titled 'Capital Escrow Account' or 'Movilización de Capital' to avoid misclassification. The bank will also verify that the sender is the same entity named in the bylaws; if the sender differs, an assignment agreement supported by a local notarial deed and DIAN notification will be required.For the export distribution of pre-packaged hotpot soup base, the company will mostly disburse outbound trade and operating payments via (a) SWIFT MT103 for raw material supplier payments, freight forwarding fees, and royalties; and (b) documentary payment mechanisms for wholesale trade settlements, including irrevocable commercial Letters of Credit (LC) and documentary collections. When paying an overseas supplier, the local bank records the transaction as an import of goods or services using Formulario No. 2 (import of goods) or Formulario No. 3 (import of services) within five (5) business days. Trade settlements with buyers in China or LATAM should be structured under Incoterms 2020 (CIF or CFR) and accompanied by the sales invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and the export declaration (Exportación Definitiva). For outbound LC issuances, Colombian banks issue MT700 with confirmation requested in the beneficiary's country; the required tenor is typically 90–180 days from B/L date. The underlying commercial invoices must reference the ISIC 4638 category and the exact Harmonized System (HS) Code observed by the product (e.g., 2103.90 for sauces/seasonings). All outbound disbursements must be executed through the same Tier-1 bank used for inbound capital to preserve a transparent onboarding trail and to facilitate the Colombian bank's own exchange-control compliance.Forwards (FX Forwards) with a licensed local bank covering a confirmed commercial transaction, with a maximum maturity of 360 days and settled against actual underlying trade flows (delivery to the exporter).Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) referencing COP/USD or COP/EUR, permitted when the underlying trade exposure is not simultaneously satisfied by a physical COP settlement, subject to the bank's internal underlying exposure validation.FX Swaps (Cross-Currency Swaps) used to manage USD/COP cash flow mismatches from simultaneous export receipts and import payments, provided the bank performs a suitability test and records the swap in the exchange declarations for capital movement.Currency Options (vanilla calls/puts) for protection against adverse COP depreciation on projected export collections, with the premium paid in foreign currency or in COP and the option embedded in a registered derivative contract.Interest Rate and Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (only for the USD-denominated debt) permitted under Decree 2555/2010, subject to the identification of the underlying asset or liability.

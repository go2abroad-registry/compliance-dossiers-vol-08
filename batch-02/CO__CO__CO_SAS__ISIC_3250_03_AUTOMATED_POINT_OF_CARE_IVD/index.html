Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Colombian law. An S.A.S. may elect: (i) the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) under Law 1943/2018 if gross revenues do not exceed 80,000 UVT, subject to activity eligibility and opt-in before the tax period; (ii) the BIC (Sociedad de Beneficio e Interés Colectivo) designation for corporate purposes, which does not alter tax classification; and (iii) application of tax treaty benefits (including the Colombia-Spain double taxation treaty and Pacific Alliance provisions) via the beneficial-owner procedure before DIAN. Hybrid mismatch rules under Law 1819/2016 (Art. 56-3 ET) and CFC rules under Art. 882 ET apply mandatory adjustments and require no election

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general sales threshold — any entity engaged in the sale of taxable goods or services, or importing goods, must register in the RUT and in the VAT regime (régimen común). Small natural persons may qualify for the simplified regime only if not S.A.S. corporate entities and if they meet the 3,500 UVT gross revenue cap (2025: COP 3,500 × UVT 47,065 = COP 164,727,500); S.A.S. entities are always in the common regime irrespective of turnover, and e-invoicing obligation applies regardless of revenue level

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods legally defined under Art. 481 ET (customs exports) are zero-rated (rate 0%) and confer a right to VAT credit refund (devolución de saldos a favor) on direct and indirect inputs; services rendered to non-residents 'from Colombian territory for use exclusively abroad' (Art. 481(e) ET) and services associated with exported goods (Art. 481(a) ET) also qualify if performed directly by the exporter, and the manufacturing/assembly of medical devices for export under an international commercialization regime qualifies provided the goods physically leave Colombia and the foreign currency is properly collected

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX/BL) — electronic customs export declaration accepted via MUISCA
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airway Bill evidencing shipment from Colombia
  • Commercial invoice (factura comercial) issued by the exporter
  • Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas / Declaración de Cambio (form 634.1) evidencing FX receipt from abroad

Mandatory since 2019-2021 for all taxpayers: e-invoicing via DIAN-accredited software providers (facturación electrónica) requires real-time validation through MUISCA web-services (API), issuance of the XML invoice (factura electrónica de venta — FE) with a CUFE/CUDS verification code, and immediate transmission of a copy to DIAN, with digital stamping (QR + CUFE code); the 'Régimen de Facturación Electrónica' also mandates the electronic 'nota crédito/débito' and the 'Documento Soporte en Adquisiciones' for purchases from non-invoicing parties

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is fully convertible under a floating, market-determined exchange rate. Colombia does not impose prior-approval exchange controls, but statutorily requires both current-account and capital-account operations with non-residents to be channeled through IMCs and reported to the Banco de la República on statutory Declaraciones de Cambio. For the CO_SAS, the practical convertibility hurdles are: (i) foreign direct investment must be registered via Formulario No. 2 to create the legal right to future dividend remittances and capital repatriation; (ii) current-account payments (royalties, services, dividends) require underlying commercial documentation; and (iii) dividend outflows to non-resident shareholders attract 5% withholding tax at the shareholder level when paid from taxed corporate earnings, with higher taxation for distributions sourced from exempt income or from unregistered investments. Non-channeling or late registration triggers fines and sanctions under the Régimen Cambiario and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia imposes no mandatory conversion ratio or forced surrender of export receipts; the legal duty is repatriation and channeling through the foreign-exchange market. Export proceeds from IVD equipment, microfluidic cartridges and immunoassay analyzers must be collected and received by the exporter within six (6) months of the date of the definitive export declaration (Declaración de Exportación), and must be brought into a local account at an IMC, sold to an IMC, or placed in a Banco de la República-registered foreign-currency account. The exporter must file Formulario No. 5 (Declaración de Cambio por Exportaciones) with the IMC, attaching the export declaration and transport/title documents. Proceeds may be retained in foreign currency without conversion, but any later disposition to Colombian residents must be executed through an IMC. Failure to repatriate within the statutory term or use of unregistered collection routes constitutes an infracción cambiaria punishable by fines up to 200% of the transaction amount.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), which administers the foreign-exchange control regime under Law 9/1991 and Decree 2245/2011, operating through licensed Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario (IMCs) supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC), with the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) enforcing customs and exchange-linked tax obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW (auxiliary only). Colombian foreign-exchange law does not recognize offshore digital EMIs or neobanks (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Payoneer) as IMCs; they cannot serve as the primary rail for channeling export proceeds or settling import obligations. Export proceeds held abroad in an unregistered EMI account breach the channeling duty and must be repatriated to an IMC within the six-month statutory term, with the external account itself registered before the Banco de la República. Offshore EMIs are tolerable only for incidental micro-collections from international distributors, provided the funds are immediately followed by a compliant SWIFT MT103 credit to the local IMC account. Using an unregistered offshore EMI as the main treasury collector is treated by Colombian IMCs as a foreign-exchange violation and an AML/KYC reputational blocker.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) the foreign shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 from its own banking institution to the CO_SAS operational account at a Colombian IMC, naming the S.A.S. with its full corporate name and NIT, and referencing 'Aporte de capital social - Inversión Extranjera Directa' in the payment details; (2) the IMC verifies the certified shareholders' resolution approving the capital increase, the updated RUT and the current Cámara de Comercio certificate before accepting the funds; (3) within the registration window (the last banking day of the month following channeling), the IMC files Formulario No. 2 (Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales) with the Banco de la República, generating the investment registration that is the legal basis for future dividend remittances and capital repatriation; (4) wire transfers exceeding USD 10,000 or the equivalent in COP activate enhanced due-diligence and same-day notification duties under the SFC rules; and (5) non-monetary capital contributions (equipment, technology, intellectual property) require a sworn independent valuation and a separate registration before being booked as paid-in capital.Clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer (TT) from the local IMC account to foreign suppliers for recurring purchases of microfluidic polymers, reagents, analyzer components and laboratory consumables; and (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700/MT707) opened by the Colombian IMC in favor of the supplier bank for contract manufacturing of microfluidic cartridges and immunoassay analyzers, with sight or usance payment terms against compliant documents (commercial invoice, bill of lading, inspection or quality certificates). Before any outbound TT, the CO_SAS must hold a valid DIAN import declaration or register the remittance as an advance payment (anticipo) for goods or services; the IMC files the corresponding Declaración de Cambio (Formulario No. 4 for goods imports, Formulario No. 6 for services) and applies applicable withholding taxes (generally 15% on cross-border services and the 5% dividend withholding regime for distributions to non-residents). All LC disbursements and TT payments must remain traceable to the underlying import transaction and aligned with the customs values declared to DIAN, with full documentary retention for the five-year statute of limitations under the Colombian exchange-control and tax regimes.OTC US Dollar/Colombian Peso forward contracts (forwards) entered into with a licensed IMC or through an onshore IMC with an offshore bank, requiring documentary evidence of an underlying commercial exposure (trade receivables or payables) and daily mark-to-market; physical delivery of COP or net cash settlement is permissible.Non-deliverable forwards (NDF) on USD/COP, settled in COP or in a foreign currency, available for hedging USD-denominated trade flows where physical settlement is impracticable; permitted for commercial hedging purposes under the flexibilized derivative framework of the Banco de la República.Cross-currency swaps and FX swaps between COP and USD/EUR, permissible for transforming the currency denomination of foreign-currency debt or capital account positions, subject to the IMC's credit approval, internal limits and registration with the Banco de la República when executed onshore.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW (auxiliary only). Colombian foreign-exchange law does not recognize offshore digital EMIs or neobanks (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Payoneer) as IMCs; they cannot serve as the primary rail for channeling export proceeds or settling import obligations. Export proceeds held abroad in an unregistered EMI account breach the channeling duty and must be repatriated to an IMC within the six-month statutory term, with the external account itself registered before the Banco de la República. Offshore EMIs are tolerable only for incidental micro-collections from international distributors, provided the funds are immediately followed by a compliant SWIFT MT103 credit to the local IMC account. Using an unregistered offshore EMI as the main treasury collector is treated by Colombian IMCs as a foreign-exchange violation and an AML/KYC reputational blocker.

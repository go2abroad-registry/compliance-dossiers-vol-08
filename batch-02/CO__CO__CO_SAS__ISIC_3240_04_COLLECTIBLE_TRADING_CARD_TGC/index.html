Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. The subscribed capital may be paid in full or in installments over a maximum of two (2) years. No escrow or bank deposit is required prior to registration; capital amounts and payment terms must be stated in the by-laws.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the local Cuerpo de Bomberos (fire brigade) after mandatory inspection of the premises; structural load certificate signed by a licensed civil engineer confirming that the printing machinery can be safely supported; electrical installation certificate prepared by an authorized electrician for the industrial power supply; occupational health and safety clearance under the Sistema de Gestión de Seguridad y Salud en el Trabajo (SG-SST) required by Decree 1072 of 2015, including an employer affiliation to a professional risk insurance (ARL) and a medical and hygiene protocol for workers exposed to inks, airborne particulates, and noise.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital. The subscribed capital may be paid in full or in installments over a maximum of two (2) years. No escrow or bank deposit is required prior to registration; capital amounts and payment terms must be stated in the by-laws.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - sole Colombian authority for national taxes, customs and foreign trade. Official portal: www.dian.gov.co; electronic registration and filing services: MUISCA (muisca.dian.gov.co); e-invoicing validation platform: RADIAN e-invoice register.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Colombian S.A.S. must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and RUT (Registro Único Tributario) as follows: (1) incorporate the S.A.S. by private written document registered in the local Cámara de Comercio - the Chamber's 'Sociedades en Línea' service automatically assigns a provisional NIT; (2) within the statutory month after incorporation, the legal representative (domiciled in Colombia) must access MUISCA and officially activate the NIT by submitting the complementary RUT data, electing the responsibility codes for income tax ('D-11 contribuyente declarante de renta y complementarios'), VAT common regime ('I-04 responsable del IVA'), and foreign trade ('C-44' where applicable); (3) in parallel, register in the VUCE (Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior) with DIAN customs and obtain the exporter registration for the physical exports of printed trading cards; (4) a foreign juridical person without local establishment obtains a non-resident NIT through a Colombian fiscal agent filing the RUT in MUISCA with a power of attorney and certificate of incorporation; (5) after RUT activation the S.A.S. must be habilitated for electronic invoicing in the same MUISCA module before issuing any sales document.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a S.A.S. is a separate juridical person (Law 1258/2008, C. Co. Arts. 98-106) with limited liability; for tax purposes it is a resident legal entity (régimen común) taxed in its own name under the Colombian income tax regime (E.T. Arts. 20-27, 55, 240). Tax base: worldwide net income with foreign tax credit for taxes paid abroad on foreign-source income; Colombian-source income from the local printing and card publishing operations is fully taxable at the 35% CIT rate (2025 fiscal year). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a S.A.S. is a separate juridical person (Law 1258/2008, C. Co. Arts. 98-106) with limited liability; for tax purposes it is a resident legal entity (régimen común) taxed in its own name under the Colombian income tax regime (E.T. Arts. 20-27, 55, 240). Tax base: worldwide net income with foreign tax credit for taxes paid abroad on foreign-source income; Colombian-source income from the local printing and card publishing operations is fully taxable at the 35% CIT rate (2025 fiscal year). The S.A.S. has no pass-through or disregarded-entity classification and no territorial election; non-resident shareholders receiving dividends incur an additional withholding of 20% on the post-CIT dividend (remittance tax), and the 35% corporate rate plus the 20% shareholder-level levy approximate an aggregate 48% effective tax on distributed profits.

Available election forms

Colombia has no 'check-the-box' entity election; classification is fixed by statute and substance. Available optional elections for this manufacturing exporter: (1) adoption of the optional aggregated tax regime 'Régimen Simple de Tributación - SIMPLE' (E.T. Art. 908, Law 2010/2019) for legal entities with prior-year gross revenue up to COP 3,500 million, replacing income tax, VAT surcharge and municipal industry-and-commerce tax with a single SIMPLE payment - election must be filed in MUISCA before 31 January of the effective year, is irrevocable for at least 2 years, and is generally unsuitable for a capital-intensive export manufacturer because SIMPLE's presumptive revenue-based charge does not allow full deduction for cost of exported goods nor equivalent carry-forward of input VAT credits; (2) fiscal consolidation (E.T. Arts. 29-31 and Decree 1874/2021) permitting a head S.A.S. owning more than 50% of Colombian subsidiaries to apply to DIAN for consolidated national income tax (a group-level election binding for 3 years); (3) the neutrality vehicle regime (E.T. Arts. 35-38) available for S.A.S. vehicles dedicated to share acquisition in Colombian companies; (4) classification of foreign subsidiaries under the Colombian controlled foreign corporation (CFC) rules (E.T. Art. 882) is by a functional analog method without elective recharacterisation; (5) free trade zone user election ('usuario industrial de Zona Franca') may be sought for the printed-card production flow, conferring reduced CIT of 20% and favourable export VAT treatments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating currency regime under a statutory foreign-exchange market (Mercado Cambiario, MCR). Current-account operations (exports, imports, services, royalties, dividends, interest) are freely convertible and channeled through authorized local intermediaries, subject to exchange-declaration reporting. Capital-account operations (foreign direct investment, foreign loans, portfolio investment) are likewise permitted without exchange licenses, but must be registered with the Banco de la República and reported on the corresponding foreign investment forms. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are legal and unrestricted provided the foreign investment has been duly registered and all applicable Colombian corporate and dividend taxes have been settled; no prior administrative authorization is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, the exporter must repatriate and channel the foreign-currency proceeds through an authorized Colombian bank within six (6) months from the date of the export customs declaration; this term may be extended under the rules of the Banco de la República. Service exports generally enjoy a collection period of up to twelve (12) months. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; Colombian law does not force exporters to sell foreign exchange to the central bank. The exporter may lawfully maintain export collections in a foreign-currency deposit account either in Colombia or abroad, but any conversion into Colombian pesos must be executed through the authorized exchange market and recorded on the corresponding exchange declaration (Declaración de Cambio) as a channeled foreign-exchange transaction.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (the Colombian Central Bank), acting jointly with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) in the administration of the exchange control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium. Non-resident digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or other offshore electronic-money institutions cannot operate as deposit-taking entities in Colombia and are not participants in the regulated Mercado Cambiario. They may be used only as auxiliary payment notification tools or ancillary collection collectors, but cannot serve as the primary treasury clearing rail or provide lawful FX channeling for Colombian export proceeds. A locally licensed SEDPE (Sociedad Especializada en Depósito y Pago Electrónico) or a regulated local bank remains mandatory for lawful foreign-exchange settlement.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Apostilled certificate of incorporation, current bylaws, full shareholders registry, updated UBO (beneficial-ownership) declaration, notarized and apostilled power of attorney for the local representative, corporate resolution authorizing account opening, and a sworn statement of lawful source of funds.

Defense evidence: Commercial contracts with global distributors, authenticated purchase orders, pro-forma invoices, warehouse receipts, bills of lading or air waybills, customs export declarations, bankable evidence of the underlying commercial reality, and audited trial balances demonstrating operational consistency.
Unregistered foreign direct investment and imminent large international wire transfers

Root cause: Unregistered foreign direct investment and imminent large international wire transfers

Lack of historic financial statements or short lifecycle of the operating subsidiary

Root cause: Lack of historic financial statements or short lifecycle of the operating subsidiary

Defense evidence: Opening-date balance sheet, projected cash-flow and budget model, estimated P&L for first 12 months, compliance with local accounting standards (NIC/NIIF as applicable), and tax filings or negative-tax declarations (Formulario 100 or 110) evidencing exposure to the Colombian tax administration.

Inbound capital injections from the foreign parent must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the company's designated operational account at the chosen Tier-1 Colombian bank. The wire must reference the official foreign-investment purpose code corresponding to direct foreign equity participation (Registro de Inversión Internacional Directa) and the bank will deliver a certificado de canalización evidencing lawful entry into the exchange market. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File / Master File documentation for taxpayers with gross revenues or gross assets exceeding 100,000 UVTs (approximately COP 4.7 billion for fiscal year 2024) and that have transactions with foreign related parties exceeding 10,000 UVTs per related party. A simpler characterization report (Declaración Informativa de Precio de Transferencia, Formulario 120) is required when revenues or assets are at least 50,000 UVTs.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) in the Gulf-country sense, but the substantive business requirement is enforced through the generally anticipated anti-avoidance rule (‘Abuso en Materia Tributaria’) and the corporate law doctrine of ‘desestimación de la personalidad jurídica.’ To pass, the S.A.S. must have its own office, operational staff, contracts, bank accounts and decision-making authority in Colombia; a purely holding or invoicing entity without commercial substance will be recharacterized and may be subject to 200% surcharge on underpaid income tax plus interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Prison sentence of 4 to 9 years under Article 356 of the Colombian Criminal Code, plus a fine equal to 100%–150% of the evaded tax. Civil administrative penalty may increase the evaded tax by 200% plus default interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who authorise false declarations become personally liable; the corporate veil is pierced for restitution, and the enforcing court may issue travel bans and detention orders.
Shell Company / Artificial Structuring

Statutory sanction: DIAN is empowered to disregard the corporate entity under the general anti-abuse rule, reallocate all income to the controlling non-resident, impose the 200% punitive understatement penalty, and disallow deductions for sham transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintain a shell structure risk joint and several liability for the full tax deficiency; a civil asset freeze and a prohibition on leaving Colombia can be imposed during the investigation.
Failure to Withhold and Pay Dividend/Royalty Tax

Statutory sanction: The S.A.S. becomes personally liable as withholding agent for the entire underpament plus interest and a 20% late-payment penalty per month (capped at 100%). If the omission exceeds 1,000 UVTs, criminal liability for tax evasion attaches.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives who sign distribution minutes without knowing the beneficial owner’s residence are personally liable for the unpaid withholding; travel bans and disqualification from conducting commercial activities for up to 10 years may apply.
Transfer Pricing Noncompliance

Statutory sanction: DIAN will re-determine the taxable income at market price and impose a 75% understatement penalty on the additional tax attributed to the gap; the taxpayer also forfeits any right to carry forward losses that underlie the manipulated transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the TP penalty when they have signed the related party agreements or knowingly submit incomplete TP documentation; imprisonment is available when the penalty exceeds 1,500 UVTs and intent to evade is proven.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a securities brokerage licensed by the Financial Supervisory Commission, virtual office and commercial mailbox arrangements are categorically impermissible. The FSC requires a physical, exclusive office with adequate security, record-keeping facilities, and personnel on-site for the conduct of client-facing brokerage activities. Bank account opening for a brokerage also mandates a physical branch visit and a registered address. Therefore, a virtual office will be rejected by both the FSC for licensing and the tax authorities for VAT/business tax registration, and banking partners will decline to open segregated client accounts.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Taiwan Company Act does not prescribe a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a limited liability company at formation; the subscribed capital must be reasonably sufficient for the proposed business and must be deposited in a Taiwan bank account evidenced by a capital confirmation certificate. No capital is blocked in escrow; after incorporation the funds are available for corporate use, but the company may not deploy capital for regulated client brokerage activity before final FSC licensing. As a securities broker, the capital position must satisfy the FSC's applicable paid-in-capital and working capital requirements for securities firms.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance is required for the occupied office under the Fire Services Act, including fire extinguishers, emergency lighting, evacuation signage, exit routes and building management fire-protection documentation. Structural load inspections are not applicable to normal office occupancy beyond the building use permit. Under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, the employer must conduct an occupational risk assessment, provide first-aid equipment, maintain electrical safety, record occupational accidents and obtain any required workplace safety approval for the office, even for low-risk financial services staff.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; official portal: www.chinatax.gov.cn; electronic filing through the Unified Electronic Tax Bureau (电子税务局) at etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

The WFOE obtains its statutory taxpayer identification number through inter-departmental data sharing after business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The unified social credit code issued on the business license serves as the legal taxpayer identification number (TIN). The WFOE must then complete tax-registration profiling on the Electronic Tax Bureau, including taxpayer-type confirmation, VAT identity, corporate income tax filing cycles, and payroll social-insurance registration. A foreign-invested enterprise must additionally file the foreign investor's corporate identity certificate, articles of association, and the resolution appointing the legal representative and tax handling person. Registration is evidenced by an online 'Tax Registration Notice' or the electronic tax registration certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not implement a 'check-the-box' pass-through regime; the WFOE is a separate Chinese corporate-income-tax taxpayer. Available statutory elections include: (i) the election to remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer or to register voluntarily as a general VAT taxpayer when annual taxable sales do not exceed RMB 5,000,000; (ii) the election of reduced corporate-income-tax rate applicable to Small Low-Profit Enterprises, provided statutory criteria are met; and (iii) the election to apply zero-rating or VAT exemption to qualifying cross-border services through STA record-filing. Hybrid-mismatch elections are not recognised under Chinese domestic law; China applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to address hybrid structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual VAT-taxable sales. A taxpayer exceeding this threshold must register as a general VAT taxpayer (standard rate 6% for brokerage services, 13% for goods). Taxpayers at or below the threshold may remain small-scale taxpayers subject to simplified collection rates (currently 1% or 3%, subject to temporary relief policies). As a WFOE with a business license, initial tax registration is mandatory irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating may be claimed for offshore securities brokerage services supplied to overseas clients where the offshore character is clearly established: the client is located outside the PRC customs territory, the service performance and consumption occur outside China, and the consideration is received in foreign exchange. The WFOE must first complete the cross-border taxable services record-filing with the local STA office. If the service is classified within the zero-rated cross-border services scope of Caishui [2016] No. 36, the export service is treated at 0% VAT; if it falls within the exempt cross-border services scope, the service is VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated. The final characterization is subject to STA review of the underlying contract, service-consumption documentation, and foreign-exchange records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口货物报关单) required for goods; for offshore services the equivalent evidence is the cross-border service contract, service-performance certificate, and offshore-consumption statement
  • Bill of Lading or transport/performance document (海运提单/物流凭证) evidencing export of goods; for pure offshore brokerage services, trade confirmations, client order records, and service-delivery logs are substituted
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange receipt record (涉外收入申报单/银行收汇凭证) proving receipt of foreign currency from the offshore client and proper repatriation

China mandates electronic invoice issuance and real-time data reconciliation under the 'Golden Tax Phase IV' system. General VAT taxpayers must use the regulated VAT invoice system (增值税发票管理系统) to issue special VAT invoices (增值税专用发票) and general invoices. All invoice data is uploaded in near real-time to the centralized STA database. The nationwide 'full digital electronic invoice' (全电发票) reform further requires the use of digitally standardised invoices subject to automated fiscal clearance; traditional paper counterfoils are progressively replaced by electronic records. There is no physical 'digital stamp' on each invoice, but the issuance process must clear the STA's tax-control validation system.

Export zero-rating and VAT-refund claims are subject to automated risk-scoring and post-refund audits. Where a claim is found to be fraudulent or unsupported, the STA will retrospectively claw back the refunded VAT plus statutory late-payment interest, impose administrative penalties of 50% up to five times the evaded tax, revoke the WFOE's export-refund qualification, and move the taxpayer to a credit-risk blacklist. Willful fabrication of export or cross-border-service documentation may be referred for criminal prosecution under Article 204 of the Criminal Law on fraudulent export tax refunds, carrying penalties from criminal fines to imprisonment, up to life imprisonment in aggravated cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains capital account controls with limited convertibility; current account transactions are generally convertible. Profit remittances from the WFOE to the Taiwan parent require SAFE approval, supporting documentation including audited financials and trade records, and are subject to bilateral China-Taiwan economic cooperation framework constraints including potential exchange rate and repatriation hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be converted to RMB and surrendered to a designated foreign exchange bank within five working days; mandatory surrender applies to trade-related foreign exchange earnings at the bank's prevailing rate, with non-compliance triggering penalties and operational restrictions.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN), Colombia's unified tax and customs authority, administered through the Registro Único Tributario (RUT), the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), the Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV) platform, and the SIGLO XXI/VUCE customs systems. Official portal: www.dian.gov.co. Foreign-exchange controls and export proceed repatriation requirements are supervised by Banco de la República.

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian S.A.S. owned by non-resident shareholders: (1) incorporate the S.A.S. before the Cámara de Comercio with a locally appointed legal representative; (2) within one month of commercial registration, complete the RUT registration on the DIAN portal, stating ISIC 4631 and declaring status as an exporter, obtaining a provisional NIT that becomes definitive upon DIAN validation; (3) obtain the company's NIT and a digital signature certificate (certificado digital) for electronic tax filings and e-invoicing; (4) register the entity in the RUT as a VAT responsible (Responsable del IVA) and an income-tax declarant; (5) for a non-resident juridical owner without a local establishment, a simplified NIT may be issued pursuant to Decreto 2788/2013 and applicable DIAN resolutions, subject to appointment of a local representative, a notarised and apostilled power of attorney, and disclosure of the beneficial owner; (6) request customs authorisation and exporter activation in the RUT before lodging any export declaration.

Territorial_Scope applies only to non-resident entities and foreign-company branches, which are taxed exclusively on Colombian-source income rather than worldwide income. — For a Colombian S.A.S. owned by non-resident shareholders: (1) incorporate the S.A.S. before the Cámara de Comercio with a locally appointed legal representative; (2) within one month of commercial registration, complete the RUT registration on the DIAN portal, stating ISIC 4631 and declaring status as an exporter, obtaining a provisional NIT that becomes definitive upon DIAN validation; (3) obtain the company's NIT and a digital signature certificate (certificado digital) for electronic tax filings and e-invoicing; (4) register the entity in the RUT as a VAT responsible (Responsable del IVA) and an income-tax declarant; (5) for a non-resident juridical owner without a local establishment, a simplified NIT may be issued pursuant to Decreto 2788/2013 and applicable DIAN resolutions, subject to appointment of a local representative, a notarised and apostilled power of attorney, and disclosure of the beneficial owner; (6) request customs authorisation and exporter activation in the RUT before lodging any export declaration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the S.A.S. is taxed at entity level on its worldwide income under the ordinary corporate income tax regime; shareholders are not attributed the company's income; dividends and profit distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to Colombian-source dividend/withholding taxation. Territorial_Scope applies only to non-resident entities and foreign-company branches, which are taxed exclusively on Colombian-source income rather than worldwide income.

Available election forms

Colombia has no US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. Statutory elections available to an S.A.S. include: (i) the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) under Law 2010/2019, electable by qualifying taxpayers whose prior-year gross revenue does not exceed 80,000 UVT; (ii) the Compañía Holding Colombiana (CHC) election, notified to DIAN, which allows qualified foreign-source dividends and capital gains to be exempt from Colombian withholding taxation; (iii) neutral tax treatment upon election for qualified business reorganisations (mergers, split-offs) under Articles 31–35 of the Tax Statute, deferring income recognition if the economic-substance condition is met; hybrid-mismatch consequences are imposed by law under the Law 2277/2022 anti-hybrid rules, not by taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Legal entities such as the S.A.S. are automatically VAT-responsible with no minimum turnover threshold. Natural persons become VAT-responsible when prior-year gross income from taxable activities exceeds 100,000 UVT (approximately COP 4.98 billion based on the 2025 UVT of COP 49,799); below that threshold natural persons may voluntarily register to obtain export-refund eligibility. Exporters of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals remain VAT-responsible and must file periodic IVA returns even though their exports are invoiced at 0%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 481 of the Tax Statute, exports of tangible goods from Colombian customs territory qualify for 0% VAT when the S.A.S. owns the goods at shipment, title passes to a non-resident purchaser, physical exit is proved through official customs procedures, and the export proceeds are collected in accordance with the foreign-exchange regime. Sales to local Colombian intermediaries that will themselves export later are domestic sales taxed at 19% IVA. Offshore services rendered from Colombia to non-residents are also zero-rated when not economically connected to Colombian territory. For dried goji berries and herbal botanicals, INVIMA/ICA sanitary and phytosanitary documentation is required, as the VAT benefit presumes lawful and regular export operations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX – Declaración de Exportación), filed with DIAN's SIGLO XXI/VUCE customs system, validated by customs authorities, and stamped as the shipment physically leaves Colombian customs territory
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill) issued by the carrier evidencing dispatch abroad from a Colombian authorised port/airport and transfer of possession to the non-resident buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Declaración de Cambio submitted through an authorised foreign-exchange intermediary) evidencing collection/repatriation of the export proceeds in conformity with Banco de la República regulations

Every VAT-responsible taxpayer must issue the Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV). Once habilitated, the S.A.S. transmits FEV XML (Schema 1.0 and subsequent DIAN versions) to DIAN in real/near-real time through SOAP/API web services or the DIAN e-invoicing portal; each invoice must contain a unique CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) generated from a DIAN-assigned seed and be signed with the taxpayer's digital certificate. DIAN returns an acceptance receipt (acuse de recibo) that constitutes the digital stamp and confers fiscal validity. Export sales are issued as FEV 'Exportación 0%' and must be reconciled with the customs export declaration (DEX) and transport manifest prior to physical dispatch.

DIAN cross-verifies the DEX, the FEV 0% export invoice, the cargo manifest, customs exit markings, and Banco de la República exchange declarations to prove actual shipment, title shift, and remittance timing. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day statutory deadline may render the zero-rated refund inadmissible and trigger foreign-exchange penalties. If the export is simulated, unsuccessful, or supported by false documentation, the exporter's claimed VAT refund is retrospectively clawed back with moratory interest and the taxpayer may be assessed the Article 648 inaccuracy penalty of 100% (up to 200%) of the tax understated or refund obtained, in addition to Article 660/661 late-filing penalties. Fraudulent export conduct also exposes the entity and its legal representatives to criminal liability for contrabando under Law 1762/2015, with imprisonment of 3 to 10 years, asset seizure, and an extended 10-year statute of limitations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia applies a free-floating exchange rate regime. Current account operations (export/import of goods and services) are freely convertible but must be channeled exclusively through an IMC or a registered foreign-currency/compensation account. Capital account inflows (foreign direct investment) are also freely permitted without prior authorization, but must be registered with Banco de la República through the direttive investment registration mechanism to guarantee profit and capital repatriation rights; undistributed earnings, royalties, and dividends may be remitted abroad after compliance with Colombian corporate/tax formalities and registration updates, subject to presentation of the corresponding exchange declaration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be surrendered to the Colombian foreign exchange market (canalización obligatoria) by the SAS within six (6) months following the date of shipment (fecha de embarque) per Resolución 8 de 2000 as amended by Decreto 810 de 2021. No compulsory conversion ratio into COP applies; 100% of the export invoice proceeds may be held in USD/EUR or another freely convertible currency in a Colombian foreign-currency account or in a compensation account (cuenta de compensación) abroad registered with Banco de la República, but the funds must be formally channeled through an IMC, reconciled with the export customs declaration, and evidenced by the related Declaración de Cambio form filed with the intermediary bank. Non-channeled or late-channeled proceeds trigger fines, disallowance of tax benefits, and potential FX violation sanctions.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), acting through its Junta Directiva as the Comisión de Cambios, which issues the foreign exchange regime, principally Resolución 8 de 2000 (as amended), and supervises the Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario (IMC) for all mandatory channeling, exchange declarations, and international investment registration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary clearing of Colombian export revenue; MEDIUM only as an ancillary treasury interface. Offshore fintech EMIs and electronic money institutions are not eligible Colombian IMCs and therefore cannot legally receive or pay COP through the local ACH/CUD clearing system, cannot register exchange declarations, and cannot hold the SAS's mandatory Export Proceeds channeling representation. Foreign e-wallet providers may be used for invoice reconciliation, client payment initiation, and settlement convenience for non-resident buyers, but every movement touching the Colombian SAS must ultimately flow into or out of a Colombian licensed IMC bank account; funds cannot be deemed channeled merely by passing through an offshore EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign direct investment must be credited exclusively through a Colombian IMC bank account. The incoming SWIFT MT103/MT202 should be denominated 'REGISTRO INVERSION INTERNACIONAL – CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' and include the S.A.S. NIT, stated purpose code corresponding to direct investment under Resolución 8/2000, as well as the foreign investor's name. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due in the calendar year following the fiscal year-end according to DIAN Resolution schedule, generally between 11 March and 15 April for calendar-year taxpayers; exact date is determined by the last two digits of the NIT (tax ID). For non-calendar fiscal years, one year from the closing date of the taxable period, aligned with DIAN calendar rules.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form 120 must be filed no later than the final date for submitting Form 110, generally in April following the fiscal year-end to which the transactions refer.

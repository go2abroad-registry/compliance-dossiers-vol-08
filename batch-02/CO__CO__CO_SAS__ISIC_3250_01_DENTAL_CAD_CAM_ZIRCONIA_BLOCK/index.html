Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - the national tax and customs administration of the Republic of Colombia. Official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; taxpayer transactional portal: https://muisca.dian.gov.co; electronic invoicing and validation services operate through the DIAN Sede Electrónica and the e-invoicing habilitación platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent establishing a Colombian S.A.S. must procure the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through the following sequence: (i) incorporate the S.A.S. before the local Cámara de Comercio and register the legal representative; non-resident shareholders may own 100% of the shares and no local partner is required; (ii) file the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) application in DIAN's Muisca portal, attaching the certificate of existence and legal representation, the apostilled and Spanish-translated corporate charter, the power of attorney appointing a Colombian fiscal agent if applicable, and proof of the registered office; (iii) obtain the definitive NIT, which is the single statutory taxpayer identifier for income tax, VAT, withholding tax, customs and electronic invoicing; (iv) update the RUT with the applicable economic activity codes, including ISIC 3250 / NAICS 339114, and confirm classification as a Régimen Común VAT taxpayer and exporter; and (v) activate the electronic signature and obtain DIAN authorization for issuing Factura Electrónica de Venta prior to any invoicing, import or export transaction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election; an S.A.S. is mandatorily taxed at entity level on worldwide income. Available statutory elections include: (i) the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) under Articles 903 to 916 of the Estatuto Tributario, optional for S.A.S. entities with gross receipts not exceeding 80,000 UVT (COP 3,983,920,000 using UVT 2025 of COP 49,799), which replaces ordinary CIT and VAT settlement with a consolidated presumptive contribution; (ii) the VAT group consolidation election for economically integrated related entities under Decree 1762 of 2020, subject to DIAN authorization; and (iii) optional presumptive income, depreciation and inventory regimes under the Estatuto Tributario. Hybrid mismatch and permanent establishment anti-abuse provisions applicable in Colombia under Law 2010 of 2019 and Decree 1291 of 2020 operate as mandatory rules rather than taxpayer elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold: a corporate S.A.S. is legally required to register as a Régimen Común VAT taxpayer from the first taxable operation, including an export, irrespective of revenue volume. For natural persons only, the simplified VAT regime threshold is annual gross ordinary income not exceeding 3,500 UVT (COP 174,296,500 for 2025), but this election is not available to an S.A.S. exporter of dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks are eligible for the 0% VAT rate under Article 481 numeral 1 of the Estatuto Tributario and Article 17 of Decree 1165 of 2019 where: (i) the goods are sold to a non-resident purchaser and are definitively removed from Colombian customs territory; (ii) the exporter holds a valid RUT/NIT and the INVIMA sanitary authorization applicable to biocompatible dental devices; (iii) the goods are shipped within the deadlines stated in the export operation and the customs authority authenticates the electronic export declaration (DEX); (iv) the zero-rating is reflected in the FEV with the export cause and DEX reference; and (v) any separately invoiced CAD/CAM or digital design services qualify as export of services under Article 481 numeral 3 of the Estatuto Tributario, are rendered to the same foreign principal and are directly connected to the exported goods. Input VAT on zirconia raw materials, milling machinery, packaging and freight may be offset against VAT payable or claimed as refund in the two-month or annual VAT returns after the physical exit of the goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación - DEX) accepted and authenticated by DIAN in the customs processing system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the physical departure of the goods from Colombian customs territory
  • Foreign exchange repatriation certificate or Declaración de Cambio issued by an authorized Colombian bank evidencing channeling of the export proceeds
  • Electronic Export Invoice (Factura Electrónica de Venta) with CUFE code and corresponding packing list
  • Commercial invoice, certificate of origin and INVIMA or health registration certificate for biocompatible dental ceramic products where applicable

Colombia mandates end-to-end electronic invoicing for all VAT taxpayers and exporters. The S.A.S. must issue Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV) through DIAN-habilitated software, software provider or the DIAN free transactional platform. Each FEV must embed the Código Único de Facturación Electrónica (CUFE), or the contingency Código Único de Facturación de Documentos Equivalentes (CUDE), transmit the XML in real time to DIAN validation web services via HTTP/SFTP/JSON or XML APIs, and receive a Validada response or a rejection code (Rechazado) before the invoice acquires fiscal effect. The representation made available to the purchaser must show the DIAN digital stamp, the alphanumeric verification code and a QR code. Export invoices must be transmitted before the corresponding DEX export declaration is submitted, and credit notes, debit notes and dispatch documents follow the same fiscal clearance channel.

DIAN applies automated cross-matching of electronic invoices, DEX declarations, shipping manifests and foreign exchange declarations, and may exchange export data with foreign tax administrations under double taxation agreements or OECD exchange-of-information instruments. Where a zero-rated export is not substantiated, DIAN retroactively reclassifies the operation as a domestic taxable sale at 19%, demands the omitted VAT with default interest, and claws back any input VAT refund already obtained. The exporter also faces the penalty for inexactitude under Article 648 of the Estatuto Tributario, generally equal to 100% of the tax difference and increased to 200% where the claim is based on fraudulent, simulated or non-existent transactions. Directors, legal representatives and fiscal agents may incur liability for use of false documentation and smuggling under Law 1762 of 2015, and the exporter may be removed from the authorized exporter register and suspended from simplified export procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

COP is freely convertible for current-account payments and receipts, but the foreign-exchange system requires that qualifying transactions be channelled through local authorised intermediaries. Capital account transactions, including paid-in capital from non-resident investors and profit remittances, are permitted if the foreign investment is registered with Banco de la República. Dividend remittances are unrestricted after Colombian income tax and withholding obligations are satisfied; the beneficiary must hold a valid foreign investment certificate. Capital repatriation and profit transfer require an updated registration, otherwise the transfer may be blocked. There are no parallel multiple exchange rates, but a mandatory financial tax along with stamp duties may apply depending on bank debit amount and instrument.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds from goods, including dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks, must be repatriated and channelled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market through an authorised intermediary. The statutory term for export earnings settlement is six months from the date of shipment, extendable under Banco de la República regulations. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% of the export FOB invoice value when no prior authorised compensation account is held; if a registered compensation account is used, the exporter must still comply with periodic canalisation rules. Only documented deductions for freight, insurance, and financing costs are allowed against the surrender amount.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia; Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) for AML and market conduct; DIAN for foreign trade and customs-related exchange formalities.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% for dividends distributed out of profits that have been subject to Colombian corporate income tax at the 35% rate. If distributed out of non-taxable or exempt profits to a non-resident shareholder, the withholding tax is 35% (or 10% when the beneficiary is a resident of a country with a comprehensive treaty that provides such a rate).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return must be filed between the 11th day of August and the 10th day of September of the following taxable year, according to the official DIAN calendario based on the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT (auto-registration number). For fiscal years ending on a date other than 31 December, the deadline falls on the sixth month following the end of the fiscal period, subject to DIAN scheduling.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Gross income ('Ingresos brutos') arising from the sale of dental zirconia blocks, CAD/CAM milling discs and any ancillary technical services. For a Colombian S.A.S., even with 100% foreign ownership, report worldwide income on this line because the S.A.S. is a Colombian tax resident entity; exclude only income sourced abroad that is subject to preferential exemption under Colombian law. Late filing penalty: a surcharge equal to 5% of the tax due for each month or fraction of a month of delay, capped at 100% of the tax; when no tax is payable, the penalty is 1% of gross income or 0.5% of gross assets, whichever is higher, with a minimum of 10 UVT. Interest for late payment runs at the daily default rate (equivalent to 1.5 times the prime rate). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File documentation is required when the taxpayer's gross income for the immediately preceding year or total gross assets at the end of the period exceed 150,000 UVT (Unidad de Valor Tributario). For fiscal year 2024, with UVT set at COP 45,653, the threshold equals COP 6,847,950,000 (approximately USD 1.65 million at an exchange rate of 4,150 COP/USD).

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code comparable to the UAE or other OECD-dependent jurisdictions. However, a taxpayer must satisfy the substantive 'business purpose' and 'substance-over-form' doctrines enshrined in the Colombian Tax Code (Article 658 E.T. and the general anti-avoidance rule of Article 869 E.T.). A S.A.S. used merely as a mail-box or conduit without physical manufacturing premises, qualified local personnel, machinery, purchase orders, warehouse activities, or real R&D in Colombia is at high risk of being recharacterized as an 'empresa fachada' (sham company). Such recharacterization results in denial of treaty benefits, assumption of Colombian PE status, disallowance of deductions, and the application of the controlled foreign company (CFC) regime to the foreign shareholder's attributable profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Omission

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability for tax fraud under Law 1819 of 2016 (amending the Criminal Code): imprisonment from 48 to 108 months and fines equal to 200% of the evaded tax when the amount exceeds COP 1,650 million; administrative penalty under Article 647 E.T. of 160% of the understatement, plus adjusted interest and default interest.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, financial officer, accountant, and responsible shareholders who participated in or consented to the fraudulent omission are jointly and severally liable for the full unpaid tax and penalties with their personal assets. They also face criminal imprisonment, forfeiture of corporate assets, a travel ban during investigation, and an order of disengagement from any commercial activity.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Documentation Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Article 260-8 E.T., a transfer pricing adjustment equal to the difference between the OECD-compliant arm's length value and the declared value, plus a penalty of 10% of the adjustment amount. If documentation is not filed, an additional penalty of 5% of the total value of related-party transactions subject to transfer pricing (minimum 1% of gross assets) applies, with a default assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors and the statutory auditor who knowingly sign false TP documentation are jointly liable for the penalty and may be barred from acting as administrators for up to 10 years. Travel bans may be issued by DIAN when amounts exceed a material threshold, and criminal money-laundering consequences arise if the mispricing is used to transfer value outside Colombia without justification.
Shell Company / Abuse of Corporate Form

Statutory sanction: Under Law 1258 of 2008 (S.A.S. law), the corporate veil is pierced specifically for tax purposes; the shareholders are jointly liable with their personal assets for all unpaid corporate taxes. The general anti-avoidance rule (Article 869 E.T.) allows DIAN to recharacterize the transactions, deny the S.A.S.'s treaty entitlement and impose a penalty of up to 100% of the tax advantage sought. If export operations are fictitious, the entity is liquidated and its assets forfeited.

Director personal liability: The directors and shareholders who created or managed the sham structure are personally liable for all corporate tax debts. They may be subject to criminal charges of 'interposition of persons' and fraud, which carry imprisonment up to 10 years; travel bans, freezing of bank accounts, and disqualification from holding commercial licences or export permits are imposed in pending DIAN procedures.
Money Laundering / Trade-Based Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under Colombian AML legislation (Law 1121 of 2006, Article 323 of the Criminal Code): imprisonment of 5 to 12 years, fines up to 1,000 (SMMLV), and forfeiture of all instruments and proceeds. Administrative penalties of up to COP 1,200 million (approx. USD 300,000) may be imposed by the UIAF (Financial Information and Analysis Unit) for failures in AML reporting. In the context of international trade, customs fraud penalties of up to 200% of the cargo value are also imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers and compliance officers who omit anti-money-laundering screening, fail to file suspicious transaction reports (ROS), or knowingly allow the use of the S.A.S. in trade-based money laundering are personally liable and can be sentenced to imprisonment (5–12 years), ordered to pay civil restitution and be subject to confiscation of their personal property. A travel ban and exit restriction is automatically applied during the criminal investigation stage.

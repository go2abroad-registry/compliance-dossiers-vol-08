Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Unidad Administrativa Especial Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: www.dian.gov.co; electronic services through the 'Sede Electrónica DIAN' and Muisca transaction platform (registration, e-invoicing and customs modules).

E-government filing portal

(1) Execute the S.A.S. private incorporation document (documento privado) with no minimum capital and register the company with the competent Cámara de Comercio in the Registro Único Empresarial (RUE), obtaining the provisional NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) automatically through DIAN's integrated online system; (2) Complete RUT (Registro Único Tributario, Form 2281) registration via Muisca, selecting the status of Responsable de IVA – Régimen Común, Exportador, and Obligado a Facturar Electrónicamente; (3) Appoint a legal representative with Colombian domicile, enrol the non-resident shareholders and final beneficial owners in the RUT, and file the required beneficial-ownership declaration; (4) Acquire a digital certificate from a DIAN-authorized certification authority and either contract a DIAN-approved technological invoicing provider or adopt DIAN's free e-invoicing application; (5) Activate the electronic invoicing 'habilitación' and verify the NIT/CUDE issuance keys before commencing sales.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: S.A.S. is a separate corporate taxpayer subject to Colombian corporate income tax (35%), VAT, ICA and complementary taxes; no pass-through, transparency, or US-style check-the-box election is available to an S.A.S.

Available election forms

(i) Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE): optional for companies with prior-year gross income below 100,000 UVT (COP 4,706,500,000 for 2024), replacing income tax, incidental gains, ICA and complementary taxes in a single return, while VAT continues to apply separately; (ii) Zona Franca user status: election to operate as Usuario Industrial de Bienes or Usuario Industrial de Servicios within an authorized free-trade zone, subject to DIAN investment and employment requirements, with 20% corporate income tax and specific VAT benefits; (iii) VAT input-credit proration elections and annual regularization for hybrid taxable/excluded activities under Articles 109-112 of the Tax Statute; (iv) Hybrid-mismatch 'check-the-box' mechanisms: not available under Colombian law; the taxpayer must instead comply with BEPS Action 2 reporting (Decree 2120 of 2017) and apply non-deduction or double-withholding adjustments where a hybrid mismatch is detected.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Nil for legal entities: an S.A.S. is mandatorily subject to the Régimen Común from incorporation regardless of turnover. The Simplified-Regime threshold of 3,500 UVT gross income (COP 164,727,500 for 2024) and 6,500 UVT total assets (COP 305,922,500 for 2024) applies exclusively to natural persons and is unavailable to corporate entities; therefore VAT registration and e-invoicing activation are required before the first transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of portable battery generators and solar panels sold through direct online retail are excluded from IVA (effective 0%) under Article 481 of the Tax Statute, with full entitlement to credit and refund of input VAT, provided: (i) the S.A.S. is registered in the RUT with export status; (ii) the goods physically leave Colombian customs territory under a DIAN-endorsed electronic export declaration (DEX), including simplified export declarations for postal or courier distance-selling channels; (iii) a Factura Electrónica de Exportación bearing a validated CUDE precedes the customs filing; (iv) the foreign buyer is genuinely located abroad, the sale is to a non-resident and the consideration is received in foreign currency through Colombia's authorized foreign-exchange market; and (v) the proof-of-export documentation is retained for at least five years. Digital/offshore services are separately excluded only when the Article 481 extraterritorial-use and foreign-exchange-channeling tests are met.

  • Electronic Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación Electrónica – DEX) accepted and closed by DIAN through the VUCE/Siglo XXI platform
  • Electronic Export Invoice (Factura Electrónica de Exportación) bearing the DIAN CUDE and the DEX reference
  • Transport document evidencing departure from Colombian customs territory (ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, including international courier consignments)
  • Commercial packing list and cargo manifest (lista de empaque y manifiesto de carga) reconciled with the DEX
  • Foreign-exchange Declaration (Declaración de Cambio) or bank settlement certificate issued by a local intermediary evidencing actual collection of the export proceeds

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance through DIAN's Validación Previa model: every sales invoice must be generated in XML, transmitted via certified software or DIAN's free application using SOAP/REST APIs, and validated by DIAN before being deemed issued to the buyer. Each validated invoice obtains the CUDE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica), a QR/digital stamp evidencing fiscal authenticity, and must include the payment-rail reference (Stripe, Affirm BNPL, card or wire) in e-commerce metadata. Export sales must reference the associated DEX export declaration; non-compliant invoices are rejected (rechazo) before issuance, and failure to transmit or issue electronic invoices triggers monthly penalties of 1% of the invoice value per conduct (capped at 10,000 UVT) and audit-related suspension of the NIT.

DIAN automatically cross-matches DEX customs records, CUDE e-invoice data and foreign-exchange settlement reports to detect fictitious, overstated or late-channeled exports. A denied or reversed zero-rating triggers: reassessment of the sale at 19% VAT; retroactive default interest (interés moratorio); the Article 648 inaccuracy penalty equal to 100% of the omitted VAT or of the improper refund (subject to the statutory reduction only for timely voluntary correction); compulsory clawback of all input-VAT refunds (saldo a favor) with indexation; loss of the exporter status and suspension of the NIT; and criminal exposure for contraband, fraudulent refunds and aggravated tax evasion (Criminal Code Articles 434 et seq. as amended by Law 2277 of 2022) against the S.A.S. and its legal representative. Repeat violations also lead to cancellation of the electronic-invoicing authorization and heightened transaction-level audits for all related marketing and logistics arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a liberalized but strictly channeled floating-peso regime. Current-account transactions (export sales of portable solar generators and battery packs by an ISIC 4791 online retailer) are fully convertible through the Mercado Cambiario: foreign-currency card collections (Stripe/Affirm BNPL) may be received by the exporter and sold to an IMC for pesos without prior authorization, subject to the filing of the corresponding Exchange Declaration (Form 1). Capital-account transactions (inbound FDI and shareholder loans) are convertible but require a prior or contemporaneous international investment registration (Form 5) with Banco de la República, with the definitive registration due within 12 months of the inflow; unregistered capital loses the legal right to remit dividends and repatriate capital through the regulated banking system. There is no rationing, waiting list, or discretionary approval for genuine trade collections, but every inbound FX movement must be traceable to a declared causal code. Dividend and profit remittance to a non-resident shareholder of a Colombian S.A.S. is lawful once the registered capital is reported, the corporate financial statements are approved by the shareholders, and the DIAN dividend withholding payment is made.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods exporters must receive the export price within six (6) months from the customs departure date; any extension requires Banco de la República approval. Once the foreign currency is received, whether in Colombia or abroad, it must be channeled through the MCR, meaning the exporter must either (i) sell or convert the currency into COP through an IMC within two (2) business days after receipt, or (ii) credit the proceeds to a foreign-currency compensation account (cuenta de compensación) previously recognized by Banco de la República and use such funds only for the permitted purposes under the exchange regime. Colombia does NOT impose a percentage-based mandatory surrender ratio or an obligatory conversion quota for exporters: the exchange is made at the freely negotiated market rate and the exporter retains the full peso equivalent. Compliance is demonstrated through the IMC-filed Exchange Declaration (Form 1 - Declaración de Cambio por Exportación de Bienes), and failure to channel funds within the legal terms exposes the company to SFC and Banco de la República administrative fines and potential loss of FX operating capacity.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia) is the monetary and foreign-exchange authority; the foreign-exchange regime is governed by Law 9 of 1991 and External Resolution 8 of 2000 of the Banco de la República Board of Directors (Régimen de Cambios Internacionales), as amended by External Resolutions 113 of 2018 and 202 of 2019. The Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) supervises the local authorized intermediaries (IMCs - Instituciones del Mercado Cambiario) that execute fx operations for residents, while DIAN provides the supporting customs documentation and tax verification framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for the primary operating and FX-channeling treasury account; MEDIUM for non-core foreign-currency disbursement functions. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Revolut Business, etc.) are not licensed by the SFC and are not authorized IMCs, so they cannot receive or settle FX movements on behalf of a Colombian resident without breaking the mandatory channeling rule; a Colombian e-commerce exporter would violate the exchange-control regime if card or export collections remained indefinitely outside the country in an unregistered non-bank e-money account. Their acceptable use is limited to (i) marginal reduction of FX friction for imports, (ii) supplier disbursement outside the MCR using already recognized compensation-account balances, or (iii) ancillary payment corridors where a regulated bank remains the final settlement point. Recommendation: route all Stripe/Affirm BNPL settlements directly or indirectly into the Colombian IMC account and use fintech rails only for low-value outbound vendor payments with a concurrent Form 2 import declaration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for the primary operating and FX-channeling treasury account; MEDIUM for non-core foreign-currency disbursement functions. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Revolut Business, etc.) are not licensed by the SFC and are not authorized IMCs, so they cannot receive or settle FX movements on behalf of a Colombian resident without breaking the mandatory channeling rule; a Colombian e-commerce exporter would violate the exchange-control regime if card or export collections remained indefinitely outside the country in an unregistered non-bank e-money account. Their acceptable use is limited to (i) marginal reduction of FX friction for imports, (ii) supplier disbursement outside the MCR using already recognized compensation-account balances, or (iii) ancillary payment corridors where a regulated bank remains the final settlement point. Recommendation: route all Stripe/Affirm BNPL settlements directly or indirectly into the Colombian IMC account and use fintech rails only for low-value outbound vendor payments with a concurrent Form 2 import declaration.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum paid-up capital for S.A.S.; founders may subscribe capital with deferred payment. Any paid-in amount must be deposited in a Colombian financial entity escrow or trust account if shares are issued against capital contributions before registration, subject to the bylaws and Superintendencia de Sociedades rules on capital integrity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

DIAN — Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (National Tax and Customs Directorate); principal corporate portal MUISCA at muisca.dian.gov.co and institutional gateway www.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor organizing a Colombian S.A.S. must: (1) execute the company deed and register the entity with the Cámara de Comercio (commercial registry); (2) file the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) application with DIAN via the MUISCA virtual portal, attaching certified incorporation documents, the appointment of the representante legal or apoderado resident in Colombia, and proof of a Colombian tax-domicile address; (3) obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), the statutory tax identifier for all fiscal acts; (4) activate the electronic-invoicing profile with an DIAN-approved digital certificate. Articles 555-2 and 631 of the Estatuto Tributario (E.T.) compel RUT inscription and NIT disclosure. Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the equity without altering the entity-level NIT.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Colombian S.A.S. is a resident capital company taxed at entity level on worldwide income; no pass-through to shareholders. Dividend distributions after 2017 are subject to additional withholding at 7.5% (listed recipients) or 10% (general) under Arts. 242 and 245 E.T. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Colombian S.A.S. is a resident capital company taxed at entity level on worldwide income; no pass-through to shareholders. Dividend distributions after 2017 are subject to additional withholding at 7.5% (listed recipients) or 10% (general) under Arts. 242 and 245 E.T. Territorial scope applies to the shareholders' foreign-source income, not to the entity's own income.

Available election forms

Colombia does not employ a check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections: (i) export-of-services characterization under Art. 481 E.T., conditional on filing the DIAN informative export-of-services return before issuing zero-rated invoices; (ii) election of the Régimen Simple de Tributación (RST), a consolidated income/VAT/payroll regime restricted to persons below the 3,500 UVT revenue ceiling, generally unavailable to a mid-market vessel-leasing S.A.S.; (iii) hybrid-mismatch elections are disallowed — Decree 2120/2017 (BEPS Action 2) mandates recharacterization override of instruments; (iv) no new legal-stability agreements (convenios de estabilidad jurídica) are being executed for new investors after their statutory closure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for juridical persons: any person habitually selling goods, rendering services, or importing goods must register as an IVA responsible taxpayer from the date of initiating activities. A narrow exclusion applies only to informal natural persons and small traders eligible for the simplified regime with annual gross income below approximately COP 164.7 million (3,500 UVT for tax year 2024). The offshore-vessel-leasing S.A.S. is accordingly unconditionally VAT-registered from day one.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% IVA is available under Art. 481 E.T. and Decree 1625/2016, Art. 1.3.1.5.1 for (i) physical exports of goods via customs exit, and (ii) services rendered to non-resident customers for use exclusively abroad, including the lease of offshore supply vessels to non-resident charterers when the vessel is operated outside Colombian territorial waters and the lease is not for the exploitation of natural resources within Colombian territory, its continental shelf, or cabotage. The payment must originate abroad and be repatriated through the authorized foreign-exchange market; the charter party must specify the overseas operational area; and DIAN's informative export-of-services return must be filed and acknowledged before invoice issuance. Leasing of vessels for platform supply within Colombian-owned continental-shelf waters is treated as a Colombian-source service subject to 19% IVA regardless of the foreign residence of the operator.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación Electrónica — DEX) transmitted through the DIAN customs module and VUCE
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Sea Waybill evidencing the vessel's delivery, sailing, and exit from Colombian customs territory
  • Charter Party Agreement or maritime lease contract, notarized and apostilled, identifying the non-resident charterer and the exclusive overseas place of use
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Liquidación de Divisas) issued by an authorized bank evidencing repatriation of the proceeds through the Colombian foreign-exchange market
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and vessel delivery minutes (acta de entrega) signed at the Colombian port of exit
  • DIAN-acknowledged export-of-services informative return for service zero-rating claims
  • Ship registry certificate or flag-state documentation confirming the vessel's international-trade or non-Colombian operational positioning

Mandatory nationwide electronic invoicing under DIAN Resolution 000165/2023. The S.A.S. must issue Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV) in XML, transmit each invoice to DIAN's 'validación previa' real-time clearance API, obtain the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) hash, and affix a qualified digital signature. Zero-rated export invoices must further include the export-of-services informative-registry reference, the customs DEX number where applicable, and the technical annex codes of the DIAN XML scheme; DIAN validates and assigns the CUDS code for supporting documents.

DIAN post-clearance audit may retroactively reverse the zero-rating if foreign-market payment, overseas-use, or customs-exit evidence is deficient. A reversed zero-rating triggers the 200% inaccuracy sanction on the omitted IVA (Art. 647 E.T.), default interest (Art. 635 E.T.), and monetary correction. Fraudulent documentation, including false bills of lading or bank remittance certificates, invokes criminal tax-fraud liability under Law 1762 of 2015 with imprisonment, aggravated fines, and extended prescription periods of up to ten years. Retrospective clawback accrues from the original due date, and the S.A.S., its compliance officers, and the foreign charterer may face joint and several liability for at-fault reinstatements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating exchange rate (TRM) and maintains substantial current account convertibility for trade, services, and profit remittances. Capital account inflows and outflows are fully convertible in the foreign exchange market, but investments, loans, and certain financial operations must be declared or registered with the Banco de la República using designated statistical forms. Dividend and profit remittances are permitted once the S.A.S. has satisfied corporate tax returns and withholding obligations; a remittance tax applies to profits distributed to non-residents if the distributing entity is a Colombian source, typically 10% on the dividend amount subject to tax, but rates vary by treaty eligibility. There are no blanket exchange surrender requirements on investment proceeds, but export proceeds and service revenues must be channeled through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries. Undertaking and documentary requirements must be evidenced through the local bank's Comprobante de Transacciones de Cambio.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts from the offshore supply vessel leasing and maritime charter activities must be repatriated to Colombia and sold through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries within the period established by the underlying contract, not exceeding six months from the date of customs export or service delivery. The Colombian foreign exchange regime mandates that at least 80% of export proceeds must be liquidated into Colombian pesos (COP) through the organized exchange market; a maximum of 20% of the export value may be retained in foreign currency in a local or authorized foreign-currency account. The mandatory surrender period may be extended by the Banco de la República for specific long-term maritime contracts if the commercial terms justify a longer receivable cycle; such extensions must be evidenced by the charter party and approved before maturity. The S.A.S. must file the corresponding foreign exchange declaration (Formulario) with the authorized bank upon collection and conversion.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (the Central Bank of Colombia), functioning jointly with the Comisión de Cambios and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83 of the Banco de la República. All foreign exchange transactions, statistical registers, and compulsory surrender rules are governed by these authorities, with practical oversight by the Financial Superintendence for AML/KYC compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Offshore digital electronic money institutions or neobanks without a Colombian banking license cannot lawfully provide COP-denominated settlement accounts, receive export proceeds, or operate local nostro accounts. They may be used only as user-facing collection front-ends or corporate card issuers when acting as auxiliary partners of a licensed Colombian credit entity; all settlement, FX conversion, and foreign exchange declaration duties must remain in an authorized commercial bank account. Legal constraints under the Financial Superintendence prevent unlicensed EMIs from executing mandatory export surrender and foreign exchange statistical forms, so treasury segregation should route arbitration-fee receipts and charter collections directly to a licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from non-resident shareholders must be received through an authorized foreign exchange intermediary using SWIFT MT103/MT202 wires. For equity contributions and additional paid-in capital, the sender must identify the beneficiary as the S.A.S. by NIT (Colombian tax ID) and state the purpose code 'capital contributions for direct foreign investment' in the wire. The receiving bank will require the shareholder to complete the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales (Formulario No. 5) with the Banco de la República and to submit evidence of the corporate board resolution approving the investment. Funds may arrive in USD or COP; USD funds received in the local bank are converted through the foreign exchange market at the TRM plus spread, and the corresponding COP balance is made available after verification that the capital contribution complies with the S.A.S. bylaws and foreign investment regime. For escrowed maritime charters, the bank may credit the account with a conditional erasure notice until the final vessel registration is verified, using maritime escrow instructions.Outbound charter hire, lease installments, operating expenses, and vessel supplier payments are cleared through the SWIFT network using MT103 for single disbursements and MT202/MT202 COV for treasury-to-treasury settlement. Each instruction must include the Colombian foreign exchange declaration form reference number, the purpose code for maritime chartering services, and a clear description of the underlying transaction, e.g., 'offshore supply vessel leasing fees - ISIC 5222'. Larger structured payments should be supported by documentary letters of credit (UCP 600) where the vessel lease or bareboat charter requires deferred payments; standby LCs may also be used to collateralize the lease. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: COP 4,706,500,000 (approximately US$1.1 million), representing the 100,000 UVT threshold for taxable year 2024 (UVT = 47,065 COP), applicable to gross assets or gross income.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations similar to offshore hubs. However, DIAN may invoke general anti-abuse rules (Article 869-1 E.T.) to disregard transactions lacking real commercial substance. A maritime charter enterprise must evidence actual vessel operation, local crew and management, and economic presence in Colombia.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanction under Colombian Penal Code Art. 426: imprisonment for 48-108 months, fines up to 200% of the evaded tax, plus loss of credit, interest sanctions, and exclusion from public contracts.

Director personal liability: DIAN may pierce the S.A.S. corporate veil under Law 1258 of 2008; administrators are jointly liable for tax debts, subject to travel bans, asset attachment, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of deductions and treaty benefits, reclassification at 35% CIT, plus 30% fraud penalty and default interest.

Director personal liability: Personal liability arises for the company's uncovered tax and customs debts; courts may order civil restitution and prohibition on leaving Colombian territory.
AML / Money Laundering in Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Art. 323 of the Penal Code carries 10-30 years imprisonment and fines up to 1,000,000 SMLMV; Financial Superintendence may impose additional administrative sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in concealment face criminal liability, seizure of assets, extradition requests from foreign states, and dissolution of the corporate vehicle.

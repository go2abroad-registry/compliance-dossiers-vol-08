Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists under the Taiwan Company Act for a limited liability WFOE; the paid-up capital must nevertheless be sufficient for the stated business scope as reviewed by the Investment Commission. Foreign share capital must be remitted from abroad into a temporary Taiwan bank account in the name of the proposed company and verified by a Taiwan certified public accountant. No blocked escrow account is required by statute, but the capital must remain in the company account for operating purposes and may not be returned to the investor before lawful distribution.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taxation Administration (Taiwan); registration and tax administration are executed by the regional National Taxation Bureaus (e.g., National Taxation Bureau of Taipei). The official online electronic portal is the 'Integrated Tax Filing and Registration System' operated by the National Taxation Bureau, accessible at https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw.

E-government filing portal

For the inbound foreign-owned Taiwan limited liability company (WFOE): (1) file for company incorporation with the Ministry of Economic Affairs and obtain the company registration certificate; (2) within 15 days of incorporation, apply for business tax (VAT) registration at the local National Taxation Bureau by submitting the statutory Tax Registration Application Form, the company registration certificate, the articles of incorporation, the list of shareholders, the responsible person's identification (passport/ID), and the physical business premises evidence (lease agreement or title deed); (3) undergo the Bureau's premises inspection; (4) receive the Tax Registration Number (unified business number) and the authoritative Uniform Invoice seal. For a foreign non-resident entity without a permanent establishment that sells electronic services to Taiwan consumers, the non-resident must: (a) appoint a local tax agent; (b) register online through the National Taxation Bureau's 'Cross-Border Electronic Services Tax Registration' portal using the home-country registration certificate, the agent's authorization, and a description of the electronic services; (c) obtain a Business Tax Registration Number for the account; and (d) begin charging Taiwan business tax at the statutory rate on transactions with Taiwan individuals, filing bimonthly returns through the tax agent.

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic VAT-registered enterprises, mandatory registration is triggered when average monthly sales exceed TWD 200,000 (annual equivalent TWD 2,400,000); for foreign non-resident electronic services suppliers, registration is mandatory when annual Taiwan turnover exceeds TWD 480,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, zero rating applies to exported goods and services. For the supply of AI avatar video generation SaaS, zero rating is available when (1) the service is used or consumed outside Taiwan, (2) the service is not deemed to be rendered within Taiwan for a Taiwan customer, and (3) the consideration is received in foreign currency. The exporter must substantiate that the customer is a non-resident and that the service was delivered electronically to a location outside Taiwan, with the transaction recorded as an export sale for business tax purposes.

  • Export service contract or purchase order evidencing the foreign buyer and the scope of the digital service
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank credit advice showing receipt of the foreign-currency consideration from outside Taiwan
  • Electronic service delivery records (e.g., server logs with non-Taiwan IP geolocation, customer account verification, or user acceptance certificate) demonstrating that the service is used outside Taiwan

VAT-registered businesses must issue 'Uniform Invoices' for all sales. The Ministry of Finance mandates electronic invoices (e-invoices) for most taxpayers, requiring issuance through the MOF e-Invoice Integrated Platform or an approved E-Invoice Turnkey service provider. E-invoice data must be transmitted to the platform via API/Turnkey within 48 hours of issuance (real-time for cloud invoices), and each e-invoice must carry a tax verification QR code. Foreign digital service providers must issue e-invoices to Taiwan consumers and upload the data through the same platform, which performs real-time anti-fraud checks and assigns a unique invoice number and QR stamp.

The National Taxation Bureau conducts document reviews and audits of zero-rated export claims. If the zero rating is disallowed due to insufficient or inconsistent documentation, the unpaid business tax at 5% is assessed together with interest, and a penalty of 1x to 1.5x the underpaid tax is imposed. For fraudulent claims, including counterfeit FIRCs, fictitious export contracts, or misrepresentation of the place of use, the penalty increases to 1x to 5x of the tax evaded, and criminal liability may arise under the Tax Evasion and Punishment Act if the evaded amount exceeds TWD 50,000. Retrospective clawbacks extend up to seven years for fraud and five years for ordinary deficiencies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan Dollar (TWD) is fully legal tender in Taiwan but is not an international reserve currency; Taiwan runs a managed floating exchange-rate system under the CBC with TWD/USD and TWD/CNY rates determined in the interbank market and smoothed by the central bank. For current-account flows, Taiwan is broadly convertible: software licensing, SaaS subscription fees, royalty payments, management charges, freight income and profit remittances from a foreign-invested enterprise can be converted into TWD or retained in designated foreign-currency deposit accounts once the underlying contract, invoices, customs declaration or audited financial statement and tax clearance are presented. There is no blanket current-account conversion ceiling, but the CBC receives electronic Exchange Settlement Reports for statistical, macro-prudential and AML purposes. Capital-account flows are far less free: inbound equity injection, shareholder loans, the acquisition of existing shares, local direct investment and outward investment by Taiwan entities must be approved by the MOEA Investment Commission (financial sectors under FSC) and then settled through an authorized FX bank. Profit remittance to a mainland Chinese parent is treated as a regulated remittance: it requires an audited Taiwan WFOE balance sheet, certified compliance with undistributed retained earnings tax under the Income Tax Act, original foreign-investment admission approval, and a tax payment/tax clearance certificate before the bank files the outward remittance report. The practical hurdle is documentary, not quantitative: no tariff or remittance cap exists for legitimate dividends, but every payment must be reconciled to approved share capital, audited earnings and paid corporate income tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must repatriate the full foreign-currency invoice value to a Taiwan-licensed bank within 180 calendar days from the export customs declaration date for goods; for service exports, the 180-day clock runs from the service completion date or invoice date and is supported by the service contract and settlement invoice. The bank must file the Foreign Exchange Settlement Report through the CBC reporting system and retain the Customs Export Declaration, marine/AWB waybill, commercial invoice and payment evidence. There is no mandatory conversion percentage: 0% of export proceeds must be converted into TWD, and the exporter may keep 100% in a Foreign-Currency Deposit Account or convert any portion at market rate. Any overdue collection, extended open account, D/A, D/P, consignment or barter arrangement must be approved in advance by the designated bank and reported to the CBC; the exporter should maintain credit insurance, collection agency correspondence or buyer default evidence in the event of delay.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), with the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) as co-regulator for banking and the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) Investment Commission for inbound foreign/mainland investment admission.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The parent must route all FDI equity capital by SWIFT MT103 from its approved corporate bank in mainland China to the Taiwan WFOE's designated TWD/FCD account at Bank of Taiwan, Mega, or CTBC, using a CBC-compliant purpose code that clearly identifies direct investment/equity subscription rather than trade. The Taiwan receiving bank will not credit funds to operational accounts until it has verified the MOEA/FSC investment approval, share subscription resolution, source-of-funds evidence and the identity of the remitter. After receipt, an appointed Taiwan CPA must issue a capital verification report, and the bank files the Foreign Exchange Settlement Report. Only after those documents are complete may the WFOE draw funds down for SaaS platform build-out, local operations and acquisition of AI compute or cloud services.For software license fees, royalties, intercompany support fees and dividend remittances, use direct SWIFT MT103 international wire transfers through the Taiwan clearing network, with the underlying contract, tax clearance and CBC purpose code attached. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (移转订价报告) is mandatory where annual related-party transactions exceed NT$200 million for goods or NT$50 million for services and intangible property licensing/transfers; the Related-Party Transaction Summary Table is required for all filers; Master File is required where consolidated group turnover reaches NT$30 billion.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the UAE sense, but the tax administration enforces equivalent substance tests via the Place of Effective Management (PEM) doctrine, the GAAR under Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act, and the cross-strait anti-avoidance coordination mechanism. A Taiwan WFOE must demonstrate real local board decision-making, physical R&D staff, source-code and server control in Taiwan, genuine risk assumption, and arm's-length intercompany agreements; otherwise profits are reallocated to the PRC parent or the entity is recharacterized as a tax-transparent shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Fraudulent Returns and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of evaded tax plus a 2x-3x penalty (up to 5x for deliberate concealment) under Articles 108 and 110 of the Income Tax Act; criminal prosecution under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act (2021 amendment) carrying imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines up to NT$5 million; administrative delisting and adverse filing status.

Director personal liability: The responsible person who signs the false return is jointly prosecuted; the National Taxation Bureau may impose an exit restriction (限制出境) where delinquent tax exceeds NT$1 million; civil restitution is enforceable against personal assets of directing officers; cross-strait tax information exchange permits the PRC tax authority to support collection.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act and the Transfer Pricing Examination Standards: upward adjustment of taxable income, 20% tax on the additional assessment, 5% retained-earnings tax on undeclared surplus, 20% WHT recapture on recharacterized royalties or fees, plus daily interest and penalties up to 3x of the adjusted tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers are jointly answerable for the unpaid withholding tax and assessed penalties; exit restrictions apply for sustained non-payment; deliberate TP fraud triggers criminal liability and personal seizure; the PRC parent may be treated as the substantive taxpayer under the PEM doctrine.
Shell Company and Place-of-Effective-Management Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization under Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act and the PEM rule: the entire profit is reattributed to the PRC parent or treated as a Taiwan permanent establishment of the parent; assessment of 20% corporate tax on recharacterized profit, 21% dividend WHT recapture, denial of loss deductions, and penalties for false reporting; possible forced dissolution for non-operation.

Director personal liability: The responsible person is liable for restitution of the recharacterized profit; travel bans under Article 24 of the Tax Collection Act; personal fraud exposure in egregious cases; cross-strait mutual assistance enables freezing of personal and corporate assets.
AML, Unlicensed Payment Activity, and Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Control Act, criminal liability of up to 7 years' imprisonment and fines up to NT$5 million; administrative fines for AML program failures by the paying entity; penalties under the Central Bank Act for unreported cross-border remittances; the concealed Taiwan-source SaaS turnover remains chargeable to 20% corporate income tax, 5% VAT, and the 21% dividend WHT on repatriation, with severe cumulative surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct the structuring or knowingly process sanctioned counterparties face indictment as principals of money laundering; payment-rail settlement accounts may be frozen at the request of the Financial Supervisory Commission; exit bans, asset confiscation, and extradition or cross-strait mutual legal assistance are available.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum authorized, subscribed or paid capital applies to an S.A.S.; the founders may fix a nominal or zero initial capital. If any portion is deferred, the unpaid instalment must be paid within two years from registration (Law 1258 of 2008, art. 4). There is no escrow account requirement; paid-in money must be deposited in the company's Colombian bank account, and in-kind contributions must be inventoried with valuation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: obtain the Concepto Técnico de Seguridad Humana y Protección contra Incendios from the local Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos, covering production floor, spray/painting room, storage racks, and retail area. Structural load: obtain a technical structural certification and load inspection from a qualified engineer or municipal curaduría verifying floor capacity for cutting tables, wig molds, and fabric/wire storage. Workplace: implement the SGSST under Resolución 0312 de 2019, obtain the occupational health and safety workplace clearance, train employees in fire response, emergency evacuation, and safe handling of synthetic fibers, adhesives, and electrical equipment.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – National Tax and Customs Directorate, operating via the official portal www.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.), a preliminary NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) is automatically assigned at incorporation via a Chamber of Commerce notary or DIAN. The entity must thereafter complete its RUT (Registro Único Tributario) profile electronically at www.dian.gov.co, designating legal representation and validating tax classifications such as IVA responsible status and industry/activity codes. A digital certificate (Certificado de Firma Electrónica) must be obtained before issuing electronic invoices. No separate TIN application is needed for foreign parent entities; the Colombian subsidiary operates under its own NIT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not employ a check-the-box or pass-through election for S.A.S. entities; all companies are taxed at entity level. Optional statutory elections include the Régimen Simple de Tributación (Simplified Tax Regime) for qualifying small taxpayers and the tax consolidation election under Decree 1766 of 2019 for economic groups. Hybrid mismatch rules under Law 2010 of 2019 apply at entity level, with no entity classification election available to circumvent Colombian income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

COP 0 for all incorporated entities, including S.A.S., which are mandatory IVA taxpayers regardless of turnover. Natural persons may enjoy the simplified IVA regime only if gross income in the prior year does not exceed 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 191,000,000 in 2025) and other statutory conditions are met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods, including licensed theatrical costume robes and synthetic wigs, qualify for a 0% IVA rate under Colombian law, provided the goods are physically removed from Colombian customs territory pursuant to a valid export declaration. The exporter must hold an updated RUT reflecting export activity, be registered with DIAN, and must file the corresponding export declaration (DEX) through the Authorized Customs Broker or direct DIAN systems. Services, including logistics and related supply-chain services, are zero-rated only when directly linked to exported goods, and must comply with local supporting documentation rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX) and DIAN export registry
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent Air Waybill evidencing removal of goods from Colombia
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Canalización de Divisas) issued by the Colombian intermediary bank

All IVA responsible taxpayers, including S.A.S. manufacturing corporations, are legally required to issue electronic invoices (factura electrónica de venta) through DIAN-certified software. Transmission must occur in real time via web services or API integration using XML/UBL 2.1, an X.509 digital certificate, and DIAN controls generating a single CUFE or CUNE digital stamping code. No physical pre-printed invoice is acceptable unless expressly authorised; DAIN performs automated validation and risk-scoring before final electronic acceptance.

DIAN retains authority to retroactively reverse export zero-rating and recover VAT previously refunded, plus default interest, where customs documentation, foreign-exchange repatriation, or the underlying export bill of lading is falsified, incomplete, or noncompliant. Penalties for inexact or fraudulent export filings include a fine of up to 160% of the VAT improperly refunded, administrative suspension of the exporter's registration, and criminal customs sanctions for smuggling when goods are misdeclared. DIAN cross-validates export declarations, shipping records, and compulsory foreign-exchange returns filed at the Banco de la República, with a five-year audit limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso is fully convertible for current-account trade and service flows; the capital account, including foreign direct investment (FDI), is unrestricted but must be channeled through authorized local foreign-exchange intermediaries and registered with Banco de la República. Profit remittance requires no prior central-bank approval: dividends are remittable via the local bank under exchange declaration concept 4011, subject only to DIAN withholding tax, up-to-date FDI registration, and SARLAFT/KYC verification. No annual convertibility quotas or repatriation caps apply to genuine trade and FDI transactions; the practical constraints are documentary (underlying commercial contracts, export/import declarations, corporate documentation, and proof of source of funds).

Export proceeds surrender rules

100% of export proceeds from the manufacture and sale of cosplay apparel, prop costumes, and synthetic wigs must be repatriated and channeled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market. For physical goods, proceeds must be received within 6 months from the date of shipment, extendable to 12 months with DIAN authorization. Receipts in foreign currency must be credited to an account at an authorized intermediary (or an approved foreign-currency compensation account) and either converted or transferred into Colombia within the business-day window fixed by Circular DCIN-83 (generally no later than three business days after receipt). Every receipt triggers a mandatory declaración de cambio; the e-commerce settlement reports from Stripe and Shopify must reconcile with the underlying DIAN export declarations.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank) together with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN). The statutory basis is Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1068 of 2015 (Único Reglamentario), Banco de la República External Resolution 1 of 2018, and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low feasibility for core treasury. Colombia does not issue EMI licenses in the EU/UK sense; foreign digital e-money institutions (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud, Wise Business) are not authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries and cannot honor a Colombian declaración de cambio. Stripe and Shopify Payments are payment-acquiring rails, not settlement banks. Such fintechs may be used only as auxiliary disbursement wallets funded from declared local-bank balances; all tax-controlled flows (export proceeds, FDI injections, dividend remittances, import payments) must pass through an authorized Colombian bank or authorized brokerage to preserve FX legality and SARLAFT compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

FDI equity injections must arrive via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's corporate account to the S.A.S. treasury account at the authorized Colombian bank, with remittance narrative 'Aporte de capital social / Foreign Direct Investment'. The bank files the exchange declaration under concept 4010 (FDI in national companies – foreign currency contributions), and the investor must register the investment in Banco de la República's FDI registry within 2 months of the exchange declaration to secure future dividend and capital repatriation rights. Supporting documents: share subscription certificate, board resolution of the Colombian S.A.S., amended bylaws registered with Cámara de Comercio, source-of-funds evidence, and shareholder SWIFT details. Contributions in kind (machinery, molds, wigs manufacturing equipment) must first be imported through DIAN and formally appraised before equity registration.Preferred rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for regular import payments (fabrics, synthetic fibers, fasteners, dyes, tooling) with the underlying commercial invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, and DIAN import declaration attached for the FX purchase; (2) commercial documentary letters of credit (MT700/MT710 under UCP 600) for recurring high-value procurement, especially from Asia-based cosplay accessory suppliers; (3) MT202 COV for bank-to-bank coverage where required by the intermediary.

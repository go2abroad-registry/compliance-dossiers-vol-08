Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

For the mainland China WFOE: State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic compliance is performed through the STA's National Electronic Tax Bureau online tax service platform and the provincial electronic tax bureaus. For any Taiwan-side tax filing obligation of the Taiwan parent: Ministry of Finance of Taiwan, acting through the National Taxation Administration and the local national tax bureaus, including the Taiwan Ministry of Finance Electronic Invoice Integration Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

Mainland China WFOE: (1) complete incorporation registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation or the competent local Market Supervision Administration; (2) obtain the business license containing the Unified Social Credit Code, which is the statutory taxpayer identification number for Chinese tax purposes and is automatically shared with the STA; (3) complete the consolidated tax registration at the competent local tax authority within 30 days of the business license issue date; (4) determine VAT taxpayer status, remaining a small-scale taxpayer below RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales or electing VAT general taxpayer status; (5) if exporting goods, register with the Customs Administration and obtain or update the customs declaration registration code. For a foreign or non-resident enterprise without a Chinese permanent establishment, such as the Taiwan parent, the non-resident enterprise must file Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Registration with the competent STA and obtain a non-resident tax identification number to support Chinese withholding obligations; the Taiwan parent does not receive a Chinese Unified Social Credit Code unless it establishes a branch or subsidiary in mainland China.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. It is not a pass-through entity, and distributions to the Taiwan shareholder remain subject to Chinese dividend withholding tax where applicable. — Mainland China WFOE: (1) complete incorporation registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation or the competent local Market Supervision Administration; (2) obtain the business license containing the Unified Social Credit Code, which is the statutory taxpayer identification number for Chinese tax purposes and is automatically shared with the STA; (3) complete the consolidated tax registration at the competent local tax authority within 30 days of the business license issue date; (4) determine VAT taxpayer status, remaining a small-scale taxpayer below RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales or electing VAT general taxpayer status; (5) if exporting goods, register with the Customs Administration and obtain or update the customs declaration registration code. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The CN WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law of the People's Republic of China and is subject to corporate income tax on its worldwide income at the entity level. It is not a pass-through entity, and distributions to the Taiwan shareholder remain subject to Chinese dividend withholding tax where applicable.

Available election forms

None for pass-through or check-the-box elections. Available statutory elections in China include the VAT small-scale taxpayer versus general taxpayer election, with general taxpayer status becoming mandatory above RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales, and accounting-method elections under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available in mainland China; the Taiwan parent remains governed by Chinese non-resident withholding rules and Taiwan domestic tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no monetary threshold for obtaining the Unified Social Credit Code-based tax identity; every mainland China enterprise must complete tax registration. The VAT general taxpayer registration threshold is RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales. Below this threshold, the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer and may enjoy the prevailing small-scale taxpayer monthly exemption, currently capped at RMB 100,000 in taxable sales per month, or may elect general taxpayer status. Exceeding RMB 5,000,000 makes general taxpayer registration mandatory. For Taiwan, a mainland WFOE supplying services to Taiwan has no Taiwan VAT registration obligation solely by reason of export supplies; Taiwan VAT or Business Tax is generally collected by reverse-charge on the Taiwan business customer, unless the WFOE establishes a Taiwan fixed place of business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

China zero-rates export goods and qualifying cross-border services, including software services, information system services, business process management services, and remote helpdesk or customer-support cloud services, where the service recipient is outside mainland China and the service is consumed entirely outside mainland China. For this WFOE, the omni-channel helpdesk and conversational-bot cloud services supplied to Taiwan customers may be zero-rated if the contract, payment evidence, service delivery logs, and place-of-consumption indicators demonstrate that the service is performed and used outside mainland China. The statutory basis includes the VAT interim regulations and the value-added tax reform framework under Caishui [2016] No. 36, together with the STA cross-border taxable services administration measures. If physical goods are sold alongside the cloud service, the goods must be properly exported through customs to support zero-rating for the goods element.

  • Customs Export Declaration and the corresponding electronic customs data from the China Electronic Port single-window system where physical goods are exported
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill where physical goods are exported
  • Commercial contract or framework agreement with the Taiwan customer identifying the service recipient, scope of services, place of consumption, and payment terms
  • Zero-rated VAT sales invoice, issued as a fully digitalized electronic invoice or special VAT invoice where required
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, Foreign Currency Collection Certificate, or bank confirmation of cross-border foreign exchange repatriation from the Taiwan customer
  • Cross-border payment gateway settlement reports from Stripe Billing or PayPal showing gross receipts, fees, currency, and payee details
  • Service delivery and consumption evidence, including IP addresses, server logs, helpdesk ticket records, conversational-bot session logs, and cloud usage reports demonstrating that services are rendered and consumed outside mainland China

Mainland China: fully digitalized electronic invoices, known as shu dian piao, are issued through the National Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform administered by the STA. Invoice numbers are allocated in real time, invoice data is transmitted to the tax authority at issuance, and the digital electronic seal and anti-fraud controls are embedded in the invoice. Paper special VAT invoices must be issued through authorized tax-control equipment or tax disk where a counterparty still requires paper-format invoices. Taiwan: domestic Taiwan sellers must transmit cloud-based electronic invoices to the Taiwan Ministry of Finance Electronic Invoice Integration Service Platform in near-real time; a foreign exporter without a Taiwan fixed place of business is not required to issue Taiwan electronic invoices for exports of services.

China: if the zero-rating claim is unsupported, false, or fraudulent, the STA will claw back any input VAT refund, reapply VAT at the applicable rate of 6% for information technology and software services, charge late-payment interest, and may impose administrative penalties from 50% up to five times the underpaid tax. Customs penalties may also apply where export declarations or supporting transport documents have been misused. The 180-day foreign exchange repatriation deadline may be extended only for genuine force majeure or documented administrative delay. Taiwan: a Taiwan business customer that fails to reverse-charge Taiwan Business Tax on imported services may be liable for tax surcharges and penalties under the Taiwan Business Tax Act.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible on current-account items, including service-trade proceeds from SaaS/helpdesk export revenue, but remains a regulated currency on capital-account items. A China WFOE with a Taiwan parent requires SAFE registration for each FDI equity injection; the injected RMB must be used within the approved business scope and cannot be arbitrarily converted into off-balance-sheet or speculative instruments. Profit remittance by the WFOE to Taiwan is treated as a current-account remittance but is subject to a combination of tax clearance certificates, audited financial statements, and bank documentary review. In practice, the bank will require the annual SAFE foreign-exchange business report and proof of dividends. Because the NTD and RMB cross-strait settlement regimes require special settlement arrangements, the Taiwan parent may receive RMB through Taipei/Shanghai clearing bank lines, but onshore capital movement remains under PBOC/SAFE oversight. Surrender requirements have been liberalised: current-account foreign-exchange receipts may be retained or voluntarily settled, but all proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic SAFE-regulated account within the statutory period. Overall, no full capital-account convertibility exists, and all treasury operations must be pre-mapped to SAFE codes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

100 percent of export service and goods proceeds must be repatriated to a SAFE-designated domestic foreign-exchange account within 180 days from the date of invoice or service completion; the mandatory conversion ratio is 0 percent because the compulsory surrender regime has been abolished, but an enterprise may elect to convert any portion to RMB; retained foreign-exchange balances are subject to the SAFE-approved current-account balance cap, and non-repatriation triggers SAFE administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People’s Republic of China, operating under the People’s Bank of China (PBOC), for the mainland China WFOE; the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) governs the Taiwan parent-side settlement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not viable as the primary treasury rails for the mainland China WFOE. SAFE and PBOC regulations require all foreign-exchange transactions of a PRC company to be settled through a licensed domestic bank or a SAFE-approved licensed payment institution. Global EMIs cannot issue a domestic RMB clearing account, perform SAFE reporting, or act as a statutory counterparty for capital-account flows. For the target rails Stripe_Billing and PayPal, PayPal may only be used to collect offshore card payments if the WFOE works through a licensed cross-border payment service provider or a foreign acquiring entity before routing funds via SWIFT MT103 through the domestic bank; Stripe does not maintain a licensed PRC onshore receiving entity for a WFOE. An offshore treasury vehicle such as a Hong Kong company may hold Stripe/PayPal balances, but the WFOE’s statutory books, SAFE FDI registration, and domestic tax filings require funds to be repatriated through the licensed banking channel. Therefore the feasibility rating is Low.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires the Taiwan parent to pass a board resolution, the WFOE to complete SAFE FDI registration and obtain the FDI registration certificate, and the Taiwan parent to remit via SWIFT MT103 directly to the WFOE FDI capital account at a Tier-1 domestic bank using the direct investment equity capital purpose code with the SAFE registration number referenced in the payment details. After receipt, a PRC-licensed CPA performs capital verification, and funds are credited to the capital account and may be converted to RMB for permitted operating expenses; third-party remitters, split remittances designed to avoid SAFE reporting, and cash injections are prohibited.For SaaS and helpdesk service disbursements, use SWIFT MT103 with the service-trade purpose code and supporting contract, invoice, and tax payment certificate. For any subordinate physical goods trade, use a commercial letter of credit (MT700) or documentary collection when cross-border title transfer occurs. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: An independent certified public accountant (CPA) audit is statutorily mandatory in Taiwan when: (a) the company has paid-in capital of TWD 30 million or more, approximately USD 967,742; or (b) annual sales or operating revenue reaches TWD 100 million or more, approximately USD 3,225,806, in which case the profit-seeking enterprise income tax return must be audited and certified by a CPA; or (c) the company operates in a regulated industry such as banking, insurance, securities, or a combined commercial and financial activity where special law mandates audit; or (d) the company is a branch or investee of a foreign enterprise whose parent requires IFRS/TFRS-audited group consolidation statements. There is no general employee headcount threshold under Taiwan Company Act that triggers a statutory audit; however, headcount-based obligations under labor pension, health insurance, and employment insurance laws remain independently applicable. Balance sheet and turnover thresholds are measured per the fiscal year before the filing obligation and are not waived for small or single-shareholder WFOE limited liability companies.

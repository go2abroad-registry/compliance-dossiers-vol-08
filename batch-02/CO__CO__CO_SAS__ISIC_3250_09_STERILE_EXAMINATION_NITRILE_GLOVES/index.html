Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope / Tax_Exempt_Zone_option / Pass_Through_Eligible: Not applicable. The S.A.S. is a full Colombian tax resident (incorporation test) taxed on worldwide income at the 35% headline rate (Arts. 12 and 240 E.T.), with a foreign tax credit. Dividends remitted to the foreign shareholder trigger Colombian source withholding at distribution. Tax_Exempt_Zone: Zona Franca user status is available and reduces manufacturing income to a 20% CIT rate.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Colombian law. Available elections: (i) Zona Franca (free-zone) user election under Art. 240-1 E.T. - a DIAN/Comité Nacional de Zonas Francas resolution granting a 20% CIT rate plus customs duty and VAT suspension on inputs, subject to an investment/employment undertaking; (ii) Régimen Simple de Tributación (Arts. 908-917 E.T.) for eligible businesses with prior-year gross income not exceeding 100,000 UVT (approx. COP 4,706.5 million in 2024); exporters generally decline this election to preserve VAT credit and refund rights; (iii) advance pricing agreement (APA) with DIAN to fix transfer-pricing margins for related-party cross-border purchases of raw materials and licensed technology.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a free-floating exchange rate regime with the TRM (Tasa Representativa del Mercado) published daily by Banco de la República. Current-account operations (goods, services, royalties) are fully convertible but must be channeled through the 'mercado cambiario' via authorized intermediaries (commercial banks, financial corporations, and stock brokerage firms). Capital-account convertibility is substantially unrestricted for foreign direct investment (FDI), portfolio investment, and external credit, provided the operation is channeled through an authorized intermediary and registered with Banco de la República (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales). Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is lawful at 100% of formally declared dividends provided the FDI is duly registered, applicable dividend withholding tax (generally 5%, with a 7.5% rate in defined cases) is paid to DIAN, and the remittance is declared through the exchange declaration mechanism; no minimum holding period applies to FDI since Decree 2086 of 2003. Non-channeled operations lose legal validity for foreign-exchange purposes and are subject to exchange fines by DIAN.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Resolución Externa 1 de 2018 (Articles 75-79), exporters of goods must channel ('canalizar') all export proceeds through the Colombian foreign-exchange market within six (6) months following the shipment date (fecha de embarque) or within four (4) months following the contractual collection due date, whichever is later. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% – there is no statutory obligation to convert FX into Colombian pesos; the exporter may sell the currency to the market at TRM or credit it to an authorized foreign-currency account maintained with a local authorized intermediary. The obligation is a 100% canalization duty: the export must be named in the Declaración de Cambio Formulario No. 4, matched to the DIAN export declaration and bill of lading; failure to channel within the statutory window triggers exchange-control fines and may jeopardize the exporter's access to FX rights.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) – Departamento de Cambios Internacionales; foreign exchange framework established by Law 9 of 1991 (Estatuto Cambiario), Decree 1735 of 1993, Resolución Externa 1 of 2018 of Banco de la República, and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83; operational oversight shared with Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and DIAN for transaction filings and sanctions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility as the primary treasury rail. Colombia does not recognize the EU/UK 'EMI' category; non-bank electronic payment firms (Nequi, RappiPay, Lulo Bank) are licensed as SEDPES (Sociedades Especializadas en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos) and cannot hold export FX, open FDI capital escrow accounts, or supply commercial letters of credit. Foreign EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Revolut Business) may serve as collection-only rails for marketplace receivables, but any export proceeds held offshore must still be repatriated to an authorized Colombian intermediary and channeled through the 'mercado cambiario' within the statutory deadline; unrepatriated offshore FMIs are not a valid canalization. The compliant architecture is a local tier-1 bank account paired with an EMI for convenience collections and immediate onward transfer to the local bank; the EMI cannot legally substitute for an authorized intermediary.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the foreign shareholder must transmit from its own account via SWIFT MT103 to the SAS's account at a Colombian tier-1 authorized intermediary, with Field 70 (Remittance Information) bearing the central-bank concept code 4011 ('Inversión directa de capital en moneda extranjera') and a reference to the share-subscription minutes. The receiving bank liquidates the USD at TRM into COP (or credits an authorized foreign-currency account) and files the Declaración de Cambio Formulario No. 1 (Inversiones Internacionales); the transaction must then be recorded in the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales of Banco de la República within the regulatory deadlines of Circular DCIN-83 (banks require immediate filing; final registration follows within the statutory term). Mandatory verification bundle: RUT, NIT, Certificado de Existencia y Representación Legal from the Cámara de Comercio, bylaws, shareholders' meeting minutes authorizing the capital increase, apostilled corporate documents of the foreign investor, bank signatory forms, and UBO forms. If the funds precede formal share issuance, they must be segregated in a temporary capital-escrow/deposit account; after subscription, they are unrestricted for working capital. Do not commingle trade payments with FDI wires, as mixed-purpose transfers prevent clean concept-code filing and can trigger exchange irregularities.For import of nitrile latex, chemicals, and production machinery, the primary rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for advance payments, open-account settlements, and reimbursement of prepaid LC bills, executed through the local bank's trade desk and declared on the Declaración de Cambio Formulario No. 3 (Importación de Bienes) with the applicable import concept code per Annex 1 of Circular DCIN-83; the bank will reject settlement if the payment does not match the DIAN import declaration; (b) Commercial import documentary letters of credit issued under UCP600 via SWIFT MT700/MT707, denominated in USD or COP, with a standard document set comprising commercial invoice, full clean-on-board bills of lading, packing list, certificate of origin, sterility/quality certificate, fumigation certificate where wooden packaging is used, and an inspection certificate if required; settlement paths include MT103, MT202/MT202COV, and reimbursement instructions (MT740/MT754); (c) documentary collections (DP/DA) for repeat mid-value raw-material shipments. All foreign payments require congruence between the commercial documents, the DIAN import declaration, the INVIMA import authorization where applicable, and the FX channeling; late or undeclared channeling exposes the SAS to exchange fines and loss of FX rights.OTC Deliverable USD/COP Forwards – authorized under Resolución Externa 1 de 2018 for genuine commercial or financial exposure (export/import contracts, registered FDI or dividend obligations); must be booked through an authorized intermediary and may be documented under ISDA or local master agreements.USD/COP FX Options (puts and calls) – permitted for hedging the SAS's export receipts and import payables, with premium payments channeled through the FX market and registered in the exchange declaration.USD/COP Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – permissible both offshore and onshore for hedgers unable to deliver the underlying FX within the TRM timeline; onshore booking through an authorized intermediary is recommended for enforceability, referencing the TRM and declared under Formulario No. 5 (Operaciones de Derivados).Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) USD/COP – available for balance-sheet hedging of foreign-currency shareholder loans or vendor financing, subject to ISDA master documentation and registration with the local intermediary.USD/COP FX Futures and standardized forwards traded on the Bolsa de Valores de Colombia (BVC) with central clearing through Cámara de Riesgo Central de Contraparte (CRCC) – margin-based, cash-settled instruments accessible to authorized exchange participants and suitable for layering price-risk hedges without bilateral credit exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility as the primary treasury rail. Colombia does not recognize the EU/UK 'EMI' category; non-bank electronic payment firms (Nequi, RappiPay, Lulo Bank) are licensed as SEDPES (Sociedades Especializadas en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos) and cannot hold export FX, open FDI capital escrow accounts, or supply commercial letters of credit. Foreign EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Revolut Business) may serve as collection-only rails for marketplace receivables, but any export proceeds held offshore must still be repatriated to an authorized Colombian intermediary and channeled through the 'mercado cambiario' within the statutory deadline; unrepatriated offshore FMIs are not a valid canalization. The compliant architecture is a local tier-1 bank account paired with an EMI for convenience collections and immediate onward transfer to the local bank; the EMI cannot legally substitute for an authorized intermediary.

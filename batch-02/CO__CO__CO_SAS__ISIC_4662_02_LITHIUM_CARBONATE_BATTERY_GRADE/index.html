Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio with jurisdiction over the registered office (e.g., Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá), operating through the Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES), under the administrative oversight of the Superintendencia de Sociedades; tax identification (NIT/RUT) coordinated with DIAN.

Governing statute

Law 1258 of 2008 (Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada regime), Articles 3, 5, 17, 22 and 27; Código de Comercio (Decree 410 of 1971), Articles 110, 354 and 372; Decree 1074 of 2015 (Unified Regulatory Decree for Commerce), Section 2.2.8; Superintendencia de Sociedades, Circular Básica Jurídica.

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento privado de constitución (private incorporation deed) executed by all shareholders, with signatures authenticated before a notary (or elevated to public deed only when real property is contributed), submitted for registration in the Registro Mercantil via the Formulario de Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; Article 5 of Law 1258/2008 permits payment of the subscribed capital, in whole or in part, up to two years from incorporation. No escrow or blocked bank account is required; the deed must state the subscribed capital, paid capital, class and number of shares, and the payment schedule. For an illustrative subscribed capital of COP 200,000,000, the departmental registration tax (0.7% of subscribed capital) plus Chamber tariff results in total official filing fees of approximately USD 430.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); electronic portal MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) at www.dian.gov.co; company incorporation and RUT pre-issuance coordinated through the Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio) under the RUES system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Colombian S.A.S. must: (1) incorporate via private deed or public instrument and register the company before the competent Chamber of Commerce to obtain the existence and representation certificate; (2) file the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) with DIAN through MUISCA using the digital signature (firma electrónica) of the legal representative, thereby obtaining the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria); (3) activate the 'responsable del IVA' status and mandatory electronic invoicing authorization in the same RUT update. Non-resident shareholders do not require a Colombian NIT for holding shares, but dividend payments to them are subject to 10% withholding. The S.A.S. must appoint a legal representative domiciled in Colombia and, for fiscal purposes, an authorized public accountant; a foreign entity acting directly as a non-resident supplier with no permanent establishment must likewise obtain an NIT and designate a tax representative before issuing invoices to Colombian recipients.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists in Colombia; the S.A.S. is an autonomous juridical person taxed under the ordinary corporate regime (35% CIT, worldwide income, foreign tax credit). Available statutory elections relevant to the lithium carbonate arbitrage trade: (1) election to claim export VAT refunds on a bi-monthly basis in Form 300; (2) election to adopt the SIMPLE combined regime (income tax and VAT integrated) where annual gross revenue does not exceed 100,000 UVT (COP 4,706,500,000 in 2024), generally unsuitable for wholesale battery metals arbitrage; (3) election to allocate profits to a foreign PE under applicable DTA rules (Andean Community Decision 578) or domestic territorial attributions; (4) hybrid mismatch and interest-stripping elections per Law 2277/2022 (including the 30% EBITDA interest cap) that permit taxpayers to characterize certain payments consistently between jurisdictions; (5) customs regime elections (free trade zone, temporary import, or transit) for structuring the import-re-export chain.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold in Colombia: all persons performing taxable acts or activities must register as 'responsables del IVA' from the first transaction (Art. 555-2 E.T.). The former simplified regime was abolished in 2018; only natural persons with annual income below 3,500 UVT (COP 164.7 million in 2024) are exempt from invoicing requirements, a privilege not available to a wholesale S.A.S. For the SIMPLE combined regime, the annual turnover ceiling is 100,000 UVT (COP 4,706,500,000 in 2024).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% IVA rate applies to goods physically exported from Colombian customs territory on the basis of a validated export declaration (Art. 481 E.T., Decree 1165/2019). The S.A.S. trader must prove: (a) sale and invoice to a foreign counterparty outside Colombia, (b) physical departure of the goods from Colombian customs jurisdiction (if goods never enter Colombia and are transferred directly from remote supplier to buyer, no Colombian IVA arises for lack of nexus and the transaction is documented as an offshore sale), (c) ownership transfer and payment by the foreign buyer, and (d) settlement of the export proceeds through the Colombian foreign exchange market (MCR) by the exporter or its marketing agent. The resulting credit balance in the bi-monthly Form 300 is generally refundable (solicitud de devolución) or compensable after DIAN's automated two-return review.

  • Bill of Lading (original negotiable transport document evidencing loading on board a vessel in a Colombian port destined to a foreign port; AWB for air freight)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Liquidación de Operaciones de Cambio / posición coordinada emitida por el intermediario cambiario) evidencing repatriation of export proceeds through the Colombian forex market

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta) under Resolution 000165/2023: invoices must be pre-validated in real time through DIAN's MUISCA web services (XML schemas 4.x), bear a unique CUFE/CUNE digital code and QR, and be transmitted to DIAN before issuance to the buyer. 'Documento soporte' electronic documents are required for acquisitions from non-obligated suppliers. Exporters claiming VAT refunds (saldo a favor) face enhanced validation, including DIAN's automated 'radar' system and mandatory certification of invoicing software providers. Failure to transmit or use unauthorized software renders invoices invalid for cost, expense, and VAT refund substantiation.

DIAN reviews under Arts. 705-773 E.T.: 3-year statute for corrective returns, 5 years for omissions, 10 years for fraudulent returns. Specific penalties for export fraud: failure to repatriate or to prove FX settlement within 180 days converts the 0% rate to taxable at 19% with moratory interest (1.5x ordinary debtor rate) and a 20% inaccuracy penalty under Art. 647 E.T., escalated to 100%-200% for simulated or reckless conduct; illegal appropriation of VAT refunds may trigger criminal tax evasion charges (Criminal Code Art. 434-B, imprisonment 4-9 years and fines up to 200% of evaded tax); unjustified refunds are clawed back with full principal, IPC indexation, moratory interest, and a penalty equal to the refunded amount. DIAN's customs intelligence unit cross-matches DEX, B/L, customs manifest, and FX certificates to detect circular transshipments, incoterm misdeclarations (EXW vs FOB), and overpricing that fabricate export refunds; lithium carbonate traders should expect intensified documentary inspection and denial of refunds if the goods do not clear customs through Colombia.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed but broadly free foreign-exchange market (Régimen de Cambios Internacionales) under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, and the BdR regulatory circuit (Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83). Current-account transactions (goods imports/exports, services, remittances of dividends) are freely convertible and require only the registration of each operation through an authorized intermediary (Declaración de Cambio). Capital-account operations, including foreign direct investment (FDI) in a Colombian S.A.S., are freely allowed but must be registered with BdR via the local bank using the international investment declaration (Form 3 of the Declaración de Cambio) within the statutory window (generally before disposition or repatriation, and practically within 12 months of the injection). Profit remittance is legally permissible and subject to no exchange-control quota; however, the paying bank will require (1) an up-to-date FDI registration, (2) proof of tax filing status and withholding levy under Article 245 of the Tax Statute (dividend withholding is applied by the bank at the withholding rate established for the resident/non-resident shareholder category), and (3) a FX declaration executed at the date of remittance. Failure to register the inbound FDI before dividend distribution blocks the remittance. For a trading company, the COP is a fully deliverable current-account currency, but offshore NDF/convertibility gaps exist for capital-account purposes; therefore, all inward arbitration receipts and outward supplier payments should be cleared through regulated local banks rather than offshore EMIs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts from the re-export of battery-grade lithium carbonate (or any Colombian-origin goods) must be repatriated through the official foreign-exchange market within the BdR statutory recaudo term (generally 12 months from the export shipment date, extendable subject to BdR authorization). The full gross export value is subject to the surrender/channeling obligation: the exporter must either convert the proceeds into COP through an authorized intermediary or credit them to a locally-held foreign-currency account or to a previously authorized compensation account abroad, in all cases filing the Declaración de Cambio de Exportación (Form 2). Funds not used for permitted current-account payments (e.g., import settlements) must be converted to COP. Import payments for lithium carbonate procurement, by contrast, are settled pursuant to the applied INCOTERM within the payment term reflected in the import declaration; payments made in advance of physical arrival are permitted but the goods must be imported within the BdR-imposed six-month term, failing which the prepayment is recharacterized as a capital outflow requiring a separate declaration.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (BdR or BanRep), acting together with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) for the authorization and surveillance of foreign-exchange intermediaries, and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) for customs/trade-related foreign-exchange records and export/import declarations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary account. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Intergiro) cannot lawfully perform the mandatory Declaración de Cambio filings with BdR, cannot issue Colombian import-payment FX declarations, and are not authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries. Their use is limited to (i) holding of funds in accounts abroad where the Colombian entity has obtained a compensation-account authorization from BdR, or (ii) receiving commission rebates/ancillary payments; even then, the funds must eventually be channeled into Colombia via the official FX market. EMIs should be used only as ancillary liquidity in transit accounts and never as the settlement rail for trade payables.

Defense evidence: (1) The import/export declarations (Form 1 / Form 2) matching each FX operation to an underlying trade; (2) a documented treasury hedging policy (forwards only, no naked FX positions); (3) board minutes approving the treasury mandate; and (4) monthly reconciliation of FX operations against outstanding trade contracts, attested by the statutory auditor (Revisor Fiscal if appointed).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary account. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Intergiro) cannot lawfully perform the mandatory Declaración de Cambio filings with BdR, cannot issue Colombian import-payment FX declarations, and are not authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries. Their use is limited to (i) holding of funds in accounts abroad where the Colombian entity has obtained a compensation-account authorization from BdR, or (ii) receiving commission rebates/ancillary payments; even then, the funds must eventually be channeled into Colombia via the official FX market. EMIs should be used only as ancillary liquidity in transit accounts and never as the settlement rail for trade payables.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for S.A.S. under Ley 1258/2008; the incorporators fix the capital freely in the bylaws. For the manufacturing scope, a declared paid-in capital of COP 50,000,000 is recommended to support industrial licensing. The capital must be fully paid up and deposited in a Colombian financial institution; a certificate of deposit or escrow receipt must be produced at the time of registry filing.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – National Tax and Customs Authority; official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; transactional services through MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) at https://muisca.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Colombian SAS obtains its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) and RUT (Registro Único Tributario) after incorporation before the Cámara de Comercio. The foreign shareholder must appoint a local legal representative or attorney-in-fact, provide apostilled and certified-translated corporate existence documents, and file the RUT registration through DIAN’s online MUISCA platform. The RUT is the sole tax identification card and must be updated within statutory timelines for any changes in address, legal representative, or taxable activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not provide federal check-the-box entity classification elections. A Colombian SAS is by default a resident tax entity subject to entity-level taxation. Available statutory elections include: (i) the business-group tax consolidation regime (Régimen de Grupo Empresarial) for economic groups holding at least 75% control, subject to prior DIAN authorization; and (ii) the simplified taxation regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación) for eligible taxpayers with gross ordinary income not exceeding the statutory threshold, replacing income tax and VAT obligations under specified conditions. Hybrid-mismatch elections are not available under local law; Colombia applies statutory nexus and controlled-foreign-company rules instead.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis VAT registration threshold. A Colombian SAS engaged in any taxable supply of goods or services must register as a VAT-responsible person (responsable de IVA) before commencing operations and maintain a valid RUT. An optional simplified regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación) is available to eligible taxpayers whose gross ordinary income does not exceed 80,000 UVT (approximately COP 3.8 billion for tax year 2024, adjusted annually by DIAN resolution).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are excluded from Colombian VAT with entitlement to input-VAT credit and refunds when the exporter is a VAT-responsible entity, the goods physically leave the Colombian customs territory under a valid export declaration, the buyer is not domiciled in Colombia, and payment is received through authorized foreign-exchange channels in foreign currency. For services, the exclusion also requires that the service be used exclusively abroad and meet the DIAN’s offshore-services conditions. The exporter must maintain contemporaneous documentation evidencing the actual export, payment, and repatriation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) accepted through DIAN/MUISCA customs platform
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airwaybill (AWB) / transport document
  • Commercial invoice expressly evidencing the export sale
  • Packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank certificate of foreign-currency deposit proving repatriation within the mandated term
  • Electronic export VAT invoice (FEVM) containing the valid CUFE

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta – FEVM) applies to every VAT-registered SAS. Invoices must be transmitted to DIAN’s electronic invoicing platform via DIAN-validated software, DIAN web services/API, or authorized electronic-billing operators. Each invoice must contain a unique CUFE code and QR representation, be signed with the taxpayer’s digital certificate, and be reported to DIAN in near-real time. Electronic dispatch documents are also required for domestic goods movement, and electronic payroll documentation is mandatory where applicable. Non-compliant invoices do not confer VAT credit or expense deductibility.

DIAN conducts targeted audits of export zero-rating and input-VAT refund claims by cross-referencing customs declarations, foreign-exchange receipts, and bank records. Failure to satisfy documentary or substantive requirements causes reversal of the VAT exclusion, disallowance of input credits, and retroactive clawbacks of refunds, together with late-payment interest from the date of undue benefit. Inaccuracy penalties under Article 647 ET may be imposed (generally up to 100% of the overstated refund or benefit), and intentional false export claims are subject to criminal customs and anti-evasion sanctions, including fines and imprisonment under applicable Colombian law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is freely convertible for current-account operations (trade in goods and services, dividends, royalties, interest, personal remittances) provided the transaction is settled through an IMC and documented in the applicable Declaración de Cambio. Capital-account transactions — foreign direct investment (FDI), external credit, and portfolio investment — are also convertible but require registration of the capital entry with Banco de la República so that future dividends and capital repatriation can leave the country without additional licenses or quotas. There are no exchange-rate bands, buy-back quotas, or prior exchange licenses; in practice, profit remittance by a Colombian SAS is hindered only when (i) the inbound FDI was not registered with the central bank, (ii) the ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO) record is incomplete under DIAN rules, or (iii) the remittance is structured as a loan repayment exceeding the registered external credit. For a Colombian SAS manufacturing dialysis consumables, dividend remittances to a non-resident shareholder are lawful after the general shareholders meeting approves the distribution, subject to the applicable dividend withholding tax and bank-level source-of-funds documentation; no DIAN or central bank authorization is required for the remittance itself.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Colombian foreign exchange regime (Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, as amended), 100% of the export proceeds from Colombian goods must be channeled through an IMC. For goods such as dialysis hemofilters, the exporter must lodge the proceeds and sell the foreign currency into the Colombian market within 24 months from the date of the transport document (bill of lading). Service export receipts must be channeled within 6 months from the invoice accrual date. There is no statutory partial conversion ratio; the entire amount is subject to the channeling obligation, although the exporter may temporarily hold the USD in a foreign-currency account at the IMC and sell to the IMC at the prevailing market rate (Tasa Representativa del Mercado, TRM) at any time within the applicable term. Failure to channel exposes the exporter to exchange-control fines (multas cambiarias) up to 200% of the unchanneled amount and may jeopardize the VAT zero-rating of the export and the deductibility of related costs.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia) is the monetary and foreign-exchange authority; FX policy is issued by the Comité de Cambios and administered through Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, while the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) supervises the authorized foreign exchange intermediaries (Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario, IMCs) and enforces exchange-control sanctions jointly with the DIAN.

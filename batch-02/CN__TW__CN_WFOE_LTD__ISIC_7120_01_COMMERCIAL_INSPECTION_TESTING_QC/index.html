Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally rejected by Chinese tax authorities and banks for a WFOE operating as a factory inspection and testing agency. Under the Administrative Measures for Certification and Accreditation and the Inspection and Testing Administration Regulations, the WFOE must maintain a physical premise capable of accommodating inspection equipment, receiving samples, hosting on-site audits, and retaining quality records. Tax authorities require the registered address to be verifiable and to correspond with the actual place of business; a virtual mailbox is deemed fictitious and may trigger an invalidity claim during VAT registration and corporate income tax filing. Banks will reject a virtual address in the KYC review because the branch, operation floor area, and employment presence cannot be verified. Therefore, a physical full-service office or laboratory space must be leased and registered before incorporation.

Competent corporate registry

Department of Commercial Services (商業署), Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA), acting through the MOEA One-Stop Company Registration Information System and the municipal commercial registration windows; foreign investment and cross-Strait investment approvals are gated by the Department of Investment Review (投資審議司), MOEA.

Governing statute

Republic of China (Taiwan) Company Act (公司法), Articles 98-113 (Limited Company principles, including the sole-shareholder Limited Company amendment), Article 387 (filing and registration formalities), and Articles 370-386 (foreign companies); Statute for Investment by Foreign Nationals (外國人投資條例), Articles 3 and 8-10; and the Statute Governing Relations between the People of the Taiwan Area and the Mainland Area (兩岸人民關係條例), Articles 73 and 93, together with the Regulations for Permission of Investment in Taiwan by Mainland Area Entities, applicable because the ultimate beneficial owner is a People's Republic of China entity.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Taiwan Limited Company after the 2009 and 2018 Company Act amendments; however, the subscribed capital must be sufficient to support the stated business scope under the MOEA pre-registration capacity review, and the foreign-currency capital must be remitted from overseas into a Taiwan bank account and verified by a Taiwan-certified public accountant pursuant to Article 156 of the Company Act. A prudent minimum paid-up capital of USD 50,000 is recommended for a mainland-PRC-controlled WFOE. Capital is verified by a licensed CPA and is not escrowed with a government agency.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the municipal Fire Bureau under the Fire Services Act, including fire-extinguishing equipment inspection, evacuation route sign-off, and building fire safety compliance for floors above the prescribed threshold; structural load clearance certified by a Taiwan-registered structural or civil engineer confirming floor loading capacity for any instruments or equipment; and occupational safety clearance administered by the Occupational Safety and Health Administration, Ministry of Labor, under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including worksite inspection, hazard communication for chemicals, employee occupational accident insurance, and annual workplace safety review for personnel performing on-site factory audit assignments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not provide a United States-style check-the-box election. A Taiwan incorporated company is generally a profit-seeking enterprise subject to entity-level corporate income tax, currently generally 20% on taxable income, with separate shareholder-level taxation on distributions subject to applicable rules. A Taiwan branch of a foreign company is generally taxed on Taiwan-source income and Taiwan-attributable profits under branch and permanent-establishment principles, without a separate subsidiary-level dividend-distribution regime. No general hybrid-mismatch election converts a Taiwan company into a pass-through entity. Limited statutory alternatives may include approved small-scale-business business-tax assessment, exempt versus taxable business-tax treatment for legally specified activities, and approved industry-specific incentives; these are qualification-based regimes rather than elective entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold permits an ordinary commercial enterprise to remain outside business-tax registration where it carries on taxable business in Taiwan. Tax registration is generally required before commencement of business. A small-scale business may qualify for an approved simplified or fixed-assessment regime only if it satisfies the statutory conditions. Common administrative thresholds associated with small-scale-business and exemption treatment must not be used as a blanket registration exemption; the competent National Taxation Bureau determines eligibility based on activity, sales, and operating facts.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% business-tax rate may apply to qualifying exported goods and to qualifying services supplied to foreign customers where the statutory conditions establish that the service is used outside Taiwan or otherwise falls within a legislated zero-rated category. For a commercial inspection, factory-audit, testing, or quality-control agency, the result depends on the actual place of performance, contractual recipient, economic use of the report, and evidence of export or offshore use. Services physically performed in Taiwan for a Taiwan customer or for use in Taiwan generally remain taxable at 5%, even if the customer is foreign. A foreign-currency invoice alone does not establish zero-rating. The taxpayer must identify the foreign customer, demonstrate the offshore use or qualifying export transaction, maintain the service agreement, purchase order, report or deliverable, work-performance evidence, invoice, payment records, and foreign-exchange or banking evidence, and apply the correct business-tax treatment on the VAT return. Exported goods generally require proof that the goods left Taiwan under customs control. Input VAT refunds are available only under the statutory rules and require substantiation of the taxable or zero-rated business activity and valid input invoices.

  • For exported goods: Taiwan Customs export declaration or equivalent customs export record showing the exporter, goods, value, and export clearance.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, transport document such as bill of lading or airway bill, and contract or purchase order.
  • Evidence of actual departure from Taiwan and, where applicable, customs release or export confirmation.
  • For exported or offshore services: service agreement, foreign customer's legal identity and tax or business number where available, purchase order, detailed invoice, inspection or audit reports, work logs, site-location evidence, correspondence, and evidence that the service was used outside Taiwan or otherwise satisfies the statutory zero-rate category.
  • Bank remittance advice, inward-remittance record, foreign-exchange settlement record, or other reliable payment evidence linking the consideration to the relevant invoice. An Inward Remittance Certificate is not universally required for every Taiwan zero-rated transaction, but equivalent payment evidence should be retained where available.
  • VAT return schedules and reconciliation between invoices, customs records, service deliverables, receipts, and claimed input-tax refunds.

Taiwan uses electronic invoices rather than a universal pre-clearance model comparable to systems requiring real-time government approval before issuance. Businesses subject to electronic-invoice requirements must issue compliant electronic invoices, use approved invoice formats and identification fields, retain transaction and certification records, and transmit invoice data to the Ministry of Finance electronic-invoice platform within the prescribed period, generally within seven days after issuance or the applicable shorter period for specified transaction types. B2B invoices normally require the purchaser's GUI; B2C invoices may use the electronic-invoice carrier, donation, barcode, or platform mechanisms. Digital signatures, certified service providers, and Ministry of Finance platform authentication may be required depending on the issuance method. Paper invoices remain available only where legally permitted. The entity must reconcile issued invoices, credit notes, export documents, customs data, and VAT return figures. Taiwan does not generally impose universal real-time fiscal clearance or a mandatory digital stamp on every invoice, but noncompliant issuance, delayed transmission, missing GUI data, or unsupported zero-rating can trigger assessments and penalties.

Taiwan has no general universal 180-day foreign-exchange-repatriation deadline that automatically governs every zero-rated export or offshore service. Foreign-exchange reporting, settlement, and inward-remittance obligations depend on the transaction, account structure, banking rules, and applicable foreign-exchange controls; payment timing should therefore be documented rather than treated as a fixed 180-day safe harbor. The National Taxation Bureau may deny zero-rating, assess the unpaid 5% business tax, recover improperly refunded input VAT, impose interest, and impose administrative penalties where export departure, offshore use, customer identity, payment, or business-purpose evidence is inadequate. Fraudulent invoices, fabricated export records, false declarations, concealed sales, or intentional tax evasion may result in statutory business-tax penalties, additional fines calculated by reference to the tax evaded or improperly refunded, invoice-related penalties, surcharge interest, customs penalties, and potential criminal liability for serious or deliberate conduct. Where a refund or zero-rating claim is later rejected, the tax is generally assessed retrospectively for the relevant limitation period, subject to statutory assessment and fraud-extension rules. The entity should retain accounting books, invoices, contracts, customs records, electronic-invoice files, work papers, banking records, and service-delivery evidence for the applicable Taiwan statutory retention period and produce a transaction-level audit trail.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi (RMB) is convertible for current-account transactions once the underlying trade in goods and services is authenticated, but capital-account convertibility remains controlled. WFOE share capital, shareholder loans and cross-border equity contributions must be registered with SAFE and settled through a designated FDI capital account; the receiving bank verifies the funds against the SAFE Foreign Exchange Account monitoring system before permitting RMB conversion. Profit and dividend remittance by the Taiwan shareholder is treated as investment income and requires the WFOE to file annual audited financial statements, approve distribution by board or shareholders resolution, withhold and pay non-resident enterprise income tax at the statutory 10% rate, obtain tax payment certificates, and complete bank-level SAFE reporting. There is no annual aggregate profit-remittance quota, but each remittance must pass authenticity review; retained earnings must be supported by a certified audit report, profit-distribution resolution and tax declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export receipts from the inspection/testing/QC agency must be repatriated to the WFOE's mainland bank account. For goods-trade exports, the standard SAFE trade-integrity verification period is 180 days from customs declaration; for cross-border services such as pre-shipment inspection, factory audit and technical testing, receipts must be received within the contract/invoice due date and reported under the bank's trade-in-services receipt and payment verification system. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, and the post-2012 FX liberalization regime sets the mandatory surrender ratio at 0%; the WFOE may retain foreign exchange in a current-account settlement account or convert it voluntarily at market rate. However, export proceeds may not be routed through shell companies, personal accounts or offshore digital wallets, and each receipt must be linked to an underlying inspection report, contract and invoice identifier.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), exercising delegated authority under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), is the primary FX regulator for the mainland WFOE. Taiwan-side remittance source is supervised by the Financial Supervisory Commission (Taiwan) and the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury rail, Medium only for small-value ancillary payouts. Offshore digital EMIs, including Wise, have no mainland bank licence and cannot open WFOE operating or capital accounts; PBOC and SAFE do not recognize an EMI account as a valid settlement account for FDI capital or export-proceeds verification. The Taiwan parent may use Wise to make low-value payments to Chinese contractors or individual suppliers through licensed mainland payment institutions, but the WFOE's treasury ledger, SAFE filings, invoice matching and tax evidence must remain on bank rails. Legal constraints include no SWIFT MT103 from an EMI for statutory FDI, no CIPS settlement through an unlicensed EMI, restricted RMB clearing, individual foreign-exchange quota limits, and the requirement that all receipts attributable to the WFOE be reported through a licensed onshore bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury rail, Medium only for small-value ancillary payouts. Offshore digital EMIs, including Wise, have no mainland bank licence and cannot open WFOE operating or capital accounts; PBOC and SAFE do not recognize an EMI account as a valid settlement account for FDI capital or export-proceeds verification. The Taiwan parent may use Wise to make low-value payments to Chinese contractors or individual suppliers through licensed mainland payment institutions, but the WFOE's treasury ledger, SAFE filings, invoice matching and tax evidence must remain on bank rails. Legal constraints include no SWIFT MT103 from an EMI for statutory FDI, no CIPS settlement through an unlicensed EMI, restricted RMB clearing, individual foreign-exchange quota limits, and the requirement that all receipts attributable to the WFOE be reported through a licensed onshore bank.

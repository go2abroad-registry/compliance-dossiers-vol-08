Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — official tax and customs portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; transactional portal: Muisca

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Colombian S.A.S. is constituted through a notarized public deed or private document, registered with the local Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio) to obtain the official certificate of existence and legal representation. The legal representative or a designated officer then obtains an NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by completing and submitting Registration Form RUT (Registro Único Tributario) through the DIAN Muisca electronic system or at a DIAN service office. For non-resident shareholders, a local attorney-in-fact with power of attorney is required to sign the RUT filing. There is no minimum capital requirement for the S.A.S.; the NIT is issued immediately upon validation and remains the single statutory taxpayer ID for income tax, VAT, withholding, and customs operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: Colombian-resident SAS entities are taxed on worldwide income under the general corporate income tax regime; no pass-through or flow-through treatment is available. Gains and income are taxed at the entity level; dividends remitted abroad are subject to a separate withholding tax. Cross-border hybrid structures are subject to the statutory control rules and the OECD-aligned anti-hybrid provisions.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' or US-style entity classification election exists. The legal entity form determines tax status: an S.A.S. is always a corporation for income tax and VAT purposes. Optional regimes that may be elected when statutory requirements are met include: (i) the Simple Taxation Regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación – SIMPLE) for small taxpayers under defined turnover thresholds; (ii) special permanent establishment treatment under a Free Trade Zone license; (iii) the VAT non-responsible status only for qualifying natural persons, not for an S.A.S. exporting coffee; and (iv) preferential tax stabilization agreements for certain large investments. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; Colombia applies Law 2277 of 2022 and Decree regulations implementing BEPS Action 2 anti-hybrid rules for cross-border payments and entity characterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general numeric obligatory registration threshold under Colombian VAT law. Any person or entity undertaking a taxable transaction, importing goods, commencing business activities, or holding itself out as a VAT responsible taxpayer must register in the RUT and obtain an NIT. The simplified 'no responsable' VAT status is limited to natural persons whose taxable activity does not involve imports or exports and whose annual gross turnover does not exceed 3,500 UVT (Tax Value Units) as annually adjusted. The Colombian S.A.S. distributing green coffee beans and exporting goods must therefore register and remain a full IVA responsible taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods, including specialty green coffee beans, are zero-rated for IVA purposes under Colombian Law 2010 of 2019 and complementary regulations. Eligibility requires that (i) the goods are physically exported from Colombian customs territory; (ii) the seller is the exporter of record and the customs export declaration is accepted by DIAN; (iii) title, risk, and ownership pass to a non-resident buyer; (iv) the export proceeds are received through the authorized foreign exchange market in accordance with rules of the Banco de la República; and (v) the exporter maintains complete documentary evidence of the exit of goods from Colombia, the receipt of payment, and the identification of the foreign buyer. Offshoring of services to non-residents may also qualify as an export when the service is used abroad and the beneficiary is a non-resident, with specific requirements for declaration and supporting contracts.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) accepted and validated by DIAN customs authorities
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving physical departure from Colombian territory
  • Commercial invoice issued in accordance with DIAN electronic invoicing rules
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the intermediary financial institution evidencing conversion and repatriation of export proceeds
  • Certificate of Origin (where a preferential tariff is claimed) issued by the competent authority or chamber of commerce
  • Insurance and inland transport documentation evidencing the route to the port or airport
  • Veterinary/phytosanitary certificate or FNC-related coffee documentation if required for destination-market admission

DIAN mandates mandatory electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta) for all VAT responsible taxpayers, including SAS exporters. Invoices must be generated in XML format using DIAN-authorized billing software or a certified technological provider; the electronic invoice must include the mandatory fiscal identification elements, an assigned serial like CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica), and a QR code. The invoice XML is transmitted in real time to DIAN through its web services/API environment (Radian and Muisca platforms) for validation and 'prevalidation' before being delivered to the buyer. No physical fiscal stamping is applied; legal validity depends on successful DIAN rejection-free validation. Export invoices must reference the customs export declaration and may be issued with 0% IVA pursuant to statutory export rules.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict DIAN audit scrutiny through cross-matching of electronic invoices, customs exit records, maritime/air transport manifests, and financial-system foreign exchange data. If export documentation is missing, false, or inconsistent, DIAN may reverse the zero-rated treatment, assess IVA plus interest, and impose penalties for inaccurate or false declarations. In the absence of good faith, the penalty can be equal to 100%–200% of the amount of output VAT omitted, and a false export VAT refund may be classified as an inexistent tax loss under Article 647 of the Tax Code, triggering a capitalization penalty and criminal liability for fraudulent VAT refund under Colombian criminal law. Additionally, repatriation beyond the 180-day term without authorized extension may trigger exchange-control sanctions under the foreign exchange regime and loss of zero-rating evidence evidence. Retrospective clawback extends to a period of five years from the filing date under DIAN's general statute of limitations, with extensions when the proceeding is formally opened.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. Current account transactions (imports, exports, services, and remittances of profit) require no prior license or central bank authorization; however, they must be routed through an authorized intermediary (AIM) using the mandatory foreign exchange declaration (Declaración de Cambio, DCC) in accordance with Resolution 2023-01-001903. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment in an SAS and profit repatriation, are subject to mandatory registration with Banco de la República but are not subject to pre-approval. Net profit remittance requires audited financial statements, evidence that the foreign investment is duly registered, and proof that corporate and tax obligations are satisfied. No surrender or forced conversion is applied, but foreign exchange proceeds from exports must be channeled to the AIM within the legally prescribed term.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All merchandise export proceeds, including specialty green coffee beans, must be channeled exclusively through the official foreign exchange market. Colombian law requires exporters to receive and settle export proceeds within six (6) months from the date of the export bill of lading (or the date of the export declaration, as applicable), unless an extended term is approved by Banco de la República under a threshaded commercial financing schedule. The proceeds must be credited to a domestic account of the exporting SAS; the mandatory conversion requirement is functionally limited to the obligation to sell the foreign currency to an authorized intermediary or use it to pay obligations, with the equivalent COP calculated at the representative market rate (TRM). For coffee exports, the general six-month rule applies, subject to the contractual maturity of the documentary collection, LC, or pre-export financing facility.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (the Central Bank of Colombia) is the regulatory authority for foreign exchange and monetary policy. The foreign exchange regime is codified in Resolution 2023-01-001903 and complementary decrees, while the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) exercises customs, taxation, and foreign exchange-related enforcement. Authorized intermediaries (bancos comerciales, financiers, and brokers registered with Banco de la República) must channel all foreign exchange operations above the regulatory threshold.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Offshore electronic money institutions and neo-banks (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Mercury) are not licensed as banking institutions in Colombia and may not provide the mandatory foreign exchange intermediation or DCC reporting required for transactions above the regulatory threshold. These entities can only provide ancillary inbound/outbound transfer services through a locally licensed payment company (authorized by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia), but they cannot replace the domestic bank account required for trade finance and FDI registration. Any routing of export proceeds through an offshore EMI without an AIM would breach Colombian foreign exchange law and trigger penalties with DIAN and Banco de la República.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Offshore corporate ownership with opaque ultimate beneficial ownership or multiple intermediate holding companies.

Root cause: Offshore corporate ownership with opaque ultimate beneficial ownership or multiple intermediate holding companies.

Defense evidence: Full corporate registry extract, notarized own-certification of the SAS's commercial structure, apostilled share register, up-to-date UBO declaration listing all natural persons with economic interest, passport copies, audited financial statements, and a clear business plan for the Colombian import/export operation.
High transaction velocity in first 90 days and counterparties domiciled in jurisdictions outside the coffee origin country without a justified trade pattern.

Root cause: High transaction velocity in first 90 days and counterparties domiciled in jurisdictions outside the coffee origin country without a justified trade pattern.

Defense evidence: Executed sales and purchase contracts, proforma invoices, container shipping schedule, B/L and airway bills, SGS or Intertek quality inspection certificates, phytosanitary certificates, country-of-origin certificates, cargo insurance, and a reconciliation report showing the sourcing and selling margin in the Colombian entity.
Wire instructions routed to a private individual or an unregulated intermediary for payment of green coffee purchases.

Root cause: Wire instructions routed to a private individual or an unregulated intermediary for payment of green coffee purchases.

Defense evidence: Commercial contract naming the beneficiary, broker or agent agreement, invoice trail, evidence that the underlying coffee is physically transiting through the specified ports, and a DIAN export/import declaration reference number. All exceptions must be supported by a risk-mitigation memo approved by the bank's compliance unit.
Lack of economic substance in Colombia or mismatch between declared objective and bank account usage.

Root cause: Lack of economic substance in Colombia or mismatch between declared objective and bank account usage.

Defense evidence: Lease agreement for a commercial office, employment contracts or payroll records of local staff, corporate tax filings (RUT, ICA, VAT), prior-period financial statements, and a 12-month treasury forecast demonstrating that the account is used exclusively for permitted coffee trade operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder must transmit a SWIFT wire in COP optionally converted at the bank's bid rate, or in USD, EUR, GBP, or another convertible currency through a correspondent bank to the AIM (e.g., Bancolombia or Banco de Bogotá). The wire instruction must reference: the CO_SAS name, tax ID (NIT), the exact purpose code 'Inversión Internacional – Capital de Empresas' (DCC code 10), and the beneficiary account number. The AIM will report the transaction to Banco de la República on the investor's behalf; the SAS must file the DCC Formulario 10 (Inversión Internacional) within the statutory window (generally thirty calendar days from receipt) and maintain the registration certificate for future profit remittance and liquidation. There is no mandatory capital escrow, but the funds must be registered as paid-in capital in the equity books and not used to finance related-party current accounts without a separate FX declaration.Primary clearing rails are Swift Wire (MT103) and Documentary Letters of Credit (UCP600). For MT103 settlements, the Colombian bank will debit the SAS's local COP account or the seller's USD account, converting at the TRM, and the payment instruction must include: contract reference, invoice number, country-of-origin certificate, and DIAN import declaration number when the transaction is simultaneous with customs clearance. For Documentary Letters of Credit, the CO bank issues an irrevocable LC for the green coffee shipment against prescribed documents including proforma invoice, phytosanitary certificate, certificate of origin, and B/L; the LC can be financed under a pre-import loan (prefinanciación de importaciones) using the coffee inventory as collateral.

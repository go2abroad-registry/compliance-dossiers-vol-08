Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN), Colombian national tax and customs authority. Official portal: www.dian.gov.co; transactional e-services platform: MUISCA (muisca.dian.gov.co).

E-government filing portal

The S.A.S. is incorporated by private deed or public deed filed with the local Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio) through the RUES system, which simultaneously generates the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) as the statutory tax identification number. Within one month of incorporation, the legal representative must register the company in the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) through DIAN's MUISCA platform, activating all national tax obligations: corporate income tax, VAT (régimen común), withholding at source, and electronic invoicing. Mandatory auxiliary steps: (i) obtain a DIAN-authorized digital signature certificate (firma electrónica) and (ii) activate electronic invoicing via certified software or DIAN's e-invoicing application. For the luxury resale e-commerce model, no statutory license is required; however, the RUT must reflect the ISIC 4791 activity and the company requires a registered address in Colombia plus a legal representative. Foreign non-resident shareholders may directly own 100% of the S.A.S.; no Colombian resident agent is required for the entity itself, although foreign entities without a permanent establishment engaging directly in Colombian taxable transactions must appoint a domiciled representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: resident S.A.S. is a separate taxpayer liable to corporate income tax (Impuesto sobre la Renta y Complementarios) on worldwide income at the 35% statutory rate for FY2024-FY2025; no pass-through or check-the-box election is available; Colombian territorial source rules apply to non-resident shareholders, and dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders trigger an additional 10% withholding tax. Controlled-foreign-corporation (CFC) rules apply to foreign subsidiaries.

Available election forms

Colombia does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections: (i) Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE): optional for legal entities with prior-year gross revenue equal or below 80,000 UVT (approximately COP 3,765,200,000 for 2024; UVT 2024 = COP 47,065); SIMPLE substitutes income tax, VAT, and ICA with a single gradual tax at combined rates ranging from 1.8% to 14.5% depending on sector and revenue bracket; election is made in the RUT/MUISCA and is irrevocable for two taxable years; (ii) fiscal consolidation regime for an economic group: a parent holding at least 50% of its subsidiaries may request authorization from DIAN to consolidate taxable income; (iii) export VAT exclusion under Article 481 of the Estatuto Tributario applies automatically upon substantive and documentary compliance, requiring no formal election; (iv) hybrid-mismatch regulations under Decree 2120 of 2017 are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions and no elective hybrid treatment is available.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating currency (COP). Current account operations, including trade in goods and services, are freely convertible via authorized exchange intermediaries (locally registered commercial banks). Capital account operations, especially foreign direct investment into a Colombian S.A.S., require prior registration with Banco de la República and must be channeled through the official exchange market. Dividends and capital repatriations are permitted once the investment is duly registered; remittances are subject to applicable dividend withholding taxes and a 5% remittance tax for branch remittances (not for dividend distributions from a S.A.S. as a sub-national? The S.A.S. is a corporation; profit distribution to non-residents is taxed as dividend withholding). There are no non-tariff quantitative restrictions on currency conversion for legitimate commercial transactions, but all foreign exchange inflows and outflows must be reported through the 'Declaración de Cambio' filed by the local intermediary bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of goods must channel hard-currency proceeds through an authorized exchange intermediary within four (4) months from the date of shipment, extendable up to six (6) months for certain agricultural and mining products. For exporters of services, the term is six (6) months from the date of completion. The mandatory conversion ratio is 100%: the full amount of received foreign currency must be sold to the authorized intermediary (converted into COP) at the prevailing market rate. An exchange declaration (Formulario OPI-1) must be submitted with each settlement. Exemptions apply only for specific free-trade-zone regimes or where the value falls below the central bank de-minimis threshold; however, the presumption is full surrender and channeling for all export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. The foreign exchange regime is governed by Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, Decree 1068 of 2015, and Banco de la República External Resolution No. 8 of 2000, as amended. The Ministry of Finance and Public Credit supervises fiscal aspects of exchange control.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for ancillary operations, Low for primary settlement rail. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not authorized by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia as exchange intermediaries, and cannot replace the mandatory 'canalización' of Colombian export proceeds through a local accredited bank. Using an offshore EMI to receive proceeds from the company's Colombian export e-commerce transactions would violate the foreign exchange regime and may be penalized under Law 9/1991 and Law 15/2001 (money-laundering offences). An EMI may be used as a downstream payment collector for non-Colombian foreign subsidiaries, provided all funds ultimately are repatriated in compliance with Banco de la República rules and are declared as exports of services or goods.

Defense evidence: Provide for each UBO: passport true-copy with notarial translation, home-country tax identification, bank reference letter, and certified statement of source of wealth. The corporate dossier must include a sector-specific AML manual ('SAGRILAFT') covering supplier due diligence, product authentication, provenance and chain-of-custody documentation, item-level valuation reports by recognized appraisers, and a transaction monitoring plan with parameters for high-risk counterparties.
Inbound capital injection from a non-resident shareholder without prior foreign investment registration with Banco de la República

Root cause: Inbound capital injection from a non-resident shareholder without prior foreign investment registration with Banco de la República

Defense evidence: Submit the preliminary investment registration form (Formulario 160-2) and the subsequent exchange declaration (OPI-1) issued by the receiving bank, along with the incorporation certificate (Cámara de Comercio), the share subscription agreement, and a legal opinion on the origin of funds. Obtain the definitive 'Registro de Inversión Internacional' (RII) within 30 days of the wire transfer. For transfers above USD 100,000, add shareholder audited financials and a notarized wealth declaration.

Root cause: High velocity cross-border incoming wires from multiple foreign e-commerce counterparties without apparent commercial rationale, triggering enhanced due diligence (EDD)

Defense evidence: Maintain and share a complete, real-time ledger of all buy/sell transactions on the online resale platform, including order IDs, shipping documents, customs declarations (where required), authentication certificates, and payment receipts. Implement IP/geo-blocking for FATF-listed high-risk jurisdictions, run sanctions against OFAC/UN/EU lists, and submit to the bank a monthly 'transaction rationale' report, evidencing that each wire corresponds to a physical or digital dispatch of a covered luxury item.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) Execute a capital contribution agreement under the S.A.S. bylaws; (2) Appoint a local commercial bank before the transfer; (3) File the 'Registro de Inversión Internacional' pre-registration with Banco de la República (through the bank); (4) Send the wire via SWIFT MT103 using the bank's intermediary/beneficiary reference (customs code 'CCP'?); the payment narrative must include the company's NIT (tax ID) and the investment registration number; (5) Upon receipt, the bank issues a 'Declaración de Cambio' (Form OPI-1) and credits the funds to the company's foreign-currency or COP account; (6) Finalize the foreign investment registration with the Banco de la República and update the company's share registry and commercial registry (RUE). Verification steps include bank KYC of the remitter (passport, source-of-funds declaration) and matching the EMI/remitter name to the registered foreign investor.For payments to overseas suppliers of vintage luxury merchandise, use standard SWIFT MT103 payment orders with the purpose code 'Imports of goods' (or 'Commissions/Logistics') in the exchange declaration.

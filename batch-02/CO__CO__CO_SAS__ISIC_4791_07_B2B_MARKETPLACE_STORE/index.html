Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital: the S.A.S. capital is freely stipulated in the bylaws; paid contributions are due on the agreed date and in all cases within two (2) years of incorporation (Ley 1258/2008, art. 5). No escrow account or blocked capital deposit is required by law.

Funding & unlock path: Office premises require an active annual fire-safety certificate (Certificado de Bomberos) issued by the competent fire brigade; the employer must implement the SG-SST occupational health and safety system (Decreto 1072 de 2015) including occupational-medical assessments and an emergency response plan; if the office accommodates public traffic, obtain the sanitary concept (concepto sanitario) from the local health authority. Structural-load and land-use compliance is confirmed through the landlord's building occupancy certificate.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital: the S.A.S. capital is freely stipulated in the bylaws; paid contributions are due on the agreed date and in all cases within two (2) years of incorporation (Ley 1258/2008, art. 5). No escrow account or blocked capital deposit is required by law.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); primary online portal: www.dian.gov.co; transactional portal: www.muisca.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor incorporates a Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.) before the Cámara de Comercio, then completes the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) application with DIAN to obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), which serves as the unified tax ID for income tax, IVA, withholding taxes, and electronic invoicing. A foreign enterprise with no Colombian branch may alternatively obtain an NIT under the DIAN simplified registration regime for non-resident digital service providers, which requires the appointment of a local legal representative and the issuance of a virtual RUT. No minimum paid-in capital is required for the S.A.S.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (the Colombian S.A.S. is a resident legal entity taxed on worldwide net income at the corporate rate; pass-through treatment is unavailable; dividends distributed to non-residents are subject to final withholding at the applicable treaty or statutory rate) — A foreign investor incorporates a Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.) before the Cámara de Comercio, then completes the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) application with DIAN to obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), which serves as the unified tax ID for income tax, IVA, withholding taxes, and electronic invoicing. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the Colombian S.A.S. is a resident legal entity taxed on worldwide net income at the corporate rate; pass-through treatment is unavailable; dividends distributed to non-residents are subject to final withholding at the applicable treaty or statutory rate)

Available election forms

No check-the-box or look-through election exists under Colombian law. Statutory elections that remain available are: (i) the consolidated income tax return option for subsidiaries meeting the 51% ownership continuity test under Articles 128-133 of the Estatuto Tributario; (ii) the SIMPLE presumptive regime election for qualifying micro/small businesses with ordinary gross income below 80,000 UVT (approx. COP 3.77 billion for 2024, annually adjusted); and (iii) the Colombian holding company regime (Régimen CHC) election for pure holding S.A.S. vehicles. Hybrid mismatch outcomes under Law 2010 of 2019 and Law 2277 of 2022 are policed by disallowance rules rather than activated by taxpayer elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold in local currency: every Colombian legal entity, including an S.A.S. marketplace operator, must register in the RUT and as an IVA responsible taxpayer from its first corporate act. Non-resident digital service providers are likewise subject to mandatory DIAN registration without any de minimis turnover exemption and invoice from the first Colombian-destination digital service.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Colombian S.A.S. that operates an electronic B2B wholesale marketplace may issue its platform commissions, listing, and subscription invoices at the 0% IVA export rate under Article 481 of the Estatuto Tributario when the recipient is not domiciled in Colombia and the digital intermediation service is economically used abroad. The claim also requires a written export service contract, a zero-rated invoice, and the channeling and repatriation of proceeds through the Colombian foreign exchange market within the applicable deadline (180 days for goods; services must satisfy the applicable Banco de la República term). The zero rate is denied if the counterparty is a Colombian resident user, if the platform facilitates consumption within Colombia, or if physical goods are sold without an actual customs export declaration and departure from the national territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX/DAB) issued by DIAN customs authorities for physical goods exported through the marketplace
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / ground waybill) demonstrating the foreign destination and physical exit of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas / Declaración de Cambio) proving the currency was repatriated and the export proceeds were dedicated to the Colombian foreign exchange market
  • Export service contract naming the non-domiciled buyer and evidencing the extraterritorial use of the platform service
  • Zero-rated commercial export invoice carrying the CUNE and the 0% IVA annotation
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and the relevant INCOTERM supporting the sale for customs provenance

Factura Electrónica de Venta is compulsory and operates as a near-real-time fiscal clearance system. Invoices must be generated in an XML/JSON file, signed with a DIAN-approved digital signature certificate, and transmitted through DIAN web services (REST/SOAP API) or a certified technology provider; DIAN validates the invoice and returns the CUNE (Código Único de Numeración); a QR code and the CUNE must be printed on the invoice. Export invoices and documents supporting purchases from non-residents use the CUDS/CUFE codes and must be transmitted to DIAN with the relevant annexes.

DIAN subjects exporter VAT refund applications to documentary pre-verification and selective post-audit. If the export condition is not sustained, DIAN retroactively assesses the 19% IVA on the gross proceeds together with moratory interest under Article 635 E.T., imposes the inaccuracy sanction of 50% to 160% of the understated tax under Article 648 E.T., and may apply the additional penalty for false operations; in customs export scenarios, sanctions include seizure of goods and customs infraction penalties, potentially escalating to criminal proceedings under the customs regime. Failure to comply with the 180-day forex repatriation requirement rebuts the export evidence and opens the clawback within the five-year statute of limitations, and fraudulent zero-rating documentation attracts criminal exposure before the DIAN and public prosecutors.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso is freely convertible for current-account transactions, including service exports and after-tax remittances of profits, provided settlement is executed through the Mercado Cambiario (MCR). Capital-account operations (initial FDI subscription, equity increases, external loans) remain fully deregulated today but require formal filing of the 'Declaración de Cambio – Formulario No. 4' with the Banco de la República through a licensed intermediary; a foreign investor that completes the Formulario No. 4 registration on or before the settlement date may later remit up to 100% of after-tax distributable profits without prior approval, but failure to register the investment before repatriation exposes the entity to FX-infringement fines and potential rejection of the outward remittance by the local intermediary. No general exchange-control freeze currently applies to ordinary current or capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of platform services must channel 100% of the aggregate foreign-exchange proceeds through the MCR within a maximum statutory period of six (6) months from the date of invoice or service completion; proceeds received by the S.A.S. through offshore collection wallets (Alibaba Pay) become subject to the same deadline once settlement takes place, and the funds must be credited to an MCR-authorized local bank account before any 'Declaración de Cambio' can be filed. There is no minimum mandatory conversion ratio to COP: upon channeling, the exporter holds free disposal over the currency and may either (i) sell it to the local intermediary against COP or (ii) retain it in an authorized foreign-currency deposit account; direct retention abroad beyond the deadline is a violation subject to DIAN fines of up to 200% of the un-channeled amount.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (the Central Bank of Colombia), acting through the Régimen Cambiario built on Ley 9 de 1991, Decreto 1735 de 1993 and Resolución Externa No. 1 de 2018; enforcement of FX channeling obligations is delegated to the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN), while the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia supervises the authorized MCR intermediaries.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM as a standalone treasury rail and MEDIUM-HIGH as a settlement gateway. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Alibaba Pay) are neither Colombian-licensed MCR institutions nor authorized FX intermediaries; using them as the final deposit location technically violates the channeling duty under the foreign-currency channeling provisions of Resolución Externa No. 1 de 2018 and can trigger DIAN non-compliance fines of up to 200% of the underlying amount. Compliant use is therefore restricted to: (a) collecting B2B marketplace receipts from foreign buyers, (b) routing weekly sweeps to the Colombian USD account of the S.A.S., and (c) maintaining settlement reports that reconcile Alibaba Pay payout files with the Declaración de Cambio forms filed by the local bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% (general corporate income tax rate effective from tax year 2023 onward under Law 2277 of 2022; no CREE surcharge applies to a standard S.A.S. operating an electronic B2B wholesale platform)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding on dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder out of profits already subjected to the 35% corporate income tax (Articles 242 and 245 E.T.); 35% withholding applies to distributions made from previously untaxed profits or from earnings classified as exempt where the exemption is not recognized at the source level. Domestic Colombian corporate shareholders receiving dividends from taxed earnings are exempt; the 10% rate is therefore the operative non-resident rate for a standard B2B marketplace S.A.S.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when the Colombian S.A.S. reports annual gross income equal or superior to COP 2,117,925,000 (equivalent to 45,000 UVT, UVT-2024 = COP 47,065) and executes transactions with related parties or with entities located in jurisdictions treated as tax havens; Master File mandatory when the ultimate parent group's consolidated gross income equals or exceeds COP 470,650,000,000 (equivalent to 10,000,000 UVT-2024). The annual UVT is re-indexed by DIAN each year, so the precise peso threshold must be re-calculated at filing date.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not enforce offshore-format Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); instead, DIAN applies the general anti-abuse doctrine (Article 869 E.T.), the principle of substance over form, and the corporate/veil-piercing rules of Law 1258 of 2008 to invalidate structures without commercial reality. The B2B marketplace S.A.S. must maintain physical or virtual office infrastructure, decision-making personnel, platform IT management, and economic risk absorption in Colombia; the foreign parent must not act as a letterbox. Without demonstrable substance, DIAN can re-characterize the entire platform commercialization structure, deny expense deductions, reframe royalty or service payments as dividends subjected to the 10% or 35% withholding, and impose the 100% inexactness penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Evasión Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code (Código Penal) Article 402: imprisonment of 48 to 108 months when the tax evaded exceeds 100 legal monthly minimum wages; DIAN inexactness penalty of 100% of the difference between the assessed and declared tax; moratory interest on all underpaid amounts; asset seizure and liquidation proceedings by the DIAN fiscal collection authority.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and the directors who sign the return are jointly and severally liable; criminal conviction carries imprisonment, travel bans preventing departure from Colombian territory, freezing of personal bank accounts, confiscation of assets, and debarment from commercial activity; Article 43 of Law 1258 of 2008 pierces the S.A.S. veil where the corporate form is used to defraud the treasury.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance (Abuso de Formas Societarias)

Statutory sanction: DIAN rejection of the legal structure under the GAAR and substance-over-form doctrine: inclusion of the full re-characterized gross margin in the S.A.S. taxable income at 35%, disallowance of all rechargeable expenses, 100% inexactness penalty on the resulting tax deficiency, and reclassification of targeted payments as unpermitted dividend remittances with withholding assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors of the S.A.S. and the foreign parent's shadow directors who act as de facto managers in Colombia face joint and several liability for the entire tax deficiency, civil restitution of diverted assets, judicial intervention orders, and criminal prosecution for fraud where creditors or the tax authority were deceived.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists under Colombian law; an S.A.S. is mandatorily taxed as a resident corporation. Voluntary elections available include: (i) the SIMPLE (Régimen Simple de Tributación) presumptive turnover tax for qualifying legal entities with prior-year gross income not exceeding 80,000 UVT (approximately COP 3,765,200,000 for 2024), replacing income tax and municipal ICA with a combined gross-receipts scale of 2.0%–14.5%; (ii) the foreign tax credit claim for taxes paid abroad on foreign-source income included in the Colombian taxable base; and (iii) election of certain valuation and inflation-adjustment treatments in the Estatuto Tributario for inventories and fixed assets. Hybrid-mismatch treatment is mandatory under Law 2277 of 2022 and is not elective; no partnership-style pass-through election is available to shareholders of an S.A.S.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold applies to corporations: every legal entity, including an S.A.S., that performs any VAT-taxable sale of goods or services in Colombia must register in the RUT as an IVA-responsible taxpayer in the 'régimen común' irrespective of revenue. The only de minimis exemption concerns natural persons with prior-year gross income not exceeding 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 164,700,000 for 2024), classified as 'no responsables'. A manufacturing S.A.S. exporting canned boba tapioca pearls therefore has a mandatory VAT registration duty from the commencement of operations, with no grace period or registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods, including canned and aseptically tubed ambient quick-cook tapioca pearls, qualify for the 0% VAT rate provided that: (i) the S.A.S. is registered in the RUT as an IVA-responsible taxpayer and exporter with a valid DIAN customs authorisation; (ii) the goods are shipped by the S.A.S. to a purchaser domiciled outside Colombia and physically leave Colombian territory, as evidenced by a definitively accepted Customs Export Declaration (DEX); (iii) the sale price is duly channelled through the Colombian foreign exchange market and the proceeds are repatriated within the statutory 180-day period; (iv) the food product holds the applicable INVIMA sanitary/export certificate; and (v) the exporter retains the full documentary trail. For services exports, the beneficiary must be a non-resident and the service must be used exclusively abroad. The resulting zero-rated credit or refund ('saldo a favor') is claimed in the applicable bimonthly IVA return.

  • Commercial Invoice reflecting export price, INCO terms, and cross-referenced to the electronic sales invoice and CUFE
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch of goods from Colombia
  • Packing List

Mandatory electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica de venta – FE) under DIAN Resolution 000170 of 2021 and complementary rules. All VAT-responsible S.A.S. exporters must issue FE in real time through DIAN's MUISCA web services or an authorized software provider, transmitting XML data and the graphical representation to DIAN for validation before delivery ('validación previa'). Each validated invoice receives a unique CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) or CUDS code, reflected in the invoice PDF and QR stamp. Invoices must be digitally signed using DIAN-issued X.509 certificates. No physical fiscal stamp applies; clearance is exclusively electronic in real time. Standard contingency procedures are permitted only when DIAN platforms are unavailable. Export sales must be represented in the FE with the export reference and cross-linked to the DEX and transportation documentation.

DIAN verifies export zero-rating claims by cross-matching the DEX, transport documents, FE export invoices, and foreign exchange repatriation certificates. If goods are not actually exported or documentation is false, DIAN retroactively claws back the 19% VAT previously zero-rated or refunded, plus default interest from the original date of benefit. Administrative sanctions for inaccurate or fraudulent declarations under Article 648 of the Estatuto Tributario range from 100% to 160% of the tax difference; separate exchange-control fines apply for unrepatriated export proceeds. Where the scheme is qualified as tax fraud ('evasión fiscal') under Law 2277 of 2022, criminal liability carries imprisonment of up to 9 years and fines of up to 200 SMLMV (statutory monthly minimum wages), in addition to suspension or removal of the exporter status and joint liability of the legal representative.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a free-market convertibility model: the COP is fully convertible on both current and capital account, but all proceeds from exports, import payments, foreign-debt disbursements, and direct foreign investment must be 'canalized' (channeled) through the exchange market (Mercado Cambiario) via authorized intermediaries (banks and financial institutions subject to SFC oversight). Profits and dividends may be remitted without restriction if the inbound investment is registered with Banco de la República through Formulario No. 3 (SIREG), the local company has filed its annual corporate income tax return, and the 10% dividend remittance withholding (7.5% in certain structures under tax reform) is settled; unregistered inbound capital loses the right to free repatriation and exposes the remitting party to exchange-control sanctions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Resolución No. 2021 de 2021 and Resolución No. 768 de 2022 of Banco de la República, exporters of goods are required to collect (repatriate) the full invoice value of export proceeds within 4 months after the customs export declaration (DEX), with an extension up to 13 months for goods whose underlying commercial payment terms exceed 4 months (subject to the entity declaring the extended term). There is no percentage-based surrender ratio (i.e., no mandatory sale of a fixed portion); however, if proceeds are held abroad in a 'cuenta de compensación' they must be brought into Colombia and channeled to the exchange market within the prescribed term. Failure to comply may trigger fines of up to 200% of the non-channeled amount and disqualification of the exporter's operations from tax benefits.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting with the Finance Ministry and Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) as co-regulator of the 'régimen cambiario' (exchange control regime); the DIAN monitors export/import trade data for exchange-control matching.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Marginal as primary banking rails. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not licensed by the Superintendencia Financiera to open COP accounts or to receive export proceeds on behalf of a Colombian SAS, and using them for export collections violates the mandatory channeling requirement, which may render the export proceeds legally unrepatriated and expose the tax returns to recharacterization. Limited viable ancillary use: disbursement of pre-invoice expenses not subject to exchange-control channeling (e.g., micro-royalties or distributor commissions in EUR), provided the value remains immaterial and is documented for DIAN transfer pricing. Recommend installing the local SFC-licensed SEDPE (Sociedad Especializada en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos) for domestic digital collections, not for cross-border settlements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends paid to non-resident corporate and entity shareholders, pursuant to Article 245 of the Colombian Tax Code as amended by Law 2277 of 2022. Dividends sourced from profits that have not borne CIT may be subject to an additional 10% levy, making the aggregate top-up taxation comparable to the ordinary shareholder rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation and filing are triggered when, in the immediately preceding tax year, the taxpayer's gross income or gross assets (patrimonio bruto) equals or exceeds 100,000 UVT (approximately COP 4,706,500,000 for tax year 2024). Additionally, any cross-border related-party transaction must be supported by contemporaneous arm's length documentation, and the Form 120 informative return is required when the thresholds are met. The tax authority may also request specific documentation for transactions below the threshold where there is evidence of manipulation.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, the Colombian Tax Code imposes an independent economic substance requirement through the arm's length standard, the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) under Article 869, and sectoral substance indicators such as physical premises, qualified personnel, real business risks, and actual decision-making in Colombia. A shell S.A.S. with no local substance, used to route boba export proceeds or intercompany profits, may be disregarded and the transactions recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under Article 402 of Law 599 of 2000 is punishable by imprisonment of 36 to 60 months and fines; DIAN may impose the inexactness penalty of up to 100% of the omitted tax plus default interest, current monetary correction, and seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign false returns or direct the evasion are jointly and severally liable; travel bans, preventive detention, the freezing of personal assets, and criminal convictions may apply.
Transfer Pricing Violation / Missing TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: DIAN may adjust the taxable income, disallow non-arm's length deductions, impute income, impose the penalty under Article 651 of up to 20,000 UVT, and charge late payment interest. Criminal liability may arise if the adjustment is part of a fraudulent scheme.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve incomplete TP documentation or intercompany agreements without economic substance assume solidary liability for penalties; personal travel bans and asset freezes are possible in tax-fraud investigations.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the entity under the GAAR and attribute all taxable income directly to the foreign owner, apply the 35% CIT plus inexactness penalties, deny input VAT credits, and request administrative dissolution. WHT underpayments may also be assessed on deemed repatriations.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing is available where the S.A.S. is an alter ego; directors and shareholders may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and criminal fines, and authorities may impose travel bans pending the outcome of tax and money-laundering investigations.
Profit Repatriation and Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: DIAN will treat the payment as taxable and disallow the deduction, assess the uncollected withholding, impose a penalty of 20% of the unrecognized transaction value, and charge monthly arrears. Underreported distributions may also be subject to the dividend top-up tax and criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and financial officers are personally liable for withholding taxes that should have been collected and paid; personal liability extends to criminal charges for misappropriation, travel bans, and seizure of personal bank accounts and real estate.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Uruguayan? Colombian AML regime under Law 526 of 1999, UAFE rules, and Article 323 of the Criminal Code penalizes money laundering with imprisonment of 9 to 15 years, heavy fines, asset forfeiture, and the freezing of corporate and personal accounts. Export invoices with false values are treated as trade-based money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and signatories can be criminally prosecuted for failure to implement SARLAFT procedures or for knowingly executing suspicious trade payment instructions; precautionary measures include travel bans, passport revocation, and pretrial detention.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio of the corporate domicile acting through the Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES) portal, with oversight by the Superintendencia de Sociedades.

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008, Art. 1-46 (Sociedades por Acciones Simplificadas) in conjunction with Código de Comercio (Decreto 410 de 1971), Art. 98-141, Decreto 1074 de 2015 (single regulatory decree for commercial registries) and Ley 1429 de 2010 (business formalization incentives).

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (private incorporation instrument) certified before the Chamber of Commerce; notarial escritura pública is required only when real property or assets subject to public registration are contributed.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for S.A.S. under Ley 1258/2008, Art. 5; the constitution instrument fixes subscribed and paid capital, and deferred capital installments may not exceed two years; no escrow account is legally mandated for cash subscriptions, but paid capital must be traceable by bank transfer and recorded in the incorporation document.

Funding & unlock path: Certificado de Bomberos (fire safety certificate) evidencing compliance with Decree 1072/2015 Chapter 6 on fire prevention, extinguishing means, and evacuation conditions; structural load and seismic-resistance review certificate for the industrial building per NSR-10 and Law 400/1997 issued through the Curaduría Urbana; workplace safety and health program (SG-SST) registered with the ARL and subject to Ministry of Labor inspection; for the food-grade silicone claim, municipal health secretary sanitary authorization or INVIMA food-contact material certification of the molding compound.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for S.A.S. under Ley 1258/2008, Art. 5; the constitution instrument fixes subscribed and paid capital, and deferred capital installments may not exceed two years; no escrow account is legally mandated for cash subscriptions, but paid capital must be traceable by bank transfer and recorded in the incorporation document.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a free-floating exchange-rate regime with no multi-tiered currency system. Both current-account and capital-account operations are freely convertible, but the S.A.S. must comply with the mandatory channeling obligation (canalizacion) of all foreign-currency payments through an IMC and must file the corresponding Exchange Declarations (Declaraciones de Cambio). Current-account flows such as export proceeds, service fees and royalties are remittable without prior authorization. Capital-account flows, including inbound equity injections, must be registered with Banco de la Republica under Form 4 (Declaracion de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales) to secure the legal right to later repatriate dividends, reduce capital and remit liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance is permitted conditioned on: (i) the foreign investment registration; (ii) the channeling of the dividend payment via an IMC; and (iii) compliance with Colombian dividend withholding tax rules. There are no exchange-rate conversion quotas; the system measures convertibility through channeling and documentation rather than quantitative controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Colombian exporters of goods (including food-grade silicone tactile fidgets classified under ISIC 3240), the foreign-currency proceeds from the export must be channeled through the foreign-exchange market within the surrender term stated in the export declaration, subject to a maximum term of six months calculated from the date of shipment/export. The exporter may request a one-time extension of up to an additional six months for justified collection reasons, subject to IMC approval. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into Colombian pesos; the exporter may either sell the currency to the IMC, maintain the proceeds in a foreign-currency checking or savings account with an IMC, or use the funds to settle authorized foreign currency obligations. The mandatory documentary compliance includes filing Form 2 (Declaracion de Cambio por Exportaciones) with the IMC, together with the definitively accepted export declaration (DEX), the transport document and the commercial invoice. Failure to channel within the deadline gives rise to exchange-control fines (multas cambiaras) and may trigger adverse risk-rating by the SFC.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la Republica de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), which administers the International Exchange Control Regime (Regimen de Cambios Internacionales) under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993 and Decree 1068 of 2015. Day-to-day channeling and declaration duties are performed exclusively through authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries (Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario, IMCs), primarily commercial banks supervised by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC). The exchange-control sub-agencies may also act under the central bank's authorization for specific non-bank transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as a primary local banking rail; Medium as an ancillary international receivable-collection corridor for platform-pay flows. Legal Constraints: non-Colombian electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are not IMCs under Colombian exchange-control law and therefore cannot be used as the exclusive repository of the S.A.S.'s export proceeds without violating the channeling obligation. EMIs cannot process DIAN tax payments, cannot serve as payroll or commercial clearing accounts in Colombia, and are not permitted to hold the S.A.S.'s regulatory capital or to be named as bank reference in foreign investment registration filings. Where a payment platform (e.g., global e-commerce settlement) transfers funds through an EMI, the S.A.S. must bring those funds into Colombia through an IMC within the surrender timeline and must annex the platform settlement statement to the Form 2 exchange declaration. The prudent treasury design is therefore: local IMC operational and escrow accounts as the primary rails, with the EMI used solely for marketplace settlement before channeling, and never as residual balance holder beyond the regulatory term.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) with no Colombian identity documentation or local tax ID.

Root cause: Non-resident Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) with no Colombian identity documentation or local tax ID.

Defense evidence: Notarized and apostilled passports of every natural-person UBO; notarized corporate constitutional documents and by-laws translated into Spanish; the Certificado de Existencia y Representación Legal issued by the Cámara de Comercio no more than 30 days prior to onboarding; a sworn UBO declaration identifying all direct and indirect shareholders with 5% or more participation and the full control chain; a signed corporate-organization chart; and, where a parent entity exists, audited financial statements of the parent for the last fiscal year.
Source of Funds on the initial foreign capital injection (USD/EUR wire) lacking transactional clarity.

Root cause: Source of Funds on the initial foreign capital injection (USD/EUR wire) lacking transactional clarity.

Defense evidence: Duly executed capital-subscription minutes and formal capital-call notice; remitting shareholder's bank statements covering not less than six months preceding the wire; source-of-wealth statement supported by investor tax returns or audited financials; declaration that the funds are unencumbered and originated from a regulated financial institution; and an investment commitment letter describing the intended use of proceeds for silicone fidget-toy tooling, food-grade raw materials and working capital.
Sanctions, OFAC or PEP screening false positive triggered by a substantially similar Latin surname or board member name.

Root cause: Sanctions, OFAC or PEP screening false positive triggered by a substantially similar Latin surname or board member name.

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the passport biometric page; national identity card displaying full name, date and place of birth; notarial certificate of name uniqueness; a signed clarification letter mapping the screening hit and confirming the entity chain of ownership, along with a legal-entity chart; where applicable, evidence of PEP status or the absence of any current PEP relationship; recommended compliance-lead-time buffer of six weeks before funding.
Platform-pay receivables (e-commerce marketplace, payment-aggregator or digital wallet) received from multiple foreign jurisdictions not matching export declarations.

Root cause: Platform-pay receivables (e-commerce marketplace, payment-aggregator or digital wallet) received from multiple foreign jurisdictions not matching export declarations.

Defense evidence: Signed marketplace or payment-aggregator agreement; platform settlement statements reconciled against export invoices; export declarations (DEX) and customs transport documents for the corresponding shipment lots; Form 2 exchange declarations filed with the IMC; and a documented arm's-length pricing policy per unit of fidget product to support the bank's transaction-narrative review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder's equity injection must be executed through the following compliant protocol: (i) formalize the capital increase or initial subscription in the S.A.S. corporate minutes and register the economic rights with the Cámara de Comercio; (ii) request the local IMC to register the capital contribution under the foreign direct investment category and obtain the Form 4 pre-registration or digital registration with Banco de la Republica; (iii) instruct the remitting bank abroad to send the USD/EUR wire via SWIFT MT103 with the payment narrative referencing the S.A.S. NIT and 'foreign direct investment - capital subscription'; (iv) upon receipt, the IMC will credit the foreign currency or convert it into COP and the S.A.S. must file the corresponding Exchange Declaration within the statutory window (normally the month following the operation) so that the investment yields full repatriation rights; (v) maintain in the permanent archive the MT103 confirmation, the IMC exchange declaration, the notarized corporate documents, the shareholder register and the Cámara de Comercio certificate. The purpose code used on the declaration must align with manufacturing of food-grade silicone articles (ISIC 3240), and the source-of-funds veracity will be attested by the IMC's compliance unit.For imports of food-grade silicone, pigments, molds and equipment, the primary clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for open-account supplier payments, with the payment narrative quoting the DIAN import declaration number, commercial invoice and LC reference where applicable, and with the mandatory Form 1 exchange declaration filed at the IMC for goods imports; (ii) irrevocable documentary commercial letters of credit (sight or short-term usance) issued through the IMC for high-value or strategic supplier relationships, subject to UCP 600, where the clearing executes against presentation of transport document, commercial invoice, insurance and certificates of analysis; and (iii) the debt-only SWIFT rail for service fees, design royalties and testing costs, filed under Form 3. All outbound disbursements must be corroborated by underlying trade documentation and reconciled against the registered foreign-investment corridor to avoid exchange-control penalties.USD/COP deliverable outright forwards (Forwards) with tenors up to 24 months, executed through an IMC to hedge export receivables, import payables and the working capital cycle of the S.A.S.USD/COP non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) available for clients with genuine underlying foreign-currency exposure, settled in COP as a net differential under an ISDA or local master agreement.COP/USD cross-currency swaps (CCS) for long-dated alignment between USD-denominated shareholder loans or equity injections and COP operating liquidity.FX swaps (swap de moneda extranjera) to bridge temporary COP/USD liquidity mismatches at the same value date or short tenors within the IMC treasury line.Foreign-currency call and put options permitted for hedging the same underlying economic commitments, subject to IMC risk approval and a board-approved derivatives policy of the S.A.S.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio del domicilio social (e.g., Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá), operating the Single Business and Social Registry (RUES), under the supervisory oversight of the Superintendencia de Sociedades.

Governing statute

Law 1258 of 2008 (Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada), Articles 4, 5, 12 and 22; supplementary Commercial Code (Decree 410 of 1971), Law 222 of 1995, Decree 1074 of 2015 (RUES regime) and Law 2069 of 2020 (expedited digital incorporation).

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (Private Incorporation Document), inscribed in Book V of the Mercantile Registry through RUES; a public deed is required only when real property or in-kind assets demanding a deed are contributed.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum subscribed or paid-in capital for the S.A.S. The incorporator may fix nominal capital (e.g., COP 1,000,000); capital must be fully subscribed but up to 100% of the subscribed value may be deferred for a maximum of two years. No escrow account or capital-parking requirement applies to cash contributions; in-kind contributions require a valuation report attached to the Incorporation Document.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate (Certificado de Seguridad Humana) issued by the Cuerpo de Bomberos for commercial premises with regulated occupancy load, covering extinguishers, emergency signage, evacuation routes, electrical panels and warehouse racking stability; structural load certificate for high-density shelving; mandatory SG-SST occupational safety and health program with ARL registration for all employees; employer enrollment in the Caja de Compensación Familiar once payroll is activated.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: www.dian.gov.co; core transactional platforms: MUISCA (RUT, NIT, IVA, income tax, electronic invoicing), SIEX (export declarations), VUCE (Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior), and RADIAN (electronic credit/debit documents).

E-government filing portal

Non-resident promoters incorporate the S.A.S. under Colombian law, appoint a legal representative domiciled in Colombia, obtain a certified electronic signature, and submit the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) application through MUISCA; DIAN then assigns the single statutory tax identification number (NIT) integrating income tax, IVA, customs, and export registration obligations. The RUT application must declare the ISIC 4652 / NAICS 423690 commercial activity, IVA-responsible status, exporter condition, and authorized electronic-invoice resolution. A foreign entity without a permanent establishment in Colombia may alternatively obtain a simplified RUT/NIT to remit IVA on qualifying services or digital transactions without acquiring full Colombian tax residency.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: an S.A.S. incorporated under Colombian law is a tax-resident corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax on worldwide income (headline CIT rate of 35% for tax year 2024 and subsequent years), with no pass-through, fiscal-transparency, or check-the-box eligibility; Territorial_Scope applies to non-resident sellers and branches, which are taxed solely on Colombian-source income; Tax_Exempt_Zone (Zona Franca) status is available as an elective preferential regime reducing CIT to 20% for qualifying export-oriented operations, but the passive electronic-components distribution activity remains under the general 35% regime. — Non-resident promoters incorporate the S.A.S. under Colombian law, appoint a legal representative domiciled in Colombia, obtain a certified electronic signature, and submit the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) application through MUISCA; DIAN then assigns the single statutory tax identification number (NIT) integrating income tax, IVA, customs, and export registration obligations. The RUT application must declare the ISIC 4652 / NAICS 423690 commercial activity, IVA-responsible status, exporter condition, and authorized electronic-invoice resolution. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: an S.A.S. incorporated under Colombian law is a tax-resident corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax on worldwide income (headline CIT rate of 35% for tax year 2024 and subsequent years), with no pass-through, fiscal-transparency, or check-the-box eligibility; Territorial_Scope applies to non-resident sellers and branches, which are taxed solely on Colombian-source income; Tax_Exempt_Zone (Zona Franca) status is available as an elective preferential regime reducing CIT to 20% for qualifying export-oriented operations, but the passive electronic-components distribution activity remains under the general 35% regime.

Available election forms

Colombia has no check-the-box regime. Relevant elections are: (i) the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLES), an optional combined small-taxpayer regime available where gross revenue does not exceed 3,500 UVT; (ii) Zona Franca operator or user qualification, granting the 20% CIT election for approved export projects; (iii) the export-treatment election, effected factually by filing the DEX and issuing export-modality electronic invoices; (iv) optional self-withholding (autorretención) arrangements for exporters; and (v) hybrid-mismatch documentation declarations under Ley 2010 de 2019, aligning cross-border payments with OECD BEPS Action 2 consistency rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Registration as an IVA-responsible taxpayer is mandatory when prior-year gross ordinary income exceeds COP 164,727,500 (3,500 UVT; UVT-2024 = COP 47,065, annually revalued by DIAN); or when year-end inventory exceeds COP 80,010,500 (1,700 UVT); or when current-year taxable or exempt sales are reasonably expected to exceed 3,500 UVT. Exporters become IVA-responsible from the date of acceptance of the first DEX even if the turnover threshold is not met; foreign suppliers of digital or cross-border services to Colombian customers must register to collect IVA without any de-minimis threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are formally 'excluidos' (excluded) from IVA — economically equivalent to a 0% rate with an entitlement to input-IVA credits and refunds. Eligibility conditions: (i) the exporter holds an active RUT with export activity and FEV habilitación; (ii) the goods physically leave Colombian customs territory, evidenced by an accepted Declaración de Exportación (DEX); (iii) the sale is concluded with a non-resident buyer and the proceeds are channeled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market; (iv) the FEV is issued in the export modality referencing the DEX number; and (v) ancillary services performed on the exported goods (e.g., warehousing, repacking, test-data delivery) are used wholly outside Colombia. For the passive electronic-inventory distribution scope, the MLCC and resistor lines must be reconciled at SKU level across purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, and DEX under HS 8532/8533 tariff headings.

  • Accepted Declaración de Exportación (DEX) filed through SIEX/MUISCA, covering HS 8532/8533 lines (multilayer ceramic capacitors and resistors) at SKU fidelity
  • Original transport document — Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical exit from Colombian customs territory
  • Export-modality commercial invoice (FEV) containing the CUNE and QR, Incoterms, unit values, and purchase-order reference
  • Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas — inward foreign-currency remittance certificate issued by the local bank confirming channeling through the Colombian foreign-exchange market
  • Packing list, and where applicable a certificate of origin for preferential trade programs
  • Supporting service statements confirming that ancillary services (storage, repacking, engineering data) are supplied exclusively to the non-resident buyer

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing (factura electrónica de venta — FEV): each invoice must be generated as validated XML incorporating the CUNE (Código Único de Numeración de Factura), a SHA-256 digital stamp, and a dynamic QR code, and transmitted to DIAN for immediate validation and clearance via MUISCA web-service APIs (REST/SOAP) or through authorized technology providers (proveedores tecnológicos); credit and debit notes plus export documents are processed through the RADIAN platform; exporters must complete the DIAN technical habilitación (pre-production certification) before live issuance; invoices that are not digitally cleared invalidate IVA input credits, tax costs, and deductions.

DIAN systematically cross-validates DEX records, electronic invoices, cargo manifests, and bank foreign-exchange records under dedicated exporter audit programs. Consequences for false or fictitious export zero-rating claims include: (i) an inexactitude penalty under Art. 647 E.T. equal to 100% of the overstated IVA refund or tax balance; (ii) retroactive refund clawback with moratory interest increased by 50%; (iii) customs penalties under Decree 1165/2019 and Law 1762/2015 of up to 200% of the customs value, plus seizure of the goods; (iv) criminal tax fraud under Art. 434A Código Penal, carrying 48-108 months of imprisonment where the evaded amount exceeds statutory thresholds, and money-laundering charges where export proceeds are structured; and (v) exclusion from the IVA refund registry, 'watchlist' targeting by DIAN, joint personal liability of legal representatives, and a five-to-six-year statute-of-limitations window permitting retrospective re-audit of historical refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) operates under a managed floating regime, with the TRM (Tasa Representativa del Mercado) as the official reference rate. Both current-account operations (goods, services, transfers) and capital-account operations (foreign investment, loans, derivatives) must be channeled through the 'mercado cambiario'. Profit remittances are freely permitted on current account after the foreign equity investment is registered with BRC on Form 9 and the applicable dividend withholding tax is paid. There is no quota system, but the local intermediary bank must verify the company's tax compliance standing and issue a remittance certification before releasing funds abroad.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under BRC Regulation DCIN-83 (as amended), exporters of goods must channel the full invoice value through an authorized local bank within six (6) months from the bill-of-lading or airway-bill date; an extension of up to six (6) additional months is available for justified commercial reasons. The funds must be credited to a local COP current account or to a registered foreign-currency deposit account; there is no mandatory percentage conversion to COP. Non-channeling is sanctioned with fines of up to 200% of the undeclared amount, plus default interest, under Law 9 of 1991.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Junta Directiva) is the supreme monetary, credit, and foreign-exchange authority under Law 31 of 1992; the DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) acts as co-enforcer of trade-related foreign-exchange declarations and customs controls. Authorized local banks (intermediarios del mercado cambiario, IMC) execute all mandatory 'canalización' operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability as a substitute for a local corporate bank account. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Mercury, Payoneer, etc.) are not supervised by the SFC or Banco de la República and cannot issue the required 'Declaración de Cambio' forms (Form 6/Form 9) or receive export proceeds for purposes of 'canalización'. Such EMIs may be used only as auxiliary treasury tools for marginal operating expenses, but the SAS must maintain a primary COP account with a Colombian IMC to remain compliant with the FX regime and corporate banking KYC expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder dispatches a SWIFT MT103 (USD/EUR/COP) to the SAS's local USD collecting account at the authorized IMC. On credit, the bank automatically generates the 'Declaración de Cambio por Inversión de Capital del Exterior' (Form 6) with economic-code 4010 corresponding to capital contributions for incorporation of a Colombian company. The equity injection must be registered with Banco de la República on Form 9 (Registro de Inversiones Internacionales) within twelve (12) months of the transfer date; the bank provides a stamped investment-registration certificate, which is the legal basis for future dividend remittance and capital repatriation. Supporting documentation: capital-resolution minutes, updated share register, RUT certificate, and the current certificado de existencia of the SAS.For open-account supplier payments, the SAS instructs its local bank to execute an SWIFT MT103 transfer, attaching the 'orden de pago al exterior' and referencing the corresponding DIAN import declaration; the FX transaction is declared with the underlyng import code through the bank's electronic channel. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory withholding tax on dividends distributed to Colombian-resident shareholders is 0% for the first tier (since 2017), while payments made to non-resident shareholders or to resident individuals are subject to a 10% withholding tax on taxable dividends (additional to the 35% corporate tax paid at entity level). Non-resident shareholders receiving dividends from a Colombian S.A.S. are subject to a 10% statutory dividend withholding tax, which is the final tax on Colombian-source dividend income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory if the taxpayer's gross income exceeds 135,000 UVT (approx. COP 6,000 million / USD 1.4 million) or net equity exceeds 45,000 UVT (approx. COP 2,000 million) as of year-end, or if the taxpayer conducts transactions with related parties. Master File: required for taxpayers with gross income above 450,000 UVT (approx. COP 20,000 million / USD 4.8 million). The documentation threshold for the TP Annex (Form 120) and TP report (Form 120 Informe) is 135,000 UVT for local file, 450,000 UVT for master file, and 15,000 UVT (approx. USD 0.3 million) for the annual TP information return (Form 120). Transfer pricing applies only to operations between Colombian related parties or with non-residents in low-tax jurisdictions or special tax regimes.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE or other offshore financial centers. However, the Colombian General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) in Article 869 of the Tax Code (Estatuto Tributario) allows the DIAN to disregard legal forms and recharacterize transactions that are artificial, lack commercial purpose, or are structured to obtain tax advantages. The OECD BEPS Action 5 (Preventing Tax Treaty Abuse) and the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) in Colombian treaties also deny treaty benefits for transactions lacking commercial substance. For a passive electronic inventory distribution company, the DIAN will scrutinize whether: (i) the company has physical premises, local management, and decision-making capability; (ii) it performs distribution functions (warehousing, logistics, title transfer) and assumes inventory risk; (iii) the profit margin is commensurate with the distribution functions; and (iv) there is no circular financing or artificial deferral of revenue.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misdeclaration of Income

Statutory sanction: Income tax evasion penalty: 100% of the tax evaded as a fine, plus moratorium interest (1.5% monthly) and criminal prosecution for tax evasion (Article 97 of Law 1819/2016) carrying imprisonment of 3 to 5 years for evasions exceeding 250 minimum wages (approx. COP 320 million). DIAN may also impose the penalty for not registering as a merchant (non-inscripción) or failure to deliver electronic invoices: 1% of gross revenue or 0.5% of the invoiced amount.

Director personal liability: Under Article 794 of the Tax Code, shareholders and directors may be jointly and severally liable for the tax obligations of a dissolved or liquidated company if they hold assets, or distribute dividends without prior payment of taxes. Court precedents (Constitutional Court jurisprudence) allow piercing the corporate veil when the S.A.S. is used fraudulently or to evade tax. Criminal liability can extend to the legal representative who signs false tax returns, resulting in imprisonment and a travel ban.
Transfer Pricing Misapplication

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income by DIAN using secondary adjustment (may be recharacterized as a loan or constructive dividend). Penalty for failure to file TP documentation: up to 5% of the value of the transactions subject to TP rules, or 1% of gross income/equity if no transaction is identified; minimum 10 UVT, maximum 10,000 UVT for the annual return. Late filing of Form 120: 5% of the value of cross-border transactions, up to 10,000 UVT. Additionally, the denied deductible expense for excess cost is re-added to taxable income and subject to the standard 35% rate, plus moratorium interest.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives who sign TP documentation that is materially false or contain reckless omissions face joint liability for the resulting tax difference, interest, and fines. If the DIAN proves an intentional scheme to shift profits to a non-resident related party, the conduct may be criminalized as tax evasion (up to 5 years imprisonment) for the director who approved the TP policy.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the legal entity and attribute the entire income directly to the foreign parent under substance-over-form rules and Article 869 (GAAR), issuing a recharacterization of the operation. Penalty: 100% of the tax evaded as assessed, plus 1.5% monthly interest, and potential criminal charges. In addition, the Colombian S.A.S. could be dissolved by the Superintendencia de Sociedades for lack of real corporate purpose. Colombian anti-money laundering (AML) authorities may report the shell structure to the UAFE (Unidad de Información y Análisis Financiero) and customs authorities for import/export irregularities.

Director personal liability: Directors who establish a shell S.A.S. with no commercial substance may be jointly and severally liable for the entire tax debt under the doctrine of abuse of the legal personality. Travel bans (salida del país) can be imposed by the DIAN or by a criminal court as a preventive measure. If the shell structure is used to move funds or to evade foreign exchange regulations, directors risk imprisonment for money laundering (up to 20 years) and for smuggling (contrabando) of the electronic inventory, as well as confiscation of assets derived from the operation.
AML / Foreign Exchange Violations

Statutory sanction: Foreign exchange violations: under Law 9/1991 and Decree 1068/2015, fines for each violation range from 50% to 200% of the sums involved. Under the AML regime (Law 1121/2006 and UIAF regulations), failure to report suspicious transactions is subject to administrative fines up to 100,000 UVT (approx. USD 500,000), and criminal money-laundering charges (Articles 323 and 324 of the Penal Code) carry imprisonment of 6 to 20 years. Failure to apply withholding tax on dividend payments: penalty of 1% of the payment per month up to 10% plus surcharge.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives and board members of a Colombian company engaged in money laundering face individual criminal liability, travel prohibitions, asset freezes, and extradition to the requesting state. Also, under the SOI (Superintendencia de Sociedades), directors who knowingly authorize the receipt of funds from unregulated digital-asset channels without compliance are jointly liable for the total funds received, including restitution to treasury.

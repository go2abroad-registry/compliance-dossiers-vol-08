Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China (国家税务总局) – unified electronic tax bureau portal: www.chinatax.gov.cn (ETax platform, 电子税务局)

E-government filing portal

The WFOE obtains its Unified Social Credit Code (USCC, 18-digit) at incorporation from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), and for tax purposes this USCC is concurrently recognised as the taxpayer identification number (纳税人识别号). Under the 'multi-license integration' (多证合一) regime, tax registration is automatic upon issuance of the business license; within 30 days thereafter the entity must log into the provincial Dongshui electronic tax bureau to complete tax supplementary registration (涉税信息补充), including bank account details, financial software information, authorised tax agent, and industry classification. The tax authority then activates the VAT taxpayer status and issues the digital tax registration certificate electronically. No separate TIN application is required for a WFOE, but a foreign parent must hold a non-resident tax identification through the CRS/EIAs process for treaty relief applications.

The tax authority then activates the VAT taxpayer status and issues the digital tax registration certificate electronically. No separate TIN application is required for a WFOE, but a foreign parent must hold a non-resident tax identification through the CRS/EIAs process for treaty relief applications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box mechanism or pass-through election exists in PRC law. The WFOE is mandatorily taxed as a resident legal person under the Enterprise Income Tax Law (CIT rate 25%) on its worldwide income. For VAT purposes, an entity whose annual taxable turnover is not more than CNY 5,000,000 may elect to be taxed as a small-scale taxpayer (lower rate of 1% currently available under temporary policy; normally 3%); any entity with annual taxable turnover exceeding CNY 5,000,000 must be classified as a general VAT taxpayer and file under the general method. No hybrid mismatch election is available, but CFC rules, thin capitalisation, and general anti-abuse rules apply to override mismatched structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must complete basic tax registration within 30 days after business license issuance, regardless of revenue. For VAT general taxpayer status, registration becomes compulsory once annual taxable turnover exceeds CNY 5,000,000. Below this threshold, the entity may remain a small-scale taxpayer (3% or 1% reduced rate) or voluntarily register as a general taxpayer if it is able to comply with accounting and invoicing requirements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from mainland China (including exports to Taiwan region as a separate customs territory) are zero-rated for VAT, with refund of creditable input VAT. Cross-border supply of discretionary portfolio advisory and multi-family office management services to offshore clients may qualify for zero-rating only if the service is completely consumed outside mainland China and the transaction meets all conditions of STA Announcement 2016 No. 32, as amended: (1) the service recipient is a non-resident enterprise or individual; (2) the service does not relate to Chinese real estate or tangible movable property located in China; (3) the service does not involve a Chinese-resident beneficiary deriving economic gain inside China; and (4) the consideration is received from abroad (foreign exchange or offshore RMB). The WFOE must retain evidence and issue a zero-rated VAT invoice prior to filing the refund application.

  • Customs Export Declaration (海关出口货物报关单) for physical goods exports
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Carrier's Cargo Manifest
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate (出口收汇核销单/结汇水单 or 涉外收入申报单)
  • Zero-Rated VAT Invoice (增值税普通发票 at 0%)
  • Cross-Border Service Agreement / Statement of Work / Service Completion Certificate
  • Tax Residence Certificate or Non-Resident Registration Proof of the Overseas Client
  • Input VAT Invoice Bundle and Export Cost Ledger for Refund Calculation

China has implemented a mandatory fully-digital electronic invoice (全电发票/数电票) system through the central electronic invoice platform (电子发票服务平台). Invoices are issued, delivered, and automatically reconciled in real time with the STA's tax digital account (税务数字账户); no physical stamping or manual clearance is used. Large issuers must integrate their ERP with the tax platform using API-based data exchange to push invoice data in real time. Legacy taxpayers continue using the Golden Tax System (金税四期) with 'UKI' taxpayer electronic key devices. The tax authorities perform automatic cross-validation of invoice data against customs declarations, FX receipts, and taxpayer profiles.

Fraudulent or unsupported claims for export zero-rating or VAT refunds are subject to retrospective clawback of the entire refund amount plus interest under the Tax Collection and Administration Law, with a 3-year limitation period that extends to 10 years where fraud is proved. The statutory penalty ranges from 50% to 500% of the refund amount falsely obtained (Article 63). Criminal exposure arises under Articles 204 and 205 of the Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refunds or false issuance of special VAT invoices, punishable by fines, asset forfeiture, imprisonment up to life, and permanent disqualification from export VAT refund treatment. The STA's 'Golden Tax Phase IV' AI risk engine automatically flags zero-rated invoices without matching customs or FX data, triggering compulsory on-site audit, cross-border information exchange requests, and anti-money-laundering referral to the PBOC.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is fully convertible for current-account trade and cross-border service flows after bank-level transaction verification; capital-account convertibility remains controlled, requiring SAFE FDI registration, permitted investment purpose, and post-entry monitoring. Profit remittance to the Taiwan parent is permitted only after statutory audit, corporate income tax clearance, and SAFE/bank verification of dividend withholding tax. Taiwan TWD is current-account convertible, but mainland-bound capital remittances require FSC/MOEA investment approval, CBC remittance declaration, and bank confirmation; unlicensed capital transfers between the PRC WFOE and Taiwan are not freely convertible.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Neither the PRC nor Taiwan imposes a fixed mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Under SAFE, PRC export and service proceeds must be repatriated to an onshore foreign-currency or RMB account within 180 days after export declaration or service completion, supported by customs declarations, contracts, invoices, and settlement records; foreign-currency balances may be retained or converted to RMB at the reporting bank's spot rate. Taiwan requires export and cross-border service earnings to be collected within the contractual settlement term and declared to a CBC-approved bank; no forced surrender percentage applies. For the MFO's cross-border advisory fees received from Taiwan clients, current-account service receipts must be matched with the advisory agreement, fee schedule, and settlement evidence for SAFE and tax audit integrity.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with Taiwan Central Bank (CBC) and Taiwan Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) exercising cross-strait remittance and banking supervision authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary treasury rails. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Payoneer, Currenxie) are not licensed as deposit-taking banks in the PRC or Taiwan and cannot hold SAFE capital-account balances, file FDI registrations, or serve as settlement banks for export/service proceeds. Rating: MEDIUM only as an ancillary collection gateway for overseas client fees, provided funds are swept into a licensed onshore account within 24 hours and the underlying advisory inflows are fully documented for SAFE, tax, and AML audit.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% of taxable income for a Taiwan profit-seeking enterprise. A separate 12% surtax applies to undistributed earnings retained by a company, subject to statutory exclusions and adjustments.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on Taiwan-source dividends paid by a Taiwan company to a nonresident company or individual, subject to an applicable tax treaty or other legally effective relief. Dividends paid to a Taiwan resident company are generally subject to Taiwan domestic inter-corporate dividend rules rather than the nonresident withholding rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due from 1 May through 31 May following the end of the calendar fiscal year. A company using a non-calendar fiscal year generally files within five months after fiscal year-end. The provisional return for the first half of a calendar year is generally filed during 1 September through 30 September. Payment is due by the applicable filing deadline. Extensions require a specific statutory or administrative basis.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan local-file documentation generally applies where annual net revenue is at least NT$300 million or where related-party transaction thresholds are met, commonly including annual related-party purchases or sales of at least NT$200 million or other related-party transactions of at least NT$100 million, subject to the detailed transaction categories, exemptions, and current regulations. A master file is generally required for a Taiwan entity belonging to a multinational group whose consolidated annual revenue reaches at least NT$7 billion, subject to statutory exclusions and filing rules. A country-by-country report generally applies to a Taiwan constituent entity of a multinational group meeting the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold, currently generally EUR750 million or its prescribed equivalent, and related reporting conditions.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a single UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime applicable to every holding or service company. Substance is nevertheless enforced through transfer-pricing rules, controlled foreign company rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, anti-avoidance provisions, permanent-establishment principles, foreign tax credit rules, withholding rules, and the general substance-over-form doctrine. A multi-family office must demonstrate real Taiwan functions, qualified personnel, premises, risk control, investment-advisory authority, client and mandate records, decision-making evidence, compliant custody arrangements, and arm's-length remuneration. Portfolio custody, payment execution, and regulated investment management cannot be outsourced in a manner inconsistent with FSC licensing requirements or used to disguise a passive shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unauthorized financial-sector activity

Statutory sanction: Regulatory orders to cease business, corrective action, license denial or revocation, administrative fines under applicable securities and financial laws, disgorgement or restitution, and potential criminal liability for unauthorized securities or investment activity.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face administrative sanctions, disqualification, civil compensation, and criminal prosecution. Directors are not automatically liable for all corporate debts, but personal liability can arise from their own unlawful acts, knowing participation, statutory breaches, or criminal conduct. Travel restrictions may be imposed only under applicable judicial or enforcement procedures; imprisonment depends on the specific offense and conviction.
Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of omitted tax, statutory surcharges, interest, administrative penalties, and criminal prosecution. Under Taiwan tax law, serious intentional evasion may result in imprisonment and criminal fines, with the exact range depending on the violated statute, amount, method, repetition, and judicial findings.

Director personal liability: The responsible person, accounting personnel, and participating advisers may be prosecuted personally. Civil restitution, tax recovery from responsible persons where legally permitted, asset preservation, and restrictions on departure may follow judicial or enforcement orders. Corporate limited liability does not protect a director from liability for personal fraud or intentional tax offenses.
False invoices and non-genuine expenses

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of deductions and input tax credits, additional tax, fines, possible invoice-related penalties, and criminal liability for fraudulent tax or accounting conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors, responsible persons, finance officers, and complicit vendors may incur joint civil exposure for loss, criminal prosecution, confiscation of proceeds, and restrictions imposed during investigation or enforcement.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustment to arm's-length income, denial of deductions, additional income tax, interest, penalties for deficient documentation, withholding consequences, and possible audit expansion to other periods or jurisdictions.

Director personal liability: Directors generally face corporate-level adjustment rather than automatic personal liability, but intentional falsification, obstruction, or participation in evasion can create personal civil and criminal exposure.
Permanent-establishment and source mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of Taiwan-source income or permanent-establishment profits, corporate tax assessment, withholding tax assessment, interest, penalties, and potential treaty-abuse investigation.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may incur personal liability for deliberate false filings or obstruction. A foreign parent may also be exposed to Taiwan tax and procedural obligations without automatically receiving the benefit of Taiwan subsidiary limited liability.
AML, KYC, and suspicious-transaction failures

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, enhanced supervision, business restrictions, license suspension or revocation, suspicious-transaction investigation, asset freezing, and criminal liability for money laundering or participation in predicate offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and senior managers may be personally prosecuted where they knowingly assist, ignore, or facilitate laundering. Confiscation, restitution, asset restraint, and judicial travel restrictions may apply; imprisonment depends on the proven offense.
Client-money, custody, and segregation breach

Statutory sanction: Regulatory intervention, restitution, asset freezing, license sanctions, administrative fines, and possible criminal charges for breach of financial-sector, trust, securities, or fraud rules.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may be liable for restoration of client assets and damages and may face criminal prosecution for misappropriation, fraud, or unauthorized financial activity. Corporate limited liability does not shield deliberate diversion of client property.
Withholding-tax default

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unpaid withholding tax from the withholding agent, surcharges, interest, administrative fines, denial of treaty relief, and possible criminal exposure for deliberate concealment or misuse of withheld funds.

Director personal liability: The company or withholding agent is primarily liable, but the responsible person and finance officers may face personal liability or prosecution for knowing non-remittance, falsification, or deliberate evasion.
Corporate shell and nominee-control structure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, tax recharacterization, disallowance of deductions, beneficial-owner and anti-avoidance adjustments, AML referrals, regulatory sanctions, and potential criminal proceedings where fraud or laundering is established.

Director personal liability: Nominee and actual controlling persons may face civil claims, tax recovery, asset preservation, and criminal prosecution. Piercing the corporate veil is fact-specific and is not automatic merely because a company is foreign-owned.
Accounting, books, and record retention failure

Statutory sanction: Estimated assessment, denial of deductions or loss carryforwards, administrative fines, audit escalation, adverse evidentiary presumptions, and separate penalties for obstruction or failure to produce records.

Director personal liability: The responsible person and accounting personnel may incur personal administrative or criminal exposure for intentional destruction, falsification, or obstruction. Departure restrictions require a lawful statutory or judicial basis and are not automatic for ordinary filing errors.

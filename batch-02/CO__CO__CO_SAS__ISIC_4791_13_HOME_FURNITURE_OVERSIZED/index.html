No statutory minimum paid-up capital for S.A.S. under Law 1258 of 2008. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

DIAN - Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (National Tax and Customs Directorate); sole competent authority for national taxes and customs. Electronic filing and clearance are performed exclusively through the MUISCA portal (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) at https://www.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

The S.A.S. obtains its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) automatically at notarial or chamber-of-commerce incorporation. It must then complete the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) enrolment with DIAN, executed by a Colombian-resident legal representative via a DIAN office or through MUISCA with a digital certificate. Procedural sequence: (1) incorporate the S.A.S. and obtain the NIT; (2) enrol in the RUT indicating income-tax status, VAT responsible status (Responsable de IVA) and electronic invoicing operator status; (3) register in the DIAN customs users' registry (Registro de Usuarios Aduaneros) as exporter; (4) enable DIAN free software or a certified billing provider for real-time e-invoicing; (5) certify the legal representative's digital signature. No minimum capital is required and non-resident ownership is expressly allowed; foreign shareholders only need to supply corporate existence documents, not a local TIN.

There is no pass-through eligibility, no check-the-box regime, and hybrid-mismatch rules have not been enacted. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Colombian S.A.S. is a separate resident taxpayer subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income under Articles 12-22 of the Estatuto Tributario (E.T.). There is no pass-through eligibility, no check-the-box regime, and hybrid-mismatch rules have not been enacted. Territorial scope is worldwide, with the CFC regime applying to certain passive income; Tax_Exempt_Zone (Zona Franca) status exists only upon express application and is not a default attribute of an ordinary commercial S.A.S.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity election exists in Colombia. Statutory elections affecting the S.A.S. include: (1) group income-tax consolidation for parent-subsidiary structures (80% or more ownership) under Law 2010 of 2019; (2) participation exemption election (Art. 30-1 E.T.) for foreign-source dividends, subject to CFC rules under Law 1819 of 2016; (3) the Simplified Taxation Regime (Régimen Simple) for small traders whose gross income stays within 3,500 UVT, generally unavailable above that ceiling; and (4) customs duty-deferral plans (Plan Vallejo) for imported inputs embedded in future exports, which do not modify VAT classification. Elections must be filed with DIAN within the corresponding tax-return term; no retroactive election is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

3,500 UVT of gross income from taxable operations in the preceding tax year (COP 174,296,500 at the 2025 UVT of COP 49,799). The threshold is also triggered when the current-year projection exceeds 3,500 UVT; for an oversized-furniture online retailer this is effectively exceeded from the first month of recurring sales (approx. COP 14.5 million monthly).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The S.A.S. may apply 0% VAT to sales of furniture and home decor only when the goods physically leave Colombian customs territory under the definitive export regime (Art. 481 E.T.; Decree 1165 of 2019). Conditions: (1) the S.A.S. must be the exporter of record on the DEX; (2) the goods must be shipped from national customs territory within the deadline contained in the DEX; (3) the buyer must be a non-resident and the invoicing must evidence an export transaction; (4) the foreign-currency proceeds must be channelled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market within 180 calendar days. Qualifying international services (such as hosting, software support and call-centre services provided to foreign clients) can also be exempt at 0% under Decree 1418 of 2020 when the service is regarded as used abroad; however, the physical furniture sale is a goods-export matter and the two regimes are not interchangeable. Input VAT (19%) borne on the export supply chain is recoverable as a refundable VAT balance (saldo a favor) in the bimonthly IVA return.

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX) definitiva validated by DIAN through MUISCA, including tariff code, FOB value, net weight and export regime
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) / Carta Porte Internacional evidencing physical departure from Colombian customs territory
  • Levante / Visto Bueno - the electronic customs clearance confirmation issued by DIAN authorising the exit of the goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list exactly matching the DEX data and the FEV issued by the S.A.S.
  • Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas (foreign-exchange settlement certificate) or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds within the 180-day term
  • Carrier pick-up proof (e.g., DHL/FedEx shipping receipt) confirming the goods entered the international leg within 48 hours after the DEX
  • Sales/export contract and, where applicable, certificate of origin supporting the trade

Full digital invoicing mandate: the S.A.S. must issue Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV) through DIAN-authorised software and transmit the XML to the MUISCA clearance engine in real time; the invoice is legally delivered only after prior electronic validation (validación previa). Each validated invoice is stamped with a CUSE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) and a QR code. Since 2024-2025, DIAN requires API/web-service integration rather than batch processing; non-compliance triggers a penalty of 1% of the invoice value per infraction and may lead to temporary closure of the establishment.

DIAN undertakes automatic documentary cross-matching between the DEX, FEV, customs controls and the foreign-exchange settlement before any IVA refund (devolución de saldo a favor) is paid. If the export is not physically accomplished within the DEX deadline, or the DEX is declared late or is false, the 0% rate is retroactively stripped: the S.A.S. must restate its IVA returns and pay 19% VAT on the gross sale plus the statutory moratorium interest fixed annually by DIAN, and is liable to penalty under Article 647 E.T. of up to 200% of the tax evaded. False export documents constitute criminal customs fraud (Ley 1762 of 2015) punishable by imprisonment of 60 to 120 months; directors and the legal representative are jointly and severally liable under Article 794 E.T. Failure to repatriate proceeds within the 180-day deadline (or the extended term granted by Banco de la República) triggers exchange-control fines per Banco de la República regulations with statutory minimum penalties per infraction, and DIAN may recharacterise the unrepatriated amount as a deemed distribution taxable as dividend income.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a market-determined floating exchange rate with free convertibility on current-account operations (exports and imports of goods and services) subject to mandatory channelling and statistical declaration through an authorized foreign-exchange intermediary (Intermediario del Mercado Cambiario, 'IMC'). Capital-account flows, including inbound foreign direct investment (FDI) into the S.A.S., are freely permitted without prior central-bank authorization, but the investment must be declared on Formulario No. 6 (Declaración de Cambio – Inversiones Internacionales) at the moment of channelling and subsequently reported annually by the non-resident shareholder through the 'Reporte Anual de Inversiones Internacionales'. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without exchange-control approval provided the FDI is registered, the company's annual financial statements are filed, dividend withholding is settled via the DIAN, and the remittance is executed through an IMC on Formulario No. 1 (Declaración de Cambio – Servicios, Transferencias y Otros Conceptos). The principal practical hurdle is not convertibility limitation but documentary discipline: full traceability of the equity injection, corporate authorization minutes, the RUT/NIT tax registry, and retention of all exchange declarations for a minimum of five years.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83 Chapter 7, 100% of the FOB invoice value of goods exported by the Colombian S.A.S. (oversized furniture and home décor sold online) must be collected from the foreign buyer and channelled into Colombia through an IMC within the contractual payment term, which may not exceed six (6) months from the date of shipment (embarque). Each collection must be declared by the exporter on Formulario No. 3 (Declaración de Cambio – Exportaciones de Bienes), and the currency may be sold to the IMC at the market TRM or deposited in a foreign-currency account maintained at the IMC pending authorized use. A justified extension of up to three (3) additional months may be requested from Banco de la República; failure to channel or declare within the statutory deadline subjects the company to enforced negotiation proceedings and monetary penalties under the exchange regime.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (the Central Bank of Colombia), exercising the compulsory Colombian foreign-exchange regime under Law 9 of 1991 as codified in Decree 1068 of 2015 and the operative compendium Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, with supervision of banks and AML/CFT conduct by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and tax/customs enforcement by the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for use as the primary treasury or operating account of the Colombian resident S.A.S.; Medium as an ancillary collection-and-disbursement rail that feeds the local IMC. Legal constraints: (i) Colombian residents must channel export proceeds, import payments, and FDI exclusively through authorized IMCs, so funds held by a foreign-incorporated EMI (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) cannot by themselves satisfy the exchange-regime obligations; (ii) a foreign EMI without a Colombian SEDPE (Sociedad Especializada en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos) license cannot offer COP-denominated local accounts or issue IMC-compliant exchange declarations; and (iii) DIAN e-invoicing and VAT/income-cost traceability require the reconciliation of Stripe/Affirm settlement flows with the local bank statement. The recommended architecture is therefore a binary rail: Stripe/Affirm payouts in USD to the EMIs' wallet as a short-term float, with an automated sweep to the local US-dollar account at the IMC within the DCIN-83 collection deadline.

The recommended architecture is therefore a binary rail: Stripe/Affirm payouts in USD to the EMIs' wallet as a short-term float, with an automated sweep to the local US-dollar account at the IMC within the DCIN-83 collection deadline.

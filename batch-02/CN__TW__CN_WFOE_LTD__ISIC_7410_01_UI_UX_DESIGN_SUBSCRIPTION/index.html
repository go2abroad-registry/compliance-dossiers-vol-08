Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taxation Administration, and the five regional National Taxation Bureaus; online e-Tax Portal (etax.nat.gov.tw) and E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform (einvoice.nat.gov.tw)

E-government filing portal

For the Taiwan-incorporated WFOE, the Company Registry's One-Stop Online Company Registration automatically assigns a Unified Business Number (UBIN), which functions as the enterprise TIN for income tax and business tax; the National Taxation Bureau completes tax registration based on the company registration record, and no separate TIN application is needed. For a non-resident foreign enterprise that lacks a fixed place of business and sells cross-border electronic services to Taiwan consumers, the foreign entity must apply for a special Taiwan Tax ID Number by using the Ministry of Finance Cross-Border Electronic Services Tax Registration portal or by filing the statutory Application for Tax Registration with the competent National Taxation Bureau, submitting: certificate of incorporation or equivalent legal existence document, board resolution and power of attorney designating a local tax agent, a description of the Taiwan activities and digital service delivery model, and projected annual Taiwan sales. The tax authority then registers the foreign enterprise and issues a Taiwan Tax ID Number for VAT filing and payment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not provide check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity classification elections. A foreign-owned limited-liability company is mandatorily taxed as a separate corporate taxpayer; it cannot elect pass-through treatment. A fiscal-year election may be approved by the tax authority for legitimate business reasons, but that election does not alter tax entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory business tax registration before commencement for all Taiwan business entities; no general turnover threshold for a newly incorporated WFOE. Small-scale businesses with monthly taxable sales below NT$80,000 may be exempt from registration and VAT filing. A non-resident foreign electronic-service provider must register for Taiwan VAT when its annual Taiwan electronic-service sales exceed NT$480,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, zero-rating applies to goods exported from Taiwan and to services provided to foreign clients that are used or consumed outside Taiwan. For the UI/UX design subscription service, zero-rating is available when the customer is a non-Taiwan or non-resident party, the design deliverables are delivered and used outside Taiwan, no Taiwan domestic consumption occurs, and the consideration is paid in foreign currency and repatriated to Taiwan. The service must be a genuine cross-border supply, not a Taiwan domestic use of the design product.

  • Customs Export Declaration for any physical/hard-copy deliverables; not applicable to pure digital design subscriptions
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical deliverables; not required for pure services
  • Executed design-subscription contract or master service agreement identifying the foreign customer, service scope, and delivery terms
  • Commercial invoice or service-revenue statement in the foreign customer's name
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank foreign-exchange settlement record (SWIFT MT103 equivalent) evidencing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds
  • Foreign-use substantiation such as client acceptance sign-off, user location logs, or offshore hosting/delivery records demonstrating that the design service is used outside Taiwan

The WFOE must issue statutory Uniform Invoices for every domestic taxable sale. If electronic invoices are used, the entity must use the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice Turnkey and transmit the invoice data to the E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform in the prescribed format; Taiwan does not operate a mainland-China-style real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamp for all invoices, but e-invoice numbers are allocated centrally and data is used for anti-fraud reconciliation. Foreign electronic-service providers registered for Taiwan VAT must issue electronic Uniform Invoices to consumers or provide an equivalent record to the tax authority for each transaction.

If the export zero-rating documentation is incomplete, the zero-rating is disallowed and the WFOE is assessed 5% output VAT on the full consideration, plus a late-payment surcharge and interest. Foreign-exchange proceeds that are not repatriated to Taiwan within 180 days of an invoice/payment are treated as evidence that the service was not genuinely exported and may trigger a retrospective VAT assessment. The ordinary tax assessment period is five years, extended to seven years for fraudulent evasion. False invoices, forged customs declarations, or artificial arrangements used to claim export zero-rating attract penalties under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act of 1 to 5 times the tax evaded, potential cancellation of registration, and criminal referral for tax fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict current account convertibility and capital account restrictions. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in UI/UX design subscription services, the current account (including service trade payments, royalty fees, and design subscription fees) is generally freely convertible provided that the transaction is genuine, documented, and complies with the SAFE registration requirements. However, capital account items such as inbound equity injections, foreign debt (external loans), and cross-border guarantees remain subject to SAFE registration, approval thresholds, and the Foreign Debt Macro Prudential Regulation. Export proceeds from design services are not subject to a mandatory surrender requirement under the current SAFE framework, but any receipt of foreign exchange must be settled into RMB through a designated bank with supporting contracts, invoices, and service trade declarations. Profit remittance by the WFOE to its offshore shareholder requires an annual audit report, tax clearance certificate, board resolution declaring dividends from accumulated after-tax profits, and SAFE verification via the bank's digital capital project port. The SAFE Foreign Exchange Account System and the banks' compliance screening will also scrutinize any transaction that exceeds the designated amount threshold, and the beneficiary and remitter names must align exactly with the registered entity documents. Practical hurdles include pricing reasonableness reviews for intragroup service fees, transfer pricing documentation requirements under CIT Article 41, and potential delays if the WFOE has not completed the SAFE foreign investment payment record or the annual investment report via the Ministry of Commerce system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current Chinese foreign exchange regulations, there is no mandatory export proceeds surrender requirement since the foreign exchange settlement system was liberalized. For service trade export receipts, the WFOE must settle foreign currency into RMB within the normal banking timeline via a designated bank after submitting the service trade foreign payment/collection application, contract, invoice, proof of service delivery, and any applicable tax vouchers. The conversion ratio is 100% at the market-driven onshore rate, with no forced retention percentage.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability. Non-bank fintech EMIs and offshore digital banks are not legally permitted to act as primary banking service providers for Chinese onshore incorporated WFOEs. The WFOE must maintain its primary corporate banking relationship with a SAFE-designated domestic commercial bank. Offshore EMIs such as Stripe Treasury or Wise Platform cannot replace the domestic bank account for capital injection, SAFE registration, RMB settlement, or tax payments. They may be use merely as downstream collection tools for overseas subscriptions if the funds are ultimately repatriated through compliant cross-border payment service providers, but reliance on them as a settlement rail is legally constrained by PBOC payment regulations and the prohibition on circumventing SAFE monitoring.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan shareholder must remit the paid-in capital via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's FDI capital settlement account at a designated domestic bank. The remittance purpose must be labeled as 'Investment Capital' and reference the FDI (Foreign Direct Investment) certificate. The funds must not enter a regular operational account; instead they are held in the FDI capital account. Upon settlement, the bank verifies the capital injection against the SAFE registration record and the certificate of payment. After the bank completes the foreign exchange settlement, the RMB funds can only be placed into a legally restricted RMB capital account. Then, to use the funds for business operations, the company submits a capital verification report issued by a registered CPA, the audit report, and real expense invoices such as salaries, rent, and subscriptions. The SAFE digital registry must be matched, and the bank will check that the settlement is for the actual operation of the WFOE and not for securities investment or real estate, which are prohibited. A tax filing for stamp duty on capital increase may be triggered and paid prior to disbursement.For outbound trade payments, the preferred rails are (1) direct SWIFT MT103 wires for service fee remittances such as software vendor payments and licensing fees, using the service trade code and bank SAFE validation; (2) commercial letters of credit are not relevant for the UI/UX design service subscription model because services are not goods; (3) documentary collections are used only when the counterparty requires a bank-mediated document flow, but for a SaaS design subscription this is rare. The WFOE will mainly use the bank's 'Single Window for Trade' portal to upload the service trade contract, the invoice, a tax certificate for cross-border service payment, and the actual service completion certificate. In case of any outbound subscription royalty to the Taiwan parent to cover its own costs, a cross-border service transaction must be supported by a genuinely executed intra-group services agreement and transfer pricing documentation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory where annual related-party transaction amounts exceed RMB 40 million for provision of services (including UI/UX design subscription services) or RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers; Master File must be prepared where total related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion in a single year, or where a foreign group parent's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: Although mainland China does not yet apply an Economic Substance Resolution in the style of the UAE, the PRC Tax Authorities will disallow treaty/statutory benefits, and will recharacterize transactions under the EIT Law Article 47 General Anti-Avoidance Rule, where an offshore Taiwan or Hong Kong conduit lacks the employees, assets, and decision-making capacity to perform the service. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) test requires that the Taiwanese recipient (or the mainland WFOE, as applicable) actually performs the design, management, and risk-taking functions in the jurisdiction in which the income is reported; mere invoicing or no-substance shareholding in Taiwan triggers the beneficial owner rejection doctrine (Announcement 2015 No. 60) and shields the mainland tax base from profit shifting.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Revenue Diversion

Statutory sanction: TCAL Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the under-payed tax and the additional daily 0.05% late surcharge; the State Taxation Administration may bank levy the WFOE's Stripe and Wise settlements and re-assess the prior six years of revenue; where unpaid tax exceeds RMB 50,000, the case is referred under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, punishable in the most severe tier by imprisonment of three to seven years and a fine.

Director personal liability: A court piercing the corporate veil allows the tax authority to seek personal restitution against the director for the unpaid CIT and penalties, and to place the director on the tax blacklist (exit blockage), freezing the individual's passport for a maximum of six years. A criminal conviction can lead to personal property seizure, a permanent travel ban, and a custodial sentence of up to seven years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under TCAL Article 62, the tax bureau may impose a RMB 2,000 to 10,000 fine for the un-filed related-party disclosures, and may apply Article 44, making a punitive upward adjustment of the captive service fee to reflect arm's-length price; the deficiency is additionally subject to the 50%-500% penalty after a tax evasion recharacterization and a 0.05% daily interest surcharge.

Director personal liability: Once the transfer pricing adjustment is reclassified as evasion, the directors become personally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties; the individual is forbidden to leave the mainland without the approval of the tax bureau, and if the adjustment exceeds the RMB 100,000 criminal threshold for fraudulent claim over-payment, the director may be arrested for fraud and face up to five years of imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Void

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (EIT Law Article 47) and may recharacterize the Taiwanese entity's charging structure as a distribution of profits, disallowing the deduction in full, and levy the statutory 10% dividend withholding tax plus a 50%-500% adjusted penalty; in cases that include false invoices, the PRC Invoice Management Regulations impose a maximum fine of RMB 500,000 and possible forced dissolution.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil exposes the director to personal liability for all CIT, WHT and surcharges; an exit ban (blacklist) assesses the director's personal bank accounts; if the veil-piercing is accompanied by cross-border fraud convictions, the director faces a prison sentence of seven to ten years in a serious-scale, cross-border setting.

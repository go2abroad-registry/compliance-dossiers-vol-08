Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under Taiwan Company Act, every company must maintain a registered office address in the Taiwan jurisdiction where governmental summons and legal process may be served. A virtual office or commercial mailbox cannot serve as the sole registered office for an ISIC 8299 / NAICS 561431 commercial mail receiving agency (CMRA) because the business itself requires a licensed physical facility with mail-reception infrastructure. Taiwanese municipal tax bureaus and banks will reject a standalone virtual-office mailbox as evidence of commercial substance, deny input VAT recovery, and treat the arrangement as a sham for corporate income tax purposes. For a CMRA licensee, the registered office must be a dedicated physical suite with a posted sign, a staffed mail reception area, secure scanning equipment, an auditable mail log, and a lease or ownership deed in the company's name. A virtual office may be used only as a secondary marketing or mail-forwarding address after the physical registered office is established and after the company obtains the relevant business license from the municipal regulator.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in Taiwan, the entity must first complete company registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) to obtain a Company Registration Certificate. Subsequently, the WFOE must apply for a Unified Business Number (UBIN) from the local National Taxation Bureau (under the Ministry of Finance) by submitting the incorporation documents, a local registered address proof, and the appointment of a local tax representative. The tax authority will issue the UBIN, which serves as the Tax Identification Number (TIN) for both income tax and VAT purposes. For foreign non-resident entities without a fixed place of business, a tax representative must be appointed to handle registration and compliance; however, a WFOE is a resident entity and follows the standard domestic registration procedure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a bifurcated FX regime. The current account, which captures service-trade revenue such as recurring virtual-office subscriptions and mail-scanning fees, is broadly convertible on documentary proof: banks verify underlying contracts, invoices, tax filing certificates, and SAFE e-declarations before executing remittances. The capital account, however, remains strictly controlled. A Taiwan-based foreign investor injecting equity into a PRC Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must complete the Ministry of Commerce/SAMR foreign-investment information filing, obtain SAFE FDI registration, and open a dedicated FDI foreign-exchange or capital-account settlement account. Foreign-currency capital may only be converted into CNY for genuine, purpose-bound operating expenditure; it may not be used for unlicensed securities investment, entrusted loans, or domestic lending. Profit remittance to the Taiwan shareholder is treated as current-account investment income: after the annual statutory audit and tax clearance, the WFOE may remit after-tax dividends with a board resolution, audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and a SAFE-approved bank remittance dossier. Liquidation proceeds and return of capital remain capital-account transactions requiring additional SAFE approval. There is no forced surrender of export receipts after the 2012 SAFE reform, but all proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic foreign-currency account within prescribed trade-monitoring windows. Overall, renminbi is not freely convertible on the capital account, and every cross-strait remittance must maintain a legal and auditable documentation trail.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The mandatory FX surrender ratio is 0% under SAFE's 2012 trade-in-goods reform; no statutory percentage of export proceeds must be converted into CNY. However, foreign-currency receipts from goods or service exports must be repatriated into a domestic designated FX account within the regulatory monitoring window, generally within 90 days after customs declaration for goods exports or within the contractually agreed service-delivery/payment period for service exports. A longer window may be filed with the bank for legitimate commercial reasons, and retained FX may be used within the permissible current-account framework.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low and effectively infeasible as a bank substitute. Under the PRC Commercial Bank Law, PBOC rules, and SAFE circulars, only licensed banking institutions may open deposit or settlement accounts for a China-incorporated WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut, Airwallex, or similar neobanks cannot maintain local RMB current accounts or clear inbound capital in the company's name. Stripe, the stated target payment rail, does not directly support merchant accounts for mainland-China-incorporated entities; therefore, recurring card revenue cannot be settled into a Chinese WFOE's local bank account without an overseas acquiring subsidiary or a licensed cross-border payment institution cooperating with a PRC bank. The legally compliant structure for this WFOE is to route all capital and card settlement through a licensed domestic Tier-1 bank, with any offshore EMI or fintech used only as a supplementary sales-collection tool outside China and then reintroduced as FDI or current-account service-trade remittance.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan parent transfers equity capital to the WFOE via SWIFT MT103 to a designated Tier-1 bank. Prior to injection, the WFOE must complete the foreign-investment information filing and SAFE FDI registration, then open a capital-account foreign-currency settlement account. Each inward remittance must include the beneficiary WFOE name and account, a payment narrative reading 'Capital injection in accordance with approved WFOE investment contract', and a valid SAFE transaction-purpose code for foreign equity investment. After the CNY/foreign-currency arrival is reconciled, the bank will settle into RMB only upon presentation of genuine payment vouchers, and the RMB may be used strictly for operating expenditures. The CPA capital verification report is issued after funds are received and the bank confirms custody; this report is then submitted to the company registry and tax filing authorities. If the Taiwan parent prefers RMB injection, the remittance can be cleared through the RMB cross-border interbank system (CIPS) as long as the cross-strait investment purpose is fully documented; otherwise, USD is the simplest, safest clearing currency.For this mail-scanning and virtual-office service business, outbound trade disbursements are primarily service-trade payments. The normal rail is SWIFT MT103 for cross-border service receipts and payments, with full SAFE service-trade documentation: commercial contract, invoice, payment narration, proof of delivery, and tax withholding certificates where applicable. Commercial Letters of Credit and documentary collections are not appropriate for pure digital scanning services; MT700 LCs and collection documents are reserved for merchandise and bill-of-lading trade. If the WFOE receives recurring credit-card revenue from overseas clients through a licensed cross-border acquiring arrangement, the settlement funds arrive as regular service-trade current-account receipts and must be matched to the underlying contract and payment log. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends paid by a Taiwan company to a nonresident company or individual, subject to applicable treaty or special cross-border relief. Dividends paid to a Taiwan-resident company are generally handled under Taiwan's intercompany dividend rules rather than the nonresident withholding rate.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is generally required where the taxpayer's annual net revenue is at least NT$300 million or the annual amount of related-party transactions reaches at least NT$200 million, subject to transaction-specific rules and exemptions. A Master File is generally relevant where the Taiwan entity belongs to a multinational enterprise group exceeding the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold, commonly NT$7 billion under Taiwan rules. Country-by-Country Reporting generally follows the OECD consolidated-revenue threshold of EUR750 million or its Taiwan-dollar equivalent and applicable Taiwan filing or notification rules.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime applicable to every holding or service company. Substance is nevertheless relevant under transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance principle, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty anti-abuse standards, controlled foreign company rules, and permanent-establishment analysis. The company should maintain real premises, employees or appropriately controlled personnel, operational systems, customer contracts, decision-making records, service delivery evidence, cybersecurity controls, mail-handling procedures, and commercially rational capitalization. A virtual office that exists only as an address, books no genuine operating activity, or performs no substantive mail or scanning functions presents material tax residency, treaty, TP, VAT, licensing, and permanent-establishment risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, interest, administrative penalties, compulsory collection, and criminal sanctions. Intentional tax evasion may result in imprisonment of up to five years and a criminal fine that may reach NT$10 million, subject to the applicable statute and facts.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face criminal prosecution, civil tax restitution, administrative disqualification consequences, asset seizure, and travel or exit restrictions where legally ordered. Limited liability does not protect a director who personally participated in fraud or unlawful tax conduct.
Business Tax and Invoice Fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, penalties, invoice-use restrictions, business suspension, and potential criminal prosecution. Serious business-tax evasion and fraudulent invoicing may carry imprisonment of up to five years and substantial fines.

Director personal liability: Directors, responsible persons, accounting officers, and participating agents may be jointly exposed to criminal investigation, civil restitution, seizure, and restrictions on departure.
Unlicensed Commercial Mail Receiving and Postal Activity

Statutory sanction: Orders to cease, corrective measures, administrative fines, confiscation or disposal of prohibited items, suspension or revocation of business permissions, and possible criminal liability under applicable postal, telecommunications, consumer-protection, or sectoral legislation.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face personal fines or criminal liability where they authorized or knowingly permitted unlicensed operations. Directors may also incur civil claims for lost, opened, misdirected, or unlawfully disclosed mail.
Personal Data, Confidentiality, and Mail Secrecy

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, corrective orders, compensation claims, deletion or cessation orders, and criminal sanctions for unlawful disclosure or misuse of protected communications or personal data.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers may face personal criminal or civil liability for knowing authorization, negligent supervision, inadequate security, or failure to respond to a breach.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: Suspicious transaction reporting failures may result in administrative penalties and supervisory action. Money laundering, concealment of criminal proceeds, or sanctions-related conduct may result in asset freezing, confiscation, imprisonment, and substantial fines under Taiwan law.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, beneficial owners, and employees may face personal criminal prosecution, asset confiscation, civil restitution, and legally imposed travel or exit restrictions.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment to arm's-length profit, denial of deductions, additional tax, interest, documentation penalties, audit expansion, and possible anti-avoidance or criminal consequences where fraud is involved.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil claims for tax losses and shareholder damages and may incur personal criminal exposure if they knowingly approved sham transactions or false records.
Withholding Tax Default

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unwithheld tax from the withholding agent, late-payment charges, administrative penalties, denial of treaty relief, and potential criminal liability for deliberate concealment.

Director personal liability: The responsible person and withholding agent may be pursued for unpaid amounts where the company cannot satisfy the liability or where deliberate non-compliance is established.
Permanent Establishment and Tax Residency Mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax, business tax, withholding tax, interest, penalties, permanent-establishment assessments, and treaty-relief denial.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal exposure for knowingly maintaining inaccurate tax residency positions, especially where contracts, staffing, bank accounts, and operating decisions demonstrate Taiwan substance.
Corporate Capacity and Cross-Strait Structural Inconsistency

Statutory sanction: Registration rejection or cancellation, orders to unwind transactions, administrative fines, foreign-investment and cross-strait sanctions, tax reassessment, and possible criminal liability for false declarations or prohibited transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors and promoters may be personally liable for inaccurate registrations, unauthorized investment, unlawful remittances, and resulting tax or creditor losses. The entity description must be reconciled before filing or repatriation.

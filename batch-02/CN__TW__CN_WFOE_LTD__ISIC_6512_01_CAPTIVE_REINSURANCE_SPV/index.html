Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the Taiwan Company Act for a limited liability company, but the paid-in capital must be adequate for the stated business scope and verified by a licensed certified public accountant. Because the formation purpose is a captive reinsurance SPV, the Financial Supervisory Commission will impose a minimum paid-in capital condition in the operating license approval; the capital must be held in a blocked escrow or dedicated reserve account in Taiwan until the license is issued and the reserve plan is approved. The inflow from the foreign parent must be documented for foreign exchange and AML purposes.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taxation Agency (Taiwan); taxpayer services are delivered by the five regional National Taxation Bureaus. Online channels include the Tax e-Filing Portal maintained by the Ministry of Finance for return filing and the E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform for electronic invoice management; tax registration enquiries are also made through the Ministry of Finance Tax Portal.

E-government filing portal

Foreign non-resident entities making taxable supplies in Taiwan must obtain a Taiwan tax identification number, locally known as the uniform number, through tax registration with the local National Taxation Bureau. Procedure: (1) secure any prerequisite industry licence; for a captive reinsurance SPV this is the statutory authorisation issued by the Financial Supervisory Commission (Taiwan); (2) appoint a resident tax-filing agent or, for qualifying cross-border service suppliers, a settlement agent in Taiwan; (3) submit the tax registration statement required by the Business Tax Act to the competent National Taxation Bureau, attaching the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, notarised or apostilled power of attorney, responsible person identity document, local place-of-business proof, board resolution approving the Taiwan branch or SPV, and the FSC authorisation; (4) receive the assigned uniform number and business tax registration confirmation; (5) begin filing periodic business tax returns on the prescribed schedule. A foreign-owned WFOE limited company is first registered with the Ministry of Economic Affairs, which issues the uniform number automatically; the WFOE must then complete tax registration with the local National Taxation Bureau before or within 15 days after the date of commencement of taxable activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not operate a check-the-box, partnership pass-through, or hybrid mismatch election for indirect tax purposes. Small-scale businesses may apply for the simplified 1% non-value-added tax status and for exemption from issuing uniform invoices, but that is an administrative classification decision, not a substantive entity-level tax election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary turnover threshold. Every person carrying on business in Taiwan is required to obtain tax registration before taxable supplies commence, regardless of sales volume. Small-scale businesses with average monthly sales not exceeding NTD 80,000 remain registrable and are taxed at the non-value-added 1% rate; foreign suppliers of cross-border services are subject to registration with no minimum turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available under the Business Tax Act for: goods physically exported from Taiwan; services supplied in Taiwan but completely used or consumed outside Taiwan and paid in foreign currency; services related to international transport; and sales made from export processing zones and free trade zones under statutory conditions. For a captive reinsurance SPV, the provider must demonstrate that the reinsured risk or benefit stream is situated outside Taiwan, that the premium income is derived from foreign ceding companies, and that foreign-currency remittance has been or will be received; if the underlying risk is Taiwan-domiciled, the premium is standard-rated at 5% and cannot be zero-rated.

  • Customs official export declaration (export customs bill of entry) generated in the Ministry of Finance Single Window and bearing the export declaration number.
  • Commercial invoice and packing list.
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing actual cross-border shipment.
  • Foreign exchange settlement certificate or bank inward remittance advice proving receipt of export proceeds by the Taiwan seller.
  • For services: the governing contract and a written confirmation from the overseas recipient that the service is wholly consumed or used outside Taiwan.

Taiwan mandates the uniform invoice system. Registered businesses must issue uniform invoices bearing the seller's uniform number; electronic invoicing is the standard channel and requires integration with the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform. B2B and B2C e-invoice data are transmitted by certified ERP, POS, or API middleware within statutorily prescribed short windows, generally 48 hours for B2C cloud-invoice consumers, and the platform validates the invoice sequence, totals, and random 4-digit identification code. There is no real-time central pre-clearance or digital stamping; the platform's near-real-time validation, duplicate-suppression logic, and subsequent cross-checking by the National Taxation Bureau constitute the anti-fraud digital control. Small-scale businesses may issue special receipts instead of uniform invoices when administratively approved.

The National Taxation Bureau will scrutinise zero-rated export claims by comparing the sales reported in business tax returns with customs export declaration data, insurance/reinsurance documentation, and bank foreign-currency receipt records. Claimed refunds may be held for documentary verification and are subject to retrospective clawback with statutory interest if any required document is missing, inconsistent, or forged. Where the 0% rate is claimed but the export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days or the underlying export is not substantiated, the zero-rating is reversed, the uncollected 5% VAT is assessed, a penalty of not more than five times the underpaid tax is imposed under the Business Tax Act, and the taxpayer may face criminal prosecution under the Tax Collection Act for fraudulent or knowingly wrongful claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating RMB exchange rate with partial capital account convertibility. Current account transactions are freely convertible; capital account items (including profit remittances and capital injections) require SAFE pre-approval or post-facto verification. WFOE profit remittances are subject to 10% enterprise income tax withholding and SAFE documentation review to ensure compliance with anti-evasion rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be surrendered to the exporter's designated foreign exchange bank within two working days. Mandatory surrender applies to all export proceeds; conversion occurs at the bank's closing rate on the surrender date. Non-compliance may result in penalties, account restrictions, or forced conversion at unfavorable rates.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Late filing penalty: 1% of unpaid tax for every two days of delay, up to a maximum of 15% of the unpaid tax; if no return is filed after a demand, the tax authority may assess the tax by estimation and impose a surcharge of 100% to 300% of the undercharged tax. Late payment interest accrues monthly, and departure controls may be imposed for taxes due.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory for related-party transactions exceeding NT$50 million (with foreign related parties) or NT$100 million (domestic related parties); Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for multinational groups whose consolidated group revenue exceeds NT$30 billion. A simplified threshold may apply for transactions with related parties in no/low-tax jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not implement a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but enforces a substance-over-form doctrine and a General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 12-6 of the Income Tax Act. A captive reinsurance SPV must prove genuine underwriting, claims management, reinsurance administration, and strategic decision-making in Taiwan; otherwise, the entity may be disregarded, treaty benefits denied, and profits re-allocated to the shareholder under anti-avoidance provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act and Article 108 of the Income Tax Act: imprisonment for up to 5 years, detention, and/or a fine up to NT$60,000; administrative penalty of 1.5 to 3 times the amount of tax evaded; interest on underpayment.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute or direct false returns may be jointly criminally liable; civil restitution for taxes, penalties, and interest; travel bans and departure restrictions under Article 24 of the Tax Collection Act.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits; re-characterization of the transactions to reflect economic reality; application of Article 12-6 GAAR resulting in additional tax and interest; administrative penalty up to 3 times the underpaid tax; potential public disclosure of tax delinquencies.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the additional taxes and penalties if they signed deliberate non-disclosures; travel bans for failure to pay; civil damages for breach of fiduciary duty to minority creditors.
Transfer Pricing Violation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority upward adjustment of taxable income to the arm's-length standard; interest on underpaid tax; penalty of up to 2.5 times the underpaid tax under Article 108-1 of the Income Tax Act; referral to criminal prosecution if documents are falsified.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly executing non-arm's-length arrangements face personal liability for the TP adjustment and fines; travel bans applied to enforce collection; criminal accountability for forged consulting agreements.
AML / Insurance Regulatory Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FSC administrative fines up to NT$3 million for unlicensed reinsurance operations; AML violations subject to fines up to NT$5 million and imprisonment up to 7 years for intentional money laundering; suspension of operations and revocation of registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers may be personally fined and imprisoned; joint civil liability for insurer obligations; asset freezes and travel controls in serious AML cases; disqualification from serving as officers of any Taiwan regulated entity.

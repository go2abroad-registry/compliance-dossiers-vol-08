Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – Colombian National Tax and Customs Directorate; official portal and transactional platform: www.dian.gov.co / MUISCA (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicios y Trámites); auxiliary company registration via the Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce) Single Business Window.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned S.A.S. is assigned its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) automatically upon incorporation before the Cámara de Comercio. The legal representative must then: (1) complete the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) registration through the MUISCA portal, electing the applicable fiscal responsibilities, including 'Responsable de IVA' status, withholding-agent obligations, and customs exporter/importer registration; (2) file the Beneficial Owner (Beneficiario Final) declaration required by DIAN Resolution 164/2021; (3) enable electronic invoicing in test/production through DIAN-approved billing software or a certified technology provider, obtaining the DIAN digital validation token; (4) maintain a resident legal representative and a Colombian tax domicile for service of process; and (5) update the RUT within the statutory filing deadlines for any change in address, activity, or foreign-exchange status. No minimum capital is required for the S.A.S. under Colombian law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the S.A.S. is a separate Colombian tax resident subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income at the 2025 general rate of 35%; dividends paid to non-resident shareholders from taxed profits bear an additional 7.5% remittance withholding tax; no pass-through or check-the-box transparency exists. Territorial_Scope rules apply only to Colombian-source activities of foreign entities or to branches/PEs of foreign companies, not to a resident S.A.S.; a standard non-Zona-Franca entity does not enjoy Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Colombian tax law. Available S.A.S. elections and regimes: (i) the integrated SIMPLE tax regime for businesses with gross annual revenue below 100,000 UVT (approximately COP 4,979,900,000 for 2025), which consolidates income tax, VAT, and parafiscal contributions into sector-banded rates; (ii) election of transfer-pricing method and voluntary submission of master and local documentation to DIAN; (iii) accelerated VAT refund election available to qualified exporters that comply with foreign-exchange reintegration; (iv) voluntary classification as 'Gran Contribuyente' (large taxpayer); (v) hybrid mismatch and controlled-foreign-company rules under Law 2277/2022 are mandatory and automatically disallow deductions arising from hybrid structures; no elective transparent treatment for the S.A.S. is permitted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a single, floating exchange-rate regime (USD/COP) with unrestricted current-account convertibility. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment (FDI) into a Colombian S.A.S., are freely convertible but mandated through the official foreign exchange market (Mercado Cambiario) and subject to registration with Banco de la República. Profit and dividend remittances are permissible provided the underlying investment is registered, the distribution meets Colombian tax documentation, and payments are channeled via an authorized intermediary. No quantitative restrictions or differentiated exchange rates apply, but a resident must settle all foreign-exchange obligations through the local market and must obtain prior approval for maintaining foreign-currency accounts. Documentary friction exists mainly in the form of investment registration timelines, tax compliance certificates and the statutory 30-day filing windows, rather than absolute convertibility barriers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds derived from goods and services are subject to mandatory channeling through the Colombian foreign-exchange market within a maximum statutory period of six (6) months from the date of shipment (for goods) or from the accrual/rendering date (for services). The mandatory surrender ratio is 100% of the net export proceeds, meaning the foreign currency must be sold to a Banco de la República authorized intermediary in exchange for Colombian pesos, unless a particular exemption is granted (e.g., compensation systems, offshore contracts approved under the Foreign Exchange Regime, or authorized foreign-currency accounts for specific use cases). The intermediary bank records the repatriation against the corresponding export customs declaration or service contract and reports compliance to Banco de la República.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) together with the Finance and Public Credit Ministry, acting through the Foreign Exchange Department (Departamento de Cambios Internacionales), under the legal framework of Decree 1735 of 1993 and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN 83, supervisory oversight provided by Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia for authorized intermediaries.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and neobanks that are not licensed as credit institutions under Colombian law cannot act as the company's primary treasury or settlement account provider. They may be used only as limited foreign-currency collection channels for ancillary customer/supplier payments, but any such settlement must ultimately be channeled into Colombia through a licensed exchange intermediary. Colombian regulations (Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia) require corporate accounts to be maintained at locally licensed banks, fintech or EMI accounts do not satisfy the 'full banking relationship' requirement for FX registration, export surrender and trading-related documentary evidence, and therefore carry material compliance and clearing-risk limitations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign-direct-investment capital injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the parent's bank to the Colombian S.A.S.'s foreign-currency or COP account held at a Banco de la República authorized local intermediary. The remitter's SWIFT field 70 shall include the purpose 'Aporte de Capital – Inversión Directa de Colombia' and the applicable foreign-exchange concept code for direct capital contributions (currently code 3301 for initial capital creation / capital expansion). Within 30 calendar days after receipt, the Colombian legal entity must present the exchange declaration form 'Formato 1 – Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional' to the intermediary bank, attaching the documentation evidencing the contribution: board/shareholder resolution, apostilled notarized incorporation deed, certification of the RUT, bank-credit certificate and the source-of-funds documentation. The bank then registers the investment with Banco de la República and issues a certificate of registration which is the prerequisite for future profit remittances, capital repatriation and hedging activities.Outbound payments to overseas suppliers for the import of diagnostic reagents are to be executed through the local corporate-internet-banking rails, principally SWIFT MT103, with purpose codes that correspond to 'Importación de Bienes' (e.g., concept code 7551 under Circular DCIN 83 as applied to imports). For high-value or deferred-payment transactions, an irrevocable documentary commercial letter of credit issued under UCP 600 is recommended; the local bank opens the L/C by SWIFT MT700 to the beneficiary's advising bank, establishing a direct and secure clearing rail. All disbursements must be channeled via the authorized intermediary; supporting customs declarations, invoices, packing lists and country-of-export certificates must be retained for exchange declaration and possible Banco de la República audit.

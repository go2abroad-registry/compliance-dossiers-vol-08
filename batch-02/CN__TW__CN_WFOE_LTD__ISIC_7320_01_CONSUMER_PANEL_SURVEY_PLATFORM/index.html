Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (国家税务总局, STA); official portal: https://www.chinatax.gov.cn; electronic tax bureau (电子税务局): https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn; Taiwan-related cross-border tax matters are coordinated under the Cross-Strait Agreement on Avoidance of Double Taxation and Enhancement of Tax Cooperation (海峡两岸避免双重课税及加强税务合作协议).

E-government filing portal

A WFOE's statutory tax identifier is the Unified Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码, USCC), automatically issued at SAMR incorporation under the 'one license, one code' (多证合一) reform and auto-registered with the competent tax authority within 30 days. The foreign owner must then: (1) file the tax registration record via the electronic tax bureau; (2) elect VAT taxpayer status (general or small-scale); (3) register on the national fully digitalized electronic invoice platform (数电票); and (4) complete the cross-border payment tax filing (服务贸易等项目对外支付税务备案) under SAT Circular 16/2016 prior to outbound remittances to non-resident vendors or Taiwan counterparties.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: a WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise (居民企业) taxed as a separate CIT taxpayer at 25% on worldwide income. Taiwan-sourced business income is includible in the mainland CIT base, subject to foreign tax credit relief under the Cross-Strait tax agreement for Taiwan tax actually paid. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise (居民企业) taxed as a separate CIT taxpayer at 25% on worldwide income. Taiwan-sourced business income is includible in the mainland CIT base, subject to foreign tax credit relief under the Cross-Strait tax agreement for Taiwan tax actually paid. No pass-through or territorial scope election is available under the EIT Law; all profits are taxed at entity level, and branch remittances are treated as dividends subject to 10% withholding when paid to non-resident parents.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (A) general versus small-scale VAT taxpayer election (small-scale permitted if annual taxable sales ≤ RMB 5M; provides simplified 3% output tax but no input credit); (B) zero-rating or exemption election for cross-border services exported to Taiwan or other overseas recipients under CaiShui 2016 No. 36, Arts. 25-26 and SAT Announcement 2020 No. 12; (C) 15% preferential CIT rate election for qualified software enterprises, advanced technology services enterprises, and enterprises in encouraged industries within designated zones (e.g., Hainan Free Trade Port); (D) direct and indirect foreign tax credit election and loss-pooling under CIT Law Arts. 23-24; (E) loss carryforward election of 5 years, extended to 10 years for qualifying high-tech enterprises; (F) no hybrid mismatch or check-the-box election exists; CFC anti-deferral rules under CIT Law Art. 45 operate to prevent hybrid arbitrage, supplemented by general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) under Art. 47.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 (¥5M); below threshold, general status is elective. Market research and consumer polling services (ISIC 7320) are taxable services subject to 6% VAT as general taxpayer outputs when supplied in mainland China, 0% when qualifying as offshore-exported services to Taiwan, and 3% under small-scale status (1% temporary relief available in designated periods).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT with refund is available where goods are physically exported under customs supervision with a valid export declaration (出口报关单) and the consideration is received in foreign exchange (or approved RMB settlement). Services: zero-rating or tax exemption applies under CaiShui 2016 No. 36 and SAT Announcement 2020 No. 12 where the consumer panel survey / market research SaaS service is supplied to an overseas enterprise or individual and is wholly consumed outside mainland China; supply into Taiwan from the mainland qualifies if the Taiwan recipient has no mainland permanent establishment and no place of supply is deemed located in mainland China; services physically rendered inside mainland China to Taiwan clients do not qualify for zero rating. The supplier must be a registered general VAT taxpayer and must reconcile the inward foreign exchange remittance with the declared export value.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is fully convertible for current-account items involving bona fide cross-border services (including ISIC 7320 market research, consumer polling, and SaaS platform operations) and for post-tax profit remittances, subject to the settlement bank's genuineness review, SAFE service-trade monitoring declarations, and balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical reporting. Capital-account items remain restricted: FDI equity injection requires SAFE FDI registration, cross-border shareholder loans require PBOC/SAFE macro-prudential registration, and any outward investment requires NDRC/MOFCOM/SAFE clearance. The WFOE may receive service-export proceeds from Taiwan and other jurisdictions in foreign currency or RMB, retain them in a current-account FX settlement account, or convert them to RMB at the onshore market rate without any mandatory conversion. Profit remittances to the Taiwan shareholder are lawful following the annual statutory audit, full Chinese tax clearance, and the bank-side cross-border profit-remittance declaration; dividend withholding tax applies at the statutory rate unless the Taiwan shareholder establishes treaty relief under the Cross-Strait Double Taxation Arrangement, the practical availability of which remains subject to documentation and enforcement limitations in both jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory foreign-exchange surrender and no statutory conversion ratio apply to export receipts. Under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and SAFE's trade-monitoring framework: (a) goods-trade export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic bank account and reconciled with customs declarations within the prescribed verification cycles for the enterprise's A/B/C risk classification; (b) service-trade receipts, being the WFOE's primary survey-platform revenue, must be credited to a domestic current-account FX settlement account and declared in the BOP statistical reporting system through the settlement bank within the statutory declaration window (typically five working days from receipt); (c) proceeds may be retained in FX or settled to RMB at the onshore rate at the enterprise's discretion; and (d) proceeds may not remain outside the PRC banking system, and any offshore retention requires SAFE approval that is not granted for ordinary service-export receipts.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), acting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the local SAFE provincial branch exercising delegated supervision over the WFOE's current-account and capital-account foreign-exchange operations, FDI registration, trade-receipt verification, and annual reporting obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% of Taiwan-source and worldwide taxable income for a Taiwan-resident company

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a non-resident individual or foreign enterprise, subject to applicable tax treaty relief, special cross-strait rules, and statutory exemptions. Dividends paid by a Taiwan company to another Taiwan company are generally not subject to dividend withholding tax at the recipient level under ordinary domestic rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year company, annual income tax returns and tax payments are generally due from May 1 through May 31 of the following year. A company using a non-calendar fiscal year must file within five months after fiscal year-end. Provisional income tax filing is generally due from September 1 through September 30 for calendar-year companies and is generally based on one-half of the preceding year's assessed tax. A company may use an alternative provisional calculation based on current-period results if statutory conditions and certification requirements are satisfied.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan Local File documentation is generally required where the Taiwan enterprise has related-party transactions and does not qualify for an exemption. A commonly applicable exemption threshold is aggregate related-party transaction amount below NT$200 million for the relevant fiscal year, subject to transaction-type, filing, and regulatory conditions. A Taiwan enterprise with annual net sales and non-operating income below NT$300 million may qualify for simplified documentation or other relief, but this is not a blanket exemption from arm's-length pricing. Master File documentation is generally relevant where the Taiwan entity belongs to a multinational enterprise group with consolidated annual revenue of at least NT$7 billion and the applicable Taiwan conditions are satisfied. Country-by-country reporting generally applies at the group level at or above the statutory consolidated-revenue threshold, commonly NT$27 billion, subject to the reporting structure and filing rules.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a single broadly framed economic substance regulation regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdiction ESR regimes. Nevertheless, economic substance is relevant through the arm's-length principle, Taiwan's general anti-avoidance rules, substance-over-form analysis, controlled foreign company rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, source-income rules, and business-purpose analysis. The Taiwan company should maintain local decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, customer and supplier records, board and management evidence, bank-account control, data-processing capability, and contemporaneous evidence that its market-research SaaS activities are actually performed. A shell entity that merely invoices customers or receives group payments without functions, assets, risks, or commercial rationale is highly vulnerable to denial of deductions, recharacterization, withholding assessment, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, penalties based on omitted or underreported tax, late-payment surcharge, interest, compulsory collection, and potential criminal prosecution under Taiwan tax laws. Serious tax evasion may expose the responsible person to imprisonment of up to five years and a criminal fine that may reach NT$10 million, subject to the applicable offense and court determination.

Director personal liability: The responsible person, director, manager, accounting officer, and participating advisers may face personal criminal liability where they intentionally direct, approve, assist, or conceal the conduct. Civil restitution, tax recovery, asset attachment, departure or travel restrictions during enforcement, and imprisonment may arise under the applicable proceedings. Limited liability does not protect intentional tax fraud.
Withholding-Tax and Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed the unpaid withholding tax, late-payment surcharge, interest, statutory penalties, and collection costs. The payer may be liable even where the foreign recipient received the funds and the Taiwan company did not retain the tax.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons and accounting personnel may incur personal administrative or criminal exposure for intentional withholding violations or falsification. Directors may also face civil claims from the company for losses caused by unpaid tax, penalties, and failed remittance controls.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may substitute an arm's-length price, deny deductions, reallocate income, impose additional tax, assess penalties and interest, require Local File or Master File documentation, and initiate an audit covering multiple fiscal years.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face civil liability for deliberate or grossly negligent tax losses. Personal criminal liability may arise if transfer-pricing records are knowingly fabricated or used as part of tax evasion, fraud, money laundering, or false accounting.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, denial of deductions, recharacterization of income, withholding-tax reassessment, controlled-foreign-company or anti-avoidance adjustments where applicable, administrative penalties, and possible investigation for tax fraud or false transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may be personally liable for intentional sham transactions, false records, tax losses, and creditor or tax authority claims. Asset preservation orders, departure restrictions, civil restitution, and imprisonment may apply where conduct constitutes a criminal offense.
Money Laundering and Illicit Payment Routing

Statutory sanction: Suspicious transactions may be reported, accounts may be frozen, funds may be seized, and the company may face prosecution under Taiwan anti-money-laundering laws. Serious money-laundering offenses may carry imprisonment of up to seven years and criminal fines, subject to the offense, amount, intent, and applicable statute.

Director personal liability: Directors, responsible persons, beneficial owners, compliance officers, and participating agents may face personal criminal liability, confiscation, civil restitution, asset freezing, departure restrictions, and imprisonment. Corporate limited liability does not protect a person who knowingly controls or assists laundering.
Unlawful Personal-Data and Consumer-Panel Practices

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, orders to correct or stop processing, data deletion, business interruption, compensation claims, and possible criminal liability may arise under Taiwan personal-data and consumer-protection legislation. Tax deductions for unlawful payments or compensation may also be challenged.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face personal civil and, in serious intentional cases, criminal exposure for unlawful data processing, concealment, or failure to implement required controls. Directors may also be liable for foreseeable corporate losses caused by inadequate compliance systems.
Unlicensed or Misrepresented Business Activity

Statutory sanction: Business registration sanctions, administrative fines, suspension or prohibition orders, tax reassessment, loss of deductions, and sector-specific criminal penalties may apply. Payment-platform activity must not be conducted as a regulated financial service without the required authorization.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face personal fines, criminal liability, civil damages, and restrictions on management where they knowingly authorize unlicensed operations or false regulatory submissions.
Accounting, Invoice, and Record-Keeping Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, rejection of deductions, deemed income assessment, tax penalties, audit expansion, and potential criminal prosecution for false books, fraudulent invoices, or obstruction of tax inspection.

Director personal liability: The responsible person and accounting personnel may incur personal administrative, civil, and criminal liability. Directors may be liable for corporate tax and creditor losses where record failure constitutes breach of statutory duties or intentional misconduct.

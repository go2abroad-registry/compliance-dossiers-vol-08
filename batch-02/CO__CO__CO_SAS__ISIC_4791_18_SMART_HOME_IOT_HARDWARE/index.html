Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

DIAN – Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (National Directorate of Taxes and Customs), portal: www.dian.gov.co; digital services via the Muisca and the VUCE (Foreign Trade Single Window).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Colombian S.A.S. must (1) incorporate before a notary and the Chamber of Commerce to obtain the Certificado de Cámara de Comercio; (2) apply for the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) online at DIAN's Muisca portal, presenting the certificate of existence, foreign tax identification proof, and a local authorized representative's identification; (3) obtain digital signature certificate for e-invoicing and regulatory filings; (4) register as VAT responsible person (Régimen Común/Responsable de IVA), as importer/exporter (Usuario Aduanero) if conducting direct foreign trade, and as employer if hiring local staff.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a Colombian S.A.S. must (1) incorporate before a notary and the Chamber of Commerce to obtain the Certificado de Cámara de Comercio; (2) apply for the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) online at DIAN's Muisca portal, presenting the certificate of existence, foreign tax identification proof, and a local authorized representative's identification; (3) obtain digital signature certificate for e-invoicing and regulatory filings; (4) register as VAT responsible person (Régimen Común/Responsable de IVA), as importer/exporter (Usuario Aduanero) if conducting direct foreign trade, and as employer if hiring local staff.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist under Colombian tax law; corporate income tax (CIT) is assessed at the entity level on worldwide income for resident S.A.S. entities. Optional regimes include: (a) Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) – a presumptive/aggregate tax election for qualifying small taxpayers under 100,000 UVT of gross revenue, integrating income tax, VAT, and municipal tax (ICA) burdens; (b) Grandfathering for newly incorporated entities is limited to a single reduced CIT rate on initial-year small profits for certain start-up entities (Ley 2010/2019, Art. 240-1), subject to strict thresholds and activity restrictions; (c) entities achieving export income may file for VAT refunds using the 'Exportador' certification procedure rather than an elective classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for VAT registration by importers, exporters, or persons engaged in the supply of taxable services/goods in commercial premises. Registrants under the ordinary 'Responsable de IVA' regime must register regardless of turnover. Simplified sales-regime status (Régimen Simplificado) is available only to natural persons (not to the S.A.S. corporate entity) if prior-year gross income < 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 152,000,000 for FY2025) and other asset/contract conditions are met; a registered S.A.S. always files as a 'Gran Contribuyente' or ordinary responsible taxpayer. Foreign digital service providers without a Colombian fixed place of business must register for IVA through the simplified non-resident regime once they have any Colombian customer (no de-minimis threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including IoT hardware) are treated as zero-rated ('excluidas' with entitlement to input VAT credit/refund) under Art. 481 E.T., provided the goods physically leave Colombia and are sold to a foreign recipient. Eligible service exports, including digital support, software design, and cloud/after-sales services that are part of the smart-home IoT business, are zero-rated if they meet the 'servicios de exportación' criteria of Art. 481(b) E.T.: the service is rendered to a foreign entity, is utilized exclusively abroad, and the benefit/risk is transferred abroad; for marketing or digital services, the recipient must be a foreign non-resident and the service must generate export income. The zero-rating is not automatic; the taxpayer must prove actual foreign currency collection and the economic reality of the export through documentary evidence.

  • Forma Única de Exportación / Declaración Electrónica de Exportación (DEX)
  • Bill of Lading (for maritime) or Air Waybill (for air) evidencing physical dispatch from Colombian customs
  • Commercial invoice matching goods description, HS codes, and values
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (if applicable)
  • Certificado de Exportación (issued by customs broker/presented with DIAN VAT refund application)
  • Bank proof of foreign currency repatriation (Formas 1, 2, or 3 or intermediary bank statements showing FX settlement)

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica) for all VAT responsible taxpayers with DIAN's Muisca web services, requiring: (1) XML invoice generation and immediate transmission/validation in DIAN's fiscal cloud before the sale is legally consummated; (2) 'CUFE' (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) for standard invoices or 'CUDE' for equivalent documents, embedded with a digital stamp/QR code; (3) mandatory use of an approved software provider (Proveedor Tecnológico) or DIAN's free tool; (4) tax authority pre-validation of invoice request numbers; (5) electronic dispatch and receiving of invoices through DIAN's platform; (6) for e-commerce, integration with Shopify/Stripe payment gateways to issue automated e-invoices through certified APIs; failure to transmit or validate invoices triggers non-deductibility of expenses, penalties under Art. 652 E.T., and possible closure of establishment.

DIAN rigorously audits export VAT refunds; false or unsupported zero-rating claims trigger: (a) immediate reversal of the refund plus default interest (intereses moratorios) from the refund payment date; (b) penalty of 100% of the tax improperly refunded (Art. 648 E.T.); (c) additional penalty of 10% of the claimed value for inaccurate documentation; (d) criminal prosecution for tax fraud (Art. 434A E.T. – Defraudación Fiscal) and for false export claims under the Customs Regime Law 1762/2015, with imprisonment up to 9 years and fines; (e) registration on the DIAN enforcement blacklist and cancellation of the exporter authorization.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is fully convertible for current-account trade in goods and services. Capital-account inflows and outflows require registration with Banco de la República and must be channelled through the Mercado Cambiario Institucional (MCI) via authorised intermediaries. Profit remittance is permitted for registered foreign investment after submitting the annual declaration of foreign investment (Declaración de Cambio) and satisfying tax clearance (e.g., DIAN certificate), subject to a withholding tax of 7.5% on dividends unless reduced by a tax treaty. Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of SAS capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For exports of goods, proceeds must be collected within six months from the customs declaration date (unless an extension is granted) and 100% of the proceeds must be surrendered to the MCI, converting foreign currency into COP. The surrender must occur within five calendar days of collection. Exemptions apply for exports of certain services (e.g., software, call centres) where proceeds may be retained in foreign currency subject to registration. Late surrender attracts fines and sanctions under the foreign-exchange regime.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Banrep) acting through the Oficina de Cambios under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1068 of 2015 (Compilation of Foreign Exchange Regulations), and Circular Regulatory (DR) 21 of 2020.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Local SEDPE (Sociedad Especializada en Depósitos y Pagos Electrónicos) licences permit account issuance and payment processing in COP; however, these entities cannot hold foreign currency or offer full cross-border deposit services. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) are not authorised as credit institutions in Colombia and cannot replace a local treasury account. Feasibility for treasury holding: LOW. Use as an ancillary settlement rail only, not as the primary local treasury account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital from non-resident shareholders must be routed as a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the local bank referencing the purpose code 324 ('Capital investment in local companies'). The bank must receive a notarised board resolution (acta) authorising capital increase, and the funds must be declared on the Formulario de Declaración de Cambio (Form For 3) and entered into the Registro de Inversiones Extranjeras within 6 months via the Oficina de Cambios. For the first injection, a notarised copy of the SAS's incorporation certificate and the shareholder's banking references are required.

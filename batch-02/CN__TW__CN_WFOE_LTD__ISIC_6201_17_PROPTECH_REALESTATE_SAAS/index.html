Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Administration of Commerce, Ministry of Economic Affairs (經濟部商業發展署); foreign investment pre-screening conducted by the Investment Commission, Ministry of Economic Affairs (經濟部投資審議司), Taipei.

Governing statute

Company Act of Taiwan (公司法), 2018 Revision as amended; Articles 18 (company name), 128-129 (incorporation and mandatory Articles of Incorporation contents), 140 (share subscription), 154 (limited liability), 387 (registration filing); combined with the Statute for Investment by Foreign Nationals (外國人投資條例) Articles 7-9 for inbound capital approval; cross-strait document authentication governed by the Act Governing Relations between the People of the Taiwan Area and the Mainland Area (兩岸人民關係條例) Article 7.

Formation / constitutional filings

Company Incorporation Application Form (公司設立登記申請書) filed through the MOEA One-Stop Online Registration Platform, accompanied by the notarized Articles of Incorporation (公司章程), bank capital deposit certificate, consents of first directors, and the Investment Approval Certificate issued by the Investment Commission.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital for limited companies in Taiwan after the 2018 Company Act reform; however, the Investment Commission review practice expects a WFOE to remit paid-in capital of at least TWD 1,200,000 (approximately USD 39,000) for operational substance. The full subscribed capital must be remitted from the foreign investor via a designated foreign-exchange bank into a Foreign Investment Capital Deposit Account, and a bank deposit certificate must be issued before incorporation registration. No escrow account is mandated; the deposit certificate suffices under Article 7 of the Company Registration Rules.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: the Taipei City Fire Department requires a fire safety equipment inspection and maintenance report (消防安全設備檢修申報) for the registered office, renewable annually; the landlord must provide an approved fire protection certificate for the commercial building. Structural load inspection: required only if the company installs an on-premise micro data center; not required for standard office use. Occupational clearance: compliance with the Occupational Safety and Health Act Article 6 for office workstation ergonomics, electrical safety, and emergency exit routes; a workplace health risk assessment is recommended prior to employee onboarding.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China (国家税务总局), administered through provincial/municipal tax service bureaus and the national e-Tax portal (https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn).

E-government filing portal

A PRC WFOE is formed as a resident limited-liability company. The foreign investor first obtains a business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which assigns an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Tax registration is completed automatically via the multi-license integration system; the WFOE files financing/accounting policy, legal representative, and bank information with the local STA within 30 days. The USCC serves as the statutory tax ID and VAT registration number. For pure foreign vendors without a PRC establishment, the domestic purchaser generally applies reverse-charge or withholding registration; the non-resident supplier may file simplified tax registration through the STA e-platform where China-source services are supplied.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

PRC tax law does not provide a check-the-box election, flow-through election, or hybrid entity election. A WFOE is always a resident enterprise taxable on worldwide income at 25% (15% for qualified high-tech/provided-encouraged industries). A small-scale VAT taxpayer may elect general VAT taxpayer status when annual taxable sales do not exceed RMB 5,000,000; this is a VAT collection method election and does not alter entity-level CIT classification. Hybrid-mismatch adjustments are not elective and are addressed under PRC special tax-adjustment rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for WFOE VAT registration; every enterprise engaged in taxable sales must register for VAT. Annual taxable sales not exceeding RMB 5,000,000 may be classified as a small-scale taxpayer using a simplified 3% levy rate, with possible temporary monthly exemption of RMB 100,000 or quarterly exemption of RMB 300,000. Domestic SaaS/IT services supplied in China by a general VAT taxpayer are normally taxed at 6%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Caishui [2016] No. 36 Annex 4, PRC VAT zero-rating applies to software services, information system services, R&D services, and other listed cross-border services where: (i) the service recipient is an overseas unit, including a Taiwan-based enterprise treated as outside the mainland customs territory; (ii) the service is wholly consumed outside mainland China; and (iii) the service fee is received in foreign currency and repatriated to the WFOE's PRC bank account. Physical goods exported from mainland China to Taiwan qualify for export VAT zero-rating/rebate where a customs export declaration is completed. Domestic SaaS services supplied to China-resident customers are not zero-rated.

  • Customs export declaration (出口货物报关单) for physical goods; not required for pure SaaS but required for software/hardware mixed shipments
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export shipment for goods
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank foreign-exchange collection/settlement certificate (收汇水单) proving repatriation of proceeds
  • Cross-border service contract or purchase order with the Taiwan-based counterparty and evidence that the service is delivered/consumed outside mainland China
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for goods; SaaS delivery reports, system logs, or usage records for digital services

PRC has implemented fully digital electronic invoices (数电票) through the national electronic invoice platform under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. Invoice data must be transmitted to the STA in near real-time through tax-control integration/APIs. Digital invoices carry a uniform digital signature/cloud stamp; paper invoice systems are being phased out. Export invoices must be electronically matched with customs declarations and foreign-exchange collection records to support zero-rating.

Export VAT zero-rating and refund claims are subject to strict STA audit. Fraudulent zero-rating, fabricated Taiwan counterparties, circular/repatriated fake export proceeds, forged customs declarations, or false FX certificates result in refund denial and clawback of all rescinded VAT rebates plus interest; administrative fines from 50% to 5 times of evaded tax under the PRC Tax Collection Law; revocation of export rebate qualification; and criminal liability for export-tax-refund fraud under PRC Criminal Law Article 204, with penalties up to life imprisonment for aggravated fraudulent claims. The STA may retrospectively reopen five tax years generally and has no limitation period for collection in cases of fraud or evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Renminbi is freely convertible on the current account for genuine trade in services and software, including the recurring SaaS subscriptions, software licenses, hosting fees and real-estate rental-screening data services contemplated under ISIC code 6201; the bank must verify the underlying contract and invoice, and the enterprise reports each receipt through SAFE's banking information system. Profit remittance (dividends) from the WFOE to its Taiwan shareholder is treated as current-account investment income: it is remittable without SAFE pre-approval, but the remitting bank requires the audited Chinese statutory annual financial statements, the corporate income tax final settlement record showing taxes fully paid, a board or shareholders' resolution declaring the distributable profit, the FDI registration certificate, and, for treaty-reduced withholding, a Taiwan tax residence certificate. Capital-account items are not freely convertible: foreign equity injection above registered capital, cross-border shareholder loans (external debt), outbound investment and offshore listing require SAFE registration or, in some cases, NDRC and MOFCOM approval. The volume and velocity of cross-strait flows must be consistent with China's real-demand FX principle; capital-account RMB converted from the FDI capital account may only be used for the enterprise's genuine operations and cannot be used for equity investment, entrusted loans, wealth-management products or real-property investment. The New Taiwan Dollar (TWD) is not an onshore deliverable currency; cross-strait trade and service payments are commonly invoiced and remitted in USD or CNH with TWD converted by the Taiwan-side bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The PRC statutory surrender and mandatory conversion-ratio system for export proceeds has been abolished; an exporter may keep any portion of its foreign-currency export receipts in a foreign-currency current account at a domestic designated bank or convert to RMB at the bank's daily quotation. For goods exports, the enforceable discipline is the collection requirement: export proceeds must be repatriated into a PRC bank account and collected within 180 days of the customs export declaration date; overdue collections must be reported to the bank and SAFE. For service exports, which cover the WFOE's SaaS and PropTech contracting, there is no fixed time-to-conversion and no mandatory conversion ratio; the proceeds may remain in a foreign-currency account, but the receipt must be reported truthfully to SAFE through the settlement bank under the service-trade and other-current-items reporting regime using the correct purpose code (for example, software-related services) and the supporting service contract must be retained for at least five years. The same treatment applies to receipts aggregated by licensed cross-border payment institutions under a centralized collection product, where the PSP and the domestic bank jointly verify the aggregated export claims.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central bank and monetary authority, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated regulator of foreign-exchange administration; SAFE operates the bank-led direct registration and current-account declaration system, and national commercial banks licensed by SAFE perform on-the-ground verification of FDI capital accounts, external-debt registrations and profit-remittance checks. Banking supervision of the account-opening institutions is exercised by the National Administration of Financial Regulation (NAFR). Cross-border RMB clearing is supported by the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) operated by the PBOC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for merchant collection and low-value subscription rails; Low for primary banking and treasury escrow. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Currenxies, WorldFirst, Revolut Business, Mercury and similar) cannot lawfully act as the primary relationship bank or SAFE-verified settlement bank for a PRC-incorporated WFOE; the WFOE's receipts must be repatriated to a domestic designated bank's foreign-currency account or through a PBOC-licensed cross-border payment institution with centralized collection. The permissible architecture: the offshore EMI or PSP collects Stripe Billing and ACH Direct proceeds, aggregates net settlement, and executes a single or batched SWIFT wire to the WFOE's domestic foreign-currency account; the domestic bank captures the aggregated receipts under service-trade reporting with the PSP's settlement statement as the supporting voucher. The legal constraints are that EMIs are payment-service enterprises rather than banks in China; they cannot open deposit accounts, cannot receive FDI capital injections or external-debt drawdowns, cannot provide onshore FX derivatives, and cannot be used for the reinvestment or distribution of capital-account funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing for the Taiwan shareholder's capital contribution is a SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own account at a Taiwanese bank to the WFOE's SAFE-designated FDI capital account at the selected domestic bank. Preconditions: completion of foreign-invested enterprise registration, SAFE direct-investment registration through the receiving bank or its digital foreign-exchange system, and completion of the bank's KYC file. The remitter must be the registered shareholder itself; third-party remittances are prohibited. Upon receipt, the bank credits the USD or CNH to the capital account and, under the payment-based conversion and use-based settlement regime, the WFOE may convert the proceeds to RMB and place them in a capital-use RMB account, then make disbursements against genuine vouchers of business use (salaries, rent, hosting, software subcontracting and marketing); surplus funds stay in the foreign-currency or RMB capital account only while awaiting genuine payment obligations. Any use beyond ordinary operations, such as an intra-group loan to another PRC entity or an equity investment, requires separate SAFE approval. An alternative parent-funding rail is an external-debt shareholder loan from the Taiwan parent: before the first drawdown the loan must be registered through the bank under the macro-prudential external-debt regulation, with the principal within the statutory borrowing ceiling calculated by reference to the WFOE's net assets and the regulatory leverage factor; funds are remitted by MT103, converted under the same use-based discipline, and repaid with interest after the loan has been closed at SAFE; debt-service payments require the pre-registered repayment plan and the bank's verification of interest invoices.The WFOE's outbound clearing architecture should combine four rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for deterministic one-off payments covering cloud-hosting invoices, Taiwan and global software subcontractors, legal and financial advisers, overseas marketing and eventual dividends; each payment is verified against the supporting contract or invoice and reported by the bank to SAFE as an outward current-account payment; (2) CIPS for RMB-denominated settlements to Taiwanese and regional counterparties, avoiding USD correspondent-chain friction; (3) commercial letters of credit (MT700 and MT707) only where physical goods such as sensor hardware or integrated PropTech devices accompany the service contract, and therefore not a principal liquidity tool; and (4) the Stripe Billing and ACH Direct collection rail, aggregating low-value subscription income through the PSP and repatriating net proceeds to the domestic settlement account under a centralized collection arrangement, with each batch mapped to the SAFE service-trade declaration. For dividend or royalty payments to the Taiwan shareholder, the bank requires the audited financial statements, corporate income tax final-settlement receipts, board resolution, SAFE FDI registration evidence, the non-resident withholding-tax payment certificate, and a tax-residence certificate where a treaty-reduced or cross-strait-reduced withholding rate is applied; the remittance is then executed by MT103 in USD or CNH with the outward-remittance voucher reported through the SAFE system.Onshore deliverable RMB forwards executed with a SAFE-licensed domestic bank on a real-demand basis, matched to forecasted USD or CNH current-account receipts and paymentsForeign-exchange swaps between RMB and USD or CNH for short-dated treasury liquidity alignmentCross-currency interest-rate swaps for long-tenor external-debt or vendor-financing exposures, subject to the bank's eligibility review and real-demand verificationPlain-vanilla FX options and option combinations (puts, calls, collars and risk reversals) with the settlement bank, used exclusively to hedge identifiable underlying current-account or registered capital-account exposure; offshore CNH non-deliverable forwards may not be overlaid because they are inconsistent with SAFE's real-demand discipline and would create an unattested FX position

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for merchant collection and low-value subscription rails; Low for primary banking and treasury escrow. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Currenxies, WorldFirst, Revolut Business, Mercury and similar) cannot lawfully act as the primary relationship bank or SAFE-verified settlement bank for a PRC-incorporated WFOE; the WFOE's receipts must be repatriated to a domestic designated bank's foreign-currency account or through a PBOC-licensed cross-border payment institution with centralized collection. The permissible architecture: the offshore EMI or PSP collects Stripe Billing and ACH Direct proceeds, aggregates net settlement, and executes a single or batched SWIFT wire to the WFOE's domestic foreign-currency account; the domestic bank captures the aggregated receipts under service-trade reporting with the PSP's settlement statement as the supporting voucher. The legal constraints are that EMIs are payment-service enterprises rather than banks in China; they cannot open deposit accounts, cannot receive FDI capital injections or external-debt drawdowns, cannot provide onshore FX derivatives, and cannot be used for the reinvestment or distribution of capital-account funds.

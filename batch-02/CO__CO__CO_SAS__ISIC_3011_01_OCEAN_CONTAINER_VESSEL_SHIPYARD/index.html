Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – principal tax and customs authority; official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co; transactional portal: https://muisca.dian.gov.co; foreign trade customs portal via SIIT/VUCE.

E-government filing portal

A Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (SAS) obtains its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through DIAN. Procedure: (1) incorporate the SAS via the local Cámara de Comercio; (2) obtain the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) online at DIAN by filing the official registration form using a legal representative; (3) for non-resident shareholders, submit legalized passport copies, power of attorney for the Colombian legal representative, and the due diligence forms required by anti-money-laundering rules; (4) DIAN assigns the NIT and updates the RUT; the NIT/RUT must be displayed on invoices and all tax filings. Foreign entities without a permanent establishment may register a limited-purpose NIT for VAT/digital services obligations when required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Colombian tax law. Available statutory elections: (i) optional consolidated taxation for business groups under Articles 21-23 of the Tax Code (Régimen de Tributación de Grupos) where sub-subsidiaries may be structurally neutralized; (ii) election to qualify as a Sociedad Holding Colombiana (CHC) to obtain reduced withholding and foreign dividend/capital gains treatment under Article 496 and related provisions; (iii) election for application of income-tax consolidation and deferred recognition rules under the Economic Growth Law (Law 1943/2018). Hybrid mismatch elections are not separately recognized; anti-hybrid rules under BEPS Action 2 are incorporated via Art. 113-2 and Treaty anti-abuse clauses.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

COP 0 / no threshold – any person that performs taxable sales of goods or services, or imports goods, must register in the RUT and be VAT-responsible; foreign digital suppliers must register through a mandatory simplified VAT registration if their sales to Colombian clients exceed the equivalent of 40,000 UVTs per year (approx. COP 0 is not applicable to goods exports).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are excluded from VAT (no IVA causado) under Article 481 of the Colombian Tax Code provided the goods physically leave the country under a customs export declaration (DEX). Services may be excluded where the service is used or exploited abroad and the final beneficiary is a non-resident, subject to the condition that the service does not constitute a taxable service performed in Colombia for domestic consumption; the operation must be supported by a contract, invoice, and foreign-exchange remittance. Exporters may recover VAT paid on inputs (saldo a favor) via DIAN refund petitions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación y/o DEX)
  • Bill of Lading (Conocimiento de Embarque) or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro presentado al Banco de la República / intermediario del mercado cambiario)
  • Commercial Invoice (Factura Comercial)
  • Packing List (Lista de Empaque)
  • Certificate of Origin (if preferential treatment is claimed)
  • Export freight and insurance documents when charged separately

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing (factura electrónica) for all VAT-responsible taxpayers. Invoices must be generated through software authorized by DIAN, transmitted via SOAP/REST web services to DIAN's clearing platform, and must contain the CUDE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) and a two-dimensional verification code. All payments for taxable sales, including installments under a export letter of credit, are covered. Customs export operations require an electronic export declaration (DEX) lodged through the SIIT (Sistema Informático de Servicios de Informática Aduanera) with automatic non-intrusive validation by DIAN.

DIAN scrutinizes export refund claims through post-clearance audits and may review the DEX, bills of lading, bank remittances, and beneficial ownership of the foreign buyer. A fraudulent or unjustified export refund triggers a clawback of the refunded VAT plus moratory interest computed under Article 635 ET, a penalty of up to 200% of the misdeclared or omitted tax under Article 655 ET, and possible criminal liability under the Customs Anticontrabando regime (Law 1762/2015). False documentation also invalidates the exclusion and exposes the taxpayer to sanctions for irregular export declarations, suspension of exporters' registry status, and legal representatives' joint liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed free-currency regime: all foreign-exchange transactions of a current-account nature are freely convertible except for certain obligatory channeling through authorized financial intermediaries. Capital-account operations, including foreign direct investment (FDI) into an SAS shipyard, are permitted without prior authorization, but must be registered with Banco de la República through the applicable 'Declaración de Cambio' forms. Profit remittances and the repatriation of registered capital are permitted provided such foreign investment is duly registered and the remittance is economically grounded in audited financial statements. The main remaining hurdle is documentary: a full tax residence or foreign identification certificate, proof of source of funds, and a local tax identification number (NIT) for the SAS; if the shipyard operates under a free-trade zone (zona franca), additional customs privileges apply but exchange-channeling obligations persist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Colombian exchange regulations (Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, Chapter 1, Title 1), all export proceeds from goods related to shipbuilding must be channeled through the foreign-exchange market (Mercado Cambiario) by selling the hard currency to a Colombian commercial bank or depositing it into a foreign-currency account registered with Banco de la República within 6 months of the date of shipment or the date the export is completed; a one-time extension of up to 3 months may be requested before the original term expires. A mandatory surrender ratio of 100% of the export invoice value, in the exact currency of the invoice, was historically observed for most goods; in effect today the exporter must exchange the proceeds through an authorized intermediary or demonstrate continued holding in a registered foreign-currency deposit account, with no unfettered retention offshore. For shipyard exports, the actual proceeds must match the customs declaration (Declaración de Exportación) and the supporting maritime vessel-purchase contract; any variance must be justified by the exporter to the DIAN and Banco de la República.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia) jointly with the Comité de Cambios Internacionales and the Fiscalía General de la Nación; the Registro de Inversores is operated by Banco de la República through the Sistema Integral de Gestión de Devoluciones y Movimientos (SIGDM) and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia supervises exchange intermediaries.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer do not hold Colombian banking licenses nor an in-country exchange-intermediary authorization from the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia; they therefore cannot legally execute the mandatory Colombian foreign-exchange channeling or provide local COP-denominated settlement for an SAS shipyard. Such EMIs may only be used as auxiliary invoicing or payment rails where no capital-account registration or export-surrender event is triggered; any attempt to use them as the primary corporate treasury vehicle would create unregistered offshore balances, triggering AML/CFT red flags and violations of Circular DCIN-83.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident beneficial ownership with no Colombian NIT or no local physical office for the SAS

Root cause: Non-resident beneficial ownership with no Colombian NIT or no local physical office for the SAS

Defense evidence: Notarized and apostilled certificate of good standing of the sole/ultimate parent; a complete organizational chart showing every individual holding more than 10% of shares; passport copies and residence certificates of all beneficial owners; audited consolidated financial statements not older than 12 months; a written statement of source of funds and source of wealth; power of attorney in Spanish, legalized by the nearest Colombian consulate or apostilled, appointing the Colombian legal representative; Colombian NIT registration certificate (Registro Único Tributario, RUT) of the SAS; commercial registry certificate from the Cámara de Comercio.
Commercial shipbuilding customer payment flows relying on offshore escrow or large pre-delivery installments

Root cause: Commercial shipbuilding customer payment flows relying on offshore escrow or large pre-delivery installments

Participation of politically exposed persons (PEPs) or heritage-oil buyers in the global container-vessel procurement chain

Defense evidence: Updated PEP declarations from all shareholders, directors, and ultimate beneficial owners; beneficial ownership registers of the customer, if available; enhanced due-diligence report prepared by a third-party KYC vendor; transaction monitoring plan; written sanctions screening; attestation that the transaction does not involve Colombian-Sanctioned or OFAC-sanctioned persons and that ESMA, BIS, or national licensing requirements are met.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection by non-resident shareholders must be effected through a wire transfer from an offshore bank in the denominated hard currency (usually USD) to an authorized Colombian commercial bank where the SAS holds its 'Capital Escrow Account'. The originator must include the precise remittance description: 'Foreign Direct Investment – Capital contribution to SOCIEDAD POR ACCIONES SIMPLIFICADA SAS / NIT 900.000.000-0 / ISIC 3011'; the receiving bank will generate the 'Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional' (the statutory purpose-code and classification form). This filing enters the investment into the Banco de la República registry within the permitted time frame, producing a registration certificate that is subsequently required for dividend remittance and the eventual repatriation of capital. All proof of remittance (SWIFT MT103, bank credit note, deed of capital increase registered at the Notaría and Cámara de Comercio) must be preserved. Exchange control verification includes matching the remittance to the audited accounting entry 'Aportes sociales / capital' and checking that the funds are not sourced from BC/FT and do not cross an OFAC-sanctioned correspondent. The omnibus account number and ABA/IBAN advices must be obtained before executing the transfer.Outbound disbursements for shipbuilding procurement and subcontracting will primarily clear via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 messages to the beneficiary banks, each citing a valid purchase order, pro-forma invoice, and a purpose description in Spanish consistent with exchange-control classification. For larger equipment purchases abroad (e.g., engines, plate steel, navigation systems), a Commercial Letter of Credit is opened on the Colombian bank’s side, using the MT700/MT707 series and subjecting the documents to UCP600; the shipyard’s purchased materials are settled against presentation of a full set of shipping documents (commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, insurance). The on-balance-sheet CKDs and raw-materials import must be accompanied by the DIAN import declaration and, if applicable, a customs-bond note; such payments are permitted through the authorized intermediary without needing the prior Banco de la República license as long as the import invoice and customs entry are filed in the SIGDM. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% (Corporation Income Tax, Impuesto sobre la Renta, for taxable years commencing on or after 1 January 2025; the 2024-2026 surtax of 10 percentage points on taxable income exceeding 120,000 UVT is not treated as headline for this shipyard scope unless the entity's annual taxable income surpasses the threshold).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% for dividends distributed to non-resident natural persons or foreign legal entities from previously taxed Colombian corporate profits (Law 2277 of 2022 increased the general rate from 5% to 10% for taxable years 2024 onwards; payments to non-resident shareholders holding a bilateral DTA may be reduced to 0% or 5% if the DTA provides for a participation exemption).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing and payment deadline is the first to ninth night according to the taxpayer's NIT last two digits, between the 1st and 9th calendar days of the ninth month after the fiscal year-end. For shipyards with maritime export turnover, a complementary ex-officio declaration may be required if the taxpayer fails to report taxable fringe benefits for crew and foreign vessel repair services.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Month-end closing date, due the 13th calendar day of the following month.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Informative transfer pricing return (Formulario 120-2) is mandatory when gross income for the taxable year exceeds 10,000 UVT (approx. COP 500 million in 2025) or assets exceed 6,000 UVT. Full Local File / TP Report (Informe de Precios de Transferencia) is required when gross income exceeds 45,000 UVT or net worth exceeds 30,000 UVT. Master File and Country-by-Country report apply only for multinational groups exceeding EUR 750 million consolidated revenue (COP equivalent defined by DIAN).

Economic substance scope: The Colombian Economic Substance Regime (Art. 57-1 of the Colombian Tax Code, as amended by Law 1430/2010) applies to S.A.S. entities with non-resident shareholders; the entity must prove premises, management, employees, functional identity and Colombian-source income; a shipyard S.A.S. must maintain a physical yard, fixed installations, hull assembly workforce, and invoicing records in Colombia. Failure to demonstrate economic substance leads to reclassification of the company as a shell colony and denial of foreign DTA benefits and possible deemed-dividend recognition.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Evasion Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion penalty: up to 200% of the evaded tax; civil surcharge of 100% of the omitted tax plus daily interest at the legal rate. Administrative fine up to 20,000 UVT.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling officers may be jointly liable for the unpaid tax if they were aware of the evasion and failed to correct; travel ban issuance by DIAN is possible; imprisonment under Art. 402 of Colombian Criminal Code up to 9 years for aggravated tax fraud, and separate asset restraining for funds misdirected into shell jurisdictions.
Transfer Pricing Malfeasance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increases the taxable income; penalty for late or non-submission of the TP Report: 1% of the transaction value for each month of delay up to 3% and no less than 1,500 UVT; possible 20% surcharge on the adjusted tax due if the taxpayer failed to keep supporting documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the surcharges if they signed the annual declaration certifying the absence of a TP adjustment; in severe causes, a director facing disclosure bears joint and several liability under Article 794 of the Commercial Code if he/she intervened in the concealment, and may be barred from re-entering Colombian territory pending fiscal criminal investigation.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DIAN can disregard the Colombian S.A.S. for tax purposes, re-attributing the profits to the foreign parent; penalty is a 100% surcharge on the diverted taxable base, and assessment of unlimited foreign interest for repatriation under the CFC-like provision.

Director personal liability: Directors like the named Colombian resident agent become jointly liable with the foreign parent up to the amount of diverted funds; directors may face travel bans until the fiscal debt is secured; if the substance deficiency was personal enrichment, they are subject to criminal confiscation proceedings under Colombian anti-money-laundering.
Money Laundering through Escrow/Letters of Credit

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under Law 599, Art. 323: imprisonment 10-15 years; financial penalties up to 1,200 minimum wages; the S.A.S. and legal representatives are subject to their own corporate penalty of a fine between 20,000 and 100,000 UVT and confiscation of the funds.

Director personal liability: Directors face immediate preventive suspension and prosecution; civil restitution to the illicit origin of the funds applies to all board members who authorised the channel; travel restriction is automatic until the seized amount is settled.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Payments

Statutory sanction: Withholding deficiency plus 100% surcharge; the Colombian withholding agent remains solely liable for the unpaid amount, including default interest from the due date. Daily interest 24%.

Director personal liability: The responsible director is jointly and severally liable for withheld sums if the entity insolvent; personal cash seizure is possible; if the omission was deliberate, imprisonment of up to 4-8 years applies as a withholding-agent crime under Art. 394 of Criminal Code.
Late Penalty / Persistent Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Automatic monthly 0.5% surcharge up to 20% of tax due; late filing penalty of 5%-120% of due tax. Accumulated fines may trigger DIAN forced-collection and asset attachment.

Director personal liability: Directorial responsibility for timeliness is statutory; if a director caused the non-filing, he/she may be reduced to a sanction of personal monetary fine of 100-200 UVT plus prohibition of leaving the country for up to two months.

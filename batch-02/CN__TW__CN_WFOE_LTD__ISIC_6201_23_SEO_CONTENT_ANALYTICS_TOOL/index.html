Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under the Companies Act, 2013 for an ordinary private limited company engaged in computer programming activities. However, the Office of the Registrar of Companies may require a reasonable authorised share capital, typically not exceeding Rs. 10,00,000, to be declared to cover expected operational expenses. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs does not mandate blocking of capital in an escrow account; the subscribed capital must be paid into the company's bank account and may be used for lawful business purposes.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for the ISIC 6201 software and analytics business. No manufacturing, chemical processing, or warehousing of industrial goods is carried out. The company remains subject to: (a) the local Shops and Establishment Act compliance, including fire safety compliance certificate issued by the local fire authority or a self-certification where permissible for commercial office premises; (b) the Information Technology (Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures and Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules, 2011 requiring reasonable security practices for processing of personal data; (c) display of a valid IT/ITES business authorization under the state industrial policy, if the company opts to avail of the software technology park or IT park incentive scheme; and (d) occupational health and safety rules applicable to office workers, including first-aid measures, safe electrical installations, and maximum working-hours notices under the relevant state labour laws. Since the company does not operate machinery, store controlled chemicals, or emit regulated wastewater from an industrial process, no environmental site clearance is required. Should the company later add a co-located server or hardware assembly activity, such activity would require an environmental registration under the State Pollution Control Board schedule and fire department certification depending on load capacity. No Civil Defense clearance is required for the office-only use case.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing and registration are performed through the China Electronic Tax Administration Portal (the national Electronic Tax Bureau electronic service system of the STA).

E-government filing portal

For CN_WFOE_LTD, a Chinese Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (limited liability) that is tax-resident in mainland China, the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is automatically printed on the business license issued by the local Market Regulation Administration during company incorporation. Tax registration is then completed online through the China Electronic Tax Administration Portal, where the USCC is recorded as the taxpayer identifier and the entity profile is created for VAT, Enterprise Income Tax, stamp duty, and payroll withholding. Because CN_WFOE_LTD is a resident enterprise, it is not required to obtain a non-resident TIN. If a foreign non-resident entity has no establishment in mainland China and derives Chinese-source income, it must separately apply for a Non-Resident Taxpayer Identification Number with the competent tax office under the non-resident tax registration procedure, submitting the foreign certificate of incorporation, articles of association, appointment of a tax representative, a written explanation of Chinese-source income, and the identity of the domestic withholding agent; no such NRTIN is required for the WFOE itself.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None (the PRC tax system does not provide check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections comparable to the United States or other jurisdictions). The WFOE's entity-level taxation is mandatory under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law. The only limited administrative elections that may apply are: (i) a small-scale VAT taxpayer may voluntarily elect to be registered as a VAT general taxpayer; and (ii) an eligible exporter of zero-rated cross-border services may file through the zero-rating and refund pathway rather than the exemption pathway; these are VAT compliance elections, not entity classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All Chinese enterprises are required to register for tax administration upon incorporation without any minimum turnover threshold. The statutory threshold for mandatory registration as a VAT general taxpayer is annual taxable sales exceeding CNY 5,000,000; a small-scale VAT taxpayer with monthly taxable sales not exceeding CNY 100,000 (quarterly not exceeding CNY 300,000) may remain within the current VAT exemption quota but must still maintain the USCC-based tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Caishui (2016) No. 36 and the cross-border zero-rating lists, a Chinese resident exporter such as CN_WFOE_LTD may claim 0% VAT on software services and information system services supplied to an overseas recipient when: (i) the recipient is a non-resident enterprise or individual located outside mainland China, including a Taiwan-based recipient that is not established or registered in mainland China; (ii) the SEO content analytics SaaS tool is completely and exclusively consumed outside mainland China, meaning no server-side user, domestic data subject, or local business activity in mainland China derives value from the service; (iii) the service is delivered remotely through internet/telecommunication channels with no physical performance in mainland China; and (iv) the export consideration is received in foreign exchange from abroad. Physical export zero-rating for goods is similarly available under the PRC export tax refund (exemption/credit) system when the goods are actually exported through a Chinese customs authority and the corresponding export declaration is filed.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration (the export declaration form accepted by the General Administration of Customs of China) - mandatory for any physical goods or embedded hardware component; not applicable to a pure SaaS supply
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - required only where physical goods are exported, not for digital subscription services
  • Commercial invoice issued in foreign currency with a zero-rated PRC VAT notation and reference to the cross-border zero-rating clause
  • Service contract or SaaS master subscription agreement with the overseas customer, including a 'fully consumed outside mainland China' service boundary clause
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt / Inward Foreign Exchange Income

CN_WFOE_LTD must issue invoices through the national Electronic Invoice Service Platform using its Tax Digital Account. The STA has mandated the Full-Digital Electronic Invoice (FDEI) for newly registered ordinary taxpayers; high-volume invoicing and ERP-integrated issuance require API connection through the STA's Leqi direct-connect platform, which transmits invoice-level data to the central tax information system in near real time. Each FDEI carries a unique invoice number, a digital tax signature, an anti-counterfeit certification code, and a dynamically controlled issuance quota, forming the anti-fraud digital stamping and central clearance mechanism. China does not utilize legacy physical fiscal memory devices.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is fully convertible for current-account items, including the WFOE's cross-border SaaS/SEO-analytics service exports and after-tax dividend remittances to the Taiwan shareholder, subject to authenticity review (contracts, invoices, tax-clearance certificates, audited financials). The capital account remains restricted: equity injection from Taiwan requires SAFE foreign-invested-enterprise (FIE) registration, a designated capital FX account, and negative-list business-scope compliance; after 'willing settlement' (意愿结汇) into the RMB settlement-pending account (结汇待支付账户), each disbursement must be traced to genuine operating expenditure. Outbound service payments ≥ USD 50,000 require the service-trade tax-filing certificate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No compulsory export-proceeds surrender applies; the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%. Service-export receipts (including intercompany fees arising from Stripe/Paddle settlements routed through the Taiwan parent) must be repatriated onshore and reported in the SAFE goods/services trade monitoring system. As a Class-A trade entity, the WFOE may retain 100% of proceeds in FX or voluntarily convert into RMB. Advance receipts more than 30 days before service performance and deferred receipts unpaid 90 days after performance must be registered in the monitoring system to avoid downgrade to Class B/C.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC, 中国人民银行) acting as central bank; the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE, 国家外汇管理局) acts as the delegated foreign-exchange regulator. SAFE provincial branches supervise designated foreign-exchange banks, which perform delegated transaction-level verification under the 'bank accountability' (银行展业) principle.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% of taxable income for a Taiwan profit-seeking enterprise. Taxable income not exceeding NT$120,000 is exempt; tax on income above that threshold is generally 20% of the excess.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 21% withholding tax on dividends paid by a Taiwan company to a nonresident shareholder, subject to applicable treaty relief and the recipient's status. The domestic rate and treatment should be confirmed for the payment date and recipient type.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a calendar-year taxpayer, file the annual return during May 1–May 31 following the tax year. Non-calendar-year taxpayers generally file within five months after the end of the fiscal year. Provisional tax is generally filed and paid during September. Extensions or special rules may apply in defined circumstances.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan's transfer-pricing rules require related-party transaction disclosures and documentation under specified conditions. A Local File is generally required where the applicable transaction-value thresholds are met; commonly relevant thresholds include aggregate related-party transaction amounts of NT$1 billion, subject to transaction-category rules and exemptions. A Master File is generally required for a Taiwan constituent entity of a multinational enterprise group meeting the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold, commonly NT$30 billion. Country-by-Country Reporting generally applies at the applicable group-revenue threshold, commonly NT$27 billion. Confirm current regulations, exclusions, currency conversion, and threshold calculations for the tax year.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not generally apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Taiwan tax authorities may nevertheless examine beneficial ownership, business purpose, actual functions, personnel, decision-making, risk control, permanent-establishment exposure, and whether related-party arrangements reflect economic reality. Anti-avoidance, transfer-pricing, controlled-foreign-company, and treaty anti-abuse rules may apply where their statutory conditions are met.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Entity and jurisdiction mismatch

Statutory sanction: Potential incorrect filings, tax assessments, interest, penalties, registration violations, and exposure to taxation in both jurisdictions. Consequences depend on the actual facts and applicable Taiwan and mainland China law.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face statutory civil, administrative, or criminal exposure for deliberate false declarations, tax evasion, or failure to comply with personal legal duties. No automatic veil piercing, travel ban, or imprisonment should be presumed.
Tax evasion and false returns

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, recovery of underpaid tax, interest, statutory fines, and potential criminal prosecution under applicable Taiwan tax and criminal laws. The penalty depends on intent, amount, conduct, and the governing provision.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face personal criminal or civil consequences where the statutory elements are established. Limited liability does not immunize an individual from liability for personal participation in an offense.
Transfer-pricing adjustment and inadequate documentation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustments, additional income tax, interest, and applicable penalties for underreporting or documentation and filing failures.

Director personal liability: Personal liability is not automatic. Exposure may arise from knowing participation in false records, fraudulent conduct, or other independently actionable violations.
Improper treaty claim or withholding failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of tax not withheld or remitted, interest, statutory penalties, and possible payer liability under Taiwan withholding rules.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers may be exposed where they knowingly authorize unlawful non-withholding, false certificates, or fraudulent treaty claims; personal sanctions require statutory grounds.
Permanent establishment and source-income exposure

Statutory sanction: Unfiled tax returns, tax assessment, interest, penalties, and possible registration or withholding consequences, subject to domestic law and applicable treaty provisions.

Director personal liability: Personal liability is not automatic; it may arise for knowing tax evasion, deliberate noncompliance, or breach of a separate statutory duty.
Withholding tax, payroll, and indirect-tax noncompliance

Statutory sanction: Tax, interest, fines, and other administrative or criminal consequences as applicable. SaaS supply and digital-service VAT treatment requires separate analysis from corporate income tax.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may be personally exposed where Taiwan law imposes liability for withholding, remittance, false reporting, or intentional evasion.
Money laundering, sanctions, and illicit payment conduct

Statutory sanction: Potential asset restraint or confiscation, administrative penalties, and criminal prosecution under applicable law. Applicable obligations depend on the entity's activities, counterparties, and payment channels.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly participate, direct, or facilitate unlawful conduct may face personal criminal and civil consequences. Travel restrictions or imprisonment require a legal basis and competent-authority action.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taxation Administration (Taiwan) and local National Taxation Bureaus; online portal: Tax.Net (https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw)

E-government filing portal

A foreign enterprise establishing an entity or branch must first complete incorporation or branch registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA). After MOEA approval, the entity must apply to the local National Taxation Bureau for profit-seeking enterprise tax registration and obtain a Uniform Business Number (UBN) within 15 days. The UBN also functions as the VAT registration number; the business must register for the Business Tax before commencing taxable sales. Applications require standard forms, foreign-language certification, and apostilled corporate documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Taiwan law; entity classification follows the legal form of incorporation (limited company). Hybrid mismatch provisions apply automatically under Income Tax Act Articles 43-2 to 43-4, with no elective treatment. Advance tax rulings may be sought for ambiguous cross-border arrangements, but no elective pass-through status is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold for registration; all business entities must register before commencing business. Monthly turnover below NT$200,000 qualifies for the simplified small-business rate of 1% in lieu of the standard 5% VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Sales of goods that are exported from Taiwan through customs clearance, and services provided to foreign enterprises for use outside Taiwan, are eligible for zero-rating under the Business Tax Act. Administrative services for an offshore ESOP trust qualify when the service recipient is a foreign entity, the services are performed for and used abroad, and consideration is receivable in foreign currency. Complete supporting documents must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口報單)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate

General VAT businesses are required to issue Electronic Uniform Invoices (EUI) and transmit invoice data to the Integrated E-Invoice Service Center through the Turnkey API or E-Invoice platform. B2C invoices must be transmitted within 24 hours; B2B invoices within 7 days. Cloud invoices display QR codes; no physical fiscal stamp is required. Late or missing transmission may trigger penalties.

Export VAT zero-rating claims are subject to risk-based audits by the National Taxation Bureau. Fraudulent claims or insufficient documentation may result in penalties under the Tax Evasion Penalty Act of 1.5 to 5 times the evaded tax, plus interest, retrospective clawback of refunds, and criminal liability for willful fraudulent invoicing. Cross-border exchange of information may be triggered.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China renminbi is generally convertible for genuine current-account transactions, including ordinary trade, service payments, payroll-related payments and properly documented profit remittances, subject to bank review, authenticity checks, tax documentation and anti-money-laundering controls. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, equity transfers, capital reductions and certain intercompany financing, remain registration- and quota-sensitive and may require SAFE or bank processing under applicable rules. A WFOE normally remits dividends only after board or shareholder approval, completion of the relevant accounting and tax formalities, annual financial statements, loss offsetting, statutory reserve requirements and evidence of applicable withholding tax payment or treaty treatment. Taiwan applies a separate foreign-exchange framework under which ordinary current-account payments are generally permitted, while capital movements and certain foreign-investment, securities and remittance activities may require reporting, approval, documentary support or bank reporting. The entity's constitutional jurisdiction, place of incorporation, bank account location and transaction purpose must be reconciled before onboarding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mainland China does not generally impose a universal statutory requirement that all export proceeds be converted into renminbi at a fixed ratio or within a single nationwide mandatory surrender period. Export proceeds must be collected through compliant bank channels, matched to genuine underlying trade, and reported or verified through applicable customs, tax and foreign-exchange systems. Banks may require export contracts, customs declarations, invoices, bills of lading, collection records and transaction explanations before crediting or settling funds. Enterprises may generally retain eligible foreign-currency proceeds in permitted foreign-currency accounts, subject to account rules and bank controls; conversion is performed when commercially required or when a regulatory program imposes a specific requirement. The applicable Taiwan regime is separate and may impose reporting, documentary and timing requirements for foreign-exchange receipts, remittances and export-related transactions, but no mainland China rule should be assumed to apply to a Taiwan account. The treasury policy must therefore specify the legal entity, account jurisdiction, export location, customs declarant and governing FX regime for every receipt.

Central bank supervision: For a mainland China WFOE, the principal authorities are the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), together with the relevant provincial or municipal SAFE branch and the appointed foreign-exchange bank. The payload identifies Taiwan (TW) as the jurisdiction; if the entity is legally established or banked in Taiwan rather than mainland China, the applicable authority is the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan), with the Financial Supervisory Commission and the Ministry of Economic Affairs also relevant. Mainland China and Taiwan are separate regulatory regimes, and a single entity must not apply both regimes to the same account or transaction without a documented legal basis.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to Low for a mainland China WFOE. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment institution cannot replace a mainland licensed bank for statutory capital accounts, RMB basic settlement accounts, regulated foreign-exchange settlement, payroll execution or foreign direct investment registration. Offshore EMI accounts may be used, subject to the provider's licence and risk policy, for limited commercial collections or payments where the transaction is lawful, the counterparty and purpose are transparent, and funds are not improperly retained offshore or routed around SAFE, tax, customs, sanctions, payment-service, data or AML controls. Use of third-party wallets, personal accounts, unlicensed virtual-asset channels, payment aggregation without the required licence, or undisclosed nominee collection structures should be prohibited. Any Taiwan-based payment account must be separately assessed under Taiwan payment, FX, AML and cross-border data rules.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a PRC resident enterprise to a Taiwan resident enterprise under Articles 3, 27 and 37 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, without treaty benefit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory when annual related-party transactions in any single category reach: tangible asset transfers RMB 200 million; intangible asset transfers or use RMB 100 million; financial asset transactions RMB 100 million; service transactions RMB 40 million. Master File is mandatory when total annual related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Neither the PRC nor Taiwan applies a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to offshore financial centres. However, China applies substance-over-form and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47, supported by SAT Announcements 2016 No. 42 and 2017 No. 6, requiring actual personnel, business premises, decision-making, risk assumption, and commercial functions. Taiwan applies substance-over-form and general anti-avoidance principles under the Income Tax Act; treaty benefits are denied to shell or conduit arrangements. An ESOP trust WFOE must hold real governance and operational substance in China before claiming China residency, transfer-pricing single-factor returns, or reduced cross-strait WHT rates.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: recovery of evaded tax plus daily 0.05% surcharge and an administrative penalty of 50%–500% of the evaded tax. Criminal under Criminal Law Article 201 where evaded tax exceeds RMB 100,000 and is more than 10% of taxable amount: imprisonment up to 3 years and fine; where amount is huge, imprisonment of 3–7 years with fine.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives, actual controllers, and financial officers may face criminal prosecution, travel bans, and exit-tax restrictions; courts may order civil restitution and pierce the veil where funds were diverted to shareholder or director personal accounts.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's Length Violation

Statutory sanction: The Chinese tax authority may make an arm's length adjustment to taxable income, levy 5% imputed interest and daily late surcharge on the adjusted tax, and impose fines for failure to maintain accounting/tax records under TCAL Article 62. Serious intentional misstatements may be reclassified as tax evasion, attracting 50%–500% penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders can be personally liable where adjusted profits have been distributed, business substance is absent, or the affected WFOE is insolvent; travel restrictions and criminal liability can attach when false or fabricated TP documentation is used.
Failure to Withhold Dividend WHT / Improper Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Under TCAL Article 69, a withholding agent that fails to withhold and remit is ordered to pay the uncollected tax and may be fined 50%–300% of the unpaid tax. Late payment surcharge and SAFE foreign-exchange penalties also apply; illegal cross-strait remittances may be criminally prosecuted.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives or responsible officers are exposed to joint and several civil liability to restore the unremitted tax; travel bans and exit-tax blocks may be imposed; criminal imprisonment can arise for intentional misappropriation of withheld tax or illegal foreign exchange operations.
Shell Company / Abuse of Tax Residency and Treaty Benefits

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may invoke GAAR to disregard the WFOE, recharacterize transactions, deny treaty benefits, and recompute taxable income; penalties of 50%–500% and daily surcharges apply; Taiwan authorities may under the cross-strait agreement disallow tax benefits and exchange information leading to dual enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders of the conduit can be held jointly and severally for adjusted tax, penalties, and civil restitution; both Chinese and Taiwan tax authorities may pursue individual assets, and exit/travel bans may be imposed during audit and collection proceedings.

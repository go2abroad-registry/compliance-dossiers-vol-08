Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is fully convertible for current-account items, including trade, services, and dividend remittance, subject to authenticity verification by designated banks under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations (2008 Order of the State Council). The capital account remains managed: FDI equity injections require a SAFE FDI Base Registration and conversion to RMB is permitted only on a payment-based settlement basis with a statutory negative list; external debt requires a SAFE Foreign-Debt Registration and cross-border guarantees must be reported. Profit remittance hurdles are substantive: dividends may be paid only from after-CIT retained earnings supported by the annual statutory audit report, a unanimous shareholder resolution, a tax-clearance certificate confirming payment of the 10% dividend withholding tax (or the reduced treaty rate under the Cross-Strait Double Taxation Arrangement, where the Limitation-on-Benefits conditions are met), and the bank's verification of the SAFE non-trade payment purpose code for return of investment. The remitting bank will also complete a money-laundering risk assessment of the beneficial owner and may require a Taiwan-side Investment Commission approval record for the original inbound investment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished the export-collection verification and surrender system in 2012; there is currently no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the central bank. Exporters may retain foreign currency in a domestic foreign-currency account or use the voluntary settlement facility. Receipts must be repatriated into China within the contractual credit term; for goods trade, any receivable beyond 90 days from customs clearance must be reported as a deferred receipt through the Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System within 30 days of the customs declaration. A-class enterprises enjoy streamlined bank processing, but B/C-class enterprises are subject to restricted payment terms and enhanced document checks. Material mismatches in excess of 15% between the declared invoice value and customs/export data require a written explanation, a revised declaration, and supporting contracts.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) is the supreme monetary and payment-system authority; the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) is the designated foreign-exchange regulator and clearing-rail supervisor. PBOC licenses and supervises non-bank payment institutions, including electronic-money operators, under the Regulations on the Supervision and Administration of Non-bank Payment Institutions (State Council Order No. 768, effective 1 May 2024). SAFE administers the onshore FX account and reporting systems, the CNAPS clearing architecture (HVPS/BEPS), and the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) for RMB international settlement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Legal constraints: (1) An offshore foreign EMI license does not confer any right to open virtual IBANs or hold digital-wallet reserves for Chinese residents; any payment activity in China requires a PBOC Payment Business License under State Council Order No. 768, and the WFOE must satisfy the minimum paid-in registered capital requirement (RMB 100 million for nationwide payment services, or the provincial-tier amount determined by PBOC). (2) SEPA_Credit is not directly reachable from within China; the WFOE must clear EUR payments through a correspondent bank or a Chinese bank's European branch, and any client-facing virtual IBAN product is permissible only as a bank-issued sub-account arrangement, not as a standalone EMI rail. (3) The Personal Information Protection Law and PBOC fintech data rules require a security assessment and local data localization before transferring client transaction data cross-border; offshore data processing by the Taiwanese parent is a material compliance risk. (4) Client funds must be segregated in a dedicated custody/reserve account with a qualified Chinese commercial bank; no offshore third-party holding of Chinese client float is permitted.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing sequence is: (1) complete the TW-side Investment Commission approval under the Taiwan-Mainland relations statute, and the PRC-side MOFCOM foreign-investment information filing for the Taiwanese investor; (2) obtain the SAFE FDI Base Registration certificate for the WFOE; (3) open a Foreign-Currency Capital Account and a RMB Basic Deposit Account at the designated tier-1 bank; (4) receive the inward remittance via SWIFT MT103 using the SAFE payment purpose code for FDI paid-in capital, with field 70 specifying the WFOE name, the SAFE FDI registration code, and the reference '/INV/CN_WFOE_LTD'; (5) the receiving bank reports the credit in the SAFE Foreign Exchange Account System within T+1; and (6) each conversion from the capital account to RMB is executed on a payment-based settlement basis, requiring the bank to verify underlying transaction documents against the negative list, which prohibits use for domestic equity investment not within business scope, securities, real estate beyond self-use, and entrusted loans. The entire flow requires the receiving bank to retain the original capital verification report and the board capital-contribution resolution for the account file.For outbound settlement, the WFOE may use: (1) SWIFT MT103/202 for direct USD, EUR, or HKD disbursements from the foreign-currency operational account for goods and services, with the bank retaining invoice, contract, and customs/service proof; (2) an irrevocable at-sight Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Chinese commercial bank under UCP600 for high-value goods transactions, including document checking against the bill of lading and commercial invoice; (3) CIPS for RMB-denominated outbound payments, including those ultimately reaching a SEPA beneficiary via the Chinese bank's European branch or a euro-correspondent bank holding an IBAN; and (4) SAFE-converted RMB from the capital account, subject to the same negative-list verification. Each outbound payment must carry the appropriate SAFE payment purpose code and the bank must file the transaction in the SAFE monitoring system; timely but not formulaic documentation is required for annual inspection and anti-avoidance review.Customer FX Forward contracts (settlement-date matched to a verified underlying trade or operating cash-flow), executed under the real-demand principle with a SAFE-approved designated bank.FX Swaps between CNY and USD/EUR for short-term liquidity rollover, with SAFE position and maturity reporting.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps to hedge cash-flow mismatch on a SAFE-registered external debt facility, with the underlying loan and the swap registered and documented.RMB FX Options (purchase of call or put options paid in CNY) with the premium and strike aligned to a genuine exposure; sale or combination strategies are permitted only as covered hedging of an established position.

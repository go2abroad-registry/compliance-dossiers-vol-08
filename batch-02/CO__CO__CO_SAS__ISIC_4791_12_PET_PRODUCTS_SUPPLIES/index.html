Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The Colombian S.A.S. is a tax-resident juridical person (domiciled by incorporation or place of effective management, E.T. Art. 12-1) subject to corporate income tax at 35% (Ley 2277/2022). No pass-through eligibility for domestic or foreign shareholders; profits distributed to non-resident shareholders attract withholding tax (10% standard, 20% for privileged jurisdictions) on dividend remittances; branch profits and other Colombian-source income are similarly taxed at entity level. Territorial scope applies only to non-resident recipients; the S.A.S. is on worldwide-income taxation for Colombian-source and foreign-source income.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Colombian law; entity classification follows corporate form. Available mechanisms: (1) Optional Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) combining VAT and income tax for small taxpayers with gross ordinary revenue below 100,000 UVT (≈ COP 4,762,000,000 for 2025) — election made in RUT; (2) mandatory transparency rules disregard foreign transparent entities/partnerships for income attribution to Colombian residents; (3) CFC rules (Ley 1819/2016 and subsequent amendments) attribute passive income of controlled foreign entities; (4) Hybrid mismatch rules introduced by Ley 2277/2022 (deduction-without-inclusion, double-deduction, and imported-mismatch anti-abuse provisions aligned with BEPS Action 2); (5) optional accelerated VAT refund filing for exporters representing >30% export revenue; (6) transfer pricing contemporaneous documentation elections under E.T. Arts. 260-1 et seq.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis monetary threshold for goods retailers: any habitual sale of taxable goods situated in Colombia, delivery to Colombian consumers, or importation triggers mandatory IVA registration and NIT issuance (E.T. Art. 437). Occasional/non-habitual sellers of exempt goods only may be outside the obligation. For reference, the optional SIMPLE consolidated regime is capped at gross revenue of 100,000 UVT (COP 4,762,000,000 for 2025); e-commerce operators clearly exceed the 3,500 UVT small-provider exemption and must register regardless of volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: physical export is permanently excluded (0% IVA) under E.T. Art. 481(1), provided the seller is the exporter, goods exit Colombian customs territory within the statutory shipment window, and a validated DEX (Declaración de Exportación) is filed. Services: zero-rating requires the 'exported services' test under Decree 1625/2016 Art. 1.6.1.13.20 – recipient is a non-resident without Colombian domicile, the service is used exclusively abroad (including offshore e-retailing support, marketplace, SaaS, and logistics services rendered to foreign merchants), the remuneration is paid from abroad in foreign currency, and the service does not generate Colombian-source income to the recipient. E-commerce sale of pet supplies to foreign end-consumers qualifies as goods export when title/risk passes abroad and goods leave Colombia; purely digital services to foreign consumers qualify under the service-export rule. Refund mechanism: exporters recover input VAT through monthly or bimonthly 'saldo a favor' refund procedures, requiring DIAN-sealed export documentation and a clean audit file; export revenue share above 30% allows accelerated monthly filing.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX) endorsed with Autorización de Embarque through DIAN's SICEX/VUCE computerized customs system
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill showing exit from Colombian customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Canalización de Divisas) issued by the local authorized FX intermediary
  • Foreign Exchange Declaration (Declaración de Cambio – Form 7 for goods / Form 8 for services) filed with Banco de la República via the intermediary
  • Commercial invoice (Spanish/English) with HS 3924-3926/3407 or applicable pet-supply classification and incoterm
  • Service-export substantiation when relevant: contract, proof of non-resident recipient and exclusive foreign use, and audited export-of-services certificate (certificación expedida por contador público or revisor fiscal)
  • Packing list and cargo manifest consistent with the DEX values and weights

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta – FEV) for all IVA-responsible taxpayers since 2021. FEVs must be generated through DIAN-authorized technology providers (Proveedores Tecnológicos) or DIAN's free tool, transmitted as XML over web services to DIAN's VALIDATION service, which performs real-time schema/legal validation and returns a CUS E (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) for each invoice before it may be legally issued. Each invoice embeds a QR code with verification URL, the issuer's NIT, and the CUS E. Offline contingency is limited to 48 hours for systems failures. Additionally, the e-commerce platform must issue the 'documento soporte en adquisiciones' for purchases and comply with the periodic transmission of operative information (exógena) and the 'Radicación de facturas' portal obligations.

DIAN has a 5-year assessment window and may reclassify any zero-rated operation if the export record is incomplete, the goods did not effectively leave the country, or proceeds were not repatriated within 180 days. Clawback consequences: (1) reversal of the 0% treatment with assessment of 19% IVA on the transaction value, (2) moratory default interest on the retroactive deficiency, (3) inaccuracy penalty (sanción por inexactitud) up to 200% of the omitted VAT under E.T. Art. 647, and (4) rejection of VAT refunds with equivalent penalties plus restitution of refunded amounts. False or simulated export documents trigger criminal liability: 'defraudación fiscal' carries 36–108 months imprisonment plus fines of 150% of the evaded tax (Ley 2277/2022); aggravated forms using fictitious operations or false invoices attract higher imprisonment; customs smuggling offenses under Ley 1762/2015 apply to merchandise leaving undeclared or with falsified export declarations; FX non-repatriation violations separately expose the exporter to foreign-exchange fines up to 200% of the operation value and mandatory reintegration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a free-floating COP with broad current-account convertibility for cross-border e-commerce trade. The capital account is liberalized but administratively monitored: every resident cross-border FX transaction must be channelled through an Authorized Intermediary (IMC) of the Mercado Cambiario, and the IMC must file an electronic Declaración de Cambio with Banco de la República. Profit and dividend remittances require no ex-ante central-bank authorization; the paying S.A.S. must evidence distributable profits under local GAAP, compliance with non-resident dividend withholding and income-tax rules, and the corresponding exchange declaration. The practical remittance hurdles are therefore documentary and tax-completeness-based rather than exchange-approval-based.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, export proceeds must be collected and 'canalizado' through an IMC within six (6) months from the date of export, with a one-time extension of up to six (6) additional months available upon justified request accepted by Banco de la República. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%: there is no statutory surrender of export receipts into pesos. Nevertheless, the full export value must be channelled through the Mercado Cambiario via an IMC or into a Banco de la República-authorized compensation account abroad, unless a de-minimis exemption applies to small-value retail e-commerce export receipts (threshold around the USD 10,000 equivalent per operation, subject to IMC confirmation). Proceeds collected through Stripe/PayPal may be parked in the authorized compensation account with quarterly balance reporting, or credited directly to an IMC foreign-currency account, and each collection event is disclosed in the applicable foreign-exchange declaration filed by the IMC.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) is the sole FX-regime authority, governing the 'Régimen de Cambios Internacionales' under Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, Decree 2555 of 2010 and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) supervising Authorized Intermediaries (IMCs) and DIAN enforcing customs and anti-money-laundering rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Viability rating: MEDIUM as a front-end collection gateway, LOW as an end-destination treasury account. Foreign EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, dLocal) are not Colombian IMCs and cannot perform the channeling and exchange-declaration duties imposed by DCIN-83. The compliant architecture is to allow Stripe and PayPal to collect card payments abroad and settle in USD into the S.A.S.'s Banco de la República-authorized compensation account or directly into an IMC account; funds are then declared and, if conversion is required, converted onshore at the free-market rate. Holding end-destination USD balances at a non-IMC EMI, without a compensation-account authorization, is a prima facie breach of the channeling regime and creates substantial KYC/AML adverse findings at Colombian banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders inject capital via SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the S.A.S.'s account at a Colombian IMC. The receiving IMC classifies the inflow under the Banco de la República concept code for direct capital investment in Colombia (code family 2101, inward FDI), files the applicable Declaración de Cambio, and credits funds to a COP or USD account after OFAC, UN-sanctions and UBO screening. If the investor seeks formal legal recognition of the investment for future dividend and capital repatriation, the IMC records it in the Registro de Inversiones Internacionales of Banco de la República. No prior central-bank authorization is required; same-day settlement is available at the free-market COP/USD rate if conversion is elected, with the IMC charging standard transaction and exchange-declaration fees.Supplier payments for imports of pet products are cleared through the IMC's corporate digital channel via SWIFT MT103 for open-account trade, or via irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit under UCP 600 for large or strategically critical replenishment orders. Every outward remittance triggers an exchange declaration under the import-of-goods concept code supported by the import invoice, purchase order, transport document and the DIAN import declaration (processed through SIISEX/Siglo XXI). Payments for services or royalties to non-residents require withholding taxes to be applied and filed, together with transfer-pricing documentation where related parties are involved. Onshore COP disbursements for logistics, customs duties and domestic suppliers settle through the CUD clearing system, with the GMF (0.4%) financial-movements tax applicable unless an exemption is available.COP/USD deliverable FX forwards (contratos a término) entered into with an authorized IMC solely to hedge the commercial export/import exposure.COP/USD non-deliverable cash-settled forwards executed onshore through an IMC, with cash settlement in COP, permissible for hedging the e-commerce revenue cycle.FX swaps and spot/forward combinations with authorized counterparties in the interbank Mercado Cambiario.Purchased plain-vanilla COP/USD OTC options through an IMC, hedging only, with no short option positions permitted for non-financial corporates.Cross-currency swaps (USD/COP) supporting long-term financing or investment, subject to IMC intermediation, Banco de la República reporting and evidence of an underlying commercial, trade or financing exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability rating: MEDIUM as a front-end collection gateway, LOW as an end-destination treasury account. Foreign EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, dLocal) are not Colombian IMCs and cannot perform the channeling and exchange-declaration duties imposed by DCIN-83. The compliant architecture is to allow Stripe and PayPal to collect card payments abroad and settle in USD into the S.A.S.'s Banco de la República-authorized compensation account or directly into an IMC account; funds are then declared and, if conversion is required, converted onshore at the free-market rate. Holding end-destination USD balances at a non-IMC EMI, without a compensation-account authorization, is a prima facie breach of the channeling regime and creates substantial KYC/AML adverse findings at Colombian banks.

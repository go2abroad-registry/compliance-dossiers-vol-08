Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia has no IRS-style check-the-box regime; an S.A.S. is always a separate taxpayer subject to Colombian corporate income tax at the entity level, with no statutory pass-through election available. The principal available elections relevant to an S.A.S. exporter are: (i) voluntary adherence to the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) under Articles 908–922 of the Estatuto Tributario, subject to rigorous turnover caps (gross income below 100,000 UVT at entry, with phase-outs), activity restrictions, and prohibition for sub-holdings or entities that are part of groups exceeding the cap; (ii) election by DIAN for 'Gran Contribuyente' status based on asset/turnover thresholds, which centralizes tax administration and accelerates refund processing; (iii) for cross-border financing structures, election of the tax-treatment characterization of instruments under the general anti-abuse and hybrid mismatch neutralization rules of Law 2010 of 2019 (BEPS Action 2), but no entity-classification election is available at the S.A.S. level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

COP 0 for legal entities: every capital company (including an S.A.S.) performing taxable acts on Colombian territory or importing goods is mandatorily registered as an IVA 'responsable' in the RUT from the first taxable operation, irrespective of turnover. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 481 E.T., exports of tangible goods are zero-rated (0% IVA), and the export sale is situated outside the taxable VAT base in Colombia. The exporter must: (i) be an IVA-responsible taxpayer; (ii) effect a definitive export of goods from Colombian customs territory, evidenced by a valid DEX; (iii) issue an electronic export invoice containing CUFE and export-specific fields; (iv) prove the physical exit of goods through transport documents and customs validation; and (v) repatriate the proceeds through an intermediary of the foreign exchange market (IMC) within the statutory term. Export-related services rendered exclusively to non-residents (e.g., logistics, loading, and technical services subject to the 'servicios vinculados a operaciones de exportación' conditions of Article 481) may also qualify for zero-rating. The exporter may simultaneously claim the refund (devolución) of IVA paid on raw materials and services physically incorporated into or used in the production of the exported goods.

  • Export Customs Declaration (DEX – Declaración de Exportación) accepted, validated, and definitively shipped by DIAN customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (transport document evidencing physical departure from Colombian customs territory)
  • Commercial Invoice (matching the DEX in value, product, and Exporter registry data)
  • Packing List (vendor's packing list detailing contents, HS codes, weights, and units)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate – 'Certificado del reintegro de divisas' issued by the authorized Colombian bank/intermediary (IMC) proving the foreign currency inflow and FX-market channeling within the statutory deadline

Mandatory electronic invoicing (Facturación Electrónica de Venta – FE) for all IVA-responsible taxpayers. Invoices must be generated in XML, digitally signed with the DIAN-issued certificate, and transmitted to DIAN via the MUISCA/DSI platform; DIAN performs near-real-time validation and the invoice is legally valid only upon its approval, requiring the unique code CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) for sales invoices or CUDS (Código Único de Documento Soporte) for export-supporting documents and special transactions. QR code generation and embedded CUFE hash are compulsory, and export invoices must reconcile with the corresponding Export Customs Declaration (DEX). Daily batch reporting of electronic invoice events (rechazo, aceptación, recibo) is mandatory, as is the electronic transmission of all export-related data areas within DIAN's numeration and validation framework.

DIAN and external auditors apply strict substance review to export zero-rating and VAT refund claims. If the export is not fully evidenced or the goods never legally left Colombia, DIAN will: (i) assess the unpaid 19% IVA on the sale plus daily moratory interest measured from the date the refund was granted; (ii) impose the sanción por inexactitud under Article 647 E.T., up to 200% of the greater of the understated VAT or overclaimed refund; (iii) order retrospective clawback of all VAT refunds paid on inputs of the non-exported goods; (iv) in cases of organized evasion, apply the criminal offense of defraudación a la DIAN under Article 402 of the Código Penal, carrying imprisonment terms of up to 9 years, fines equivalent to up to 200% of the evaded sum, and disqualification from contracting with the State; and (v) exclude the exporter from the Registro de Exportadores and suspend future refund filings for two or more taxable periods. To mitigate audit risk, the exporter must retain the complete documentary chain (DEX, transport title, IMC repatriation certificate, commercial contracts) for at least five years, in accordance with Article 632 E.T. document-retention rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed, market-based convertibility regime for the Colombian peso (COP): current-account transactions (goods, services, royalties, and dividend remittances) are freely convertible once channeled through an authorized Intermediario del Mercado Cambiario (IMC); capital-account operations (foreign direct investment, external credits, and capital repatriation) are likewise permitted but are conditioned on the registration of the investment and the filing of the corresponding Declaración de Cambio. For the S.A.S. engaged in ISIC 1079 self-heating food manufacturing, profit remittance and capital repatriation hurdles are documentary rather than licensing-based: registration of the foreign investment within six months of the inflow, the mandatory channeling threshold of US$10,000 per transaction, the requirement to maintain a Colombian tax identification (NIT) and registered legal representation, and the obligation to preserve exchange declarations for audit by the DIAN. No prior Central Bank approval is required for lawful commercial transfers, and profit remittance is legally enforceable once the investment registration is perfected.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83, export proceeds derived from the sale of goods (Formulario No. 2 – Declaración de Cambio por Exportaciones de Bienes) must be channeled through an authorized IMC within six (6) months from the due date of the export credit (fecha de vencimiento del crédito de la exportación), supported by the customs export declaration (DEX). There is no fixed statutory percentage requiring compulsory conversion of export receipts into COP; however, 100% of the commercial value of the exported goods, net of permitted deductions and financing costs, must be repatriated and channeled to Colombia. The resulting FX balance must be maintained either in the exporter's foreign-currency account (Cuenta en Moneda Extranjera – CME) held at an IMC or converted and settled in COP within the same channeling window. Export operations below US$10,000 per transaction are exempt from mandatory channeling but remain subject to documentary preservation and to the IMC's anti-fragmentation aggregation rights for related transactions.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), operating under the Colombian Foreign Exchange Regime (Régimen Cambiario) established by Decree 1068 of 2015, Resolución Externa No. 1 of 2018, and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83; as from Decree 1193 of 2023, the DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) exercises foreign exchange supervision and enforcement functions, while the Banco de la República retains authority over monetary policy, international reserves, the payment system, and the exchange market infrastructure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for core regulatory purposes and Medium only as an auxiliary rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Mercury, etc.) are not authorized Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario and cannot lawfully receive or settle Colombian export proceeds, import payments, or FDI declarations that are subject to mandatory channeling. Use of an offshore EMI to bypass the IMC creates an unmet canalización obligation and exposes the S.A.S. to DIAN and Banco de la República enforcement actions. Offshore EMIs may be used only for incidental operating expenses below the US$10,000 channeling threshold, e-commerce collections, corporate cards, and intra-group expense clearing, provided the local COP treasury remains consolidated at an authorized IMC and no export or investment declaration dependency is created.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity contributions and shareholder loans must be wired from abroad via SWIFT MT103 to the S.A.S. operating account held at an authorized Colombian IMC. The incoming message must identify the S.A.S. tax identification (NIT) in field 59, and the remitter should insert in field 72 the purpose reference 'FDI – investment registration pending' or 'external credit' as applicable. The receiving IMC records the operation through the Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales (for equity) or the Formulario No. 4 Declaración de Cambio por Operaciones de Endeudamiento Externo (for shareholder loans) within the regulatory five-business-day declaration window. The foreign investment registration must be perfected within six months of receipt of the funds through the IMC's foreign investment registration system, thereby securing the legal right to future dividend remittances, capital repatriation, and access to the official exchange market for the corresponding COP conversions. Funds must never be routed through unlicensed EMIs or personal accounts, as any such diversion extinguishes the ability to present the lawful channeling declaration.Trade settlements for imported raw materials, self-heating componentry, and packaging machinery are cleared via SWIFT MT103 outward payments using the import channeling declaration (Formulario No. 1) filed through the IMC, supported by the underlying commercial invoice and customs declaration. For larger or secured transactions, irrevocable documentary commercial Letters of Credit (LC) are issued by the Colombian IMC under the MT700 SWIFT chain, with at-sight or deferred payment clauses, partial shipment tolerances, and presentation instructions aligned with the UCP 600. Dividend and profit remittances are routed via MT103 as investment-return channeling operations and are conditioned on the perfected registration of the underlying FDI, the payment of the S.A.S. corporate income tax, and the application of the applicable Colombian dividend withholding tax. All outgoing MT103 messages must contain the beneficiary tax identifiers and the local foreign-exchange purpose codes, and the supporting exchange declarations must be preserved in the treasury compliance ledger for a minimum of five years.COP/USD deliverable FX forward contracts (forwards de tasa de cambio) contracted through an authorized IMC for hedging commercial export receivables and import payablesCOP/USD non-deliverable forward contracts (NDFs/NDOs) referenced to the official TRM, permissible with local and eligible offshore counterparties for synthetic hedging where physical COP delivery is impracticalCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) between COP and USD for matching long-term shareholder loans, export receivables, and capital expenditure liabilitiesEuropean-style over-the-counter FX options on COP/USD acquired from authorized IMCs for corridor protection and event-risk management

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for core regulatory purposes and Medium only as an auxiliary rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Mercury, etc.) are not authorized Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario and cannot lawfully receive or settle Colombian export proceeds, import payments, or FDI declarations that are subject to mandatory channeling. Use of an offshore EMI to bypass the IMC creates an unmet canalización obligation and exposes the S.A.S. to DIAN and Banco de la República enforcement actions. Offshore EMIs may be used only for incidental operating expenses below the US$10,000 channeling threshold, e-commerce collections, corporate cards, and intra-group expense clearing, provided the local COP treasury remains consolidated at an authorized IMC and no export or investment declaration dependency is created.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transfer pricing annual documentation (Local File) is mandatory where related-party cross-border transactions exceed 10,000 UVT for the tax year (UVT2024=COP47,064; 10,000 UVT=COP470,640,000); Master File is mandatory when consolidated gross revenue exceeds 150,000 UVT; a Transfer Pricing Informative Return (Formulario 120) is due in all cases where the 10,000 UVT related-party threshold is met

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not operate a separate ESR regime comparable to certain Latin American free-trade zones, but substance-over-form, general anti-avoidance rules, limited beneficial ownership tests, and CFC rules are enforced by DIAN; an S.A.S. without employees, premises, production activity, or business purpose will be disregarded for income tax, dividend, and DTA purposes

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false return

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under Criminal Code Article 308 carries imprisonment of 48 to 108 months and a fine equal to 50%-200% of the evaded tax; DIAN inaccuracy penalty equals 100% of the understated tax or overstated loss; civil default interest accrues from the date the tax should have been paid

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; after veil-piercing, personal assets may be subject to civil restitution, court travel bans, and imprisonment upon criminal conviction

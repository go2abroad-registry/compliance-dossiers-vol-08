Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax and Customs Directorate – Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN). Electronic tax operations portal: MUISCA (muisca.dian.gov.co); public portal: www.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first appoint a legal representative resident in Colombia and file a duly apostilled power of attorney with the competent Cámara de Comercio. The Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.) is then registered in the RUES (Registro Único Empresarial y Social). Subsequently, the entity must obtain a DIAN-issued NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by completing the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) through the MUISCA portal. A digital signature certificate (firma electrónica) issued by a DIAN-authorized certification provider is required for all electronic tax transactions. After NIT issuance, the company is automatically registered as a VAT responsible party and must activate the electronic invoicing mechanism before commencing export operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. An S.A.S. is mandatorily a separate entity-level taxpayer; no pass-through treatment is available. Available statutory elections include: (i) the elective Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) for taxpayers with gross income below 100,000 UVT, subject to DIAN registration and annual recertification; (ii) the irrevocable annual election under Article 896-1 of the Colombian Tax Code to treat certain foreign entities as transparent for anti-hybrid purposes; and (iii) fiscal consolidation for business groups through simultaneous filing and DIAN pre-approval under Law 223/1995 as amended.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for S.A.S. and foreign-owned corporate exporters; every S.A.S. must obtain NIT/RUT and register as an IVA responsible party before starting export activities. The 3,500 UVT simplified-regime threshold (COP 164,727,500 for tax year 2024) applies only to natural persons and never to a corporate S.A.S.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible movable goods, including raw cane sugar in bulk, qualifies for the VAT zero rate (0%) under Article 481 of the Colombian Tax Code when the goods are physically shipped from Colombian customs territory to a foreign destination, ownership passes to a non-resident buyer, the sale is denominated and paid in foreign currency through the Colombian foreign exchange market, and the export is documented under a valid customs export procedure. The exporter may credit input VAT incurred on raw cane purchases, freight and processing against its VAT output liability and obtain a cash refund of any VAT balance in favor if formalities are completed with DIAN.

  • Customs export declaration (DEX / Exportación) validated and accepted by DIAN's customs office
  • Commercial export invoice and DIAN-validated electronic export invoice linked to the DEX
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or equivalent shipping document evidencing vessel shipment and foreign destination
  • Inward remittance certificate or foreign exchange channeling certificate showing the 180-day FX repatriation
  • Certificate of origin (where applicable for tariff preference), quality certificate and polarization report for raw cane sugar
  • Packing list, weight/cargo manifest, insurance certificate and any phytosanitary or free-sale certificate required for the destination market

DIAN Mandatory Electronic Invoicing (Factura Electrónica) requires real-time transmission of the invoice XML and electronic events to DIAN's online validation service through SOAP/REST APIs, either directly or via an authorized technology provider. Each invoice must contain a CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) or, in contingency, a CUSE, and must be digitally signed. Export invoices must be transmitted to DIAN before the cargo leaves the customs jurisdiction and must be cross-linked with the export customs declaration. Fiscal clearance is remote and automated; DIAN returns a validation response, and no physical digital stamp is attached to exported goods.

DIAN may scrutinize export transactions within the statutory five-year audit window. Failure to prove physical exit, foreign ownership, or currency repatriation within 180 days leads to retroactive VAT reassessment at 19%, denial or recapture of input VAT refunds, monthly interest on the amount clawed back, and inaccuracy sanctions under Article 648 E.T. of 10% to 20% of the misstated VAT. Falsified bills of lading, virtual export invoices or forged inward remittance certificates constitute customs smuggling and tax evasion, subject to corrective penalties of up to 200% of the evaded VAT, suspension or cancellation of the exporter's NIT, and criminal prosecution. Permanent zero-rating eligibility may be revoked retrospectively and assets seized if the exporter fails to demonstrate good faith and complete documentary compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia allows free convertibility under a managed floating exchange rate for current-account transactions, subject to filing of the Declaración de Cambio and use of authorized intermediaries. Capital-account transactions (e.g., foreign direct investment) above USD 100,000 must be registered with Banco de la República to obtain remittance rights. Dividend and capital repatriation are permitted after verification of the investment registration and DIAN tax status; no exchange permission decree is required, but all wires must be routed through a Colombian authorized bank and declared within five business days. The practical hurdle is not convertibility but documentary completeness of the underlying trade and tax-compliance certificate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Colombian Foreign Exchange Resolution 1 of 2018 (as amended), proceeds of exported goods must be collected within 180 calendar days after the date of shipment (embarque), extendable by up to 90 additional days upon justified request to Banco de la República. Once the export proceeds are received by the local authorized commercial bank, the exporter must liquidate (sell) the foreign currency to the Colombian market within five business days. The export must be submitted through Form 2 'Declaración de Cambio por Exportaciones', itemizing the importer, FAS/FOB value, and vessel particulars; non-compliance may lead to fines, tax withholdings and loss of customs privileges.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), with enforcement and registration coordination through Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia and the tax/foreign-exchange authority DIAN.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility as the chief banking/treasury rail. Colombian law reserves the taking of deposits and foreign exchange intermediation to supervised credit institutions and the National Treasury. Cross-border EMIs (Borderless, Revolut Business, Wise Business, Monese, etc.) can provide only auxiliary creditor-side payments or wallet settlements and cannot lawfully issue export proceeds surrender declarations on behalf of a Colombian SAS, hold the required Banco de la República code, or provide the certificate of foreign exchange verification. They may be engaged for payroll or supplier micro-payments, but core principal capital, export proceeds collection, and FX hedging must be executed by a Colombian commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound shareholder loan or foreign investment wiring must be sent through SWIFT MT103/202 from the investor's regulated account to the SAS operational account at the chosen Colombian commercial bank. The payment field must reference the foreign investment registration form number and the SAS NIT. The receiving bank submits the Declaración de Cambio por Servicios y Transferencias and the investment appears in the Banco de la República electronic foreign investment register (Form No. 8); without this registration and the subsequent restitution certificate, remittance of dividends or capital returns is suspended. It is not permitted to inject capital through virtual-asset exchanges, unlicensed EMI accounts or personal accounts of the SAS administrators.The primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103/MT202 for direct payment of sugar invoices and documentary letters of credit, expressed as SWIFT MT700/MT799 for the collection cycle. For direct disbursements, the SAS issues the payment instruction to the Colombian bank, which debits its USD or COP account and performs the foreign exchange conversion respecting the export-proceeds-surrender rules, then sends the payment through its New York correspondent tier. For larger vessel quantities, an irrevocable revolving L/C subject to UCP600 is opened by the Colombian bank, with the shipping documents and a certificate of sugar polarization as the settlement documents. Local COP payments to Colombian vendors are cleared via the Banco de la República CENIT high-value system.Over-the-counter forward FX contracts (foreign exchange swaps between COP and USD) with maturity up to 270 days entered into with a Colombian authorized local credit institution, underlying the physical sugar export/import cash flows.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in USD/COP with offshore banks, where settlement is made in a convertible currency outside Colombia, used exclusively to hedge commodity price and payment-date exposure.Cross-currency swaps (USD-COP) that postpone principal delivery and convert the final settlement into an obligation on the Colombian peso side, booked through the authorized bank.Plain-vanilla FX call/put options and collars involving the COP/USD pair, offered by local banks or their correspondents, to cap conversion losses on the vessel-payment cycle.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

7.5% where the distributed profits have already borne the 35% general corporate income tax; 35% where distributions derive from exempt or preferentially taxed profits. For non-resident shareholders, this withholding is final. DTA rates can reduce the ordinary 7.5% withholding to 5% or 10% depending on shareholding percentage and treaty vehicle.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return is due on the date assigned by DIAN's official tax calendar, normally between February and April of the following tax year, based on the last digit of the taxpayer's NIT (tax identification number). For a calendar-year trader with NIT ending in a late digit, the deadline is typically no later than the 21st of April. No extension is available beyond the published schedule; failure to file by the assigned date is considered an extemporaneous declaration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Transfer Pricing Local File and Master File apply to taxpayers whose gross income or gross equity exceeds approximately COP 5,700 million (150,000 UVT for FY 2023; indexed annually by DIAN). Separate annual TP information return is due where cross-border related-party transactions exceed the applicable UVT-based threshold. The S.A.S. sugar trader must prepare contemporaneous documentation regardless of threshold if it executes related-party commodity transactions with foreign buying or trading affiliates.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as a separate statutory regime. Instead, Colombian tax law applies substantive anti-abuse principles, including the substance-over-form doctrine under Article 869 E.T., general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR), CFC rules, and the OECD BEPS principles. For a sugar commodity arbitrage S.A.S., the entity must demonstrate real business purpose, decision-making authority, operational personnel, storage or trade management capacity, and material commercial risk to avoid characterization as a shell or intermediary. If the entity is inserted solely to route profit through a Colombian invoice hub with no substance, DIAN may disregard the entity, apply TP adjustments, and pierce the corporate veil via fraud claims.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanction under Article 434 of the Colombian Penal Code: imprisonment of 9 to 15 years, plus prohibition from engaging in foreign trade. Civil fiscal penalties include a fine of up to 200% of the tax evaded and monthly default interest, plus the obligation to file corrected returns and pay omitted tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign false tax returns are jointly and severally liable for the omitted tax with personal assets. In criminal proceedings, precautionary asset freezes, travel bans, restraints on disposal of property, and seizures may be imposed on the responsible officers.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Under the anti-abuse doctrine, DIAN may disallow the entity for tax purposes, reallocate profits to the underlying principal, deny treaty benefits, and impose a fine equal to the non-declared tax plus interest. The transaction may also be recharacterized as a concealed dividend, triggering additional 35% withholding tax on distributions.

Director personal liability: Colombian commercial law permits veil piercing under Law 222/1995 where the entity is used to defraud creditors or the tax authority. Directors are personally liable for civil restitution and may face criminal conspiracy charges if the sham is part of a money-laundering scheme.
Money Laundering (Lavado de Activos)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 323 of the Colombian Penal Code, money laundering is punishable with imprisonment of 9 to 15 years and a fine of 650 to 15,000 current legal monthly minimum wages. Failure to implement and comply with the AML/SARLAFT control system exposes the company to administrative fines by the UIAF and DIAN, and commercial partners may terminate banking relationships.

Director personal liability: Directors who structure, approve or willfully ignore laundering transactions are criminally liable as principals or accomplices. Preventive measures include travel bans, prohibition of disposal of assets, and temporary seizure of passports while the investigation is pending.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may make an arbitrary adjustment based on the determined Colombian price benchmark, impose a fine of up to 10% of the undisclosed transaction value for undocumented operations, and assess the omitted 35% tax plus interest. Penalties for filing incomplete or inaccurate TP documentation may reach 100% of the tax underpaid as a result.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify or approve TP documentation without reasonable economic and financial support are subject to professional liability and, in cases of deliberate falsehood, criminal liability for tax evasion. Personal assets can be attached if the director signed the offending tax return.
Hidden Dividend / Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Interest paid to foreign related creditors is disallowed if the debtor's equity exceeds the applicable debt-to-equity ratio of 3:1 under Article 118-1 E.T.; disallowed interest is recharacterized as hidden equity distribution subject to the 7.5% or 35% dividend withholding tax. Additional penalties of up to 160% of the tax underpaid may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors approving foreign-related financing structures without corporate benefit documentation risk being held personally liable for the recharacterized withholding tax and penalties in cases of bad faith, reckless management, or premeditated profit stripping.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital applies. A Colombian S.A.S. may be incorporated with zero paid-up capital, and subscribed capital may be paid in installments under a maximum two-year term if the bylaws provide for it. No escrow account or capital blockage is required for online retail activities.

Funding & unlock path: For any physical office or warehouse, the company must obtain the Cuerpo de Bomberos fire-safety certificate, the municipal land-use certificate, and an ARL occupational health and safety affiliation. Structural load inspections are only mandatory for unusually high-density warehousing; ordinary low-load retail e-commerce storage falls under general commercial building clearance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (full corporate taxpayer — 'Régimen Ordinario del Impuesto sobre la Renta'). Under Articles 12 et seq. of the Estatuto Tributario, the Colombian-resident S.A.S. is a separate juridical person taxed on worldwide income at 35% for tax year 2025 (Law 2277 of 2022 preserves the 35% rate). The entity is classified as 'Gran Contribuyente' or ordinary taxpayer in the RUT, is an 'Responsable del IVA' in the common regime without pass-through eligibility, and is subject to dividend withholding at source on distributions to non-resident shareholders (generally a 10% surcharge on distributions from profits taxed at the corporate level), plus the applicable municipal industry and commerce tax (ICA). Territorial-source rules apply only to non-resident entities; the S.A.S., being a resident company, is subject to entity-level taxation on its Colombian and foreign-sourced income. No check-the-box pass-through transparency is available for a Colombian S.A.S.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Colombian law for an S.A.S.; the entity is irrebuttably treated as a corporation for income tax and IVA purposes. Available elective regimes: (1) Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLES) — an optional presumptive/combined income-tax-plus-IVA regime under Law 1943 of 2018 and Law 2010 of 2019, available only when annual gross turnover does not exceed 100,000 UVT (approximately COP 4.98 billion in 2025) and other activity-specific thresholds are satisfied; retail e-commerce operators may opt into SIMPLES but the full turnover of a major consumer-electronics retailer generally disqualifies it. (2) Régimen ZESE (Zonas Económicas y Sociales Especiales) — a 20% income tax rate election for new companies established in qualifying underdeveloped municipalities; not applicable to a main establishment in a major city such as Bogotá. (3) Régimen de Compañías Holding Colombianas (CHC) under Law 2010 of 2019 — electable by a Colombian S.A.S. that holds shares in foreign entities, providing territorial exemption for dividends from abroad, capital gains on foreign share disposals, and reduced withholding on outbound interest/dividends; requires a minimum qualified shareholding, substantial domestic substance, and formal election in the annual tax return. (4) Hybrid mismatch limitations: Colombia has enacted OECD-compliant hybrid instrument and hybrid entity rules in Decree 1625 of 2016 and Law 2277 of 2022; a resident S.A.S. participating in cross-border structures must document that no double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcome arises. (5) Fiscal consolidation is not permitted; each S.A.S. files on a stand-alone basis.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is a freely floating currency with unrestricted convertibility for current-account transactions and a broadly open capital account subject to mandatory channeling through the Mercado Cambiario (MCR) via SFC-authorized intermediaries. Foreign direct investment (FDI) into a Colombian SAS must be registered with Banco de la República through the local receiving bank (via the FIC/SIE electronic declaration system) under concept code 4010 for equity capital. Profit and dividend remittances are permitted without separate exchange-control authorization provided that (i) the foreign investor's equity interest is duly registered, (ii) the entity has filed and paid its corporate income tax obligations, (iii) the applicable dividend withholding tax regime has been honored (generally 5% on profits previously taxed at 35% and up to 10% in preferential cases), and (iv) the outgoing transfer is declared under the correct dividend-channeling concept code. Practical remittance hurdles for a SAS with non-resident owners include DIAN insistence on updated RUT/NIT documentation for the foreign investor, SFC-mandated UBO disclosure, and the local bank's policy requiring audited financial statements before any profit distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of physical goods must bring 100% of export proceeds into Colombia through the Mercado Cambiario within six months from the date of shipment (Bill of Lading or airway bill). Foreign-exchange proceeds are deemed 'chanelled' when either (a) converted into COP through an SFC-licensed bank with submission of the Formulario de Información Cambiaria (FIC), or (b) deposited into a declared foreign-currency account (cuenta corriente en moneda extranjera) held with a local bank; no mandatory conversion into COP is required if the company keeps and uses the foreign-currency balance for authorized outflows such as imports, foreign-debt service, or re-export settlements. For e-commerce transactions settled in COP by Stripe or PayPal through a Colombian licensed acquirer, the underlying export declaration (DEX) must reconcile with the PSP batch reports and no separate FX surrender is triggered because the proceeds are already routed through the local settlement system; however, any sales proceeds accumulated abroad in USD/EUR through foreign PSP balances must be repatriated into the MCR within the same six-month statutory window for goods exports. Services-related digital sales, including software and digital value-added services, benefit from a five-year channeling window from the service accrual date under Resolución Externa 1 of 2021, but the same 100% channeling obligation applies to all export revenue regardless of the receiving rail.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), jointly administering the Estatuto Cambiario with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) as supervisor of foreign-exchange-licensed intermediaries and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) as customs and enforcement authority; the normative base is Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, and Resolución Externa 1 of 2021 (as amended), which consolidates all foreign-exchange (cambiario) regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-TO-MEDIUM with material legal constraints. Stripe (through local licensed acquirer partners such as CredibanCo/Bambú) and PayPal (with local bank settlement to a Colombian account) are permissible PSP collection rails that settle in COP and reconcile with the Estatuto Cambiario. However, a Colombian SAS cannot lawfully treat an offshore EMI balance (Wise Business, Airwallex, Mercury, Dukascopy, Revolut Business) as a treasury settlement account: the FX regime obliges exporters to channel all proceeds through the MCR via SFC-licensed banks; leaving collected funds in an unlicensed foreign EMI breaches the channeling obligation and exposes the entity to cambiario sanctions (fines up to 200% of the transaction value imposed by SFC/DIAN), complicates import-payment substantiation, and triggers enhanced AML classification at the local bank. The compliant structure is: PSP collection in COP through local acquiring settlement; any foreign EMI usage limited to minor operational expenses and always repatriated/declared within the statutory timeline.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholder equity is routed by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's upstream bank to the Colombian bank's USD correspondent (nostro), referencing the beneficiary SAS's foreign-currency or peso account. The receiving bank verifies the UBO against sanction lists, credits the company's USD capital-import account, and converts to COP at the market reference rate (TCR) upon presentation of the Cambiario declaration; the bank then files the FDI registration with Banco de la República under concept code 4010 within the statutory deadline. For shareholder loans classified as external credit, the drawdown must be registered under the external-loan concept code and the entity remains subject to quarterly information declarations. The complete routing documentation set comprises: the MT103 confirmation, the signed bank-internal foreign-investment declaration, the Cámara de Comercio certificate, the NIT/RUT, a certified passport copy of the foreign investor, and the board resolution approving the capital increase and account signatories. All funds must be reflected in the investment registration before the SAS distributes dividends or repays equity.Import and supplier payments for consumer electronics procured from China, the United States, and regional warehouses settle through: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers denominated in USD/EUR/CNY via the local bank's FX desk, with the import customs declaration (Declaración de Importación) referenced to substantiate the payment; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) issued by the Colombian bank and advised through the supplier's confirming bank for large recurring procurement loads; and (iii) direct USD debits from the declared foreign-currency account when accumulated export balances are applied. PSP payouts from Stripe and PayPal settle in COP through the local acquiring network (ACH Colombia / Cámara de Compensación) into the company's operational current account, eliminating FX conversion on the collection side. Disbursements for cross-border platform fees, cloud subscriptions, overseas advertising, and digital services are payable via SWIFT MT103 with the correct import-of-services or royalty concept code, supported by commercial invoices and applicable DIAN withholding-tax certificates.Deliverable USD/COP forward contracts (contratos forward) offered by authorized local banks, requiring evidence of underlying commercial exposure from import/export flows and internal board delegation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing the USD/COP rate, permitted for local entities hedging real exposures without physical delivery, executed through an SFC-authorized intermediary or a recognized offshore bank in mirror with the local bank's hedge transaction.Cross-currency swaps (COP/USD and occasionally EUR/COP) used to match foreign-currency assets and liabilities or to convert variable-rate foreign debt into local-currency obligations.Vanilla FX options on USD/COP purchased to cap the entity's maximum import cost or to protect export margins; option-writing is restricted for non-financial corporate entities.Exchange-traded USD/COP futures and options quoted on recognized international venues (such as CME) and executed through a local broker, permissible for micro-hedging of high-frequency e-commerce settlement flows subject to the company's documented derivative policy and real-exposure test.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-TO-MEDIUM with material legal constraints. Stripe (through local licensed acquirer partners such as CredibanCo/Bambú) and PayPal (with local bank settlement to a Colombian account) are permissible PSP collection rails that settle in COP and reconcile with the Estatuto Cambiario. However, a Colombian SAS cannot lawfully treat an offshore EMI balance (Wise Business, Airwallex, Mercury, Dukascopy, Revolut Business) as a treasury settlement account: the FX regime obliges exporters to channel all proceeds through the MCR via SFC-licensed banks; leaving collected funds in an unlicensed foreign EMI breaches the channeling obligation and exposes the entity to cambiario sanctions (fines up to 200% of the transaction value imposed by SFC/DIAN), complicates import-payment substantiation, and triggers enhanced AML classification at the local bank. The compliant structure is: PSP collection in COP through local acquiring settlement; any foreign EMI usage limited to minor operational expenses and always repatriated/declared within the statutory timeline.

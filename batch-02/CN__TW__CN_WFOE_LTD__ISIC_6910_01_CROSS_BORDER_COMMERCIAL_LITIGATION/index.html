Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taxation Administration (Taiwan) and the local National Taxation Bureau at the company's registered address; official online portals: Ministry of Finance Tax Portal (https://www.tax.nat.gov.tw) and Electronic Uniform Invoice Integration Service Platform (https://www.einvoice.nat.gov.tw).

E-government filing portal

A Taiwan-incorporated WFOE obtains its statutory tax identifier (Unified Business Number, UBIN) automatically upon incorporation through the Ministry of Economic Affairs Company Registration System. The foreign parent/investor is not required to hold a Taiwanese TIN for the WFOE. The WFOE must (1) complete company registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs; (2) use the MOEA-issued UBIN as its TIN/VAT ID; (3) file profit-seeking enterprise income tax and business tax registration with the local National Taxation Bureau before starting commercial operations; (4) apply for Uniform Invoice purchase authorization and, if electronic invoicing is adopted, join the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integration Service Platform; and (5) register as a withholding tax agent for employee salary and foreign payments. If the foreign parent itself makes taxable supplies in Taiwan without a Taiwan branch, it must obtain a non-resident business-tax registration number through the Ministry of Finance Tax Portal and appoint a local tax agent.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (the WFOE is a Taiwan resident profit-seeking enterprise taxed on its own income; no pass-through treatment exists under Taiwan's Income Tax Act). — A Taiwan-incorporated WFOE obtains its statutory tax identifier (Unified Business Number, UBIN) automatically upon incorporation through the Ministry of Economic Affairs Company Registration System. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the WFOE is a Taiwan resident profit-seeking enterprise taxed on its own income; no pass-through treatment exists under Taiwan's Income Tax Act).

Available election forms

None (Taiwan has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election for a WFOE limited company; entity-level taxation is compulsory; only administrative elections such as electronic invoice filing mode are available).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold for a Taiwan-incorporated WFOE; tax registration is compulsory before commencing taxable business. For unincorporated small-scale businesses, simplified taxation applies when average monthly taxable sales are below NT$200,000, but that status is not available to a WFOE limited company.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, a 0% Business Tax rate applies to the export of goods and to qualifying export-related services supplied for use or consumption entirely outside Taiwan, including cross-border commercial litigation advisory, arbitration and asset-tracing services delivered to an overseas recipient and not used in Taiwan. The claim is available only where the Taiwan entity retains contemporaneous evidence that legal title and risk passed overseas, the service recipient is outside Taiwan, and the proceeds are attributable to foreign currency receipts. Sales of digital services to Taiwan consumers are not zero-rated; such cross-border digital services become subject to 5% Business Tax.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export customs declaration/electronic export entry issued by the Customs Administration, Ministry of Finance)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical export
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List and the underlying export contract/purchase order
  • Foreign-exchange repatriation record (e.g., bank inward remittance advice, foreign exchange settlement slip, or Letter of Credit negotiation record)
  • For zero-rated services: a written service agreement, delivery/engagement records and recipient confirmation stating that the service is performed and consumed outside Taiwan

Taiwan mandates the issuance of Uniform Invoices for all taxable sales. VAT-registered businesses must file Business Tax returns every two months and transmit electronic Uniform Invoice data to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integration Service Platform using the E-Invoice Turnkey API/EDI connectivity in the prescribed XML structure. Each electronic Uniform Invoice carries a tax-issued invoice code and an anti-counterfeit random code encoded in the QR/matrix barcode. There is no real-time prior fiscal clearance at the point of issuance; instead, the tax authority performs automated central-database cross-checks after transmission, and the taxpayer must retain the electronic invoice files and audit logs for the statutory records period.

The National Taxation Bureau and Customs Administration cross-match customs export declarations, e-invoice data and bank foreign-exchange receipt records. If the zero-rating substantiation bundle is incomplete, forged or untimely, or if export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day statutory foreign-exchange deadline, the tax authority will reverse the zero-rated treatment, assess the 5% Business Tax, add delinquent interest, disallow any related input VAT credit or VAT refund, and impose monetary penalties in proportion to the tax evaded; intentional fraud or misrepresentation may further trigger criminal liability under Taiwan's tax collection laws.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the Chinese foreign exchange regulatory framework, RMB is not fully convertible on the capital account. The current account (trade in goods and services, profits, dividends, interest, and wages) is largely convertible after furnishing authentic transaction documentation to the designated bank for verification. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment injections, outbound direct investment, cross-border loans, and equity transfers, require prior approval or registration with SAFE and are subject to periodic quotas and real-demand tests. Export proceeds in foreign currency are subject to the mandatory repatriation regime: Except for certain prudent exemptions, all export receipts must be repatriated into China and, while 100% may be retained on foreign exchange settlement accounts, the exporter's currency conversion is closely supervised, and any surplus above the account ceiling must be converted into RMB. Profit remittance by a WFOE to its Taiwan shareholder requires audited financial statements, completed annual corporate income tax filing, evidence of paid-in capital, and prior SAFE registration of the FDI capital injection. The bank will match the remitted amount to the verified distributable profit, and any mismatch triggers enhanced due diligence. Dividends are taxed under any applicable cross-strait or other double-taxation arrangement, and withholding tax certificates must be filed. Overall, the regime is administratively rigorous and stringent at the capital account level, while current account convertibility is achievable through documented, transparent transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current account administration, export proceeds must be repatriated into mainland China no later than 180 days after customs declaration (or a longer approved period) and must be credited to the enterprise's foreign exchange settlement account; there is no fixed compulsory conversion ratio to RMB, but the proceeds must pass through designated bank verification, and SAFE may mandate conversion of the balance above the enterprise's verified FX account ceiling. In practice, the mandatory surrender ratio is zero for legitimate export receipts, but repatriation, reporting in the Export Trade in Foreign Exchange Collection Confirmation system, and documentary authenticity review are strictly enforced.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBoC), together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), is the apex monetary and foreign exchange control authority for the mainland China banking system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers such as Revolut, Wise Business, or Airwallex are not licensed as banks in mainland China and cannot hold a WFOE's primary corporate account, accept FDI capital injections, or clear CNH settlements through local clearing infrastructure. They may be used only as auxiliary foreign payment rails for non-regulated disbursements, provided funds do not touch domestic custody. Legal constraints under the PRC Banking Law and the E-CNY framework prohibit unlicensed deposit-taking and payment services; therefore, the viability rating is Very Low as a primary banking solution, and any deployment must be relegated to cross-border invoicing support via a licensed partner bank in Hong Kong or Singapore.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a WFOE receiving equity capital from a Taiwan parent, the compliant route is: (i) obtain SAFE foreign investment registration (FDI) for the WFOE before any capital injection, generating a unique Foreign Exchange Business Registration Number; (ii) execute the inbound wire transfer from the Taiwan parent in foreign currency (e.g., USD) and remit to the WFOE's domestic Capital Account at a designated Tier-1 bank; (iii) ensure the SWIFT MT103 carries the SAFE BOPS transaction code category for direct investment in equity capital (purpose code 621012) and the benefactor name matches the registered investor; (iv) upon receipt, the bank verifies the inbound against the SAFE system, processes the capital verification declaration, and permits conversion to RMB for operating expenses only upon submission of payment orders to the bank; (v) a Chinese certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report confirming shareholder contribution before the company can record the capital as paid-in equity.Clearing options for outbound settlement payments from the WFOE include: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct remittances of invoices, settlement awards, and legal fees; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) for procurement of services or goods where the beneficiary requires a bank undertaking; (iii) Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) for lower-cost settlements with secured trade documents. Each payment must be initiated through a Tier-1 bank with a validated under-the-current-account transaction form, supported by the contract, invoice, financial statement, and, when above the threshold, the SAFE foreign exchange receipt/payment declaration form for BOPS statistical reporting.

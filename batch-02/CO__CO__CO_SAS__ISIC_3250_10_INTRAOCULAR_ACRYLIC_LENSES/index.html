Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); electronic portal: MUISCA at www.dian.gov.co; electronic invoicing and customs declaration APIs under DIAN Web Services.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned S.A.S. first registers its incorporation deed with the Cámara de Comercio; through its legal representative or attorney-in-fact, it files the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) application in MUISCA, attaching the incorporation certificate, ID documents, and Colombian tax domicile. DIAN issues the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), which is used for income tax, VAT, and customs purposes. No separate VAT registration ID is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned S.A.S. first registers its incorporation deed with the Cámara de Comercio; through its legal representative or attorney-in-fact, it files the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) application in MUISCA, attaching the incorporation certificate, ID documents, and Colombian tax domicile. DIAN issues the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), which is used for income tax, VAT, and customs purposes. No separate VAT registration ID is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

There is no Colombian check-the-box or general passthrough election. An S.A.S. is mandatorily treated as a separate taxable entity. Available elections include tax-neutral reorganization rules, the 'Régimen Simple de Tributación' for small businesses under statutory thresholds, and legal stability contracts or mega-investment agreements approved by the national government. Hybrid mismatch elections are not separately available; BEPS-style rules operate by default.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for legal entities; an S.A.S. must register in the RUT as a 'régimen común' VAT-responsible person before commencing taxable operations. Simplified-régime thresholds (e.g., prior-year gross income below 3,500 UVT) apply only to natural persons and are irrelevant to this entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The sale of intraocular acrylic lenses is zero-rated for VAT if the goods are physically exported from Colombia, the seller is a DIAN-registered 'régimen común' taxpayer, the DIAN-validated electronic export invoice is issued, and the export is effected under a definitive customs export declaration. The exporter must substantiate the real exit of the goods and, for related-party exports, maintain transfer pricing documentation to support the export price.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación - DEX) accepted by DIAN customs authority
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill) proving shipment from Colombian territory to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Negociación de Divisas / Formulario No. 5) proving receipt of export proceeds
  • Electronic export invoice (Factura Electrónica de Venta) with DIAN validation and CUFE/CUDE stamp
  • Packing List and commercial invoice supporting the customs declaration

All sales, including exports, require a DIAN-validated electronic sales invoice (Factura Electrónica de Venta). The taxpayer transmits the XML via DIAN Web Services/API with an X.509 certificate; DIAN performs near-real-time validation and returns a CUFE/CUDE corresponding number and QR stamp. Export invoices must be validated before the customs export declaration and must use the configured export operation code.

DIAN may retrospectively revoke the 0% rate if the export documentation is incomplete, the goods did not exit Colombia, or the proceeds are not collected and repatriated within the 180-day foreign-exchange deadline. In such cases, the VAT becomes 19% on the sale, input VAT is clawed back, and penalties apply under Articles 668/651 of the Tax Code, including up to 100% of the tax omitted, daily interest, and sanctions under the Colombian exchange-control regime; fraudulent export schemes may also be prosecuted as customs crimes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with a broadly liberal current account. Export and import settlements in foreign currency are freely permitted, but all foreign-exchange operations must be routed through the authorized foreign-exchange market by a licensed intermediary, and each transaction must carry the corresponding statistical FX declaration. Current-account transactions are not subject to quantitative approval, but documentary and AML verification apply. Capital-account flows, including foreign direct investment into a Colombian S.A.S., require registration with the Banco de la República. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without an exchange-control approval hurdle, provided the original FDI is properly registered, the dividend tax has been paid in Colombia, the shareholders has approved the distribution, and the remittance is declared as a capital-account outflow. No special exchange convertibility or residency licence is required for the intraocular lens exporter, but misdeclared purpose codes or unregistered foreign-currency liabilities can trigger fines and bank account restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Merchandise export proceeds must be channeled into Colombia through a local authorized intermediary (reintegro) within the term fixed by Banco de la República, which is generally six months from the date of the export customs declaration for standard goods; an extension may be requested and granted before the original deadline. One hundred percent of the declared export FOB value is subject to channeling. Colombia does not mandate a fixed conversion ratio into pesos: the proceeds may be held in a local USD foreign-currency account, deposited in a foreign-currency account abroad only where expressly allowed for treasury management, or converted to COP at the applicable exchange-rate market. The exporter must preserve the DEX customs declaration, bill of lading, commercial invoice, payment evidence, and bank credit advice to substantiate the mandatory FX declaration.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Comité de Cambios and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia; the operational foreign-exchange and customs-control counterpart is the DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales). Legal source: Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1068 of 2015, External Circular DCIN-83 of 2020, and the Banco de la República international investment registration regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Low for offshore digital EMIs (for example Wise or Airwallex) as the sole treasury rail. Under Decree 1068 of 2015 and Circular DCIN-83, only Colombian authorized intermediaries, being banks or licensed payment-company channels settling through a supervised bank, may intermediate foreign-exchange declarations for residents. An offshore EMI cannot hold COP-denominated accounts on behalf of a Colombian resident for export/import operations, cannot submit the required FX and trade declarations, and is not licensed to operate as a clearing bank in Colombia. Offshore EMIs may be used only as ancillary foreign-currency collection accounts outside Colombia, with all funds repatriated thereafter through a tier-1 bank rail into the Colombian S.A.S. onshore account. Local SEDPE fintechs can improve collection speed, but ultimate settlement must go through a licensed commercial bank; using an unlicensed EMI as the principal operating account creates exchange-control and tax-reporting risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) adopt a shareholders' resolution approving the foreign direct investment and the issue or transfer of shares in the Colombian S.A.S.; (2) notify the commercial registry and obtain an updated certificate of existence and legal representation; (3) effect the capital wire through SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to a tier-1 Colombian bank, using the beneficiary account registered in the name of the S.A.S. and placing in Field 70 the exact text Capital Contribution FDI for CO_SAS together with the shareholder name and reference number; (4) instruct the receiving bank to credit funds to a USD account and to record the transaction under the Banco de la República Foreign Direct Investment category; (5) require the bank to submit the corresponding international investment declaration and to obtain the central-bank registration acknowledgement before funds are classified as permanent capital; (6) ensure that any conversion from USD to COP is executed through the authorized foreign-exchange market and recorded on the daily exchange-rate registry. The verification bundle for the bank comprises the shareholders' resolution, the notarized capital-correction or subscription agreement, the NIT tax identification certificate, the Cámara de Comercio certificate showing the current authorized capital, evidence of source of funds from the investor, and passport copies of signatories. Use only the official bank-approved purpose code for foreign direct investment; a generic remittance advice may cause the account to be blocked or the capital contribution to be recharacterized as local debt requiring a loan registration.Export-collection clearing rails comprise SWIFT MT103 direct wire receipts from distributors and ODM buyers into the onshore USD settlement account, and documentary letters of credit confirmed by the buyer's bank. For an LC operation, the Colombian bank issues SWIFT MT700 confirming the credit; the exporter presents documents through the negotiating bank, and payment is received either by MT754 advice or MT202 cover to the onshore account, followed by settlement credit to the USD account. Import disbursements for acrylic raw materials or high-precision lathing equipment are executed by SWIFT MT103 from the USD account for open-account terms, or by issuing an irrevocable, confirmed, sight letter of credit through the local tier-1 bank for high-value equipment; the issuing bank sends MT700, amendments are made by MT707, and the paying bank receives cover after document compliance through MT202 or MT103. All disbursements must reference the commercial invoice and purchase order in Field 70, and the bank must make the corresponding import or export foreign-exchange declaration to DIAN/Banco de la República. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% (general CIT, Law 1819/2016 Article 48 and Law 2277/2022 confirmed for fiscal years 2023-2025)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% WHT on dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders where the distributed profits have borne Colombian CIT at the 35% rate; 7.5% WHT applies to the portion of profits that enjoyed corporate-level exemptions (Art. 242 E.T.).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Fiscal year is the calendar year. The DIAN publishes every January a staggered expiry table based on the last two digits of the NIT (tax ID); for ordinary taxpayers the window runs mid-March through April/May of the year following the taxable year; large taxpayers (those with gross income ≥ COP 53,000 million in 2023) file in the April-May window. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: COP 450,000,000 (approx. USD 110,000 for FY2024; 450,000 UVT threshold updated annually by DIAN) for the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions in a fiscal year; exceeding it mandates (i) the Form 120 TP Informative Return and (ii) the full Transfer Pricing Study (Local File) under the OECD-aligned methodology of Arts. 260-1 to 260-11 E.T.; the Master File is required for multinational groups above the DIAN consolidated-revenue threshold and the Country-by-Country report applies where consolidated group revenue exceeds the equivalent of €750 million (COP threshold annually issued by DIAN, approx. COP 2.9 trillion for FY2024).

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations filing, but the DIAN applies a GAAR/substance-over-form doctrine under Arts. 869 and 870 E.T., the rule on fictitious companies (Art. 12-1 E.T.) and the sham-transaction doctrine of Art. 9 of Civil Code. A Colombian SAS carrying on ISIC 3250 lens manufacturing must maintain genuine substance: owned/leased cleanroom production infrastructure, qualified manufacturing and QA staff, real procurement and logistics flows, INVIMA medical-device sanitary registration (Registro Sanitario) and a special sanitary license for Class D intraocular implants, and bank accounts matching commercial value. A SAS lacking these elements—while routing lens production or profits abroad—is rebuttable as a façade, enabling DIAN to recharacterize all revenue at 35% plus inexactitude penalties and criminal fraud exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Evasión Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Prison 48-108 months and a fine of 100%-300% of the evaded tax (Criminal Code Art. 434A, as introduced by Law 1819/2016); automatic civil consequence is 'inexactitud' at 100% of the omitted tax (Art. 599 E.T.) plus daily arrears interest; judicial seizure of the SAS assets and freezing of LC/SWIFT settlement accounts.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and the accounting officer who signed the false return become jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; personal assets are attachable, the director receives an exit ban (impedimento de salida del país), and may be barred from holding public or corporate office; prison applies personally to directors who authored the fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Foreign-Supplier Window Dressing

Statutory sanction: DIAN administrative adjustment recharacterizing the transactions at the arm's-length price, with additional 35% CIT, moratory interest, and the 100% inexactitude penalty on the differential; submitted false TP documentation can escalate into criminal evasion; the TP Local File must be filed with Form 120 under penalty of 1%-2% of transaction value (min. 10 UVT, max. 60,000 UVT).

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false TP studies or sign intercompany agreements with artificial values are personally liable for the deficits; travel bans and asset embargoes may be ordered during the DIAN audit; in aggravated cases the prosecutor may indict the CFO for fiscal fraud, with imprisonment potential.
Fictitious Invoicing and AML Offences (High-Risk Special License)

Statutory sanction: False invoicing (Law 1778/2016, Art. 286 E.T.): prison 60-144 months and a fine up to COP 1,500 million (approx. USD 360,000); aggravated money laundering (Criminal Code Art. 323-325): prison 10-30 years, fine up to 50,000 minimum wages; DIAN may also apply a 100% penalty on the disallowed deduction, and the proceeds are confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors, factoring officers and beneficial owners face personal money-laundering prosecution; broad personal asset confiscation under the unexplained-wealth procedure; mandatory exit bans and a preventive prison during trial for the legal representative who signed the false invoices.
Regulatory Violation — Unlicensed Medical Device Manufacturing

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to COP 1,000 million (approx. USD 240,000), product confiscation, closure of the plant, cancellation of the registry and prohibition to contract with the Colombian state; counterfeit or adulterated device offences attract prison 48-144 months under Criminal Code Arts. 372-373; INVIMA additionally reports the matter to DIAN for the 35% tax recharacterization of undeclared sales.

Director personal liability: Under Decree 4725/2005 and the INVIMA sanctioning regime, the legal representative is jointly liable for the unauthorised manufacturing activity; personal fines, travel embargoes, public blacklisting and disqualification from the health-products sector apply; the corporate veil of the SAS is pierced because the business is operated without the mandatory special license.

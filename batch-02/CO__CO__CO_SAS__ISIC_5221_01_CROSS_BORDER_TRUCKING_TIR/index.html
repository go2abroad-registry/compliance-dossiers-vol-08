Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – National Tax and Customs Directorate; official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co (electronic services portal: https://muisca.dian.gov.co)

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian SAS, the legal representative must obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) as part of the company's registration procedure. This is effected by submitting the Certificate of Incorporation and the tax/legal registration form through the DIAN virtual office (MUISCA) or at an authorized DIAN branch. The SAS is then enrolled in the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) and a permanent NIT is assigned. Non-resident service providers receiving Colombian-source income are also mandatorily registered in the RUT through a local proxy/tax representative and are attributed an NIT. An electronic signature certificate (Decreto 2242/2015) must be obtained for DIAN web-service clearance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Colombia has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch pass-through election for corporate entities. However, qualifying SAS companies may elect the simplified taxation regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación – SIMPLE) under Law 2010 of 2019 if turnover and other conditions are met; a group of related entities may elect to file consolidated income tax under the régimen de consolidación; and exporters may elect to claim periodic VAT refunds rather than awaiting credit carry-forward. For customs, the TIR carnet procedure is an authorized customs transit election that does not alter VAT classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – Colombian SAS legal entities are unconditionally subject to VAT registration as responsables de IVA (régimen común) regardless of turnover. For natural persons qualifying for the simplified regime, the annual VAT threshold is 3,500 UVT (annual UVT value adjusted; approx. COP 180 million for reference).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated (excluded) from VAT. For cross-border trucking under TIR, the service of international land cargo transport performed for a foreign recipient from Colombian territory to a foreign destination qualifies as an export service under Article 481 of the Estatuto Tributario and Article 99 Integrity TIR Doctrine, provided (i) the service is directly linked to goods leaving Colombia, (ii) the transport provider is a Colombian SAS with RUT enrollment, (iii) an export declaration is lodged with DIAN customs, and (iv) the transport is documented under a valid TIR carnét. Foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated within 180 days from the date of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación / DEX) validated by DIAN
  • TIR Carnet (Carné TIR) evidencing the bonded overland transit
  • Bill of Lading / Cargo Manifest (Regular Manifest or LCM) for road freight
  • Packing List (Lista de Empaque) and commercial invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas) from a Colombian bank
  • Certificado de Facturación Electrónica de Exportación (if applicable)

Mandatory under Resolución DIAN 000165/2023: all VAT-responsible taxpayers must issue factura electrónica de venta through authorized technology providers, transmitting each invoice to the DIAN in real time via web services/API. Each accepted invoice obtains a CUNE (Código Único de Numeración de Factura) and must carry a digital signature (QR + CUDE). Fiscal clearance is thereby effected ex ante to operation, with no printed non-electronic invoice allowed for B2B/B2C operations concerning export freight services.

DIAN may audit the export dossier under fiscal-risk criteria. Failure to substantiate zero-rating gives rise to the obligation to pay VAT omitted plus monthly default interest. For deliberate export misdeclaration, the sanction for 'exportaciones ficticias' (fictitious exports) is a penalty of 160% of the tax evaded, alongside retroactive clawback of VAT refunds and prohibition to offset credits in subsequent periods; criminal tax fraud liability may attach under Article 108 of the Código Penal. Statutes of limitation for DIAN assessment are five years, extendible where the return has not been filed or presents anomalies.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is convertible under a floating exchange-rate regime with a market reference rate (TRM - Tasa Representativa del Mercado) published by BANREP. Current-account proceeds from cross-border TIR freight (export of transport services) are freely convertible and can be kept in COP or USD at an IMC, but all flows exceeding USD 10,000 per calendar month per concept must be channeled through an IMC. Capital-account foreign direct investment (FDI) requires no prior license, but every inbound equity contribution must be registered with BANREP (Formulario No. 4 - Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales) within 6 months of disbursement; full USD or EUR contributions are permitted. Dividend and capital repatriation to non-resident shareholders is allowed once the FDI registration is updated and the applicable withholding is settled (the statutory dividend withholding for non-residents currently being 10% on distributions sourced from taxed earnings, subject to treaty relief, with no separate remittance tax). Practical hurdles are documentary rather than discriminatory: each foreign payment over the de minimis threshold must be reported and backed by invoices and transport documentation; there is no forced conversion of export FX into COP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombia does not impose forced surrender or mandatory conversion of export currencies. Instead, the law imposes a collection-and-channeling obligation: (i) proceeds from international freight services must be collected within 6 months of the invoice date or completion of service, extendable once for a further 6 months with the IMC's authorization; (ii) the full monthly amount exceeding USD 10,000 per concept must be channeled through an IMC; (iii) collection must be declared on Formulario No. 1 - Declaración de Cambio por Servicios, Transferencias y Otros Conceptos using the official code for international freight services; (iv) the exporter must retain the underlying transport contract, CMR/TIR Carnet manifest, commercial invoice, SWIFT confirmation and the foreign debtor's proof of payment for at least 5 years. Non-compliance, late collection, or channeling through non-IMC entities attracts exchange sanctions (multas cambiarias) under Law 9 of 1991 and Circular DCIN-83.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (BANREP) is the central bank and principal foreign-exchange authority, acting through its Junta Directiva; the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) supervises the authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries (Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario, IMC) and enforces AML/CFT and SAGRILAFT rules; DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) controls customs and the TIR Carnet at the borders. The binding legal instruments are Law 9 of 1991, Decreto 1735 de 1993 (Régimen de Cambios Internacionales), Circular Reglamentaria Externa DCIN-83 of BANREP, and the SFC's Circular Básica Jurídica.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contributions must arrive by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank routing) directly to the tier-1 IMC in the corporate name of the SAS; SWIFT field 70 (Remittance Information) must state: 'APORTE CAPITAL SOCIAL - INVERSION EXTRANJERA DIRECTA - SAS ISIC 5221'. The IMC completes the Formulario No. 4 - Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales with the direct investment code for the real sector, and BANREP's electronic platform (SINEO) is used for the Registro de Inversión Internacional within 6 months of the disbursement. Verification protocol: reconcile the SWIFT wire to the signed capital contribution minutes (acta de asamblea) and the bank's Certificado de Canalización; retain the payment order, SWIFT confirmation, and FDI registration certificate for 5 years. Routing through a personal account, a third-party, or an unlicensed EMI voids the FDI registration and exposes the company to exchange sanctions.Local disbursements: interbank low-value clearing via ACH Colombia (CUD) and bank-issued fuel cards settled through local card rails for domestic fuel, tolls (TAG), repairs, and payroll. International disbursements: SWIFT MT103 for fuel suppliers, toll authorities, border fees, insurance premiums, TIR Carnet fees, and driver payroll in transit countries; MT101/MT202 batch messaging for consolidated payments. Commercial letters of credit (MT700) are the appropriate rail for capital asset imports (tractors, trailers, spare parts) when a guarantee instrument is required, whereas operational payments should use plain MT103 wires to minimize friction. Every foreign payment exceeding USD 10,000 per month per concept must be reported via Formulario No. 1 and paired with the electronic invoice, route manifest, and TIR/CDF transit evidence; deemed FX conversions use the official TRM of the transaction date and the IMC must issue the corresponding Certificado de Canalización.COP/USD deliverable forward contracts (Forward TRM) with an IMC - requires documentary proof of underlying USD inflow from freight operations (contracts and invoices) and is settled through Formulario No. 1; typically collateralized with a cash margin in COP.COP/USD non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) with SFC-regulated dealers - interbank standard for non-COP obligations, cash-settled in COP against TRM without physical exchange of principal; subject to the same underlying exposure test and master agreement.USD/COP cross-currency swaps - permissible under the capital-account derivative framework to match the currency profile of USD-denominated external financing for fleet or working capital.USD/COP options (call/put options on TRM) - allowed for corporate hedging at SFC-licensed brokerage firms, with premium payable in COP and documentation supporting the hedged foreign currency risk.Interest rate swaps (fixed/floating in COP or USD) - permissible as a treasury overlay for the fleet capex program, executed under an ISDA/CCIL-style master agreement with the IMC and supported by the underlying loan and cash-flow projections.

Declaración de Renta y Complementarios (Annual Corporate Income Tax Return) Filing window in April of each year for the preceding fiscal year; the exact due date is assigned by DIAN according to the last two digits of the NIT. $0 USD Delayed filing incurs a monthly penalty of 5% of the tax payable, capped at 100% of the tax due; unpaid taxes attract daily default interest, and continuing default can block the RUT for customs and foreign trade operations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% of gross dividends distributed to a non-resident shareholder out of Colombian-source profits already subject to the 35% corporate income tax (E.T. Art. 246-2). Dividends paid from exempt untaxed profits are subject to a 20% withholding, unless the beneficiary is a resident of a DTA-limited tax jurisdiction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For tax year 2024, the Local File is compulsory where the taxpayer's gross income exceeds COP 21,179,250,000 (450,000 UVT; UVT 2024 = COP 47,065) AND transactions with related parties exceed 150,000 UVT; or when the taxpayer has assets exceeding 450,000 UVT and carries out related-party transactions. The Master File is required when the MNE group's consolidated gross income exceeds COP 70,597,500,000 (1,500,000 UVT). A Country-by-Country Report is filed by ultimate parents with consolidated group revenue above COP 1,547,460,000,000. TP return is Form 2281, prepared in COP.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply a Caribbean ESR statute, but fiscal substance is essential for treaty access and anti-abuse protection. A cross-border trucking S.A.S. must evidence real premises, a local board or commercial mandate, own or leased fleet, local drivers with Colombian labor contracts, fuel and maintenance accounts, TIR Carnet operational control, and commercial decision-making in Colombia. DIAN may apply the sham transaction doctrine (E.T. Art. 55) or the GAAR to disregard an entity lacking substance, reattributing income to the true foreign principal. In addition, CFC rules may attribute passive income of controlled foreign corporations to Colombian shareholders.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty: 4 to 9 years imprisonment under Colombian Criminal Code Art. 402 plus a fine of up to 1,000 UVT. Tax assessment: official liquidated tax, penalty of 160% of the evaded tax, default interest, cancellation of customs and TIR authorization.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives are solidarily liable with the company under E.T. Arts. 793-794. Travel bans, attachment of personal assets, freezing of bank accounts, disqualification from managing corporations for up to 5 years, and in severe cases preventive detention are available.
Transfer pricing non-disclosure

Statutory sanction: Official primary adjustment plus secondary adjustment; penalty of 2% of the value of each undocumented transaction with a floor of 300 UVT and a ceiling of 3,000 UVT (E.T. Art. 658-3); default interest at the monthly DIAN rate; possibility of criminal prosecution if concealment is proven.

Director personal liability: The director who signs the TP certificate and the accountant who underwrites the study may be personally liable for 100% of the penalty if they knowingly use false data. Professional suspension by the Central Board of Accountants and a personal travel ban can be imposed. An international arrest warrant may follow in cross-border fraud cases.
Shell company / sham entity

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the entity under E.T. Art. 55, include 100% of the revenue in the true Colombia-based taxpayer's income, apply the 35% tax plus a 100%-160% fraud penalty, cancel the company's NIT and impose fines of up to 100,000 UVT. The company may be struck from the Cámara de Comercio registry.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: directors become personally liable for taxes, penalties and interest. In addition, criminal proceedings may be opened for false accounting and document forgery; directors face up to 8 years imprisonment, a travel ban, and forfeiture of assets under Colombia's confiscation regime (extinción de dominio).
Withholding tax evasion on profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: The paying company is liable as withholding agent for 100% of the unpaid withholding tax, plus monthly default interest and a penalty of 160% of the amount not withheld (E.T. Art. 651). The Colombian manager may also be charged with the misdemeanor of non-compliance with withholding obligations and receive a custodial sentence under Art. 402.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid WHT. Their personal assets shall be attached; they are subject to a travel ban until payment and may be extradited to Colombia if the beneficiary of the illegal distribution resides overseas and the conduct constitutes cross-border tax fraud.
Money laundering and trade-based AML

Statutory sanction: Laundering of assets (Ley 1766/2016, Art. 323 C.P.): 10 to 15 years imprisonment and a fine of 50,000 minimum wages (approx. COP 130 billion). The entity loses its Aduana cart authorization and TIR Carnet accreditation; operating permits are suspended; assets are subject to confiscation and an 'extinción de dominio' action.

Director personal liability: Directors face pretrial detention, preventive seizure of personal bank accounts and properties, international travel bans, and a pending arrest warrant if they leave the country. On conviction, they serve the term of imprisonment, must pay civil restitution to the freight clients and the State, and are permanently disqualified from holding a commercial leadership position in any Colombian company.

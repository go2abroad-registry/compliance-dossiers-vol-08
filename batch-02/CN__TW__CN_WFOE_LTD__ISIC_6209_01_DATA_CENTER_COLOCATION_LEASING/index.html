Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Taiwan Company Act does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a private limited company, and no blocked escrow account is required. The registered capital must nevertheless be fully paid by remittance into a Taiwan bank account established in the company's name, and a certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report evidencing receipt before the incorporation filing is accepted. The foreign investor must also comply with the approved investment amount stated in the Foreign Investment Approval Certificate.

Funding & unlock path: The operator must obtain a Fire Safety Certification under the Fire Services Act, including the fire safety equipment certificate and annual fire safety management plan filed with the local fire department. The building must pass a structural load inspection and receive a Building Use Permit for the data center occupancy class. Workplace safety approval is required under the Occupational Safety and Health Act: electrical installations must be inspected by a licensed electrical contractor, battery rooms must be ventilated with hydrogen detection and eye-wash stations, emergency exits and alarms must be tested, and a workplace accident insurance policy must be in force. If the building is required to provide civil defense shelter space under Taiwanese building regulations, the operator must preserve that designated space and include it in the emergency response plan.

Paid-in capital rule: None. Taiwan Company Act does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a private limited company, and no blocked escrow account is required. The registered capital must nevertheless be fully paid by remittance into a Taiwan bank account established in the company's name, and a certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report evidencing receipt before the incorporation filing is accepted. The foreign investor must also comply with the approved investment amount stated in the Foreign Investment Approval Certificate.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance (MOF) of Taiwan, exercised through the regional National Taxation Bureaus; the online registration and electronic filing portal is the MOF Tax Entry Portal (etax.nat.gov.tw), supported by the MOF e-Invoice Integration Service Platform for indirect tax administration.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise formed as a Taiwan limited company, the foreign investor must first obtain investment approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs under the Foreign Investment Act, then complete company incorporation and business registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs under the Company Act. Following approval, the company must apply to the regional National Taxation Bureau for Business Tax and profit-seeking enterprise tax registration to obtain the statutory Unified Business Number, which serves as the tax identification number for VAT, corporate income tax, electronic invoices, customs declarations, and employment matters. The application package includes the investment permit, articles of incorporation, company registration certificate, legal representative identification, business premises lease or ownership evidence, bank account proof, and a signed authorization for tax affairs. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a Taiwan establishment is not granted a Unified Business Number; instead, Taiwan-source payments are handled through withholding, and the non-resident may receive a temporary tax identity for filing purposes where required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the WFOE Ltd is treated as a Taiwan-resident profit-seeking enterprise subject to corporate income tax on its taxable profits; it is not a pass-through entity and has no statutory pass-through election.

Available election forms

None. Taiwan law does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election or a hybrid mismatch election for a WFOE Ltd; the entity must file as a profit-seeking enterprise under the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold applies to incorporated entities: every business engaged in taxable activity in Taiwan must complete Business Tax registration before commencing operations. Small-scale businesses with average monthly taxable turnover not exceeding NT$200,000 may qualify for a special presumptive business tax regime, but a data-center colocation and bare-metal server hosting WFOE is required to be fully VAT-registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated Business Tax applies to goods exported from Taiwan where the goods leave Taiwan customs territory under a valid customs export declaration and the export proceeds are repatriated in foreign exchange. Zero-rating also applies to services supplied to foreign enterprises without a Taiwan fixed place of business, or to foreign individuals, where the services are used or consumed outside Taiwan and the consideration is received in foreign exchange. For data-center colocation and bare-metal server hosting, physical rack space, power, and cooling located in Taiwan are deemed consumed in Taiwan and remain taxable at 5%; zero-rating is available only for separately documented qualifying offshore services such as remote management of servers located outside Taiwan or subscription services delivered and consumed entirely outside Taiwan, provided the foreign-exchange and service-usage evidence is substantiated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export customs report issued by Taiwan Customs)
  • Ocean or air Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing export movement
  • Commercial invoice for the exported goods or offshore services
  • Packing list (for physical goods)
  • Bank foreign-exchange settlement certificate / Inward Remittance Advice evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Certificate of origin or export permit where applicable
  • Service contract or performance evidence substantiating offshore use for zero-rated services

The business must issue a statutory Uniform Invoice for every taxable sale. Under the Electronic Uniform Invoice regime, VAT-registered businesses use the MOF e-Invoice Integration Service Platform, connecting through a certified Turnkey service provider or a value-added e-invoice service provider. E-invoices must carry the serialized statutory Uniform Invoice number, include the buyer’s Unified Business Number where required, be transmitted to the Ministry of Finance platform for fiscal control and audit access, and be available for real-time inquiry by the National Taxation Bureau. There is no physical fiscal stamp, but digital transmission and preservation of the e-invoice records are mandatory.

The National Taxation Bureau and Taiwan Customs may audit the complete documentary chain, including customs declarations, transport documents, invoices, contracts, and bank foreign-exchange records. If a zero-rating claim is not substantiated within the statutory deadline or the export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days without an approved extension, the tax authority will reassess the Business Tax at 5% on the unsubstantiated receipts, impose delinquency interest, and may levy administrative fines under the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act. In cases of fraudulent invoices, fictitious export declarations, or deliberate evasion, the responsible person may be subject to criminal prosecution under the Tax Evasion and Punishment Act, and retrospective clawbacks for prior periods will be assessed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current account convertibility is effectively granted to trade-in-services flows: data center colocation and bare-metal hosting fees received from Taiwan and other non-resident clients qualify as computer and information services income and may be settled through a domestic Foreign Exchange Settlement Account upon submission of the service contract, commercial invoice, proof of service delivery, and tax payment certificates. Capital account items remain restricted: FDI equity injections into the WFOE require prior Foreign Investment Information Registration and SAFE verification through the designated bank, with funds confined to the registered business scope and the negative list discipline; use of capital proceeds for equity investment, entrusted lending, or non-operating expenditures is prohibited. Profit remittance to the Taiwan shareholder is permissible after the annual audit, full corporate income tax settlement, and completion of the SAFE Foreign Exchange Remittance Application, which is processed on a true-and-legal basis. The New Taiwan Dollar (TWD) is not a convertible onshore settlement currency in mainland China; cross-strait receipts are therefore routed through USD or RMB via the Cross-Strait RMB clearing mechanism. Overall, the regime is best characterized as full current-account convertibility with post-event verification, and controlled capital-account convertibility with bank-mediated administration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio applies. China abolished compulsory foreign exchange surrender for export proceeds in 2012. For the WFOE's service exports (ISIC 6209 colocation receivables), foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic designated settlement account and reported through the international balance-of-payments statistical regime (涉外收支申报) within five banking days of credit. Funds may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency, converted at market rate at the exporter's discretion, or applied directly to current account disbursements. No minimum percentage is mandated for conversion into RMB. Cross-strait Taiwan-origin proceeds are additionally captured under the cross-strait trade settlement statistical framework but are not subject to surrender ratios.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the latter being the statutory foreign exchange administration under the PBoC's authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rated Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currenxie, PingPong, and WorldFirst are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in mainland China and cannot open domestic RMB settlement accounts, cannot receive FDI capital injection proceeds, and cannot discharge the WFOE's SAFE reporting obligations. Their practical role is limited to offshore collection of Taiwan and global colocation client fees in USD, HKD, or CNH, followed by remittance to the WFOE's domestic bank account via MT103. Legal constraints include the requirement that cross-border payment institutions hold a PBoC Payment License or operate under the NRA/OSA framework; for B2B service receipts of a regulated nature, over-reliance on an unlicensed EMI is inadvisable from an audit-integrity and tax-verification perspective. The recommended architecture is a domestic bank primary account with an offshore EMI used only as a sub-collection rail for small recurring fees.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant FDI routing sequence: (i) the Taiwan shareholder executes the equity subscription agreement and the WFOE completes the Foreign Investment Information Report with the market regulatory authority and the SAFE Foreign Investment Registration through the settlement bank; (ii) the WFOE opens a SAFE-designated Capital Account (资本金账户) at a tier-1 Chinese commercial bank; (iii) the inward remittance is transmitted via SWIFT MT103 from the Taiwan shareholder's bank, using SAFE BOP declaration code 621011 (外国来华直接投资——投资资本金, Foreign Direct Investment capital injection). The receiving bank authenticates the underlying, verifies the registration quota, credits the Capital Account, and enters the transaction into the SAFE Foreign Exchange Administration System. In pilot free-trade zones, the capital-income payment convenience policy enables direct payment of wages, rent, utilities, and taxes without per-transaction prior approval, subject to a 30-day post-event documentary review. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rated Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currenxie, PingPong, and WorldFirst are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in mainland China and cannot open domestic RMB settlement accounts, cannot receive FDI capital injection proceeds, and cannot discharge the WFOE's SAFE reporting obligations. Their practical role is limited to offshore collection of Taiwan and global colocation client fees in USD, HKD, or CNH, followed by remittance to the WFOE's domestic bank account via MT103. Legal constraints include the requirement that cross-border payment institutions hold a PBoC Payment License or operate under the NRA/OSA framework; for B2B service receipts of a regulated nature, over-reliance on an unlicensed EMI is inadvisable from an audit-integrity and tax-verification perspective. The recommended architecture is a domestic bank primary account with an offshore EMI used only as a sub-collection rail for small recurring fees.

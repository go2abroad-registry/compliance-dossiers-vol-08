Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: www.dian.gov.co; taxpayer e-services portal: muisca.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.): (1) appoint a local legal representative; (2) obtain the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) before DIAN, either electronically or at a DIAN service center; (3) the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) is automatically assigned upon RUT issuance; (4) update the RUT to reflect 'Responsable de IVA' status for VAT purposes; (5) activate mandatory electronic invoicing authorisation before commencing taxable operations; (6) if engaging in international trade, register the NIT as customs code with DIAN and obtain a digital signature.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. An S.A.S. is a separate taxpayer subject to Colombian corporate income tax and VAT. A qualifying small enterprise may elect the Régimen Simple de Tributación (RS) under Articles 905–930 of the Colombian Tax Code if gross turnover does not exceed 80,000 UVT per year; this election replaces standard corporate income tax and certain local taxes, but VAT obligations remain applicable where required. Hybrid mismatch rules under Law 2010 of 2019 apply automatically and do not require a taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for S.A.S. legal entities: VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable activity, regardless of turnover. The simplified-taxpayer threshold of 3,500 UVT per year applies only to natural persons and is not available to S.A.S.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a sale qualifies for the 0% IVA rate when the goods are physically exported from Colombian customs territory under a valid export declaration and the seller is a registered VAT taxpayer. Services: a service may qualify as zero-rated if it qualifies as an 'exported service' (servicio exportado) — rendered to a non-resident recipient, not connected to any local establishment, and used exclusively abroad. Cold-storage warehousing physically performed in Colombia is generally taxable unless DIAN interprets the service as forming part of an integrated export logistics chain supplied to a foreign principal with economic benefit abroad; the taxpayer must prove foreign receipt and benefit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX / SAD)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (BL / AWB)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Comprobante de Liquidación de Divisas issued by an authorised Colombian foreign-exchange intermediary

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing under Decree 2242 of 2015 and DIAN Resolution 000165 of 2022. Invoices must be generated by DIAN-authorised software or through certified e-invoicing service providers, transmitted electronically to DIAN for validation before issue, and include the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación) and a QR code. Credit/debit notes and 'Nota de Ajuste' documents must also be transmitted electronically. Taxpayers must submit exógena information returns and may be subject to operational audits through DIAN's e-invoicing transaction-monitoring system.

DIAN may reassess zero-rated exports if the documentation is incomplete or if the foreign-exchange repatriation deadline is missed. Consequences include: disallowance of the 0% rate and imposition of 19% VAT with interest, an inaccuracy penalty equal to 160% of the omitted VAT for fraudulent or inaccurate returns, customs-fraud penalties (including goods seizure or fines), and retroactive clawback within the ordinary five-year statute of limitations. Criminal liability may attach for false customs declarations and fraudulent export-documentation schemes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) operates under a managed float with the representative market rate (TRM) published by the Banco de la República. COP is fully convertible for current-account transactions and for foreign direct investment (capital account) inflows and outflows, but every foreign-exchange transaction must be channelled through the Mercado Cambiario using an authorized IMC and recorded via an electronic Declaración de Cambio. Profit remittances (dividends) and capital repatriation are freely permissible once the foreign investment is registered with the Banco de la República and applicable Colombian taxes have been settled. There is no pre-approval license for the initial equity remittance, but the remitter must match the registered investor; mischaracterized or unregistered transfers risk being classified as illegal exchange operations under Law 9 of 1991.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the warehousing and refrigerated logistics services (ISIC 5210) billed to non-residents, 100% of the foreign-currency proceeds must be channelled through an IMC and reported on Declaración de Cambio Form No. 3 at the moment of collection; the proceeds must be received within 3 months following the service performance or billing event, subject to limited extensions granted by the Banco de la República. For any ancillary goods exports, Form No. 1 applies, requiring 100% reintegro within 3 months of the export declaration. There is no mandatory conversion into COP; the exporter may retain foreign currency in a foreign-currency account at the IMC, whereas amounts held abroad are permitted only through a pre-authorized cuenta de compensación.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), acting jointly with the Ministerio de Hacienda y Crédito Público; day-to-day administration and enforcement are exercised through the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) and the authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries (IMCs) under Decree 1068 of 2015 and Resolución Externa No. 1 of 2018 (Régimen Cambiario), as amended.

The blueprint permits EMI rails only for non-Colombian disbursement legs after conversion through the primary IMC.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% flat corporate income tax (Art. 240 E.T., as amended by Law 2277 of 2022); no progressive brackets for legal entities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding on dividends paid from profits that have already borne the 35% CIT; 35% withholding on distributions sourced from untaxed or exempt profits (with a further 10-percentage-point surcharge applicable in specific high-tier distributions); Colombian-resident corporate shareholders are exempt from WHT on dividends from taxed profits.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within the calendar year following the tax period, between early August and mid-September, under the annual DIAN deadline schedule keyed to the last two digits of the NIT (approximately 8 months after the December 31 year-end). For tax year 2024 the window spanned August 11 - September 9, 2025.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File (annual Form 220 transfer-pricing report) when the taxpayer's gross equity or gross income in the preceding tax year exceeded COP 100,000 million (≈ USD 25 million). Transactions with related parties in tax havens or preferential regimes must be documented regardless of the threshold. The Country-by-Country report is due where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeded COP 1,200,000 million (≈ USD 300 million). Documentation must be prepared in Spanish and delivered to DIAN within audit deadlines (typically 10-20 business days upon request).

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply Caribbean-style ESR statutes, but DIAN applies the general anti-abuse rule (Art. 869 E.T.) and the substance-over-form doctrine. A cold-storage SAS lacking real premises, operational personnel, incumbent risk management, and a genuine commercial purpose may be disregarded as a shell, with profits reattributed to the actual economic owner and the corporate veil lifted. Anti-shelter scrutiny is heightened for entities transacting with foreign related parties in low-tax jurisdictions, and the 2022 CFC rules further tax passive income of controlled entities abroad.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: DIAN inaccuracy penalty of 160% of the omitted tax (Art. 647 E.T.) plus moratory interest; criminal evasion under Art. 434A of the Colombian Criminal Code carries imprisonment of 36 to 108 months and fines quantified in UVT; the tax authority may also order the confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Administrators and de facto managers who authorized the fraudulent return are jointly liable under Art. 793 E.T. for the unremitted tax, face criminal prosecution as co-authors, and may be subject to precautionary travel bans and disqualification from managing commercial companies.

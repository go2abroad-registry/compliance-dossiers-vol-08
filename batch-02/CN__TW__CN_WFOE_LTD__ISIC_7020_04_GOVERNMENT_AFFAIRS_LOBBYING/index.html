Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a general consultancy limited company under the Taiwan Company Act; however, the capital must be fully subscribed and remitted from abroad in foreign currency into a designated escrow bank account in Taiwan before registration, and the amount must be reasonably commensurate with the business plan. The escrowed funds may be released for business operations only after the company registration certificate has been issued.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taxation Administration (財政部賦稅署) and the National Taxation Bureau of the local jurisdiction; electronic portal: 'Taxation E-Government Portal' (電子稅務入口網) and 'E-Invoice Platform' (電子發票整合服務平台)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in Taiwan must first complete company incorporation with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) to obtain a Company Registration Certificate. Subsequently, the entity must apply for a Business Registration Certificate (營業登記) and a Uniform Number (統一編號) at the local National Taxation Bureau. The application requires submission of the incorporation documents, lease agreement for the registered office, identification of the responsible person, and a completed Business Registration Application Form. The tax authority will issue the Uniform Number, which serves as the Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all tax filings, including VAT and income tax. For non-resident entities without a fixed place of business, a tax representative must be appointed to handle registration and compliance.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Monthly sales exceeding NT$200,000 (New Taiwan Dollars) triggers mandatory VAT registration; however, for a WFOE engaged in government affairs lobbying, registration is typically required from the outset due to the nature of services and expected turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Value-Added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act, the following are eligible for zero-rating: (1) export of goods, (2) services provided to foreign clients and used outside Taiwan, (3) services rendered in connection with export trade, and (4) international transport. For the WFOE providing government affairs lobbying and cross-border trade policy analysis, zero-rating applies if the services are provided to a foreign client (i.e., the recipient is a non-resident) and the services are not used within Taiwan. The service must be performed in Taiwan but the benefit must accrue to the foreign client. The WFOE must retain evidence of the foreign client's identity and the nature of the services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口報單) for goods; for services, a detailed invoice and contract
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (for goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate (外匯結售證明) evidencing receipt of foreign currency
  • Service contract and proof of service delivery (for services)

Taiwan mandates the use of electronic invoices (E-Invoice) for businesses that meet certain criteria, including those with annual sales above NT$30 million or those designated by the tax authority. The E-Invoice system requires real-time transmission of invoice data to the E-Invoice Platform (整合服務平台) via API or web interface. For anti-fraud purposes, the platform assigns a unique invoice number and performs validation. However, there is no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping requirement for all businesses; the mandate applies to specific sectors and sizes. The WFOE must adopt E-Invoicing if it exceeds the threshold or if the tax authority requires it.

The tax authority may audit export zero-rating claims. If the required documentation is incomplete or the foreign exchange is not repatriated within 180 days, the zero-rating is disallowed, and the VAT becomes payable at 5% with interest. Fraudulent claims or deliberate misrepresentation may result in penalties up to 5 times the tax evaded, plus criminal liability under the Tax Evasion and Penalty Act. Additionally, the WFOE may face retrospective clawback of any VAT refunds received, along with fines and suspension of business operations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains de jure current-account convertibility: service-trade fees, consulting retainers, and dividend remittances are convertible upon bank verification of authentic contracts, invoices, and tax clearance. The capital account remains regulated; FDI equity subscription and external debt require SAFE registration or filing before bank execution. A Taiwan-invested WFOE receives FDI capital into a dedicated FX capital account (外汇资本金账户) and may convert 100% of funds under the voluntary settlement (意愿结汇) regime, subject to the SAFE negative list prohibiting use for securities investment, entrusted loans, land purchases, or domestic equity acquisition. Dividend remittance requires (1) annual statutory audit, (2) corporate income tax clearance, (3) a board resolution approving distribution, and (4) conversion of surplus into CNY at prevailing market rates before the offshore transfer; the full chain normally takes 10–15 business days across tax filing, bank enhanced due diligence, and SWIFT settlement. The enterprise is additionally protected by the Law on the Protection of Investment by Taiwan Compatriots, which guarantees equal profit-remittance treatment, while the FDI Negative List (2021 edition) does not categorically prohibit advisory and public-relations services for government affairs, provided the registered scope excludes social investigation (社会调查) and other prohibited industries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exporters, proceeds must be repatriated to China and generally received within 210 days after the customs export declaration, with a one-off extension available through the bank for longer payment terms. Mandatory conversion ratio: none; China abolished compulsory FX surrender in 2012, and exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in FX settlement accounts or convert voluntarily at market rates. For this service-oriented WFOE with no physical exports, the applicable rule is that cross-border service fees (Taiwan parent recharge, retainer income from foreign clients) must be received in the FX settlement account (外汇结算账户) within the contractual payment term, supported by service contracts and invoices; receipts are freely retainable or convertible, with a 0% mandatory conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (中国人民银行, PBOC), the central bank with primary monetary and settlement policy authority, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (国家外汇管理局, SAFE), the exclusive FX regulator administering current-account verification and capital-account registration. Cross-strait RMB clearing operates through the Taipei RMB Clearing Bank (Bank of China, Taipei Branch) under the 2012 Cross-Strait Currency Clearing Memorandum.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail and legally impermissible as a substitute for SAFE-registered banking. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Currenxie, Payoneer, Wise Business, and similar) are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE as deposit-taking institutions; they cannot hold capital-escrow accounts, cannot open corporate basic RMB accounts, and cannot execute the SAFE 'Business Registration Certificate' workflow required for FDI. Their lawful onshore role is limited to auxiliary collection of small-value service-trade receivables for convenience, followed by a true-up transfer into a licensed Chinese bank account; they are wholly unsuitable for capital injection, retainer clearing, hedging, or dividend distribution. Any domestic substitute must be a PBOC-licensed payment institution, and cross-border RMB payment services require a specific filing; this does not replace the tier-1 banking layer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) the designated bank completes the SAFE direct-investment information registration and issues the Business Registration Certificate before any wire; (2) the Taiwan parent remits via SWIFT MT103 with the receiving bank's BOP receipt purpose code 622971 (foreign-investment capital contribution) to the WFOE's dedicated FX capital account; if remitted in RMB, the funds are routed through the Taipei RMB Clearing Bank under the same registration certificate; (3) the receiving bank verifies the certificate ID, remitter identity against the capital-subscription contract, the board resolution on capital contribution, and the paid-in-capital schedule before crediting the restricted capital; (4) voluntary FX conversion is executed against a signed Use Declaration covering salaries, rent, procurement, and office operating costs, with converted RMB credited to the domestic expenditure account; (5) the full injection package (FDI information report, capital-account statements, MT103 confirmations, and FX transaction notes) is archived for five years to satisfy PBOC/SAFE post-event audits.For the WFOE's own service imports (Taiwan parent recharges for policy data, Hong Kong counsel opinions, EU research): SWIFT MT103 with BOP payment purpose codes 228033/223019 (consulting and professional services), supported by invoice, service contract, and proof-of-consumption, cleared through the bank's CNAPS front-end for the local-currency leg. For trade settlements related to advised clients, preferred rails are: (a) open-account settlements via SWIFT MT103 against customs declarations and commercial invoices; (b) irrevocable commercial letters of credit (sight or usance 30–90 days) issued by the counterparty's issuing bank and confirmed or advised by the WFOE's bank with electronic-bill-of-lading matching; and (c) bank payment obligations on SWIFT BP/TSU rails where both counterparties are onboarded. Every outbound payment must carry a valid six-digit SAFE/BOP purpose code and pass trade-based-money-laundering checks, including invoice-price reasonableness, bill-of-lading consistency, and counterparty whitelist verification before release.Onshore deliverable foreign-exchange forwards (远期结售汇) in USD/CNY, EUR/CNY, and where bank quotes exist TWD/CNY, permitted only against a genuine current-account transaction (retainer income, dividend distribution, service import) with documentary proof; naked speculative positions are prohibited.Plain-vanilla foreign-exchange options (外汇期权), including European-style options and option combinations, permissible for corporate risk management on a real trade or repayment background and subject to the bank's appropriateness assessment.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (货币掉期/CCIRS), allowed solely to hedge the interest and principal exposure of a SAFE-registered external foreign-debt facility or other capital-account item; the underlying debt must be registered in the SAFE system before the derivative is booked.Non-deliverable forwards (NDF) are unavailable onshore: the WFOE cannot enter RMB/USD NDF contracts with onshore banks; the offshore Taiwan parent may access CNH NDF markets, but any internal offset arrangement must be at arm's length and will not be recognized by PBOC for onshore hedging, so the pragmatic onshore hedge is a series of short-dated deliverable forwards rolled at settlement.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary treasury rail and legally impermissible as a substitute for SAFE-registered banking. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Currenxie, Payoneer, Wise Business, and similar) are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE as deposit-taking institutions; they cannot hold capital-escrow accounts, cannot open corporate basic RMB accounts, and cannot execute the SAFE 'Business Registration Certificate' workflow required for FDI. Their lawful onshore role is limited to auxiliary collection of small-value service-trade receivables for convenience, followed by a true-up transfer into a licensed Chinese bank account; they are wholly unsuitable for capital injection, retainer clearing, hedging, or dividend distribution. Any domestic substitute must be a PBOC-licensed payment institution, and cross-border RMB payment services require a specific filing; this does not replace the tier-1 banking layer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$50,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transaction amount, with certain lower NT$20,000,000 disclosure thresholds applicable to cross-border service and intangible-property transactions under the Profit-Seeking Enterprise Income Tax Assessment Rules on Transfer Pricing.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan has not codified offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the Income Tax Act's general anti-abuse rules and DTA beneficial-ownership requirements demand real business substance. A government-affairs advisory WFOE must maintain a physical office, qualified professional staff, board decisions, and actual performance of lobbying services in Taiwan; otherwise tax authorities may disregard the Taiwanese entity as a shell and reattribute its profit to the foreign parent or to a permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Income Tax Act, a fine of up to three times the underpaid tax, plus delinquency interest and assessment of omitted tax; criminal tax evasion may lead to imprisonment under the Tax Collection Act and Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors and the responsible officer who sign or direct false returns may be treated as jointly liable for the company's tax debt; the corporate veil may be pierced under Company Act Article 154 where abuse of limited liability causes injustice; personal assets may be attached, a travel ban imposed, and criminal imprisonment may follow.
Cross-Border Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Taiwan's transfer pricing adjustment rules permit the tax authority to reallocate income based on the arm's-length standard, and the underpayment penalty under Article 110 may be imposed up to three times the additional tax; interest and late-payment surcharges accrue.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or approve false intercompany contracts or fail to maintain TP documentation can be held personally responsible for restitution; tax authorities may seize personal property of the designated tax responsible person and impose departure restrictions.
Illegal Lobbying and Bribery of Public Officials

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment and fines under Criminal Code Articles 121–122 and the Anti-Corruption Act; the lobbying service contract is void; all related expenses are nondeductible and may trigger punitive withholding and tax penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or conceal corrupt payments face personal criminal liability, confiscation of assets, civil restitution to the injured party, and a travel ban; the corporate form will not shield principals where bribery is carried out for personal or corporate benefit.
Shell Company / Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under Taiwan's Money Laundering Control Act, including imprisonment for up to seven years, fines, and confiscation of proceeds; the competent ministry may revoke the lobbying business license if AML controls are absent.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are subject to criminal liability, asset freezes, ancillary confiscation orders, and civil damages; courts may disregard the WFOE's separate personality if it is used as an instrumentality of illicit fund movement.
Non-Resident Withholding and Repatriation Defaults

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is personally liable for the uncollected tax and a penalty of up to several times the amount; under Article 88 of the Income Tax Act, the payer is required to withhold and remit, with late remittance attracting daily delinquency interest.

Director personal liability: The designated tax responsible person, usually the managing director or representative, is jointly liable for unpaid withholding tax; tax authorities may pursue the director's personal assets and impose a restriction on leaving Taiwan until the arrears are settled.

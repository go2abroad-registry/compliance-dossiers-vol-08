Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Taiwan does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Limited Company. The sole shareholder must fully subscribe and pay the capital stated in the Articles of Incorporation, and the bank must issue a capital verification certificate. If the shareholder is foreign, the MOEA Investment Commission approval and inward remittance documentation must be furnished; the law does not mandate a blocked escrow account.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance (MOF), Taiwan, acting through the regional National Taxation Bureaus (National Taxation Bureau of Taipei, National Taxation Bureau of Kaohsiung, National Taxation Bureau of Northern Taiwan, National Taxation Bureau of Central Taiwan, and National Taxation Bureau of Southern Taiwan). The statutory electronic portals are the MOF E-Tax Portal (etax.nat.gov.tw) for tax filing and registration administration, and the MOF E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform (einvoice.nat.gov.tw) for Uniform Invoice and e-invoice administration.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: the foreign investor incorporates the Taiwan subsidiary, a wholly foreign-owned limited company under the Taiwan Company Act, through the Ministry of Economic Affairs / Department of Commerce; the company receives an 8-digit Company Registration Number. Step 2: within 15 days after incorporation approval, the company must complete business tax registration with the competent regional National Taxation Bureau by submitting the incorporation certificate, articles of incorporation, and proof of identity and residence of the appointed responsible person. Step 3: the National Taxation Bureau assigns the 8-digit Unified Business Number (UBIN/TIN), which is the single statutory tax identification number used for income tax, business VAT, withholding tax, customs declarations, and electronic invoice reporting. Step 4: the company must apply for permission to purchase and issue Uniform Invoices before effecting any taxable sale in Taiwan. Step 5: for a foreign non-resident enterprise that has not established a Taiwan subsidiary or branch but derives Taiwan-source income, the foreign entity must appoint a designated local withholding agent or tax representative; the agent applies to the National Taxation Bureau for a TIN on behalf of the non-resident principal and registers for the relevant withholding and business-tax obligations. Step 6: if the Taiwan-incorporated entity will export goods, a separate customs export registration code is obtained through the customs filing system; for pure consultancy services under ISIC 7020, no customs registration is required, but the UBIN and Uniform Invoice registration are mandatory before the service revenue is recognized.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Taiwan-incorporated foreign-invested limited company is a separate juridical person and a profit-seeking enterprise taxable at entity level under the Income Tax Act; no pass-through attribute is available).

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not provide a check-the-box pass-through election for limited companies or WFOE-equivalent subsidiaries. A resident company is taxed at entity level. For a foreign enterprise, the only structural election is between operating through a Taiwan branch, which may create a fixed place of business or permanent establishment, and incorporating a separate Taiwan subsidiary. Taiwan has no hybrid mismatch election mechanism under the Income Tax Act for this structure. For VAT, a small-scale business is governed by the simplified 1% assessment where eligible; there is no elective pass-through VAT carve-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

TWD 200,000 per month of taxable sales: businesses whose average monthly taxable sales exceed TWD 200,000 must register as standard VAT taxpayers, issue Uniform Invoices, and charge 5% VAT with entitlement to input VAT credit. Businesses with average monthly sales at or below TWD 200,000 are generally classified as small-scale businesses and assessed at the simplified business tax rate of 1% of taxable sales; those with monthly sales of goods below TWD 80,000 or services below TWD 40,000 may qualify for full business-tax exemption. Registration itself is required before commencing any taxable business, and the TWD 200,000 monthly turnover threshold is the operational threshold distinguishing simplified small-scale taxation from standard 5% VAT with input-credit mechanics.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 7 of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act. For goods exported from Taiwan, the seller must establish physical exit from Taiwan customs territory through a valid customs export declaration. For services, including overseas market-entry consultancy under ISIC 7020, zero-rating applies when the services are supplied to a foreign enterprise or individual, the services are not actually used or enjoyed within Taiwan, and the consideration is received in foreign exchange or the contract requires payment in foreign currency. The consultancy should file the transaction in the zero-rated sales column of the periodic business tax return, allowing associated input VAT to be refunded or offset rather than charged as output VAT.

  • Customs export declaration evidenced through the Taiwan customs single-window system, required for exported goods and generally not applicable to pure consultancy services
  • Commercial invoice and signed consultancy services agreement identifying the overseas client, the scope of services, the delivery milestones, and the non-Taiwan use and benefit of the consultancy
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or courier receipt where goods, samples, or physical deliverables support or accompany the exported service
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Settlement Receipt issued by an authorized foreign-exchange bank in Taiwan evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Digital service delivery evidence, including log data, access records, and online usage reports, where the market-entry consultancy is delivered through digital or information platforms

Taiwan does not operate a real-time fiscal authorization or anti-fraud digital stamping model comparable to Latin American invoice clearance systems. The statutory control mechanism is the Uniform Invoice system: every standard VAT business must issue tax-authority-serialized Uniform Invoices for each taxable sale, retain the invoice sequence, and report sales on the business tax return filed every two-month period. For electronic invoices, the business connects to the MOF E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform through the statutory Turnkey API/interface; e-invoice XML data must be transmitted to the platform within the required reporting cycle and made available to B2B counterparties to support input VAT claims. B2B buyers download e-invoice details through the Turnkey interface for input-credit substantiation. Failure to issue Uniform Invoices, serialization errors, or untransmitted e-invoice data is sanctioned as failure to invoice and exposed to additional tax assessment and penalties.

The National Taxation Bureau applies heightened audit scrutiny to zero-rated export claims. Where goods or services have been treated as zero-rated but the documentation fails to prove physical export, foreign receipt, offshore use, or foreign-exchange repatriation, the zero-rating is reversed and 5% VAT is assessed on the gross sales amount with statutory interest. Under the Tax Collection Act, the assessment period is up to 7 years, extended to 15 years where tax evasion has been committed by fraud or misrepresentation. The Business Tax Act authorizes a penalty of up to five times the underpaid tax for false or improper zero-rating claims. The 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline is an evidentiary requirement for export proceeds; failure to repatriate the export consideration within 180 days without valid regulatory justification will cause the zero-rated claim to be clawed back, and intentional invoice fraud or false export documentation may constitute criminal tax evasion under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act, punishable by imprisonment.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% profit-seeking enterprise income tax. A 5% additional tax applies to current-year earnings retained and not distributed, subject to statutory exceptions. Taiwan-source payments to non-residents are generally subject to withholding at separate statutory rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a non-resident shareholder, subject to any applicable special rule, exemption, or relief. Taiwan-resident corporate shareholders may be subject to different domestic treatment. A Taiwan company must withhold and file the applicable withholding statement when dividends are paid or otherwise made available.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the final income tax return and payment are generally due from 1 May through 31 May of the following year. A taxpayer using a non-calendar fiscal year generally files within five months after the fiscal year-end. Qualifying enterprises generally file provisional income tax for the first six months during 1 September through 30 September.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally filed from 1 September through 30 September for the first six months of the calendar year, with equivalent timing for a non-calendar fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan Local File documentation is generally required where annual net sales and non-operating income reach at least NTD 300 million, or where aggregate related-party transaction amounts reach at least NTD 200 million, subject to statutory exceptions. A Master File is generally required for a Taiwan entity that is part of an MNE group meeting the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold, commonly NTD 27 billion, and associated conditions. Country-by-country reporting is generally linked to the same NTD 27 billion consolidated-revenue threshold and the applicable reporting-entity rules. Even below formal thresholds, arm's-length evidence must be retained.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a single generalized Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless material under transfer-pricing, anti-avoidance, beneficial-ownership, controlled foreign company, withholding-tax, permanent-establishment, and business-purpose rules. The Taiwan entity should maintain local management authority, personnel or demonstrable operating capacity, premises where commercially appropriate, books and records, decision-making evidence, customer and supplier files, deliverables, and bank activity consistent with its claimed functions and risks. A pass-through entity with no personnel, no control over services, and only intercompany invoicing is vulnerable to deductions denial, recharacterization, withholding disputes, and anti-avoidance challenge.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, statutory penalties, interest, estimated assessment, loss of deductions or loss carryforwards, and potential criminal prosecution under Taiwan tax laws. Deliberate evasion may result in imprisonment and criminal fines, with corporate fines or tax recovery also possible.

Director personal liability: The responsible person, director, manager, accountant, or other participant may face criminal prosecution, fines, civil restitution, tax recovery, asset attachment, and restrictions arising from enforcement. Imprisonment and travel restrictions depend on the offense, judicial orders, and enforcement status; the corporate form does not protect intentional personal misconduct.
Non-Arm's-Length Related-Party Charges

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustment to arm's-length income, denial of deductions, imposition of additional corporate income tax, interest, penalties, and possible withholding-tax reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may incur civil liability to the company for approving unsupported payments, breach of fiduciary duties, or tax losses caused by improper transactions. Criminal exposure arises if documentation is knowingly falsified or the arrangement forms part of tax evasion.
Improper Profit Repatriation and Dividend Concealment

Statutory sanction: Dividend recharacterization, 21% withholding-tax assessment where applicable, additional tax, interest, penalties, denial of deductions, and potential foreign-exchange or company-law consequences.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face repayment claims, shareholder or creditor actions, penalties for withholding failures, and criminal exposure if funds were misappropriated or records falsified.
Beneficial Ownership and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, withholding at the domestic rate, back taxes, interest, penalties, information requests, and possible anti-avoidance reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors, tax agents, and signatories may face civil liability and criminal exposure for knowingly false certifications or fraudulent tax claims. The intermediary may also be treated as a conduit.
Permanent Establishment and Unregistered Local Activity

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax, withholding-tax, business-tax, registration, interest, and penalty exposure; possible recharacterization of foreign enterprise income as Taiwan-source income.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face administrative fines, civil claims, and criminal liability where unregistered operations or false filings are deliberate. Immigration and work authorization violations may create separate personal consequences.
Undistributed Earnings Tax and Improper Reinvestment Claim

Statutory sanction: 5% additional tax on disallowed undistributed earnings, interest, penalties, and reassessment of related deductions or incentives.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution and fiduciary-duty claims for approving unsupported distributions or sham investments; intentional false reporting may create criminal exposure.
Withholding-Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of tax from the payer, interest, statutory penalties, filing penalties, and possible liability for failure to submit withholding statements.

Director personal liability: The responsible person and withholding officer may face personal administrative or criminal consequences where non-withholding is intentional, records are falsified, or withheld tax is collected but not remitted.
Transfer-Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, transfer-pricing adjustments, denial of deductions, adverse estimation, additional corporate income tax, and related withholding consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face civil liability for negligent governance or deliberate obstruction. Criminal liability is more likely where records are destroyed, falsified, or used to evade tax.
AML, Sanctions, and Suspicious Payment Conduct

Statutory sanction: Suspicious transaction reporting, account restrictions, payment rejection, asset freezing, AML administrative penalties, forfeiture, and potential criminal prosecution under Taiwan AML and related laws.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, compliance officers, and participating employees may face fines, confiscation, civil restitution, criminal prosecution, and imprisonment. Travel restrictions may follow arrest, bail, court orders, or enforcement proceedings.
Corporate and Accounting Record Falsification

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, company-law sanctions, accounting penalties, loss of evidentiary defenses, civil damages, and criminal prosecution for document or accounting fraud.

Director personal liability: Personal civil liability, restitution, asset attachment, disqualification consequences, fines, and imprisonment may apply to directors, managers, accountants, or other persons who ordered, approved, or executed the misconduct.
Unlicensed or Misdescribed Business Activity

Statutory sanction: Business suspension, administrative fines, contract invalidity risk, tax adjustments, customer claims, and criminal penalties where a regulated activity is performed without authorization.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face personal fines, civil claims, regulatory disqualification, and criminal liability for knowingly operating an unlicensed business.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio del domicilio social (e.g., Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá), acting through the Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES) platform, with registration data synchronized to the DIAN for NIT issuance.

Governing statute

Ley 1258 de 2008 (Law 1258 of 2008), Articles 1-5 and 17-21 governing the Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.) regime; subsidiarily the Código de Comercio (Decreto 410 de 1971), Articles 110-126 and 353-377; complemented by Decreto 1074 de 2015 (business law regulation) and Decreto 2364 de 2010 (digital signature recognition).

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución de Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (private incorporation instrument executed by the shareholders); must be elevated to a public deed (Escritura Pública) before a Notary when real property is contributed to the company.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 12 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum authorized, subscribed, or paid-in capital (Article 4, Ley 1258/2008). The bylaws set the capital terms; the initial subscribed capital must be paid within the term fixed in the bylaws, which may not exceed two years from incorporation (Article 5, Ley 1258/2008). No escrow or blocked bank account is required by Colombian law for S.A.S. formation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: 'Concepto Técnico de Seguridad Humana y Protección contra Incendios' from the Cuerpo Oficial de Bomberos covering the warehouse premises, with annual reinspection where occupant load or building area so requires. Structural: engineering certification (memoria de cálculo / structural load inspection) validated by the municipal planning office or the Dirección de Gestión del Riesgo. Occupational: implementation of the SG-SST (Sistema de Gestión de Seguridad y Salud en el Trabajo) registered with the Administradora de Riesgos Laborales (ARL), including risk assessment, emergency plan and first-aid supplies, under Ley 1562/2012 and Resolution 0312/2019.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – official portal: muisca.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent or non-resident investor establishes a Colombian S.A.S. through a notary public registration of the bylaws. The company must then register with DIAN through the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) using electronic filing procedures to obtain its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria). If the foreign entity will operate directly without local incorporation, it must register for a NIT through the appointment of a legal representative or agent in Colombia, and the RUT will reflect the non-resident status and applicable tax regime. All registrations require prior creation of a user account on DIAN's MUISCA portal and digital certification (cédula digital or foreign equivalent file uploaded).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign parent or non-resident investor establishes a Colombian S.A.S. through a notary public registration of the bylaws. The company must then register with DIAN through the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) using electronic filing procedures to obtain its NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria). If the foreign entity will operate directly without local incorporation, it must register for a NIT through the appointment of a legal representative or agent in Colombia, and the RUT will reflect the non-resident status and applicable tax regime. All registrations require prior creation of a user account on DIAN's MUISCA portal and digital certification (cédula digital or foreign equivalent file uploaded).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists; a Colombian S.A.S. is a per-se corporation subject to entity-level tax under Colombian income tax law. No hybrid mismatch election is needed for ordinary domestic tax classification; when relevant, taxpayers may apply the election rules contained in applicable tax treaties or align with OECD hybrid mismatch provisions for cross-border structures, but no separate statutory election creates check-the-box transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No minimum economic turnover threshold; any person or entity performing taxable sales of goods, services, or imports in Colombia must register as an IVA responsible party (responsable del IVA) on the RUT prior to commencing operations, except for persons falling under the simplified regime (Régimen Simplificado) who are expressly exempted by law and only for certain retail activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods sold from Colombian customs territory to a non-domiciled foreign buyer qualify for the 0% VAT rate only if physically exported and the exporter submits the corresponding customs export declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX) proving the goods left Colombia. Services provided from Colombia to a non-resident beneficiary, including digital platform support and subscription-commerce back-office services performed for a foreign principal, may be zero-rated as export services under Article 481 of the Colombian Tax Statute when the beneficiary is located abroad, the service is not principally used in Colombia, and all documentary requirements are met. The zero-rating is applied as a 20% imputed output VAT credit benefit in the period; actual refund of any input VAT is available through the periodic IVA declaration if properly substantiated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX), including the definitive export registration, modality code, and customs approval
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical goods movement from Colombian customs warehouse to a foreign port of destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas) issued by the bank proving inbound FX receipt within the mandatory repatriation period, or a certificate of non-payment when the operation is insurance/freight or otherwise exempt from FX surrender requirements
  • Commercial invoice issued under DIAN electronic invoicing rules
  • Packing list (for subscription box goods with multiple components)
  • Certificate of origin or proof of foreign destination when requested by DIAN

All taxpayers classified as IVA responsible must invoice through DIAN-authorized electronic invoicing systems (factura electrónica). Invoices must be generated in real time and transmitted through DIAN's OAuth/API web services for validation and fiscal clearance, receiving a CUFE (Código Único de Factura Electrónica) or CUDE (for credit notes/related documents) and a QR-embedded digital stamp issued by DIAN. Software providers must be approved and enable XML generation, periodic batch validation, and the delivery of a validation response within DIAN operating windows; there is also a mandate for electronic transmission of all sales information in digital tax records.

DIAN and customs authorities apply heightened scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. If the export declaration, physical shipment evidence, or FX repatriation certificate is missing, false, or incomplete, the VAT zero-rating is disallowed and the full 19% IVA is assessed on the transaction, plus interest. Fraudulent or abusive zero-rating claims trigger penalty under Article 67 of the Colombian Tax Statute (up to 160% of the evaded tax for egregious underpayment or false information), customs penalties under Decreto 920 and Law 1762 (sanctions up to 100% of the goods' value for false customs declarations), and the exporter may be targeted under tax-fraud criminal provisions (Articles 402 and 403 of the Criminal Code) if intention to evade is shown. The refund claim is also stayed until the exporter proves legal repatriation of FX, and clawback authority extends for the general limitation period (five years) for corrections by DIAN.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a partially liberalized, managed foreign-exchange regime. Current-account transactions (trade in goods, services, remittances) are freely convertible through authorized local exchange intermediaries. Capital-account transactions—including foreign direct investment in the S.A.S., external debt, and portfolio flows—require execution through the exchange market and registration under the appropriate Declaración de Cambio before Banco de la República. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are legal but require: (i) prior registration of the direct investment; (ii) proof that Colombian income tax on the underlying profits has been paid; (iii) the corresponding foreign-exchange declaration filed through the intermediary; and (iv) applicable withholding tax to be deducted. The Colombian S.A.S. may lawfully receive USD/EUR equity contributions only through an authorized Colombian bank account, with the source of funds and purpose documented; the absence of a statutory minimum capital does not remove the exchange-registration duty for inward investment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For S.A.S. exporters of physical goods, 100% of the gross export proceeds must be repatriated and channeled through the Colombian exchange market through an authorized intermediary within six months from the date of shipment (embarque). For exports of services, the six-month term runs from completion or contractual invoicing of the service, in line with Banco de la República regulations. The proceeds must be surrendered (i.e., sold to or credited through the exchange market) unless a specific exception authorizes retention in a registered foreign-currency account or compensation account abroad; exceptions require prior authorization and must be evidenced. Failure to comply triggers sanctions and renders the export documentary chain invalid for tax benefits.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), with the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia as the enforcement authorities for exchange-market rules under Law 9 of 1991 and Resolution Externa 1 of 2018, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary settlement and clearing rail; MEDIUM as an ancillary global wallet or payables card. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., Revolut Business, Airwallex, Wise Business) cannot act as an authorized exchange intermediary in Colombia and cannot open COP clearing accounts with local participation. Using an EMI as the principal route for export receivables violates the Colombian channeling rules and may lead to FX fines, blocked import/export declarations, and treasury control risk. The EMI may serve only for small, non-regulated USD/EUR working balances outside the Colombian merchant clearing chain, with all corresponding cross-border payments mirrored into the Colombian regulated banking system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign equity contributions must flow by SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the investing entity's regulated bank into the S.A.S.'s COP or USD account at a local authorized bank. The payment must bear the exchange-market concept code for direct investment and be reported to Banco de la República through the Declaración de Cambio by the end of the month after receipt. No capital injection should enter through merchant card rails or an offshore EMI; if the investor advances a shareholder loan, the loan must be registered as external credit and follow specific amortization rules. Supporting documents—capital call, board resolution, incorporation registry and investor passport/power of attorney—must remain with the bank file and the S.A.S.'s treasury records.Cross-border payments for merchandise and foreign vendors are settled by SWIFT MT103 (customer transfers) or MT202 (bank-to-bank cover) via the Colombian bank's treasury desk. For high-value or new-supplier purchase orders, the S.A.S. should use a commercial documentary letter of credit issued under UCP600, payable at sight or by usance draft, with the supplier's bank receiving an authenticated SWIFT message and presentation documents.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Cámara de Comercio with territorial jurisdiction over the registered seat (for Bogotá D.C.: Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá), operating through the national Registro Único Empresarial y Social (RUES) portal.

Governing statute

Primary: Ley 1258 de 2008 (Sociedades por Acciones Simplificada), Articles 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 17, 22, 40-45; Supplementary: Código de Comercio (Decreto 410 de 1971), Articles 110, 353-356; Consolidated Regulatory Decree: Decreto 1074 de 2015.

Formation / constitutional filings

Documento Privado de Constitución (Private Incorporation Instrument) filed for commercial registration; where contributions include real estate or in-kind assets above the statutory threshold, the instrument must be elevated to an Escritura Pública (Public Deed) executed before a Notario under Article 5 of Ley 1258 de 2008.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 8 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum subscribed or paid-in capital is required for an S.A.S. under Ley 1258 de 2008; the founders may establish any capital from zero upward. Any subscribed capital must be fully paid within two (2) years from incorporation (Article 4). Payments may be made in cash, in-kind, or by capitalization of credits; no escrow or blocked-account mandate applies at the formation stage. The Chamber registration tax is calculated at 0.7% of declared capital for private instruments.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) – national tax and customs authority; digital clearance portals: www.dian.gov.co and the MUISCA transactional system (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) for RUT, NIT, electronic invoicing and export declarations.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident establishing a Colombian S.A.S.: (1) incorporate via private document filed with the Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce), with notarized legalization and foreign-investment registration (Registro de Inversión Extranjera with Banco de la República) when equity is contributed in foreign currency; (2) obtain the chamber certificate (certificado de existencia y representación legal) appointing the legal representative; (3) apply to DIAN through MUISCA or a DIAN office for RUT (Registro Único Tributario) and NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), which is typically assigned automatically upon incorporation; (4) update the RUT to reflect the status of 'Responsable de IVA' and the economic activity code 4633 (wholesale of dairy products), plus exporter status (exportador); (5) register in DIAN customs' exporter systems and obtain any required sanitary authorization (INVIMA free-sale certificate) for dairy powder exports; and (6) register as an employer for payroll/social security if local staff are hired. Registration must be completed before the first taxable transaction or import.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: A Colombian S.A.S. is a tax-resident juridical person subject to entity-level corporate income tax (35% for tax year 2025) on worldwide income; it is not eligible for pass-through treatment and no tax-consolidation regime applies. Foreign-source income is generally taxable with a foreign tax credit (Article 254 ET); the territorial dividend exemption may apply only to qualifying equity participations in foreign companies. The S.A.S. is also subject to dividend withholding tax (10% plus the 7.5% surtax under Law 2277/2022 where applicable) on distributions to non-resident shareholders. The entity files Form 110 (income tax) and Form 300/350 (IVA). No tax-exempt zone applies to the standard commercial export activity. — For a non-resident establishing a Colombian S.A.S.: (1) incorporate via private document filed with the Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce), with notarized legalization and foreign-investment registration (Registro de Inversión Extranjera with Banco de la República) when equity is contributed in foreign currency; (2) obtain the chamber certificate (certificado de existencia y representación legal) appointing the legal representative; (3) apply to DIAN through MUISCA or a DIAN office for RUT (Registro Único Tributario) and NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), which is typically assigned automatically upon incorporation; (4) update the RUT to reflect the status of 'Responsable de IVA' and the economic activity code 4633 (wholesale of dairy products), plus exporter status (exportador); (5) register in DIAN customs' exporter systems and obtain any required sanitary authorization (INVIMA free-sale certificate) for dairy powder exports; and (6) register as an employer for payroll/social security if local staff are hired. Registration must be completed before the first taxable transaction or import.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Colombian S.A.S. is a tax-resident juridical person subject to entity-level corporate income tax (35% for tax year 2025) on worldwide income; it is not eligible for pass-through treatment and no tax-consolidation regime applies. Foreign-source income is generally taxable with a foreign tax credit (Article 254 ET); the territorial dividend exemption may apply only to qualifying equity participations in foreign companies. The S.A.S. is also subject to dividend withholding tax (10% plus the 7.5% surtax under Law 2277/2022 where applicable) on distributions to non-resident shareholders. The entity files Form 110 (income tax) and Form 300/350 (IVA). No tax-exempt zone applies to the standard commercial export activity.

Available election forms

Colombia has no US-style check-the-box pass-through election for corporations. Available statutory elections affecting the dairy export trade include: (1) election to claim refundable VAT 'saldos a favor' for exporters (Articles 481, 855 ET) via periodic bimonthly refund filings instead of offsetting future tax liabilities; (2) election to treat dividends and capital gains from non-resident entities as exempt under the territorial-divident regime (Article 49 ET) if the 20% minimum holding period and substance conditions are met; (3) election to enter into advance pricing agreements (APA) with DIAN to fix transfer-pricing margins for commodity sales to related parties; (4) election between general and accelerated depreciation for fixed assets; (5) exclusion from the simplified 'no responsable' VAT regime, which is reserved exclusively for individual small merchants and is not available to S.A.S. companies; and (6) optional application of the special VAT treatment for certain agricultural inputs where the dairy commodity qualifies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold applies to corporations: every S.A.S. performing a taxable activity in Colombia must register for IVA in the RUT before the first transaction or import, regardless of revenue. The only low-turnover simplification (gross income below approximately COP 174,296,500, equal to 3,500 UVT for 2025) is restricted to individual merchants under the 'no responsables de IVA' regime and is unavailable to S.A.S. entities, importers or exporters of dairy goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of whole and skimmed milk powder are 'excluidas' (excluded, with entitlement to input-VAT recovery) from IVA under Article 481 ET. Eligibility conditions: (1) the goods must physically depart Colombian customs territory under a valid Declaración de Exportación (DEX) accepted by DIAN; (2) the seller must be an IVA-responsible S.A.S. exporter; (3) title and risk must pass to a foreign buyer situated outside Colombia; (4) the FOB export value must be reported as an excluded sale in the periodic IVA return; (5) the net input-VAT balance ('saldo a favor') must be claimed through the formal refund mechanism (Form 110, Article 855 ET) within the statutory limits; and (6) export proceeds must be channelled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market (mercado cambiario) through an authorized intermediary bank. For dairy products, compliance with INVIMA export sanitary requirements is a precondition to customs clearance.

  • Declaración de Exportación (DEX) – export customs declaration accepted, validated and ratified by DIAN
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Colombian customs territory
  • Commercial invoice issued as an electronic invoice bearing CUNE/CUFE with the 'exportación' modality reference
  • Packing list, weight certificate and certificate of origin (where preferential tariff treatment is claimed)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidenced by the Declaración de Cambio (foreign-exchange conversion declaration) filed through the authorized Colombian intermediary bank
  • Visto bueno de embarque or terminal/port documentation supporting the export movement
  • INVIMA export sanitary certificate or free-sale certificate for dairy food products

Mandatory electronic invoicing – 'factura electrónica de venta' (FEV) – applies to all DIAN-registered taxpayers including S.A.S. exporters. Each invoice must be generated as a signed XML, transmitted in real time to the MUISCA electronic-invoicing platform (either directly through the DIAN portal or via an authorized technology provider – proveedor tecnológico), pre-validated before completion of the transaction, and stamped with the CUNE (Código Único de Numeración Electrónica) or CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica). Colombia does not use physical fiscal stamps; the DIAN's digital transaction record serves as the fiscal control instrument. Export invoices must identify the 'exportación' modality, link to the corresponding customs export declaration (DEX), and be transmitted simultaneously with the dispatch. No cash-register ticket alone may evidence an export sale.

DIAN's automated risk engine flags exporters for verification of DEX validation, foreign-buyer identity, e-invoice CUNE integrity, port and customs-movement traces, and foreign-exchange repatriation. Penalties: (1) inaccurate-return penalty ('sanción por inexactitud', Article 647 ET) of up to 100% of the tax difference attributable to erroneous zero-rating; (2) improper-refund penalty ('devoluciones improcedentes', Article 670 ET) of 10% on the claimed refund, plus interest ('intereses moratorios') at the effective bank lending rate; (3) mandatory clawback of the refunded amount, with DIAN authority to reassess the VAT position for up to four fiscal years (five where returns were not filed); (4) criminal liability for falsified export documentation or over-invoicing designed to fabricate refunds (Articles 402 and 434 of the Criminal Code; Law 1762 of 2015 anti-smuggling regime), carrying imprisonment and fines; (5) for failure to channel export proceeds within 180 days, forced channeling and administrative fines under the Banco de la República foreign-exchange regulations (Circular DCIN-83); and (6) administrative consequences including suspension or cancellation of the exporter and IVA-responsible status in the RUT and inclusion in DIAN's public risk lists.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is convertible for current-account transactions (trade, services, remittances) and for registered capital transactions. Profit remittances from foreign direct investment are freely payable through the official foreign exchange market once the FDI is registered with Banco de la República. No prior license is required for trade-related FX, but all transactions must be carried out through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries or bank accounts in Colombia. Capital-account operations (loans, injections, investment liquidation) require filing Form No. 4 and are subject to documentary verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from outward shipments of goods must be repatriated through authorized foreign exchange market intermediaries within six months of the date of shipment/customs declaration (or the payment term reflected in the commercial invoice, not exceeding the mandatory legal term). 100% of export proceeds must be sold in the official exchange market, or deposited in a foreign-currency deposit account held with a local authorized bank, unless a specific exemption applies under Resolution No. 1.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) and the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia. Foreign exchange policy is implemented via the Junta Directiva del Banco de la República under External Resolution No. 1 of 2018 and Decree 1735 of 1993.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low. Offshore digital EMIs cannot legally open COP-denominated local accounts or independently access the Colombian official FX market. Colombian regulations (Ley 1735/2014) allow only supervised banking institutions and licensed payment providers to provide local accounts; cross-border EMIs can only partner with a local bank as a proxy, which adds operational risk and AML overhead without neutralizing FX formality.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the Colombian SAS must be received via an authorized local bank using SWIFT wire instructions; the incoming remittance must include purpose code 'Direct investment in Colombia (Inversión Extranjera Directa)' and the beneficiary's NIT (tax ID). The investment must be registered with Banco de la República using Form No. 4 before the funds are disposed of, supported by the constitutive deed, UBO certificate, and bank certificate. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% for tax year 2024 (Articles 240 and 240-1, Colombian Tax Statute E.T.), applicable to all Colombian resident legal entities including an S.A.S. engaged in wholesale dairy trading.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends distributed by a Colombian S.A.S. to a non-resident shareholder are split: (i) the portion attributable to profits already subject to 35% corporate income tax bears a 5% additional withholding tax (Article 245 E.T.); (ii) the portion attributable to untaxed or exempt profits bears a 35% withholding tax (Article 246 E.T.). For a company distributing fully taxed profits, the effective additional repatriation charge is 5%; the combined corporate-plus-withholding burden on a distribution of untaxed profits can reach 57.75%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return is due within the national DIAN filing calendar for the relevant tax year, generally running from 12 September through 17 October of the year following the fiscal year-end; the exact date is assigned by the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT. For fiscal years not ending on 31 December, the same national window applies after the close of the chosen fiscal period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation, plus the annual transfer-pricing informative return (Form 120), applies where gross income exceeds 135,000 UVT (COP 6,353,775,000 for tax year 2024; UVT = COP 47,065), or where cross-border related-party transactions exist and gross income exceeds 100,000 UVT. Even below these thresholds, the S.A.S. must maintain arm's length support for, inter alia, raw-milk-powder purchases from foreign affiliated suppliers, interest on shareholder loans, commissions and logistics fees.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations regime to ordinary trading S.A.S. entities. However, the DIAN applies substance-over-form principles under Article 5 E.T., the general anti-abuse rule of Article 869 E.T., and the controlled-foreign-corporation rules of Article 882 E.T. A Colombian S.A.S. interposed in the milk-powder trade with no employees, no physical office, no local warehousing, and no commercial decision-making is at risk of being recharacterized as a shell conduit, with transactions re-sourced to the non-resident principals and the 5% dividend surcharge disregarded in favor of full Colombian taxation of the underlying trade income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Defraudación Fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Article 402 of the Colombian Criminal Code (Ley 599/2000): imprisonment of 48 to 108 months where the evaded tax exceeds 2,500 monthly minimum wages (COP 3,250,000,000 for 2024; SMLV = COP 1,300,000), plus a fine equal to 100% of the evaded tax and forfeiture of assets used in the fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who endorse or file the fraudulent return face criminal imprisonment, joint and several civil restitution of principal, interest and the 100% inaccuracy penalty, freezing of personal assets, and a DIAN-ordered travel ban (prohibition on leaving Colombian territory) pending criminal and administrative proceedings.
False or Late Tax Filing / Inaccuracy (Inexactitud)

Statutory sanction: Article 647 E.T. imposes an inaccuracy penalty of 100% of the greater amount of tax omitted; Article 641 E.T. imposes 5% per month late-filing penalty up to 100% of tax due with a minimum floor of 10 UVT or 0.5% of gross income; monthly default interest accrues on every unpaid peso from the original due date.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and the certified public accountant (revisor fiscal, where required) are jointly liable for penalties when the declaration is signed without substantive verification. The sanction is not dischargeable in bankruptcy and may trigger involuntary dissolution of the S.A.S. and director ineligibility.
Sham Entity / Piercing the Corporate Veil of the S.A.S.

Statutory sanction: Article 42 of Law 1258/2008 (S.A.S. regime) allows Colombian courts to disregard the separate legal personality where the company is used as an instrument of fraud, tax evasion or injury to third parties; the DIAN may recharacterize the transactions under Article 869 E.T. (general anti-abuse rule) and reassess the tax at the 35% corporate rate plus the 100% inaccuracy penalty.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors become personally liable for the full corporate tax debt, civil restitution, interest and sanctions; personal assets may be attached; directors face prohibitions on managing companies in Colombia and referral to criminal authorities for fraudulent misuse of the corporate vehicle.
Trade-Based Money Laundering and Customs Fraud (Contrabando)

Statutory sanction: Law 1762/2015 (anti-smuggling framework) and Article 323 of the Criminal Code (money laundering): imprisonment of 10 to 30 years and fines up to 1,000 SMLV; administrative customs sanctions include seizure and forfeiture of the milk-powder cargo, cancellation of the importer/exporter license, and disqualification from foreign-trade operations.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign, instruct or benefit from false trade documentation face criminal imprisonment, personal asset forfeiture (extinción de dominio), travel prohibitions, and permanent debarment from Colombian customs brokerage and cross-border trading roles.

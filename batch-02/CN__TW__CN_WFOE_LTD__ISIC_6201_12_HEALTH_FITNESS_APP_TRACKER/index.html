Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi is generally usable for current-account transactions, including genuine trade in services, software, royalties, and other documented operating payments, subject to bank review of transaction authenticity and tax compliance. Capital-account transactions remain controlled and require registration, reporting, permitted-account usage, and banking verification. Foreign-invested enterprise capital contributions generally require foreign-investment registration and capital-account processing before remittance and use. Conversion of foreign currency into renminbi is not unrestricted treasury discretion: banks assess the registered business scope, source and purpose of funds, invoices or contracts, tax evidence where applicable, and anti-money-laundering risk. Profit, dividend, interest, service-fee, royalty, and liquidation remittances are generally possible after satisfaction of corporate approvals, audited or accounting support, applicable tax filing or withholding obligations, and bank documentation requirements. Restrictions may arise from undistributed losses, unpaid taxes, inadequate statutory records, sanctions screening, related-party pricing concerns, incomplete foreign-investment reporting, or inconsistent business activity. Mainland China and Taiwan must not be treated as a single banking or FX-control perimeter; cross-strait payments may be subject to additional banking, trade, sanctions, and remittance controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mainland China does not impose a universal rule requiring every export receipt to be converted into renminbi at a fixed percentage or within a single statutory surrender period. Export proceeds may generally be retained in permitted foreign-currency accounts or converted when commercially required, subject to genuine-trade verification, timely receipt, bank reporting, and SAFE rules. Goods-export proceeds must be collected through compliant banking channels and matched to customs, trade, and accounting records; service-export proceeds must be supported by the underlying service contract, invoice, tax materials, and evidence of performance. There is no general mandatory conversion ratio applicable to this digital-health application business. The enterprise should maintain a transaction-level reconciliation of contract, invoice, customs or service evidence, bank receipt, tax treatment, and account purpose. Banks may impose internal deadlines, documentary cutoffs, or enhanced reviews, and specific export-support, trade-finance, or regulatory programs may impose additional conditions. No receipt should be delayed, split, netted, or routed through an unrelated account without a documented legal and commercial basis.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange, with foreign-exchange banking operations conducted through authorized mainland Chinese commercial banks. This dossier assumes that CN_WFOE_LTD is a mainland China wholly foreign-owned enterprise. Taiwan is a separate legal and foreign-exchange jurisdiction; a Taiwan entity or Taiwan bank account requires a separate Taiwan regulatory analysis.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary payment collection and treasury technology; Low as a substitute for the mandated mainland corporate banking stack. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment institution cannot generally be assumed to open or operate the PRC entity's core RMB, capital, payroll, tax, or foreign-exchange accounts. Use requires a licensed payment provider, an authorized bank, compliant cross-border payment documentation, data and cybersecurity controls, transaction monitoring, and a legally supported commercial purpose. Apple App Store and Google Play settlement flows must be reviewed separately for payer location, merchant-of-record structure, tax invoicing, foreign-exchange classification, platform withholding, chargebacks, and repatriation. Offshore collection accounts must not be used to conceal domestic sales, bypass capital-account controls, evade tax, or aggregate unrelated third-party funds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$50,000,000 (New Taiwan Dollars) of aggregate annual related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File; a Master File is additionally required where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue reaches NT$20,000,000,000; country-by-country reporting follows the same group threshold.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations code, but the Taiwanese tax administration applies the substance-over-form doctrine and anti-abuse rules. A WFOE lacking real offices, employees, decision-making power and business activities may be treated as a sham; related-party contracts, TP documentation, payroll and office records are audited to verify commercial substance before profit attribution or treaty benefits are granted.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Under Taiwan's Tax Collection Act, fraudulent tax evasion carries imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of up to NT$60,000; administratively, the tax authority may levy an additional tax equal to 3 times the underpaid amount, plus interest, with an enhanced surcharge of not more than 10 percentage points for clear documents fraud.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons are co-obligors for the company's unpaid tax; directors face civil restitution of the evaded amount, court-ordered travel bans, asset freezes, and up to 5 years imprisonment; veil-piercing applies when bad faith or corporate formalities are ignored.
Transfer Pricing Compliance Failure / Shell Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may re-price the transactions, disregard the arrangements, and impose an additional tax of up to 100% to 200% of the corrected tax deficiency, with fines under Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act; interest and late-payment penalties accrue continuously.

Director personal liability: Directors and the tax responsible person may be personally assessed for deficiencies after repeated failure to comply; Taiwan courts have permitted piercing to officers who knowingly sign false TP submissions, exposing them to civil damages and possible prosecution for forging accounting books.
Anti-Money Laundering / Suspicious Financial Activity

Statutory sanction: Taiwan's Money Laundering Control Act imposes imprisonment of up to 7 years on natural persons and corporate fines up to NT$50,000,000 for money laundering; financial institutions are required to file suspicious transaction reports and freeze assets where predicate tax fraud is indicated.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly control laundering structures are criminally liable, subject to asset forfeiture, travel bans, foreign-exchange sanctions and extradition in DTA cases; the corporate veil is pierced for sole or controlling shareholders who treat corporate assets as personal.

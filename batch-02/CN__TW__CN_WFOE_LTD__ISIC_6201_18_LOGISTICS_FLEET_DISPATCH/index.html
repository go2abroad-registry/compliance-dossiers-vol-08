Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Taiwan Company Act does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a general limited liability company or a company limited by shares engaged in ISIC 6201 software and digital SaaS activities. No blocked escrow account is mandated by law. Under foreign investment practice, the full amount subscribed by the parent must be remitted to a Taiwanese authorized bank and deposited in the company's name before final registration; the deposited funds are restricted and cannot be released to shareholders before the Company Registration Certificate is issued. Any later capital increase requires a new Investment Commission filing.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing and registration are performed via the official Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform (e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

A WFOE is incorporated with the State Administration for Market Regulation, which issues a business license containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number. Within 30 days after obtaining the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration through the Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform or the locally competent tax authority office, submitting the incorporation certificate, articles of association, proof of the foreign investor's identity and beneficial ownership, and legal representative identification. The tax authority then activates the taxpayer profile, determines VAT taxpayer status (general or small-scale), and registers the entity for monthly or quarterly corporate income tax and VAT filings. The foreign non-resident shareholder must satisfy non-resident withholding registration obligations; where no Chinese-source income exists, no separate Chinese TIN is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law at the standard 25% corporate income tax rate; no pass-through classification is available.

Available election forms

None. China's enterprise income tax and VAT regimes do not provide a check-the-box election or a hybrid-mismatch entity classification election. The only available statutory election is between general VAT taxpayer and small-scale VAT taxpayer status, which is an indirect tax status election rather than an entity classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must register for VAT from formation irrespective of turnover. The mandatory turnover threshold for general VAT taxpayer classification is RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales; below this amount the entity may remain a small-scale taxpayer or elect general taxpayer status, while exceeding it requires an application for general taxpayer status within 30 days.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A general taxpayer WFOE may apply the zero VAT rate to exported goods and to qualifying cross-border services, including information technology and software services such as fleet route optimization and telematics SaaS, where the recipient is located outside Mainland China, the service is wholly consumed outside Mainland China, and the foreign exchange is received within the statutory period. The service must be included in the cross-border taxable services zero-rating catalogue of Caishui [2016] No. 36 and STA Announcement No. 29 of 2016.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry / Export Goods Declaration) for any exported hardware or telematics devices
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export transportation of dutiable goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) proving foreign-exchange receipt for the exported service or goods
  • Signed cross-border service agreement specifying the offshore recipient and the fleet telematics SaaS scope
  • Commercial invoice marked zero-rated and supported by the Special VAT Invoice issuance record
  • Technical service consumption evidence such as IP logs, server access logs, and offshore user acceptance statements
  • General VAT taxpayer registration certificate

The Golden Tax Phase IV system requires all VAT invoices, including fully digitalized electronic invoices, to be issued through the Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform and uploaded in real time with cryptographic digital signatures and QR codes. B2B supplies by general taxpayers require a Special VAT Invoice to support input VAT credits; anti-fraud controls include invoice serialization, fiscal digital stamping, and real-time invoice authentication before the invoice is legally valid.

The State Taxation Administration may audit zero-rated export claims and claw back any refunded VAT and input VAT credits if the documentation is incomplete, false, or unverified. Penalties include a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, administrative fines of between 50% and 500% of the VAT evaded under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and the VAT Implementing Rules, and criminal referral for fraudulent VAT-refund claims under the Criminal Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime. The Renminbi is fully convertible for current account transactions once a genuine trade or service underlying is verified; the bank performs electronic trade verification through SAFE's monitoring systems. Capital account convertibility remains regulated and asymmetric. An inbound FDI capital injection into a WFOE is a permitted capital account item, but it requires a SAFE foreign exchange registration, a designated FDI capital account, and a bank-issued settlement certificate before the funds can be converted into RMB. After settlement, the RMB funds enter a payment-pending RMB account and must be spent in accordance with the business scope and the national foreign investment negative list. Profit remittance is possible after annual audit, tax clearance, and approval by the board, but the remittance is subject to current account verification and the preparation of a foreign exchange declaration. Export proceeds are not forcibly surrendered; they may be retained in foreign exchange. The overall system is therefore asymmetric: current account payments flow quickly after document verification, while capital account flows are permissioned lanes with registration, filing, and use conditions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic current-account foreign exchange account within 90 days from the later of the customs declaration date or the transport document date unless an extension is reported to SAFE. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% (no forced surrender); the entity may keep the foreign currency or convert to RMB at market rate. The bank verifies underlying trade data through the SAFE Trade Monitoring System; if the export payment is not received by the deadline and no extension report exists, the WFOE may be downgraded from Class A to Class B/C payment verification status, which will restrict subsequent FX transactions.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC), exercised through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary account-holding legal entity; medium only for ancillary receivables collection via a licensed local payment institution. Foreign digital EMIs without PRC payment institution permits cannot open RMB settlement accounts, issue LCs, or route SAFE capital account funds; using such an EMI for the WFOE's equity injection would constitute illegal cross-border business without a license. Consequently they are not recommended as primary clearing rails; if used, they can only be engaged by the offshore parent or sales entities outside mainland China.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

From Taiwan corporate shareholder to the WFOE in mainland China: 1) Complete SAFE FDI registration through local SAFE/government network and obtain the FDI registration certificate via the bank's capital project information system. 2) The Taiwan remitter sends SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated Capital Account FX Account. Payment must include the SAFE balance-of-payments purpose code for FDI equity injection (e.g., transaction codes under 601010/601020 in the SAFE BOP code list) and the FDI registration business number. 3) The receiving bank credits funds to the capital account, performs electronic verification; then for RMB usage, the entity submits a foreign-exchange settlement application, and the bank converts funds into RMB into a pending settlement RMB account subject to negative-list use restrictions. Capital verification is evidenced by a CPA report and the approved SAFE certificate; funds cannot be deployed until verification.Clearing rails for outbound payments: 1) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border service payments to software vendors, cloud hosting providers, advertising platforms, and logistics subcontractors, supported by invoice, contract, tax withholding certificate, and service trade payment declaration. 2) Commercial Letters of Credit for importing physical fleet hardware such as telematics units, GPS routers, and IoT sensors, with the issuing bank requiring import contract, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and inspection certificate. 3) Documentary collections (D/P) or (D/A) are permitted for low-risk moderate-value trade settlements but do not provide payment guarantee. 4) Cross-border RMB settlement through CIPS may be used where the overseas counterparty accepts RMB, otherwise USD/EUR/CNY MT103 clearing is applied. Late filing penalties

Late filing before tax authority investigation: 10% surcharge on underpaid tax, minimum NT$2,000; late filing after investigation or non-filing: up to 100% of the underpaid tax; unpaid tax accrues statutory interest and triggers compulsory enforcement, asset seizure, and ultimately administrative dissolution or revocation of company registration.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under Taiwan Company Act Article 20(2), an independent certified public accountant audit is mandatory when the company's paid-in capital exceeds NT$50 million (balance-sheet trigger). Additional mandatory audit triggers: (i) the company is a bank, credit cooperative, insurance company, securities firm, futures enterprise, or financial holding company; (ii) the Ministry of Economic Affairs or Financial Supervisory Commission expressly orders an audit; (iii) the company is a foreign-invested enterprise required by the Investment Commission or a financing institution to provide CPA-audited reports as a condition of registration or credit support. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$200,000,000 (approximately USD 6.2 million) in annual related-party transactions or NT$300,000,000 (approximately USD 9.3 million) in annual operating revenue, whichever is met, triggers mandatory preparation of a Local File/TP Report; full Master File required at a consolidated group revenue of NT$27 billion; Country-by-Country reporting for groups with consolidated revenue above NT$27 billion.

Economic substance scope: Although Taiwan does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime like zero-tax jurisdictions, the Tax Collection Act Article 12-1 and Income Tax Act Article 10 empower the National Taxation Bureau to disregard transactions lacking commercial substance and to tax the real economic owner. A logistic-dispatch SaaS WFOE must possess a fixed place of business, core personnel performing route optimisation and software development, and active decision-making in Taiwan. If the company is deemed a pure shell or its management is exercised wholly offshore, the Taiwan authorities may treat the entity as not taxable or, conversely, subject it to CFC rules if the parent is a Taiwanese resident. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) principle is applied to allocate income to the location of the personnel who supply the technology and manage client relationships.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Underreporting Cross-Border SaaS Revenue

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Act Article 110: fine up to 3 times the underpaid tax amount, and criminal penalty under Article 41: imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine of up to NT$60,000,000 (payable by the responsible person).

Director personal liability: In addition to criminal imprisonment, directors are jointly and severally liable for the outstanding tax and fines; the NTA may assert piercing the corporate veil under general civil law to attach personal assets. An exit/blacklisting order under Tax Collection Act Article 24 will be imposed, preventing the director from leaving Taiwan until the unpaid tax is secured.
Failure to Withhold Tax on Outbound Dividends and Royalties

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Act Article 114: penalty equal to 0.5 times to 1.5 times of the withheld amount; if the failure was intentional and for evasion, criminal liability under Article 42 may result in imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: The responsible person must remit the unpaid withholding tax and penalty from personal funds. The tax authority can attach bank accounts and other personal property. Furthermore, the director's ability to travel is restricted through a departure ban; repeated wilful non-compliance triggers full criminal prosecution and a civil suit for restitution.
Abusive Transfer Pricing and Lack of Documentation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 43-1: the tax authority adjusts the income upward to reflect arm's length; the underpaid tax is subject to a fine of up to 3 times under Article 110, plus criminal liability for tax evasion if the conduct was intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false certificates or direct the artificial pricing are personally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties. In severe cases the corporate veil is pierced to reach personal assets; the director also faces a travel ban and potential imprisonment for fraud related to tax evasion.

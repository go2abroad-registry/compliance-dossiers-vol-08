Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Company Act; a Taiwan limited company has no statutory minimum paid-in capital. However, a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) must obtain Investment Commission approval of its total capital amount and must remit that approved investment amount into a Taiwan bank account in New Taiwan Dollars. The remitted funds must be verified through a bank certificate before filing company registration. No mandatory escrow blocked account is required, but the funds may not be withdrawn or disbursed prior to incorporation except for lawful pre-incorporation expenses, and all disbursements must align with the approved business plan. The registered capital may be reduced or increased only with subsequent Investment Commission approval.

No Civil Defense industrial permit is needed because no hazardous materials, energy facilities, or civil-defense critical infrastructure are operated; and no physical factory fire license is applicable.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; online portal: National Electronic Taxation Bureau (etax.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

For a Chinese resident WFOE, incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation and obtain a business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which functions as the primary tax identification number. Complete the tax registration record with the competent local State Taxation Administration office within 30 days of issuing the business license; this is typically achieved automatically via the 'multi-license integration' data-sharing reform. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China that derives China-sourced income or conducts taxable activities, register for a non-resident taxpayer identification number (NTIN) by submitting the enterprise's constitutional documents, foreign business registration certificate, a designated agent appointment letter, and details of the withholding agent or property/project location, either through the National Electronic Taxation Bureau or in-person at the competent tax authority. The STA will issue the NTIN, which must be used for all withholding, filing, and payment obligations.

Available election forms

None (China does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime). However, an enterprise may elect between simplified small-scale taxpayer status and general VAT taxpayer status under the VAT legislation, subject to statutory thresholds and registration requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for tax registration: every enterprise performing taxable activities must register with the tax authority upon establishment. For general VAT taxpayer status, the compulsory threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5 million; below this, the enterprise may elect simplified taxation at the applicable reduced rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are generally zero-rated, excluding goods restricted or prohibited by state policy. Qualifying offshore digital services — including software development, information system services, data processing, and technology-intensive services such as esports matchmaking and tournament platform services — are eligible for the 0% VAT rate whenprovided to entities or individuals outside China and wholly consumed outside China. Conditions: (i) the recipient is outside Chinese customs territory; (ii) the services do not arise from domestic consumption; (iii) the supply is evidenced by a service contract, technical delivery records, and offshore user or server activity logs; and (iv) the payment is received from abroad and repatriated through normal foreign exchange channels.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form) – mandatory for physical goods; for pure digital services, no customs declaration is legally required
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill – mandatory for physical goods exports; for digital services, equivalent electronic delivery evidence must be maintained
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank collection receipt – mandatory for both goods and services to evidence foreign exchange repatriation
  • Export Service Contract / Software License Agreement – mandatory for qualifying digital service zero-rating
  • Completion and Offshore Consumption Certification (e.g., server logs, offshore user records, service performance reports) – required to substantiate the offshore location of consumption

China operates the Golden Tax System Phase IV, requiring all VAT taxpayers to issue invoices through certified tax-control equipment or the fully digitalized electronic invoice platform (Full Digital Electronic Invoice). Invoice data is transmitted in near-real time to the State Taxation Administration's central database. High-volume issuers must integrate via API for automated issuance, reporting, and clearance. Anti-fraud safeguards include mandatory certified invoice numbers, tax-control encryption, automated cross-reconciliation with VAT returns, and severe penalties for issuing false or altered invoices.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to rigorous post-audit verification by the State Taxation Administration. If the taxpayer fails to produce the required documentation within the statutory retention period (typically five years), or the claimed transaction is found to be non-genuine, the tax authority will claw back the zero-rated benefit, impose late payment surcharges, and assess a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. Fraudulent export tax refund claims may trigger criminal liability under the Criminal Law, including fines, asset forfeiture, and imprisonment. Furthermore, the exporter may be suspended from VAT refund processing and placed on a high-risk control list.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a classified current and capital account convertibility regime. For current account items (trade in goods, services, and remittances of dividends), full convertibility has been achieved in principle; however, each transaction must be substantiated with genuine supporting documents and routed through designated foreign-exchange banks with digital verification under SAFE's cross-border receipt and payment monitoring system. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment into a WFOE, remain subject to strict registration, approval, and quota requirements. Foreign investors must register the investment with SAFE (either directly or via a bank) to establish a corresponding foreign exchange capital account, and each capital contribution must be individually verified. Profit repatriation is permissible through the current account after the annual audit, provided the WFOE has no tax arrears, the board has declared dividends, and the remittance is matched by prior tax clearance certificates and audited financial statements. Capital item conversions—such as the settlement of foreign currency capital into renminbi for operational expenses—are restricted under the payment-settlement mechanism, meaning RMB funds derived from foreign exchange capital must be used only for those genuine business purposes disclosed in the settlement application. In practice, this full documentation trail creates significant friction for unplanned cross-border payments and liquidity movements, but it provides a well-defined compliance path for a legitimate esports community platform.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As of 2012, China no longer imposes a mandatory surrender ratio on export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in their domestic foreign-currency accounts or settle into renminbi at their own discretion, but all export receipts must be collected through the bank and verified under SAFE's trade in goods monitoring system within 90 days from the date of export declaration. In case of non-receipt, the exporter must file an adjustment and provide justification. For a WFOE deriving revenue from cross-border digital services (which are classified under services trade), the same principle applies: receipts from overseas must be repatriated and reported within the service trade statistics timeframe, and conversion is free of mandatory ratios, but the transaction must be supported by a valid service contract and invoice.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign exchange regulator

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech and electronic-money institution (EMI) involvement in onshore corporate banking for a Chinese WFOE is legally constrained. Neither the PBOC nor SAFE licenses offshore EMIs (e.g., digital-only cross-border banks or non-bank e-wallet providers) to maintain cash accounts or perform foreign exchange settlement for a PRC enterprise. All cross-border payments must transit through a bank holding a payment/settlement license or a licensed Chinese payment institution that partners with such banks. The target payment rails mentioned (Stripe, PayPal) can only operate as overseas collection vehicles for outbound or inbound transactions, but they cannot serve as the WFOE's primary treasury or clearing rails. In practice, a WFOE must open its main domicile account with a licensed domestic commercial bank, and use Stripe/PayPal only as downstream payment badges or as a customer-facing sales channel, with funds constantly swept into the Chinese bank via appropriate trade/service filings. Therefore, the feasibility rating of a fintech EMI serving as a primary liquidity rail is Low, and its use must be strictly supplemental.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital will be remitted from the Taiwan shareholder via high-value interbank wire using SWIFT MT103, with the field 70 (remittance information) explicitly stating 'Equity Contribution to CN_WFOE_LTD' and field 33B denoting the contracted currency amount (usually USD or CNY). Before the first injection, the WFOE must complete the SAFE foreign investment record (usually via the bank after obtaining the business license and tax registration). A dedicated capital escrow account (Foreign Exchange Capital Account) will be opened at the chosen Tier-1 bank, and each contribution will be matched against the share-based capital schedule. Upon receipt, the bank posts the funds, and the company obtains an FDI Capital Verification integrated declaration. For each RMB conversion, the company must submit a 'Payment-Settlement' Application stating the purpose, e.g., salaries, office rent, platform hosting, and later substantiate with invoices and contracts. The funds cannot be injected for speculative or financial purposes.For operational outflows to global gaming content providers, servers, and tournament prize winners, the approved rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 for ordinary service payments (e.g., cloud services, licensing fees, prize remittances to international players); (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) for large equipment or supply contracts, although unlikely for an esports platform; (3) Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) as a cost-effective alternative for trade in physical goods, if the platform expands into merchandise. All outbound payments must be backed by a retained copy of the contract, invoice, and where applicable, a notarised certificate of import/export. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech and electronic-money institution (EMI) involvement in onshore corporate banking for a Chinese WFOE is legally constrained. Neither the PBOC nor SAFE licenses offshore EMIs (e.g., digital-only cross-border banks or non-bank e-wallet providers) to maintain cash accounts or perform foreign exchange settlement for a PRC enterprise. All cross-border payments must transit through a bank holding a payment/settlement license or a licensed Chinese payment institution that partners with such banks. The target payment rails mentioned (Stripe, PayPal) can only operate as overseas collection vehicles for outbound or inbound transactions, but they cannot serve as the WFOE's primary treasury or clearing rails. In practice, a WFOE must open its main domicile account with a licensed domestic commercial bank, and use Stripe/PayPal only as downstream payment badges or as a customer-facing sales channel, with funds constantly swept into the Chinese bank via appropriate trade/service filings. Therefore, the feasibility rating of a fintech EMI serving as a primary liquidity rail is Low, and its use must be strictly supplemental.

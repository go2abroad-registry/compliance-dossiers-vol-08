Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Capital account and current account transactions are generally convertible under the framework of Ley 9 de 1991, the Régimen de Cambios Internacionales, and Circular Reglamentaria DCIN 83 of 2018 of the Banco de la República (compiled in the new External Exchange Manual). Legal profit remittances by foreign direct investors in Colombian S.A.S. entities are permitted without prior exchange authorization provided the investment is duly registered with the Banco de la República, the remittance is made through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries or local banks using the appropriate declaration codes, applicable taxes (e.g., dividend withholding tax and CREE-related levies) are evidenced, and the 11% remittance tax on branch profits (impuesto de remesas) is not applicable to dividends paid out of taxed profits of an S.A.S. (only to the net income of permanent establishments that are not branches of a foreign entity; for branches, the 33% remittance tax applies on remitted profits net of tax). Foreign exchange market participation in Colombia is compulsory for most cross-border payments and receipts, meaning payments must be channeled through the foreign exchange market (canal de la cuenta corriente) with the corresponding market participant, unless the transaction falls within a listed exception. The Colombian peso is convertible under a managed floating exchange rate regime. Restrictions apply to non-financial private sector borrowing and certain capital flows; all foreign credit, portfolio investment, and direct investment must be declared to the central bank. For the sale of automotive parts via e-commerce to foreign customers, export of services and goods export proceeds must be surrendered and settled through the Colombian foreign exchange market within specified timelines, and retained foreign currency must be declared. Settlement delays can be authorized only under specific circumstances (e.g., temporary import/export, advance payments, guarantees, or accounts abroad previously authorized by the central bank). Dividends to non-resident shareholders are freely transferable once registered, but the remittance must be processed through an authorized intermediary with an exchange declaration and the exporting company must evidence that the relevant tax obligations have been fulfilled.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Colombian foreign exchange regime, proceeds from exports of goods (including online retail sales of automotive parts where the goods physically leave Colombia) must be channeled through the foreign exchange market. General rule: export proceeds must be received through the foreign exchange market within 6 months from the date of shipment or from the filing of the export declaration, unless the exporter has obtained an extension from the Banco de la República under Circular Reglamentaria DCIN 83. Of those proceeds, 100% must be surrendered/settled by converting into Colombian pesos if the proceeds are received in a foreign currency, except for the portion that the exporter wishes to maintain in foreign currency under an authorized foreign currency account (cuenta de compensación or cuenta en el exterior) previously declared to the central bank; however, even in that case the surrender requirement is not a fractional partial conversion but an obligation to bring all proceeds into the Colombian foreign exchange market within the legal term. Funds may be received directly in COP in the exporter's local account if the foreign buyer pays via a wire transfer in COP, although such COP transfers from offshore are subject to exchange control and must be declared as a service or goods export under the corresponding declaration code. Specific statutory timeline: The 6-month period is applied from the date of the export declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX) or the date of shipment for goods, and from the date of accrual/invoice issue for services. Non-compliance triggers a fine under the regulatory enforcement powers of DIAN and the Banco de la República, plus late surrender interest obligations.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia) and the Comisión de Cambios, with supervisory enforcement by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) for exchange declaration purposes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-Low for principal treasury rails and High only as a supplementary fringe route. Legal constraints: (i) Non-bank fintech companies in Colombia are not authorized by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia to offer deposit-taking, current account, or cross-border payment services unless licensed as a Sociedad Especializada en Pagos (SEDPE) or as a financial institution; foreign Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) without a local license may not provide regulated payment services to Colombian residents. (ii) Foreign EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex cannot substitute for a Colombian domestic bank account for the surrender of export proceeds, because the Colombian exchange regime requires all export-related foreign currency to be returned to Colombia through the foreign exchange market and to be declared through a local authorized intermediary or through the simple export declaration process where no local financial intermediary is involved if the funds remain abroad in an authorized foreign currency account. (iii) For an S.A.S. with Colombian e-commerce operations, inbound buyer payments from abroad via PayPal and Stripe are generally paid out to a local bank in COP; if the company maintains a foreign currency account with a foreign EMI, that account must be declared as a cuenta en el exterior to the Banco de la República, and the funds must be repatriated within the 6-month surrender window unless a specific exchange authorization is obtained. (iv) The target payment rails (Stripe and PayPal) are viable as acquiring subprocessors that settle in COP or USD through local bank partners, but legal clearance must be handled by a Colombian-licensed commercial bank.

Defense evidence: Written opinion of local AML counsel that the flows qualify as export proceeds; registrations/declarations before Banco de la República for any foreign currency account (cuenta de compensación/cuenta en el exterior) if the company elects to hold settlement funds offshore; evidence of the 6-month repatriation plan (bank statements showing conversion of USD to COP in the local account), and a legal compliance manual establishing the obligation to declare all cross-border receipts to DIAN.
SAS capital under the statutory minimum of an SME or zero-issued shares, raising a suspicious contradiction with declared business volume

Root cause: SAS capital under the statutory minimum of an SME or zero-issued shares, raising a suspicious contradiction with declared business volume

Defense evidence: Notarial and registration documents of the SAS under Ley 1258 de 2008, with the share subscription agreement and time schedule for payment; an independent accountant's certified statement that no statutory minimum capital is required and that the corporate commercial registry (cámara de comercio) certification evidences the registered paid-in capital in line with the business scale; a plan and shareholder commitment letter to capitalize via a bank transfer subject to exchange control declaration.
Directors or UBOs residing in jurisdictions classified as risky by FATF/GAFILAT or in jurisdictions with no tax information exchange with Colombia

Root cause: Directors or UBOs residing in jurisdictions classified as risky by FATF/GAFILAT or in jurisdictions with no tax information exchange with Colombia

Defense evidence: Source-of-wealth report prepared by a Colombian-licensed attorney; passport and residence card; tax residency certificate issued by the home-country tax authority; official registry entry (RUT) showing Colombian tax ID; list of all companies and affiliations; evidence of economic substance in Colombia (physical office in Bogotá/Medellín, payroll, utility bills); sworn declaration under Article 2.14.1.3 of the Superintendencia's UBO rule with documentary appendix.
Unusual cross-border international trade settlement patterns where funds are received from a foreign buyer but the shipping route is Colombia-to-third-country (triangulation)

Root cause: Unusual cross-border international trade settlement patterns where funds are received from a foreign buyer but the shipping route is Colombia-to-third-country (triangulation)

Defense evidence: Commercial agreement shipped, bill of lading with Incoterms, customs transit declarations, DIAN export declaration for the triangulated trade, exchange declaration code for transit trade, Acuerdo de Reintegro if the seller is a mere intermediary; evidence of prior authorization if a foreign currency account is used.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Colombian S.A.S., inbound capital injections from a foreign shareholder must be routed through a Colombian authorized foreign exchange intermediary (e.g., Bancolombia, Banco de Bogotá, Davivienda) using SWIFT MT103/MT202, with the payment reason encoded as foreign direct investment (FDI). The transfer must be coded as Concept 3610 (Importación de capital para inversión directa de capital) or Concept 3620/3640 depending on the capital type; the receiving bank must file the Declaración de Cambio – Inversión Internacional (Formulario 3) with the Banco de la República within the same business day or by the next business day. The company must obtain a Registro de Inversiones Internacionales (the 'Formulario No. 3' registration) within 2 months from the date of receipt if the capital exceeds US$100,000; if below that threshold, the registration may be simplified or waived. The capital injection must be evidenced by: (i) a corporate resolution approving the capital increase and share subscription; (ii) the legalization of the capital increase before the Cámara de Comercio; (iii) the receipt through a registered local bank account; (iv) certified translation of the resolution into Spanish. After registration, the FDI capital has right to free repatriation and dividend remittance subject to tax and exchange declaration.Outbound trade payments for the purchase of automotive aftermarket parts (e.g., from US, China, Germany, Japan) must be routed through the Colombian foreign exchange market. Standard clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct wire payments to the foreign supplier; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) issued by a local bank via SWIFT MT700/MT707 with the corresponding documents (AWBs, shipping invoices, certificates of origin, inspection certificates); (iii) documentary collections (CAD) via MT400/MT798. All payments must be declared to the Banco de la República under the appropriate exchange declaration code (import of goods, concept 3010 or similar) within the month following the transaction or upon the 'momento de declaración' according to Circular DCIN 83. The allocations must include proper importer registration (RUT), previous import declaration, valid inscription in the Registry of Importers/Exporters of DIAN, and retention of the formed MIC/DTA (Declaración de importación anticipada) if applicable. For payments to related companies, beware of the adjusted transfer-pricing documentation rules under Colombian tax law and the thin-capitalization rules.Non-deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – COP/USD non-deliverable forwards with local banks or international counterparties satisfying the Superintendencia Financiera marks and eligible through the forward market (Mercado de Derivados Cambiarios)Currency forwards (deliverable) on the Colombian Peso executed with authorized intermediaries and recorded in the exchange declarations (Formulario 4 for derivative transactions)Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) entered into by qualified counterparties for management of long-term debt and investmentsPlain-vanilla and financially settled currency options (calls and puts) on USD/COP with a Colombian authorized financial institutionFX swaps (swap cambiario) with bank counterparties, including the Banco de la República's standing facilities for market makersExchange-traded futures on TRM (the Colombian indicative exchange rate) traded through licensed local futures-clearing members (Cámara de Riesgo Central de Contraparte) – limited to hedging actual commercial exposuresForwards on a basket of currencies or on foreign currency (USD/EUR/CNY) as OTC derivatives with local banks subject to the Superintendencia's derivative registration rules and the ban on speculative positions without real commercial exposure

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium-Low for principal treasury rails and High only as a supplementary fringe route. Legal constraints: (i) Non-bank fintech companies in Colombia are not authorized by the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia to offer deposit-taking, current account, or cross-border payment services unless licensed as a Sociedad Especializada en Pagos (SEDPE) or as a financial institution; foreign Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) without a local license may not provide regulated payment services to Colombian residents. (ii) Foreign EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex cannot substitute for a Colombian domestic bank account for the surrender of export proceeds, because the Colombian exchange regime requires all export-related foreign currency to be returned to Colombia through the foreign exchange market and to be declared through a local authorized intermediary or through the simple export declaration process where no local financial intermediary is involved if the funds remain abroad in an authorized foreign currency account. (iii) For an S.A.S. with Colombian e-commerce operations, inbound buyer payments from abroad via PayPal and Stripe are generally paid out to a local bank in COP; if the company maintains a foreign currency account with a foreign EMI, that account must be declared as a cuenta en el exterior to the Banco de la República, and the funds must be repatriated within the 6-month surrender window unless a specific exchange authorization is obtained. (iv) The target payment rails (Stripe and PayPal) are viable as acquiring subprocessors that settle in COP or USD through local bank partners, but legal clearance must be handled by a Colombian-licensed commercial bank.

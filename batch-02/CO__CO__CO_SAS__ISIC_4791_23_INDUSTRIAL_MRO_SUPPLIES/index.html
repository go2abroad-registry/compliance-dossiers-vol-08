Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: https://www.dian.gov.co

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must appoint a local legal representative or attorney-in-fact in Colombia, obtain a power of attorney (poder especial), apply for pre-registration with DIAN to obtain a provisional NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), complete the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) form declaring the entity's legal nature, principal activity (ISIC 4791), and tax regime, and then file for final registration using the DIAN electronic portal (MUISCA) or through an authorized accountant. The NIT is issued within approximately 5–10 business days once the documentation is validated.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must appoint a local legal representative or attorney-in-fact in Colombia, obtain a power of attorney (poder especial), apply for pre-registration with DIAN to obtain a provisional NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), complete the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) form declaring the entity's legal nature, principal activity (ISIC 4791), and tax regime, and then file for final registration using the DIAN electronic portal (MUISCA) or through an authorized accountant. The NIT is issued within approximately 5–10 business days once the documentation is validated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' regime exists under Colombian law. A Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.) is by default treated as a separate taxable entity subject to Colombian corporate income tax (retefuente, VAT, and national consumption tax as applicable). No hybrid mismatch or transparent entity elections are available for an S.A.S. Special tax stabilization contracts under Law 1955/2019 may be available only for certain investments exceeding minimum thresholds.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

COP 0 (no monetary de minimis threshold). VAT registration is mandatory for any legal entity, including non-resident e-commerce operators, that routinely or occasionally makes taxable supplies of goods or services to Colombian customers. Foreign digital suppliers must register through the simplified electronic registration procedure (RUT simplificado) before the first taxable transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for 0% VAT if physically exported out of Colombian customs territory and supported by an export declaration (Declaración de Exportación) accepted by DIAN/Customs. Services qualify for 0% VAT if rendered for the exclusive benefit of a recipient abroad and the use and exploitation occurs outside Colombia, provided the exporter is registered as an exporter (Exportador). Supplies of industrial MRO goods via electronic catalog and cross-border sales must demonstrate that title passes outside Colombia and that the goods leave the national territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación)
  • Bill of Lading (BL) or Air Waybill (AWB)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas)
  • Commercial Invoice issued under DIAN electronic invoicing rules
  • Packing List
  • Evidence of payment and FX repatriation through authorized intermediaries

Colombia mandates the use of electronic invoicing (Factura Electrónica de Venta) for all registered VAT taxpayers. Invoices must be generated in a standardized XML format, digitally signed, and transmitted in real time to DIAN for validation and fiscal clearance via web services or an authorized technology operator (Tecnólogo Autorizado). DIAN validates each invoice before it may be delivered; there is no per-invoice digital stamping, but the DIAN response code constitutes the clearance. Missing validation exposes the taxpayer to sanctions for unregistered invoices.

DIAN may retroactively assess VAT, plus default interest, for up to 5 years if zero-rating documents are missing or false. Penalties for fraudulent export claims range from 100% to 200% of the tax evaded, with additional sanctions up to 5,000 UVTs for invoicing irregularities. Criminal prosecution under Colombian customs law (Law 1762/2015) may apply, including prison terms up to 9 years for export tax fraud, and cancellation of the taxpayer's export registration and VAT status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed convertibility regime under Law 9 of 1991 and External Resolution 8 of 2000. Current-account transactions are convertible through authorized foreign exchange intermediaries, while capital-account inflows require registration as foreign investment in the Banco de la República electronic system. Profit remittance and capital repatriation are permissible once the foreign investment is registered and applicable domestic taxes are cleared. The target ISIC 4791 B2B MRO e-commerce scope is a standard permitted current-account activity, not subject to sector-specific foreign ownership restrictions, and present low convertibility risk provided all cross-border payments are channeled through the official FX market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For merchandise exports, Colombian law requires that export proceeds be collected and channeled through the authorized foreign exchange market within 6 months from the date of the export customs declaration, unless an express exception or bank-supported extension is obtained. The mandatory surrender/canalization ratio is effectively 100% of the export invoice value; the exporter may either convert the foreign currency into Colombian pesos through the authorized intermediary or apply the funds to permitted foreign-currency obligations, and may retain foreign currency offshore only through a registered compensation account (cuenta de compensación). Failure to reintegrate in time can result in exchange-control fines and loss of statutory benefits.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (BANREP) - Foreign Exchange Subdirectorate; DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) validates exchange declarations; Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia supervises authorized foreign exchange intermediaries

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMI accounts (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) are not licensed Colombian deposit-taking institutions, cannot legally hold COP, and are not authorized to act as foreign exchange intermediaries for Colombian statutory FX obligations. They are acceptable only as front-end payment collection rails or ancillary payout cards, but they must never replace a main corporate account with a supervised Colombian bank for export proceeds, tax payments, payroll, or dividend remittance. Use PSP/EMI channels for credit-card MRO catalog checkout, while maintaining the legal entity settlement account at an authorized Colombian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital must be received through an authorized foreign exchange intermediary. The nonresident shareholder should issue a SWIFT MT103 to the SAS's Colombian bank account, clearly referencing the beneficiary NIT and the purpose of 'inversion directa de no residentes en Colombia' (direct nonresident investment in local companies). The receiving bank will execute the statutory exchange declaration (Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional) and may require the Santander-like purpose code category for direct investment; no generic 'remittance' label should be used. The bank verifies the remitter, source of funds, beneficiary identity, and sanctions status before final credit. The capital contribution must be registered in the Banco de la República foreign investment registry through the authorized intermediary to secure future dividend and capital repatriation rights.For day-to-day MRO supplier payments, the primary clearing rail is an outgoing SWIFT MT103 executed by the Colombian bank after purchase of foreign currency from the authorized FX market. For larger, repetitive procurements requiring security, use irrevocable and, where prudent, confirmed documentary letters of credit governed by UCP600, advised through the Colombian bank. Each import payment must be supported by commercial invoice, packing list, transport document (B/L, AWB, CMR) and, when controlled goods are involved, any applicable import registration or prior license. The bank will raise the goods import exchange declaration and require the statutory import purpose concept code.

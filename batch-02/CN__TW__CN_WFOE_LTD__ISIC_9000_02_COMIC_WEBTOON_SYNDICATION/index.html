Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Taiwan's Company Act does not prescribe a minimum paid-up capital for limited liability companies. The subscribers determine the paid-up capital and deposit it into a temporary company bank account; a certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report. No escrow or blocked account is required, provided funds are fully paid before registration and the capital plan is consistent with the Foreign Investment Approval.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable as a Civil Defense fire permit for manufacturing. However, the office premises must comply with the Taiwan Fire Services Act, including fire extinguishers, escape route signage, and building occupancy safety. General workplace safety requirements under the Occupational Safety and Health Act apply. If a small creative studio is included, the local building management office may require an interior decoration or building-use inspection, but no Civil Defense manufacturing license is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic portal: the STA's official online tax service platform accessible via the China Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) system at https://tpass.etax.chinatax.gov.cn and provincial-level e-Tax portals for foreign-invested enterprises.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident investor establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in the People's Republic of China, the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which also serves as the taxpayer identification number. The procurement procedure is: (1) complete the establishment registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) through the enterprise registration online platform to obtain the USCC; (2) within 30 days of the issuance of the business license, complete tax registration with the competent local tax authority (a branch of the State Taxation Administration) through the electronic tax bureau or in-person service hall, submitting the investor's passport or equivalent identity documents, certificate of incorporation, board resolutions, articles of association, registered address proof, and lease agreement for the registered Chinese business premises; (3) upon completion, the tax authority issues the taxpayer registration certificate and activates the enterprise's e-tax account; (4) the WFOE must thereafter use the USCC for all VAT CIT withholding and reporting obligations. No minimum registered capital is required for the tax registration itself, but foreign exchange capital injection rules under the Foreign Investment Law and SAFE circulars must be observed.

Default tax classification

Available election forms

None: China does not operate a check-the-box pass-through election regime for foreign-owned limited liability companies. The WFOE is a resident enterprise taxed on its worldwide income at entity level under the Corporate Income Tax Law. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the digital webtoon publishing and electronic content distribution services rendered by a WFOE, the mandatory VAT registration threshold is effectively zero: enterprises are required to register for VAT regardless of turnover because a limited liability company must issue special VAT invoices and is considered a VAT general taxpayer under the Measures for the Administration of VAT Registration. For small-scale taxpayers the annual sales threshold is RMB 5 million; however a newly established WFOE typically applies for general taxpayer status immediately to facilitate input VAT credits and export zero-rating claims. The statutory threshold for small-scale taxpayers is RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of goods and to qualifying offshore services provided to overseas parties under China's VAT zero-rating and VAT exemption-with-credit policies for cross-border services. A Chinese WFOE in the digital content business can claim zero-rating for services rendered to overseas customers, including digital publishing and syndication of copyrighted webtoon content, where: (1) the service recipient is domiciled outside China; (2) the services are consumed outside China; (3) the consideration is received in foreign exchange or settled through cross-border payment channels; and (4) the enterprise maintains contractual documentation proving the service is exported. The specific regime is the 'VAT zero-rated cross-border services' catalogue under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and supplementary STA announcements. For digital content delivered via online application stores (Apple App Store and Google Play), the WFOE must evidence that the app store acts as a distribution platform and that end-user consumers are located outside mainland China; for the offline export of physical comic books or promotional materials, zero-rating requires physical goods to leave China through customs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Foreign Exchange Receipt Voucher or bank SWIFT credit confirmation evidencing cross-border consideration
  • Export contract or service agreement with the overseas counterparty
  • VAT special invoice or general invoice for export sales (digital e-invoice) cross-referenced to the export declaration
  • Digital service delivery log or download/access records evidencing overseas consumption for zero-rated digital services

China mandates the use of the State Taxation Administration's fully digitalized electronic invoice (e-invoice) system for general VAT taxpayers, with real-time fiscal clearance through the e-Tax platform and the nationwide Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Enterprises are required to adopt the 'fully digitalized electronic invoice' (shuzi dianzi fapiao) regime in the pilot expansion areas and must issue VAT invoices through the STA-authorized tax control system or the newly mandated digital e-invoice system. The system uses cryptographic digital signatures, enterprise digital credentials, and real-time upload of invoice metadata to the STA's central systems for anti-fraud matching. There is no physical fiscal stamping; the nationwide platform conducts automated cross-verification against VAT returns, customs declarations, and export documentation. For cross-border transactions, the electronic port authority (China International Trade Single Window) interoperates with the VAT system to reconcile export invoices and customs declarations.

The State Taxation Administration and the General Administration of Customs jointly scrutinize export zero-rating claims through the Golden Tax System and the China International Trade Single Window data matching engine. Penalties for fraudulent or unsubstantiated export zero-rating include: (1) forced reversal of the zero-rated treatment and retroactive collection of the applicable VAT rate (13% or 9% depending on service classification) plus late surcharge; (2) administrative penalties of 50% to 500% of the unlawfully obtained tax credit or refund under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and the VAT implementing rules; (3) forfeiture of the VAT refund or input credit claimed; (4) inclusion in the tax violation blacklist with enhanced supervision and reduced tax credit rating; and (5) criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund crimes under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, which imposes imprisonment and fines where the defrauded refund amount exceeds the criminal threshold. Foreign exchange repatriation beyond the 180-day statutory deadline without an approved extension causes the export proceeds verification records to be marked non-performing, leading to mandatory VAT reversal and penalties under the foreign exchange administration rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China's FX regulatory framework divides the current account and capital account. Payments for digital content licensing, webtoon subscription settlements, and related service trade fees are classified as current account transactions and are freely convertible once genuine transaction documents are presented and verified by a designated bank. Profit remittance by the WFOE to its Taiwan shareholder is permitted as current account profit remittance after statutory annual audit, corporate income tax filing, avoidance of double taxation verification, and a FIE profit distribution resolution; SAFE and the remitting bank will require proof of audited financials, tax clearance, board resolution, and prior capital verification. Capital account transactions including equity investment, shareholder loans, and liquidation proceeds remain subject to pre-registration with SAFE, bank quotas, and strict use restrictions. No general convertibility right exists for capital account items; each transaction requires a legal basis or regulatory approval.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: generally required where annual net sales and non-operating income reach at least NT$300 million or the aggregate amount of controlled transactions reaches at least NT$200 million, subject to statutory exemptions and transaction-specific rules. Master File: generally required for a Taiwan constituent entity of an MNE group where consolidated group revenue reaches at least NT$3 billion. Country-by-Country Reporting generally applies at the group threshold of approximately NT$27 billion or the equivalent EUR 750 million threshold, subject to current Taiwan rules.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a single broad offshore Economic Substance Regulation regime equivalent to certain low-tax jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant under transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance rule, beneficial-owner analysis, controlled foreign company rules, treaty anti-abuse standards and source-of-income rules. The company should maintain Taiwan personnel, decision-making records, premises or operational infrastructure, IP and licensing control, risk assumption, customer and platform functions and commercially credible accounting records consistent with the claimed profit allocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment, recovery of unpaid tax, statutory surcharges, interest and criminal prosecution. Intentional evasion may result in imprisonment of up to five years and a criminal fine of up to NT$10 million under the Tax Collection Act, subject to the specific offense and court determination.

Director personal liability: The company is ordinarily a separate legal person, but the responsible person, director, manager, accountant or other participant may face criminal prosecution, civil restitution, tax recovery, asset preservation and restrictions on departure from Taiwan for serious tax arrears or statutory violations. The corporate veil is not routinely pierced solely because tax is unpaid.
False invoices and VAT fraud

Statutory sanction: Rejection of input credits, additional business tax, statutory fines that may be multiple times the improperly claimed or evaded tax, interest and criminal sanctions for aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons and participating officers may incur criminal liability, joint tax recovery, civil damages and restrictions on departure where statutory conditions are met.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent may be assessed for the unpaid tax, interest, penalties and certificate-related fines. Treaty relief may be denied and the payer may become liable for the shortfall.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may be pursued where they authorized or knowingly participated in non-withholding, concealment or fraudulent documentation.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustment to taxable income, denial of deductions, deemed income or deemed interest, withholding reassessment, interest, penalties and documentation-related sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors generally face corporate rather than automatic personal liability, but may be liable for intentional fraud, breach of fiduciary duty, unlawful distributions or participation in tax evasion.
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reallocation of income, disallowance of expenses, permanent-establishment or Taiwan-source income assessment, interest, penalties and possible anti-avoidance adjustment.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face civil claims, tax recovery and criminal liability if sham arrangements, false records or fraudulent declarations are used. A mere low-substance profile is not automatically criminal but is a severe audit indicator.
Unlawful profit distribution and capital impairment

Statutory sanction: Invalid or challengeable distribution, repayment obligation, director liability for damages, shareholder repayment exposure and possible corporate-law fines.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for restitution and damages caused by unlawful distributions. Criminal liability may arise where false accounts, fraud or breach of trust is established.
AML, sanctions and suspicious payment activity

Statutory sanction: Account freezing, transaction rejection, regulatory reporting, administrative fines and criminal prosecution under Taiwan AML and related laws. Serious money-laundering offenses may carry substantial imprisonment and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and beneficial owners who direct, facilitate or knowingly ignore laundering activity may face criminal imprisonment, fines, confiscation, civil restitution and travel restrictions.
Intellectual-property and unauthorized content exploitation

Statutory sanction: Civil injunctions, damages, disgorgement, destruction of infringing materials and potential criminal copyright penalties. Unlicensed royalties may also create tax deduction and withholding disputes.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers may face personal liability where they knowingly authorize infringement, participate in commercial exploitation or abuse the corporate form to evade judgments.
Data, consumer and platform compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, corrective orders, service restrictions, consumer refunds and civil damages under applicable Taiwan data-protection and consumer-protection laws.

Director personal liability: Personal liability depends on the applicable statute, knowledge, authorization and degree of participation. Directors may face damages claims for negligent governance or failure to implement required controls.
Mainland-China and Taiwan jurisdiction misclassification

Statutory sanction: Invalid filings, missed tax registrations, dual-jurisdiction assessments, penalties, withholding failures, foreign-exchange issues and possible licensing or investment-compliance consequences.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face administrative sanctions, tax recovery, civil liability and criminal exposure where the misclassification involves intentional concealment or false declarations.

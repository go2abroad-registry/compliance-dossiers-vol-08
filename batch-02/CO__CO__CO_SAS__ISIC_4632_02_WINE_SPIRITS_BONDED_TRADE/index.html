Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); official portal: www.dian.gov.co (Sede Electrónica DIAN)

E-government filing portal

A Colombian SAS formed by foreign shareholders must first obtain NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) via notarial deed, commercial registry filing with the local Chamber of Commerce, and mandatory enrollment in RUT (Registro Único Tributario) at DIAN. NIT serves as both corporate tax ID and VAT ID. The RUT application must be signed through the electronic Sede Electrónica DIAN and may require a resident legal representative if no Colombian branch office exists.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; resident SAS is a separate corporate taxpayer subject to Colombian income tax on worldwide income. No pass-through treatment. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; resident SAS is a separate corporate taxpayer subject to Colombian income tax on worldwide income. No pass-through treatment. A foreign Parent is not attributed the SAS tax results except under controlled foreign corporation rules.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Colombia. Available elections include (i) income tax consolidation for qualifying domestic groups with >70% indirect ownership, (ii) election to be taxed under the optional SIMPLE regime for low-turnover taxpayers (provided business activity restrictions permit; bonded liquor wholesale is generally excluded), and (iii) prior authorization for fiscal price-transfer margins in the customs bonded regime. Hybrid mismatch elections are unavailable; Colombia applies anti-hybrid rules under its CFC legislation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No fixed economic turnover threshold triggers VAT liability. Any entity, including a bonded warehouse wholesaler of wine and spirits, is legally obliged to obtain RUT registration, NIT, and VAT identification before commencing taxable shipments or imports. Simplified VAT regime applies only to certain small retailers, not to bonded trade operators.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rate VAT applies to exports of tangible goods, including Scotch whisky, Bordeaux wines, and other spirits, when the goods physically leave the Colombian customs territory under a definitive export regime. Sales from a customs bonded warehouse to a foreign buyer are eligible only if followed by a final export declaration (Form 470). Supply of services to foreign beneficiaries qualifies if the services are exclusively used outside Colombia and the recipient has no domicile or establishment in Colombia. Specific documentary and customs disposal rules apply to bonded stock transfers into mainland Colombia.

  • Form 470 – Declaración de Exportación (Customs Export Declaration) definitively accepted by DIAN
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or equivalent multimodal transport document) evidencing departure from a Colombian port or airport
  • Commercial export invoice referencing NIT, RUT, and bonded warehouse regime code
  • Export contract or purchase order issued by the foreign customer
  • Foreign-currency payment certificate (Certificado de Ingreso y Pago al Exterior) or equivalent inward telegram evidencing repatriation of export proceeds

Mandatory electronic invoicing (facturación electrónica) is required at the time of each taxable transaction. Invoices must be generated as XML/CII and transmitted to DIAN for validación in near-real-time through an authorized software provider, web-service API, or DIAN ’s free electronic platform. Each invoice receives a CUFE or CUDS digital stamp; no invoice is deemed valid for tax offsetting or export refund without DIAN reception and acceptance. Indexed customs and logistics records must be synchronized with the Muisca system for bonded movements.

DIAN may undertake post-audits for up to five years after export filing. False or unsupported zero-rating claims result in immediate re-invoicing of the omitted IVA at 19%, plus moratorium interest (approximately 12% per annum, subject to monthly actuarial updates) and penalties up to 200% of the inaccurate tax claim. Pure export fraud can trigger a two-year prohibition on obtaining export refunds and CIDC (Comité de Impuestos); criminal customs liability under Law 1762/2015 may impose imprisonment for smuggling or customs false documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed but broadly convertible exchange system. Current-account transactions are fully channeled through the authorized foreign-exchange market with minimal documentary burden, while capital-account inflows and outflows require investor registration, declaration of change, and compliance with the national exchange regime. Profit remittance is permitted for registered foreign investment after the applicable annual income tax return, the formal registration of the investment with the Banco de la República, and provision of the corresponding Formulario No. 4 or equivalent channeling declaration. Bonded-warehouse wholesale of wine and spirits requires careful documentary support because alcohol trade is treated as enhanced-risk for money-laundering and tax-evasion controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be channeled through an authorized bank in Colombia within six months of the invoice date or the date of exportation, unless a verified extension is granted. Mandatory conversion ratios are governed by the national exchange regime; current practical application does not impose a fixed surrender percentage but requires all export proceeds to be negotiated or sold in the foreign-exchange market through an authorized intermediary, typically converting at the market rate on the day of channeling. For bonded-trade operations, proceeds from re-export of wines and spirits remain subject to the same channeling duty, and no direct offshore retention is permitted unless previously authorized by the central bank.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia; foreign-exchange supervision and enforcement coordinate through the Comité de Cambios and the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) for customs-linked currency formality.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility for the core treasury function. Colombian law does not recognize offshore digital EMIs, such as Wise or Revolut-style platforms, as authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries for corporate capital-channeling purposes. Such entities may assist informational transfers but cannot satisfy the legal obligation of channeling export proceeds or making COP-denominated tax and payroll payments. Domestic fintech platforms (e.g., Nequi and Filigrania) are limited to local retail payment instruments and do not replace a licensed commercial bank for cross-border trade settlements, FX declarations, or imports under documentary credits.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound foreign-direct-capital routing must first channel funds to the local bank account held in the name of the Colombian S.A.S. through an authorized intermediary, using a wire message that names the foreign investor as the remitter and the Colombian company as beneficiary. The funds must be identified with the proper channeling purpose code in the Banco de la República's foreign-exchange form—typically Formulario No. 4 (Declaración de Cambio para Inversiones Internacionales) or the applicable computerized declaration for capital contributions—and the investment must be registered within the applicable window after the operation. The bank will verify the tax identification numbers of both parties, the declared amount, and the consistency of the beneficiary's business purpose with the registered economic activity under ISIC 4632. Funds for working capital that are not registered as direct investment may be blocked by the bank's foreign-exchange compliance desk, so the company must instruct the remitting investor to follow an equity or capital-contribution purpose code and must not combine trade financings with equity injections.Outbound trade disbursements for the purchase or clearance of wine and spirits from bonded warehouses must be settled through the local bank using SWIFT MT103 for plain wire transfers of import-payment channeling declarations, or through a Commercial Letter of Credit—typically an irrevocable documentary credit per UCP600—for larger or sensitive trade disbursements in which the bank must authenticate invoices, bills of lading, and excise certificates. Payment instructions must carry the correct purpose code (importaciones de bienes), the DIAN import declaration number, and a reference to the purchase order, to allow the bank to complete the national exchange regime reporting. Disbursements from an operational account in COP to a foreign beneficiary in USD will be automatically converted at the bank's prevailing FX rate; the corporate must however pre-register the operation through the bank's electronic platform to avoid a compliance hold.Forward exchange contracts with authorized commercial banks, entered into for the documented foreign-exchange exposure arising from wine and spirits import or export contracts.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted for non-resident counterparties hedging their exposure on COP where genuine underlying commercial exposure is evidenced.FX swaps (compraventa simultánea) and cross-currency swaps, available from tier-1 banks provided the operation is registered in the national exchange system and has a lawful underlying transaction.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% (Colombian national corporate income tax under Art. 240 Estatuto Tributario; financial and utilities surcharges disregarded for a standard S.A.S. bonded wholesale trader).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends paid or credited to non-resident individuals or foreign entities from profits already taxed at the S.A.S. level. If profits are distributed from untaxed or exempt income, an additional compensatory 35% income tax is generally imposed under the Colombian dividend rules (Art. 245 E.T. and related provisions).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual return due during April of the following fiscal year in accordance with the DIAN-filed 'calendario tributario' keyed to the last digit of the NIT; e.g., FY2024 returns generally fall between 14 April and 22 April 2025. No general extension for ordinary filers.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 – NIT y razón social Tax identification number, corporate name, S.A.S. form, registered address, and foreign shareholder identification data. Foreign shareholders are not taxed on this return; the S.A.S. must identify each non-resident partner and maintain a Colombian RUT registration and designated agent for service. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing obligations apply to an S.A.S. whose gross assets or gross income exceed COP 4,706,500,000 (100,000 UVT for fiscal 2024) and which carries out transactions with related parties, tax-haven jurisdictions, or entities established in no-tax/weltwirt regimes. The Local File threshold is additionally met where intercompany transactions with related parties exceed COP 900,000,000 in the taxable year; Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply the Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, substance and economic reality are enforced through the Estatuto Tributario general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR, Art. 658 E.T.), anti-abusive reunion of legal entities, substance-over-form jurisprudence by the Consejo de Estado, and the OECD BEPS LOB clauses embedded in Colombia's DTAs. A bonded wine/spirits trading S.A.S. must demonstrate real business functions, physical premises, employees, and assumed risk to withstand tax and AML challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misdeclaration of Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion carries imprisonment from 4 to 9 years and a fine equal to 100% of the tax evaded; civil penalties from DIAN include the 100% inaccurate-declaration penalty, monthly default interest, and confiscatory tax reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders who knowingly sign false financial statements or authorise fictitious costs may be personally jointly liable; under Law 1258/2008 (S.A.S. regime) the separate legal personality is pierced when liability is an instrument of fraud. Travel bans and asset embargoes may be applied during criminal tax proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may reassess the taxable base at arm's length, add a 30% punitive adjustment on the corrected income, and impose administrative fines of up to 70,000 UVT; excess interest deductions may be disallowed and carryforwards forfeited.

Director personal liability: Officers who sign fraudulent transfer pricing documents without real control or substance risk joint criminal responsibility and civil restitution for the unpaid tax; DIAN can reject the corporate veil where the S.A.S. is used to shift profit artificially.
AML / Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Abuses

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Colombian Criminal Code Art. 323 carries 8–15 years' imprisonment plus fines up to 10,000 current legal monthly minimum wages; the UIAF may impose administrative fines and freeze corporate assets; the entity may be subject to liquidation if found to be a front company.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to criminal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and extradition where they knowingly facilitated untraceable payments; corporate veil is pierced automatically for money laundering proceeds; professional sanctions, including disqualification from managing commercial companies, may be imposed.
Bonded Warehouse / Customs and Spirits Trade Violations

Statutory sanction: DIAN and customs authorities may seize the goods, impose fines equal to 100%–300% of the customs value, revoke the bonded warehouse license, and apply the alcohol tax and import duties retroactively with default interest; repeated breaches justify permanent closure.

Director personal liability: Company officers who ordered or tolerated the unlawful movement of goods bear direct criminal and customs liability, including imprisonment of 2 to 8 years, personal restitution of lost duties, and prohibition from holding a bonded trade license or serving as directors in customs-regulated companies.

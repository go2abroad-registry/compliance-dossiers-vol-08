Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum paid-up capital is required for an S.A.S. under Ley 1258 de 2008. The by-laws may set any capital amount, in any currency, and payment may be deferred up to two years, unless the by-laws specify a shorter term. No escrow deposit is required for incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Cuerpo de Bomberos through the Oficina de Prevención de Incendios; structural load inspection for factory racking and battery storage areas; occupational risk prevention plan registered with the ARL; and workplace inspection certificate (Certificado de Condiciones Sanitarias) from the Secretaría Distrital de Salud where assembly and storage activities are conducted.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum paid-up capital is required for an S.A.S. under Ley 1258 de 2008. The by-laws may set any capital amount, in any currency, and payment may be deferred up to two years, unless the by-laws specify a shorter term. No escrow deposit is required for incorporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Unidad Administrativa Especial Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) — official portal: www.dian.gov.co; electronic taxpayer services (Oficina Virtual, Muisca, RUT pre-registration and e-invoicing systems) are accessed through sede.dian.gov.co and the 'Sede Electrónica DIAN'.

E-government filing portal

Foreign founders of a Colombian S.A.S. must (1) execute the private deed of incorporation before a notary and register it with the competent Cámara de Comercio (Chamber of Commerce) obtaining the certificate of existence and legal representation; (2) obtain a local fiscal address and appoint a legal representative resident in Colombia; (3) complete DIAN's online 'Pre-RUT' and 'Pre-NIT' registration, which requires the chamber of commerce certificate, the corporate document, the identification documents of directors/legal representatives, and evidence of fiscal domicile; (4) undergo DIAN biometric verification and digital signature ('firma electrónica') enrollment to obtain the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria); and (5) finalize the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) with the applicable CIIU codes for ISIC 3091 (CIIU 3091) and tax responsibilities for corporate income tax, VAT, self-withholdings, and exporter status. The RUT is mandatory for the SAS to issue electronic invoices and carry out customs operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the S.A.S. is a separate juridical person taxed as a Colombian resident company on worldwide income; Colombian law does not authorize partnership-style pass-through treatment for an S.A.S.; non-resident shareholders are subject to dividend withholding tax on distributed profits, with possible reduction under applicable tax treaties).

Available election forms

Colombia has no 'check-the-box' election. Available statutory mechanisms in the defined context: (a) election by a qualifying parent/holding company for the 'Régimen de Compañías Holding Colombianas (CINCE)' under Law 2277 of 2022 and Decree 1325 of 2023, which exempts dividends received from foreign subsidiaries and capital gains on indirect disposals; (b) election for 'Usuario Zona Franca' status (decree 2147/2016) in an authorized Free Trade Zone, yielding a 20% corporate income tax rate and special customs treatment for export-oriented manufacturing; (c) election by entities carrying out only export services to claim 'exporter' VAT status in the RUT; and (d) hybrid-mismatch election rules are not elective in Colombia — E.T. Article 130-1 and related provisions apply against deduction/non-inclusion mismatches automatically, not through an affirmative election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to SAS legal entities: any company performing taxable IVA activities in Colombian territory is obligated to register with DIAN as an 'Responsable del Régimen Común' from the date of corporate activation, irrespective of revenue level. The 'Régimen Simplificado' exclusion is reserved for natural persons meeting restrictive conditions (income below approximately COP 164.7 million (3,500 UVT for 2024), gross assets below COP 150 million, and non-import/export activity), so an electric cargo e-bike manufacturer cannot rely on de minimis exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including electric cargo bicycles under CIIU 3091) are zero-rated for VAT pursuant to E.T. Articles 481 and 482, provided the exporter holds the 'Exportador' classification in the RUT, invoicing reflects a foreign buyer, the goods physically leave Colombia as declared, and shipment occurs within the statutory term (generally 90 days from the invoice date, extendable by DIAN in justified cases). Provision of services strictly related to the export process (international cargo transit, customs logistics, and services used or consumed exclusively abroad by foreign recipients) may also qualify for zero-rating when formalized with a clear export service contract and foreign currency payment. The manufacturer must simultaneously submit the export declaration (DEX) through DIAN's 'VUCE'/'SIGMA' platform and retain evidence of the physical shipment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX/DUE) accepted by DIAN customs office (with physical exit verified)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Cargo Manifest evidencing international shipment
  • Commercial invoice coherent with the electronically transmitted FEV (including amount, parties, Incoterms 2020 terms)
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (where applicable to bilateral trade preferences)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Negocio de Cambio) or SWIFT/telegraphic transfer confirmation proving receipt of export proceeds in Colombia
  • Proof of RUT 'Exportador' status and, if applicable, free-zone user authorization

All VAT-responsible S.A.S. taxpayers must issue a 'Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV)' using UBL 2.1 XML signed with the taxpayer's digital certificate and transmit it to DIAN in real time for validation and legal clearance either through DIAN's free 'Facturador Electrónico DIAN' or an authorized service provider (FSE/TSP). Once validated, DIAN assigns a unique CUNE (Código Único de Numeración), and the invoice must present a QR code and be issued in a PDF/A-2.1 representation as required by Decree 2242/2015 and later amendments. This mandate applies to issuing 'notas crédito/débito', 'documento soporte en adquisiciones', and 'remisiones' and requires filing of the daily 'Información Exógena' (population registers of sales/purchases) with DIAN.

DIAN's fiscal control division will scrutinize documentation consistency, CUNE-valid invoices, DEX filing, transportation terms, and the physical exit of goods. Failure to meet the shipping window (90 days, unless extended) converts the zero-rating into a 19% VAT obligation retroactively, generating value-added tax, daily moratory interest, and the ‘sanción por inexactitud’ (up to 100% of the omitted tax, increased to 200% when fraudulent misrepresentation is established). If the manufacturer claims a refund of prior export VAT credits and the DEX is subsequently annulled or the shipment is not properly validated, DIAN orders retrospective clawback of the refunded amounts plus interest and penalties, may impose ‘sanción por devolución y/o compensación improcedente’ (usually 20% of the improperly refunded amount), and may refer the case for criminal tax fraud prosecution under Law 1819/2016 (punishable for aggravated fraudulent export procedures).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) operates under a managed-float (dirty float) regime with full current-account convertibility covering goods, services, and profit remittances, conditional on mandatory channeling through an IMC. Capital-account transactions (FDI, foreign loans) are free but must be channeled via an IMC and registered with the Banco de la República when cumulative direct investment equals or exceeds USD 500,000 in order to preserve dividend-remittance and capital-repatriation guarantees. Dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders require proof of payment or certification of colombian corporate income tax, execution of the Declaración de Cambio through the remitting IMC, and settlement of the 10% non-resident dividend withholding (subject to treaty relief); no pre-approval is required and no fixed percentage conversion quota exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

One hundred percent (100%) of goods-export proceeds must be channeled (canalizadas) through the regulated foreign-exchange market within the statutory term: 6 months from shipment date under the baseline rule of Resolución Externa 1/2018, Art. 96, extended to 13 months for goods exports by Decreto 1194 of 2023. The exporter must elect one of three compliant routes: (i) sell the foreign currency to an IMC at the prevailing TRM, (ii) credit the proceeds to a locally held foreign-currency compensation account (cuenta de compensación) opened with an IMC, or (iii) credit an eligible offshore account under the compensation regime; deductible expenses (freight, insurance, export financing) are excluded from the channelable amount, and all compensation accounts are subject to quarterly reporting to the Banco de la República under Circular DCIN-83.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (Junta Directiva), the sole monetary and exchange-rate authority, governs the foreign-exchange regime through Resolución Externa No. 1 of 2018 (as amended, including Decreto 1194 of 2023) and implementing Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83; the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC) supervises all Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario (IMC) authorized to execute regulated FX operations, while the DIAN enforces customs, import/export, and tax declaration integration with cross-border flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as the sole treasury or FX-clearing channel; MEDIUM as an ancillary expense-card utility. Legal constraints: Colombia's FX regime (Resolución Externa 1/2018) requires all exchange-market transactions, including export-proceeds channeling, import payments, FDI inflows, and dividend remittances, to be routed exclusively through SFC-supervised IMCs. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Mercury) are not licensed as IMCs, cannot issue COP accounts, cannot connect to the domestic clearing system (ACH/CENIT), and cannot fulfill DIAN tax payments; interposing an EMI in any FX-market transaction triggers SFC administrative sanctions, potential criminal FX exposure, and DIAN transfer-pricing recharacterization. EMIs are therefore limited to immaterial operating purchases (software subscriptions, digital advertising) and must be reconciled back to the IMC channel in the company's books.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Form 120 are required when gross income or gross assets exceed 92,000 UVT (approximately COP 4.58 billion for tax year 2025) and related-party cross-border transactions exceed 45,000 UVT (approximately COP 2.24 billion). Master File is additionally required when the multinational group achieves consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million, as provided by Colombian transfer pricing regulations.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not have a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the Law 1819 of 2016 general anti-avoidance rule and Articles 260-1 to 260-11 E.T. require economic reality, business purpose, and substance for intercompany transactions. The e-bike manufacturing S.A.S. must maintain physical premises, production equipment, actual personnel, inventory movement, commercial risk, and decision-making functions in Colombia to support its invoice flows and to defend transfer pricing positions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Returns

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanctions under Articles 642 and 647 E.T.: 100% inexactness penalty on the underpaid tax, monthly moratorium interest, extemporaneous filing surcharges, and automatic disqualification from certain tax benefits. Criminal prosecution under Law 2277 of 2022 and Law 1762 of 2015 carries imprisonment of 48 to 108 months, fines up to 150% of the evaded tax, and public-contracting debarment.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency under Article 794 E.T. During criminal proceedings, courts may order travel bans, asset embargoes, personal passport surrender, and seizure of personal and bank assets under money-laundering and extinction-of-ownership rules.
Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the entity under the Colombian anti-avoidance rule and Article 42 of Law 1258 of 2008, recharacterize income as belonging to the real economic owner, disallow transfer pricing documentation, and impose a TP penalty computed on the value of related-party transactions. The resulting deficiency carries interest, a penalty of 100% for misstatements, and the loss of treaty or Free Zone benefits.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierceable when the S.A.S. is used as an instrument of fraud or tax evasion. Directors and shareholders who caused the shell arrangement are personally liable for the corporate tax debt, and their personal property may be attached; criminal liability for fraudulent tax evasion may also result in imprisonment and travel restrictions.
AML / Financing of Prohibited Actors

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under Law 526 of 1999 and UIAF rules can reach approximately COP 2.5 billion. Criminal money-laundering liability under Article 323 of Law 599 of 2000 carries imprisonment of 10 to 30 years, fines of not less than 1,300 times the legal minimum monthly wage, and permanent asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute, direct, or tolerate such transactions become criminally co-authors. Courts impose preventive travel bans, freezing orders on personal accounts, and civil restitution; the limited liability structure of the S.A.S. will not protect a director who personally benefited or knowingly orchestrated the illicit flow.

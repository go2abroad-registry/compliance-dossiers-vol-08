Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A WFOE Limited Liability Company is an entity-level resident corporate taxpayer in Taiwan with corporate income tax on worldwide income and Business Tax registration on Taiwan supplies; no pass-through or territorial-scope election is available.

Available election forms

None under Taiwan's corporate income tax and Business Tax Act. No check-the-box election, no partnership pass-through election, and no Taiwan hybrid-mismatch election is available for a WFOE limited liability company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No TWD turnover threshold triggers VAT registration. Under the Business Tax Act, any business selling taxable goods or services in Taiwan must apply for tax registration before starting operations. Certain small-scale businesses with monthly sales below the Ministry of Finance's small-scale business standard may qualify for a simplified 1% levy, but registration remains mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to WFOE executive search and headhunting services under Business Tax Act Article 7 when the service is supplied to a foreign client, the service is not consumed in Taiwan, and the WFOE receives consideration in foreign currency remitted from abroad through a Taiwan designated foreign-exchange bank. The service contract, retainer agreement, and final placement documentation must demonstrate that the placed executive is employed in a position outside the Taiwan territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) for any physical goods exported from Taiwan, where applicable
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical cross-border movement where goods are included in the export sales
  • Commercial Invoice / zero-rated Uniform Invoice clearly marked 0% and referencing the export service
  • Signed executive search retainer agreement with the foreign client
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate (bank-issued inward remittance record) through the Central Bank Foreign Exchange System
  • Evidence of service performance and overseas usage, including the placed candidate's overseas employer, work location, and final placement report

Taiwan operates a Uniform Invoice regime. Electronic invoices must be issued through the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Cloud platform and transmitted to the E-Invoice Turnkey Service using encrypted XML client-to-gateway APIs near real-time. The platform validates invoice numbers and line-item data; it does not pre-approve each invoice with a fiscal stamp. Paper Uniform Invoices remain acceptable without real-time clearance. A zero-rated export service must be evidenced by a Uniform Invoice marked with the zero rate, not the standard 5% rate.

Where zero-rating cannot be substantiated, the tax authority will reclassify the sales as 5% taxable supplies, assess underpaid Business Tax, disallow associated input VAT credits, and may impose a surcharge of 5 to 15 times the amount of tax evaded under the Business Tax Act. Fraudulent or unverified zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective clawback, criminal penalty provisions for tax evasion, and the relevant period for assessment extends to the statutory limitation period applicable to Business Tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under China's legally administered float and the PRC Foreign Exchange Control Regulations, the yuan (CNY) is convertible for current account items but not freely convertible for capital account items. The WFOE, being a resident entity, may settle service trade receipts (executive search retainers and placement fees) freely with documentary evidence, provided the transactions are genuine and not structured. Capital account operations, including foreign capital injection, shareholder loan repayment, and cross-border profit remittance, are subject to prior SAFE registration, verification, and sometimes quota restrictions. Dividends distributed from after-tax profits may be remitted offshore only after an annual statutory audit and tax clearance; the bank will require the audited financial statements, the tax payment certificates, and the shareholders' resolution. Additionally, the historical requirement to surrender foreign exchange receipts to the central bank has been fully replaced with voluntary retention in foreign currency settlement accounts. The regime is therefore workable for a licensed human-resources firm, but any attempt to convert capital account flows through current account avenues, such as mispriced service fees, would trigger civil and criminal liability.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods trade, the current regulations require export proceeds to be repatriated into China within a maximum of 30 days after the customs declaration, and the enterprise can choose to retain the foreign currency in a current account settlement account or settle it into RMB on a market basis; no fixed surrender ratio applies. For service trade (executive search and headhunting), there is no mandatory surrender or fixed settlement ratio; receipts must simply be collected into the WFOE's foreign currency current account through a designated bank and supported by the underlying service contract, invoice, and proof of service completion. All service trade amounts must be reported under the correct trade item and are subject to bank authenticity review.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), supported by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the administering authority for cross-border foreign exchange transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Not viable. China's licensing regime prohibits offshore digital EMI schemes (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut, or similar) from serving a China-registered WFOE without a domestic bank license or a formal joint-venture with a licensed Chinese clearing bank. No standalone fintech EMI can hold a CNY operational account or execute cross-border RMB settlement; the only lawful rails are the eligible designated foreign currency bank accounts at authorized institutions. Any attempt to route payments through a third-party inline EMI would violate SAFE's 'Real Transaction' principle and expose the entity to account freezing and administrative sanctions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwanese parent must first complete SAFE FDI registration for the newly established WFOE, and the WFOE will then open a Foreign Exchange Capital Account (Currency Converted Account) at a licensed bank. The incoming wire must specify the SAFE registration number, remitter name, purpose code 'Investments in Foreign-Invested Enterprises' (code 622), and reference the shareholder resolution. After the transfer arrives, a PRC Certified Public Accountant must issue a capital contribution verification report before any funds can be disbursed. The capital account funds are monitored through the bank's SAFE portal, and every use must be declared against a predefined operational purpose.For service trade disbursements such as sub-agent retainers to overseas search partners or refunds to clients, the WFOE should initiate SWIFT MT103 payments from its foreign current account or RMB basic account. Because executive search services do not involve physical goods, commercial documentary letters of credit and documentary collections are inapplicable; the appropriate clearing rail is the standard wire transfer. Each payment must be backed by an FX purpose code (e.g., 'Service Trade - Commercial and Consulting Services'), the underlying service contract, tax withholding certificate, and any applicable overseas contract fee invoice. Payments must be made to the contracted beneficiary's named corporate bank account, not to a shell or personal account.Deliverable forward foreign exchange contracts (USD/CNY) on a real-demand hedging basis, available only for crystallized current account exposuresFX swaps and currency swaps with a licensed bank, limited to the notional matching the service-trade receivables/payables and approved by SAFE under the 'real transaction' principlePlain-vanilla FX options purchased on an outright hedging basis (straddles not permitted if speculative); onshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are prohibited for the Chinese WFOE, though the offshore Taiwanese parent can source offshore NDFs independently.

Turnover: Under Taiwan Company Act Article 20 and the MOEA audit certification rules, an independent certified CPA audit is legally mandated where any one of the following quantitative triggers is met at fiscal year-end: (i) paid-in capital exceeds NT$30 million (approximately USD 1,000,000); (ii) annual net operating revenue exceeds NT$100 million (approximately USD 3,330,000); (iii) year-end total assets exceed NT$100 million (approximately USD 3,330,000); (iv) year-end bank borrowings exceed NT$30 million (approximately USD 1,000,000); or (v) the WFOE is a parent company required to produce consolidated financial statements. Headcount is not a general audit trigger under Taiwan company law; however, for an ISIC 7830 executive-search licensee, placing 30 or more workers or performing cross-border placements in a calendar year triggers a Ministry of Labor statutory inspection regime requiring a CPA-certified annual statement of service fees and escrowed labor-contract funds, making a certified audit effectively compulsory at that headcount threshold.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 40 million in related-party service transactions (e.g., executive search management fees, shared services and commission payments) triggers mandatory Local File preparation. Higher thresholds are RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers/use, and RMB 100 million for financial asset loans. Master File is required when the group's consolidated annual global related-party revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion and related-party transactions in China are recorded. The annual related-party transaction form (A109000) and the summary schedule (A105000) must be filed regardless of amount when any related party transaction occurs.

Economic substance scope: China applies the economic substance doctrine through Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules. The tax authority may recharacterize transactions, disregard entities lacking CIGA, and deny treaty benefits. A WFOE must maintain substantive physical office, full-time qualified staff including the executive search researchers, local decision-making authority in the placement process, independent risk management, and bank accounts in China. Core income-generating activities - candidate sourcing, interviews, background checks, offer negotiation and client relationship management - must occur in China. Where these CIGA remain in Taiwan, the Chinese tax authority will treat the WFOE as a low-risk commissionaire or realize upward transfer pricing to reflect returns to the functions, assets and risks actually borne in China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Violates Article 63 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law (TATC): the tax authority shall order the taxpayer to pay underpaid tax plus a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and impose a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes criminal liability for tax evasion: a fine and imprisonment of up to 7 years for large evasion amounts exceeding RMB 50,000 after repeated administrative punishment.

Director personal liability: Directors who designed or signed such fraudulent returns are jointly and severally liable with the WFOE for tax underpayments under Article 20 of the Company Law (abuse of corporate personality). The legal representative is subject to a border exit ban under TATC Article 44 until taxes, fines, and interest are fully settled. Directors are also listed on the Joint Punishment Memorandum for Tax Dishonesty, leading to a national blacklist affecting credit, banking, aviation and luxury consumption. If criminal amounts are involved, imprisonment up to 7 years applies to responsible directors and CFO.
Transfer Pricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 44 of the EIT Law and Implementation Regulation Article 123, the tax authority may make a special tax adjustment, recalculating taxable income at arm's length, and will impose late-payment interest of the base rate plus 5 percentage points for failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation. Administrative penalties under TATC Article 63 may amount to 50%-150% of the underpaid tax if the transfer pricing adjustment is based on misreporting; daily 0.05% surcharge continues to accrue.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize a transfer pricing scheme that results in underdeclared tax are personally exposed to civil restitution. The Chinese tax authority may pierce the corporate veil if the WFOE is a pure shell performing no CIGA, imposing joint liability on the Taiwan parent. Personal consequences include travel bans, confiscation of luxury assets of the responsible director, inability to leave mainland China pending settlement, and criminal liability for tax evasion in aggravated cases.
Withholding Tax and Profit Repatriation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Violates Article 37 of the EIT Law and Article 69 of the TATC. The withholding agent is required to pay the amount that should have been withheld, and the tax authority imposes a fine equal to 50% to 300% of the un-withheld amount, plus a daily late surcharge of 0.05%. The non-resident taxpayer remains liable for the underlying tax and may be denied refunds.

Director personal liability: The legal representative who executes the dividend remittance without proper WHT is personally subject to exit restrictions: the tax authority may apply to the court or border police to ban the individual from leaving China until all withholding taxes and penalties are paid. Directors who knowingly used a second-level offshore pass-through to conceal dividend beneficiaries face tax criminal liability; the corporate veil will be pierced to hold the actual controlling directors personally liable to the Treasury for the unpaid WHT, plus civil damages to minority shareholders, and up to 7 years' imprisonment under Article 201.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, via the National Electronic Tax Bureau portal (e-Tax platform) at etax.chinatax.gov.cn; local tax service branches administer entity-level registration and compliance.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) obtains a business license carrying the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The SAMR data is automatically shared with the State Taxation Administration, and the WFOE must complete tax registration within 30 days by accessing the National Electronic Tax Bureau, submitting the business license, legal representative identification, articles of association, registered address proof, bank account information, and the completed tax registration declaration. The tax authority issues the tax registration certificate and the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), which is the 18-digit USCC. For a non-resident enterprise such as the Taiwanese parent company that has no establishment in China, a non-resident TIN is obtained separately by filing the non-resident enterprise tax registration form, either directly with the competent tax authority or through the local withholding agent appointed by the WFOE.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Upon incorporation, the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) obtains a business license carrying the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The SAMR data is automatically shared with the State Taxation Administration, and the WFOE must complete tax registration within 30 days by accessing the National Electronic Tax Bureau, submitting the business license, legal representative identification, articles of association, registered address proof, bank account information, and the completed tax registration declaration. The tax authority issues the tax registration certificate and the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), which is the 18-digit USCC. For a non-resident enterprise such as the Taiwanese parent company that has no establishment in China, a non-resident TIN is obtained separately by filing the non-resident enterprise tax registration form, either directly with the competent tax authority or through the local withholding agent appointed by the WFOE.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for basic tax registration; every Chinese entity must register within 30 days of business license issuance. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000 for modern services, including SaaS; voluntary registration is permitted below that threshold. Small-scale taxpayers with monthly sales below CNY 100,000 may use the simplified exemption regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border supply of information technology services, including cloud vulnerability scanning and penetration testing SaaS, qualifies for 0% VAT under the State Taxation Administration's Catalogue of Cross-Border Taxable Services Eligible for Zero Rating or Exemption, provided the recipient is an offshore entity or individual, the service is wholly consumed outside China, no Chinese non-resident entity derives a benefit from the service within China, and the taxpayer maintains contractual, invoicing, payment, and delivery evidence proving the offshore place of use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) - applicable to any physical media, appliance, or hardware delivered from China
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill - for physical goods exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) / International Revenue Declaration Form evidencing cross-border receipt
  • Executed service contract with the offshore recipient and a place-of-consumption statement
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice issued through the electronic invoicing platform

China operates the fully digitalized electronic invoice (shu-dian-piao) system through the State Taxation Administration's Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform. VAT invoices must be issued via tax-authority-approved software or the official electronic invoice platform, which performs automatic real-time transmission, taxation control, anti-forgery digital stamping, invoice verification, and risk analysis. There is no public third-party API; certified invoicing systems must integrate with the tax authority's official electronic tax clearance interfaces.

Tax authorities may perform post-export verification and require the full documentation bundle above. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the output VAT becomes payable at the applicable domestic service rate, with late payment surcharge (0.05% per day) and administrative penalties for tax evasion under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, ranging from 50% to 500% of the deficient tax amount. Fraudulent export zero-rating or invoicing carries civil monetary penalties, retrospective clawback of refunded input VAT, and criminal liability under the Criminal Law for export tax fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating RMB exchange-rate regime with current-account convertibility and strict capital-account supervision. A WFOE may legitimately convert RMB for genuine trade, service, and salary-related operational payments, but conversion of foreign equity capital, shareholder loans, divestment proceeds, and repatriated profits remains subject to SAFE scrutiny. Each conversion must be executed by a designated foreign-exchange bank with truthful documentation, including the underlying contract, invoices, tax receipts, and, for capital items, the FDI foreign-exchange registration. Surplus RMB generated from converted capital cannot be freely re-converted into foreign currency; outbound remittances are limited to audited distributable profits, approved reductions, or liquidation proceeds. For a cybersecurity SaaS exporter, current-account receipts must correspond to actual service contracts and cannot be used to justify disguised capital flows. Dividend remittances require annual statutory audit, tax clearance, board resolution, and bank-level verification that the profit has legal retained-earnings basis. Therefore, treasury officers should treat onshore RMB as operationally convertible only under genuine underlying transactions and maintain clear segregation between FDI capital, operational cash, and taxed profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export-proceeds surrender ratio applies in mainland China; the mandatory conversion ratio is zero and the WFOE may retain 100% of export receipts in its foreign-exchange settlement account. Export proceeds must still be repatriated to a mainland bank account, unless SAFE-approved offshore retention is obtained, and goods-export receivables are generally expected within 90 days from the customs export declaration date under the total-amount verification system; longer collection terms require enterprise-level registration with the bank/SAFE. Any conversion from foreign currency to RMB is made at the WFOE's election and must be supported by genuine underlying trade or service documents. For cybersecurity SaaS/service exports, the collection timing follows the contract and invoice maturity, with the bank verifying service delivery before allowing settlement.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, delegated by the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Low. Offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie) are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to open a WFOE's FDI capital account, to receive equity capital contributions, or to complete SAFE foreign-exchange registration and capital verification. They cannot convert FDI capital into RMB or issue the statutory inward post-registration notices. The WFOE must maintain primary clearing with a domestic Tier-1 bank. EMIs may only be used as ancillary technical rails for outbound operating payments if they have an onshore partner-bank settlement route and the underlying service is recorded as a current-account transaction; even then, all funds flow into and out of the domestic bank account, so the EMI does not act as an independent bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Before any remittance, the Taiwan parent must obtain Taiwan-side outbound investment approval, and the WFOE must complete the PRC foreign-invested enterprise registration and FDI foreign-exchange registration with the local SAFE branch through a designated bank. The WFOE then opens an FDI capital settlement account with a Tier-1 domestic bank. The inward wire must arrive via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's own account, with the beneficiary account owned by the WFOE and the remittance purpose clearly stated as FDI equity capital contribution (the SAFE business-code field shown on the FDI registration form). The bank will place the funds in the capital escrow account, perform receipt verification, issue the FDI capital contribution confirmation notice, and, after the supporting documents are validated, permit conversion of the foreign currency into RMB. A licensed CPA then performs statutory capital verification and issues a capital verification report, which is necessary for subsequent changes to registered capital.Outbound treasury payments are split into current-account trade/service settlements and capital-account repatriations. For service or trade payments, use SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers with SAFE purpose codes, commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT799) for documentary import/export, and documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for open-account trade; all require underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration or service-trade certificate, and tax withholding proof where applicable. For RMB-denominated outflows, use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) or cross-border RMB clearing through the settlement bank. Dividend remittances require audited PRC GAAP financial statements, board dividend resolution, tax payment certificate for distributable dividends, and the bank's outbound payment verification; profits cannot exceed audited distributable retained earnings.Onshore deliverable RMB/FX forwards are permissible only where the WFOE has a genuine current-account or approved capital-account exposure and the trade is fully documented; no speculative positioning is allowed.Onshore RMB/FX currency swaps are permissible for hedging the conversion risk of trade settlements, service fees, and short-term working capital flows, subject to SAFE's real-deal-underlying test.Onshore foreign-exchange options are permissible with a real exposure, with maximum tenors and strike ranges set by the bank's derivatives suitability policy; options must not create leveraged speculative loss.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for the onshore WFOE; the Taiwan parent may execute NDFs offshore to hedge consolidated RMB translation risk, but cannot enter into contracts in the name of the WFOE without SAFE approval.

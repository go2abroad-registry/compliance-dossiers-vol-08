Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is impermissible for an ISIC 6619 cross-border payment gateway WFOE. The People's Bank of China payment services licensing framework and SAMR require a fixed physical business premises in China, with a valid lease or ownership certificate, on-site operational management, staffing, servers, and books. Municipal tax authorities reject virtual office arrangements for VAT invoice issuance and tax registration because they cannot substantiate actual business substance. Commercial banks likewise require physical inspection of the registered office before opening settlement accounts and refuse to approve virtual-office-based applicants. The WFOE must therefore maintain a substantive office with operational equipment, corporate signage, staff presence, and original financial records available for inspection.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic tax service at the National E-Tax Bureau portal (https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and central invoice system (国家税务总局电子税务局 / 全面数字化的电子发票平台).

E-government filing portal

On incorporation, the local Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) automatically transmits the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) to the STA, which serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT registration number. The WFOE must, within 30 days of the business license date, complete supplemental tax registration and taxpayer-type identification (税费种认定) through the e-Tax Bureau, designate a tax accountant, open the digital taxpayer account, and apply for electronic invoice quota. No separate physical tax registration certificate is issued; the electronic record in the STA system constitutes full tax registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — On incorporation, the local Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) automatically transmits the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) to the STA, which serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not provide check-the-box or partnership pass-through elections. A WFOE is a resident enterprise subject to Corporate Income Tax (CIT) on worldwide income at the standard 25% rate. Limited elections exist: (1) simplified VAT accounting as a small-scale taxpayer if annual taxable turnover does not exceed RMB 5 million (currently reduced 1% rate available through specified policy windows); (2) deemed-profit CIT assessment may be granted by the tax authority in exceptional cases; (3) no hybrid mismatch or cross-border entity classification elections are recognised, and China applies General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR), controlled foreign corporation rules, and substance requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Tax registration is unconditional for all locally incorporated enterprises, including WFOEs, from the date of SAMR registration. The economic threshold of RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover applies solely to determine mandatory general VAT taxpayer status; a WFOE below the threshold may elect small-scale taxpayer treatment, but once above the threshold it must register as a general VAT taxpayer. No separate turnover threshold exists for the VAT registration itself.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border payment services and financial-intermediation services provided to an overseas recipient qualify for zero-rating under the VAT zero-rating catalogue (Tax Policy for Cross-Border Taxable Services) when the services are completely consumed outside China, the recipient is located outside China, and the payment settlement is made from abroad. The WFOE must hold a written cross-border service contract, issue a 0% VAT invoice, establish that no goods or services are physically used or enjoyed within China by the recipient, and demonstrate that the foreign bank or payment gateway counterparty is a non-resident. The services must not be supplied to a Chinese resident or used to earn taxable income in China.

  • Customs Export Declaration (if goods are bundled with the service)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (for goods exports)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT voucher evidencing receipt of foreign currency
  • Cross-border service contract signed between the WFOE and the overseas recipient
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice issued to the overseas recipient
  • Proof of offshore service consumption (e.g., platform usage logs, confirmation from foreign counterparty, destination-country tax residency certificate)

China mandates the issuance of fully digitalised electronic invoices (全面数字化的电子发票) through the national e-invoice platform under the Golden Tax IV system. Invoices must be generated in real time and uploaded to the STA cloud; each invoice carries a unique digital stamp and dynamic QR code. High-volume service enterprises are required to integrate their billing systems with the STA e-invoice API. There is no physical fiscal stamping device; fiscal clearance is effected by automatic online validation and reporting of each invoice to the tax authority at the moment of issuance.

Export zero-rating and refund claims are subject to intensive STA and SAFE audit review. If the STA determines that the services were not in fact consumed outside China, or that the invoicing and FX repatriation requirements are unmet, it will claw back the VAT refund, impose late payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, and levy a penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law. Fraudulent export refund claims in excess of RMB 5 million trigger criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, with sentences ranging from fixed term to life imprisonment for especially huge amounts, plus confiscation or forfeiture of refunded amounts. The taxpayer may also be suspended from further export refund eligibility for a period not exceeding three years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China applies current-account convertibility and capital-account control. Trade and service payments are freely convertible upon bank verification of genuine underlying transactions. Capital-account items, including WFOE capital injections, loans and domestic reinvestment, require SAFE registration and bank compliance checks. Profit and dividend remittances by a Taiwan shareholder to Taiwan are classified as investment income under the current account; they are remittable through banks after the WFOE has certified audited distributable profits, the board resolution and legal documents have been submitted, and corporate income tax withholding obligations on the non-resident shareholder, if any, have been discharged and the relevant tax certificate or verification for remittance is presented. CNY is not freely convertible offshore; the onshore market is regulated via banks and the China Foreign Exchange Trade System, and cross-border RMB settlement proceeds may be used only in accordance with RMB cross-border rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, exporters must repatriate 100% of export proceeds to a domestic bank account within the statutory period. The default bank-monitored collection period is 90 days from the Customs export declaration; where collection is expected later, a trade-credit extension report must be submitted through the SAFE Goods Trade Monitoring System. There is no mandatory percentage-based conversion into RMB: all export receipts are subject to mandatory repatriation, but they may be retained as foreign exchange in an approved current-account foreign-exchange account or voluntarily settled into RMB. For the WFOE PSP, cross-border service revenue is separately reported in the Service Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System and, where required, a tax certificate for the remittance must be obtained before the bank processes the payment.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) is the central bank; foreign exchange regulation is administered by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations of the People's Republic of China.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for direct use. Offshore electronic-money institutions and digital EMI licences, including Hong Kong, Singapore and EU stored-value facilities or EMI authorisations, are not authorised to open onshore settlement accounts in mainland China or provide CNY clearing through CNAPS or CIPS. A cross-border payment gateway WFOE must use a PBOC-licensed bank or a PBOC-licensed non-bank payment institution for onshore client fund custody and clearing. Offshore EMI platforms may be used only as ancillary tools for the WFOE's offshore treasury sub-ledger, not as the statutory clearing rail or as the account provider for the Chinese PSP licence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan investor must first obtain Taiwan Investment Commission approval for investments in mainland China and file the PRC foreign-investment information report. The WFOE opens an FDI capital foreign exchange account with a designated onshore bank and obtains the SAFE FDI foreign exchange registration code or QR code. Remittances from Taiwan must be sent via SWIFT MT103 directly to the beneficiary WFOE capital account, with the stated purpose as direct investment equity contribution, the SAFE balance-of-payments transaction purpose code applicable to inbound direct investment equity, the SAFE FDI registration number, and the invoiced capital contribution schedule. The receiving bank verifies the incoming capital against the SAFE system, reports the transaction in the BOP and RCPMIS systems and, for foreign-currency remittances, either keeps the funds in the capital foreign exchange account or converts them into RMB under the capital settlement rules. The RMB after settlement, if any, is held in a settlement-pending-payment account and each utilisation is truthfully declared with the applicable payment-use purpose; use for prohibited investments, including securities investments outside the negative list or non-business guarantees, is not permitted.For operational payments, merchant payouts and cross-border service fees, use SWIFT MT103 for international USD, EUR or other currency payments; for interbank positioning use MT202 or MT202COV. For goods-import payments, a commercial letter of credit is issued via SWIFT MT700, amendments are made via MT707, presentation and payment follow UCP600, and the opening bank debits the WFOE's account against conforming documents. For cross-border RMB payments, the bank routes via CIPS or the CNAPS and RCPMIS channel; for USD clearing, the bank uses its correspondent through Fedwire or CHIPS as applicable. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% corporate income tax on taxable profit for a Taiwan profit-seeking enterprise. A 5% tax applies separately to undistributed current-year earnings, subject to statutory exceptions and investment-reinvestment rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a nonresident individual or foreign enterprise, subject to any applicable approved treaty or special cross-border arrangement. Taiwan-resident corporate shareholders may have different treatment.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the annual return is generally due from 1 May through 31 May of the following year, being the fifth month after fiscal year-end. A non-calendar fiscal year generally follows the same fifth-month rule. Provisional tax filing is generally due during the first month of the ninth month of the fiscal year, subject to statutory exceptions and applicable electronic-filing rules.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Withholding tax is generally paid by the 10th day of the month following the month of payment, crediting, or availability of income. Annual withholding statements are generally issued and filed by the statutory January deadline for the preceding calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan generally requires a local file for material related-party transactions, subject to statutory exemptions and simplified documentation. A commonly applied exemption threshold is annual net operating revenue and non-operating income below NT$300 million together with aggregate related-party transaction amounts below NT$200 million; transaction-specific thresholds and filing-period rules must be checked for the relevant year. A master file is generally required where the Taiwan enterprise meets the statutory large-enterprise threshold, commonly including annual revenue of at least NT$3 billion or other prescribed multinational-group conditions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR750 million and Taiwan reporting or constituent-entity conditions.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a single standalone economic-substance regime identical to the United Arab Emirates or Cayman Islands ESR framework. Substance is nevertheless relevant under transfer-pricing rules, permanent-establishment principles, beneficial-ownership analysis, general anti-avoidance provisions, controlled foreign company rules, treaty anti-abuse standards, and regulatory licensing requirements. The Taiwan payment entity should maintain real local decision-making, appropriately qualified personnel, operational systems, risk control, compliance capability, capital, premises, books, customer and merchant oversight, and authority consistent with its claimed functions. A pass-through entity with no meaningful control over payment, credit, fraud, settlement, technology, or compliance risks is vulnerable to profit reallocation, withholding-tax denial, permanent-establishment findings, and license action.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unlicensed payment-service operation

Statutory sanction: Regulatory orders may include cessation, corrective action, license refusal or revocation, administrative fines, disgorgement, and referral for criminal prosecution. The exact sanction depends on whether the activity constitutes electronic payment, electronic payment institution activity, banking, foreign-exchange, remittance, or another regulated service.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face administrative sanctions, criminal prosecution, personal fines, restitution, disqualification, and restrictions on operating regulated businesses. Imprisonment may apply where the unauthorized activity falls within a criminally regulated financial activity.
Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax may be reassessed with interest, administrative penalties, and penalties generally calculated by reference to tax evaded or underreported. Intentional evasion can trigger criminal imprisonment and fines under Taiwan income-tax legislation.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons who participate in, instruct, approve, or knowingly facilitate evasion may face criminal liability, personal fines, civil restitution of tax losses, and asset-preservation measures. Corporate limited liability does not protect intentional individual misconduct.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The Taiwan entity may be required to pay the tax that should have been withheld, together with interest and statutory penalties. Unsupported treaty relief may be denied retroactively.

Director personal liability: The responsible person and withholding officers may face personal administrative or criminal exposure where non-withholding is deliberate, fraudulent, or accompanied by false documents.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: The National Taxation Bureau may adjust taxable income, deny deductions, impose additional tax, interest, penalties, and require local-file or master-file production. Repeated or deliberate non-compliance increases audit and penalty risk.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal exposure for intentional false accounting, fabricated invoices, concealment, or tax evasion, including civil restitution and criminal sanctions.
Shell-company and substance failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may deny deductions, reallocate profits, assert Taiwan-source income or a permanent establishment, deny beneficial-owner or treaty claims, impose withholding tax, and refer the matter for regulatory review.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for tax and regulatory losses, fiduciary-duty claims, personal fines, management disqualification, and criminal liability where the arrangement involves fraud or unlicensed financial activity.
Money laundering and AML/CFT breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, compliance orders, business restrictions, license suspension or revocation, and criminal liability may arise under Taiwan AML/CFT laws. Money laundering offenses may carry imprisonment and substantial fines, with aggravated consequences for organized, repeated, or high-value conduct.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may be prosecuted for knowing participation, assistance, concealment, or negligent governance where statutory duties apply. Consequences may include imprisonment, personal fines, confiscation, restitution, and travel or asset restrictions imposed under applicable proceedings.
Customer-funds commingling and safeguarding failure

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include regulatory remediation, restitution, administrative fines, license suspension or revocation, insolvency-related claims, and criminal prosecution for misappropriation or fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may face fiduciary, insolvency, civil restitution, and criminal liability. Limited liability does not protect misappropriation, knowing diversion, or grossly improper handling of customer money.
Foreign-exchange and cross-border settlement breach

Statutory sanction: Orders to stop the activity, transaction restrictions, administrative fines, confiscation or reversal of unlawful proceeds, and criminal sanctions may apply depending on the regulated activity.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face personal fines, criminal prosecution, restitution, asset seizure, and management or licensing disqualification.
False accounting and record destruction

Statutory sanction: Tax penalties, accounting-law sanctions, regulatory enforcement, criminal fraud or document offenses, and loss of tax deductions may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors, accountants, and officers may face imprisonment, personal fines, civil damages, restitution, professional sanctions, and asset-preservation measures.
Sanctions, export-control, and restricted-party breach

Statutory sanction: Transaction blocking, administrative fines, license action, confiscation, and criminal prosecution may apply under Taiwan sanctions, AML, national-security, foreign-trade, or other applicable laws.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal criminal liability, imprisonment, fines, asset confiscation, travel restrictions, and disqualification where they knowingly approve or ignore prohibited transactions.
Beneficial-owner and treaty-abuse misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Treaty relief may be denied, tax may be collected at the full domestic rate, and penalties and interest may apply. False certificates or documents may trigger criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Individuals signing or authorizing false residency, ownership, or beneficial-owner documents may face civil restitution, personal fines, criminal prosecution, and restrictions on travel or asset disposition where ordered by competent authorities.
Permanent-establishment and source misclassification

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may assess Taiwan-source income, corporate income tax, withholding tax, penalties, interest, and transfer-pricing adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil and criminal exposure for deliberate concealment or false filings, including restitution and personal penalties.

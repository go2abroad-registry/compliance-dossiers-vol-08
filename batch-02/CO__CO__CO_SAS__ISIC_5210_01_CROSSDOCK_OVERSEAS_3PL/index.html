Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN); online portal: https://www.dian.gov.co and transactional platform MUISCA (https://muisca.dian.gov.co)

E-government filing portal

A foreign person or entity must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by completing the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) registration through the MUISCA portal, in person or via a duly appointed attorney-in-fact, and providing apostilled constitutional documents and proof of local representative designation. For a Colombian S.A.S., the NIT is assigned at the time of incorporation in the Cámara de Comercio and must be validated with DIAN prior to commencing operations. A foreign non-resident with no permanent establishment is given a simplified RUT only when Colombian-source VAT or customs formalities require tax identification.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign person or entity must obtain a NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) by completing the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) registration through the MUISCA portal, in person or via a duly appointed attorney-in-fact, and providing apostilled constitutional documents and proof of local representative designation. For a Colombian S.A.S., the NIT is assigned at the time of incorporation in the Cámara de Comercio and must be validated with DIAN prior to commencing operations. A foreign non-resident with no permanent establishment is given a simplified RUT only when Colombian-source VAT or customs formalities require tax identification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No IRS-style check-the-box election exists. The Colombian S.A.S. is per se an income-tax taxpayer and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Certain eligible small taxpayers may elect the 'régimen simple de tributación' (SIMPLE) under Law 2010 of 2019, which consolidates income and VAT advance collections, subject to turnover and shareholder conditions. Hybrid mismatch consequences are addressed through Colombian transfer pricing rules and the anti-abuse framework implementing BEPS Action 2; no elective transparency mechanism is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies to legal entities; every S.A.S. performing an IVA taxable activity must register as an IVA responsable regardless of revenue. Natural persons may fall under the 'régimen simplificado' only if their annual gross income does not exceed 3,500 UVT and the statutory asset test is met (approximately COP 164.7 million for 2025 using UVT = COP 47,065).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Colombia under a definitive customs export declaration are treated as excluded from IVA at the 0% rate, and VAT paid on inputs attributable to such exports creates a 'saldo a favor' refundable by DIAN. Services rendered from Colombian territory to users located abroad, used exclusively abroad, and meeting the 'exportación de servicios' requirements under Article 481 E.T. are also eligible for 0% VAT treatment. The taxpayer must demonstrate ownership and physical transmission, and where cross-dock warehousing is involved, maintain extra-territorial warehouse inventory records proving the goods left Colombia and were delivered to the final overseas buyer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DEX/DUS – Declaración de Exportación)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Colombia
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Reintegro de Divisas)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of foreign buyer purchase order and payment
  • Inventory and delivery records showing cross-dock and overseas warehouse receipt

Colombia mandates electronic invoicing for all taxpayers in respect of sales, export dispatches and operating expenses. The invoice XML must be generated through a DIAN-authorised software or service provider and validated in real time against DIAN web services, obtaining a CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) or CUNE (Código Único de Numeración Electrónica) before delivery. Export invoices require the inclusion of the export declaration code and, for services, the 'declaración de exportación de servicios'. There is no paper stamping, but a QR code and a digital 'CUFE' validation must appear on the printed representation.

False export zero-rating, inflated input VAT recovery, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day term exposes the taxpayer to DIAN retrospective reassessment for the prior 5-year statute of limitation, clawback of all refunded VAT, default interest, and the inexactness penalty of 100% of the underdeclared tax (extending to 200% in case of improper asset-based computations). In fraud cases involving fictitious exports or forged inward remittance certificates, criminal charges for contraband and illegal VAT refund may be prosecuted, with monetary fines and custodial penalties of up to the value of the refunded sums.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a restricted but partly liberalized exchange control regime under Law 9 of 1991 and the External Exchange Regulations of Banco de la República. Current-account transactions are generally freely convertible through authorized foreign-exchange intermediaries, while capital-account operations require official registration and channeling through the Mercado Cambiario. Foreign direct investment must be registered with Banco de la República to secure dividend remittance rights. Profit remittance is permitted after corporate tax compliance and dividend declaration, subject to the foreign investor's registration being active and the remittance being executed through an authorized local bank using the appropriate foreign-exchange causal. Practical hurdles include FDI registration lead time, AML/KYC bank checks, documentary verification of distributable reserves, and periodic reporting to Banco de la República.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All proceeds from exports of goods must be channeled into the foreign-exchange market through an authorized intermediary and repatriated to Colombia within the timelines established by Banco de la República: generally four (4) months from the date of customs clearance for goods, and up to one hundred eighty (180) days for services and certain cross-border e-commerce transactions, unless a valid extension is granted. The Colombian foreign-exchange regime no longer imposes a mandatory percentage conversion into COP for all proceeds; exporters may maintain eligible foreign currency in foreign-currency deposit accounts opened in authorized local banks. However, the proceeds must be fully declared and channeled through the Mercado Cambiario before the applicable deadline; failure to surrender constitutes an exchange infraction punishable by fines and customs penalties.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) jointly with the Subgerencia de Cambios Internacionales; DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) exercises customs and foreign-exchange enforcement authority for export declaration and surrender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility for auxiliary clearing purposes only. Airwallex or comparable offshore digital EMI wallets may be used as non-regulated payment aggregators for store proceeds and cross-border vendor disbursements, subject to contractual terms and sanctions compliance. They cannot legally replace an authorized local Colombian bank because foreign exchange surrender, FDI registration, and profit remittance must execute through a local authorized intermediary operating in the Mercado Cambiario. Colombian clients' Peso-denominated collections may not be lodged in an offshore EMI without triggering unauthorized foreign-exchange intermediation risk. Any EMI relationship must be downstream of the local operational bank, never the primary treasury account holder, and should be supported by a written treasury policy segregating inflow, FX surrender, and payout flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound shareholder equity or subordinated loan capital from the non-resident parent to CO_SAS must be received through an authorized Colombian foreign-exchange intermediary, using the SWIFT network and the local beneficiary account in the name of the Colombian entity. The funds transfer must carry the appropriate foreign-exchange causal (e.g., direct foreign investment or external credit); the bank will issue a 'Comprobante de Liquidación de Operación de Cambio' evidencing the transaction. The foreign investment must be registered with Banco de la República on the electronic FDI registration form within ninety (90) days of receipt to maintain dividend remittance rights and repatriation protection. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding on dividends or participations paid, credited, or put at the disposal of a non-resident beneficial owner out of Colombian taxed profits; if profits were not taxed at the entity level, additional characterization as taxable dividends plus the 35% residual tax may apply to the non-resident.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar year ending 31 December, DIAN issues an annual tax calendar with filing windows based on the last two digits of the taxpayer's NIT; generally, corporate returns are due in the months after the tax year-end, including the last business day of the batch applicable to the NIT. Extraordinary extensions are not granted for a foreign-owned S.A.S. except on verified force majeure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and the transfer pricing annex to the CIT return are mandatory when gross income or gross assets exceed 135,000 UVT, or when cross-border related-party transactions exceed 40,000 UVT; for 2025 UVT = COP 47,065 the main threshold is COP 6,353,775,000. Master File is required for multinational groups whose consolidated operating revenue exceeds COP 100 billion.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not operate an offshore Economic Substance Regulations filing regime; instead, substance is required through the substance-over-form principle, PE rules, CFC rules, and the anti-abusive GAAR in Article 869 E.T. A Colombian cross-dock S.A.S. must show actual employees, leased or owned warehouse premises, inventory control, local logistics decisions, and contracts with arm's-length customers to avoid recharacterization as a paper conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent understatement

Statutory sanction: DIAN inaccuracy penalty of 160% of the understated tax, late-filing surcharge of 5% per month up to 100%, monthly default interest, and criminal tax fraud under Código Penal Article 402 with imprisonment of 48–108 months and an economic fine.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or authorize fictitious intercompany invoices become jointly and severally liable for the tax, interest, and penalties; criminal proceedings may impose asset embargoes, travel bans, and disqualification from managing Colombian companies.
Shell company / abusive S.A.S. structure without substance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the corporate entity under GAAR, recharacterize income at the true beneficial owner, disallow all logistics deductions, assess the 160% inaccuracy sanction, and recapture dividend withholding on constructive distributions.

Director personal liability: Colombian courts can pierce the veil under Law 1258 Article 42 when the S.A.S. is used as an instrument of fraud or abuse; the sole director and controlling shareholder may be ordered to restore misappropriated assets personally and face insolvency-related trading restrictions.
Transfer pricing manipulation and missing documentation

Statutory sanction: Mandatory upward adjustment of Colombian taxable income, disallowance of deducted cross-border fees, 160% inaccuracy penalty, fixed transfer pricing sanctions, and default interest from the original due date of the return.

Director personal liability: The legal representative is personally liable for failing to present required transfer pricing information; directors who approve non-arm's-length contracts with actual knowledge of the distortion face personal assessment and can be named in DIAN fraud complaints.
Unreported profit repatriation and withholding failures

Statutory sanction: Withholding penalty equal to 100% of the tax not withheld, recapture of the 10% dividend tax, 35% tax on disguised profits, 160% inaccuracy sanction if return positions are false, and monthly interest for each unpaid remittance.

Director personal liability: Administrators are jointly liable for unreported withholding taxes and interest; directing funds abroad after overlooking withholding obligations is treated as fraudulent misappropriation and may trigger personal criminal liability and travel restrictions.
AML, customs, and illicit logistics facilitation

Statutory sanction: Customs seizure and fines under the Colombian customs and anti-smuggling legislation, suspension of the authorized economic operator status, AML penalties under Law 1765, and criminal money-laundering prosecution for rates in aggravated circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge or willful blindness to suspicious shipments are personally indictable as money-laundering participants; asset freezes and international travel bans apply from the investigation stage, and corporate limited liability does not shield intentional misconduct.

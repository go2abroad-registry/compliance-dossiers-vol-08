Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - National Directorate of Taxes and Customs; official portal: www.dian.gov.co (DIAN Virtual Office / MUISCA transactional platform for tax registration, filing, electronic invoicing, and customs declarations).

E-government filing portal

A Colombian S.A.S. must enroll with DIAN through the MUISCA Virtual Office by submitting the RUT (Registro Único Tributario) application, which automatically assigns the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) as the statutory tax identifier. The legal representative must hold a Colombian tax ID (NIT or citizenship card) and digitally sign the enrollment. The RUT must record the principal and secondary CIIU economic activity codes (with code 3250 for surgical instrument and suture manufacture), IVA responsible-entity status (Responsable de IVA - Régimen Común), exporter status (Exportador) for zero-rated operations, and the authorized electronic invoicing resolution. Foreign shareholders may incorporate remotely; if the S.A.S. has no local physical establishment, a local agent or domicile address is required for DIAN notifications. The NIT must appear on all income tax returns, VAT returns, withholding certificates, electronic invoices, and export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the Colombian S.A.S. is a distinct resident corporate taxpayer subject to entity-level corporate income tax under a worldwide income principle (renta mundial), with foreign tax credits for eligible foreign-source taxes; no pass-through or partnership look-through applies to an S.A.S. for income tax purposes. Territorial_Scope: resident entities are taxed on worldwide income, while non-resident recipients are taxed only on Colombian-source income. Tax_Exempt_Zone is not the default classification but is contractually available under Zona Franca legislation subject to DIAN approval and export/employment commitments.

Available election forms

Colombia has no US-style check-the-box election and no elective hybrid-mismatch regimes; entity-level taxation is mandatory for an S.A.S. Available statutory elections relevant to an S.A.S. medical-device manufacturer: (i) registration as a Free Trade Zone user (Usuario Industrial de Bienes) under Law 1780/2016 and Decreto 2147/2016, contractually electing a 20% reduced CIT rate in exchange for export and employment commitments; (ii) optional consolidated tax group (consolidación fiscal) under E.T. Art. 19-1 for commonly controlled companies, elected before the fiscal year-end for a renewing three-year term; (iii) the simplified SIMPLE regime under Law 2010/2019 for small businesses, generally unavailable to exporters with CIIU 3250 above the SIMPLE size thresholds or activities subject to special regimes; and (iv) unilateral or bilateral Advance Pricing Agreements with DIAN for intercompany transactions. Anti-hybrid rules aligned with BEPS Action 2 disallow hybrid-mismatch deductions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with a regulated foreign-exchange market (Mercado Cambiario). The Colombian peso is freely convertible through licensed Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario (IMCs), principally commercial banks. Current-account transactions are fully convertible for trade in goods, services, royalties, and profit remittances, provided the underlying transaction is supported by the appropriate Declaracion de Cambio. Capital-account inflows, including foreign direct investment (FDI) and external credit, are lawful without prior authorization but must be registered with Banco de la Republica through an IMC within the applicable statutory declaration windows. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted only where the originating foreign investment has been duly registered; unregistered capital cannot be repatriated. Capital outflows such as capital reductions, share disposals, and repayment of external debt are likewise conditioned on registration and on the satisfaction of Colombian withholding taxes. No dual exchange rate, no surrender requirement, and no pre-approval for trade-related FX operations apply; the practical impediments are documentary, comprising registration deadlines, exchange-declaration concept codes, and bank-level source-of-funds verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods exporters must repatriate (reintegrar) export proceeds through an IMC within six (6) months from the date of shipment, with Banco de la Republica empowered to grant extensions up to a maximum of fourteen (14) months on justified grounds. Service exporters must collect proceeds within four (4) months from the date of service provision, extendable up to twelve (12) months. Statutory mandatory conversion ratio into COP: 0%. Colombian law imposes no obligation to convert export receipts into pesos; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a Banco de la Republica-registered foreign-currency compensation account (cuenta de compensacion) held with an IMC or elect to sell the currency at the market indicative rate (TRM). Notwithstanding the absence of conversion requirements, the entirety of each export receipt must be channeled through the foreign-exchange market and evidenced to the IMC by the Export Proceeds Exchange Declaration (Declaracion de Cambio - Form 1) supported by the Dian export registration. Expiry of the statutory collection period without reintegration constitutes an exchange-regime infraction subject to administrative penalties imposed under the Colombian foreign-exchange penalty regime.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la Republica de Colombia (Central Bank of Colombia), acting through its Board of Directors (Junta Directiva) as the foreign-exchange regulatory authority, together with the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia for banking and AML/CFT supervision, and the Direccion de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales for trade and foreign-investment documentation controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW as the principal treasury rail; MEDIUM for ancillary small-ticket receivables collection. Colombian SEDPEs (Sociedades Especializadas en Depositos y Pagos Electronicos) established under Law 1735 of 2014 are confined to domestic electronic deposits and payment wallets; they are not licenced IMCs and cannot open USD compensation accounts, execute foreign-investment registration, or issue the statutory exchange declarations required for capital injections and trade settlements. Unlicensed offshore EMIs and digital banks (for example Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, and Payoneer) are not authorized IMCs in Colombia; channelling Colombian-resident foreign-exchange transactions through them would breach the mandatory reintegro regime, compromise the SAS's ability to obtain timely Bank of Colombia registrations, and materially impair the domestic bank's KYC and source-of-funds attestation. Such EMIs may be used only as an auxiliary collection vehicle for low-value digital-service or R&D receivables, with subsequent onward transfer to the Colombian IMC compensation account, and must be disclosed to the primary relationship bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor remits USD via SWIFT MT103 (with MT202 cover where applicable) from its own account at a non-sanctioned, reputable bank to the beneficiary SAS account held with the Colombian IMC, earmarked as the foreign-investment sub-account. The MT103 field 72 (or designated purpose field) must state the payment concept: 'Foreign Direct Investment - capital contribution in foreign currency', and include the beneficiary's full legal name, NIT, address, and the underlying share-subscription reference. The IMC verifies the remitter against sanctions and PEP lists and issue the exchange-declaration receipt; the legal representative then submits the Foreign Investment Exchange Declaration (Form 4 of the Declaracion de Cambio) to Banco de la Republica through the IMC within the statutory filing window from the effective date of the investment, thereby obtaining the Registro de Inversion Internacional (RII). The proceeds may be monetized into COP at the TRM or credited to the company's USD compensation account. The SAS may not use the funds for operational outlays before the registration is perfected; any later dividend or capital repatriation is conditioned on a completed RII. If the injection is structured as debt rather than equity, the parent loan must be registered with Banco de la Republica as external credit under the external-debt regime, and any encaje (reserve requirement) then prevailing must be complied with by the borrowing SAS.For open-account export receivables, the buyer's bank executes a SWIFT MT103 to the SAS USD compensation account at the Colombian IMC, quoting the commercial invoice number and the HS/INVIMA product reference, followed by the SAS submission of the Export Proceeds Exchange Declaration (Form 1). For documentary-credit sales, the Colombian IMC advises and settles the irrevocable letter of credit (SWIFT MT700 issuance by the buyer's bank, advice in MT710/MT720, amendment MT707); payment against compliant presentation of the commercial invoice, packing list, inspection certificate, INVIMA export certificate, insurance certificate, and transport document is effected via MT754/MT756 and covered by MT202, after which the export proceeds are declared on Form 1. For import disbursements of raw materials and components, the SAS instructs its IMC to execute outbound SWIFT MT103 (telegraphic-transfer payment) or, for recurring supply relationships, opens a commercial documentary credit locally through the IMC (MT700 issued by the Colombian bank), accompanied by the Dian import declaration and the corresponding Import Exchange Declaration (Form 1 for goods, Form 2 for services) within the stipulated declaration period. Every outbound wire must quote the underlying invoice or purchase-order number so the IMC can reconcile the transaction against the exchange-declaration record; repayment of intra-group foreign-currency loans must reference the external-debt registration number issued by Banco de la Republica.Deliverable USD/COP forward (forward de tasa de cambio) executed with the relationship IMC bank, limited to hedging the SAS's real commercial exposure arising from USD export receivables and USD-denominated imported-input payables, with tenors customarily up to 12 months.USD/COP currency swap (swap de tasa de cambio) used for tenor-matched conversion and recharacterization of the SAS's USD operating cash flow into COP funding requirements, permissible where both legs settle through the IMC.USD/COP option-based structures, principally zero-cost collars and capped forwards, permitted for non-speculative hedging of net open FX position, subject to the IMC's credit approval and supporting underlying trade documentation.Non-deliverable USD/COP forward (NDF) allowed where settlement cannot involve physical delivery of pesos to a non-resident counterparty; the NDF's taxable gain or loss must be recognized in the SAS's income tax computation and reported for balance-of-payments statistics through the IMC.Cross-currency interest rate swap (CCIRS) between USD floating and COP fixed rates used to hedge the interest and principal exposure of a registered external-credit facility, conditional upon full Banco de la Republica external-debt registration and hedged-effect documentation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: LOW as the principal treasury rail; MEDIUM for ancillary small-ticket receivables collection. Colombian SEDPEs (Sociedades Especializadas en Depositos y Pagos Electronicos) established under Law 1735 of 2014 are confined to domestic electronic deposits and payment wallets; they are not licenced IMCs and cannot open USD compensation accounts, execute foreign-investment registration, or issue the statutory exchange declarations required for capital injections and trade settlements. Unlicensed offshore EMIs and digital banks (for example Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, and Payoneer) are not authorized IMCs in Colombia; channelling Colombian-resident foreign-exchange transactions through them would breach the mandatory reintegro regime, compromise the SAS's ability to obtain timely Bank of Colombia registrations, and materially impair the domestic bank's KYC and source-of-funds attestation. Such EMIs may be used only as an auxiliary collection vehicle for low-value digital-service or R&D receivables, with subsequent onward transfer to the Colombian IMC compensation account, and must be disclosed to the primary relationship bank.

35% (general corporate income tax rate under Colombian Tax Code as amended by Law 2277 of 2022)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For FY2024, mandatory Local File and Master File arise when gross income exceeds 135,000 UVT (approximately COP 6,353,775,000); mandatory Transfer Pricing Informative Return (Form 120) is required when gross income exceeds 45,000 UVT (approximately COP 2,117,925,000). Thresholds are updated annually by DIAN resolution based on the UVT.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not maintain a UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations filing, but the DIAN applies the Tax Code anti-abuse rule and substance-over-form doctrine under Article 869 of the Colombian Tax Code. A S.A.S. engaged in surgical consumables manufacturing must maintain real operational substance in Colombia - physical factory, personnel, inventory, board decisions, and bank accounts - to defend its taxable presence, deduct costs, and avoid recharacterization of transactions as sham or lacking business purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanction of 100% of the omitted tax or false credit; default interest; exclusion from large-taxpayer privileges; criminal tax evasion carries imprisonment of 48 to 108 months and fines equal to the value of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil applies: directors and legal representatives who sign fraudulent returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax debt and sanctions; civil restitution of assets and embargoes may be ordered; travel bans and preventive detention may be imposed in criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may adjust taxable income to the arm's length result and charge the resulting tax plus default interest; failure to file transfer pricing documentation carries significant monetary penalties based on the value of the undocumented transactions; inaccurate filings attract an additional penalty of 5% of the transaction gross amount or the disallowed adjustment.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil applies: executive officers who decide on the controlled transaction are jointly liable for the assessment and penalties if they knowingly signed incorrect documentation; deliberate profit-shifting may trigger money-laundering exposure and personal criminal liability.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the legal structure, recharacterize the operations as Colombian-source generation, deny all associated deductions, impose the 100% evasion penalty, and apply the integrity sanctions of Article 869; if the manufacturing license from INVIMA is not properly held or the facility does not exist, regulatory cancellation follows.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil applies: shareholders and directors who benefit from the sham structure are personally liable for the recomputed tax, interest, and penalties; courts may seize personal assets and issue domestic/international travel prohibitions pending tax-fraud proceedings.
Money Laundering / AML Breaches through Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Article 323 of the Colombian Criminal Code is punishable by 10 to 30 years of imprisonment and a fine of up to 50,000 current legal monthly minimum wages; proceeds are subject to forfeiture; banking relationships are terminated and the company is reported to the financial intelligence unit (UIAF).

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil applies: directors who direct, approve, or benefit from illicit payment routing face individual criminal liability, travel bans, asset freezes, and possible extradition under mutual legal assistance agreements; compliance officers may be sanctioned independently for failing to implement AML controls.

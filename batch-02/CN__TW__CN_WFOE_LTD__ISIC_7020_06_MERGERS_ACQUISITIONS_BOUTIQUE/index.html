Competent corporate registry

State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) - Enterprise Registration Bureau; the local municipal or district Market Regulation Administration with territorial jurisdiction over the registered office in mainland China; foreign investment information is filed to the Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) through the integrated Foreign Investment Information Reporting system.

Governing statute

Company Law of the People's Republic of China (2023 Revision, effective 1 July 2024), Articles 23, 24, 27, 29, 32, 33, 38, 47 and 53; Foreign Investment Law of the People's Republic of China (2019), Articles 2, 4, 5, 28, 29 and 31-33; Regulations on the Administration of Market Entity Registration (State Council Decree No. 746), Articles 2 and 8-22; and the negative-list provisions of the Special Administrative Measures for Foreign Investment Access (2021), read together with the Regulations on the Administration of Investment by Taiwan Compatriots because the sole investor is domiciled in Taiwan.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a wholly foreign-owned limited liability company under PRC law. The subscribed capital must be fully paid in by the shareholder within five years of registration under Company Law Article 47, unless a shorter period is required by a specific industry regulator. There is no blocked escrow account rule; instead, foreign capital from Taiwan must be remitted through an FDI capital account at a PBOC-designated bank, and the company must maintain the inward capital verification record. The Taiwan investor may contribute in USD or CNY-equivalent, and the contributed capital cannot be used for unauthorized equity investments or restricted activities outside the approved business scope.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the provincial Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal; for any Taiwan-side taxable nexus, the Ministry of Finance, R.O.C. and the National Taxation Bureau are the competent counterpart authorities.

E-government filing portal

For the CN WFOE: (1) incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); (2) complete tax registration through the STA's one-stop data sharing process within 30 days after issuance of the business license, with the USCC serving as the de facto tax identification number; (3) file the foreign investment information report through SAMR and complete SAFE-designated bank foreign exchange registration for capital and dividend remittances; (4) apply for VAT general taxpayer status when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, while a below-threshold WFOE may be treated as a small-scale taxpayer with a simplified collection rate; (5) for a non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China, a withholding registration is still required for China-source income; for a Taiwan-side branch or permanent establishment, registration with the Ministry of Finance, R.O.C. is required to obtain a Taiwan Uniform Invoice Number and a tax file reference.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None; China does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A WFOE is per se a resident limited-liability enterprise taxed at entity level under Corporate Income Tax, and hybrid mismatch or treaty-based classification choices are not available as elective domestic classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for mandatory tax registration; every legal entity must obtain VAT registration within 30 days of business registration. Mandatory general taxpayer registration threshold is annual VAT taxable sales above RMB 5,000,000; below this threshold the WFOE is a small-scale taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported physical goods qualify for 0% VAT when they are actually exported through customs with an export customs declaration and the seller is a VAT general taxpayer holding the full statutory export document set. Cross-border services qualify for zero-rating only if listed in the PRC cross-border taxable services catalogue (e.g., R&D, design, software, data processing, offshore outsourcing, international transportation, and similar specified services); M&A and management consulting services wholly consumed offshore are generally VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated, meaning no output VAT is charged but input VAT may not be refunded unless the service specifically falls into a zero-rated catalogue item. The recipient must be outside mainland China and the service must be wholly consumed outside China, with contemporaneous foreign currency receipts substantiating the offshore character.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry / China export customs declaration) with customs release notation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher from a SAFE-licensed bank
  • Signed export contract, commercial invoice, packing list, and any restricted-goods export licenses
  • VAT export tax refund declaration and electronic document matching with the STA tax and customs systems

Golden Tax IV mandate: all Special VAT Invoices and fully digitalized electronic invoices must be issued through the STA electronic invoice platform, reported in real time to the national VAT invoice database, and contain a cryptographically verifiable unique invoice number and QR-based digital stamping. ERP-to-platform API integration is required for all automated billing environments; paper-only issuance is reserved for narrow statutory transition cases.

False export declarations, inflated export values, fabricated offshore service contracts, or missing FX repatriation within the 180-day window trigger retrospective clawback of zero-rated VAT treatment, repayment of output VAT, and late-payment interest. Under the PRC Tax Administration and Collection Law, fraudulent export tax recovery is punishable by recovery of the full amount and a fine of up to three times the tax evaded; severe cases are prosecuted as criminal export tax rebate fraud under PRC Criminal Law, with imprisonment, fines, and asset confiscation. Regulatory non-compliance with SAFE reporting may independently subject the entity to foreign-exchange penalties and disqualify the export zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates an essentially convertible current account and a heavily managed capital account. Current account FX receipts and payments for trade in goods and services may be transacted freely through designated banks if evidenced by contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and tax vouchers, under the real intention and real transaction background principles. Export proceeds are not subject to a mandatory surrender ratio and may be retained in foreign currency accounts or converted at the market rate through a designated bank. Capital account flows, including FDI equity injection, shareholder loans, establishment of escrow capital, and cross-border M&A funding, are subject to pre-approval, filing, and post-monitoring by SAFE. For the WFOE, the registered foreign exchange capital can be converted and used only within the approved business scope and the negative list framework. M&A success fee inflows are classified as service trade current account receipts and are remittable after contract verification, tax payment, and bank-level SAFE reporting. Dividend remittances to the Taiwan investor are allowed after the annual statutory audit, tax settlement, and SAFE reporting, but require evidence of bookkeeping accuracy, tax clearance, and absence of historical false declarations. The New Taiwan Dollar is not an onshore deliverable currency in mainland China, so all funding from Taiwan should be wired in USD or RMB via SWIFT or CIPS.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE monitoring rules, an exporter must repatriate export proceeds to a domestic foreign exchange account within 180 days from the date of customs declaration unless an extension is approved by SAFE. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may either retain the foreign exchange in a current account or sell it to a designated bank at the prevailing market rate. Any conversion must be supported by the export cargo declaration, logistics documents, invoice, and the SAFE trade-inspection code. For cross-border service exports such as M&A advisory fees, the applicable 180-day collection requirement is enforced through the service trade item, with bank-level declaration submitted via the SAFE system and retained with the underlying advisory contract.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, the foreign exchange administration authority operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with local SAFE branches administering cross-border capital registration, FX market supervision, and balance of payments reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary or sole treasury rail. Under the Administrative Measures for Non-bank Payment Institutions and SAFE foreign exchange regulations, a foreign fintech EMI cannot open onshore deposit-taking accounts, accept FDI capital injections, or execute SAFE-registered FX settlement without a PBOC payment license and cross-border payment institution designation. Offshore EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Wise may only serve as auxiliary collection tools in partnership with licensed Chinese banks and cannot replace the bank for capital account transactions. For a WFOE engaged in M&A advisory, all core treasury clearing should be executed through licensed banks; fintech EMIs are viable only for small-volume, low-value service fee collections and only if the bank has previously cleared the counterparty and the underlying transaction documentation is retained.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan investor converts New Taiwan Dollars to USD or RMB and executes a cross-border wire via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's FDI capital escrow account at a designated licensed bank. Before the transfer, the WFOE must complete SAFE foreign exchange registration for the foreign-invested enterprise and obtain the FDI compliance registration certificate. The remittance instruction must identify the SAFE registration code and use the SAFE purpose code category for FDI paid-in capital injection. Upon receipt, the receiving bank verifies the SAFE registration, credits the capital account, and settles the foreign exchange into RMB under the real need principle. The WFOE then engages a Chinese certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report, and all downstream disbursements must be matched against business contracts, vouchers, and the approved business scope. Any transfer of escrowed capital must not be commingled with operational income without proper account separation and bank approval.For cross-border service trade expenditures related to M&A advisory work, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for USD or RMB wire transfers, accompanied by the appropriate service trade declaration code, tax withholding certificate, and underlying contract. For large or milestone-based payments, a Commercial Letter of Credit may be opened via SWIFT MT700 when the counterparty requires trade finance protection, although this is uncommon for advisory fees. Documentary Collections via SWIFT MT400 or MT422 can be used for time-gated deliverables linked to supporting documents. For RMB-denominated payments to Taiwan or other counterparties, the Cross-border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) provides an efficient alternative to SWIFT and reduces correspondent bank delays. Every outbound payment must be supported by a board resolution, invoice, contract, and a negative-list compliance check confirming the transaction is not a prohibited capital flight or money-laundering scheme.Onshore FX forward contracts (remote and regular forwards) with licensed Chinese banks, executed only when the WFOE demonstrates genuine current or capital account exposure under SAFE real transaction background rules.Onshore FX options and option combination structures such as risk reversals, collars, and participating forwards, limited to hedging existing or highly expected trade settlement flows and requiring bank credit approval.Cross-currency swaps between RMB and USD for term-based corporate financing or M&A funding, subject to SAFE risk management accreditation and strict prohibition on speculative net positions.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are prohibited for the onshore WFOE as a hedging instrument because SAFE requires physical RMB delivery and a genuine underlying transaction; NDFs are only permissible for offshore non-resident counterparties transacting outside mainland China.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation under STA Bulletin [2016] No. 42 is triggered when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 100 million for a single type or RMB 40 million for multiple transaction types; a contemporaneous transfer pricing report must be prepared above thresholds; lower threshold applies for related-party intangible property or financial transactions.

Economic substance scope: China applies the substance-over-form doctrine and General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. A WFOE M&A advisory boutique must maintain real economic substance in China—employees, office premises, decision-making, execution of core advisory functions, and risk management. The Cross-Strait Tax Agreement benefits are denied if the Taiwan entity is deemed a conduit without substantive business activity. Any arrangement solely to obtain treaty benefits may be recharacterized, and the Chinese entity could be subject to deemed distributions, additional CIT, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (逃税)

Statutory sanction: Revocation of tax exemptions, additional tax assessment, daily interest of 0.05%, administrative fine of 50% up to 500% of the underpaid tax; criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 for evading tax exceeding RMB 50,000 with imprisonment up to 7 years and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the unpaid tax if the corporate veil is pierced due to fraud or abuse of corporate form; travel bans and exit-control orders can be imposed; personal assets may be frozen for restitution; imprisonment for the legal representative and directly responsible supervisors is possible.
Shell Company and Benefit-Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of the transaction, full statutory withholding of 10% (or 25% CIT on service income deemed received by a permanent establishment), plus late payment interest and 50%–500% penalties; GAAR reassessment with unlimited look-back period for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors of the WFOE and the offshore intermediary can be held jointly and severally liable for tax, interest, and penalties; travel restrictions and freezing of bank accounts are commonly enforced during cross-border tax investigations; criminal liability for organizing a tax-evasion scheme may lead to imprisonment.
AML and Financial Compliance Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines on the company up to RMB 5 million; suspension or revocation of licenses; asset forfeiture; criminal liability under China's Anti-Money Laundering Law for money laundering with imprisonment up to 10 years; inclusion on the financial blacklist.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers must personally ensure AML controls; penalties include fines, travel bans, custodial detention, and criminal imprisonment for willful blindness; personal assets can be traced and confiscated if complicity is established.
Unlicensed Financial / M&A Advisory Activity

Statutory sanction: Cease-and-desist order, confiscation of illegal gains, administrative fine of RMB 100,000–1,000,000 (or 1–5 times the illegal income); criminal prosecution for illegal business operations (非法经营罪) with imprisonment up to 5–10 years and property confiscation; invalidation of advisory contracts and no right to collect success fees.

Director personal liability: Directors and the actual controller are subject to personal fines and imprisonment; regulatory authorities can impose industry bans and travel restrictions; clients may sue for restitution, and courts may pierce the corporate veil to satisfy judgments from personal assets.

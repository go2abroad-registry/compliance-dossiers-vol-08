Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN). Official portal: www.dian.gov.co; taxpayer electronic services platform: muisca.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian S.A.S., the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) is automatically generated upon notarized incorporation and Chamber of Commerce registration. The company must complete the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) with DIAN, declaring the ISIC 4721 economic activity and VAT obligations. A foreign entity without local incorporation but engaging in taxable activities in Colombia must appoint a fiscal representative, submit apostilled powers of attorney, and apply to DIAN for a NIT via the RUT foreign-investor protocol. All procedures require a DIAN-authorized digital signature certificate (Firma Digital).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a Colombian S.A.S., the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) is automatically generated upon notarized incorporation and Chamber of Commerce registration. The company must complete the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) with DIAN, declaring the ISIC 4721 economic activity and VAT obligations. A foreign entity without local incorporation but engaging in taxable activities in Colombia must appoint a fiscal representative, submit apostilled powers of attorney, and apply to DIAN for a NIT via the RUT foreign-investor protocol. All procedures require a DIAN-authorized digital signature certificate (Firma Digital).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An S.A.S. is irrevocably classified as a national corporation taxable at entity level; no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch pass-through election is permitted under Colombian law. Eligible small entities may elect the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) if annual gross income does not exceed 80,000 UVT, resulting in a unified contribution covering income tax, VAT, and municipal taxes. Otherwise, the entity remains in the standard corporate income tax and common IVA regime. No further US-style classification elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Legal entities have no economic turnover threshold; every S.A.S. must register as a common-regime IVA taxpayer upon NIT assignment. For natural persons, the simplified-regime threshold is 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 148,442,000 for tax year 2023); above this threshold registration in the common regime is mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Colombian customs territory, as well as services supplied to foreign clients, may be zero-rated for IVA if the seller is an exporter registered in the RUT, the export is covered by a valid customs export declaration, and the foreign-currency proceeds are channeled through the Colombian foreign-exchange market and remitted to Colombia within statutory deadlines. For an Asian specialty grocery supermarket, zero-rating applies only to bona fide sales that physically leave Colombia and are documented as exports; local domestic sales of imported condiments remain subject to 19% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación – DEX) authorized by DIAN Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch from Colombia
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Consignación de Divisas) issued by a Colombian foreign-exchange intermediary
  • Commercial Invoice with unit prices and commodity classification
  • Packing List with weights and quantities
  • ICA or INVIMA sanitary permits where foods or condiments are exported

DIAN mandates real-time electronic invoicing ('Facturación Electrónica') for all VAT taxpayers. Invoices must be transmitted through DIAN-authorized software, direct web-service API, or cloud POS with X.509 digital certificate; each invoice receives a CUFE/CUDE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica) for legal clearance. Supermarket retail sales must issue an electronic sales invoice at the point of sale, validated immediately by DIAN, with QR code and CUFE printed. No post-emission digital stamping certificate is used; clearance is performed online through DIAN's computer service.

DIAN audits export VAT refunds for up to five years and may retroactively claw back the zero-rate benefit where the foreign exchange is not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, the customs declaration is incomplete, or the goods are not actually dispatched. The taxpayer is exposed to administrative penalties of up to 200% of the improperly refunded amount, daily default interest, disqualification from refund procedures, and criminal sanctions under Law 1762 of 2015 for customs fraud and false declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

COP is currently-account convertible for retail grocery imports and operational trade; capital-account convertibility for FDI is conditional on registration with Banco de la República through an authorized intermediary in Form No. 3 within the statutory post-receipt window (generally four months) and on the investor retaining a valid NIT and foreign-investment registration. Profit remittance is lawful when the investment registration remains valid, the S.A.S. files annual tax returns, and the remittance uses a registered profit-distribution concept; there is no blanket surrender of proceeds, but all payments and collections must pass through the formal FX channel and be documented on BDC declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For any exported goods, the S.A.S. must channel 100% of invoiced and collected foreign currency through an authorized intermediary within six months from the date of shipment; a single six-month extension may be requested from Banco de la República. The proceeds must be reported in the applicable Declaración de Cambio (Form No. 5) and either sold to the market or held in an authorized foreign-currency account. No fixed percentage peso conversion is currently operative, but the requirement is to demonstrate effective repatriation and channeling for every export operation.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República de Colombia (foreign exchange administration), acting jointly with DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) for customs, validation and capital-movement control; legal framework: Régimen Cambiario (Decree 1194 of 2020, Resolution 14 of 2021 and Banco de la República regulatory circulars).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

FEASIBILITY_RATING: LOW — Colombia does not recognize European ‘EMI’ or offshore electronic-money institutions as regulated corporate banks; offshore EMIs cannot issue Colombian BDC forms, cannot maintain centralized capital accounts, and cannot serve as statutory intermediaries for the Régimen Cambiario. An authorized local fintech or payment entity may provide ancillary payment initiation but cannot replace a licensed bank for international capital injection, import settlement or FX hedging.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

7.5% (for dividends paid out of profits already taxed at 35% CIT to resident shareholders). For non-resident shareholders, the standard statutory dividend withholding tax rate is 10% on the gross dividend where the distributing company is not listed as a 'treaty-protected' entity; where the underlying profits were subject to the full 35% CIT, the additional 10% withholding applies, resulting in a combined statutory rate of 7.5% for resident individuals and 10% for non-residents. In specific cases, an extra 3% or 7.5% surtax may apply for excessive profits, but standard retail dividends attract the base rates.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within the calendar year following the taxable year, generally between March and April depending on the last two digits of the NIT. The exact deadline is set by DIAN resolution each year. For a December 31 fiscal year-end, the filing deadline typically falls in mid-April. Quarterly prepayments are not applicable to ordinary S.A.S. entities unless income exceeds thresholds.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Renglón 1 Total gross income from grocery sales, including debit and credit card receipts and cash sales. Report all Colombian-source income; non-resident owners must ensure that only the local S.A.S. reports sales income, not a foreign related entity. Late filing penalty: 5% of the tax due for each month or fraction of delay, up to a maximum of 100% of the tax due. If no tax is due, a fixed sanction of approximately COP 500,000 (equivalent to 10 UVT) applies. Monthly default interest on unpaid tax: 1.5%, increasing with the usury rate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: COP 1,250,000,000 (approx. USD 300,000) in gross assets or gross income; or transactions with related parties exceeding COP 150,000,000 (approx. USD 36,000). When thresholds are exceeded, the taxpayer must prepare and file Local File, Master File and an annual Transfer Pricing Information Return (Form 120) with DIAN.

Economic substance scope: Colombia’s tax framework requires economic substance under the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) and Article 122-1 ET. For an S.A.S. retailing imported products, substance is evidenced by physical store premises, operating payroll, inventory risk, and management decisions in Colombia. The entity must not be a mere conduit or pass-through for royalty or service fees; the OECD/G20 BEPS Action 6 principles and local case law require that the entity has the capability and actual exercise of control over its functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 4 to 9 years under Article 434A of the Criminal Code; fine up to COP 1,000,000,000 (approx. USD 250,000); tax exclusion and forfeiture of goods; civil restitution equal to the evaded tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable under the doctrine of veil-piercing when acting in bad faith; they face joint and several liability for tax debts and criminal imprisonment, travel bans, and asset freezes for the duration of proceedings.
Shell Company / Sham Structure

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disallow advantages, apply the GAAR, and assess tax on a presumptive basis; fines for defamation of tax advantage and potential money laundering charges under Law 1121/2006.

Director personal liability: Shares may be ignored, and directors become personally liable for the company’s debts and taxes; travel bans and refusal to renew RUT may be imposed.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Money laundering offenses are punishable under Law 1762/2015 with imprisonment of 10 to 30 years; assets may be forfeited; fines up to 50% of the laundered amount.

Director personal liability: Directors must file Suspicious Transaction Reports (ROS) with the Financial Information Unit (UIAF); failure exposes them to criminal liability, personal fines, and prohibition from holding office in financial or commercial entities.
Municipal Operating License / Tax Violation

Statutory sanction: Closure of the establishment by the mayor; fines of up to 2,000 UVT for failure to remit ICA; arrest of responsible parties for up to 5 years under certain public order rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives of the S.A.S. are directly liable for unpaid ICA taxes and penalties; in case of closure, they may be suspended from re-entry to the municipality and face administrative fines.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Automatic increase in taxable income by 10% of transaction value (or higher at DIAN’s discretion); penalty of 200% of the omitted tax for non-compliance; daily fine of up to 50 UVT for each day of delay; formal penalty up to COP 1,000,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the return remain liable for adjustments; failure to produce documentation may lead to criminal investigation for false documentation; personal insolvency proceedings may follow if assessed penalty cannot be paid by the company.

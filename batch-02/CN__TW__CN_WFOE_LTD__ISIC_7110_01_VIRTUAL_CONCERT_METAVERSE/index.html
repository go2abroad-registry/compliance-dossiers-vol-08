Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office or commercial mailbox services are generally not permissible for a WFOE in China. The SAMR and the State Taxation Administration require a physical registered address that is verifiable and where the company's actual business operations are conducted. For a virtual concert and metaverse company, the business may be conducted online, but the registered office must still be a physical location (e.g., a leased office or co-working space) that can receive official correspondence and be subject to inspection. Banks in China typically reject virtual office addresses for corporate account opening, as they require a physical presence for KYC and AML compliance. The local tax bureau may also reject a virtual office for VAT registration and invoice issuance. Therefore, a physical office lease is mandatory, and the lease agreement must be submitted to the SAMR and tax authorities. The office must have a signboard, and the company must maintain a physical presence during business hours.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through the in-charge local tax service bureaus; electronic filing and registration are performed on the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal of the STA.

E-government filing portal

Establish the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) via the enterprise registration process. The issued business license carries the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which operates as the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration identifier under China's multi-license integration reform; no separate TIN or VAT ID application is required after SAMR registration. Within 30 days of business-license issuance, the WFOE must complete post-registration tax information confirmation through the Electronic Tax Bureau, select the in-charge tax bureau, set up fiscal accounting records, register VAT invoice schemes, apply for digital invoice issuance qualification, and, if exporting goods or cross-border services, complete export-tax-filing qualification. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China is not issued a standalone VAT ID; China-sourced income is subject to withholding by the payer, and the non-resident entity effects registration by filing through the withholding agent or, in the absence of an agent, by making a one-time registration in the Electronic Tax Bureau for the specific income item.

Entity_Level_Taxation

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for basic VAT registration where any taxable business is carried on; mandatory General VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000. For virtual performance hosting and digital ticketing services the applicable VAT rate is 6%; standard goods sales are 13%/9% as applicable; exports and qualifying cross-border services are zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from the People's Republic of China may be subject to VAT at 0% when the exporter files a customs export declaration, the goods physically leave China's customs territory, the export sale is supported by a valid overseas contract and invoice, and export proceeds are received from abroad. Cross-border services, including qualifying offshore digital services such as virtual event hosting and metaverse ticketing platforms, are eligible for the 0% rate or VAT exemption when the service is supplied to a recipient located outside China, the service is consumed entirely outside China, and the WFOE retains a copy of the overseas service contract, the bank receipt of offshore payment, and supporting metaverse platform records demonstrating no use of the service in China.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Export) filed through the China International Trade Single Window showing the goods description, quantity, value, destination country and customs clearance status
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from the Chinese customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank foreign-exchange collection voucher matched to the export declaration and SAFE trade-monitoring records
  • Export VAT invoice issued under the zero-rating code with 0% tax rate and containing the USCC/TIN of the WFOE
  • Export contract and commercial invoice specifying the overseas buyer and settlement currency

China operates the Golden Tax system and the fully digitalised electronic invoice (e-invoice) administration regime. All VAT invoices for taxable sales must be issued through the STA-controlled e-invoice platform; issuance data is transmitted in real time to the tax authority. Fully digitalised e-invoices carry a unique 20-digit invoice number, an electronic signature, and a dynamic QR anti-fraud stamp; paper invoices, where still authorised, must be generated through STA-certified tax control equipment. High-volume issuers may use system-to-system interfaces, but every invoice must originate from the official fiscal environment; no third-party private invoice stamping is permitted.

Unverified or fraudulent export zero-rating claims are subject to recovery of all VAT input credits refunded, collection of the underpaid VAT, and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax amount involved under Article 63 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. Cases involving forged customs documents, counterfeit export declarations, or circular repatriation schemes are referred for criminal liability for tax fraud under the Criminal Law; tax authorities may also suspend the WFOE's export-rebate qualification, deny future zero-rated invoices, and conduct retrospective audits of at least the previous five tax years together with related-party foreign-exchange flows.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime. The New Taiwan dollar is freely convertible for current-account transactions, including cross-border trade settlements, service fees, wages, and profit remittances, subject to standard reporting through authorized foreign-exchange banks. Capital-account transactions are generally liberalized for direct investment, portfolio investment, and loans, but remain subject to prior approval or declaration to the CBC under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act. A critical nuance for this structure is the mainland-China source of the inbound equity: because the ultimate parent is a people's-republic-of-china enterprise, the inbound remittance will be examined under Taiwan's Regulations Governing Investment in Taiwan by Mainland Chinese Investors and will require the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs to screen the direct investment. Profit remittance is permitted on an arm's-length, tax-cleared basis: dividend distributions to the mainland shareholder require audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and a CBC-approved foreign-exchange declaration, and are subject to a 10% withholding under the Income Tax Act. Excess volatility is smoothed by the CBC's intervention, which is neither pegged nor a clean float, so treasurers must maintain a disciplined hedging policy. No mandatory conversion ratio exists for export proceeds; enterprises may retain foreign currency in bank accounts, but all receipts must be reported or settled through designated FX banks, and any large-value or suspicious foreign-exchange transaction is reported to the CBC's anti-money-laundering database.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act Article 7 and the CBC Measures Governing Certification of Foreign Exchange Receipts and Disbursements, an exporter of goods is required to receive, settle, or deposit the foreign-currency proceeds with an authorized foreign-exchange bank within 10 calendar days from the date of the export customs declaration; for cross-border services such as virtual-concert ticketing, the trigger date is the service-invoice or contract completion date. There is no mandatory surrender conversion ratio: 100% of export proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency deposit account or converted into NTD at the exporter's discretion, subject to the CBC large-value foreign-exchange reporting thresholds and a five-year documentary retention rule covering the commercial invoice, bill of lading, service contract, customs entry stamp, and CBC declaration form.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), with the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) as co-supervisor for banking and AML licensing; the CBC implements the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, operates interbank NTD clearing, and receives all bank-filed foreign-exchange declarations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury and clearing rails; Medium only as an ancillary collection aggregator. Offshore fintech EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Stripe and similar digital clearing houses) are legally barred from providing Taiwan-domestic deposit-taking, NTD clearing-membership, or local-currency settlement services, because Banking Act Article 29 and the Electronic Payment Institution Act require an FSC license before an intermediary may hold customer funds or clear New Taiwan dollar payments on behalf of a domestic entity. An EMI may enter the flow only as an upstream card-acquiring or payment-aggregation layer processing ticket sales, with all settled receipts swept daily in fiat to a licensed Taiwan Tier-1 bank account. For the USDC leg of this metaverse concert business, the conversion must be executed offshore by a compliant virtual-asset service provider or a fully licensed counterparty before any fiat remittance touches Taiwan; no crypto settlement may occur in the domestic NTD account, and the bank will require written assurance of this separation at onboarding.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant sequence: (1) obtain Investment Commission (MOEAIC) approval for the mainland-China investor; (2) open at a Tier-1 bank a foreign-investment capital escrow account functioning as the NT dollar injection vehicle; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury and clearing rails; Medium only as an ancillary collection aggregator. Offshore fintech EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Stripe and similar digital clearing houses) are legally barred from providing Taiwan-domestic deposit-taking, NTD clearing-membership, or local-currency settlement services, because Banking Act Article 29 and the Electronic Payment Institution Act require an FSC license before an intermediary may hold customer funds or clear New Taiwan dollar payments on behalf of a domestic entity. An EMI may enter the flow only as an upstream card-acquiring or payment-aggregation layer processing ticket sales, with all settled receipts swept daily in fiat to a licensed Taiwan Tier-1 bank account. For the USDC leg of this metaverse concert business, the conversion must be executed offshore by a compliant virtual-asset service provider or a fully licensed counterparty before any fiat remittance touches Taiwan; no crypto settlement may occur in the domestic NTD account, and the bank will require written assurance of this separation at onboarding.

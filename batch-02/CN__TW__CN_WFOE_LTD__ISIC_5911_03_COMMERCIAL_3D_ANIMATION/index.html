Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Taiwan law does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a wholly foreign-owned limited liability company. The capital amount stated in the approved investment plan must nevertheless be remitted through an Authorized Foreign Exchange Bank and placed into a Foreign Investor Investment Account before a certified public accountant can issue the capital verification report. There is no statutory blocked escrow account requirement, but the funds must remain traceable and available for the approved business purpose; if the ultimate investor is a Mainland Chinese person or entity, additional source-of-funds evidence and prior approval under the Mainland investment regulations will be required.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable as a standalone industrial permit, but the registered office/studio must obtain the standard fire safety equipment inspection and workplace facility clearance required by the Fire Services Act and the Occupational Safety and Health Act for lawful office use, including emergency exits, fire extinguishers, and electrical safety inspections.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; the official online electronic tax service portal is the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) accessible at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, which handles corporate income tax, VAT, withholding, and export tax refund administration.

E-government filing portal

A Chinese WFOE LLC is incorporated under the Company Law and obtains a business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license is the statutory TIN for all tax, customs, and social insurance matters. Within 30 days of license issuance, the WFOE must complete comprehensive tax registration with the State Taxation Administration through the e-Tax portal, disclosing the legal representative, registered capital, industry classification (ISIC 5911), bank account details, and tax registration type (general or small-scale VAT taxpayer). For a foreign non-resident enterprise with no permanent establishment in China but deriving China-source income, the enterprise must obtain a Chinese tax identification number by filing a non-resident enterprise tax registration application (directly or through a withholding agent) with the competent STA office, providing its foreign incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, proof of board resolution appointing a tax contact, and details of the China-source transaction; the STA will issue a non-resident TIN independent of a USCC for use in assessments and withholding returns.

The Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license is the statutory TIN for all tax, customs, and social insurance matters. Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not provide check-the-box or pass-through entity classification elections for limited liability companies. Every Chinese-incorporated WFOE is automatically a resident enterprise taxed at the entity level on worldwide income, and there are no hybrid mismatch elections or fiscal unity (consolidation) elections permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must register for VAT with the STA upon incorporation regardless of turnover. Mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer applies when annual taxable sales (including export sales) exceed RMB 5,000,000; below this threshold, the entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer at a simplified rate. For a non-resident service supplier without an establishment in China, a simplified VAT registration is triggered if its China-source taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT is available for exported goods under Article 15 of the Provisional Regulations on value-added tax. For cross-border services, zero-rating or exemption applies to Chinese taxpayers that supply qualifying services (including commercial animation, CGI visual effects and post-production services) to non-resident customers where the services are performed entirely or substantially in China but the economic exploitation and delivery occur outside China, and the consideration is paid from outside mainland China. The services must be listed in the STA/MOF Cross-Border Taxable Behavior VAT Zero-Rating and Exemption Catalogue; the taxpayer must prove non-resident recipient status, offshore business purpose, and foreign currency repatriation.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (for any physical media or tangible master deliverables; the export entry number must be linked to the VAT invoice and export refund application)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (for physical exports), or a service delivery acceptance certificate digitally signed by the overseas client (for digital 3D animation file exports)
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Collection Receipt / Export Proceeds Verification Form evidencing SWIFT or Business-to-Business payment rail receipt of the export consideration
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice, the export service contract, and any intellectual property license or buy-out agreement demonstrating the economic use outside China

China operates a mandatory centralized VAT invoice clearance system under the Golden Tax Phase IV framework. All VAT special and ordinary invoices must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's electronic invoice service platform or with a tax control U-Key, with invoice data transmitted in real time to the STA. The official all-digital electronic invoice (increasingly standard) is issued via the national electronic invoice platform, which assigns each invoice a unique encrypted invoice code and digital stamp; real-time API reporting, automated tax-policy matching, and anti-fraud validation are integral to the platform. No physical fiscal stamping devices are required for the all-digital electronic invoice, but every invoice must reconcile with the taxpayer's VAT return and export zero-rating documentation.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to documentary verification by the STA, including cross-matching of customs data, invoice data, and bank foreign exchange receipts. If the required evidence is not obtained within the statutory period, or if foreign exchange is not repatriated within 180 days from the date of export/service completion (unless a waiver is granted), the claimed zero-rating is reversed and VAT becomes payable with late payment interest. Fraudulent or erroneous zero-rating claims may be penalized at rates of 0.5 to 3 times the amount evaded under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and the Criminal Law for VAT fraud, with additional clawback of any export tax rebate previously granted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is fully convertible on current account for trade in services, trade in goods, royalties, and profit remittances, subject to authenticity and documentation review by a designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions remain administratively controlled: inbound equity capital from the Taiwan shareholder requires SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) registration before remittance, and the capital contribution is subject to China's negative list screening. Profit remittance to the Taiwan shareholder is permitted after the WFOE completes its annual statutory audit, accrues and pays corporate income tax, withholds dividend tax in accordance with the applicable cross-strait tax arrangement, and obtains the tax payment certificate and SAFE verification through the account bank. The WFOE may freely settle export receipts from 3D animation and VFX services into RMB for operating purposes, but may not use capital-account RMB for prohibited speculation, equity investments outside its registered business scope, or unauthorized related-party lending.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from animation production services, audiovisual content licensing, and VFX service contracts must be repatriated into a SAFE-monitored current-account foreign exchange settlement account in mainland China within the statutory collection period; for trade-in-goods flows the customary statutory window is 180 days from the export declaration date, while for services the controlling timeline is the invoice or contract milestone date as verified by the receiving bank. SAFE has abolished mandatory conversion ratios: the WFOE may retain the full export receipt in foreign currency or sell it to the bank for RMB at the prevailing market rate. Every receipt must be reconciled to the underlying contract, invoice, and delivery evidence, and must be declared under SAFE's balance-of-payments reporting system. Overdue unrepatriated or unreconciled export proceeds can trigger reduced trade facilitation status, enhanced documentary audits, and administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium as a direct onshore bank substitute, but Medium-to-High as an auxiliary collection and disbursement rail. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not licensed Chinese deposit-taking institutions and cannot hold SAFE-registered FDI capital or provide a statutory RMB account for the WFOE. They may be used by the Taiwan parent or an offshore collection vehicle to aggregate inbound subscription, licensing, or streaming revenue, followed by an MT103 wire into the WFOE's licensed PRC bank account. Onshore payment processing by the WFOE itself requires either a licensed bank sponsor or a PBOC-licensed non-bank payment institution; maintaining the WFOE's primary treasury balance on an offshore EMI would breach SAFE repatriation, tax filing, and audit-evidence requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from the Taiwan shareholder must be routed by SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's SAFE-designated capital contribution foreign exchange account at a licensed PRC bank. Before remittance, the WFOE must complete SAFE FDI foreign exchange registration through the receiving bank and obtain the FDI registration number; the remittance field is required to state the purpose as 'FDI equity capital injection from Taiwan shareholder' and to quote the SAFE FDI registration code. The bank will verify the board resolution, the capital contribution schedule, the parent bank statement of account, and the negative-list compliance certificate before crediting the account. 20% of taxable income for Taiwan profit-seeking enterprises

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan dollars (NTD). A Local File is generally required where annual net revenue reaches at least NTD 300 million or the aggregate amount of relevant controlled transactions reaches at least NTD 200 million, subject to transaction-type exemptions and specific thresholds. A Master File is generally required for an enterprise with annual net revenue of at least NTD 3 billion that has cross-border controlled transactions. Country-by-Country Reporting generally applies to Taiwan constituent entities of multinational groups meeting the prescribed consolidated revenue threshold, commonly EUR 750 million, subject to Taiwan filing and notification rules.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a single OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations regime covering every holding, financing, or IP entity. Substance is enforced through Taiwan transfer-pricing rules, general anti-avoidance principles, beneficial-ownership analysis, controlled foreign company rules, place-of-effective-management considerations, permanent-establishment rules, business-purpose requirements, and anti-abuse provisions. A CGI or animation subsidiary should maintain real personnel, production functions, decision-making, equipment or controlled production capacity, project records, customer contracts, risk control, and banking activity proportionate to its reported profit. Contractual labels cannot replace actual functions and risk assumption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, interest, tax evasion penalties, assessment based on estimated income, compulsory collection, and potential criminal prosecution. Serious tax evasion may expose the responsible person to imprisonment and substantial fines under Taiwan tax and criminal legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors, responsible persons, accounting personnel, and participating agents may face joint or individual criminal liability, civil restitution, tax recovery, asset attachment, restrictions on departure or travel in enforcement circumstances, and imprisonment where statutory elements are proven. Limited liability does not protect deliberate personal misconduct.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unpaid withholding tax from the payer, penalties, interest, possible disallowance of deductions, and collection enforcement. The payer may remain liable even where the foreign recipient has already received the funds.

Director personal liability: The responsible person and persons controlling payment or tax reporting may incur administrative and criminal exposure for intentional withholding-tax concealment, together with civil liability for unpaid tax and penalties.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Inadequate Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustment to arm's-length income, denial of deductions, additional corporate income tax, interest, penalties, documentation penalties, and possible withholding-tax reassessment. Repeated or material non-compliance increases audit and enforcement risk.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face civil liability for tax loss caused by intentional misrepresentation or breach of fiduciary duties. Criminal exposure may arise if TP manipulation is part of fraud, tax evasion, false accounting, or money laundering.
Shell Company, Beneficial Ownership, and Anti-Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or tax benefits, recharacterization of payments, tax reassessment, penalties, interest, beneficial-owner challenges, and possible criminal investigation for fraud, false documents, or tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may be subject to civil damages, restitution, asset freezing, departure restrictions in enforcement proceedings, disqualification consequences, and imprisonment if fraud, document falsification, or tax evasion is established.
Anti-Money Laundering and Suspicious Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: Transaction blocking, reporting obligations, account restrictions, administrative penalties, confiscation, and criminal sanctions under Taiwan anti-money-laundering legislation. Money laundering offenses may carry imprisonment of up to seven years and substantial fines, depending on the offense and amount.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, finance officers, and facilitators may face personal criminal prosecution, asset confiscation, civil restitution, travel or departure restrictions, and imprisonment. A company-level compliance failure does not immunize knowing participants.
False Invoicing and VAT Fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, denial of input tax credits and expense deductions, fines, interest, compulsory collection, and criminal prosecution for material or intentional invoice fraud.

Director personal liability: Company representatives and accounting personnel who authorize or knowingly participate may face joint liability, civil recovery, asset attachment, departure restrictions, and imprisonment under applicable tax and criminal laws.
Unauthorized Distribution and Capital Maintenance

Statutory sanction: Recovery of improper distributions, director liability for company losses, accounting restatement, tax reassessment, and penalties for unlawful distributions or inaccurate financial reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for restitution and damages, may face shareholder or creditor claims, and may incur criminal liability where the conduct involves fraud, misappropriation, false accounting, or intentional prejudice to creditors.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) - Colombian Tax and Customs Authority; official portal: www.dian.gov.co; transactional portal: muisca.dian.gov.co.

E-government filing portal

For a Colombian S.A.S., incorporate by private instrument filed with the Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio); obtain the certificate of existence and legal representation; complete the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) as a resident corporation with IVA-responsible and exporter status; DIAN assigns the NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria). Foreign non-resident entities without local incorporation obtain a NIT/RUT through DIAN by appointing a Colombian legal representative; digital services providers must complete the simplified e-service registration before charging Colombian VAT.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. An S.A.S. formed in Colombia is a resident legal entity taxed separately on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment is available. Non-resident branch/permanent establishment is taxed only on Colombian-source income unless a tax treaty applies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An S.A.S. formed in Colombia is a resident legal entity taxed separately on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment is available. Non-resident branch/permanent establishment is taxed only on Colombian-source income unless a tax treaty applies. Zona Franca incentives require separate qualification and are not the default classification.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists; Colombian entity classification is statutory and not elective. The S.A.S. may elect the SIMPLE tax regime if it meets legal turnover and staff thresholds; may elect accelerated deduction for qualifying fixed-asset investments; may apply exporters' customs programmes (e.g., Plan Vallejo) when qualified; and may adopt IFRS for tax accounting. Hybrid mismatch rules under Law 2010 of 2019 apply mandatorily and do not provide an elective opt-out; treaty benefits require economic substance and the corresponding DIAN reporting.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Corporations have no de minimis VAT exemption: an S.A.S. is required to register as an IVA-responsible taxpayer from the first taxable operation. The natural-person exemption threshold under Article 499 E.T. is gross income not exceeding 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 174,296,500 for tax year 2025), but this threshold is not available to legal entities. Export transactions additionally require customs and RUT reporter status as exporter.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods such as dental clear-aligner sheets qualify for the 0% IVA rate if the goods are sold for value to a foreign-domiciled buyer, exported through Colombian customs territory under an accepted export declaration, and physically removed from Colombia. Zero-rating of related services is allowed only where the service is performed entirely abroad or directly supports the exported goods. The exporter must be registered in the RUT as exporter and must hold the full documentary chain proving departure and receipt by the foreign buyer.

  • Accepted Declaración de Exportación (DEX/DAE) transmitted through VUCE/DIAN
  • Bill of Lading (BL), air waybill, or equivalent transport document evidencing physical departure
  • Commercial invoice with electronic CUFE validation
  • Packing list and cargo/booking documentation
  • RUT certificate confirming exporter status
  • Certificate of origin where applicable or requested by the buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Canalización de Divisas) or foreign-currency settlement declaration from a Colombian financial intermediary
  • Proof of foreign-currency payment matching the export invoice

All VAT-responsible taxpayers must issue electronic sales invoices (Factura Electrónica de Venta - FEV) using DIAN-authorized software or DIAN's free platform. The invoice is transmitted to DIAN for validation before or at issuance through interoperable APIs; each validated invoice carries a CUFE/CUNE digital stamp and QR code. Physical wet stamps are not accepted. Export sales must be linked to the electronic export declaration and customs acceptance records.

DIAN applies pre-audit validation, random inspections, and red-channel customs checks to export VAT refunds. Claiming 0% without the required export declaration, shipment evidence, or FX settlement causes reversal of the VAT credit, reclassification of the supply at 19%, assessment of unpaid VAT plus default interest, and the inaccuracy penalty under Article 648 E.T. Fraudulent export zero-rating or forged FX certificates can lead to exporter status cancellation, goods seizure, and criminal liability under Colombian customs and anti-smuggling legislation (Law 1762).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is freely convertible for current-account transactions. Capital-account inflows, including foreign direct investment (FDI) into a SAS, require registration with Banco de la República in the International Investment Register (Registro Internacional de Inversiones) to secure future dividend remittance and capital repatriation rights. Profit remittances must be routed through the official foreign exchange market (Mercado Cambiario) via authorized intermediaries and are subject to the submission of Foreign Exchange Declarations (Declaración de Cambio). Delays or omissions in registration, in form or in substance, may render the remittance non-channeled and subject to fines, loss of tax benefits, and restrictions on upstreaming funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds derived from goods or services (including production and sale of clear aligner sheets) must be collected and channeled into Colombia within six (6) months from the date of shipment or service completion. Channeling must be made through an authorized intermediary (a commercial bank) in the foreign exchange market. The mandatory surrender/conversion obligation applies to 100% of the export invoice value, except for de minimis transactions or specific exemptions granted under the FX regime; non-channeled amounts trigger penalties and are admitted only under the mechanisms set forth in the Colombian exchange-control rules.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia), acting through the foreign exchange regime (Régimen Cambiario), with DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) responsible for exchange-control declaration filings and customs validation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Digital-only offshore EMI accounts cannot replace the mandatory local bank channeling obligation imposed by the Colombian FX regime. While Stripe and comparable payment gateways can collect consumer card payments on behalf of the Colombian SAS, settlement funds must ultimately be received through a licensed Colombian financial entity or an authorized FX intermediary, and the underlying cross-border payments must be recorded as export proceeds. Offshore EMIs that lack a Colombian license cannot legally act as the primary repository for operational funds, and funds held overseas without Banco de la República authorization may violate foreign-exchange disclosure duties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign shareholders must wire capital contribution through an authorized Colombian commercial bank using the SWIFT MT103 network. The local bank records the inflow in the Declaración de Cambio under the concept code for 'Direct Investment – Capital in Companies' (or the equivalent code in force). Prior or simultaneous registration in Banco de la República's International Investment Register is required. Supporting documentation includes: apostilled incorporation documents of the investing entity or individual, the SAS bylaws, shareholder resolutions, identity and suitability documents, source-of-funds statements, and the bank's own KYC forms. The bank will verify the transaction against FDI regulations, sanctions lists, and tax forms; no funds may be released from a temporary account without deliverable confirmation of the registered investment.Permitted clearing rails include: (i) SWIFT MT103 wires for supplier payments, licensing fees, intercompany purchases, and dividend distributions; (ii) documentary commercial letters of credit for high-value equipment imports or plastic/resin raw material consignments; (iii) local-currency (COP) wire through CUD/CENIT systems for domestic settlements; and (iv) centralized treasury batches via SWIFT/SCORE or API-connected platforms at the local bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

35% (general corporate income tax rate under Law 2277 of 2022; no industry-specific surcharge applicable to medical-device / aligner manufacturing)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

5% withholding on dividends remitted to non-resident shareholders when the distributing profits were already subject to the 35% corporate income tax; 35% withholding when dividends are paid from profits that were untaxed or exempt at the corporate level (impuesto adicional de dividendos).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing window is set annually by DIAN Resolution and depends on the last two digits of the tax ID (NIT); for calendar-year taxpayers the statutory deadline generally falls between 12 August and 9 October of the following year (e.g., FY2023 returns were due 12 August 2024 – 9 October 2024). No automatic extension exists.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall

Automatically populated by the DIAN portal; no action required from the non-resident shareholder. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: COP 7,059,750,000 (150,000 UVT of gross income or total net assets for FY2024) triggers the TP informative return and the Local File; COP 21,179,250,000 (450,000 UVT) triggers the Master File; transactions with a single related counterparty equal or exceeding 10,000 UVT (approx. COP 470,650,000) require separate transaction-level TP substantiation.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not apply a standalone ESR code, but enforces substance over form through the general anti-avoidance rule (Article 869 E.T.), OECD/BEPS-aligned controlled-foreign-company rules, and permanent-establishment attribution (Article 884 E.T.). A foreign-owned SAS that allegedly manufacturers clear aligner sheets but lacks Colombian premises, production staff, board minutes, or genuine decision-making risks having related-party transactions disregarded and being recharacterized as a conduit of the foreign parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Omission of Assets

Statutory sanction: Tax correction plus penalty of 100-200% of the omitted tax (Articles 648-649 E.T.); criminal tax evasion under Article 434A of the Colombian Criminal Code (as introduced by Law 1943 of 2018) when the evaded amount exceeds 250 legal minimum monthly wages (approx. COP 325 million in 2024), punishable by 48-108 months of imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The legal representative is jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes when the omission is willful or grossly negligent; criminal judges may impose travel bans (prohibición de salida del país), asset freezes, and, upon conviction, imprisonment for both the administrator and the foreign beneficial owner.
Shell Company / Artificial Transactions (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the abusive transaction under Article 869 E.T., re-determination of the taxable income of the parent or the SAS, penalty of up to 100% of the tax avoided, and an additional fine of 10% of the transaction value; the entity may be declared a façade and dissolved.

Director personal liability: Substance-over-form recharacterization pierces the limited-liability shield; directors face civil restitution for recomputed taxes and fines, and may be prosecuted for conspiracy if the shell is used to disguise the origin or destination of funds.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment increasing taxable income, secondary adjustment recharacterized as a constructive dividend subject to 5%/35% withholding, plus a sanction of 1.5% of the value of the related-party transactions covered by the declaration.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign knowingly false TP documentation are treated as joint and several debtors of the resulting tax assessment; DIAN may petition precautionary seizures (embargos) over their personal assets.
AML / Foreign Exchange Infraction

Statutory sanction: Penalties for unreported FX operations of up to 200% of the amount involved; money-laundering offenses under Law 599 of 2000 (Articles 323-325) carry prison terms of 10-30 years for willful concealment of the origin or destination of funds.

Director personal liability: Directors must appoint a compliance officer (oficial de cumplimiento) where transaction volumes exceed UIAF thresholds; failure to implement AML procedures exposes directors to criminal liability, travel restrictions, disqualification from corporate office, and personal seizure of assets.

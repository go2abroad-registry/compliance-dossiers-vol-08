Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital. At least twenty-five percent (25%) of the subscribed share capital must be paid in cash at incorporation (Ley 1258 de 2008, art. 12); the balance must be paid within two years. No escrow account is required; contributions in kind must be itemized and valued in the bylaws. Pending subscriptions do not prevent registration.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN). Primary digital portals: Muisca (Modelo Único de Ingresos, Servicio y Control Automatizado) and 'Sí@' e-services on www.dian.gov.co; exporter and customs filing platforms: VUCE (Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior) and SIIS; corporate acts are registered with the Cámara de Comercio before DIAN tax enrollment.

E-government filing portal

Procedure for a foreign-owned Colombian S.A.S.: (1) incorporate the S.A.S. by notarial deed or private instrument and register it with the Cámara de Comercio to obtain the certificate of existence and legal representation; (2) obtain the Registro Único Tributario (RUT) and the definitive 10-digit NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria) through DIAN, either electronically on Muisca or at a DIAN service point; (3) complete the RUT with the ISIC 4791 e-commerce activity line and elect the status of 'responsable de IVA'; (4) enroll in the VUCE national exporters registry and secure the authorized digital signature for electronic export declarations; (5) activate mandatory electronic invoicing by obtaining an accredited digital signature certificate and requesting DIAN authorization for the Factura Electrónica de Venta, with the corresponding real-time pre-validation credentials; (6) where a foreign entity sells directly into Colombia without a Colombian entity, it must appoint a local apoderado and obtain its own NIT/RUT before commencing taxable sales.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The S.A.S. is a Colombian-resident juridical person subject to entity-level corporate income tax on worldwide income (35% general rate for tax year 2024; 20% inside an authorized Zona Franca only). It is not a pass-through for Colombian income tax purposes, and no Tax_Exempt_Zone status applies to this structure unless separately authorized. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The S.A.S. is a Colombian-resident juridical person subject to entity-level corporate income tax on worldwide income (35% general rate for tax year 2024; 20% inside an authorized Zona Franca only). It is not a pass-through for Colombian income tax purposes, and no Tax_Exempt_Zone status applies to this structure unless separately authorized. Territorial scope: Colombian residents have a worldwide tax base; non-resident sellers are taxed only on Colombian-source income.

Available election forms

Colombia has no U.S.-type check-the-box election. Relevant mechanisms: (i) optional election to apply the combined simplified regime 'Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE)' for eligible S.A.S. entities with prior-year gross income of not more than 100,000 UVT (approx. COP 4,706.5 million in 2024), substituting income tax and local trade tax with a single periodic turnover tax; (ii) voluntary registration as a VAT-responsible taxpayer by otherwise excludable small merchants (gross income under 3,500 UVT) to obtain input VAT credits; (iii) election for consolidated income tax filing by economic groups satisfying DIAN ownership and integration thresholds; (iv) anti-hybrid treatment: Colombian anti-deferral and mismatch rules under Law 1819/2016 and subsequent reforms generally respect the foreign jurisdiction's entity classification unless a deduction-without-inclusion or double-deduction outcome arises; no unilateral protective election form exists, and taxpayers must align documentation where Colombian and foreign classifications diverge.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary minimum triggers VAT responsibility: every supplier of taxable goods in Colombian territory, including a non-resident online retailer delivering 3D printers and consumables to Colombian consumers, must register in the RUT and charge IVA. The only statutory exclusion concerns small merchants whose gross income does not exceed 3,500 UVT (approx. COP 164,727,500 in 2024) and who are not importers or exporters; an e-commerce seller that imports or exports does not qualify for that exclusion and must be a full VAT-responsible taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical exports of desktop 3D printers and resin filaments by the Colombian S.A.S. to foreign final customers qualify for 0% VAT as excluded sales with full right to input VAT credit/refund, provided: the exporter is registered in the RUT and the national exporters registry; the transaction is a definitive export under Decree 1165/2019; the goods physically depart Colombian customs territory within the applicable legal dispatch period; an electronic Declaración de Exportación is accepted by DIAN; export proceeds are repatriated through the Colombian foreign-exchange market within 180 days of the relevant shipment/declaration date; and contemporaneous documents prove order, payment, dispatch, and foreign receipt. Offshore services forming part of the hardware export may qualify under Estatuto Tributario Article 481 only if effectively used abroad; local delivery to Colombian addresses is never zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Declaración de Exportación) electronically lodged and accepted via VUCE/SIIS, identifying the DEX control number and DIAN customs validation
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or International Consignment Note proving physical departure of the goods from Colombian customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificado de Comercialización de Divisas / foreign-exchange settlement statement) issued by an authorized Colombian FX intermediary, evidencing repatriation of the export sale proceeds

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing applies to all VAT-responsible taxpayers. The S.A.S. must issue the Factura Electrónica de Venta (FEV) in DIAN-approved XML/UBL format using accredited technology providers or certified in-house software; each invoice must pass DIAN 'Validación Previa' in real time and carry a unique CUNE (Código Único de Numeración) validation number (or issuer-generated CUFE in transitional modalities); digital signature certificates from DIAN-accredited certification authorities are mandatory; the payment rails (Stripe and Shopify Payments) must integrate with the DIAN-authorized invoicing flow to issue compliant FEV or consumer POS receipts. Export transactions are cross-controlled through the electronic Declaración de Exportación filed in VUCE/SIIS, with customs departure validation and automatic cross-matching of zero-rated export refund claims.

DIAN systematically cross-audits zero-rated export refunds against electronic invoicing, customs departure data, and FX settlement records. If the exporter fails to evidence a real, definitive export, the goods are not physically shipped, or the 180-day FX repatriation deadline is missed, the zero-rating is revoked and VAT becomes due on the invoice value, with moratory interest from the original filing date. Ancillary consequences include: the inexactness penalty under Estatuto Tributario Article 648 (100% of the tax difference, increased to 200% in abusive or artificially structured cases); the improper-refund penalty under Article 855 (restitution plus 100% of the amount improperly refunded); deregistration or suspension from the exporters registry; criminal tax-evasion or fraud prosecution under Law 1819/2016 as amended, carrying imprisonment of approximately 4 to 9 years and fines when the evaded or fraudulently refunded amount exceeds the statutory threshold; and joint liability of legal representatives, accountants, and board members who signed or certified the false returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Colombia maintains a managed floating exchange regime with a fully regulated 'mercado cambiario' (foreign exchange market). Current account transactions (goods imports, services, royalties, and profit remittances derived from registered investment) are freely convertible and channeled through authorized intermediaries without prior central bank approval. Capital account transactions, including inbound direct investment, must be registered with Banco de la República to legitimize the right to remit dividends, capital gains, and repatriate principal. Colombia retains a 10% remittance tax (impuesto de remesas) on net after-tax profits remitted to non-resident shareholders, without treaty reduction; the SAS must therefore document that Ecuador/EU/Other treaty sourcing does not otherwise alter local remittance taxation. FX convertibility for e-commerce collections from Stripe/Shopify is operationally straightforward, subject to correct purpose-code classification and monthly/quarterly filing of the Declaración de Cambio through a local bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from goods (3D printers, resin filaments, consumables) must be channeled through the Colombian foreign exchange market via an authorized local intermediary within six (6) months from the date of shipment, extendable by up to an additional six (6) months upon justified request to Banco de la República. There is no compulsory surrender/conversion quota to the Central Bank; the exporter may hold the foreign currency in a local foreign-currency checking account ('cuenta de compensación') or a foreign overseas account, but the proceeds must still be registered through the 'Declaración de Cambio' (form code 3101 for goods exports) and must remain identifiable against the export customs declaration (exportación). Services and digital platform remuneration must be channeled within six (6) months from the accrual date. Failure to channel within the statutory term constitutes an exchange infraction under Article 5 of Law 9 of 1991, punishable by fines calculated on the unpaid or late-regulated amount.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (Central Bank of Colombia) as the monetary and exchange control authority, with the Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (DIAN) enforcing customs and exchange infractions; regulatory framework comprises Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1068 of 2015 (Decreto Único Reglamentario del Sector Hacienda), Circular Reglamentaria DCIN-83 (foreign exchange regulations), and the Foreign Investment Statistical Registration regime administered by Banco de la República.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail, but never a statutory substitute for a local bank account. Foreign EMIs (e.g., Stripe, PayPal, Payoneer, Wise, Airwallex) are not licensed credit institutions under Colombian law; they operate as foreign payment service providers and their accounts are classified as 'cuentas en el exterior' for regulatory purposes. They are viable for aggregating card-based e-commerce receipts from buyers in Latin America and for low-value supplier disbursements, but the SAS must (i) maintain a local authorized-intermediary account to channel regulated FX transactions, (ii) file Formulario No. 13 (reporte de cuentas en el exterior) with Banco de la República for any overseas account whose monthly balance exceeds USD 12,000, and (iii) incorporate the third-party settlement flows into the Declaración de Cambio as services or goods receipts. Legal constraints include lack of DIAN-backed receipts for VAT/ICA purposes, potential commission erosion, and the impossibility of obtaining an export channeling certificate directly from an EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must remit equity contributions via SWIFT MT103 to the SAS's dedicated USD or COP account at a local authorized intermediary, using the standard purpose code for direct investment (3201 - 'Inversión de capital') in the 'Declaración de Cambio por Inversiones Internacionales'. The foreign investment must be registered with Banco de la República using Formulario No. 5 within twelve (12) months of disbursement; registration is a precondition to the lawful remittance of dividends and repatriation of capital. Funds should be isolated in a capital escrow sub-account to maintain an unbroken audit trail. For working-capital loans from the parent or third party, the routing uses external credit codes 3602/3603, and if the maturity exceeds one year, the credit must be registered as 'crédito externo' with the Central Bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail, but never a statutory substitute for a local bank account. Foreign EMIs (e.g., Stripe, PayPal, Payoneer, Wise, Airwallex) are not licensed credit institutions under Colombian law; they operate as foreign payment service providers and their accounts are classified as 'cuentas en el exterior' for regulatory purposes. They are viable for aggregating card-based e-commerce receipts from buyers in Latin America and for low-value supplier disbursements, but the SAS must (i) maintain a local authorized-intermediary account to channel regulated FX transactions, (ii) file Formulario No. 13 (reporte de cuentas en el exterior) with Banco de la República for any overseas account whose monthly balance exceeds USD 12,000, and (iii) incorporate the third-party settlement flows into the Declaración de Cambio as services or goods receipts. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Documentation is mandatory for taxpayers whose gross assets or gross income as of the prior fiscal year equals or exceeds 100,000 UVT (COP 4,706,500,000 for taxable year 2024). The multinational group's Master File and Country-by-Country report are required when consolidated group revenue exceeds COP 1,247,000 million (approx. USD 350 million) or the equivalent of EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Colombia does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) akin to the Gulf jurisdictions. However, subjective substance requirements are embedded in Article 868-1 ET (transactions without economic substance may be disregarded) and Article 869 ET (general anti-avoidance rule – GAAR). In practice, DIAN expects a Colombian S.A.S. to demonstrate a physical presence (office, warehouse, employees), real operational decision-making, and sufficient business functions related to e-commerce sales. If the entity is deemed to be a shell or captive intermediary, DIAN may recharacterise the transactions, deny deductions, and hold the directors jointly liable for the resulting tax deficiency.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Law 1819 of 2016 (Art. 33, Colombian Criminal Code), tax evasion is a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment of 48 to 108 months (4 to 9 years), fines equivalent to 200% of the evaded tax, and disqualification from contracting with the State for up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who knowingly participate in the evasion face criminal liability, civil restitution obligations, asset seizure, and a travel ban pending investigation. The corporate veil can be pierced under Article 372 of the Commercial Code, rendering directors jointly liable for the company's tax debts.
Shell Company (Lack of Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: DIAN may disregard the legal entity under Article 868-1 ET, subjecting the entire taxable income to immediate taxation. Penalties can reach up to 200% of the improperly avoided tax (Law 1943 of 2018, Art. 99). Alternatively, the company may be voided and liquidated as a fictitious entity.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or silently benefit from sham transactions may be jointly and severally liable for all tax, fines and interest; they can also be subjected to commercial disqualification and criminal prosecution for conspiracy to commit fraud.
Money Laundering / AML Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Under Law 1121 of 2006 and Article 323 of the Criminal Code (as amended by Law 1762 of 2015), money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of 6 to 15 years and fines of up to 50,000 S.M.M.L.V. (monthly minimum legal salary); administrative sanctions from UIAF can include monetary fines up to COP 1 billion and suspension of the company's commercial activities.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are criminally liable for negligent AML compliance. Personal assets can be seized, and directors may be subject to a travel ban and imprisonment; under the corporate veil, courts may impose direct liability for reparations.

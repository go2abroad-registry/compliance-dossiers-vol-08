Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) — official website: www.dian.gov.co; electronic services are provided through the DIAN Sede Electrónica (successor platform to the legacy muisca.dian.gov.co and catastro.dian.gov.co portals).

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent entity forms a Colombian S.A.S. (Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada) by public deed or private instrument recorded before the Cámara de Comercio in the company's registered office; the company's legal representative domiciled in Colombia completes the mandatory Pre-RUT (Registro Único Tributario) filing with DIAN and obtains the RUT and the statutory NIT (Número de Identificación Tributaria), which serves as the unified TIN and VAT registration identifier for income tax, sales tax, customs, and foreign-trade purposes. Foreign shareholders must submit apostilled incorporation documentation, a power of attorney appointing the Colombian legal representative, and evidence of foreign legal existence; no minimum paid-in capital is required for an S.A.S., though the representative must be a natural person resident in Colombia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or general pass-through election exists for an S.A.S.; the entity is mandatorily treated as a Colombian resident taxpayer for income tax and VAT. Available statutory elections relevant to cross-border structures include: (i) election to be classified as a Gran Contribuyente (large taxpayer) for centralised filing and refund handling where revenue/asset thresholds are met; (ii) election of the Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE) for eligible small taxpayers with gross ordinary income not exceeding 100,000 UVT, which combines stamp, income and municipal taxes, but is generally unattractive for exporters requiring periodic VAT refunds; (iii) application of Colombia's anti-hybrid and tax-consolidation rules under Law 2010 of 2019, which operate as mandatory anti-avoidance provisions rather than elective check-the-box regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no de minimis turnover registration threshold for a legal entity such as an S.A.S.: the S.A.S. must register as a VAT 'responsable del régimen común' from its first Colombian VAT-taxable transaction, including export sales and export services. Only natural persons may qualify for the simplified IVA regime, and the relevant annual simplified-regime threshold is 3,500 UVT (approximately COP 174,296,500 for tax year 2025, using the 2025 UVT of COP 49,799); an S.A.S. is statutorily ineligible for the simplified regime and must obtain the NIT/RUT through DIAN.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including shredded non-ferrous scrap and recycled copper wire) qualify as 'ventas exentas' at a 0% VAT rate under Article 481 of the Estatuto Tributario. The exporter must be a registered IVA 'responsable del régimen común', the goods must be physically shipped outside Colombian customs territory, the export must be evidenced by the electronic DEX and transport document, and the resulting 'saldo a favor' (excess input VAT) is refundable through DIAN upon documentary substantiation. Export of services is zero-rated only where the beneficiary is resident abroad, the service is entirely foreign-use, and the foreign beneficiary has no establishment or economic benefit within Colombia.

  • DIAN-approved electronic customs export declaration (DEX) filed through SIEX evidencing the consignment and the ISIC 4669 scrap commodity code
  • Original Bill of Lading (B/L) or air waybill proving physical departure of the shredded copper scrap from Colombia to the foreign consignee
  • Inward Foreign Currency Remittance Certificate ('certificado de cambio') issued by a Colombian authorized foreign-exchange intermediary, evidencing receipt of export proceeds within the mandatory repatriation window

Colombia operates a mandatory electronic invoicing and real-time clearance system. Every 'factura electrónica de venta' must be generated by DIAN-authorised billing software or an approved technology provider and submitted for pre-validation (validación previa) through DIAN's Sede Electrónica or API before delivery to the acquirer; the invoice must include the CUFE (Código Único de Facturación Electrónica), QR code, and electronic signature. Export sales require the electronic export declaration transmitted to DIAN via SIEX, the issuance of the electronic export invoice, and, for acquisitions from non-VAT-responsible persons, an electronic document supporting ('documento soporte').

DIAN and customs authorities rigorously audit export zero-rating and refund claims, verifying actual physical exit, customs declarations, FX repatriation, and the commercial substance of the foreign buyer. Fraudulent, fictitious, or non-exported consignments trigger retrospective VAT recovery with moratory interest and inflation adjustment, the 160% inaccurate-declaration penalty under Article 648 E.T., clawback of refunds through the 'devolución improcedente' procedure, criminal tax-fraud liability, customs smuggling penalties under Law 1762 of 2015, and enhanced anti-money-laundering exposure under applicable Colombian AML/CFT rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Colombian peso (COP) is convertible on current-account items without prior approval, while capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment (FDI), require mandatory channeling through an IMC and subsequent registration with Banco de la República using Form 4 (Declaración de Cambio por Inversión Internacional). Profit remittance is unrestricted for registered FDI only, subject to tax compliance, transfer-pricing rules, and annual or final dividend declarations; unregistered profit remittances are treated as capital ruch? The principal practical hurdles are (i) failure to register subsequent capital increases, (ii) routing through non-IMC accounts, (iii) source-of-funds rejection by the receiving bank, and (iv) withholding tax and 'no rebuttal' tax clearances from DIAN.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Colombian law requires exporters to 'reintegrate' 100% of gross export proceeds from goods, including shredded non-ferrous scrap, through an IMC in COP. The legal channeling window for goods exports is 4 months from the date of shipment when the payment is on a sight basis; if documentary credit or usance payment terms exceed 120 days, the exporter may use the negotiated payment term up to a maximum of 12 months, provided the underlying operation is evidenced by the DIAN Exportation (DEX) declaration and commercial documents. The exporter must also file the corresponding exchange declaration (Form 1) and obtain a 'certificado de canalización' to close the transaction; any un-chanellized export proceeds expose the exporter to penalties calculated under SFC and customs regulations.

Central bank supervision: Banco de la República (BdbR); foreign exchange regulation is legally exercised through Law 9 of 1991, Decree 1735 of 1993, External Resolution No. 1 of 2018, and Circular DCIN-83, administered exclusively through authorized Intermediarios del Mercado Cambiario (IMC) under the oversight of the Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia (SFC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low and, for Colombian regulatory purposes, legally non-viable as a primary clearing channel. Colombian exchange control requires every domestic or cross-border payment involving export proceeds, import disbursements, and FDI to pass through a locally licensed IMC. Offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise, Currencycloud, Mercury) are not authorized to submit Banco de la República exchange declarations, hold COP settlement accounts, or receive export proceeds under the Colombian foreign exchange regime. They may only be used as auxiliary treasury information tools; the actual flow must be cleared through a Colombian Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from the non-resident shareholder must be wired by SWIFT MT103 to the Colombian SAS's operational account at a Tier-1 IMC. The wire instruction field 70 must clearly state 'Foreign Direct Investment / subscription of shares in SAS Law 1258/2008' and refer to the SAS NIT and pending Banco de la República registration. No third-party or inter-company cash pooling should be used; the capital injection must originate from the shareholder's own corporate bank account. The receiving IMC will complete Form 4, register the FDI with Banco de la República, and issue a channelization certificate. The legal entry currency should be USD, EUR, or other convertible currency; the bank will convert into COP at the market exchange rate (TCRM) and credit the SAS account. The documentation bundle for the bank should include the FDI declaration, apostilled board or shareholder resolution approving the capital increase, legalized powers, company NIT certificate, and the bank's own source-of-funds questionnaire. A SWIFT error in purpose code or beneficiary NIT may trigger temporary freeze and SFC reporting.Trade settlements to foreign suppliers of recycled copper scrap must be executed via (a) documentary letters of credit issued under UCP600 by the local IMC using SWIFT MT700/MT707, or (b) open-account bank transfers using SWIFT MT103 with mandatory supporting documentation. For open-account trade, the SAS must provide the DIAN import declaration, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading/air waybill, and a copy of the supplier contract; the IMC files Form 2 (Declaración de Cambio por Importación de Bienes) to accompany the payment. For LC transactions, the payment is covered by the LC terms, and the SAS must execute the local exchange declaration within the LC settlement date. All outbound disbursements should carry the correct ISO currency code and the exchange declaration is mandatory even where the invoice is denominated in USD but paid in COP.

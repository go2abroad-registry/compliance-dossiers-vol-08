For a China WFOE engaged in ISIC 7810 staffing and EOR services, a virtual office or commercial mailbox is rejected. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taxation Administration (財政部賦稅署) and regional National Taxation Bureaus (國稅局); official online portals: https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw and https://www.mof.gov.tw

E-government filing portal

A foreign enterprise must first obtain approval from the Ministry of Economic Affairs under the Company Act for a branch or representative office; upon approval, the Ministry of Economic Affairs issues the Uniform Number (統一編號), which thereafter functions as the TIN. The enterprise must then file tax registration with the local National Taxation Bureau. For cross-border e-commerce or offshore services without a fixed place of business in Taiwan, registration for a tax ID and VAT collection may be completed through the Ministry of Finance's overseas e-commerce seller registration mechanism, with liability to appoint a domestic tax agent where required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No general check-the-box election exists. Taiwan applies entity-level taxation to corporate entities. A foreign enterprise with a fixed place of business in Taiwan is taxed as a branch; a foreign enterprise without a fixed place or business agent in Taiwan is subject only to withholding on Taiwan-source income. Taxable-period election for VAT may be monthly or bi-monthly depending on turnover and assessed status. Taiwan's Income Tax Act Articles 43-3 and 43-4 contain hybrid mismatch and anti-avoidance provisions, and Article 43-4 restricts interest deductions on back-to-back loans from foreign related parties; elections under these rules must be documented contemporaneously for transfer pricing and thin-capitalization purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NT$80,000 average monthly taxable sales for small-scale business exemption purposes; VAT registration is generally mandatory for businesses with fixed premises and monthly sales above NT$80,000, while foreign electronic services suppliers must register for VAT when generating Taiwan-source digital service revenue, regardless of physical presence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies when exported goods actually leave the customs territory under a valid export declaration and are sold by a VAT-registered Taiwan exporter. Cross-border services, including international transport and services rendered to foreign businesses and used wholly outside Taiwan, qualify as zero-rated export services. An employer-of-record or staffing agency may zero-rate cross-border payroll and administrative services to a foreign client if the place of use and customer affiliation are outside Taiwan; documentary evidence must demonstrate the foreign receiving enterprise and actual offshore use. Services consumed in Taiwan or benefiting a Taiwan-resident recipient remain taxable at 5%, with reverse-charge or withholding obligations for foreign-cross-border suppliers where applicable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口報單)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank foreign-exchange settlement certificate
  • Commercial invoice and full export sales contract
  • Electronic uniform invoice or zero-rated invoice evidence
  • Duty-paid, insurance, inspection, and customs packing lists where applicable
  • Third-party confirmation of offshore service use for service exports

Taiwan mandates the Uniform Invoice (統一發票) system. VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic uniform invoices and transmit them to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integration Platform (電子發票整合服務平台) via an approved electronic-invoice service provider or direct API/M2M transmission, within the statutory filing window after the tax period. Cloud-based e-invoices are digitally archived; there is no per-invoice physical stamping, but real-time electronic submission, encryption standards, and audit trail obligations are enforced. B2B and B2C transmission formats must conform to the Ministry of Finance e-invoice UBL/JSON specifications.

The National Taxation Bureau cross-matches customs data, port manifests, VAT returns, and inbound foreign-exchange receipts. Claiming zero-rating on goods or services not actually exported, or failing to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory periods, triggers reassessment of VAT at 5% plus interest and a surcharge under Business Tax Act Articles 51 and 52. Fraudulent zero-rating claims may be penalized up to five times the undercollected tax, and criminal liability for tax evasion under Articles 41 and 52 of the Business Tax Act can lead to imprisonment where fraudulent intent is established. Retrospective examination may extend up to seven years in cases involving deliberately misleading documentation or tax evasion.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China maintains current-account convertibility under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, meaning cross-border payments and receipts for trade in goods, services, and legitimate profit remittances are permitted upon submission of authentic underlying documentation and completion of bank-based verification. Capital-account transactions remain subject to a case-by-case registration/approval regime administered by SAFE; foreign direct investment into a WFOE, including a Taiwan-origin capital injection, requires FDI registration with SAFE or a designated bank, followed by the opening of a foreign-currency capital account. Profit repatriation from a CN_WFOE_LTD is routed through the current account only after the annual audit report, corporate income tax settlement, withholding tax clearance (where applicable), and bank review of the board resolution and audited financial statements have been completed; Taiwan-registered shareholders benefit from preferential tax treatment under the ECFA framework but must still comply with the same remittance documentary chain. Dividends remitted to Taiwan are generally permitted once the relevant tax payment certificates are filed with the remitting bank, yet the bank remains responsible for reputational and source-of-funds diligence. In practice, the convertibility bottleneck is documentary integrity rather than exchange-control denial for genuine EOR payroll and service-fee transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The compulsory surrender of export proceeds has been abolished; there is currently no statutory mandatory conversion ratio for export or service income received by a WFOE. Instead, SAFE requires that goods-trade export proceeds be repatriated into mainland China and banked into the exporter's export-earnings verification account or a current-account FX account; the proceeds may remain in FX unless the enterprise voluntarily elects settlement into RMB. For goods exports, the enterprise must receive the payment within 90 days from the customs declaration date unless a deferred-receipt registration is lodged with SAFE through the goods-trade monitoring system; prepayments received more than 30 days prior to export require advance-receipt reporting. All service-trade receipts generated by the Global EOR and cross-border payroll business must be reported to the bank under the service-trade income and expense declaration code corresponding to ISIC 7810, and must be supported by the underlying EOR service agreement, beneficiary confirmation, and PBOC/SAFE-compliant payment instruction, but no minimum retention or forced conversion timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC); the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) is the delegated foreign-exchange supervisory authority operating under the PBoC and is the sole regulator for cross-border capital flows, FX registration, and the licensing of FX business conducted by banks in mainland China.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for the core treasury function. A global EMI such as Airwallex, Wise, or Currenxie is fully viable for auxiliary cross-border disbursement initiation, but each is classified as a non-bank payment institution rather than a licensed mainland-China depository institution. Such platforms cannot host the WFOE's statutory capital account, cannot perform SAFE FDI registrations, and cannot execute legal-current RMB payroll disbursements that require direct-deposit rails into Chinese bank cards. The WFOE may legally use these fintech platforms only as an instruction front-end; the underlying clearing must settle through a Chinese-licensed partner bank within China. Banks will generally require that the fintech account be identified as a regular sanctioned payment aggregator and that the WFOE's name on the fintech account be identical to its Chinese business license name; otherwise, the account can be rejected at bank onboarding.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire-transfer protocol for Taiwan-investor capital injection: (1) the Taiwan shareholder remits via SWIFT MT103 to the mainland WFOE's FDI capital account, with the referencing field carrying 'INVESTMENT CAPITAL WFOE [Legal Name]' and purpose code '712030 – direct investment foreign exchange settlement'; (2) after receipt by the mainland bank, the FDI registration in SAFE's Capital Account Information System must be updated and the capital transaction reported as 'balance of payments direct investment inflow'; (3) the bank then settles the foreign currency into RMB into the WFOE's RMB registered-capital account, or holds it as FX at the customer's direction; (4) if capital is injected in RMB via cross-border RMB settlement, the remitter must use the PBoC RCPMIS system and open a cross-border RMB capital account. All inbound remittances must match the nominal shareholding percentage stated in the latest FDI registration; any change in equity proportion triggers an updated FDI amendment registration within 30 days.For the Global EOR payroll and contractor settlement model, outbound flows are classified into (a) domestic payroll — executed via the China National Advanced Payment System (CNAPS) using direct debit/credit transfers on the same-day by the bank, and (b) cross-border contractor/vendor payments — completed through SWIFT MT103 in USD/EUR or through CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System) for RMB-denominated payments, using the applicable service-trade purpose code for EOR-related fees. Commercial letters of credit are not typical for payroll processing and should be restricted to cargo-based back-to-back trade finance. The fintech gateway Wise may be engaged as a customer-facing disbursement accelerator, but the underlying settlement currency must be debited from the WFOE's Bank of China or China Merchants Bank account and must pass through a China-licensed counterparty bank; Wise will not be recognized as a legal clearing rail by SAFE. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% corporate income tax in Taiwan; a 5% additional tax applies to current-year earnings not distributed within the statutory period, subject to applicable exemptions and exclusions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a non-resident shareholder, subject to applicable tax treaty relief, approved special rules, and the shareholder's legal status. Dividends paid to a Taiwan-resident individual are generally subject to Taiwan's separate resident taxation mechanism rather than the non-resident withholding rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, filing and payment are generally due from May 1 through May 31 of the following year. A taxpayer using a non-calendar fiscal year generally files within the fifth month after fiscal year-end. Extension rules are limited and must be confirmed for the relevant year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally filed and paid during September for the first six months of a calendar year, subject to statutory exceptions, prior approval, and the taxpayer's filing method.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally filed and paid by the 15th day of the month following each two-month VAT period. The standard VAT rate is 5%, subject to zero-rating, exemption, and special rules.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Tax withheld during a month is generally paid and reported by the 10th day of the following month. Annual withholding statements and certificates are generally issued and filed by the applicable statutory deadline, commonly by January 31 for the preceding calendar year, subject to category-specific rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan generally requires a Local File when annual net operating revenue and non-operating income reach NT$300 million or when the aggregate annual amount of specified controlled transactions reaches NT$200 million, subject to prescribed exemptions. A Master File is generally required for an enterprise meeting the prescribed large-enterprise threshold, commonly annual revenue of at least NT$3 billion together with relevant cross-border controlled transactions. Country-by-Country Reporting generally applies to an ultimate parent or designated surrogate parent in Taiwan where consolidated group revenue reaches NT$27 billion and the statutory conditions are met. Thresholds and exemptions must be checked for the relevant fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a single, standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation regime applicable to all entities. Substance is enforced through transfer-pricing rules, controlled-foreign-company rules, permanent-establishment principles, general anti-avoidance provisions, beneficial-ownership analysis, tax-source rules, business-tax rules, and anti-abuse doctrines. A Taiwan EOR company should maintain real decision-making, appropriately skilled personnel, payroll and operational capability, customer and vendor control, commercial risk assumption, local records, and a defensible delineation between principal, agent, payroll processor, staffing provider, and technology platform. A foreign company cannot avoid Taiwan taxation merely by contracting offshore if its people or dependent agents habitually conclude contracts or perform core services in Taiwan.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unauthorized Mainland China investment or prohibited ownership

Statutory sanction: Possible rejection or revocation of investment approval, forced disposal, confiscation or forfeiture of prohibited investment gains, administrative fines, restrictions on business operations, and criminal exposure under applicable cross-Strait investment laws. The amount depends on the statute and conduct.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors, managers, nominees, and beneficial owners may face administrative and criminal liability. Civil restitution and disgorgement may apply. Travel restrictions may be imposed in criminal or enforcement proceedings where legally authorized; imprisonment is fact-dependent and not automatic. Limited liability does not protect persons who personally participate in unlawful conduct.
Unlicensed labor dispatch, staffing, or recruitment

Statutory sanction: Orders to cease, administrative fines, license denial or revocation, labor-law penalties, back-pay and benefit assessments, and potential criminal exposure for serious or repeated violations. Penalty amounts depend on the violated Labor Standards Act, Employment Service Act, or other applicable statute.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible managers may be fined or prosecuted where they ordered, approved, or knowingly tolerated the conduct. The company may remain liable for wages, social insurance, labor pension, tax, and damages. Imprisonment is possible for specified offenses but is not an automatic consequence.
Corporate income tax evasion and false returns

Statutory sanction: Supplemental tax, interest, tax-loss denial, administrative penalties, audit adjustments, and criminal prosecution for deliberate tax evasion or fraudulent reporting. Criminal sanctions may include imprisonment and criminal fines under the Income Tax Act and related laws; the precise range depends on the offense and amount involved.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face criminal liability, civil restitution of unpaid tax and penalties, asset attachment, and restrictions on leaving Taiwan where authorized by procedural law. The corporate veil is not automatically pierced, but fraud, personal participation, commingling, and abuse of the company may support direct liability.
VAT fraud and fictitious invoicing

Statutory sanction: Input VAT denial, supplemental VAT, fines, invoice restrictions, business suspension, and criminal sanctions for fraudulent tax evasion or false invoicing. Criminal exposure may include imprisonment and fines depending on the transaction value and intent.

Director personal liability: Directors, accounting personnel, and beneficial owners who knowingly participate may incur criminal and civil liability. Restitution and asset preservation are possible. Travel restrictions and detention depend on the criminal process and evidence.
Withholding tax failure

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed the unwithheld tax, interest, penalties, and administrative fines. Deliberate concealment or falsified withholding certificates can create criminal tax exposure.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers and withholding agents may be personally liable under applicable tax provisions where they intentionally or negligently fail to perform statutory duties. Civil recovery and enforcement measures may follow; imprisonment is reserved for qualifying criminal conduct.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and undocumented related-party payments

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustment to arm's-length income, denial of deductions, interest, penalties, withholding tax reassessment, VAT consequences, and potential CFC or anti-avoidance adjustments. Failure to provide required documentation may trigger statutory penalties and adverse estimation.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil claims for breach of duty and, where deliberate falsification or evasion is proven, criminal liability. Ordinary transfer-pricing adjustment does not by itself establish criminal conduct or pierce the corporate veil.
Permanent establishment and source concealment

Statutory sanction: Retroactive Taiwan income tax, business tax, withholding tax, interest, penalties, payroll and labor assessments, and possible registration or licensing orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers may face civil and, for intentional concealment, criminal exposure. Personal travel restrictions are possible only under applicable enforcement or criminal procedures; they are not a routine tax consequence.
AML, KYC, sanctions, and suspicious payment activity

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, enhanced monitoring, account freezing, transaction rejection, license or registration consequences, confiscation, and criminal prosecution under Taiwan AML and financial laws. Penalties vary materially by offense and amount.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners may face criminal fines, imprisonment, confiscation, civil restitution, asset freezing, and legally authorized travel restrictions. Limited liability does not protect knowing participants or facilitators.
Payroll tax, labor insurance, and pension non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Back taxes, insurance and pension contributions, surcharges, labor-law fines, employee compensation, interest, and possible suspension or licensing consequences.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may incur fines and civil liability for unpaid wages, contributions, and damages. Criminal liability can arise for intentional withholding, fraud, or repeated unlawful conduct.
Beneficial ownership concealment and corporate veil abuse

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the arrangement, tax reassessment, confiscation, fines, dissolution or liquidation measures, transaction invalidation, creditor remedies, and criminal prosecution for fraud, forgery, money laundering, or tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Courts may impose direct civil liability where the company is abused, undercapitalized, commingled, or used for fraud. Directors and controllers may face restitution, asset attachment, criminal fines, imprisonment, and legally authorized travel restrictions.

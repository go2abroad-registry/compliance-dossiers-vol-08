Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no general statutory minimum paid-up capital for a WFOE limited liability company incorporated under the Taiwan Company Act; the capital amount is freely determined by shareholders and must be fully subscribed and paid in cash. The Ministry of Transportation and Communications may impose financial fitness and liability insurance conditions for the dangerous goods freight forwarding license, but no blocked escrow account is required for incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification in the form of a Fire Safety Equipment Certificate issued by the local Fire Bureau; building structural load inspection and dangerous goods warehouse approval by the municipal Public Works Department; occupational hazard prevention and workplace clearance under the Occupational Safety and Health Act administered by the Ministry of Labor, covering gas detection, ventilation, spill containment, personal protective equipment, and hazardous materials driver or handler training.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, National Taxation Bureau (Taiwan); online electronic portal: Ministry of Finance Electronic Tax Filing and Payment Service Network (tax.nat.gov.tw).

E-government filing portal

1) Determine whether the foreign non-resident entity has a fixed place of business in Taiwan. 2) For a Taiwan branch or fixed place of business, first apply to the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) for branch/company registration under the Company Act and obtain the MOEA approval certificate. 3) After MOEA registration, file tax/business registration (Shui-ji deng-ji) with the competent regional National Taxation Bureau submitting (i) the MOEA registration certificate, (ii) the foreign parent entity's incorporation certificate and board resolution legally authenticated by an ROC overseas mission, (iii) the appointed Taiwan branch manager's identification documentation, and (iv) proof of the Taiwan business address (lease or ownership). 4) The National Taxation Bureau will issue a Unified Business Number (UBN), which is the statutory TIN/VAT ID used for profit-seeking enterprise income tax, business tax, uniform invoice issuance, customs importer/exporter code, and withholding registration. 5) A foreign non-resident entity without a Taiwan branch but with Taiwan-source business or freight-related income must have a Taiwan withholding agent registered with the National Taxation Bureau and obtain a withholding unit UBN/tax file number, while the foreign entity's overseas corporate tax identifier is cross-referenced in tax filings. 6) For hazardous materials forwarding under ISIC 5229, the entity must also obtain the specialized dangerous-goods logistics license and safety approval from the transport and customs regulators; tax registration is completed after or concurrently with business licensing, and the UBN is then activated for customs declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None in the form of check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. Taiwan does not provide a check-the-box classification regime. A qualifying wholly-owned Taiwan subsidiary may elect consolidated profit-seeking enterprise income tax filing with Ministry of Finance approval under the consolidated filing rules for corporate groups, subject to the statutory ownership and holding-period conditions. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed by statutory disallowance or administrative anti-hybrid rules, not by taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold: every profit-seeking enterprise selling goods or services in Taiwan must apply for business tax registration before commencing taxable activity. Small-scale enterprises using the simplified 1-percent mechanism still require tax registration. For foreign cross-border electronic services providers only, a NT$480,000 annual Taiwan-source turnover threshold applies; specialized dangerous-goods freight forwarding services are physically performed and therefore are not eligible for the cross-border e-services threshold, making registration mandatory from the first Taiwan business transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Value-Added and Non-Value-Added Business Tax Act, zero-rating applies to (1) goods that are sold and actually exported from Taiwan, evidenced by an approved customs export declaration; (2) zero-rated services, including international freight forwarding and DG/hazmat forwarding services, supplied by a Taiwan VAT-registered enterprise to foreign carriers, shippers, consignees, or other foreign enterprises where the service is performed in connection with exported goods or is not otherwise used or consumed in Taiwan; and (3) receipts in foreign exchange or settlement by letter of credit for such export freight services. The taxable enterprise reports zero-rated turnover in the periodic business tax return and may claim a refund of associated input VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the Taiwan port/airport as the shipment origin
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Earnings Statement from an authorized Taiwan bank
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list for the export consignment
  • Dangerous Goods Declaration / Multimodal Dangerous Goods Form and IMDG/IATA DG certification for the hazardous consignment
  • Export service contract, booking note, or letter of credit evidencing the foreign service recipient and the service consideration

Taiwan mandates the use of the Ministry of Finance uniform invoice (unified invoice) system. VAT-registered enterprises, other than small-scale retailers, must issue uniform invoices for each taxable sale and file business tax returns every two months. Electronic uniform invoices, when used, must be transmitted to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Platform through the approved e-Invoice integration and API channel; B2C e-invoices must be uploaded within 48 hours of issuance, and B2B e-invoices by the statutory deadline under the E-Invoice Implementation Guidelines. Taiwan does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping for ordinary invoices. Import and export declarations are separately cleared through the Customs Administration using the trade network (TPNet) electronic portal, and the UBN serves as the customs importer/exporter code.

The National Taxation Bureau and Customs Administration cross-match customs export declarations, bills of lading, cargo records, and bank inward remittance certificates. Zero-rated treatment is provisional until all documentation is produced. If an export zero-rating is disallowed, the taxpayer is assessed the standard 5% VAT on the previously zero-rated turnover plus interest, and the input VAT refund may be clawed back. Under Articles 51 and 52 of the Business Tax Act, fraudulent failure to substantiate zero-rating, false invoicing, or forged export and foreign-exchange documentation triggers penalties of up to five times the tax evaded, possible criminal prosecution under Taiwan's tax and customs statutes, and an extended retroactive assessment period of seven years under the Tax Collection Act for concealment or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a split current-account and capital-account FX control regime. Under the Regulations on the Foreign Exchange Administration of the People's Republic of China and SAFE implementing rules, current-account items such as trade settlements, service revenue, freight-forwarding income, interest and lawful dividends are generally convertible and available for prompt payment through licensed designated banks, provided the underlying transaction documents are consistent and anti-money laundering screening is passed. Capital-account items, however, are not freely convertible: foreign direct investment, equity subscription, cross-border loans, loan principal and capital repatriation require prior SAFE registration, approval or reporting. A Taiwan-funded WFOE must first obtain a SAFE foreign investment registration and open a dedicated capital account; the Taiwan investor's equity remittance is classified as inbound direct investment and must be settled into RMB only within the approved business scope. Profit remittance hurdles include the completion of annual statutory audits, corporate income tax clearance, verification of prior-year losses, and a current SAFE registration; banks will require a lawful dividend resolution and will reject dividends that would impair registered capital. For current-account receipts, there is no mandatory fixed surrender ratio or central-bank conversion requirement; export proceeds must be repatriated into China but may be held as foreign currency in an onshore settlement account or converted to RMB at market rates. Practical friction includes strict scrutiny of dangerous-goods invoices, Taiwanese beneficial ownership and any mismatch between customs declarations and actual freight revenue.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, all foreign exchange proceeds must be repatriated into the PRC banking system within 90 days from the date of export customs clearance; there is no statutory fixed mandatory conversion ratio. 100% of the proceeds may be retained in the exporter's designated foreign exchange settlement account or voluntarily sold to a licensed bank for RMB at the prevailing spot rate. For cross-border DG freight-forwarding service income, the WFOE must use its current-account foreign exchange settlement account and must match all receipts with issued invoices, transport contracts, booking confirmations, dangerous goods declarations and auditable books of account. The bank will report each transaction to SAFE through the online foreign exchange reporting system; unexplained excess receipts are subject to suspicious transaction reporting.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: LOW, legally barred as a primary rail. PRC law does not recognize foreign electronic-money institutions, offshore EMI wallets, or digital-only banks as permissible settlement channels for a mainland-incorporated WFOE. Such EMIs cannot hold a corporate bank account in the WFOE's name for SAFE-regulated capital flows, cannot issue Chinese account or routing identifiers, cannot access CNAPS (China National Advanced Payment System), and do not satisfy the SAFE requirement that every cross-border receipt and payment be settled through a licensed domestic bank. An EMI may be used only in an ancillary, non-clearing advisory/ledger capacity; the actual regulated funds must always move through an onshore tier-1 bank and be individually reported to SAFE. Any structure that attempts to replace the onshore bank with a Hong Kong, Singapore, or EU EMI for the WFOE's principal treasury flows would be non-compliant and expose the entity to FX penalties and de-banking risk.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (CIT) for PRC resident enterprises; non-resident enterprises without a permanent establishment are taxed at 10% withholding on China-source passive income unless the Cross-Strait Double Taxation Arrangement grants a lower rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding on dividends paid by a PRC resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise; under the Cross-Strait Double Taxation Arrangement (if in force and fully implemented), the rate is reduced to 5% where the Taiwan beneficial owner holds directly at least 25% of the paid-in capital of the China WFOE.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly prepayment returns are due within 15 days after the end of each quarter; the annual return and final tax payment are due within five months after the end of the fiscal year (for a calendar-year WFOE, no later than May 31 of the following year).

Deadline: · Frequency:

The WFOE as withholding agent must file the withholding return and pay the tax to the State Treasury within 7 days from the date of payment, or from the date the amount becomes payable if not paid.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is required when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible assets, RMB 100 million for intangible assets, RMB 100 million for financial assets, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions; a Master File is triggered at substantially higher group-level aggregate thresholds (generally RMB 1 billion aggregate related-party transactions or the group's consolidated revenue benchmark with related-party volume per SAT Notice 42/2016).

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not apply a Caribbean-style ESR code, but the tax authorities enforce an equivalent substantive substance doctrine through the Enterprise Income Tax Law GAAR, the beneficial-ownership rule, and SAT's special tax-adjustment measures. For the DG hazmat forwarding WFOE, Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in China: statutory hazmat transport licenses, operational employees, physical warehouses, safety and emergency-response systems, risk-bearing contracting, and strategic decision-making. If the Taiwan parent merely forwards contracts, licenses, or capital without performing insurance, financing, or risk-management functions, China may recharacterize the profit entitlement and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion by false invoicing and under-declared DG revenue

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law mandates recovery of the underpaid tax plus a financial penalty of 50%-500% of the evaded amount; fraudulent issuance or use of VAT invoices also triggers Criminal Law Articles 205-206 with heavy monetary fines and confiscation; serious cases are referred for criminal investigation under Criminal Law Article 201 on tax evasion.

Director personal liability: A director who orders, signs, or knowingly tolerates false invoicing/under-declaration may have the corporate veil pierced; the court can impose personal civil restitution of diverted profits, freeze personal bank accounts, impose a tax-related travel ban and entry/exit restriction, list the director on the national serious-untrustworthy-taxpayer blacklist, and impose imprisonment of up to 7 years (or a life term where fraudulent VAT invoices cause massive state revenue loss).
Unremitted non-resident withholding tax on Taiwan profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: Under Article 40 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and Article 69 of the Tax Administration Law, the tax authority may order the WFOE to collect the unpaid withholding tax and impose a penalty of 50%-300% of the amount that was not withheld, together with a 0.05% daily overdue surcharge; the Taiwan recipient remains liable for the underlying China-source tax.

Director personal liability: If the chief financial officer or director knowingly authorized the distribution without withholding, he or she may be fined personally, subject to a travel ban and exit restriction, denied clean tax clearance for cross-border relocation, and held jointly and severally liable when the corporate assets are insufficient; intentional failure to collect tax can escalate to criminal accountability for tax evasion by the responsible person.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and empty-shell DG forwarding structure

Statutory sanction: Articles 41-48 and 114 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Announcement 2017 No. 6 permit the tax authority to re-determine taxable income by the arm's-length principle, impose interest and late-payment surcharges on the adjusted underpayment, apply the 2:1 thin-capitalization rule to deny excessive debt interest, and levy penalties for failure to provide contemporaneous documentation; misuse of treaty benefits triggers GAAR re-characterization and denial of the 5%/7% rates.

Director personal liability: Directors who architect artificial cross-strait pricing or drain profits from the China logistics operation face personal civil liability when the corporate veil is disregarded for abuse of the WFOE form; tax blacklisting restricts business travel, banking, and exit; under cross-strait administrative cooperation, Taiwan's tax authority may share bank and corporate records; criminal prosecution for intentional tax evasion can lead to imprisonment of up to 7 years for the responsible director.

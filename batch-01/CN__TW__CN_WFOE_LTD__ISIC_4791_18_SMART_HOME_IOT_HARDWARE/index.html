Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), administered nationally through the China Electronic Tax Bureau online portal (Electronic Taxpayer/e-Tax system) at etax.chinatax.gov.cn and provincial-level tax service offices.

        
E-government filing portal
Upon incorporation, the WFOE obtains an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the USCC is the statutory taxpayer identification number for Chinese tax purposes. The SAMR shares the incorporation data with the STA under the multi-license integration mechanism, and the WFOE must then activate its tax account through the Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 calendar days after the business license issuance, submitting the business license, articles of association, registered address, legal representative identity documents, capital contribution records, and finance and accounting system details. The tax authority activates the VAT taxpayer profile, determines small-scale or general VAT taxpayer status, assigns invoice quotas, and links payroll, social insurance, stamp duty, and other filing modules. A foreign non-resident entity without a permanent establishment in China that derives China-source income or makes taxable supplies in China must separately obtain a non-resident taxpayer TIN by completing the non-resident enterprise registration or withholding-agent registration with the competent STA office through the Electronic Tax Bureau, supported by the home-country certificate of incorporation, the China-source contract or invoice, and the appointed withholding agent's tax registration details. The WFOE itself uses its own USCC as its TIN and does not rely on the foreign parent's TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation - the WFOE is a Chinese tax-resident limited liability enterprise subject to Enterprise Income Tax at entity level on worldwide income; no pass-through or transparent classification is available.

        
Available election forms
None - China does not maintain a check-the-box entity classification election, and no domestic hybrid mismatch election exists for WFOEs. The WFOE may elect general VAT taxpayer treatment if it meets the statutory conditions, but this is an indirect-tax status election, not an entity classification. Anti-avoidance rules and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule may deny any purported mismatch benefit.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CNY 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales - this threshold triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration and access to input VAT credit. The WFOE must register for tax from establishment regardless of turnover; there is no economic registration threshold for a resident company, while smaller taxable persons below the threshold may be small-scale taxpayers subject to the simplified levy rate.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
A WFOE that is a VAT general taxpayer may apply the zero percent export VAT rate (exemption and refund) to tangible smart-home IoT hardware that physically leaves mainland China customs territory, including exports to Taiwan, provided the sale is a true cross-border supply, title passes to the overseas buyer, the goods are declared to customs as exports, and the export proceeds are received and repatriated in accordance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange rules. Qualifying digital offshore services are separately subject to cross-border service zero-rating; IoT hardware retail is not a service. The export must be supported by matching shipping, customs, commercial, and payment documentation.

China mandates issuance of VAT invoices through the STA-controlled electronic invoice service platform under the fully digitalised electronic invoice reform. Every valid invoice receives a unique tax-authority invoice number and a digital fiscal stamp, and invoice data is transmitted in real time to the STA's Golden-Tax information system. Special VAT invoices and ordinary invoices must be issued using the approved electronic invoice modules or API integrations; the tax authority validates invoice issuance for anti-fraud purposes, reconciles sales information, and links customs export data through the China International Trade Single Window. Paper-only or off-platform invoicing is not a lawful clearance method for VAT purposes.

      
If the export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day statutory window, or if the customs, transport, commercial, or foreign-exchange documents fail to substantiate the physical export, the STA reclassifies the sale as a domestic taxable supply, charges VAT at the applicable standard rate (normally 13%), assesses late-payment surcharge, and recovers any previously refunded input VAT. For fraudulent or unverified export refund claims, the STA may impose a fine of 50 percent to five times the tax refunded or evaded under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection and Levy, and confirmed export-refund fraud may be referred for criminal prosecution for tax fraud; the STA and customs also use export-tax-rebate risk classification and stop refunds for high-risk enterprises until documentation, logistics, and foreign-exchange transactions are fully verified.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Mainland China operates a carefully administered partial-convertibility system. The RMB is fully convertible on the current account for genuine trade and service transactions, subject to authenticity verification through SAFE's Trade Monitoring System and the banking system's electronic records. A mainland WFOE may hold a basic CNY settlement account and a foreign currency current account, convert export proceeds into RMB, and remit profits, royalties, salaries, and service fees upon submission of audited financial statements, tax proof, and underlying contracts. Capital account transactions, however, remain restricted and require SAFE registration or approval: inbound equity investment funds must enter a designated capital account, be verified by a certified public accountant, and be used only within the WFOE's approved business scope; foreign debt must be registered and may be constrained by macro-prudential risk parameters. Profit remittance is permissible but demands a cumulative tax clearance certificate and audited accounts. There is no system-wide surrender obligation or fixed conversion ratio for export proceeds; rather, the bank verifies each conversion against customs and logistics data. The overall effect is a 'managed convertibility' framework that supports trade but controls speculative capital flows.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to the WFOE's foreign currency current account in China and reported through SAFE's Trade Monitoring System generally within 180 days from the Customs export declaration date; high-integrity enterprises can benefit from extended facilitation windows. There is no mandatory percentage surrender; the WFOE may retain the foreign currency or sell it to a domestic bank for RMB, but each conversion must be supported by export customs declarations, invoices, logistics documents, and a recognized SAFE transaction purpose code.

      

      
Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the Foreign Exchange Administration authority operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Taiwanese parent remits USD or TWD via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated foreign currency capital account at a Chinese licensed bank. The WFOE should therefore treat these tools as sales channel utilities only, not as the licensed corporate treasury and FX rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: TWD 300 million annual operating revenue threshold; or controlled transactions with related parties reaching TWD 200 million for tangible property transfers; or TWD 50 million for intangible property transfers, service transactions, or capital use/financing transactions. Companies meeting any threshold must prepare a Master File and Local File (TP Report) in Chinese, and file an annual transfer pricing report with the tax return.

      
Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute of the type seen in commonwealth jurisdictions, but its General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act Article 43-1) and the Supreme Administrative Court doctrine of ‘substantive taxation’ allow the tax authority to re-characterise transactions lacking real economic substance. For treaty relief, the Taiwan tax authority will apply the ‘main purpose test’ under the Multilateral Instrument (where applicable) and will deny benefits if the entity does not perform substantial business activities in the residence state. For intercompany transactions to be respected, the Taiwan entity must show real staff, premises, decision-making, and risk-taking functions (CIGA) commensurate with the income it reports.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion – False Revenue Reporting
        
Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 110 – additional tax penalty of up to twice the understated tax amount; Tax Collection Act Article 41 – imprisonment for up to 5 years, detention, or a fine of up to NT$3,000,000 (if fraud is proven).

        
Director personal liability: Corporate veil can be pierced when the director uses the company as a mere facade. Directors causing fraudulent underreporting are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes, penalties, and interest to the extent of the amount recovered. The Innovation Court may impose civil restitution orders, travel bans (entry control under Article 24 of the Tax Collection Act), and revocation of departure permits. Criminal conviction carries a prison term of 6 months to 5 years, plus personal fines and confiscation of illicit proceeds.

      

      
      

        Withholding Tax Evasion on Non-Resident Payments
        
Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 114 – withholding agent must pay the unwithheld tax plus a penalty of 3 times the amount. Tax Collection Act Article 42 – criminal liability of up to 3 years imprisonment, detention, or a fine of NT$60,000 or more. For serious fraud, the court may order asset freeze.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who act as withholding agents are personally criminally liable if they knowingly omit withholding or remittance. Individual assets can be attached to cover the shortfall. A blacklisting order restricts the director from leaving Taiwan. In egregious cases, imprisonment is mandatory, and the director also faces disqualification from serving as a directorship in any company for up to 10 years.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Documentation and Substance Failure
        
Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 43-1 and Article 110-1 – re-assessed taxable income at arm's length, resulting in additional tax plus a penalty of 1–2 times the underpaid tax. Under the TP Audit Rules, penalties for non-disclosure of controlled transactions range from NT$3,000 up to NT$30,000 per omission, with further ad hoc adjustments.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow fictitious transactions can be sued for breach of fiduciary duty; the tax authority may apply the anti-abuse rule to pierce the corporate veil. Directors' personal assets are subject to restitution claims for unpaid corporate taxes. Blacklisting prevents travel until the tax is satisfied. In cases of egregious false documentation, directors face criminal fraud charges under the Criminal Code (Article 210), carrying up to 7 years imprisonment.

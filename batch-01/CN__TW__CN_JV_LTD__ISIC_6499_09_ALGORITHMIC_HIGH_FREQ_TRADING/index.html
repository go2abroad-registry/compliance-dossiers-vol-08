Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, R.O.C. (Taiwan), acting through the National Taxation Administration and its regional National Tax Administrations (for a Taipei-based JV, the Taipei National Tax Administration applies); primary taxpayer portal is the Ministry of Finance Tax Web (税務入口網, www.etax.nat.gov.tw), with the E-Invoice Integration Service Platform (www.einvoice.nat.gov.tw) for invoice clearance. Company law registration is administered separately by the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) through the primary registration portal (公司及商業登記一站式申請作業).

E-government filing portal

A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture incorporated in Taiwan as a limited company or company limited by shares first obtains incorporation and foreign-investment approval from MOEA, after which an 8-digit Unified Business Number (UBN) is automatically allocated through the integrated company registration workflow; the UBN serves as the single statutory tax ID for corporate income tax, business tax (VAT), withholding tax, and customs. The company must then file the standard profit-seeking enterprise registration application (營利事業設立登記) and the VAT registration application (營業人設立登記) with its Regional National Tax Administration, and register as a tax withholding agent. Foreign enterprises without a fixed place of business in Taiwan obtain a separate withholding tax ID (扣繳統一編號) through a tax agent, while foreign electronic service providers meeting the NTD 480,000 annual threshold register under the simplified VAT scheme for foreign e-service providers on the MOF E-Service platform.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the JV, being a juridical person under the Taiwan Company Act, is a resident enterprise subject to 20% corporate income tax on worldwide income plus a 5% retained-earnings surcharge on undistributed earnings; branches of foreign enterprises are taxable only on Taiwan-source income under territorial sourcing rules; no pass-through election exists for corporate shareholders, whereas sole proprietorships and partnerships are fiscally transparent and pass profits to owners; the entity is simultaneously registered as a VAT business entity at the 5% standard business tax rate.

Available election forms

Taiwan does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch neutrality election regime. Available statutory elections include: (1) consolidated corporate income tax filing election available to a domestic parent holding 100% of the shares of domestic subsidiaries, subject to MOF conditions; (2) voluntary standard VAT registration for businesses below the NTD 200,000 monthly turnover threshold; (3) a one-off simplified VAT registration election for foreign electronic service providers; (4) Free Trade Port Zone and export-processing-zone status elections carrying zero-rating or exemption treatments; (5) place-of-effective-management (PEM) rebuttal rules under Article 43-4 of the Income Tax Act that may recharacterize an offshore company managed and controlled in Taiwan; and (6) CFC attribution rules effective from 2023 with specified de-minimis exemptions and controlled-company elections. Thin capitalization and interest limitation rules under Article 24-3 operate irrespective of entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NTD 200,000 in average monthly taxable sales for a Taiwan-established business, below which the JV may either register voluntarily as a standard VAT taxpayer or be taxed as a small-scale operator at a reduced 1% rate; foreign electronic service providers supplying eservices or cloud services to Taiwan persons become compulsorily registrable where annual consideration exceeds NTD 480,000. Proprietary high-frequency trading profits obtained by a securities or futures trader are not within the business tax base and instead attract the Securities Transaction Tax at 0.3% (temporarily 0.15% for ETFs or other elective rates) or the Futures Transaction Tax at statutory contract-specific rates.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% business tax applies to: (1) export of goods with an accepted customs export declaration; (2) services rendered in Taiwan but used or consumed outside Taiwan, and services in connection with international trade, provided the consideration is received in foreign currency and documented by a foreign-exchange settlement certificate; (3) international transport and transport-related services; (4) sales of goods to foreign vessels or aircraft; and (5) sales within Free Trade Port Zones to foreign or non-customs-territory customers. For the JV, only separately contracted cross-border algorithmic trading, research, or consultancy services consumed offshore by non-Taiwanese clients may qualify; the JV's own proprietary trading and market-making profits fall outside the business tax scope and therefore generate no input VAT credit entitlement, requiring careful segregation of service revenue from trading principal gains.

  • Customs Export Declaration for goods (出口報單, Form N5203/N5204) or, for service exports, a signed cross-border service agreement identifying the offshore recipient and foreign-currency consideration
  • Commercial invoice issued by the JV stating the 0% VAT rate and referencing the export or cross-border service transaction
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods from Taiwan customs territory
  • Foreign exchange settlement book (結匯水單) or bank inward remittance certificate evidencing crediting of foreign currency proceeds to a Taiwan or offshore account of the JV
  • Zero-rated E-invoice and packing list as applicable
  • Supporting operational records for algorithmic services (trade logs, order-routing reports, service reports) demonstrating offshore consumption for any zero-rated service claim

All standard VAT taxpayers must issue Uniform Invoices; electronic invoices (E-invoices) are the default statutory instrument and must be transmitted to the MOF E-Invoice Integration Service Platform through API, XML, or JSON machine-to-machine integration. Clearance rules are time-boxed rather than real-time: B2B E-invoices must be uploaded within 48 hours of issuance and B2C cloud invoices within 48 hours, after which the platform attributes a unique invoice number and random check code; no Latin-American-style pre-approval stamping exists, but the 48-hour upload duty is enforced through bi-monthly Form 401 returns and system-based cross-matching audits. Issuance outside these windows is treated as late filing subject to penalties and loss of input-credit substantiation rights.

Export zero-rating is an audit priority. Where documentation is incomplete or the export or offshore service is fictitious, the tax authority reassesses the transaction at 5% VAT, adds interest, and under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act imposes a penalty up to five times the evaded tax; Article 52 additionally imposes up to five years imprisonment, detention, or fines for fraudulent evasion, and retroactive clawbacks of incorrectly claimed input VAT may reach five prior tax years. Under the Central Bank foreign-exchange regulations, export proceeds must be repatriated within 90 days of shipment (automatically extendable to 180 days; longer periods require central bank approval), and failure to repatriate may trigger recharacterization of revenue as non-export domestic taxable income at 20% CIT, loss of zero-rating status, and exchange-control penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan Dollar (TWD) is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including dividend distributions, licence fees, and trade-related service payments, subject only to post-event declaration thresholds. Capital-account flows, including equity injections, shareholder loans, and derivative-related remittances, require CBC pre-approval or enhanced declaration through a domestic designated foreign-exchange bank. Profits remitted by a mainland China investor are permitted under the Statute for Investment by Foreign Nationals and the Regulations Governing Permission for Investment in Taiwan by People from the Mainland Area, provided the investment remains FSC-approved, the Taiwan JV files annual audited financial statements, corporate income tax and withholding tax obligations are settled, and the remittance is executed through a CBC-designated bank with an approved profit-repatriation certificate. For an algorithmic HFT market-making entity, trading spreads, rebates, and venue incentives are classified as service-export proceeds; the CBC will require foreign-exchange declaration on a financial-investment basis to the extent such proceeds are used to satisfy margin calls or clearing obligations at offshore venues.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Management of Foreign Exchange Act and the Regulations Governing Foreign Exchange Declaration, all export and service-export proceeds generated by the Taiwan JV must be credited to a designated foreign-exchange account at a CBC-authorized bank within 180 days from the earning event or declared electronically to the CBC upon receipt, whichever is earlier. There is currently no mandatory forced-conversion ratio for onshore export receipts; the JV may retain 100% of eligible foreign-currency proceeds in a designated multi-currency account, provided the balances are declared and categorised as export/service proceeds rather than investment or arbitrage balances. For algorithmic market-making revenues received from overseas exchanges, the same 180-day repatriation and declaration timeline applies, and any idle foreign-currency balance exceeding the entity's documented trading, hedging, and settlement needs must be converted to TWD at prevailing market rates or supported by an approved treasury hedging programme.

Central bank supervision: The Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (中央銀行, CBC), acting in coordination with the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) for the licensing and foreign-exchange declaration requirements applicable to a proprietary algorithmic high-frequency trading entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

China-to-Taiwan equity injections must be routed through designated foreign-exchange banks via SWIFT MT103 or MT202, using the parent company's registered ODI capital-remittance documentation established under SAFE. The Taiwan receiving bank must submit a CBC foreign-exchange declaration under the financial-investment purpose category applicable to a mainland-area investor's equity subscription, and the funds must initially be lodged in a capital escrow account opened at a Tier-1 Taiwan bank. Supporting documents include the FSC investment permit, share-subscription agreement, board minutes approving the capital call, source-of-funds evidence from the PRC parent's audited financial statements, and a tax-compliance certificate. Escrowed injection funds are released only after the CPA-issued capital verification certificate is filed with the Investment Commission and the corporate registration is updated. Routine working-capital remittances exceeding NTD 500,000 require CBC declaration; receipts in excess of NTD 10 million in a single session trigger enhanced verification and potential a 30-day verification hold if the purpose or beneficial owner cannot be fully substantiated. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions aggregating to TWD 50 million or more per year trigger the Local File; TWD 20 million triggers documentation when a related party is in a no-tax or low-tax jurisdiction. A Master File is required when the worldwide group revenue exceeds TWD 3 billion and the Taiwan entity's cross-border related-party turnover exceeds TWD 50 million. Contemporaneous documentation must be completed by the tax filing deadline and produced within 30 days of request.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a standalone economic substance registration. However, the general anti-avoidance rule in Article 12-3 of the Income Tax Act, the principal-purpose test under the Cross-Strait Agreement, and the CFC rules apply to re-characterise entities without genuine commercial substance. The JV must maintain a real Taiwan head office, licensed trading systems, substantive trading staff, board minutes, risk and clearing functions, and decision-making records to withstand scrutiny.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion via Sham Trading Losses

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Income Tax Act, a penalty of two to three times the underpaid tax; fraudulent evasion is criminally punishable under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act with up to five years' imprisonment and a fine not exceeding NT$1,000,000. Interest accrues from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct the false return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. The tax authority may freeze personal bank accounts, attach real estate and remuneration, and apply for a travel ban on the responsible person until tax is secured. Fraud convictions result in personal imprisonment of the controlling director.
Shell Company Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard the legal form, re-characterise the foreign affiliate's income as Taiwan-source, impose the 21% withholding on constructive dividends, and assess understatement penalties up to three times. Treaty relief under the Cross-Strait Agreement is denied under the principal-purpose test.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure the shell are personally liable under the Tax Collection Act for unpaid tax; personal assets may be seized and passports cancelled. The controlling shareholder, as principal organiser, may receive a five-year criminal sentence for fraudulent tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Administration may adjust taxable income upwards, impose one to three times the understated tax as a penalty, and add overdue interest. Failure to provide contemporaneous documentation shifts the burden of proof to the taxpayer and attracts a fixed penalty of up to NT$50,000.

Director personal liability: The CFO and general manager who executed the TP policy without review can be named as responsible persons, subjected to restrictive travel during audit, and held jointly liable for penalties. Knowing falsification of the TP file exposes the certifying director to Article 41 criminal liability and imprisonment.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Suspicious Flows

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Control Act, the entity faces fines from NT$1 million to NT$50 million for serious internal-control failures; knowingly handling criminal proceeds carries fines up to NT$100 million and imprisonment up to seven years for responsible persons. The Financial Supervisory Commission may revoke the firm's trading licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who ignore suspicious transactions are criminally indictable as accomplices, face civil liability to the clearing house, receive a travel embargo, and may be disqualified from financial-sector directorships for up to five years. Personal assets are attachable in regulatory enforcement actions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through provincial and municipal tax services; taxpayer portal: China Electronic Tax Bureau (etax.chinatax.gov.cn); trade facilitation and customs clearance portal: China International Trade Single Window (singlewindow.cn); foreign exchange monitoring: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) through designated commercial banks.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) as a Chinese limited liability company with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the business licence carrying the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which by operation of law constitutes the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN), the VAT registration number, and the Customs enterprise code base. Step 2: Complete supplementary tax registration particulars (legal representative, registered address, accounting system and standards, bank settlement accounts, tax handling officer) through the Electronic Tax Bureau portal within 30 days of business licence issuance. Step 3: Elect general VAT taxpayer status; this election is mandatory where the projected annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 and is commercially necessary for any exporter because small-scale status forfeits input VAT credits and export refunds. Step 4: Enrol in the national fully digitalised electronic invoice (EDI) platform and obtain electronic invoice issuance quotas for both ordinary VAT invoices and special VAT invoices. Step 5: Register with Customs for declaration purposes, obtain the Customs enterprise registration number, file for export tax rebate qualification under the manufacturer exemption-credit-refund method, and complete SAFE foreign-exchange registration for the export proceeds collection account. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the WFOE is a Chinese tax-resident limited liability enterprise taxed on worldwide income at the 25% headline Corporate Income Tax (CIT) rate. China does not recognise pass-through or partnership tax treatment for a foreign-invested limited liability company, and no fiscal transparency election exists. The WFOE is simultaneously a VAT taxpayer, an urban maintenance and construction tax payer, a stamp duty taxpayer, and a statutory withholding agent for non-resident dividends, interest, royalties, rents, and service fees. Distributions of after-tax profits to the ultimate foreign shareholder are in principle subject to 10% dividend withholding tax, reducible to 5% where the beneficial owner is a tax-treaty resident (including the position under the Cross-Strait Tax Cooperation Agreement where the Taiwan shareholder holds at least 25% capital and meets substance conditions).

Available election forms

China maintains no check-the-box, pass-through, or OECD-style hybrid-mismatch elective regime; hybrid arrangements are neutralised mechanically under the categorical anti-deferral and deductibility rules contained in Caishui [2019] No. 88 and the associated announcements. The elections that are legally available to the WFOE: (i) VAT taxpayer status election: general taxpayer (input-VAT crediting, 13%/9%/6% rates) versus small-scale taxpayer (3% simplified levy, reduced to 1% in the 2023-2027 preferential window), the former being mandatory above RMB 5,000,000 annual turnover; (ii) export rebate method election: manufacturers self-producing exported goods apply the exemption-credit-refund mechanism, while trading intermediaries use the exemption-refund mechanism; (iii) transfer pricing method election among the five OECD-aligned pricing methods (comparable uncontrolled price, resale price, cost-plus, transactional net margin, profit split), including the option to petition for a unilateral Advance Pricing Agreement; (iv) foreign tax credit election: direct credit per-country limitation, with an indirect credit election for dividends received from qualifying lower-tier foreign subsidiaries; (v) fixed-asset depreciation method election, including accelerated depreciation for qualifying equipment specified in the CIT implementing regulations; (vi) CIT loss carryforward utilisation election: 5-year general carryforward, extended to 10 years for qualifying high-technology enterprises (including certain advanced medical device manufacturers); (vii) no group registration, fiscal unity, or consolidated CIT filing is available; the WFOE must file on a standalone legal-entity basis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable turnover in the preceding 12 consecutive months is the statutory threshold above which registration as a general VAT taxpayer is mandatory. Below this threshold, the enterprise may elect small-scale taxpayer status (simplified 3% levy rate; reduced to 1% for small-scale taxpayers during the 2023-2027 preferential window), but any export-oriented WFOE should elect general taxpayer status to preserve input VAT credits and export refunds. VAT registration as such is compulsory for every enterprise upon incorporation regardless of turnover; a WFOE can never operate outside the VAT net. Additional de minimis exemptions apply to micro businesses with monthly turnover of RMB 100,000 or less, but these exemptions do not eliminate the registration duty.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim the 0% export VAT rate, coupled with the refund or offset of creditable input VAT under the exemption-credit-refund method, for self-manufactured medical devices, including ultra-high-definition 4K minimally invasive rigid endoscope towers classifiable under ISIC 3250 / HS heading 9018. The conditions that must coexist are: (i) the goods physically leave the PRC customs territory, including a supply to Taiwan, which is treated de facto as a separate customs territory in cross-strait trade, or to any other foreign destination; (ii) the WFOE has elected and holds general VAT taxpayer status and possesses a valid Customs enterprise registration code; (iii) the export proceeds are collected in convertible foreign currency (or in RMB under approved cross-border settlement channels) and are repatriated through a designated bank within the statutory 180-day period; (iv) the data sets contained in the Customs export declaration, the sales invoices, the logistics documents, and the bank receipts are materially consistent and truthful; (v) the WFOE has not committed export-rebate fraud, smuggling, or a serious tax or customs infringement in the preceding 36 months; (vi) the complete substantiation docket is retained for at least 5 years from the export date, being the applicable audit-claim period. Cross-border services associated with the laparoscopy-tower supplies (commissioning, installation, operator training, and embedded software) qualify for zero-rating only as offshore services where the service is physically rendered from the PRC for consumption entirely in Taiwan or another foreign jurisdiction, supported by the overseas contract, performance evidence, and foreign-currency receipt.

China operates a mandatory fully digitalised electronic invoice regime: all VAT invoices, both ordinary and special, must be generated through the STA's national e-invoice service platform and transmitted in real time to the Golden Tax Phase IV databases. Every invoice carries a machine-verified digital stamp, a unique nationwide invoice code, and a two-dimensional tax-control mark. A special VAT invoice is a strict precondition for claiming any input VAT credit and must be verified and matched online before the credit is allowed. No private third-party fiscalisation intermediary or physical stamping exists; the government platform performs real-time validation and release at the moment of issuance. For exports, the China International Trade Single Window interconnects the customs export declaration, the export sales invoice, the transport document (bill of lading or waybill), and the bank's foreign-exchange receipt data, creating a compulsory four-agency reconciliation chain (Customs, Tax, Bank, Logistics). Post-issuance invoice cancellation or red-flag corrections are systematically audited and may trigger automatic blockage of the exporter's rebate quota.

The export-rebate system is enforced through compulsory four-agency data reconciliation whereby the Customs export declaration, the VAT sales invoice, the bank foreign-exchange receipt, and the logistics movement record are matched algorithmically; any discrepancy triggers an automatic risk-control investigation before the rebate is disbursed. The applicable penalties are: (i) clawback of the entire VAT refund together with late-payment surcharge accruing at 0.05% per day from the refund payment date; (ii) administrative penalty of 50% up to 500% (fivefold) of the fraudulently obtained refund amount under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection; (iii) denial or suspension of export-rebate qualification for up to 3 years, downgrade of the enterprise tax credit rating, and institution of restricted invoice issuance quotas; (iv) separate Customs Law penalties for false declarations, under-declaration, or smuggling of medical devices, including confiscation of goods and fines of up to 3 times the dutiable value; (v) criminal liability under the Criminal Law for fraudulent export refund, carrying fines plus imprisonment from less than 3 years up to life imprisonment where the defrauded amount reaches RMB 5 million or more or the circumstances are especially serious, together with mandatory confiscation of property; (vi) SAFE administrative fines between 5% and 30% of the unrepatriated amount where the 180-day export-proceeds collection deadline is missed without an approved extension; (vii) personal joint and several liability and personal travel bans imposed on the legal representative and the responsible finance officer where wilful fraud is established in enforcement proceedings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations (1996, as amended), RMB is convertible for current account transactions, including goods trade, services trade, investment income (dividends), royalties, and current-account expenses, provided that the underlying transaction is genuine, lawful, and supported by documentary evidence. Capital account transactions remain subject to a registration or approval system: inbound foreign direct investment (FDI) into the capital account requires a SAFE FDI registration and bank-level verification; the paid-in capital is routed through an approved foreign exchange capital account and may be settled into RMB only in accordance with SAFE's capital account settlement rules. Profit remittance by the WFOE to the Taiwan parent is classified as investment income on the current account and is permitted after the board of directors has declared dividends, the distributable profit is verified by a Chinese certified public accountant, the required tax payment or tax exemption certificate has been issued, and the settlement bank has received the audit report, dividend resolution, and supporting tax documents. Key remittance hurdles include the possibility that accumulated losses restrict distributable profits, the requirement to pay withholding income tax at a rate generally around 10% absent an applicable arrangement to reduce it, and the need to ensure bank compliance with PBOC's financial account reporting (FATCA/CRS) obligations. Capital injections, by contrast, cannot be remitted as ordinary trade payments; they must follow the specific inbound FDI wire-purpose-code routing and be verified against the SAFE registration code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory mandatory surrender ratio is nil. Since 2012, China has abolished mandatory conversion of export proceeds; a WFOE may freely retain export receipts in a foreign-currency settlement account or settle them into RMB through a licensed bank, subject to genuineness and trade-verification review. The repatriation requirement, however, requires that export proceeds for goods declared by the WFOE be brought into China and deposited in a domestic foreign-exchange account. Under the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Measures (Huifa [2012] No. 38, as amended), export proceeds should generally be collected within 90 days from the date of the export customs declaration; if collection occurs more than 90 days after export, the enterprise must register the delayed collection in the foreign exchange monitoring system through the bank. Failure to comply may generate an abnormal trade-inspection record and downgrade the enterprise's A/B/C administrative classification, which in turn increases the documentation burden for all subsequent cross-border transactions. No forced percentage conversion applies to the export receipts of ordinary manufacturing enterprises.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium as a secondary collection channel, and not acceptable as the primary treasury rail. Under PRC law, only licensed banks and licensed payment institutions are permitted to conduct cross-border RMB or foreign-currency clearing; offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Payoneer, and Currenxie are not licensed to serve as the collecting bank for PRC export proceeds. The WFOE's export proceeds arising from goods customs-declared in China must be repatriated through a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account at a licensed bank. Receiving funds into an offshore EMI account and then transferring them into the PRC may violate the SAFE repatriation requirement and expose the WFOE to anti-structuring and AML scrutiny. EMIs may be used for ancillary transactions, such as inbound payments for pre-sale R&D or service fees, but only if the underlying transaction is current-account compliant, the funds are remitted in accordance with the purpose code, and the bank can see the full documentary trail. A formal Legal-Compliance assessment must be completed before any offshore EMI is used for actual treasury receipts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan parent's capital injection must be remitted to the WFOE's approved 'FDI Capital Escrow Account' (Foreign Exchange Capital Account) at a domestic designated bank. Prior to remittance, the WFOE must complete the SAFE FDI registration with the bank and obtain the FDI registration number tied to the unified social credit code. The inbound wire (SWIFT MT103) should state the purpose as 'FDI Capital Injection / Paid-in Capital' in field 70 and include the FDI registration number, the company name, and the account number in the beneficiary bank instruction. The remitting bank in Taiwan may need a correspondent bank for USD/HKD/TWD routing; settlement into RMB is permitted only after the receiving bank verifies the capital account against SAFE systems. Verification files include the completed FDI registration form, the business licence, the foreign investor's passport/corporate documents, the board and shareholder resolution approving the injection, and a notarized capital contribution schedule. The capital account balance cannot be used for purposes unrelated to the approved business scope, nor can it be applied to pay off intercompany loans without SAFE permission.The WFOE will use standard documentary trade and treasury rails for outbound disbursements: (i) irrevocable commercial letters of credit (LC) opened by the WFOE's domestic bank for large or medium-high value import/procurement transactions, with the Taiwan supplier as beneficiary; (ii) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account payments for suppliers and service providers, subject to SAFE's genuine-transaction verification and trade monitoring; (iii) documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for repetitive, low-risk trade partners; (iv) Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) RMB clearing for settlements permitted in CNY between mainland China and Taiwan corporate counterparties; and (v) for dividend and royalty remittances to the Taiwan parent, bank-settled MT103 wires with the tax withholding certificate, audited financial statements, and board dividend resolution attached. All outbound trade payments must be matched to an underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration (where goods cross borders), and the relevant SAFE 'trade verification' entry; payment instruction fields must include the transaction purpose code and supporting reference numbers, or the bank will suspend the transfer.Onshore USD/CNY foreign exchange forward contracts (current-account and trade-based hedging) with a licensed domestic bankOnshore USD/CNY foreign exchange swaps (cash/forward combinations) for short-term liquidity and trade cash-flow equalizationCross-currency swaps (CCS) for medium and long-term USD/CNY hedging related to the top-up equity loan or business cash-flow matchingForeign exchange option and option-combination products for eligible enterprise treasury clients with a declared hedging objective and risk-management capacityOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for the onshore WFOE; they may be used only by the offshore Taiwan parent on the ultimate economic exposure and must remain outside the WFOE's domestic regulatory perimeter

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium as a secondary collection channel, and not acceptable as the primary treasury rail. Under PRC law, only licensed banks and licensed payment institutions are permitted to conduct cross-border RMB or foreign-currency clearing; offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Payoneer, and Currenxie are not licensed to serve as the collecting bank for PRC export proceeds. The WFOE's export proceeds arising from goods customs-declared in China must be repatriated through a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account at a licensed bank. Receiving funds into an offshore EMI account and then transferring them into the PRC may violate the SAFE repatriation requirement and expose the WFOE to anti-structuring and AML scrutiny. EMIs may be used for ancillary transactions, such as inbound payments for pre-sale R&D or service fees, but only if the underlying transaction is current-account compliant, the funds are remitted in accordance with the purpose code, and the bank can see the full documentary trail. A formal Legal-Compliance assessment must be completed before any offshore EMI is used for actual treasury receipts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory if annual related-party tangible asset transactions reach RMB 200 million; intangible transactions RMB 100 million; financial transactions RMB 100 million; other related-party transactions RMB 40 million. Master File is required if aggregate related-party transaction value exceeds RMB 1 billion in the same tax year.

Economic substance scope: China does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) statute; however, the GAAR and substance-over-form principles under Article 47 of the Tax Collection Administration Law and the BEPS MLI require that a manufacturing WFOE maintain real economic presence in China (physical premises, qualified personnel, operational decision-making, and risk control functions). The Taiwan parent's control over technology and strategic manufacturing decisions must be supported by actual service agreements and cost allocations; otherwise tax authorities may disregard the Chinese entity's separate existence and re-characterise profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Understated tax is subject to 100% penalty (Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law), daily late surcharge of 0.05% per day, criminal fine of up to five times the evaded tax, and imprisonment up to seven years for responsible personnel under PRC Criminal Law Article 201.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false tax returns may be held personally liable for restitution, face travel bans, asset freezing, and imprisonment if the evasion is egregious; the corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may recharacterise the entire taxable income under GAAR, disallow cost deductions, deem a dividend distribution, and levy 10% WHT on the recharacterised amount; civil fraud penalties may reach 300% of underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may become jointly liable for the company's tax debts under the alter-ego doctrine; travel restrictions and prohibition from departing Mainland China are commonly imposed; serious cases trigger criminal investigation for tax evasion.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violation

Statutory sanction: Institution fines up to RMB 1 million per violation; freezing of bank accounts; suspension of VAT rebate qualification; revocation of foreign-exchange registration; referral to the Anti-Money Laundering Bureau for criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 191.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives and AML compliance officers may face personal criminal fines and up to ten years' imprisonment for money laundering offences; civil liability for damages arising from undisclosed UBO structures.

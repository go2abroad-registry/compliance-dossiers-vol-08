Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a company limited by shares under the Taiwan Company Act. For a wholly foreign-owned enterprise, however, the paid-up capital must equal the amount expressly approved by the Investment Commission, MOEA, and must be remitted from abroad into a temporary foreign-investment capital account maintained at a licensed bank in Taiwan. The funds must remain unencumbered and must be verified by an independent Taiwan CPA through a capital verification report before company registration is granted. Blocked escrow account rules: None. A share capital release is not conditioned on any escrow arrangement, but the remittance must be completed and verified prior to final registration approval.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance (MOF), National Taxation Administration, Taiwan; the applicable local National Taxation Bureau for the WFOE's registered address; online portals: MOF eTax Portal and the E-Invoice Integration Service Platform

E-government filing portal

For the Taiwan WFOE (wholly foreign-owned limited liability company): (1) Complete incorporation under the Taiwan Company Act through the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) Company and Commercial Registration Portal; if the ultimate investor is a mainland China entity, obtain prior approval from the Investment Commission, MOEA under the Foreign Investment Act. (2) Before commencing business, file the Profit-Seeking Enterprise Tax Registration Application with the local National Taxation Bureau (NTB); the NTB issues an 8-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN), which is also the Uniform Invoice Number. (3) If a foreign non-resident company has no fixed place of business in Taiwan but conducts Taiwan-source sales, it must appoint a local tax agent and register under the same tax registration procedure before collecting Business Tax. (4) Register as an exporter with the Customs Administration, Ministry of Finance, if physical goods are to be exported; the same TIN is used for customs and VAT. (5) Register for electronic Uniform Invoice issuance through the E-Invoice Integration Service Platform and obtain Turnkey/API credentials. (6) Open a foreign exchange account with a domestic authorized bank for export proceeds repatriation and retain all corporate authorization documents, board resolutions, and apostilled/notarized certificates for the Taiwan tax file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not apply a US-style check-the-box election. The WFOE is a resident profit-seeking enterprise taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Act; no pass-through or disregarded-entity election exists. Anti-hybrid and controlled-foreign-company rules apply to cross-border structures. For Business Tax, the value-added/non-value-added system is compulsory; no election to register as a different indirect tax payer type is available except the small-scale-business assessment at 1% for low-turnover operators.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for tax registration; every profit-seeking enterprise must register with the National Taxation Bureau before commencing taxable activity. For the standard VAT/uniform-invoice regime, compulsory Uniform Invoice issuance applies once average monthly taxable sales exceed NT$200,000; below that threshold, an eligible small-scale business operator may be taxed at 1% and is not required to issue Uniform Invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, 0% VAT applies to (i) physical goods exported from Taiwan evidenced by a valid customs export declaration; (ii) B2B platform services supplied to a foreign enterprise if the service is not used or consumed within Taiwan; and (iii) cross-border B2B marketplace sales where the goods are shipped from Taiwan to a foreign buyer. The WFOE must retain evidence of the foreign counterparty, foreign place of use or benefit, physical export movement, and actual foreign exchange repatriation. Zero-rated sales retain refundable input VAT credits after offsetting output tax.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export entry/export report) issued by the Customs Administration, Ministry of Finance
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight, with port or airport of discharge outside Taiwan
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and freight or insurance documents
  • B2B marketplace purchase order, sales contract, platform transaction log, and payment routing evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Export Proceeds Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate issued by an authorized foreign-exchange bank
  • Zero-rated Uniform Invoice / electronic Uniform Invoice record transmitted to the E-Invoice Integration Service Platform
  • Foreign enterprise status evidence (e.g., foreign company registration certificate or equivalent) for service/offshore platform supplies

Taiwan uses a post-sale e-invoice reporting model, not real-time fiscal clearance. Registered WFOE must issue Uniform Invoices for taxable B2B sales and transmit electronic Uniform Invoice data to the MOF E-Invoice Integration Service Platform through the Turnkey connection or approved API. The platform validates invoice numbering, business TIN matching, and time stamps; there is no government anti-fraud digital stamp. B2B e-invoices are reported within the legal filing period; B2C receipts are printed or transmitted with lottery random numbers. Non-compliance with e-invoice transmission triggers Business Tax Act penalties on the output tax and reduces input credit integrity.

The National Taxation Bureau cross-matches customs export records, e-invoice zero-rating codes, foreign-exchange settlement records, ocean/air manifests, and platform transaction data. If the WFOE cannot prove physical exit, foreign counterparty identity, foreign usage, or timely repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days, the zero-rating claim is reversed and the 5% Business Tax is assessed with interest from the original due date. Under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act, a false or unsupported zero-rating claim carries a penalty of up to five times the tax evaded; under the Tax Collection Act, the collection and assessment period may extend up to seven years for fraudulent concealment, and egregious cases are referred for criminal tax evasion prosecution. The tax authority may also suspend Uniform Invoice supply until the audit is resolved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with RMB current-account convertibility while maintaining robust capital-account controls. For a CN_WFOE_LTD, trade-related current account receipts and payments, including import/export settlement, freight, insurance, and platform transaction fees, are generally convertible through designated banks after documentary verification of genuine underlying trade. Capital account flows, however, are strictly regulated: foreign direct investment capital injection must be registered with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and routed into a dedicated FDI capital account; voluntary conversion into RMB is permitted but must be supervised through a settlement-change account system to prevent idle fund speculation. Export proceeds are subject to the Exports Collection Verification System, requiring repatriation of all proceeds into China for verification against customs declarations; the WFOE may retain foreign exchange without forced surrender, but there is no automatic right to delay repatriation, and overdue collections trigger alarms. Dividend and profit remittances to the Taiwan shareholder require SAFE registration, an audited financial statement showing accumulated earnings, corporate income tax settlement certificate, and evidence that foreign exchange for remittance comes from legal operations. The practical effect is an asymmetric regime: current account flows are fast and liberalized, while capital account exits require pre-audit and are time-bound. The WFOE must keep perfect underlying transaction files, invoice log, customs records, and e-tax stamps or its designated bank will suspend FX settlement and clearing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds are required to be repatriated through designated foreign-exchange banks within 90 days after customs export declaration; trade-contract extended periods up to 180 days are allowed with application. All export receipts must enter a domestic Foreign Exchange Settlement Account and go through SAFE's electronic verification. The statutory surrender ratio is 0% as mandatory conversion to RMB has been abolished, allowing 100% retention in foreign currency or voluntary conversion to RMB at market rate. Failure to repatriate or late verification will trigger suspension of tax rebates and downgrade by the bank's foreign-exchange compliance rating.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Cross-border digital banking platforms and EMI challengers generally cannot act as primary deposit-taking institutions for a Chinese WFOE because China's commercial banking law requires bank licenses and SAFE only permits settlement through designated foreign-exchange banks. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Neat may be useful for outbound supplier payments but cannot replace the domestic RMB capital account or corporate bank account. Legal use requires partnership with a licensed Chinese bank and payment institution, for example Alipay cross-border, under PBOC and SAFE licenses; direct account opening with a foreign EMI is not legally viable as a primary settlement rail for this WFOE.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under Taiwan Company Act Article 20, a non-public company must obtain an independent certified CPA audit if its paid-in capital is NT$30 million or more (approx. USD 1 million). There is no statutory balance-sheet total, turnover, or employee-headcount trigger for a Taiwan company-law audit; however, the Ministry of Economic Affairs may order a special audit when deemed necessary, and minority shareholders holding 3% or more of total issued shares may petition a court for an appointed auditor under Company Act Article 245. Public companies under the Securities and Exchange Act are always subject to IFRS-based audited financial statements. For this WFOE B2B platform entity, the only mandatory CPA audit threshold is the NT$30 million paid-in-capital rule; if capital remains below that threshold, no external statutory audit is required but the company must still prepare financial statements under the Commercial Accounting Act and present them for shareholder approval.

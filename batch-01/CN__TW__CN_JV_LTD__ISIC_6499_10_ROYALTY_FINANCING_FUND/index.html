Applicable AML statute: Primary AML/CFT legislation comprises the Money Laundering Control Act, as amended; the Counter-Terrorism Financing Act; the Company Act, particularly Article 22-1 concerning company information and beneficial ownership-related filings; the Regulations Governing Company Registration; the Regulations Governing Anti-Money Laundering of Financial Institutions; and the Directions Governing Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Terrorism Financing of Businesses Required to Conduct Customer Due Diligence. Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office address in Taiwan is mandatory. A virtual office may be acceptable only if it is a genuine usable address, the company has lawful occupancy or use rights, mail and official notices can be received, and the premises satisfy registration, tax, banking, AML, and sectoral compliance requirements. For revenue-based financing involving royalty, receivables, direct-debit, ACH-like payment collection, wire transfers, or investor funds, a purely nominal address is high risk. The company should maintain local books and records, responsible management, transaction documentation, customer and counterparty files, a Taiwan bank account where commercially practicable, and demonstrable decision-making and compliance capability. The proposed activity must be separately tested against Taiwan rules on money lending, factoring, financial leasing, securities, collective investment, trust, payment services, and foreign exchange; the ISIC and NAICS classifications do not establish licensing status.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a Taiwan-incorporated Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the foreign investor first obtains investment approval from the Ministry of Economic Affairs Investment Commission; the company is then incorporated with the MOEA Department of Commercial Affairs, which assigns the Unified Business Number (統一編號) that serves as the statutory tax ID. Within 15 days of commencing business, the company must complete sales-tax/business registration (營業登記) with the competent National Taxation Bureau. The foreign parent not maintaining a Taiwan fixed place of business may register as a foreign legal entity for withholding-tax purposes through the same National Taxation Bureau and obtain a temporary tax ID / business tax registration when appointed a withholding agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not have a US-style check-the-box or domestic pass-through election regime. No hybrid mismatch classification election is available. Available statutory elections include: (i) adoption of a non-calendar fiscal year with prior approval from the tax authority; (ii) election of depreciation and inventory accounting methods; (iii) per-country or overall foreign tax credit limitation election; and (iv) treaty-based reduced withholding-rate application, subject to approval and filing through the tax portal. A Taiwan JV is a resident company taxable at entity level on its worldwide income, with foreign-source income eligible for foreign tax credit subject to limitation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold; any taxable business conducting sales within Taiwan must apply for tax registration upon commencement. For small-scale business exemption from standard 5% VAT and uniform-invoice issuance, the de minimis thresholds are average monthly sales exceeding NT$80,000 for goods or NT$40,000 for services; businesses exceeding such thresholds are required to register, issue standard electronic uniform invoices, collect 5% output VAT, and file every two months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods: zero-rating applies when goods are actually exported outside Taiwan customs territory, evidenced by a valid customs export declaration, with the exporter being a registered Taiwan taxable business. Export of services: zero-rating applies to offshore services provided for use entirely outside Taiwan, including services performed for overseas clients with settlement in foreign currency, regardless of whether the services are performed in Taiwan. Revenue-based financing and royalty services to non-residents may qualify as export services if the economic use occurs outside Taiwan and foreign-currency settlement is substantiated. The taxpayer must retain contracts, invoices, customs declarations, proof of export, and FX receipts for at least five years.

Taiwan mandates electronic uniform invoices for taxable businesses through the MOF e-Invoice Integration Service (Turnkey). Businesses issue e-invoices via certified API interfaces and transmit data to the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice Platform, generally within 48 hours of issuance. The system assigns invoice numbers and random codes through the central platform; there is no continuous real-time fiscal stamping or government pre-clearance, but failure to timely upload e-invoice data subjects the business to warnings, penalties, and possible revocation of simplified-tax treatment. B2C cloud receipts include a QR code and random number for buyer validation.

Export zero-rating and input-VAT refunds are subject to strict post-clearance audit and cross-validation of customs declarations, shipping documents, and FX receipts. If the documentary chain is incomplete or inconsistent, the tax authority will reclassify the supply as taxable, assess 5% VAT on the invoice amount, deny or claw back the input VAT refund, and add delayed interest. Penalties under the Business Tax Act include an administrative fine of one to five times the underpaid tax. Where fraudulent documentation or deliberate misrepresentation is established, the responsible person may be subject to criminal liability under the Tax Evasion Act, including imprisonment for up to five years and monetary fines, together with retroactive recovery of all improperly refunded VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates under current-account convertibility while maintaining a supervised capital account. Bona fide trade, services, royalty fees, and profit remittances are settled via banks after verification, but every outward transfer to Taiwan is screened for SAFE registration and tax clearance. Dividend/profit remittances from the Sino-Foreign JV require an audited financial statement, a board-approved distribution, an annual enterprise income tax return, and a withholding payment certificate (10% withholding for Taiwanese corporate recipients absent an effective cross-strait double-taxation agreement; the 2015 Cross-Strait DTA is signed but not yet operational). Capital-account inflows from Taiwan, registered as FDI, are generally convertible to RMB after SAFE bank registration using a foreign exchange capital account; any outbound equity return, intra-group financing, or debt service must be separately approved or registered under SAFE (e.g., PBOC macro-prudential or MOFCOM approvals). Profit remittance is not blocked once tax proof and the SAFE foreign-invested enterprise remittance form are presented; the practical hurdle is the 10-15 business-day bank compliance review, not the absence of distributable profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished mandatory surrender for general trade in 2012; exporters are classified under A/B/C foreign-exchange status for monitoring. Exporters are not required to sell foreign currency proceeds at a fixed statutory ratio, but proceeds must be collected by the contractually agreed or generally expected date, normally within 90 days after export customs declaration, and must be entered into a trade settlement account. Where receipt is overdue, the exporter must report or reclassify the transaction through the bank to SAFE. For cross-strait commerce, mainland exporters and Taiwan importers may agree to settle in RMB via the Bank of China (Taipei) clearing bank and the CIPS, effectively eliminating mandatory FX surrender, provided the underlying goods flow and customs declarations are matched.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for the People's Republic of China; Taiwan-side monetary and FX authority: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for offshore perimeter collection and disbursement in USD/EUR/HKD; Low as a replacement for onshore RMB clearing. PRC law does not recognise foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) as licensed payment or banking institutions. An offshore EMI can open a segregated treasury/merchant account for holding and disbursing foreign currency to support downstream royalty payments to digital brands outside China, but it cannot lawfully collect RMB from PRC consumers, maintain a domestic clearing account, or accept deposits from PRC residents. For any RMB-denominated in-country receipts, the Chinese JV must use a licensed onshore third-party payment company or a clearing bank. EMI services are usable as a quarantined cross-border sidecar with capped balances and full audit documentation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% corporate income tax on taxable income under Taiwan's Income Tax Act. A 12% surtax applies to undistributed earnings generally retained after the statutory computation, subject to statutory exclusions and investment offsets.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a nonresident individual, foreign company, or foreign entity, subject to any applicable treaty or statutory relief. Dividend distributions must also comply with Taiwan Company Act procedures, including approval of financial statements and appropriation of earnings.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The annual settlement return and final payment are generally due from 1 May through 31 May of the following year for a calendar-year taxpayer. Corporate taxpayers with an approved accounting period ending on a date other than 31 December must apply the statutory deadline calculated from that year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan does not use a single universal threshold for every Local File obligation. A Local File is generally required for material cross-border or domestic related-party transactions unless a specific exemption applies. Common exemption thresholds include annual net operating revenue and non-operating income below NT$300 million or aggregate annual controlled transactions below NT$200 million, subject to transaction-specific exceptions and current regulations. A Master File is generally relevant where the Taiwan entity meets the large-enterprise threshold of approximately NT$3 billion in annual revenue and has cross-border related-party transactions, or where another prescribed condition applies. Thresholds, exemptions, and filing deadlines must be tested for the relevant tax year.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan has no separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime generally applicable to this business. Substance is nevertheless enforced through transfer-pricing rules, the arm's-length principle, the general anti-avoidance rule, controlled foreign company rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, source rules, withholding rules, thin-capitalization provisions, and business-purpose requirements. A financing or royalty entity should maintain local decision-making, personnel or outsourced functions under effective control, risk management, records, banking capacity, credit analysis, contractual control, and evidence that it performs and bears the risks for which it receives income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, tax recovery, interest, civil penalties, and potential criminal prosecution. Tax evasion can result in imprisonment and fines under Taiwan tax law, with the exact sanction depending on the statute, amount, conduct, and prosecutorial findings.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face criminal liability, fines, tax-related civil restitution, disqualification consequences, and asset enforcement. Travel restrictions or detention are not automatic and require statutory or judicial grounds; piercing of the corporate veil is fact-specific and is not presumed merely because a director signed the return.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax, interest, withholding-agent penalties, potential collection enforcement, and possible criminal exposure for intentional concealment or evasion.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons and withholding agents can be personally exposed where the law attributes withholding duties to them or where intentional misconduct, gross negligence, falsification, or unlawful distributions is established.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's-length income or deductions, denial of deductions, additional corporate income tax, withholding tax reassessment, interest, penalties, and possible anti-avoidance treatment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability to the company or shareholders for approving abusive transactions and criminal exposure if records or filings are falsified. Personal liability is fact-dependent and does not arise solely from an unfavorable transfer-pricing adjustment.
Illegal financial business or unauthorized lending

Statutory sanction: Business suspension, license or registration consequences, confiscation or disgorgement, administrative fines, and criminal sanctions under Taiwan banking, company, securities, or financial-supervision laws, depending on the activity.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face imprisonment, fines, management disqualification, civil restitution, and enforcement against personal assets where unauthorized financial activity or fraudulent conduct is proven.
AML, CFT, and sanctions noncompliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, remedial orders, reporting restrictions, business suspension, and criminal sanctions under Taiwan anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorism-financing laws where the elements of an offense are satisfied.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and responsible personnel may be fined or prosecuted for knowing, intentional, or seriously negligent participation. Civil restitution and asset freezing may apply; travel bans are case-specific and require competent-authority action.
Beneficial-ownership and shell-company abuse

Statutory sanction: Corporate and tax reassessment, forfeiture or disgorgement, invalidation or suspension of registrations, AML penalties, and potential criminal charges for fraud, forgery, money laundering, or tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly use the company as an instrument of fraud may be subject to civil damages, restitution, asset seizure, imprisonment, and management restrictions. Limited liability does not protect intentional personal wrongdoing.
Illegal profit distribution and capital impairment

Statutory sanction: Restoration of improperly distributed amounts, director liability for damages, company-law penalties, tax reassessment, withholding exposure, and possible criminal consequences for fraudulent accounting or creditor prejudice.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for restoration and damages and may face criminal sanctions if they knowingly approve unlawful distributions or false accounts.
Foreign-exchange and cross-Strait payment violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, transaction reversal, confiscation, payment restrictions, and criminal liability where fraud, evasion, or prohibited transfer conduct is established.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may incur personal administrative or criminal liability, civil restitution, asset seizure, and management restrictions. Travel limitations require separate legal authority and are not an automatic consequence of a compliance breach.
Recordkeeping and document destruction

Statutory sanction: Estimated assessment, denial of deductions or treaty relief, administrative fines, audit adjustments, and enhanced scrutiny. Destruction or fabrication of evidence may create independent criminal exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face civil damages and criminal liability for intentional destruction, falsification, obstruction, or knowing submission of inaccurate records.

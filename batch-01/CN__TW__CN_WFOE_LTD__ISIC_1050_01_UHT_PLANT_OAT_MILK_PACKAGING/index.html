Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up share capital is required for a limited-liability wholly foreign-owned enterprise in Taiwan unless the Investment Commission or an industry regulator imposes a higher reviewed capital amount. The declared capital must be fully subscribed and paid into a Taiwanese NT$ corporate or temporary capital account by inbound wire transfer; the capital remittance must be evidenced by an FCDA foreign exchange certificate and by a certified public accountant capital verification report. A blocked escrow account is not generally mandated for cash subscriptions, but the Investment Commission may impose an escrow, lock-up, or capital retention condition when the investor contributes intangible assets, promises future plant investment, or receives conditional tax incentives.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Chinese VAT General Taxpayer that physically exports goods from the PRC customs territory is eligible for 0% VAT on the export sale. The aseptic oat milk product must be sold to an overseas buyer, cleared by PRC Customs under the exporter's own name, leave the PRC customs territory, and have the export proceeds collected in an approved manner. Qualifying exports are handled under the exempt, credit, and refund method, allowing the exporter to claim an input VAT refund on the exported products, subject to production and export verification rules. Goods shipped to the Taiwan jurisdiction are treated as exports from the PRC customs territory when the export customs declaration is made.

  • PRC Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (export rebate copy) submitted through the China International Trade Single Window and released or customs-endorsed
  • Transport document evidencing physical export: original or electronic Bill of Lading for ocean shipments, Air Waybill for air cargo, or international road or rail waybill where applicable
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, export sales contract or proforma invoice reflecting the export value, Incoterms, and overseas consignee
  • Foreign exchange or cross-border RMB receipt proof: bank SWIFT MT103 or MT202 credit advice or foreign exchange settlement voucher showing payment credited to the WFOE's designated domestic account
  • Export food safety or quarantine certificate, product inspection certificate, or export licence, as required by China Customs or the General Administration of Customs of China for plant-based beverage exports

China mandates issuance of Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices through the STA Digital E-Invoice Service Platform for the national VAT invoice management system. Invoice data is transmitted in real time to the tax authority; each invoice is cleared online and receives a unique tax-control invoice number and anti-counterfeit QR code before delivery to the buyer. Taxpayers integrate ERP or accounting systems through STA-approved APIs or use the official e-invoice platform. Physical stamping is replaced by digital authentication and online verification.

The STA and Customs jointly cross-check the customs export declaration, logistics waybills, invoice issuance, VAT refund calculations, and bank foreign exchange receipts. If zero-rating is unsubstantiated or documentation is incomplete, the export refund is denied; if already paid, it is retroactively clawed back with statutory late-payment interest and penalties. Fraudulent or artificially generated export refunds attract administrative fines of up to five times the tax involved, referral to public security authorities, and, under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, potential imprisonment or life imprisonment plus fines and confiscation. The retrospective recovery period for fraudulent VAT refund claims is unlimited; for non-fraudulent documentary errors the ordinary recovery period is generally three years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with effective current-account convertibility and strict capital-account controls. Current-account transactions, including trade settlement, transport, insurance, and business travel, may be settled in RMB or foreign currency after submission of genuine commercial documentation to a licensed bank. Capital-account transactions, such as FDI capital injections, cross-border loans, equity exits, and securities investments, are not freely convertible; they require prior SAFE registration, a designated capital or foreign-debt account, and compliance with the Negative List and macroprudential foreign-debt quotas. Export proceeds need not be forcibly surrendered; an exporter may retain the foreign currency in a current-account FX account or voluntarily convert to RMB, but the proceeds must be repatriated to China and reported under the trade-in-goods verification system. Profit remittance to the Taiwan shareholder is treated as a dividend distribution under the FDI framework: the WFOE must prove accumulated audited profits, have completed the statutory reserve allocations, and present a certified tax clearance and board resolution. Any mismatch between transaction code, invoice, customs data, or fund flow will trigger an automatic hold by the receiving bank and a targeted SAFE inquiry. Consequently, treasury planning must separate operational RMB liquidity from FX-registered capital and earmark documentation for every cross-border transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory export surrender ratio under current SAFE rules. The WFOE must repatriate export receipts to a domestic current-account foreign exchange account, normally within 90 days after the customs export declaration, and may settle them into RMB at any time or retain them in foreign exchange. B/C-class trade entities face stricter 30-day reporting windows and additional verification for extraordinary receipts.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary onshore banking. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, PingPong, Nium) cannot receive FDI capital injections, hold the SAFE capital-account registration, or provide a lawful RMB settlement account for a China-incorporated WFOE without a PBOC cross-border payment license and an onshore banking partnership. Chinese FX rules require all FDI capital transactions to flow through a licensed domestic bank's capital-account system. Offshore EMIs are therefore legally barred from acting as the primary clearing bank. They may serve only as supplementary collection and payout rails for low-value e-commerce transactions if they hold a valid PBOC Cross-Border Payment Institution license and are linked to a domestic bank account, but they cannot replace the Tier-1 commercial bank relationship.

Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, PingPong, Nium) cannot receive FDI capital injections, hold the SAFE capital-account registration, or provide a lawful RMB settlement account for a China-incorporated WFOE without a PBOC cross-border payment license and an onshore banking partnership. Chinese FX rules require all FDI capital transactions to flow through a licensed domestic bank's capital-account system. Offshore EMIs are therefore legally barred from acting as the primary clearing bank. They may serve only as supplementary collection and payout rails for low-value e-commerce transactions if they hold a valid PBOC Cross-Border Payment Institution license and are linked to a domestic bank account, but they cannot replace the Tier-1 commercial bank relationship.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law, Article 4)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory PRC WHT on dividends paid by a Chinese resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise shareholder; the statutory 20% rate is reduced to 10% by State Council implementation. Under the Cross-Strait Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement, signed 25 August 2015 but not yet internationally in force at the relevant cutoff, the dividend rate would be reduced to 5% if the Taiwan-resident beneficial owner directly holds at least 25% of the WFOE capital and satisfies all substantive conditions, and 10% in other cases.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the Taiwan Company Act, no statutory minimum paid-up capital is required, but the subscribed capital must be fully paid into a temporarily blocked escrow bank account opened in the prospective company name, verified by a licensed Taiwan CPA, and released only after the MOEA issues the Company Registration Certificate. In administrative practice, the Investment Commission expects a paid-in capital of at least NT$500,000 for a wholesale trading entity, and the escrow mechanism prevents early withdrawal of funds before legal formation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (国家税务总局), electronic filing and registration through the Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax Portal) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn. Where applicable to cross-strait trade with Taiwan, the Taiwan competent authority remains the National Taxation Bureau of the Ministry of Finance for Taiwanese counterparty obligations, but the Chinese WFOE is exclusively registered with the State Taxation Administration.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE must first obtain a Unified Social Credit Code from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) through the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System. Tax registration is then completed automatically under the 'one code, one license' system, but the WFOE must complete taxpayer qualification filing through the Electronic Tax Administration portal or in person with the competent local tax bureau. The foreign parent must provide notarized and legalized incorporation documents, passport or corporate identity documents, the WFOE's approved articles of association, business license, ownership or lease documentation for the registered address, bank account information, and identification of the legal representative, financial officer, and tax agent. The WFOE must elect VAT registration status; because it will engage in wholesale exports and require input VAT credits, it should apply for General VAT Taxpayer status by submitting accounting system documentation and projected turnover. It must also register for the electronic fiscal invoice system, obtain a tax electronic UKey or fiscal digital certificate, and enroll with the General Administration of Customs under the customs registration number for export declarations and export VAT refund/rebate procedures.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Registration as a VAT taxpayer is mandatory upon issuing a business license. The mandatory threshold for classification as a General VAT Taxpayer is annual VAT taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000. Below that threshold, the entity is a Small-Scale VAT Taxpayer unless voluntary General VAT Taxpayer registration is approved. For export trading of SSD and CPU chips, the WFOE should be registered as a General VAT Taxpayer to access 0% export zero rating and input VAT refund rights.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A General VAT Taxpayer exporting goods out of the customs territory of the People's Republic of China is eligible for a 0% VAT rate on the export sales. For the WFOE's wholesale trade of solid-state drives and server processors to Taiwan or any other jurisdiction, the goods must physically leave China through a formal customs export declaration; the seller must retain evidence of the export customs declaration, transport movement, and receipt of foreign currency or settlement in accordance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange rules. The export sales proceeds must be repatriated into China through designated foreign exchange channels or justified under the SAFE trade settlement records. The export zero-rating applies to the merchandise sold if the WFOE is the exporter of record and all statutory documentation is complete. Concurrently, the WFOE may claim refund or credit for input VAT embedded in purchases of goods and services used in the export trade.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口货物报关单) issued by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment from a Chinese customs port to the Taiwan destination or other foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the WFOE to the overseas purchaser, clearly stating the export goods, unit prices, total price, and 0% VAT treatment
  • Packing List matching the commercial invoice and customs declaration
  • Export Contract or Purchase Order between the WFOE and the overseas buyer
  • Foreign Exchange Loan/Proceeds Collection Certificate or Bank Receipt issued by a Chinese designated bank evidencing receipt of export payment through lawful cross-border settlement channels
  • Customs clearance release notice and customs tax payment or exemption documents, if applicable
  • Export VAT refund declaration and related STA export tax rebate filing evidence (submitted via the electronic tax system after customs export data cross-matches)
  • Transportation documents, including forwarders' cargo receipt or carrier certificate where a multimodal move is used

China mandates the issuance of electronic invoices through the State Taxation Administration's unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform and the fully digitalized electronic invoice system. VAT special invoices and ordinary invoices must be issued using the national tax-controlled system with real-time data transmission. The transaction data, invoice number, amount, VAT rate, and taxpayer identification are cleared and verified through the STA's digital anti-fraud stamping and invoice verification system; the system assigns a unique invoice code and streams the fiscal data to the tax authority in near real time. No private or non-official invoicing software may issue legally valid VAT invoices.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict post-clearance audits by the State Taxation Administration and the General Administration of Customs. If the WFOE fails to substantiate the physical export, misdeclares the HS classification, or does not repatriate export proceeds within the foreign exchange regulatory deadline, the 0% VAT treatment is reversed. The tax authority will assess output VAT at 13% retroactively, impose a daily late payment surcharge of 0.05% of the unpaid tax, and may impose administrative fines from 50% to five times the amount of tax evaded. Fraudulent export tax refund behavior can result in refusal of refunds, revocation of export tax rebate eligibility, and criminal liability under the Criminal Law for the legal representative. Customs penalties and the collection of unverified VAT amounts may compound with civil clawback and foreign-exchange penalties under SAFE regulations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed dual-account foreign exchange convertibility system. Current account transactions, representing cross-border goods trade in SSD and CPU chips with Taiwan, are broadly convertible through designated domestic banks after genuine-trade verification. The WFOE may receive export proceeds in USD or RMB and can pay imports using the retained foreign currency, provided the customs and SAFE trade verification data base matches invoice, shipping, and contract documents. On the capital account side, convertibility is restricted and controlled: foreign equity injections from the Taiwanese parent must be registered with SAFE under the Foreign Direct Investment negative list, booked as an equity investment or external debt, and converted via a formal foreign exchange capital account. Profit remittance from the WFOE to the Taiwan shareholder is a capital item and requires a post-audit distributable profit, a tax clearance from the Chinese State Taxation Administration, and proof of reserve allocation; dividends are freely remittable thereafter but subject to 10% corporate income tax withholding in the absence of a cross-strait tax treaty, and full disclosure of the ultimate beneficial owner is demanded. Since 2012, SAFE has removed compulsory surrender of export proceeds, but the funds must be repatriated within the statutory documentary reconciliation period and cannot remain offshore without a special trade-plant reason. In practice, frequent regulatory barriers arise where the authenticity of the underlying trade or the final destination of the semiconductor goods is unclear; banks will apply strict trade-based anti-money-laundering checks and may reject or suspend any remittance that lacks a complete chain of evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As of the current SAFE centralised trade settlement regime, there is no statutory mandate to convert all export proceeds into RMB; the enterprise may elect to retain foreign exchange in its foreign exchange settlement account or convert it at will. However, export proceeds must be repatriated into China through the designated bank and be verified against the customs export declaration within the usual 90-calendar-day trade term (extendable under documentary credit or consignment arrangements). Any proceeds not repatriated within the agreed payment term must be justified to SAFE and, if necessary, the enterprise must apply for a foreign exchange delay filing. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero; the only requirement is that conversion, if made, must be in the name of the WFOE and must be reported under the actual trade purpose code.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Foreign fintech EMIs, including Hong Kong-licensed SMEs and Europe-based digital banks such as Airwallex or Currenxie, are not allowed to open domestic RMB or foreign currency settlement accounts for Chinese WFOEs without an approved payment institution licence from the People's Bank of China and the CBIRC. They can only act as unlicensed foreign payment service providers facilitating collection outside mainland China; they cannot channel imported capital, verify trade declarations, or process RMB-clearing payments inside China. Consequently, the formation treasury should treat EMIs as low-viability alternatives for the WFOE's actual account structure. If the corporate treasury seeks to support non-residential expense accounts for the Taiwan parent or overseas vendors, an EMI is permissible only for external FX deposits and must be backed by a regulated MSB licence in the relevant overseas jurisdiction; it still cannot replace a domestic Tier-1 bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For injecting initial paid-in capital from Taiwan to the CN WFOE, the Taiwan shareholder first obtains a capital verification through the issuance of a RMB or foreign exchange capital account after SAFE registration of the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) agreement. The transfer should be executed via SWIFT MT103 using the SAFE FX declaration purpose code applicable to inward direct investment. The remittance instruction must reference the Investment File Number issued by SAFE; the beneficiary is the FX capital account of the WFOE at a designated Tier-1 bank. After arrival, the WFOE instructs the bank to encumber the funds into RMB based on a capital-payment purpose declaration, and a CPA then issues an independent capital verification report confirming the subscribed capital has been fully paid before the commercial registry recordal is updated.Trade payments for the procurement of SSDs and processors shall be primarily settled through SWIFT MT103 from the WFOE's current foreign exchange account, or through a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) issued by the Chinese bank, using documentary collection (D/P or D/A) for transferable trade. The bank will require a completed Application for Cross-Border Trade Payment, a proforma or final invoice, the sales contract, customs declaration for imports if already cleared, and a bill of lading or air waybill. For RMB settlement with counterparties within China, the clearing rails are through the RMB cross-border interbank payment system (CIPS) or China National Clearing Centre (CNAPS) with actual funds settled via CNAPS. For USD or other foreign-currency payments, the domestic bank will debit a foreign exchange settlement account after verifying the authenticity of the underlying trade; the WFOE is not allowed to pay commission to uninvoiced third parties without evidence of the incentive service, which is a TBML red flag.Bona fide rolling forward contracts with a domestic designated bank for genuine trade-backed exposure in USD/RMB and EUR/RMB, based on verified underlying contracts.Cross-currency swaps including interest-rate swaps on eligible external debt service where the principal amount remains outstanding and the derivative transaction is registered with SAFE.Currency options (both call and put) on offshore or onshore underlying cash flows, but only with the trade or capital account background; NDFs are not permissible for the onshore WFOE, although the Taiwan parent may use offshore NDFs outside mainland jurisdiction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million aggregate annual related-party tangible property transactions for mandatory Local File preparation; RMB 100 million for related-party intangible property or financial asset transfers; RMB 40 million for related-party service transactions. The annual related-party transaction declaration applies separately, and the Master File / CbCR thresholds may trigger at group consolidated revenue of RMB 2 billion / RMB 5.5 billion respectively.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply Gulf Cooperation Council-style Economic Substance Regulations, but PRC tax law applies substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, and reasonable commercial purpose doctrines. The Taiwan-parented WFOE must demonstrate that it employs substantive local staff, maintains decision-making authority and physical premises in China, bears meaningful wholesale inventory and customer-credit risk, and performs the core income-generating activities (CIGA) associated with SSD and CPU distribution. A passive or paper WFOE with no local business reality and no economic substance will be treated as a conduit or shell for treaty and GAAR purposes, with transactions recharacterized and profit reallocated to the ultimate economic owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Profit Shifting / Missing Contemporaneous Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 and Article 44, the competent tax authority may make a special tax adjustment to the WFOE's taxable income, impute an arm's length profit, and levy interest plus a 5% late-payment surcharge on the adjusted tax. Under the Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63, intentional understatement of taxable income attracts a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax and criminal liability where the amount is significant.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors and the legal representative may be held jointly and severally liable for the entity's tax shortfall where they directed or knowingly benefited from the pricing scheme. They may be placed on the Serious Dishonest Taxpayer blacklist, triggering exit bans and restrictions on high consumption, and may face criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment of up to 7 years for serious tax evasion.
Tax Evasion Through Artificial Recharacterization of Dividends as Fees, Interest, or Royalties

Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authority has the power to recharacterize the payments as dividends under the general anti-avoidance rule in Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the special anti-abuse rules for treaty benefits under State Taxation Administration Announcement 2015 No. 60. The WFOE, as withholding agent, will be ordered to pay the under-withheld 10% withholding tax, daily surcharges, and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded; fraudulent TRC or beneficial-ownership filings carry debtor's imprisonment risk under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 205.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors who approved false agreements, fabricated invoices, or directed the round-tripping of funds face personal civil restitution, a travel ban, blacklisting by the tax administration, and criminal liability as principals under PRC Criminal Law Article 201. If foreign-exchange controls are circumvented, additional liability under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange anti-money-laundering regime may include asset freezing and personal fines.
Shell Company Structure, False VAT Invoicing, and Money Laundering in Semiconductor Trade

Statutory sanction: Issuing false Special VAT Invoices violates PRC Criminal Law Article 205 and is punishable, in serious cases, by life imprisonment and property forfeiture. The tax authority will impose monetary penalties, deny VAT deduction and export refunds, disallow all related deductions, and assess corporate income tax plus late-payment surcharge and penalties. Under the AML framework, the suspicious Swift/TT and escrow payments may be reported to the Financial Intelligence Unit, triggering account freezing and forfeiture proceedings.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors and beneficial owners who forged trade documents, controlled shell bank accounts, or directed the false invoicing scheme are personally liable for restitution of the tax and customs duties defrauded. They face criminal imprisonment, travel bans, entry restrictions on cross-strait movement, blacklisting as seriously dishonest persons, and joint and several civil liability to creditors, the tax authority, and the customs authority.

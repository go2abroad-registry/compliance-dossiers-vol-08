Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally not permissible as the sole registered address for a WFOE engaged in the freight-forwarding and cargo-charter brokerage sector. A physical office space in the specific city-level market-regulation district is required to obtain the business license, because the tax authority (State Taxation Administration local office) and the applicable postal/courier license regulator treat a mere commercial mailbox as lacking the actual premises needed for operational supervision, invoice control, and unannounced inspections. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Taiwan has no general statutory minimum paid-up capital for a limited company. The paid-in capital must be sufficient for the proposed business and commencement costs. Capital must be remitted through an approved Taiwan bank account, verified by a Taiwan-certified public accountant, and contributed before or in connection with company registration. No general statutory escrow account is required, but the bank remittance account and capital-verification procedure must be maintained. The stated fee is an approximate USD equivalent of the standard NT$700 company-registration filing fee and excludes investment approval, notarization, certification, professional and sector-licensing costs.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (official portal: www.chinatax.gov.cn); day-to-day registration and compliance are administered by the local competent tax bureau (provincial/municipal) where the WFOE is domiciled, predominantly through the national Electronic Tax Bureau online platform (etax.chinatax.gov.cn). The General Administration of Customs and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange are auxiliary authorities for export zero-rating and foreign-exchange verification.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the WFOE with the local Market Regulation Administration and obtain the business license bearing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the sole statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN). (2) Complete tax registration (initial filing of entity status) through the Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days after the business license issuance date, supplying constitutional documents, shareholder/identity records, registered address, bank details, and accounting-policy declarations. (3) Determine VAT status: apply for registration as a general VAT taxpayer if projected annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; otherwise remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer subject to the simplified levy method. (4) Enrol in the electronic invoice system, obtain an allocation of VAT invoice quotas and maximum per-invoice amount, and subscribe to the fully digitalized electronic invoice service. (5) Open a foreign-currency settlement account with a licensed bank and complete any foreign-exchange controls registration required for foreign-invested enterprises; for exports, the exporter must connect to the customs single window and SAFE monitoring systems. (6) A foreign (including Taiwan-connected) investor with no permanent establishment in China is not required to secure a TIN for investment-holding purposes; if it receives China-source income, the WFOE must withhold tax and remit it to the tax authority under the paying-entity withholding mechanism.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope. The WFOE is a PRC tax resident enterprise by incorporation, taxable on worldwide income at the standard 25% Corporate Income Tax rate. It is also a VAT taxable person in its own right; dividends and interest paid to a Taiwan-resident investor are China-source income subject to withholding at the general 10% rate, subject to statutory reductions or exemptions applicable to qualifying Taiwanese investment vehicles. The WFOE is not eligible for pass-through or partnership taxation; there is no tax-transparent status for limited liability companies under PRC law.

Available election forms

PRC law does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box entity classification election. Relevant statutory elections for the indirect-tax identity of the WFOE are: (a) election to be a general VAT taxpayer despite having taxable sales below RMB 5,000,000, enabling input-VAT crediting at standard rates (6%, 9% or 13%) instead of the small-scale simplified levy; (b) election to apply VAT exemption (with non-creditable input VAT) or zero-rating (with refundable input VAT) for qualifying cross-border international transportation services and international freight forwarding agency services; (c) election of the refund method under the zero-rating regime, whereby retained input VAT attributable to the zero-rated cross-border turnover may be refunded; and (d) for intercompany financing, no hybrid-mismatch election is available; the CIT thin-capitalisation and CFC rules override debt-equity mixes, and a formal anti-hybrid notification may need to be filed for arrangements involving Taiwanese intermediate holding companies if bilateral treaty or arrangement benefits are sought.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales (the VAT general taxpayer registration threshold). All established enterprises must complete basic tax registration within 30 days of business licence issuance regardless of turnover. A foreign enterprise providing services into China through an establishment or where economic nexus is established is also required to register; in the absence of registration, the Chinese recipient (the WFOE) must self-withhold and pay VAT on imported services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate (with entitlement to a refund of retained input VAT) applies to international transportation services for export cargo performed between a Chinese port or airport and a foreign destination. The air-cargo charter broker qualifies for zero-rating only when it acts as the principal contracting carrier issuing the air waybill in its own name and assuming transport liability for the international leg. Where the WFOE acts strictly as an intermediary arranging the charter for a third party, the service is classified as international freight forwarding agency service and is VAT-exempt (not zero-rated), with input VAT on the exempt portion not creditable. Exemption and zero-rating eligibility is evidenced by the broker's cross-border qualification filing, and by the fact that the service recipient or the transport performance occurs outside China; the domestic-leg carriage and any domestic value-added service remain taxable at 9% or 6%. The claim period and documentation requirements follow the export-tax-refund procedures administered jointly by the tax authority and customs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Goods Export Declaration from the customs single window) showing the exporter name, the WFOE, cargo description, quantity, value, port or airport of loading and destination, and the customs reference number;
  • Air Waybill / Bill of Lading (master and house air waybills) evidencing the charter flight number, origin and destination, consignor and consignee, total gross weight and cargo description;
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign-exchange bank settlement voucher) proving receipt of the charter freight or service fee in foreign currency from a non-resident payer, matched to the declared invoice;
  • Signed charter party agreement or air-cargo service contract specifying the route, aircraft details, charter rate, payment terms and the parties' obligations;
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued in the name of the WFOE;
  • Customs clearance and proof-of-export records, together with the electronic cargo manifest extracted from the customs information system;
  • Foreign-exchange verification record from the State Administration of Foreign Exchange monitoring platform confirming repatriation of the export proceeds.

China operates a real-time fiscal digitalisation mandate under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. The WFOE must issue invoices through the national fully digitalized electronic invoice service, which transmits every invoice to the tax authority in real time at issuance and assigns a unique digital serial number; there is no physical pre-printing or analogue stamping, and the tax authority performs automated digital clearance by cross-checking invoice data against the customs single-window export declarations, banking settlement records and CIT/VAT returns. API integration is required for enterprises above a designated scale: the e-invoice platform exposes machine-readable interfaces for direct issuance, red-letter reversals and bulk rejection, and all entries are reflected in the taxpayer's electronic ledger accessible to tax inspectors in near real time.

The tax authority, customs and SAFE cooperate in a one-stop audit file for every export refund and zero-rating claim. Detection of a false export declaration, mismatch between invoice data and customs or bank records, or failure to repatriate foreign currency proceeds within the 180-day statutory deadline results in retrospective denial of the zero rate, recomputation of VAT at the full standard rate (9% or 6% depending on the service segment), daily late-payment interest at 0.05% from the original due date, and disallowance of all related input-VAT credits. Under Articles 63 and 66 of the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law and Article 204 of the Criminal Law, fraudulent export refund or zero-rating claims attract fines of 50% up to 500% of the wrongfully refunded amount, confiscation of illegal gains, cancellation of export-refund eligibility for up to three years, blacklisting as a serious untrustworthy taxpayer (with restrictions on export clearance, bank credit and legal representative movement), and criminal prosecution for the crimes of fraudulent export tax refund or issuance of false VAT invoices, punishable by imprisonment ranging from three years to life imprisonment for aggravated amounts (typically above RMB 2.5 million). The clawback look-back for fraud extends to the full statutory ten-year assessment period, and the triad of administrative, customs and foreign-exchange penalties may be applied cumulatively.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is current-account convertible; capital account transactions require case-by-case registration/approval. A WFOE engaged in air cargo charter brokerage may freely convert RMB for current account items such as service fees, commissions, and operational disbursements. Profit remittance to the Taiwanese parent requires verification of tax clearance, audited financial statements, and foreign exchange registration; capital account repatriation such as equity divestment or liquidation proceeds requires SAFE registration, settlement via a domestic custodian bank, and compliance with circular 38 on offshore indirect transfers where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China eliminated mandatory export proceeds surrender requirements; enterprises may retain export receipts in foreign currency or convert them at market rate via their designated bank. Export proceeds must be repatriated to China and recorded in the trade monitoring system within the statutory period, generally 30 days for advance receipts and 90 days for deferred payment terms, with reporting and verification through SAFE's digital platform for cross-border receipts and payments.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign currency capital is remitted to the WFOE's foreign currency capital account (资本金账户) opened with a domestic designated bank, followed by RMB conversion through a safe-designated bank under the foreign investment registration system; the funds are then transferred into a RMB basic deposit account with a clearly stated payment purpose such as 'equity investment' or 'paid-in capital'. All remittances must be declared under SAFE's foreign exchange receipt/payment code system (e.g. code 622 for incorporation capital contributions or 620 for acquisition of equity) and supported by the FDI registration confirmation, board capital call resolution, and a CPA capital verification report. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% profit-seeking enterprise income tax in Taiwan; a separate 5% undistributed earnings tax may apply to retained earnings distributed or otherwise used after the statutory assessment period.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends paid by a Taiwan company to a non-resident shareholder, subject to any applicable tax agreement, statutory exemption, or approved relief. Dividends paid to a Taiwan resident company are generally subject to different domestic rules and should not be treated as non-resident dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the annual return and final tax payment are generally due from May 1 through May 31 of the following year. Provisional income tax is generally filed and paid from September 1 through September 30 of the same year. A taxpayer with an approved non-calendar fiscal year generally files within five months after fiscal year-end. Tax authorities may require liquidation, cessation, merger, or dissolution returns within the applicable statutory period.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, provisional filing and payment are generally due from September 1 through September 30. The amount is commonly based on the prior year's assessed tax or the current year's estimated income, subject to statutory method and exemption rules.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Withholding tax is generally remitted within the statutory period following payment or maturity of the income. Annual withholding statements and recipient certificates are generally due by the end of January following the relevant calendar year, subject to the payment type and applicable exception.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan transfer-pricing rules generally require a Local File when annual controlled transactions exceed the applicable statutory thresholds, commonly NTD 200 million for the relevant reporting year, subject to transaction-category thresholds and exemptions. A Master File is generally required for a Taiwan entity belonging to a multinational enterprise group with consolidated annual revenue of at least NTD 3 billion, subject to the Taiwan filing rules and applicable exceptions. Country-by-Country reporting may apply where the ultimate parent or surrogate filing entity meets the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold, generally EUR 750 million, and the Taiwan reporting conditions are satisfied.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a standalone OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant under Taiwan general anti-avoidance principles, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, permanent-establishment rules, source taxation, controlled foreign company rules, and restrictions on deductions. The Taiwan entity should maintain local personnel, decision-making authority, premises, operational systems, commercial risk, customer and carrier functions, and contemporaneous evidence demonstrating that charter brokerage activity is actually performed in Taiwan. A pass-through entity with no functions, assets, risks, or independent commercial purpose is vulnerable to income reallocation, denial of deductions, withholding reassessment, and anti-avoidance challenge.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and omitted Taiwan-source income

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, late-payment surcharge, interest, administrative fines, denial of deductions, tax-loss disallowance, compulsory collection, and possible criminal prosecution for intentional tax evasion. Criminal consequences depend on the amount, intent, evidence, and applicable Taiwan tax statute.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face criminal liability, fines, civil restitution, tax collection measures, asset enforcement, and possible restrictions on departure from Taiwan where legally ordered. Limited liability does not protect a director who personally participated in fraud, falsification, concealment, or unlawful distributions.
False invoices, sham expenses, and commercial-document fraud

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of costs and input VAT, reassessed income tax and VAT, fines, invoice-related sanctions, business restrictions, and criminal prosecution for document fraud or tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, accountants, and beneficial owners who knowingly authorize or use false records may be prosecuted personally and may incur civil restitution, professional disqualification, asset seizure, and departure restrictions.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustment to arm's-length income, denial of deductions, additional income tax, interest, penalties, documentation fines, and possible withholding reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal exposure where the arrangement involves deliberate deception, unlawful distributions, breach of fiduciary duty, or knowing submission of false tax information. Ordinary good-faith pricing disagreement generally does not by itself pierce limited liability.
Illegal profit repatriation and unlawful dividend distribution

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unlawful distributions, shareholder and director liability, tax reassessment, withholding tax, interest, administrative fines, and possible criminal consequences for fraudulent or unauthorized distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore distributed amounts, compensate the company or creditors, and respond personally to insolvency or fiduciary-duty claims. Departure restrictions or imprisonment require a specific statutory basis and competent-authority order.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: Payer liability for unpaid withholding tax, surcharges, interest, administrative fines, denial of treaty relief, recipient certificate failures, and possible criminal exposure for intentional concealment.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers may be liable for deliberate or grossly negligent withholding failures and may face civil recovery, criminal prosecution, asset enforcement, and departure restrictions where statutory conditions are met.
Permanent-establishment and fixed-place-of-business concealment

Statutory sanction: Assessment of Taiwan business income, withholding and VAT liabilities where applicable, penalties, interest, denial of treaty protection, and anti-avoidance adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers may incur personal liability for intentional misrepresentation, sham arrangements, or fraudulent tax reporting. Mere non-resident status does not shield persons directing a Taiwan business operation.
Unlicensed air-freight forwarding, charter brokerage, or logistics activity

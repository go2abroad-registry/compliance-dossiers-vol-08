Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic portal: e-Tax (https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) established in China, the entity automatically obtains a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) upon registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). This USCC serves as the Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all tax purposes. The WFOE must complete tax registration with the local tax authority within 30 days of business registration, submitting the business license, articles of association, and legal representative identification. The tax authority issues a Tax Registration Certificate (now integrated into the USCC) and assigns a tax payer classification (general or small-scale). For foreign non-resident entities without a permanent establishment, a separate TIN may be required for withholding tax purposes, obtained via the non-resident tax registration process through the same e-Tax portal.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods (including ophthalmic eyewear frames) are eligible for VAT zero-rating under Article 15 of the VAT Law, provided the goods physically leave China and are sold to a foreign buyer. The exporter must be a registered general taxpayer and hold valid export contracts. Services and intangible assets are generally not zero-rated unless specifically listed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) - Form 1 (Customs Declaration for Export Goods)
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill) evidencing shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Receipt Voucher from the bank
  • Export Invoice and Packing List
  • Sales Contract with the overseas buyer
  • Export VAT Refund Application Form (Form 2) submitted via the e-Tax portal

China mandates the use of the Golden Tax System (Phase III) for all VAT invoices. Invoices must be issued through the official e-invoicing platform, with real-time transmission to the STA's central database. The system requires API integration for automated invoice issuance and validation. Anti-fraud measures include digital stamping with a unique invoice code and QR code, and mandatory reconciliation of invoice data with VAT returns. Non-compliance results in fines and suspension of invoicing privileges.

The STA conducts rigorous audits of export refund claims. Any false declaration or failure to meet documentation requirements results in denial of the refund, recovery of previously refunded VAT, and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded. Criminal liability may arise for fraudulent export refunds under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, with imprisonment up to 10 years. Additionally, the exporter's VAT registration may be revoked, and the entity blacklisted from future export refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a managed dual-track foreign exchange regime under which the Renminbi is not freely convertible on the capital account. Current account items, including the wholesale export of ophthalmic eyewear frames, are broadly liberalised; onshore banks may settle receipts and payments on the basis of verified trade documents and the SAFE trade monitoring system. Capital account items, however, require a dedicated FDI foreign exchange registration, an onshore FDI capital settlement account for injection of equity, and strict supervisory follow-up after each use of funds. Export proceeds must be repatriated to China, after which the WFOE can either retain them in a current account settlement account or convert them into Renminbi; no mandatory surrender ratio applies. Profit remittance to the Taiwan parent is permitted after the WFOE has completed its annual statutory audit, settled corporate income tax and any at-source dividend withholding, and submitted board minutes, audited financial statements, tax payment proof and the FDI registration file to the remitting bank. The standard 10% dividend withholding can be reduced under the Cross-Strait Taxation Agreement if the Taiwan parent supplies a valid tax residency certificate. The regime is designed to block speculative cross-border arbitrage and offshore shell financing while supporting genuine trade and direct investment, subject to daily banking true-transaction scrutiny.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China has abolished mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts. Exporters must repatriate foreign currency proceeds into China and, within the general trade-reporting benchmark of 180 days from export customs declaration, either retain the funds in a foreign exchange settlement account at an onshore bank or convert them to Renminbi at market rate. A 0% surrender ratio therefore applies; the obligation is repatriation, not conversion. The supervising bank matches export receipts with customs declaration data; overdue or unreturned proceeds require written explanation and can trigger SAFE administrative investigation.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable. Foreign digital EMI banks and challenger neobanks are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open domestic Renminbi corporate accounts, execute SAFE registrations, provide RMB clearing accounts, or access CIPS membership for the WFOE. Using an unlicensed EMI for core treasury operations in mainland China is legally impermissible and would create Banking Law, foreign exchange control, and cash-flow integrity risks. The WFOE should run all cross-border and RMB clearing through licensed onshore commercial banks; fintech platforms can only be used as supplementary expense-management tools where the actual settlement still occurs at a licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

From the Taiwan parent to the WFOE, the wire must be sent by SWIFT MT103 from the parent's account in Taiwan to the WFOE's SAFE-registered FDI Capital Settlement Account at the onshore Tier-1 bank. Before receipt, the WFOE must complete FDI foreign exchange registration with the bank, file any required FDI contract approval or negative-list declaration, and record the remittance purpose as Investment in WFOE registered capital. Upon funds arrival, the bank will match the remittance with the capital payment confirmation letter, require a certified Chinese public accountant to issue a capital verification report, and then allow the RMB settlement of the foreign exchange capital for permitted business expenses under the negative list; each settlement and use of funds must remain traceable and reported through SAFE's foreign exchange information system.For trade settlements, the WFOE should clear using SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments, commercial letters of credit executed through the bank's trade finance desk for larger or first-time counterparties, and documentary collections such as D/P and D/A for low-to-medium risk transactions. RMB cross-border trade settlement should use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) via an onshore licensed bank; foreign currency payments require underlying trade documents and matching under SAFE's trade verification framework.

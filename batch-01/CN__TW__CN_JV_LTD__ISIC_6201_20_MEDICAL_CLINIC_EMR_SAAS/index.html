Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a Taiwan tax-resident company limited by shares, subject to 20% corporate income tax on worldwide income; no pass-through or fiscal-transparent election is recognized for this entity type. Territorial_Scope — resident companies are taxable on worldwide profits; EMR SaaS foreign service income is assessable for CIT but zero-rated for VAT when used abroad; retained earnings undistributed are subject to an additional 5% entity-level retained earnings surtax under Article 21-1 of the Income Tax Act. Tax_Exempt_Zone — not applicable; the JV is a standard mainland-jurisdiction software company and cannot claim export processing zone, free trade zone, or offshore company concessions.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Taiwan for a locally incorporated limited company. The available statutory elections relevant to this JV include: (i) the R&D expenditure tax-credit election under Article 10 of the Statute for Industrial Innovation (15% current-year credit capped at 30% of income tax payable, or an alternative 10% multi-year uncapped credit option); (ii) the foreign income tax credit election under Article 3 of the Income Tax Act for foreign-source EMR SaaS revenue; (iii) the zero-rated export invoice election under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act requiring documentary substantiation; (iv) the election to issue cloud e-invoices via the Turnkey automated transmission or certified value-added center API; and (v) no elective CFC/PEM arrangement is permitted — Articles 43-3 and 43-4 of the Income Tax Act mandate attribution/recharacterization of offshore controlled subsidiaries and offshore fixed places of business, supplemented by the substance-over-form clause of Article 12-1 of the Tax Collection Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NT$0 for limited liability corporations — the JV must complete tax registration and Business Tax (VAT) registration within 15 days of commencement, regardless of revenue; only unincorporated micro-operators selling services with average monthly turnover below NT$40,000 (or goods below NT$80,000) may be exempted from full VAT-invoice issuance and are instead taxed under the special 1% small-business operator regime; a corporate EMR SaaS exporter cannot claim this de minimis regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7, Item 2 of the Business Tax Act, the EMR SaaS service supplied by the Taiwan JV qualifies for the 0% VAT rate as a service provided in the Taiwan area but used abroad, provided: (i) the recipient is a foreign-located medical clinic or foreign enterprise; (ii) the contract, governing law, and performance place evidence offshore consumption and the overseas clinic's actual use of the EMR workflow; (iii) subscriptions are invoiced to the foreign entity and collected in foreign currency; (iv) the foreign exchange proceeds are remitted to Taiwan and evidenced by an FX settlement certificate; (v) the SaaS is not substantially re-supplied or used for domestic Taiwan medical operations; and (vi) all zero-rated sales are separately reported on the bimonthly VAT return with an export document set retained. Pure software-as-a-service exports do not require a customs goods declaration, but any associated hardware/dongle shipments require a standard customs export declaration.

  • Written EMR SaaS subscription agreement and purchase order evidencing offshore delivery, governing law, place of use, and payment schedule
  • Foreign exchange earnings certificate / inward remittance certificate (出口外匯收入水單) issued by an authorized Taiwan foreign-exchange bank tied to the specific export invoice

Taiwan mandates Uniform Invoice (統一發票) issuance for all VAT-registered enterprises. The JV must adopt the MOF e-Invoice Integration Platform by transmitting e-invoice XML data through the Turnkey endpoint or a certified Value-Added Center. B2B and B2C cloud-invoice data must be transmitted to the platform within 48 hours of issuance; the platform validates the invoice serial numbers and, for B2C consumer invoices, auto-generates a two-digit random code. Zero-rated export invoices are identified by a special export/zero-rating code section and do not undergo the same domestic consumer clearance, but remain subject to the bi-monthly consolidated VAT return and export documentation review requirement.

Zero-rating claims are audited by the National Taxation Bureau with heightened scrutiny on related-party offshore clinics, repeated refund claims exceeding input tax balances, invoice-remittance mismatches, and insufficient place-of-use evidence. Where zero-rating is disallowed or refunds are obtained by misrepresentation, the tax authority reverses the refund, assesses the underpaid 5% VAT with interest, and imposes an administrative penalty of up to five times the evaded tax under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act; deliberate fraud triggers criminal liability under Article 41 (imprisonment up to five years) with responsible corporate officers personally liable under Article 47. The full audit window for fraudulent evasion is seven years under Article 21 of the Tax Collection Act, and failure to remit export proceeds within the 180-day foreign-exchange deadline automatically shifts the supply to domestic 5% taxation with a delayed payment surcharge of 1% for every two days overdue, capped at 15% of the assessed tax.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is generally convertible for genuine current-account transactions, including bona fide trade in goods and services, subject to bank-level authenticity, anti-money-laundering, tax, customs, and transaction-document reviews. Capital-account convertibility is controlled rather than unrestricted. Equity capital contributions, shareholder loans, dividends, liquidation proceeds, intercompany financing, and other capital transactions require the relevant investment registration, foreign-exchange registration, bank verification, and permitted-use analysis. A foreign-invested enterprise may normally open foreign-exchange capital accounts and settle or use capital funds through an authorized bank, but funds must be used for permitted business purposes and may not be used for prohibited securities investment, real-estate speculation, direct lending to unrelated parties, or other restricted purposes. Profit remittance generally requires audited financial statements, board or shareholder approval of distributable profits, tax clearance or evidence of tax compliance, statutory reserve allocation, and bank review of the dividend resolution, tax documentation, and underlying financial records. Dividends cannot be remitted from accumulated losses or before legally required reserves and tax obligations are addressed. Cross-border payments involving a Taiwan-resident shareholder require additional review of investment legality, beneficial ownership, permitted business scope, cross-strait transaction restrictions, and the relevant Taiwan-side remittance controls. The medical EMR SaaS activity also requires assessment of data-localization, personal-information, cybersecurity, telecommunications, and health-sector licensing obligations before a bank will accept a foreign-related payment purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mainland China does not impose a general statutory requirement that all export proceeds be surrendered to renminbi at a fixed mandatory ratio. Export receipts may generally be retained in foreign currency in permitted current-account arrangements or settled into renminbi at the enterprise's discretion, subject to trade authenticity review, customs and foreign-exchange reporting, bank monitoring, and applicable foreign-exchange risk controls. There is no universal fixed statutory conversion percentage or blanket same-day conversion deadline for ordinary export receipts. The enterprise must ensure that export proceeds are collected through an authorized bank, properly matched to customs and trade documentation, and reported or corrected through the foreign-exchange monitoring system where required. Banks may require export contracts, invoices, customs declarations, bills of lading, collection records, tax records, and evidence explaining delayed, split, offset, or third-party payments. Export proceeds should not be routed through personal accounts, unrelated third parties, undisclosed offshore accounts, or artificial circular arrangements. Where receipts involve Taiwan, the bank may apply additional cross-strait documentation, counterparty, sanctions, and transaction-purpose checks. Any foreign-exchange settlement or retention treatment must be confirmed with the servicing bank because local implementation, enterprise classification, and transaction-risk status can affect operational requirements.

Central bank supervision: For a mainland China Sino-foreign equity joint venture, the principal authorities are the State Administration of Foreign Exchange, the People's Bank of China, the State Administration for Market Regulation, the Ministry of Commerce, and the relevant local branch of the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. The People's Bank of China administers monetary and payment-system matters, while the State Administration of Foreign Exchange administers foreign-exchange registration, capital-account controls, current-account administration, and cross-border remittance compliance. If the Taiwan jurisdiction represents a Taiwan-resident shareholder, Taiwan-side transactions may additionally be subject to the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan), the Financial Supervisory Commission, the Ministry of Economic Affairs, and cross-strait investment and remittance rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for collection and payment support; Low as a substitute for a mainland China operating bank. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment service provider cannot generally bypass mainland China foreign-exchange controls, bank account requirements, tax reporting, trade authenticity review, or cross-border data obligations. Offshore card acquiring and billing may be used only where the provider is licensed for the relevant services, the merchant-of-record structure is documented, customer funds are segregated as required, and the arrangement does not constitute unlicensed mainland payment clearing, deposit-taking, foreign-exchange dealing, or illegal fund transfer. Stripe or another offshore processor may face mainland availability, settlement, restricted-industry, cross-border data, chargeback, and beneficial-owner limitations. A mainland payment institution or authorized bank is normally required for domestic RMB collection. The company should maintain a bank-led settlement structure, conduct customer and counterparty screening, reconcile processor reports to invoices, and document the tax and foreign-exchange treatment of every settlement flow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The investor should remit funds from an account held in the name of the registered shareholder or approved financing entity to the joint venture's designated foreign-exchange capital account or other bank-approved capital contribution account. The remittance instruction should identify the legal entity, investment or financing purpose, currency, amount, and applicable bank purpose classification. Before receipt, the company should provide the bank with the business license, investment registration or filing evidence, capital contribution agreement, board or shareholder resolution, ownership chart, source-of-funds evidence, and any required foreign-investment and foreign-exchange registration. The bank should verify the remitter against the registered investor, confirm that the funds are not from an unidentified third party, and complete the relevant foreign-exchange reporting. Capital funds should be converted or transferred only through the designated bank and only for permitted business expenditures. Shareholder loans require separate eligibility, registration, quota or leverage analysis, loan agreements, interest and tax documentation, and transfer-pricing support. For Taiwan-linked funding, the parties should separately verify Taiwan-side outward-investment and remittance permissions and mainland cross-strait investment restrictions. The company should prohibit cash funding, personal-account funding, informal netting, undisclosed third-party settlement, and circular transfers. Each inbound transfer should be reconciled to the capitalization table, bank advice, accounting entry, and statutory filing.For genuine cross-border service, software, cloud, licensing, and trade payments, the primary rail should be an authorized-bank international transfer using SWIFT MT103 or the bank's current equivalent payment message, with complete originator, beneficiary, invoice, contract, purpose, and intermediary-bank data. The bank may require tax invoices, service agreements, delivery or acceptance evidence, withholding-tax analysis, customs records where relevant, and proof that the amount is commercially reasonable. Commercial letters of credit may be used for qualifying goods transactions, generally through an irrevocable documentary letter of credit subject to the agreed UCP rules, with document presentation, discrepancy controls, sanctions screening, and settlement through the issuing and confirming banks. Documentary collections, banker's acceptances, open-account payments, and supply-chain finance may be available subject to bank approval and trade authenticity controls. Cross-border RMB settlement may be available through authorized banks and CIPS-connected institutions where the transaction, counterparty, currency, and bank eligibility qualify. The company should maintain separate payment workflows for dividends, royalties, service fees, intercompany charges, payroll, capital expenditure, refunds, and trade settlement because each category can have different tax, foreign-exchange, and supporting-document requirements. Payments to Taiwan should be screened for counterparty eligibility, permitted purpose, exchange-control requirements, and any applicable cross-strait restrictions. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for collection and payment support; Low as a substitute for a mainland China operating bank. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment service provider cannot generally bypass mainland China foreign-exchange controls, bank account requirements, tax reporting, trade authenticity review, or cross-border data obligations. Offshore card acquiring and billing may be used only where the provider is licensed for the relevant services, the merchant-of-record structure is documented, customer funds are segregated as required, and the arrangement does not constitute unlicensed mainland payment clearing, deposit-taking, foreign-exchange dealing, or illegal fund transfer. Stripe or another offshore processor may face mainland availability, settlement, restricted-industry, cross-border data, chargeback, and beneficial-owner limitations. A mainland payment institution or authorized bank is normally required for domestic RMB collection. The company should maintain a bank-led settlement structure, conduct customer and counterparty screening, reconcile processor reports to invoices, and document the tax and foreign-exchange treatment of every settlement flow.

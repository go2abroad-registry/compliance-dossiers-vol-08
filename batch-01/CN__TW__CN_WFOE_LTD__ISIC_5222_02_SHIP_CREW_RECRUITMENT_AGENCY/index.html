Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Taiwan foreign-invested limited liability company. However, the subscribed capital must be remitted in full to a designated bank account in Taiwan and verified by a capital verification certificate before the company registration application is accepted. The bank verification statement must reflect the blocked escrow nature of the pre-incorporation capital account. For the ship crew recruitment agency license, the Maritime Bureau may require the entity to maintain a paid-up capital of at least US$100,000 or an equivalent NT$3,000,000, or to post a wage guarantee bond sufficient to cover crew salary claims.

Funding & unlock path: The office premises must obtain a Fire Safety Management Certificate from the local Fire Bureau, confirming fire extinguishers, escape routes, emergency lighting, and fire-resistant doors satisfy the Fire Services Act. A building lawful occupancy certificate and structural safety inspection record must be maintained. For occupational safety, the entity must register its workers and perform a workplace safety self-inspection under the Occupational Safety and Health Act; if the office exceeds the employment threshold, an on-site labor inspection by the Occupational Safety and Health Administration will be required, including electrical safety, ergonomics, and emergency evacuation drills.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Taiwan foreign-invested limited liability company. However, the subscribed capital must be remitted in full to a designated bank account in Taiwan and verified by a capital verification certificate before the company registration application is accepted. The bank verification statement must reflect the blocked escrow nature of the pre-incorporation capital account. For the ship crew recruitment agency license, the Maritime Bureau may require the entity to maintain a paid-up capital of at least US$100,000 or an equivalent NT$3,000,000, or to post a wage guarantee bond sufficient to cover crew salary claims.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, accessible via the electronic tax portal 'New Electronic Taxation Bureau of China' (Xin Dian Zi Shui Wu Ju); for cross-province and cross-border matters, the portal integrates with the 'International Tax Service Platform' of the STA.

E-government filing portal

1. A foreign non-resident enterprise establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in China must first complete incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain a unified social credit code (USCC), which functions as the primary Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all tax filings. 2. Following SAMR registration, the WFOE must complete 'tax registration information confirmation' (shui wu deng ji xin xi que ren) against the USCC within 30 days by submitting to the competent local branch of the STA through the New Electronic Taxation Bureau portal; required data include the foreign investor's home-country TIN or equivalent, articles of association, M&A/ownership chart, legal representative passport copy, registered address proof, business scope, and banking details. 3. The STA will issue an electronic 'Taxpayer Registration Notice' evidencing the TIN and will assign a local competent tax authority office; the WFOE must also complete an 'Accounting and Bookkeeping Filing' (cai wu kuai ji zhi du bei an) and payroll/employer registration for individual income tax (ge ren suo de shui) on the same portal. 4. For VAT purposes, the WFOE must register as a small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer; general VAT taxpayer status is mandatory once annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5 million, or voluntarily elected. 5. Where the WFOE is also considered a non-resident enterprise with income derived within China, a separate 'Non-Resident Enterprise Registration' (fei ju min qi ye deng ji) may be made for withholding purposes, but this does not replace the domestic WFOE TIN registration. 6. All registrations are processed online, and the USCC/TIN is immediately validated for e-invoicing, customs declaration, export rebate, foreign exchange settlement, and social-security contribution interfaces.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not provide check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity classification elections for domestic WFOEs. The WFOE is a resident enterprise taxable on worldwide income at standard CIT rates and is not eligible for pass-through treatment. The only available election of relevance is the small-scale vs. general VAT taxpayer election, and residual hybrid mismatch rules in China's general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) permit downward-structuring adjustments but do not constitute an elective classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5 million annual taxable turnover for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; however, all WFOEs must complete basic VAT registration upon incorporation. Small-scale taxpayer status applies to turnover not exceeding RMB 5 million and allows a simplified 3% collection rate (reduced to 1% under certain preferential periods), but cross-border zero-rated export transactions may still be claimed by small-scale taxpayers through the entrusted-export or tax-bureau-supervision procedure. The mandatory threshold is assessed on rolling 12-month taxable sales of goods/services, excluding VAT-exempt turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Articles 15 and 25 of the Provisional Regulations on VAT and the State Taxation Administration's Announcement on Export Tax Rebates, zero-rating or export VAT refund applies to exported goods, internationally provided services, and certain cross-border services listed in the 'Zero-rated Cross-border Taxable Transactions' catalogue. For a maritime crew recruitment and crew-management WFOE, the qualifying offshore services include: (a) crew recruitment and placement services to foreign shipowners, vessel operators, or manning agents located outside China, where the service recipient is non-resident, the services are rendered entirely outside mainland China, or the services are supplied to a foreign vessel engaged in international transport; (b) international shipping agency services, including crew exchange, repatriation, and port-clearance-related administrative services, to the extent they directly relate to international voyages; (c) commissioned payroll administration and wage disbursement services for non-resident shipowners, provided the underlying crew wages are paid into crew accounts via foreign-currency wire transfers and no domestic passenger/cargo transport is involved; (d) supply of seafarer secondment to foreign-flagged vessels, provided the contract is signed with a non-resident entity and the service consumption is outside China. For services not on the statutory zero-rating list but involving non-resident recipients, zero-rating is not automatic; an exemption (VAT exemption) may apply, or 6% VAT at standard rate with no credit. The eligibility criteria require: a written export-service contract in English or Chinese, a service specification, and the STA's administrative confirmation through the 'Cross-border Taxable Transaction Declaration' (跨境应税行为免税备查) or the export-rebate declaration process for qualifying services. Export of goods by a trading WFOE is zero-rated with refund of input VAT based on the customs export declaration; the crew-agency WFOE should document any direct shipping-fee, consular-visa, or travel-expense inputs separately to ensure input credit recovery.

  • Customs Export Declaration (a.k.a. Export Goods Declaration Form) with the assigned 18-digit customs declaration number on the China International Trade Single Window; for service exports, the corresponding 'Export Service Declaration/Supervision Form' or the STA cross-border transaction record
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or the equivalent sea-waybill/Master B/L and house B/L, or for crew services, the official Crew List endorsed by the vessel's master, port clearance, and the International Maritime Labor Certificate (MLC) evidence
  • Signed offshore crew management/service agreement, itinerary/laytime schedule, and vessel identification (IMO number and flag state), plus calculation of the service consideration attributable to international shipping
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign-currency credit advice evidencing the foreign-currency wire receipt from the non-resident counterparty, with the SWIFT-reference and exchange-rate conversion record
  • VAT special invoice or e-invoice, the export contract, customs entry records, and the annual export-rebate declaration books (where physical goods are co-shipped)
  • Non-resident recipient's overseas tax registration certificate or equivalent, as required by the competent tax authority for services zero-rating
  • Accounting ledger evidencing the settlement of payroll disbursements and commission income segregation, including payroll funding wire records from the shipowner

Since December 2022, China has operated the 'Full-amount Digitalized Electronic Invoice (Totally Digital Electronic Invoice, 全面数字化的电子发票)' system, branded as the 'Digital E-Invoice' (shu zi dian piao). All general VAT taxpayers must issue VAT special invoices and VAT ordinary invoices in digital format through the New Electronic Taxation Bureau's e-invoice platform or through service providers integrated with the STA's API. Approved regions require real-time transmission and descriptive AI-powered risk-screening for each invoice. The system assigns a unique 20-digit invoice code and utilizes an OMS (One Machine System) encryption seal; physical stamping is replaced by the statutory electronic seal. Paper invoices may be used only during transition periods in certain jurisdictions, but all newly incorporated WFOEs are expected to apply for the digital e-invoice quota through the e-tax portal and acquire the necessary 'Tax Control Device/Platform' configuration. The STA operates an invoice clearance gateway where every VAT invoice is checked against the taxpayer's registration scope, blacklist databases, and unit-price tolerance; invoices for services classified as crew recruitment, manning, payroll administration, and maritime labor supply must precisely describe the service and the underlying vessel/IMO number to avoid risk flags. In addition, for wages paid on behalf of shipping clients, VAT treatment requires strict segregation of own-commission vs. disbursement/pass-through payroll charges to prevent mis-invoicing.

Misapplication of export zero-rating or utilization of false export documentation triggers the full VAT clawback of the zero-rated output tax previously excluded, plus a late-payment surcharge equal to 0.05% per day from the original due date, and an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law for fraud or attempted fraud. Where fraudulent export rebate claims are detected, the State Taxation Administration will impose a penalty of one to five times the amount fraudulently refunded, suspend export-rebate qualification for up to 24 months, blacklist the entity, refer the matter to the Public Security Bureau for criminal investigation under Article 204 of the Criminal Law (fraudulent export tax refund), and fine the legal representative and responsible finance officer. The currency-repatriation deadline for export proceeds is generally 180 days for most exports, but for certain services, foreign-exchange settlement must be completed no later than the date of filing the annual enterprise income tax reconciliation; failure to repatriate within the deadline revokes the zero-rating and converts the original export VAT into an output VAT liability. Full retrospective clawback may extend back to the first date of the erroneous zero-rating claim, with fines compounding. In addition, if the crew-management WFOE issues an e-invoice for export services without retaining the required customs/immigration documentation, it will be deemed as invoicing for a non-compliant transaction and subjected to penalties for false invoicing under the administrative rules, including disqualification of digital e-invoice quota and criminal liability for invoicing fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The CNY is convertible on the current account for legitimate trade and service transactions, including maritime crew recruitment fees and seafarer payroll; however, every payment must pass the bank's real-trade authenticity review and be matched against contracts, invoices, customs/service declarations, and tax records. Capital account inflows, such as the equity injection from the Taiwanese parent and any shareholder loans or cross-border financing, are not freely convertible and require prior SAFE registration, a valid purpose code, and a designated bank account. A WFOE must open an FDI capital account for equity injection, settle the funds under the direct settlement policy, and observe the negative list, for example prohibiting use for equity investment or entrusted loans. Profit repatriation to the Taiwan shareholder is allowed only after an annual audit, tax filing, and completion of withholding tax, with remittance supported by board resolutions, audited financial statements, and SAFE dividend remittance filings. In practice, the Taiwan parent should inject capital via USD or HKD rather than TWD because mainland banks have no direct TWD clearing lane; TWD is considered a restricted currency in cross-strait banking. The convertibility hurdle is therefore not current account convertibility, but strict capital monitoring.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a China WFOE providing crew management services to overseas shipowners, the foreign exchange receipts are treated as current account service receipts. Under SAFE rules, there is no fixed mandatory surrender ratio; the WFOE may retain the foreign currency in an approved foreign currency settlement account or convert it to CNY at any time. The statutory requirement is that all export/source proceeds must be repatriated to the onshore bank account within 180 days from the date of the underlying transaction or service completion, and the bank must record the collection in the SAFE trade monitoring system. Any deviation beyond 180 days requires a documented overdue collection explanation and may trigger a reduction of the entity's A-class FX administration rating. The purpose code for crew recruitment remittances must be the corresponding SAFE service-trade code, and supporting contracts and invoices must be retained for five years.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Currencycloud are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to open onshore CNY accounts for a PRC WFOE. They cannot be used for statutory tax, social security, or customs purposes, and cannot legally hold payroll for Chinese seafarers. The legal constraint is the PRC's Non-bank Payment Institution Supervision Measures, which reserve RMB payment and cross-border foreign exchange settlement for institutions holding a Payment Business License; offshore EMIs generally hold only Money Service Operator or Electronic Money Institution licenses in other jurisdictions. An offshore EMI may be used peripherally by the Taiwan parent for treasury visibility, but all onshore receipts and payments for the WFOE must be routed through a licensed domestic bank. Feasibility rating: Low as a primary onshore banking rail; Medium only as a supplementary cross-border collection tool when paired with a licensed domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Taiwan parent equity injection must follow SAFE's Foreign Direct Investment administration: the WFOE obtains a foreign investment record-filing with MOFCOM or the local commerce authority; opens an FDI Capital Account at a domestic bank; the Taiwan parent wires USD or HKD via SWIFT MT103 with remittance purpose 'Equity investment in WFOE' and reference to the FDI registration; the bank verifies the receipt into the capital account and records it in SAFE's Capital Account Information System; the WFOE settles the capital into CNY under the direct settlement policy on the condition that funds are used only for the registered business scope; a Chinese certified public accountant issues a capital verification report for submission to the company registry and the bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A mandatory Transfer Pricing Report (Local File) is triggered when annual related-party transactions exceed NT$20 million. The Master File requirement applies to a multinational enterprise group whose consolidated annual revenue exceeded NT$30 billion in the previous fiscal year; a Country-by-Country Report is required at group revenue above NT$27 billion. Transactions below these thresholds must still respect the arm's length principle.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rules contained in Article 12-1 of the Tax Collection Act and Article 66-8 of the Income Tax Act enable the tax authority to disregard artificial transactions lacking economic substance. A crew recruitment agency must maintain a physical office, licensed manning operations, employment contracts, crew payroll records, and insurance oversight; otherwise the entity or transaction may be recharacterized, with income imputed to the real Taiwanese owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act, conviction carries imprisonment of up to 5 years or detention. Article 110 of the Income Tax Act administratively imposes a penalty of up to 3 times the underpaid tax. The company is also liable for the evaded tax, monthly surcharges, and interest.

Director personal liability: Corporate directors and the legal representative are treated as responsible persons; if the corporate form is used to conceal the evasion, the veil is pierced and personal assets are subject to execution. Travel bans are automatically imposed for tax arrears exceeding NT$2 million, and criminal conviction triggers director disqualification.
Offshore Shell Company / Cross-Border Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard the shell, disallow its invoices as deductions, impute the income to the Taiwan entity, apply 20% corporate income tax plus a 5% retained-earnings surcharge, and impose a penalty of up to 3 times the underpaid tax. Taiwan's CFC rules may also allocate the shell's passive profits to the controlling Taiwanese shareholder.

Director personal liability: Directors approving the sham arrangement may be assessed personally as co-obligors for the unpaid corporate tax; where the shell is used to move funds disguised as service fees, responsible individuals face money-laundering liability, asset freeze, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for up to 7 years.
Unlicensed Maritime Crew Recruitment / AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed operations are subject to administrative fines of NT$100,000 to NT$500,000 per violation under the Merchant Marine Personnel Act and Employment Service Act, plus license revocation. Violations of the Money Laundering Control Act attract fines for corporations up to NT$50 million and imprisonment for individuals up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who directly participate in unlicensed remittance or payroll schemes face criminal prosecution and confiscation of personal proceeds; the corporate veil may be pierced for intentional misconduct, allowing civil restitution against personal assets. Travel bans are likewise enforceable for delinquent tax assessments arising from the illegal operation.

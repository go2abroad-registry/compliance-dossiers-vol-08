Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Taiwan branch or permanent establishment is taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act for profit-seeking enterprises on Taiwan-source income at the prevailing corporate rate, and there is no tax transparency, pass-through, or disregarded-entity treatment for a branch of a foreign equity joint venture. For business tax/VAT purposes, the Taiwan branch is a distinct VAT taxpayer obligated to charge Business Tax on taxable sales and to file periodic Business Tax returns.

Available election forms

None. Taiwan tax legislation does not provide check-the-box or hybrid entity elections analogous to the United States rules. A foreign corporation cannot elect partnership, transparency, or disregarded-entity treatment for Taiwan tax purposes; classification follows actual incorporation and permanent-establishment principles.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for a Taiwan resident or branch entity; tax registration is mandatory before taxable business activity commences. For non-resident businesses supplying cross-border electronic services or offshore trade into Taiwan, mandatory registration applies when annual Taiwan-source taxable turnover exceeds NT$480,000 (New Taiwan Dollars).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the zero-rating provisions of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act, zero rating is available where: (a) physical goods are genuinely exported from Taiwan under a Customs export declaration; (b) services are rendered in Taiwan to an offshore customer and are actually used or consumed outside Taiwan, with no direct enjoyment or consumption in Taiwan; and (c) services are directly connected with international transportation. For a Contract Research Organization serving foreign pharmaceutical sponsors, qualifying zero-rated services may include central protocol design, regulatory dossier preparation, central data management, biostatistics, medical writing, and pharmacovigilance delivered to an offshore sponsor where the benefit is realized outside Taiwan. Services tied to Taiwanese trial sites, subject-facing activities, local regulatory filings with the Taiwan Food and Drug Administration, or warehousing of investigational products in Taiwan are generally treated as used in Taiwan and remain subject to 5% Business Tax. The zero rate must be substantiated by contract, invoice, foreign exchange receipt, and evidence of offshore use.

  • Customs Export Declaration issued by Taiwan Customs in the exporter's name for any exported clinical supplies, biological specimens, investigational drugs, or trial-related goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing Taiwan as origin and the foreign sponsor or offshore consignee as destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Income Certificate issued by an authorized Taiwan bank evidencing receipt of the contract consideration from the foreign sponsor through SWIFT or wire transfer
  • Executed clinical trial master service agreement and work orders identifying the offshore sponsor, trial sites, and precise scope of CRO services
  • Commercial invoices, milestone invoices, and service completion certificates signed by the foreign client
  • Zero-rated sales list and electronic Uniform Invoice records showing the 0% tax code
  • Certificate of actual offshore consumption, sworn declaration from the foreign recipient, and any Taiwan trial authority or ethics committee approvals needed to demonstrate the place of use

A VAT-registered Taiwan business must issue Uniform Invoices for taxable sales. Where electronic Uniform Invoices are used, the taxpayer connects to the Ministry of Finance Electronic Invoice Integration Service Platform through a certified turnkey/API channel and transmits invoice data within 48 hours of issuance, storing the cloud invoice record in the central platform. Taiwan does not impose a real-time per-transaction government pre-clearance or digital-stamping protocol, but the platform validates allocation codes, sequential numbering, and data integrity, and automatic audit matching reconciles B2B and B2C invoices against the Business Tax return.

On audit, the tax authority will require all export documentation, evidence of actual physical exit for goods, offshore use for services, and proof of foreign exchange repatriation within the statutory period. If zero-rating is disallowed because documents are missing, inconsistent, or fraudulent, the transaction is recharacterized as a domestic taxable sale at 5%, the underpaid Business Tax is assessed with overdue interest, and penalty provisions of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act permit fines of one to five times the underpaid tax. Fabricated Customs declarations, false FIRCs, or fictitious offshore contracts may also trigger criminal investigation under the Tax Collection Act and the Customs Anti-Smuggling Act, with imprisonment and fines, and any input VAT previously refunded in respect of the zero-rated transaction is clawed back together with penalties. Repeated non-compliance can lead to suspension of electronic Uniform Invoice privileges and mandatory audit scrutiny of all future zero-rating claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan dollar is convertible for ordinary current-account transactions through authorized foreign-exchange banks. Taiwan does not generally require conversion of foreign-currency receipts into NTD or impose a general profit-remittance restriction. Capital-account transactions, foreign investment, securities transactions, and large or otherwise regulated foreign-exchange settlements remain subject to applicable reporting, approval, and documentation requirements. A Taiwan company generally must support dividend remittances with corporate approvals, financial statements, tax-payment evidence, and bank-required documentation. Foreign-exchange settlement declarations and annual limits apply; transactions exceeding applicable limits or requiring prior approval must be referred to the bank and, where required, the CBC. The precise limits and process must be confirmed against current CBC rules and the company's transaction classification before execution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan generally has no mandatory export-proceeds surrender ratio or general statutory deadline requiring exporters to convert receipts into NTD. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to bank KYC, transaction-purpose evidence, applicable foreign-exchange reporting, and tax and accounting requirements. The bank may request contracts, invoices, shipping or service-delivery evidence, and export declarations where applicable. This conclusion is for Taiwan and must not be applied to mainland China, where separate foreign-exchange and export-proceeds rules may apply.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC); the Ministry of Economic Affairs' Investment Commission administers relevant foreign-investment approvals, and the Financial Supervisory Commission regulates banks. The payload identifies China as the sovereign country but Taiwan as the jurisdiction. This dossier treats Taiwan as the place of incorporation and banking; it does not establish that a PRC-incorporated JV may invest in or operate in Taiwan.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a substitute for a Taiwanese corporate bank account. Taiwan does not provide a general passport allowing an offshore electronic-money institution to conduct regulated domestic banking or foreign-exchange settlement without required local authorization. An offshore EMI may be considered for limited payment services only after confirming its licensing status, Taiwan cross-border service restrictions, permitted funding and withdrawal flows, foreign-exchange reporting treatment, and the receiving bank's acceptance. It should not be used to receive registered capital, bypass investment controls, or avoid bank KYC.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a substitute for a Taiwanese corporate bank account. Taiwan does not provide a general passport allowing an offshore electronic-money institution to conduct regulated domestic banking or foreign-exchange settlement without required local authorization. An offshore EMI may be considered for limited payment services only after confirming its licensing status, Taiwan cross-border service restrictions, permitted funding and withdrawal flows, foreign-exchange reporting treatment, and the receiving bank's acceptance. It should not be used to receive registered capital, bypass investment controls, or avoid bank KYC.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% corporate income tax on taxable income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a nonresident individual, foreign company, or other nonresident recipient, subject to applicable statutory exemptions or treaty relief. Dividends to Taiwan-resident corporate shareholders are governed by separate domestic rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File generally required where annual Taiwan revenue is at least NTD 300 million or annual cross-border controlled transactions reach at least NTD 200 million, subject to statutory exclusions and simplified documentation rules. Master File generally applies where the multinational group's consolidated revenue reaches at least NTD 7 billion and the Taiwan entity meets the applicable cross-border related-party conditions. Country-by-Country reporting may apply to an ultimate parent or surrogate parent with group consolidated revenue meeting the statutory threshold, commonly NTD 27 billion or the prescribed equivalent threshold.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a separate general-purpose Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the regimes applicable in certain offshore financial centers. Nevertheless, substance is material under corporate income tax, transfer pricing, permanent-establishment, withholding, anti-avoidance, beneficial-ownership, and deductibility rules. The JV should maintain Taiwan personnel, premises, decision-making, operational controls, trial-management capability, risk management, books, and contemporaneous evidence proportionate to the CRO functions it claims to perform. Artificial arrangements, conduit payments, circular cash flows, and contracts inconsistent with actual conduct may be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, interest, administrative penalties, tax assessment, possible prohibition from claiming unsupported deductions, and criminal prosecution. Intentional tax evasion may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a criminal fine that may reach NT$10 million under the Tax Collection Act, subject to the applicable offense and court determination.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face criminal liability, civil restitution, tax-related personal sanctions where statutory conditions are met, asset seizure, and disqualification consequences. Travel restrictions may arise during criminal or enforcement proceedings; imprisonment is possible for aggravated or intentional offenses.
False invoicing and accounting fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax penalties, denial of deductions, recovery of tax credits, criminal liability under tax, accounting, commercial, or criminal law, and potential forfeiture of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors, managers, accountants, and employees may be prosecuted personally. Civil damages, restitution, asset preservation, travel restrictions, and imprisonment may apply depending on the conduct and loss amount.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustment to arm's-length income, denial of deductions, withholding-tax reassessment, interest, penalties, and documentation penalties where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability to the company or shareholders for intentional or negligent misconduct. Criminal exposure generally requires additional fraudulent, false-document, or evasion conduct.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the payer, interest, withholding penalties, and possible restrictions on tax clearance or remittance processing.

Director personal liability: The withholding agent's responsible person may face personal administrative or criminal exposure where the failure is intentional, falsified, or part of tax evasion. Civil recovery claims and enforcement measures may follow.
Permanent establishment and Taiwan-source income risk

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of Taiwan-source income, corporate income tax, withholding tax, penalties, interest, and possible permanent-establishment compliance obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for knowingly implementing an artificial structure or submitting false filings. Criminal consequences depend on proof of intentional evasion or falsification.
Unlicensed clinical-trial operations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, suspension or revocation of approvals, corrective orders, trial suspension, invalidation of data, civil claims, and criminal sanctions under applicable pharmaceutical, medical, personal-data, and human-subject protection laws.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face personal criminal, civil, and administrative liability, including professional disqualification, travel restrictions during investigation, and imprisonment for serious unlawful conduct.
Personal data and clinical-trial confidentiality breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, corrective orders, data-processing suspension, contractual damages, compensation to affected persons, and criminal liability for serious unlawful disclosure or misuse.

Director personal liability: Responsible individuals may face personal criminal and civil exposure. Directors may be liable for inadequate governance, failure to implement security controls, or knowingly authorizing unlawful transfers.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial-ownership failure

Statutory sanction: Customer due-diligence deficiencies, reporting failures, account restrictions, administrative fines, transaction suspension, asset freezing, and money-laundering prosecution where knowledge or wilful blindness is established.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal fines, civil liability, criminal prosecution, asset forfeiture, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for laundering or facilitating illicit proceeds.
Shell-company and conduit arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, tax reassessment, withholding-tax recovery, anti-avoidance adjustment, penalties, and potential criminal investigation if documents or filings are false.

Director personal liability: Directors may face breach-of-duty claims, restitution, asset-preservation measures, travel restrictions, and criminal sanctions where the structure involves fraud, false documents, or intentional tax evasion.
Illegal foreign-exchange or profit-repatriation activity

Statutory sanction: Foreign-exchange penalties, tax reassessment, withholding-tax liability, repayment orders, bank reporting, account restrictions, and criminal sanctions for serious or fraudulent violations.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and officers may face personal fines, civil restitution, asset seizure, travel restrictions, and imprisonment where illegal remittance or concealment is intentional.
Improper dividend distribution

Statutory sanction: Recovery of improperly distributed amounts, director liability for company losses, shareholder repayment obligations, corporate-law sanctions, and tax reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for restitution and damages and may face criminal liability where the distribution involves false accounts, insolvency abuse, or intentional creditor prejudice.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taxation Agency (財政部賦稅署) as policy-making authority; operational tax registration is administered by the regional National Taxation Bureaus (各地區國稅局), e.g., the Taipei National Taxation Bureau for Taipei-registered entities. Primary e-services portal: Tax e-Gateway (財政部稅務入口網), www.etax.nat.gov.tw; e-invoice integration platform: www.einvoice.nat.gov.tw; corporate registry gateway: Ministry of Economic Affairs, Department of Commerce (經濟部商業司), www.gcis.nat.gov.tw.

E-government filing portal

The 8-digit Unified Business Number (統一編號) serves as the single statutory tax ID and is issued automatically upon company incorporation; no separate tax-account application exists. Procedure for a foreign-invested Taiwan JV: (1) obtain investment approval from the Investment Commission under the Ministry of Economic Affairs (Foreign Investment Permission Act; PRC shareholders fall under the Mainland Investment Permission Act with added restrictions); (2) file company incorporation (有限公司 or 股份有限公司) with the central or special-municipality registry, which electronically notifies the competent National Taxation Bureau and triggers simultaneous tax registration (稅籍登記) and VAT-registration status under Business Tax Act Article 28; (3) after incorporation, apply for the MOEA company digital certificate (工商憑證) enabling e-filing and e-invoice transmission. A foreign enterprise without a Taiwan entity or fixed place of business must instead apply for a withholding-unit tax ID (扣繳單位統一編號) at the regional National Taxation Bureau to fulfill Taiwan-source income withholding and reporting obligations; such an enterprise does not obtain VAT registration unless it maintains a fixed place of business or PE in Taiwan.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist under Taiwan law. Statutory elections and relief mechanisms available: (a) 10-year net operating loss carryforward under Income Tax Act Article 39, available only to corporations with audited financial statements and approved shareholders' minutes; (b) foreign income tax credit election under Income Tax Act Article 3(2) for overseas-source income taxed abroad, subject to the per-country credit limitation; (c) deemed-profit-rate election under Income Tax Act Article 25 for foreign enterprises providing technical or consulting services in Taiwan where cost allocation is impracticable, subject to Ministry of Finance approval; (d) voluntary VAT registration election for small-scale service businesses whose average monthly sales fall below the NT$40,000 exemption threshold (Business Tax Act Article 13), allowing input VAT credit entitlement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NT$40,000 average monthly sales over a rolling year for service businesses (including PR, advertising and communications agencies) and NT$80,000 for goods-selling businesses. Any business reaching or exceeding the applicable threshold must complete tax/VAT registration before commencing supply (Business Tax Act Article 28). Foreign enterprises with a fixed place of business, branch or agent in Taiwan are registrable regardless of turnover; below-threshold businesses are classified as small-scale taxpayers, exempt from output VAT but ineligible to claim input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Business Tax Act Article 7 to: (1) exported goods; (2) services supplied in Taiwan for use abroad or services connected with export transactions; (3) services rendered to foreign clients (clients having no fixed place of business in Taiwan) where consideration is received in foreign currency and the proceeds are remitted into Taiwan and settled through an authorized-dealer bank. A global PR and communications agency qualifies at 0% for overseas media-relations and crisis-communication retainer fees paid by foreign principals when the written contract, service-delivery evidence, and bank foreign-exchange settlement records substantiate the cross-border supply. Services supplied to Taiwan-resident clients or performed for local use are standard-rated at 5%; where the supplier is a foreign enterprise without Taiwan registration, the Taiwan buyer must self-assess the 5% business tax by reverse charge.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口報單) evidencing goods export clearance through the Customs Administration electronic system
  • General Uniform Invoice (統一發票) issued at zero rate with the applicable transaction and zero-rating code
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (提單/航空運單) as physical movement evidence
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance and Settlement Certificate (外匯收入水單/結售水單) proving receipt and conversion of foreign currency through an authorized-dealer bank in Taiwan
  • Executed service contract, statements of work, delivery reports, and media-placement or crisis-communication performance records (for services zero-rating)

Nationwide mandatory e-invoice (電子發票) issuance fully phased in by 2022 for all VAT-registered businesses. Taiwan does not operate real-time transactional pre-clearance by the tax authority; however, all e-invoice records must be transmitted to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integration Service Platform within 48 hours of issuance for both B2B and B2C transactions. High-volume issuers must integrate via direct platform APIs (SOAP/REST web services) or government-certified value-added network providers. B2C receipts carry a QR-code random code (隨機碼); B2B invoices must reference the counterparty's Unified Business Number. Failure to upload within the 48-hour window triggers late-filing penalties, risks disallowance of input VAT credits, and may lead to audit flags.

Zero-rated claims are audited against customs export declarations (goods) and bank FX settlement records (services), with cross-checks against the counterparty's residence and the substance of service delivery. Where zero-rating is disallowed, the tax authority retroactively assesses the 5% output VAT with daily interest on the underpaid tax, imposes a penalty of up to five times the tax evaded, may order suspension of the business license (Business Tax Act Article 51), and reassesses income tax with additional Penalty Act surcharges. Knowingly false claims—such as fabricated remittance certificates, fictitious foreign clients, or inflated foreign-currency amounts—constitute tax evasion under Tax Collection Act Article 41, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years, detention, or a fine of up to NT$6 million, and the assessment period for fraudulent under-filing is extended to seven years. Such conduct may also trigger offenses under the Money Laundering Control Act and loss of future zero-rating privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a bifurcated convertibility system under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and SAFE rules. Current account items (e.g., trade in services, advertising and public relations service fees, agency retainers) are generally freely convertible upon verification of genuine underlying transactions. Capital account items (e.g., equity injection, shareholder loans, repatriation of capital) remain subject to registration, approval, or post-transaction filing requirements. For the proposed China-Taiwan JV entity, the principal remittance hurdles are the need to document the commercial reality of each PR retainer and service disbursement, the possibility of a tax treaty rate reduction on dividend or service withholding, and the requirement to route proceeds through a compliant onshore designated foreign exchange account. Taiwan imposes no general restriction on current account convertibility; profits may be repatriated after distribution formalities and satisfaction of withholding tax obligations. Practical friction includes the longer documentary examination cycle for cross-Strait payments, where local banks may apply enhanced scrutiny to remittances referencing 'Taiwan' or 'China' in the beneficiary/purpose fields, and the need to demonstrate that the payments do not constitute prohibited indirect capital flows or unlicensed financial services.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For mainland China, the former mandatory export proceeds surrender system has been liberalized. As of 2012 and subsequent SAFE circulars, enterprises are no longer required to convert export proceeds into CNY at a fixed ratio; instead, foreign exchange receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts within an approved amount ceiling or settled in CNY according to enterprise preference, subject to the Foreign Exchange Account Quota system. For the JV's PR services exported to overseas clients, receipts must be settled within the prescribed processing period under the SAFE 'trade in services' monitoring framework: enterprises must complete the State Administration of Taxation cross-border receipt verification and SAFE's digital declaration within the time limits set by their bank's 'receipt and payment' control list (generally same-day or T+1 for electronic filings, but the underlying transaction must be fully documented). The statutory requirement is that export receipts of goods and services must be repatriated to China via designated channels and be subject to the 'Who earns, who settles' principle; the actual conversion ratio is 0% mandatory, but the enterprise must justify why the proceeds were not repatriated or were offset against current account liabilities if kept offshore. For Taiwan, there is no forced surrender of export proceeds; exporters may retain earnings in foreign currency accounts at Foreign Exchange Banks (FEBs), subject to declaration if above US$200,000 per year for cross-Strait remittances under the Cross-Strait Banking Supervision Memorandum.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBoC); for Taiwan operations, the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC) and Taiwan Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) are the regulatory counterparts.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, with material legal constraints. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as UK FCA-authorised EMIs, Lithuanian EMIs, or multi-currency virtual account providers (e.g., Payoneer, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may be used for collection of retainer fees from global clients, but they are not a substitute for a licensed bank account in mainland China or Taiwan for statutory capital injection, tax payments, VAT issuance, and customs-related banking. Inbound remittances to such EMIs are generally permissible for services exports, but using an EMI to receive payments on behalf of the Chinese JV without a direct underlying client contract may trigger SAFE anti-avoidance review and the EMI's own compliance restrictions. PRC law only recognises payment institution licences issued by the PBoC; foreign EMIs cannot open domestic settlement accounts, cannot issue CNY-denominated accounts for the JV, and any client aggregation or collection from third parties without a legitimate commercial relationship may be deemed illegal payment settlement business under Article 4 of the Administrative Measures for Payment Services of Non-Financial Institutions. The Taiwan side similarly restricts foreign EMI operations: foreign electronic payment institutions must obtain FSC approval for a branch or apply for a third-party payment licence; an unlicensed offshore EMI cannot be used for the JV's local payroll disbursements. The optimum use of an EMI is as a secondary collection rail for foreign-client retainers, with all funds swept within 1-3 business days to the designated onshore bank account, and each use backed by a corresponding service contract and invoice.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial capital injection from the Taiwan shareholder or other offshore investors into the mainland China JV must comply with the SAFE Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration regime. Routeing protocol: (1) obtain the FDI registration certificate with the local SAFE bureau or the delegated bank before opening the capital escrow account; (2) open a Renminbi/Foreign Currency capital account at the designated bank, using the purpose code '622' (FDI capital injection) or the appropriate SAFE code for equity investment; (3) the remittance should be sent by corporate bank transfer, with the remitter's account name matching the shareholder of record, and the wire message must state the purpose as 'capital contribution toward registered capital of [JV name] under FDI approval [number]' and reference the SAFE FDI Registration Code; (4) upon receipt, the bank will verify the amount against the registered capital quota, and the funds must be used within the scope approved by SAFE, generally for operating expenses, purchase of goods/services, or payment of wages; direct conversion of foreign currency capital into CNY is allowed with no mandatory conversion ratio, but any use for entrusted loans, real estate development beyond scope, or fixed-asset investment may require additional registration; (5) the JV must perform the SAFE outbound declaration within the statutory timeline and preserve the bank receipts, capital verification report, and payment instructions for at least five years. If the JV later increases capital, a separate SAFE registration must be amended before the new injection. For the Taiwan JV side (if capital flows from China into Taiwan), the same routeing logic applies under the Taiwan Ministry of Economic Affairs Investment Commission approval, and the remittance should use the purpose code 'ROI: Investment in Taiwan' with a hard copy of the approved plan and the FEB's prior e-declaration.For cross-border payments of trade settlements, retainer fees, media-buying disbursements, and vendor payments, the following clearing rails are permissible: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct bank-to-bank wire transfers where the JV's onshore bank transmits to the overseas beneficiary bank, using purpose codes under the SAFE 'Current Account- Trade in Services' (e.g., code '217' for media, public relations, and communication services) and the OECD/UN 'Service' codes; the bank will validate the e-tax invoice (fapiao) and the contract; (2) Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) is permissible where the JV acts as importer of services or goods for PR production (e.g., production equipment or licensed broadcast materials), with the issuing bank in China/Taiwan and the beneficiary's confirming bank; however, L/C is less common for recurring retainer payments and may be used only for high-value project milestones; (3) Cross-border RMB clearing through the CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System) and the Taiwan RMB clearing mechanism is allowed for CNY-denominated settlements with counterparties in Taiwan, particularly for fees paid to Taiwanese media agencies; this route requires the Taiwan counterparty to maintain a RMB account at a Taiwanese foreign exchange bank; (4) For payroll and local expenses in Taiwan, the JV should use Taiwan's domestic clearing rails (CHIPS in Taiwan / FEB interbank transfer) after converting CNY/USD into TWD; the conversion is unregulated at the bank level but must be evidenced for tax and audit purposes. All outbound trade disbursements must cross the bank's anti-money-laundering screening; the JV should maintain a robust evidence pack including the fully signed service agreement, itemized invoice, delivery/publish results report, tax withholding certificates, and if above a bank threshold (often USD 50,000), a copy of the SAFE payment certificate.Onshore CNY/USD forward contracts with a licensed PRC bank, permissible only for hedging the JV's genuine current account or capital account exposure (IAS 39 / ASC 815 trade documentation required; no speculative forwards).Taiwan USD/TWD deliverable forward contracts through a Taiwan FEB, for hedging the Taiwan branch's retained earnings and media procurement exposure.CNY/TWD non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) offered by offshore banks (mainly in Hong Kong or Singapore), permissible for managing CNY/TWD risk if the JV has no natural CNY/TWD exposure and requires an underlying demonstrable forecast; risk: NDF settlement in USD may create a separate tax/bookkeeping event.Cross-currency swaps (CNY/USD or TWD/USD) with a licensed bank for matching longer-duration service contracts where the JV has a multi-year retainer in one currency and operating costs in another; only cost-neutral hedging with underlying debt or receivable exposure is permissible.Currency options (limited, bank discretion) for structured hedging of tender/retainer payments in emerging-market client currencies; due to regulatory restrictions on PRC banks' options salesbooks, these will likely be executed on the Taiwan books where FSC regulations permit plain-vanilla options for professional investors.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium, with material legal constraints. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as UK FCA-authorised EMIs, Lithuanian EMIs, or multi-currency virtual account providers (e.g., Payoneer, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may be used for collection of retainer fees from global clients, but they are not a substitute for a licensed bank account in mainland China or Taiwan for statutory capital injection, tax payments, VAT issuance, and customs-related banking. Inbound remittances to such EMIs are generally permissible for services exports, but using an EMI to receive payments on behalf of the Chinese JV without a direct underlying client contract may trigger SAFE anti-avoidance review and the EMI's own compliance restrictions. PRC law only recognises payment institution licences issued by the PBoC; foreign EMIs cannot open domestic settlement accounts, cannot issue CNY-denominated accounts for the JV, and any client aggregation or collection from third parties without a legitimate commercial relationship may be deemed illegal payment settlement business under Article 4 of the Administrative Measures for Payment Services of Non-Financial Institutions. The Taiwan side similarly restricts foreign EMI operations: foreign electronic payment institutions must obtain FSC approval for a branch or apply for a third-party payment licence; an unlicensed offshore EMI cannot be used for the JV's local payroll disbursements. The optimum use of an EMI is as a secondary collection rail for foreign-client retainers, with all funds swept within 1-3 business days to the designated onshore bank account, and each use backed by a corresponding service contract and invoice.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where annual related-party transactions exceed NT$200 million. Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the entity's aggregate revenue exceeds NT$3 billion and related-party transactions exceed NT$200 million.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan applies the functional substance-over-form doctrine. A JV entity in Taiwan must evidence substantive staff, premises, and decision-making functions to support its transfer pricing positions and to avoid being recharacterised as a conduit or permanent establishment of the mainland China parent. While Taiwan does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, the Revenue Service denies benefits to shell companies lacking real business purpose, and the application of the Cross-Strait Tax Agreement, if any, is conditioned on genuine economic activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: A surcharge of up to 2.5 times the underpaid tax, plus a criminal fine and imprisonment of up to five years under the Tax Evasion and Punishment Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate deceptive invoicing are personally liable for the unpaid tax, face travel bans, attachment of personal assets, and imprisonment; the corporate veil can be pierced upon proof of intentional abuse of the JV entity.
Shell Company and Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment with a 20% additional tax on recharacterised profits, plus a penalty of 1.5 times the additional tax; denial of treaty or cross-strait relief; possible deregistration of the Taiwan branch.

Director personal liability: Directors may be compelled to restore diverted profits to the tax authority, prohibited from leaving Taiwan pending investigation, and subject to receiver orders on personal assets if they signed false substance declarations.
AML and False Documentation

Statutory sanction: Money laundering penalties under the Money Laundering Control Act: imprisonment up to seven years, fines up to NT$50 million, and confiscation of illicit proceeds; tax fines for falsified invoices up to NT$1 million per violation.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge of the laundering structure face criminal joint liability and civil restitution to the defrauded tax authority; in severe cases, a permanent departure ban is imposed pending full repayment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for tax registration: any person or entity carrying on taxable business in Taiwan must apply for tax registration with the National Taxation Administration before commencing operations. The NT$200,000 per month figure is the rate-and-invoicing boundary: small-scale businesses with monthly taxable sales at or below NT$200,000 may be assessed at the simplified 1% Business Tax rate, while regular taxpayers with monthly taxable sales exceeding NT$200,000 must issue Uniform Invoices and account for the standard 5% VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act, a 0% Business Tax rate is available for goods exported out of Taiwan and for services provided by a Taiwan business to a non-Taiwan customer where the service is delivered for use or consumption entirely outside Taiwan. For the ISIC 7420 stock-photo/generative visual assets activity, the taxpayer must prove that the purchaser is non-resident, the licensed digital assets are delivered offshore by electronic transfer or download, the consideration is payable in foreign exchange, and the foreign exchange receipt is timely repatriated. The zero-rating also makes input VAT attributable to the export sale refundable.

  • Customs Export Declaration, being the official export declaration filed via the Ministry of Finance Customs Administration online clearance system for physical exported goods.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill covering the export shipment where physical media or goods are moved to the overseas customer.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate issued by an Authorized Foreign Exchange Bank, evidencing receipt and repatriation of the overseas customer payment.
  • Executed international sales contract, license agreement or purchase order naming the offshore customer and specifying the exact license fee/value.
  • Zero-rated Uniform Invoice issued to the foreign purchaser with the statutory zero-rate tax code.
  • Digital delivery evidence for stock photos or generative visual assets, including download logs, usage reports, IP address records and platform reports showing delivery to a non-Taiwan customer.
  • Attribution record matching the export sales consideration to the corresponding input tax credits claimed.

Taiwan operates a mandatory Uniform Invoice system administered by the Ministry of Finance. Regular VAT taxpayers and businesses above the NT$200,000 monthly sales boundary must issue Uniform Invoices for every taxable sale. Under the E-Invoice mandate, the taxpayer must connect to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform using the statutory Turnkey/API interface; each invoice is issued with a ministerial invoice number, random check code and QR code, and the invoice data is transmitted to the platform within the prescribed filing window for tax-authority validation, discrepancy matching and anti-fraud control. There is no separate physical fiscal stamp; the electronic record in the Ministry of Finance platform is the fiscal clearance evidence.

Taiwan tax authorities audit zero-rating claims by cross-checking the Customs export declaration, transport documents, Uniform Invoice data and the Authorized Foreign Exchange Bank remittance ledger. If the taxpayer cannot substantiate a genuine offshore customer, offshore delivery/use, or receipt and repatriation of export proceeds within the statutory period (normally 180 days), the supply is recharacterized as domestic taxable and assessed at the standard 5% VAT, with interest and penalties. Under the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act, false zero-rating, issuance of false Uniform Invoices or intentional understatement of taxable sales attracts a penalty of five to eight times the tax evaded, suspension of business, and criminal referral for tax fraud where fraudulent documentation is shown. Any previously allowed zero-rating or input-tax refund can be clawed back retrospectively if the supporting customs, transport, contract, digital-delivery or foreign-exchange documents are found to be fabricated, incomplete or not verified in audit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime administered by the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC). The New Taiwan Dollar (TWD) is effectively convertible for current-account transactions, including trade settlements, service fees, royalties, dividends, and branch remittances, provided the underlying business documentation and a foreign-exchange declaration are submitted through a CBC-designated bank. Capital-account flows, especially an inbound equity injection into a Taiwan JV from mainland China, are not automatically convertible: the investment must first receive authorization from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs and the remittance must be released under a specific CBC transaction code after bank compliance review. Export proceeds no longer bear a mandatory surrender ratio; they can be held in Foreign Currency Deposit accounts or converted at market rates. Dividend and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders require audited financial statements, tax clearance, and withholding tax receipts. The CBC retains emergency powers to impose limits, require advance approval for large transactions, and direct designated banks to collect additional declarations, so full balance-of-payments data visibility is a structural reality.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds are subject to repatriation rather than surrender: the exporter must bring the foreign-currency receivables into Taiwan through a CBC-designated bank by the contracted payment maturity or, for open-account export bills, within 180 days of the export shipment date unless an extension has been approved by the CBC. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively zero; the exporter may retain 100% of the proceeds in a Foreign Currency Deposit (FCD) account or convert any portion to TWD at the prevailing bank counter rate. The CBC foreign-exchange declaration must be filed for each receipt through the bank's reporting platform, with the export declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and tax invoice attached. Failure to repatriate or declare on time subjects the JV to a written rectification order, administrative fines, and potential downgrade of the bank's internal KYC rating.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), with the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) and the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA-IC) as co-regulators for foreign investment and banking compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital must arrive via the international banking network as a SWIFT MT103, or as a Fedwire or CHIPS wire in USD, to the JV designated Capital Escrow Account at a CBC-authorized designated commercial bank. The wire reference line must show the MOEA-IC approval number and a clear purpose code of Inward Remittance for Direct Investment rather than a trade-goods code. The remitting bank, being the mainland Chinese parent bank, must use the PRC SAFE-approved ODI remittance purpose code and attach a copy of the foreign-exchange registration certificate. Upon receipt, the Taiwan bank notifies the CBC through the FX declaration system, conducts source-of-funds verification, and credits a restricted capital-verification account; the JV CPA then verifies paid-in capital and issues a capital verification report before funds can be moved to an operating account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low. Taiwanese law reserves the taking of deposits, execution of foreign-exchange transactions, and operation of domestic clearing membership to licensed banks and CBC-authorized designated banks; a foreign fintech EMI such as Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex does not hold a Taiwan bank license and cannot clear NTD through the CBC interbank settlement system or perform the statutory capital-verification and foreign-exchange declaration duties required for an inbound equity JV. An EMI account may be used for sublimit operational expenses in a non-core jurisdiction, but it cannot substitute for the Taiwan resident bank relationship, and the FSC will treat the JV as unbanked if it relies solely on an EMI.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% on net taxable income exceeding NT$120,000 for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2018; retained earnings not distributed within 3 years attract an additional 5% retained earnings surcharge.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed to non-resident enterprise shareholders; 21% applies to distributions from earnings subject to the 5% retained earnings surtax. No further withholding or enterprise income tax applies to the remainder because the Taiwan company has already borne full corporate income tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documenting related-party transactions is mandatory if annual related-party purchases, sales, royalty, interest, or other monetary transactions exceed NT$50 million in aggregate. A Master File is required for multinational groups that report consolidated group revenue above NT$30 billion or when a Taiwan entity closes related-party transactional volume above NT$200 million. Country-by-country reporting applies to ultimate parent groups with consolidated revenue over NT$30 billion.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, tax authority audits apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 12-1 of the Income Tax Act and the substance-over-form doctrine. A Taiwan company licensing AI-generated stock imagery through structures lacking a local de facto operational core — no local managers, no servers or data preprocessing infrastructure, no local contract negotiation capacity, and no payroll substance — is highly vulnerable to recharacterization as a conduit entity. Application of Article 43-4 (controlled foreign corporation rules) will impute undistributed profits to Taiwanese resident shareholders if the company has low substance and avoids dividend distributions for over 3 years.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Income Tax Act, the taxpayer is liable for a surcharge of up to 3 times the underpaid tax amount; with intentional bookkeeping fraud, under Article 43 of the Tax Collection Act, criminal fines and confiscation of avoided profit may apply. False invoicing costs an additional 5%–10% penalty under the Uniform Invoice Violation Penalty Guidelines.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fraudulent tax returns or authorize misreporting are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax plus penalties. The Taiwan tax court may impose civil restitution; prosecutors may apply a travel ban under the Bail Law when tax evasion exceeds NT$2 million, and imprisonment of up to 5 years plus a personal tax penalty is possible. The corporate veil will be pierced where the director used the JV as a mere alteration ego to shield overseas revenue.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The National Taxation Bureau will recharacterize transactions under the GAAR, imposing 20% tax on deemed Taiwan-source income, adding a 1.5x surcharge on unpaid tax, and denying carryforwards. In addition, the Commercial Registration Authority may revoke the company's registration if the 'limited purpose' of the legal entity cannot support the business scope under the Company Act; this triggers automatic tax collection within 30 days.

Director personal liability: Under the Company Act Article 23, directors who cause the company to conduct a fictitious commercial design are liable to creditors for full repayment. A director may be held criminally liable for false financial disclosure and imprisoned for up to 3 years. Escaping China/Taiwan may be prevented by travel restraints; joint asset freezes are routinely issued by the Taipei District Court on application by the National Taxation Bureau.
Anti-Money Laundering / Suspicious Cash Flow

Statutory sanction: The Money Laundering Control Act imposes criminal liability on legal persons and responsible individuals: fines up to NT$10 million, and imprisonment of up to 7 years for intentional laundering. The Financial Supervisory Commission may impose a banking ban and freeze the company's payment rails; Stripe and PayPal have contractual termination clauses that mirror FATF list sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure trade through interposed shell entities to conceal beneficial ownership face personal seizure of Taiwan financial assets and real estate. The Supreme Court has upheld civil piercing where corporate formalities are ignored for AML avoidance; the director may also be barred from serving as manager within the financial sector under the Securities and Exchange Act. A travel ban is automatically considered by the prosecutor if the amount exceeds NT$15 million and the defendant has residences abroad.
Transfer Pricing Penalty & Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust the taxable income to an arm's length amount and impose a surcharge of 100%–200% of the additional tax (per Article 43-2 of the Income Tax Act as reinforced by Transfer Pricing Assessment Rules). If documentation is absent, the same surcharge is doubled; a civil penalty of NT$20,000–NT$200,000 is imposed per missing file.

Director personal liability: Transfer pricing aggression does not automatically invoke personal liability unless the director signed confirmations on comparability adjustments that are fraudulent. If the director causes the company to issue a false intercompany conclusion letter with intent to evade tax, he or she becomes directly and personally liable for the surcharge under the 'responsible person of profit-seeking enterprise' doctrine in Article 15 of the Tax Collection Act. Criminal imprisonment of up to 2 years may follow after transfer pricing tax adjustments exceed NT$6 million.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online portal is the State Taxation Administration Electronic Tax Bureau at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

For the China WFOE, the initial step is incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that serves as the national taxpayer identification number. Within 30 calendar days of the business license issuance, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the local STA office through the Electronic Tax Bureau, submitting the business license, articles of association, legal representative identification, registered address proof, bank information, and required shareholder documentation. The electronic registration automatically enrols the entity for corporate income tax, value added tax, stamp tax, and social insurance levies. After registration, the entity must enrol either as a small-scale VAT taxpayer or as a general VAT taxpayer through the VAT Invoice Management System; the general taxpayer election is mandatory once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. In the case of a foreign non-resident investor or cross-border e-commerce platform service provider operating without a permanent establishment in China, the foreign entity must separately obtain a Chinese taxpayer identification number through the non-resident enterprise registration module of the Electronic Tax Bureau, using its home-jurisdiction tax residence certificate and appointing a China withholding agent where necessary.

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC tax system does not provide a US-style check-the-box entity classification election, and the China WFOE is mandatorily treated as a resident enterprise subject to entity-level corporate income tax on worldwide income. Available administrative elections are confined to VAT small-scale versus general taxpayer status and the reduced corporate income tax treatment for qualified small and low-profit enterprises; these are not hybrid mismatch or pass-through check-the-box elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All enterprises must complete VAT registration upon incorporation; no turnover-based exemption exists. The mandatory threshold for classification as a general VAT taxpayer is annual taxable turnover of RMB 5,000,000. Below this threshold, the enterprise qualifies as a small-scale taxpayer and may apply a reduced levy rate (typically 1% to 3%) unless it voluntarily elects general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of custom mechanical keyboard keycaps from the PRC customs territory qualifies for zero-rating (exemption and credit/refund) provided the exporter is a VAT general taxpayer, is the declared consignor under the customs export declaration, the goods are physically exported via a PRC customs port, the sale is supported by genuine cross-border commercial documentation, export proceeds are repatriated to China and substantiated through the banking system, and the export-tag VAT invoice has been issued and filed with the STA. Qualifying sales through cross-border e-commerce platforms to overseas consumers using declared trade data are eligible under the integrated cross-border e-commerce retail export policy.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Sales Contract / Purchase Order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued export proceeds receipt under SAFE
  • Export-goods VAT e-invoice
  • Export Goods Tax Rebate declaration in the Electronic Tax Bureau

China mandates real-time invoice data transmission through the State Taxation Administration's VAT Invoice Management System and the nationwide Digital Electronic Invoice (fully digitalised e-invoice) platform. All VAT invoices, including special VAT invoices, must be generated through STA-approved fiscal infrastructure and carry a unique encrypted invoice code assigned by the national e-invoice clearance database. E-invoices are delivered in structured data format through API integration with the taxpayer's accounting or e-commerce system; issuance, cancellation, and red-document reversal are processed online with mandatory filing of each invoice before legal circulation. The use of tax-controlling encrypted stamps and the real-time reporting of sales data constitute the anti-fraud clearance mandate.

The STA and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange perform cross-referenced scrutiny of export declarations, transport documents, payment rails, and bank foreign-exchange receipts. If zero-rated export VAT treatment is claimed without complete physical exportation, without the required customs clearance documents, or without verified repatriation of the export proceeds within the 180-day statutory window, the zero-rating is denied, the VAT refund is clawed back with retrospective interest, and a penalty ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax amount evaded is imposed. Deliberate fraudulent export-rebate schemes are referred for criminal investigation under the Criminal Law, with possible imprisonment, fines, forfeiture, and downgrading of the taxpayer's credit status, which may trigger interruption of VAT e-invoice issuance and export declaration privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PBoC and SAFE framework, RMB is conditionally convertible: current account transactions, including trade in goods and reasonable service payments, are freely convertible and require real-trade supporting documentation. Capital account transactions, including inbound direct investment contributed by a Taiwan shareholder to a China WFOE, remain subject to SAFE registration, foreign exchange account controls, and post-verification settlement. A Taiwan-funded WFOE may receive capital contributions in a foreign currency only after completing the Foreign-Exchange Business Registration for direct investment; once credited, the capital funds may be settled into RMB only against verified operating use such as the purchase of PBT resin, molds, or manufacturing equipment. Profit remittance to the Taiwan shareholder is permitted after annual audit, corporate income tax filing, and provision of a board resolution, audited financials, and tax clearance; no separate repatriation tax is imposed, but the outbound remittance is executed through a domestic bank and may be delayed or rejected if the tax certificate or capital verification files are incomplete. The convertibility risk is therefore concentrated in the capital account and profit repatriation process rather than in ordinary trade settlement, but the WFOE must maintain disciplined SAFE filing and documentation hygiene to keep the clearing rails open.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE goods-trade administration, there is no mandatory percentage for conversion to CNY; export proceeds may be retained in a foreign exchange settlement account or sold to banks. However, all proceeds from the export of custom mechanical keyboard keycaps must be repatriated into China within 180 days after the export customs declaration, unless the WFOE obtains SAFE approval for offshore retention under the pilot programme. After repatriation, the WFOE must complete trade-in-flow verification through the Digital Foreign Exchange Management Platform and may keep funds in foreign currency or settle them to RMB at a negotiated spot or forward rate. Any excess unverified inflow may be treated as an export under-declaration or trade-based money laundering trigger, so the statutory 180-day timeline and the 0% forced conversion ratio require active treasury controls.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan shareholder must route investment funds via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's Foreign Currency Capital Account at a tier-1 domestic commercial bank, using the remitter name exactly matching the SAFE FDI registration. The bank will require the SAFE business registration code, a detailed remittance purpose referencing direct investment, and an internal remittance instruction. The funds must be held in the capital escrow account until the bank validates the Foreign-Exchange Business Registration and the underlying investment contract. Once validated, the WFOE may apply for capital settlement into RMB against verified operating uses such as equipment import, raw material purchase, rent, and salaries. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File (移轉訂價文據—「移轉訂價報告」) where the WFOE's related-party transaction amount in a fiscal year reaches NT$200 million (aggregate controlled transactions); the Master File (集團主檔報告) is required when the multinational group's combined global turnover exceeds NT$5 billion and cross-border controlled transactions exceed NT$500 million. Below the NT$200 million threshold, the taxpayer must still provide a related-party disclosure schedule and contemporaneous substantiation upon audit.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not apply the offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as codified in the UAE/Cayman framework. However, Taiwan's Income Tax Act contains equivalent substance-over-form doctrine: (i) General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 43-1 and recognized judicial substance requirements permit the NTB to re-characterize or ignore a transaction arranged without a bona fide commercial purpose; (ii) the CFC regime (Art. 43-3) and Place of Effective Management (PEM) rules (Art. 43-4) attribute offshore income of a controlled foreign corporation to the Taiwan shareholder. For a WFOE in the custom keycap manufacturing sector, the doctrine requires physical manufacturing activity, local payroll (injection moulding operators), inventory risk, intellectual-property substance (ownership or licensed use of double-shot moulding tech), and an actual decision-making office in Taiwan. A WFOE merely outsourcing production to a PRC contract manufacturer to strip profit without operational substance will be re-characterized and its income attributed to Taiwan.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Invoicing (E-fapiao fraud)

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Collection Act Art. 41, perjury or fraudulent means to evade income tax are punishable with imprisonment of up to five years, detention, and/or a fine up to NT$6 million; income tax additionally assessed and a surcharge of two to three times the underpaid tax under Income Tax Act Art. 110. For electronic invoice violations, the Unified Invoice penalty ranges between NT$15,000 and NT$1,500,000 per instance together with loss of the invoice-printing licence.

Director personal liability: Management and the sole shareholder of the WFOE are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax arrears after lawful final assessment. Directors can be personally named in the NTB collection summons; travel bans under Tax Collection Act Art. 24 (restriction of departure when arrears exceed NT$1 million) are imposed personally on the responsible director/controller. Fraud participants, including the accountant who designed the false invoicing, face the same criminal exposure under Art. 41.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Unjustified Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under the TP Regulations and Income Tax Act Art. 43-1, the NTB re-computes the arm's-length consideration, increases taxable income, and imposes a surcharge of between 100% and 200% of the incremental tax (Article 110 regime). Failure to timely submit contemporaneous Local/Master File documentation triggers administrative fines of NT$3,000 to NT$300,000 per return and disallowance of the company's intercompany deductions; interest on underpayment is additionally assessed.

Director personal liability: The chief executive and finance director are treated as responsible collectors; when the increase arises from deliberate dishonest price-setting, criminal liability under Tax Collection Act Art. 41 attaches personally. The NTB may freeze the Taiwanese bank accounts of the directors where the company's main operating receivables have been diverted abroad, and the court may order preservative seizure of personal assets.
Artificial Shell / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of the WFOE under the GAAR (Art. 43-1): the agency/PE structure is disregarded; the Chinese parent is directly assessed on Taiwan-source revenue, resulting in back taxes plus a 1.5- to 2-fold surcharge. The corporate registration authority may initiate company dissolution procedures for inactive or sham enterprises; the business registration can be revoked, affecting VAT zero-rating entitlements.

Director personal liability: Where the sham enterprise results in underpaid corporate tax, directors become jointly and severally liable for the full tax arrears. When the shell is used to move funds to a non-transparent foreign account structure, the responsible director also faces Anti-Money-Laundering Act prosecution for money laundering (up to seven years' imprisonment) and a personal civil restitution claim by the tax authority as creditor.
AML / Cross-Border Payment & Currency Evasion

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Foreign Exchange Control Act and the Central Bank declaration rules attract fines of NT$3 million to NT$30 million per unreported inflow, plus the seizure of undeclared funds; falsified customs declarations in the export of keycaps to the parent attract Customs Anti-Smuggling Act penalties of a fine of up to 3x evaded foreign-currency earnings and criminal sanctions of up to one year. The under-declared profit is additionally taxed as unreported revenue at 20% with a 100–200% penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who open offshore collection accounts to receive e-commerce sales—without booking the revenue in the WFOE—convert corporate income into personal wealth; this exposes them to self-dealing/breach of fiduciary claims and to criminal fraudulent concealment. Travel bans, asset-preservation orders on personal bank accounts, and up to five years' imprisonment are available to the tax and money-laundering enforcement authorities; the shareholders of a WFOE can also lose the limited liability shield because the abuse constitutes fraud or gross misconduct under Taiwanese company law.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

The foreign-invested JV must obtain company incorporation approval from the Ministry of Economic Affairs or the Investment Commission, complete ROC company registration, and then apply to the competent local National Taxation Bureau for a Unified Business Tax Number (統一編號 / VAT ID) before commencing business operations. Required documents include the certificate of incorporation, approved articles of incorporation, board/shareholder resolutions, foreign investor identity or certificate of incorporation, registered office evidence, responsible person identity documents and authorization, and bank account confirmation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not provide a general check-the-box election; entity classification follows the Companies Act, Income Tax Act, and Ministry of Finance administrative rules. Available elections and special regimes include the deemed-profit determination for non-resident branches under Article 25 of the Income Tax Act, the Offshore Banking Unit exemption for segregated foreign-currency offshore books, CFC and hybrid-mismatch allocation elections for cross-border structures, and thin-capitalization safe-harbor elections. A Taiwan-incorporated JV is classified as a resident profit-seeking enterprise taxed at the entity level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for Taiwan-incorporated companies or JVs; registration is mandatory from commencement. Small-scale simplified businesses may benefit from reduced assessment below NT$200,000 monthly sales, and foreign cross-border electronic service suppliers are required to register when annual Taiwan-source digital-service turnover exceeds NT$480,000. For portfolio-advisory and family-office export services, zero-rating applies without a turnover threshold if documentary conditions are met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies where goods are actually exported from Taiwan customs territory, evidenced by a customs export declaration. Services: zero-rating applies to services sold to foreign enterprises or non-resident principals where the services are performed outside Taiwan and are not used in Taiwan. For a multi-family office, discretionary portfolio-advisory fees, investment-consulting services, and offshore custody coordination may qualify when the client is non-resident, the engagement is performed offshore, deliverables are consumed outside Taiwan, and the fee is paid in foreign currency and repatriated. Services connected to Taiwan-domiciled clients, Taiwan-listed securities, or local asset management remain subject to VAT at 5% or the applicable special financial-sector business tax.

Taiwan enforces a Uniform Invoice regime. Most VAT-registered businesses must issue uniform or electronic invoices at the time of supply, use approved e-invoice numbers, and transmit invoice data through the MOF e-Invoice Integration Service Platform using authorized API/turnkey interfaces and digital certificates. Taiwan has not yet adopted a universal real-time fiscal clearance and stamping system, but invoice issuance, data retention, and periodic VAT reconciliation are subject to mandatory electronic reporting and audit trails. Export zero-rated supplies must be recorded electronically with the applicable zero-rating reason code and retained supporting documents.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to enhanced audit scrutiny by the National Taxation Bureau. If export shipment or actual foreign-exchange receipt cannot be substantiated, the supply is reclassified as domestic, output VAT is recaptured with interest, and penalties of up to five times the evaded tax may be imposed under the Business Tax Act and the Tax Evasion Penalty Act. Fraudulent zero-rating documentation may result in criminal prosecution, imprisonment for tax evasion, joint liability of responsible persons, and a seven-year retroactive assessment or clawback period for concluded tax years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime for the New Taiwan Dollar (TWD). Current-account transactions, including trade in goods and services, dividend distributions, interest, royalties, and management fees, are generally freely convertible through licensed banks upon submission of underlying documentation. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment, inward equity injections, shareholder loans, M&A proceeds, and financial derivatives, are subject to ex ante regulatory approval by the MOEA Investment Commission and to post-event reporting to the CBC. Profit remittance by a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is permitted after audited financial statements are filed and tax clearance is completed; however, PRC-origin shareholders are subject to enhanced cross-Strait review, including restricted-industry screening, beneficial-ownership assessment, and documentary verification of the remitted amount. There is no blanket TWD convertibility restriction, but amounts at or above TWD 500,000 equivalent must be reported into the CBC Foreign Exchange Reporting System.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan does not currently impose a mandatory export-proceeds conversion ratio. Exporters must repatriate export proceeds to Taiwan within 180 days from the date of export declaration or, for collection-based trade, from the due date of the documentary collection, and must then settle the proceeds into a licensed banking account or retain them in a foreign-currency deposit account. All export settlement transactions must be reported to the CBC through the bank using the export customs declaration reference. Temporary mandatory surrender ratios may be invoked only by the CBC under emergency balance-of-payments conditions, and no such ratio is currently in force.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), acting through its Foreign Exchange Department and licensed domestic banks pursuant to the Foreign Exchange Regulations and the Act for the Governance of Cross-Strait Relations for PRC-origin capital.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Nil as a substitute for a primary Taiwan banking relationship. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, or similar payment service providers) may be used only as auxiliary multi-currency payment facilitators. They cannot hold or disburse TWD through Taiwan's domestic clearing system, cannot issue Taiwan-based dedicated account numbers, and cannot act as the receiving bank for equity capital escrow required by the MOEA Investment Commission. Use of such EMIs must not create unlicensed deposit-taking in Taiwan, and all underlying client funds for the multi-family-office custody and investment business must be held through a Taiwanese commercial bank or licensed custodian.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% Taiwan corporate income tax on taxable income. A separate 5% surtax applies to undistributed earnings retained by a Taiwan company for purposes other than actual business needs.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a non-resident individual or foreign corporate shareholder, subject to any applicable statutory exemption, special regime, or enforceable tax agreement. Taiwan does not have a generally applicable comprehensive income tax treaty with the People's Republic of China; cross-Strait treatment must therefore be checked under current Taiwan domestic rules and any separately effective cross-Strait arrangement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due from 1 May through 31 May following the end of the calendar fiscal year. A company using a non-calendar fiscal year generally files within the prescribed period beginning on the first day of the sixth month after the fiscal year-end. Provisional income tax is generally filed in September for the first six months of the year, subject to statutory exemptions and election rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan generally requires a Local File for a taxpayer meeting the applicable annual revenue and controlled-transaction thresholds, commonly including annual net operating revenue of at least NTD 3 billion or annual cross-border controlled transactions of at least NTD 1 billion, subject to exemptions and transaction-specific rules. A Master File is generally required for a Taiwan entity belonging to a multinational group meeting the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold, commonly NTD 7.5 billion, together with the prescribed cross-border related-party conditions. Thresholds, exemptions, submission timing, and transaction categories must be verified for the relevant fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a universal UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance is nevertheless material under Taiwan transfer-pricing, controlled foreign company, general anti-avoidance, beneficial-ownership, withholding, permanent-establishment, and business-purpose rules. A multi-family office should maintain Taiwan-resident directors or responsible officers with genuine decision-making authority, qualified investment and compliance personnel, appropriate premises and systems, documented client mandates, risk controls, books and records, and evidence that advisory and discretionary services are actually performed by the charging entity. A company that is merely a conduit for shareholder funds, lacks functional capacity, or invoices services performed elsewhere faces denial of deductions, income reallocation, withholding disputes, and possible licensing or AML consequences.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unlicensed securities investment consulting or discretionary investment management

Statutory sanction: Regulatory orders, business suspension, license denial or revocation, administrative fines, disgorgement or restitution, and criminal penalties may apply depending on the activity, scale, intent, and applicable statute. Unauthorized securities business may carry imprisonment and significant fines.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons and directors may face personal criminal prosecution, civil damages, administrative disqualification, travel restrictions where legally ordered, and asset preservation. The corporate form does not shield intentional participation, knowing approval, fraud, or unlawful receipt of client assets.
Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, back tax, interest, late-payment surcharges, tax penalties, denial of deductions, and criminal prosecution may result. Fraudulent tax evasion can lead to imprisonment and criminal fines in addition to civil tax liability.

Director personal liability: Directors, responsible persons, accountants, and participating officers may be jointly or individually liable where they directed, approved, knowingly assisted, or concealed the conduct. Personal assets may be pursued for restitution or fines; travel restrictions and imprisonment may arise through judicial or enforcement proceedings.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: The Taiwan tax authority may recharacterize or adjust taxable income, deny deductions, impose additional tax, interest, penalties, documentation penalties, and withholding assessments, and apply audit escalation for repeated or material non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability to the company or shareholders for approving non-commercial transactions and may face criminal exposure if the arrangements involve falsification, fraud, embezzlement, or intentional tax evasion.
Illegal dividend, capital, or shareholder-fund extraction

Statutory sanction: Invalid distributions may require repayment, corporate and shareholder tax reassessment, withholding tax, interest, penalties, and enforcement under company, tax, foreign-exchange, and insolvency rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may be liable for restoration of improperly distributed assets, creditor damages, breach of fiduciary duty, and criminal offenses involving unlawful distribution, breach of trust, or asset dissipation.
AML, CFT, sanctions, and suspicious-transaction failures

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, compliance orders, business restrictions, license suspension or revocation, freezing or confiscation of assets, and criminal penalties may apply under Taiwan AML and related legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and responsible persons may face personal fines, criminal liability, confiscation, professional disqualification, civil restitution, and legally authorized travel restrictions where they knowingly permitted or recklessly ignored laundering or sanctions conduct.
Beneficial-ownership concealment and nominee structures

Statutory sanction: Registration and AML penalties, tax reassessment, forfeiture, license consequences, corporate dissolution measures, and criminal prosecution may apply depending on the underlying offense.

Director personal liability: Directors and nominees may be personally exposed to civil restitution, confiscation, breach-of-duty claims, criminal fines, imprisonment, and enforcement-related travel restrictions. Veil piercing is fact-dependent and is most likely where the company is an alter ego, instrument of fraud, or vehicle for unlawful asset diversion.
Unlawful custody, client-money commingling, or asset misappropriation

Statutory sanction: Immediate regulatory intervention, account freezing, restitution, disgorgement, administrative penalties, license revocation, and criminal prosecution for breach of trust, fraud, or embezzlement may follow.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, portfolio managers, and controlling persons may face joint civil liability, criminal imprisonment, fines, confiscation, and personal asset recovery. Insolvency does not ordinarily eliminate liability for fraud or misappropriation.
Permanent-establishment and source-of-income mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: Taiwan may assert corporate income tax, withholding tax, penalties, interest, and registration or licensing obligations. Related-party income may be allocated to Taiwan based on functions performed and risks assumed.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face civil and administrative consequences for deliberate misrepresentation, and criminal exposure if false documents or fraudulent declarations are used. Ordinary tax reassessment alone does not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Books, records, and data integrity failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, adverse tax assessment, denial of deductions, recordkeeping penalties, audit escalation, license sanctions, and criminal liability for obstruction or falsification may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may be liable for deliberate concealment, obstruction, or false records. Personal criminal and civil exposure increases where the conduct impairs tax collection, client protection, or regulatory supervision.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally not permissible as the sole registered address for a WFOE engaged in international trade, because the PRC Company Law and the Regulations on the Administration of Market Entity Registration require a registered address that is a fixed, physical location capable of receiving legal documents, and local SAMR and tax authorities routinely reject addresses that are only postal mailboxes or shared virtual offices. Some commercial secretarial addresses in free-trade zones such as Shanghai Pilot Free Trade Zone or Qianhai may be accepted if the zone expressly provides a clustering registration system (集群注册) for trading companies, but under banking and customs due diligence standards for ISIC 4620 raw cotton trade, banks generally require a lease agreement, a physical office inspection report, or a site visit to confirm operational substance. The registered address must match the lease, the business license, tax registration, customs registration, and foreign exchange registration. Failure to maintain physical substance increases the risk of deemed non-tax-resident shell status, customs audit refusal, and bank de-risking on SWIFT LC transactions.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The renminbi is generally convertible for current-account transactions, subject to authenticity, compliance and bank review. Capital-account transactions remain regulated. A WFOE must complete foreign-investment registration and related bank or SAFE procedures before receiving registered foreign-invested capital. Capital contributions, shareholder loans, intercompany funding, outbound dividends and other capital-account movements require transaction-purpose verification, applicable quota or leverage limits, tax compliance and documentary support. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign currency or converted into renminbi through an authorized bank. Profit remittance is normally permitted after annual financial statements, audit where applicable, tax filing and payment, statutory reserve requirements and board or shareholder approval. Banks may require evidence of distributable profit, tax clearance or tax payment records, ownership documents, resolutions and proof that no regulatory restriction applies. Dividend remittances cannot be made from unrecorded or fictitious profits and may be delayed by incomplete foreign-investment, customs, tax, beneficial-owner or sanctions documentation.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
China has no general statutory requirement that all export proceeds be converted into renminbi or surrendered to a bank at a fixed mandatory ratio. Export proceeds may generally be retained in eligible foreign-currency accounts or settled at the enterprise's discretion, subject to SAFE classification, bank review, customs and trade authenticity controls, anti-money-laundering requirements and accurate reporting. Export receipts should be collected in accordance with the underlying contract and declared trade terms; overdue, abnormal or uncollected receivables may trigger bank and SAFE scrutiny. Banks may require customs declarations, commercial invoices, contracts, transport documents, bills of lading, collection records and explanations for discrepancies. Export foreign-exchange income must be recorded and reconciled through the applicable foreign-exchange monitoring framework. No universal statutory conversion deadline or mandatory conversion percentage should be stated without confirming the enterprise's SAFE classification, transaction type and current local implementation rules.

      

      
Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), including the relevant local SAFE branch. The People's Republic of China mainland regime is assumed for the CN WFOE. Taiwan's Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) and Taiwan's foreign-exchange rules apply only if the business is separately incorporated or banked in Taiwan; a Taiwan jurisdiction identifier does not, by itself, change the governing regime of a mainland China WFOE.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Low to Medium for core treasury functions. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment institution cannot replace a mainland bank account for registered foreign-invested capital, regulated foreign-exchange settlement, export-proceeds reporting, dividend remittance or certain trade-finance functions. It may support collection, payment initiation, virtual accounts or foreign-currency disbursements only where its licenses, correspondent-bank arrangements, transaction geography, beneficiary controls and Chinese payment or foreign-exchange rules permit. Offshore funds must not be used to bypass SAFE registration, capital-account controls, customs verification, tax withholding or beneficial-owner screening. A licensed mainland payment institution may be used for permitted payment services but is not a substitute for a bank authorized to conduct the required foreign-exchange and trade-finance transactions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Mismatch between the WFOE's registered business scope, actual raw-cotton or synthetic-yarn trading activity, customs declarations and expected account turnover
        
Root cause: Mismatch between the WFOE's registered business scope, actual raw-cotton or synthetic-yarn trading activity, customs declarations and expected account turnover

        
Defense evidence: Business license; articles of association; approved or recorded foreign-investment information; detailed business model memorandum; supplier and customer contracts; product specifications; logistics flowchart; customs registration; sample invoices; projected monthly turnover; expected currencies; and board-approved treasury policy.

      

      
      

        Opaque beneficial ownership, nominee shareholders, layered offshore ownership or inconsistency between registered investors and the ultimate beneficial owner
        
Root cause: Opaque beneficial ownership, nominee shareholders, layered offshore ownership or inconsistency between registered investors and the ultimate beneficial owner

        
Defense evidence: Ownership chart to natural-person ultimate beneficial owners; passports or national identity documents; proof of address; corporate registry extracts; constitutional documents; shareholder register; certified authorization documents; source-of-funds evidence; and explanations for every ownership layer.

      

      
      

        Foreign-invested capital received before completion of foreign-investment registration, capital-account setup or bank validation
        
Root cause: Foreign-invested capital received before completion of foreign-investment registration, capital-account setup or bank validation

        
Defense evidence: Foreign-investment registration or information-reporting record; business license; capital contribution plan; shareholder resolution; contribution notice; bank account-opening confirmation; remitting shareholder's bank details; remittance instruction; and legal-source-of-funds evidence. Do not remit capital until the receiving bank confirms the correct account and purpose.

      

      
      

        Export receipts inconsistent with customs records, commercial contracts, invoice values, shipment terms or customer geography
        
Root cause: Export receipts inconsistent with customs records, commercial contracts, invoice values, shipment terms or customer geography

        
Defense evidence: Export contract; purchase order; commercial invoice; packing list; customs declaration; bill of lading or transport document; certificate of origin where relevant; inspection or weight certificates for cotton; insurance documents; customer due-diligence file; payment reconciliation; and written explanation of discounts, advances, returns, offsets or delayed collections.

      

      
      

        Cotton, textile raw materials or counterparties linked to sanctions, forced-labor restrictions, embargoed jurisdictions, military end users or enhanced-risk trading routes
        
Root cause: Cotton, textile raw materials or counterparties linked to sanctions, forced-labor restrictions, embargoed jurisdictions, military end users or enhanced-risk trading routes

        
Defense evidence: Country and counterparty screening results; sanctions-screening logs; beneficial-owner verification; end-use and end-user certificates; supply-chain origin records; mill, gin or producer information; forced-labor due-diligence records; shipping-route review; contractual compliance representations; and escalation records for any match or adverse media.

      

      
      

        High-volume pass-through activity, rapid receipt-to-payment cycles, unrelated third-party payments or use of personal accounts
        
Root cause: High-volume pass-through activity, rapid receipt-to-payment cycles, unrelated third-party payments or use of personal accounts

        
Defense evidence: Cash-flow forecast; transaction-by-transaction payment matrix; segregation-of-funds policy; approved supplier and customer lists; intercompany agreement; treasury approval records; reconciliation reports; evidence that payments are made only to contractual counterparties; and written prohibition of personal or unrelated third-party accounts.

      

      
      

        Requested dividend, service-fee, royalty or intercompany repayment remittance without demonstrable tax, accounting and transfer-pricing support
        
Root cause: Requested dividend, service-fee, royalty or intercompany repayment remittance without demonstrable tax, accounting and transfer-pricing support

        
Defense evidence: Audited or approved financial statements; distributable-profit calculation; board or shareholder resolution; tax returns and payment records; withholding-tax analysis; intercompany agreement; transfer-pricing support; invoice; service-delivery evidence; related-party disclosure; and bank remittance application.

      

      
      

        Foreign director or authorized signatory unable to satisfy identity, presence, signature, translation or notarization requirements
        
Root cause: Foreign director or authorized signatory unable to satisfy identity, presence, signature, translation or notarization requirements

        
Defense evidence: Valid passport; notarized and legalized or apostilled documents where required; certified Chinese translations; proof of residential address; power of attorney; specimen signatures; corporate authorization; video-interview availability; local contact details; and evidence of the signatory's role and authority.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Complete the WFOE's foreign-investment registration or information-reporting procedures and confirm the receiving bank's approved foreign-exchange capital-account structure before remittance. The shareholder should remit directly from an account in its own name to the WFOE's designated capital account, using the exact legal entity name, bank account number, SWIFT details and bank-provided purpose or transaction code. The remittance instruction should identify foreign-invested capital or the precise approved funding purpose and must not describe a capital contribution as a trade payment, loan or service fee. Provide the bank with the capital contribution notice, shareholder resolution, ownership documents, source-of-funds evidence, registration records and remittance advice. The bank should verify authenticity, register or report the transaction where required, and document any subsequent settlement into RMB. Shareholder loans require separate foreign-debt, leverage, registration or reporting analysis and must not be commingled with registered capital. Retain SWIFT messages, bank advice, registration records, accounting entries and use-of-funds evidence.Use a mainland bank authorized for cross-border RMB and foreign-exchange settlement. For documentary trade, use a commercial letter of credit governed by the agreed rules, commonly UCP 600, with issuance or confirmation by an acceptable bank and documents matching the credit. For open-account or documentary-collection transactions, use SWIFT payment messaging, documentary collection instructions under URC 522 where applicable, and bank-to-bank settlement to the contractual beneficiary. A conventional customer payment may be transmitted through SWIFT MT103 or its ISO 20022 successor equivalent, subject to the bank's migration and messaging arrangements. The payment file should match the purchase contract, invoice, customs and transport documents, commodity description, quantity, price, currency, beneficiary and sanctions-screening results. Banks may require customs documentation, tax invoices, import or export records, bills of lading, inspection certificates and evidence of the underlying trade. Avoid third-party settlement, split payments, unexplained prepayments, personal accounts and payments to jurisdictions unrelated to the transaction. For cotton, retain weight, grade, origin, inspection, warehouse and shipping records sufficient to reconcile quantity and price.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with an authorized mainland bank, supported by a genuine underlying exposure and documented tenor, currency, notional amount and settlement purpose.Renminbi foreign-exchange swaps or other permitted currency swaps with an authorized financial institution, subject to bank suitability review, underlying exposure requirements and applicable market rules.Foreign-exchange options or structured hedges only where permitted by the authorized bank, suitable for the enterprise and supported by documented risk-management objectives; speculative or excessively leveraged structures should be prohibited.Non-deliverable forwards may be available through offshore or authorized institutions for selected currencies and exposures, but their use requires separate review of the bank's authorization, cross-border settlement mechanics, accounting, tax, derivatives documentation, reporting and China's foreign-exchange rules. An offshore NDF must not be used to evade mainland FX controls.Plain-vanilla forwards, swaps and options should be governed by the bank's master agreement, confirmations, collateral terms, valuation and close-out provisions. The enterprise should maintain a board-approved derivatives policy, exposure register, hedge designation records, counterparty limits, independent valuation and prohibition on uncovered speculative trading.

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
20% standard Profit-Seeking Enterprise Income Tax rate for Taiwan resident companies; applies to taxable income exceeding NT$180,000

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
21% withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders not having a permanent establishment in Taiwan (Article 88 and Article 73 Income Tax Act).

    

    

      
Mandatory Return Breakdown

      
      

        
        
Deadline:  · Frequency: 

        
Annual return must be filed within the fifth month after the fiscal year end; for calendar year companies the deadline is May 31. For companies adopting a special fiscal year, the deadline is within five months of the year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Local File / Transfer Pricing Report required where related-party transactions exceed NT$50 million in any taxable year; Master File and Country-by-Country Report may be required for MNC groups with consolidated group revenue above NT$54 billion or as designated by the Ministry of Finance.

      
Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing regime comparable to offshore financial centers; however, economic substance is directly enforced through transfer pricing examination, the general anti-avoidance rule under Article 12-1 Income Tax Act, and Article 43-1 Transfer Pricing Adjustment. A WFOE engaged in raw cotton export must demonstrate real physical office, operational staff, decision-making control, contract negotiation, risk retention, and bank account management in Taiwan; otherwise the tax authority may recharacterize transactions and reattribute income to the foreign parent or a main-controlling entity.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion Through Fictitious Invoicing / Revenue Undervaluation
        
Statutory sanction: Under Article 41 Tax Collection Act, imprisonment for up to 5 years, detention, or fine for fraud-based tax evasion; the tax authority will assess the undeclared tax plus an underpayment penalty of up to 20% of the shortfall, together with monthly interest. Serious cases may trigger criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code for fraud.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who sign or direct fraudulent returns can be criminally liable as principals. In sham-entity situations, courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold actual controllers jointly and severally liable. Tax arrears over the statutory threshold may trigger departure restrictions under Article 24 Tax Collection Act.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Base Erosion
        
Statutory sanction: Under Article 43-1 Income Tax Act, the tax authority may recharacterize the transaction and recompute taxable income at arm's length. The adjustment creates additional tax liability, an understatement penalty up to 20% of the assessed deficiency, and interest. Intentional failure to file mandatory transfer pricing documentation strengthens the authority's discretionary estimate of profit.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or approve artificial pricing arrangements may face personal criminal liability for tax evasion if the conduct is fraudulent; civil joint liability may arise where the subsidiary is merely an alter ego of the foreign parent.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Taiwan tax authorities may invoke the general anti-avoidance rule under Article 12-1 Income Tax Act to disregard the legal form, attribute business income to the real controlling entity, disallow losses, and assess income under the deemed-profit method. Company registration may be cancelled for false filings, and bank accounts may be frozen during enforcement.

        
Director personal liability: Nominee directors and beneficial owners who create a facade with intent to evade tax may be personally liable for taxes assessed, subject to civil restitution, and face criminal imprisonment. Travel bans and asset freezing orders can be imposed under tax collection enforcement measures.

      

      
      

        Money Laundering / AML Red Flags
        
Statutory sanction: Under Taiwan's Money Laundering Control Act, laundering proceeds derived from a serious crime carries imprisonment of up to 7 years and a fine of up to NT$50 million. Financial institutions are obliged to file suspicious transaction reports; failure to comply with AML obligations exposes designated entities to administrative fines and remedial orders.

        
Director personal liability: Company directors and officers complicit in laundering schemes face direct criminal prosecution; they may be jointly liable for confiscation of proceeds and civil damages. Individual travel bans can be imposed during investigation, and participation may trigger extended imprisonment terms.

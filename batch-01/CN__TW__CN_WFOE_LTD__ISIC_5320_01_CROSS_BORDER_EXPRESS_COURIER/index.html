None under the Taiwan Companies Act; no statutory minimum paid-in capital and no blocked escrow account is required by law. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Taxation Agency, Ministry of Finance (MOF), Taiwan, and the regional National Taxation Bureau (NTB); electronic portals: MOF Electronic Tax Filing and Payment Services (e-Tax Portal) and MOF Electronic Invoice Integrated Service Platform

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain registry recognition under the Company Act through the Department of Commerce, Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA), and, if the shareholder is mainland-of-China based, obtain the Investment Commission approval required for mainland-invested enterprises. Step 2: Before commencing business, file the statutory tax registration application with the NTB under Article 28 of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act; the NTB issues the Unified Business Number/TIN and registers the WFOE as a VAT taxpayer. Step 3: For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a Taiwan fixed place of business, register online through the MOF e-Tax Portal or by appointing a Taiwan tax representative; the foreign incorporation certificate, power of attorney and service/sales contract are submitted, and the NTB assigns a withholding/TIN and, where Taiwan-source digital/e-service sales exceed the announced de minimis amount, a business-tax VAT registration. Step 4: After TIN issuance, enrol in the MOF Electronic Invoice Integrated Service Platform and set up the Electronic Uniform Invoice Turnkey/API channel before issuing invoices.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a WFOE with a Taiwan fixed place of business, there is no de minimis turnover threshold: tax registration is mandatory before commencement. Small-scale Taiwan operators with average monthly taxable sales below TWD 200,000 may qualify for simplified assessed business tax at 1% instead of the standard 5% rate, but remain tax-registered; non-resident suppliers of cross-border e-services to Taiwan individuals are required to register when Taiwan-source taxable sales exceed the Ministry of Finance announced de minimis threshold currently applied in the cross-border e-service regime (TWD 480,000 per year).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, the following cross-border express courier revenue is eligible for the 0% rate: (i) export shipments where goods are covered by a Taiwan customs export entry and physically leave the territory; (ii) international transport/courier services that are directly attributable to the carriage of export goods from Taiwan to a foreign destination; and (iii) services supplied to a foreign contracting party that are used outside Taiwan and paid in foreign currency, evidenced by contract and bank foreign-exchange settlement records. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated export revenue is creditable and refundable as an excess credit. Domestic pickup, storage, sorting or final-mile delivery performed solely in Taiwan and not part of the export carriage remains taxable at 5%, so the WFOE must maintain a documented allocation between taxable Taiwan services and zero-rated export services.

  • Taiwan Customs Export Entry (Export Declaration) bearing the customs approval number and export statistics
  • Carrier-issued Master Air Waybill or House Air Waybill for air express exports, or Bill of Lading for ocean/combined transport
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration
  • Proof of delivery (POD) or overseas consignment tracking record showing receipt by the foreign consignee
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate / export proceeds collection record issued by an authorized foreign-exchange bank (Taiwan equivalent

Taiwan mandates a centralized e-invoice reporting model rather than pre-approval fiscal clearance. Under Article 32 of the Business Tax Act, every taxable sale of courier/express services must be documented with a Uniform Invoice. The MOF requires active corporate taxpayers to issue Electronic Uniform Invoices (EUI/cloud invoices) and transmit the EUI XML payloads through the MOF Turnkey connection or a certified Value-Added Service Center API to the MOF Electronic Invoice Integrated Service Platform: B2C invoices are transmitted within 48 hours of issue and B2B invoices within 7 days. Each EUI contains a mandatory 8-digit random serial number, a QR code, and an encrypted message authentication code/digital stamp, allowing anti-fraud validation by the NTB in near-real time. Untimely or non-transmission is treated as failure to issue a Uniform Invoice, attracting penalties and possible administrative suspension.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PBoC/SAFE regime maintains a managed floating RMB system with current-account convertibility but not full capital-account convertibility. Current-account items such as cross-border courier charges, logistics fees, and payments to overseas agents are generally settled and paid through designated domestic foreign exchange banks after the bank reviews genuine transaction documentation. Capital-account flows, including the Taiwanese shareholder equity contribution into the WFOE, shareholder loans, and subsequent profit repatriation, are separately controlled. The Taiwan-invested WFOE must complete SAFE foreign direct investment registration, open a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, and receive the equity injection through that account. Conversion of capital into RMB is permitted only on a payment-purpose basis under the SAFE 'settlement based on payment' principle; funds cannot be used for equity or securities investment or any non-operating purpose. Export and service receipts must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange settlement account; there is no mandatory conversion ratio, but retained foreign currency is subject to SAFE account quotas and bank monitoring. Profit remittance to Taiwanese shareholders is permitted only after audited financial statements are prepared, tax clearance including dividend withholding is confirmed, SAFE/FDI compliance is verified, and a lawful board resolution authorizes the dividend. The onshore remitting bank will require the correct balance-of-payments declaration purpose code and will process the outward remittance only after the full compliance file is cleared.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Measures for Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Administration, there is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts; 0% of export proceeds are required to be surrendered. The exporter may retain the full amount in a foreign exchange settlement account or convert to RMB as it chooses. However, all export proceeds must be repatriated to China and credited to a domestic foreign exchange account within the commercial settlement period agreed in the underlying contract. If collection exceeds 90 days from the customs declaration date, the WFOE must file a deferred export receipt collection report with SAFE through the trade credit reporting system. The receiving bank will match the incoming remittance against the export customs declaration number and the applicable SAFE trade-in-goods receipt code before crediting the proceeds to the settlement account.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Non-viable as a primary treasury rail in mainland China. Offshore fintech EMIs and non-bank digital banks are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to hold WFOE deposit balances, execute foreign exchange settlement, or clear local payroll and tax payments. A Taiwan-invested WFOE in China must maintain its operational liquidity and perform all RMB and FX settlement through a licensed onshore banking institution. An offshore EMI may at most act as an auxiliary payment aggregator for international online collections if it partners with a licensed PRC payment institution, but it cannot substitute for a Tier-1 domestic clearing bank. Attempting to route WFOE operating funds through an unlicensed EMI would create an immediate AML red flag and cause the domestic bank to reject remittance onboarding.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwanese investor remits the equity contribution via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account. The remittance underlying purpose must be stated as the equity capital contribution into a wholly foreign-owned enterprise under the direct investment category of the PBOC balance-of-payments classification. Both the Taiwan remitting bank and the mainland receiving bank must reference the SAFE FDI registration certificate. After receipt, the WFOE applies to convert the capital into RMB under the settlement-based-on-payment rule by submitting the underlying commercial contracts, invoices, and payment instructions; the bank will release RMB only to designated operating or construction payees. No converted RMB may be used for equity investment, securities purchase, entrusted lending, or real estate not connected to the approved business. Within 30 days of the capital injection, a licensed Chinese CPA issues a capital verification report and the report is retained by the company, the market regulator, and the bank. For day-to-day operational RMB funding, the investor can remit through a current-account settlement entry using the correct courier/logistics fee purpose code; that route avoids capital-account restrictions but still requires genuine service transaction documentation.Outbound payments to overseas courier agents, interline carriers, and logistics vendors are cleared primarily through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers. High-value or recurring freight obligations can be supported by Commercial Letters of Credit issued under ICC UCP 600, while documentary collections in either DP or DA form are used when the WFOE needs to exchange transport documents against payment. All outbound foreign exchange payments require the underlying contract, customs or transport evidence where applicable, the correct SAFE payment purpose code, and any necessary withholding tax clearance certificates. Domestic operating costs, payroll, and tax payments are settled through the PBOC high-value payment system and local RMB clearing channels, with same-day or T+1 settlement. The WFOE must maintain a separate tax payment account and the bank's cross-border invoice reconciliation file to prove that outbound payments correspond to real logistical services received.Deliverable CNY/USD forward contracts are permissible for up to 12 months where the WFOE can demonstrate a genuine courier fee receivable or payable exposure; the hedge must be on a real-demand basis and cannot be speculative.Cross-currency swaps between CNY and USD or TWD are permissible when the notional and maturity match an underlying foreign currency loan, capital account flow, or long-term freight contract, and the transaction is filed with and governed by SAFE.FX swaps are permissible for short-term liquidity transformation within the operating settlement cycle, subject to the bank's internal credit line and SAFE reporting.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally not permissible for onshore Chinese entities because RMB is deliverable onshore; NDF access is reserved for offshore counterparties, so the WFOE should execute a deliverable forward or cross-currency swap through its licensed onshore bank instead.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Non-viable as a primary treasury rail in mainland China. Offshore fintech EMIs and non-bank digital banks are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to hold WFOE deposit balances, execute foreign exchange settlement, or clear local payroll and tax payments. A Taiwan-invested WFOE in China must maintain its operational liquidity and perform all RMB and FX settlement through a licensed onshore banking institution. An offshore EMI may at most act as an auxiliary payment aggregator for international online collections if it partners with a licensed PRC payment institution, but it cannot substitute for a Tier-1 domestic clearing bank. Attempting to route WFOE operating funds through an unlicensed EMI would create an immediate AML red flag and cause the domestic bank to reject remittance onboarding.

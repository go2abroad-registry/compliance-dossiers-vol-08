Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Taiwan Company Act: there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a company limited by shares. Nevertheless, the full authorized capital must be subscribed, and 100% of subscribed cash capital must be paid into a NT$ designated bank capital account in Taiwan before the statutory registration application is filed. A licensed Taiwan CPA must issue a capital verification report confirming the cash deposit. Where a mainland China investor participates, the capital remittance must be from the approved mainland investment project and must remain blocked from operational disbursement until the Certificate of Incorporation is issued and the company bank account mandate is perfected. A zero-share or shell-capital structure cannot satisfy the capital verification rules.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Tax Administration, Republic of China (Taiwan), acting through the applicable regional National Taxation Bureau; electronic filings are submitted through the MOF e-Filing (Tax E-Net) portal and the Cloud Invoice Integration Service Platform for uniform invoice/e-invoice data.

E-government filing portal

The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is first incorporated as a Taiwan limited company, with foreign investors obtaining investment approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs. After incorporation, the company must file business tax registration with the regional National Taxation Bureau using the statutory application package, company registration certificate, articles of incorporation, legal representative and shareholder identity documents, business premise proof, and accounting/book records; the Bureau will issue the eight-digit Business Administration Number that functions as the TIN and VAT registration number. For foreign non-resident corporate shareholders, certified corporate existence documents, board resolutions, powers of attorney naming a Taiwan resident tax agent, and evidence of inward capital remittance must be submitted, translated and notarized as required. A non-resident entity selling digital services solely to Taiwan B2C customers may instead apply for simplified cross-border electronic service provider registration through the Ministry of Finance online registration mechanism; if it does not register, a Taiwan B2B customer must self-assess the 5% VAT by reverse charge.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A Taiwan-established joint venture has no general monetary turnover threshold and must register before starting taxable business. A non-resident cross-border electronic service provider selling digital services to Taiwan individuals must register if annual B2C turnover exceeds TWD 480,000; below that threshold registration is optional, and B2B supplies by an unregistered foreign provider are subject to reverse-charge VAT by the Taiwan buyer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 7 of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act where export goods are actually dispatched from Taiwan customs or where services, including remote screen-share and IoT telemetry software, are sold to non-resident customers and are performed/used entirely outside Taiwan. For services, the export contract, delivery/usage evidence, and receipt of consideration in foreign currency are required; the zero-rated turnover must be stated separately on the uniform invoice and included as zero-rated export turnover in the bimonthly business tax return. Input VAT attributable to such exports may be carried forward or refunded.

  • Customs export declaration (export declaration form) released by Taiwan Customs
  • Commercial invoice and, where applicable, zero-rated uniform invoice
  • Bill of lading (ocean) or air waybill (air cargo)
  • Packing list
  • Foreign exchange inward remittance certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange declaration form evidencing collection from the foreign buyer
  • Export service contract and performance evidence (service logs, telemetry access records, or delivery reports) demonstrating offshore use for remote software and IoT services

Taiwan mandates the Uniform Invoice system. Businesses that use electronic invoices must transmit invoice data to the Ministry of Finance Cloud Invoice Integration Service Platform via the certified Turnkey gateway or an API connector, submit the bimonthly business tax return by the 15th day of the month following the two-month reporting period, and retain invoice issuance and payment records. The regime is not an ex-ante real-time fiscal clearance model, but it combines serialized uniform invoice numbers, printed random four-digit anti-counterfeit validation codes, return/payment cross-matching, and periodic tax authority audits to deter fraud.

The regional National Taxation Bureau audits zero-rated export documentation on a transactional basis. If documentation is incomplete, forged, or shows the service was actually used in Taiwan, the tax authority will reclassify the sale as taxable, issue a deficiency assessment plus interest, and impose administrative penalties up to five times the evaded business tax, with possible suspension of business. Fraudulent export documentation or false foreign-exchange repatriation evidence may additionally trigger criminal tax-evasion liability and disqualify the taxpayer from simplified cross-border registration and export-refund incentives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) regulates the interbank FX market. The New Taiwan Dollar is freely convertible for current-account purposes; ordinary receipts from software licensing, telemetry subscription, technical support, royalties and third-party payment settlement can be processed through authorized FX banks under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act without prior central-bank approval, but each transaction requires a CBC foreign-exchange declaration and supporting commercial documentation. Capital-account transactions are controlled: inbound equity investment from a mainland Chinese shareholder requires prior MOEA Investment Commission clearance, SAFE outbound-investment registration, and a CBC settlement declaration before the offshore remittance is booked as paid-in capital. No general surrender requirement applies in normal times, so export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency deposit accounts or converted to TWD at the prevailing market rate. Profit remittance to the PRC parent will be treated as a capital-account dividend outflow and requires audited financial statements, tax clearance, board dividend minutes, joint-venture contract provisions and a validated outward remittance declaration. Direct TWD/RMB clearing is limited; cross-strait treasury flows are preferentially cleared in USD or CNH via Hong Kong.

Export proceeds surrender rules

In normal operations Taiwan does not impose a mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; the CBC has removed the old 100% surrender rule. Exporters must repatriate and collect the proceeds through an authorized FX bank within the 180-day settlement window measured from the date of export customs declaration, unless an extension is granted for service exports, and then either deposit the funds in a foreign-currency account or settle to TWD at the bank's posted rate. A zero-percent conversion ratio applies in ordinary circumstances; if the CBC exercises its emergency balance-of-payments powers, it can require forced conversion of specific export proceeds within the timelines stated in the emergency notice, but this has not been enacted in the current regulatory environment.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC); for mainland China sourced outbound flows, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China must also approve and file the PRC parent's outbound investment.

E-government filing portal

A foreign company operating through a Taiwan branch must complete branch establishment and tax registration and obtain a unified business number before commencing taxable operations. Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a business with no fixed place of business, the general monthly-sales thresholds are NT$80,000 for sales of goods and NT$40,000 for sales of services. Foreign electronic-service providers supplying services to individuals in Taiwan generally have a separate annual-sales registration threshold of NT$600,000. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The Business Tax Act generally permits zero-rating for qualifying exports of goods, qualifying services related to exports, and services supplied in Taiwan but used outside Taiwan, subject to statutory conditions and documentary substantiation. The fact that a customer is overseas is not, by itself, sufficient to establish that a service qualifies. A regulatory-consulting engagement must be assessed by reference to where the service is performed and used, its connection to an export, the customer and transaction facts, and the prescribed evidence. Eligible zero-rated sales may support input-tax refund claims subject to statutory restrictions, verification, and filing requirements.

  • Customs export declaration and evidence of export clearance for exported goods
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, and transport evidence, such as a bill of lading or airway bill, where applicable
  • For services, engagement contract, invoices, evidence of the recipient and the place of use or benefit, work records, and payment evidence supporting the claimed zero-rating category
  • Bank remittance or other payment records where relevant to substantiate consideration; an inward remittance certificate is not a universally prescribed document for every zero-rated transaction

Taiwan’s uniform-invoice system and e-Invoice Platform apply to businesses within the relevant invoicing rules, with phased or category-specific requirements and prescribed invoice transmission and reporting deadlines. Businesses must issue, transmit, and retain invoices in the required formats and comply with buyer-specific or government-platform requirements. Taiwan does not generally impose universal real-time pre-clearance or digital stamping of every invoice before issuance; applicable requirements depend on taxpayer category and transaction.

Taiwan does not impose a general 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline applicable to all export proceeds. Foreign-exchange reporting, declaration, and settlement requirements may apply depending on the transaction and amount. The taxpayer must retain records supporting zero-rating and input-tax refunds and produce them for tax review. Disallowed zero-rating can result in assessment of business tax, interest and applicable penalties; false declarations, fabricated evidence, or improper refund claims may trigger additional statutory fines and, where the facts satisfy applicable elements, criminal liability. The authority may recover improperly refunded tax and assess tax retrospectively within the applicable statutory assessment period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese Renminbi (CNY) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, subject to verification of authenticity by the designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions remain heavily regulated and require prior approval or filing with SAFE. Profit remittance by a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is treated as a current account item, but requires verification of prior tax payment via the Tax Payment Certificate and Audited Financial Statements. Foreign-invested enterprises may freely remit FDI-related dividends once the relevant tax obligations under the Corporate Income Tax Law and the Double Tax Agreement between China and Taiwan (Applied through the Cross-Strait Agreement on Avoidance of Double Taxation) have been satisfied. For MedTech regulatory consulting fees receivable from an overseas entity, an 'Original Receipt' and a 'Service Trade and Foreign Exchange Payment/Receipt Deed' are required to support the current account debit. No mandatory CNY conversion applies to current account receipts; however, the enterprise must retain 30% of export proceeds in a foreign currency account if the proceeds exceed the statutory threshold, unless an exemption is granted by SAFE. For detailed cross-strait payment routing, the use of third-party payment institutions or 'Cross-Border RMB Settlement' may apply, but full CAB remittance documentation must be retained for a minimum of five years.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As of the current SAFE regime (post-2010 liberalization), export proceeds in foreign currency are to be repatriated into China within 90 days from the date of export declaration (unless an extension is approved, up to 6 months). The exporter must register the foreign exchange receipt date and amount with SAFE via the SAFE Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Current account export proceeds have no mandatory conversion ratio; they may be held in a foreign currency settlement account or converted at the enterprise's discretion. However, if the proceeds arise from a cross-strait trade with a Taiwan counterparty, the 'offshore' nature of Taiwan in China's FX regime requires a cross-strait remittance to be reported as 'Cross-strait Trade Settlement'; proceeds must be routed through a bank licensed for cross-strait RMB and foreign exchange clearing, and the Taiwan counterparty's bank must be a participant in the Cross-Strait Clearing Bank Mechanism. For forward foreign exchange hedging, the enterprise must evidence a real underlying trade exposure with valid customs declarations and invoices. The surrender timeline for export proceeds from consulting services (ISIC 7490) is not a statutory 'surrender' per se; rather, the service receipt must be credited to a SAFE-monitored account within 30 days of the invoice maturity, and the related contract must be filed with SAFE if the contract value exceeds USD 300,000 (or equivalent). Non-compliance can trigger a penalty of up to 5% of the non-compliant amount, a fine against the designated bank, and possible revocation of the FX qualification.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and virtual banking platforms are NOT viable as the primary corporate bank account for the China-based JV entity for actual settlement, given China's closed capital market and the requirement for physical licensed bank presence within the jurisdiction. EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Mercury cannot serve as the legally recognized receiving bank for the JV's inbound regulatory consulting fees (ISIC 7490) or for corporate tax payments to the local tax bureau. They are viable only as ancillary collection conduits for overseas receipts generated by a separate Hong Kong or Singapore subsidiary that bills the foreign client outside China; the funds can then be injected into the Chinese JV only via a registered FDI equity injection or via a current-account service fee payment with full SAFE documentation. Legal constraints include: (i) no ability to obtain a RMB account number assigned by PBoC; (ii) no direct participation in the China National Advanced Payment System (CNAPS) or the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS); (iii) rejection by Chinese tax authorities for trade-related documentary compliance (Fapiao cannot be issued from an EMI account); (iv) potential violation of the 'Penetration Testing' rule under the Personal Information Protection Law if credit-card processing is routed offshore without a domestic clearing license. Therefore, feasibility rating: Low (for primary treasury infrastructure), Acceptable (as an offshore collection rail only if a clear inter-company service agreement exists).

Defense evidence: A clear explanation that the entity is not a payment institution and that card payments are received only through a licensed domestic acquiring bank (e.g., UnionPay merchant account) or through a compliant third-party aggregator (e.g., PingPong) that is registered with SAFE for cross-border card settlement. A copy of the Payment Institution Business License and the cross-border payment agreement, together with a self-assessment that the entity's reliance on card payments is capped at 20% of total turnover, and a declaration that all card payments are accompanied by a service order and a Fapiao (special VAT invoice) to support current account receivables. If the card payment is received through a Taiwan or US payment gateway, the entity must ensure that the gateway remits the net amount via a compliant bank wire, and that no card-scheme settlement is received into the Chinese JV without a fully cleared documentation trail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The JV's initial capital injection (from Taiwan or other non-China shareholders) must be routed as follows: i) The Taiwan investor remits the capital amount through a bank in Taiwan that is a participant in the Cross-Strait RMB Clearing Mechanism (e.g., Bank of Taiwan or CTBC Bank) to the JV's Capital Foreign Exchange Settlement Account (FDI Account) opened at any Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank; ii) The remittance instruction must use SAFE's capital account purpose code 'FDI Equity Subscription' (code 601101) or 'Foreign Investor's Contribution to Registered Capital' (code 601102) and must clearly indicate the 'Capital Injection' purpose to avoid being misclassified as a current account transfer; iii) The Chinese bank will require a pre-registration certificate issued by SAFE, or the bank's internal 'FDI Foreign Exchange Registration' system receipt, evidencing the approved total FDI quota; iv) The inbound wire must be done in the original currency (USD or TWD) or CNY if using cross-strait RMB clearing; v) Within 5 business days after the credit, the bank initiates a 'Capital Paid-in Verification' process with SAFE; vi) Disbursement of the capital into operational CNY accounts is permitted only after the bank has verified the 'Confirmation of Deferred Payment' and the tax registration certificate for the capital contribution (mainly for stamp tax). No cash withdrawal is permitted from the capital account; all fund flows downstream must be supported by invoices, contracts, and payroll records. For inbound payments of regulatory consulting fees (service export), the routing is simpler: the Taiwan or US client wires funds via SWIFT MT103 to the JV's Current Account (Open FX Settlement Account), with the remittance purpose 'Service Trade – Medical Device Regulatory Consulting' (code 114010), and the JV must upload digital contracts with a legitimate signature to the bank's 'Service Trade Deed' system. Verification of the end-client's identity and the project timeline is required; the bank must see the consulting contract, the invoice, and the receipt of prior payments to evidence fair market value. All inbound wires are subject to the 180-day 'receipt recognition' window and the 5-year documentary retention rule.The JV must disburse trade-related outbound funds (contractually owed to the home/parent entity for software costs, licenses, or sub-contractor fees) using the following rails: i) For any payment to the Taiwan parent or entity (in TWD), use the Cross-Strait RMB Settlement (if payer/payee agree) or USD via SWIFT MT103; the Chinese bank will require a 'Transaction Payment Deed for Cross-Strait Service Trade', the signed consulting agreement, and a Fapiao (issued by the Chinese JV to the Taiwan entity) reflecting the service revenue; ii) Use of a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) is reserved for goods-based trade; for the MedTech consulting scope, issuance of a commercial L/C is not appropriate, and the correct rail is an 'Account Payable Transfer' using MT103 with purpose code 'Service Fee for Medical Device Consulting' (code 114020) and supporting documents; iii) To control conversion risk, the JV may transact with its bank to sell CNY forward through a 'Dividend Swap' or 'Trade Forward' – the bank will require the JV to prove the underlying payable with a contract signed at least 30 days prior, and the JV can only use the bank's admissible forward product; iv) For any US/EU sub-consultant payments, the JV should route via the same bank's international wire module, specifying the beneficiary's IBAN and purpose 'Payment for FDA/CE Regulatory Sub-consulting', and must retain the sub-consultant's invoice, an IRS Form W-8BEN-E (for US payee) or EU VAT proof (for EU payee), to ensure that the withholding tax obligations under China's Domestic Tax Law (10% on service fees paid abroad) are settled and that the bank can verify the tax clearance; v) To minimize duplicate FX spreads, the JV should open a multi-currency account with a 2-way 'Designated FX account' and use natural hedging: if liabilities are primarily in USD and receivables in USD, settle intra-group balances via netting once a month, provided the net settlement does not exceed USD 100,000 and the bank approves a netting arrangement; vi) All outbound wires for trade (service) settlement must have a speed of execution between 1 and 2 business days, subject to the bank's internal verification limits; for amounts above USD 300,000, SAFE's Electronic Tax-Payment Certification system (or TCS) clearance must be obtained on the same day before the wire can be executed.CNY/USD Bank Forward (through a permitted bank with a qualified underlying trade or capital transaction)CNY/USD Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) permitted only to hedge cross-strait service fee flows, strictly requiring SAFE's 'Real Need' verification and a maturity date aligned with the invoice due dateCNY/USD Currency Swap (Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap – CCS) for hedges matching the tenor of a long-term consulting contract, subject to a one-year minimum and evidence of continuous trade exposureCross-Strait RMB Swap (CNY/TWD spot or offshore swap) via a bank that participates in the Cross-Strait Clearing Mechanism, only if the underlying is a verified Taiwan service trade or capital investment transactionFX Option (European-style outright) permitted for hedging a confirmed future receipt from a US medical-device manufacturer, with the bank requiring an acknowledgment that no speculative option premium arbitrage existsIntra-group balance sheet netting and natural hedging (multi-currency account) – permitted as a treasury technique without requiring SAFE approval if all underlying exposures are real trade-related

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and virtual banking platforms are NOT viable as the primary corporate bank account for the China-based JV entity for actual settlement, given China's closed capital market and the requirement for physical licensed bank presence within the jurisdiction. EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Mercury cannot serve as the legally recognized receiving bank for the JV's inbound regulatory consulting fees (ISIC 7490) or for corporate tax payments to the local tax bureau. They are viable only as ancillary collection conduits for overseas receipts generated by a separate Hong Kong or Singapore subsidiary that bills the foreign client outside China; the funds can then be injected into the Chinese JV only via a registered FDI equity injection or via a current-account service fee payment with full SAFE documentation. Legal constraints include: (i) no ability to obtain a RMB account number assigned by PBoC; (ii) no direct participation in the China National Advanced Payment System (CNAPS) or the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS); (iii) rejection by Chinese tax authorities for trade-related documentary compliance (Fapiao cannot be issued from an EMI account); (iv) potential violation of the 'Penetration Testing' rule under the Personal Information Protection Law if credit-card processing is routed offshore without a domestic clearing license. Therefore, feasibility rating: Low (for primary treasury infrastructure), Acceptable (as an offshore collection rail only if a clear inter-company service agreement exists).

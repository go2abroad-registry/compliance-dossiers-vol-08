Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Taiwan has no general statutory minimum paid-up capital for an ordinary limited company or company limited by shares. The subscribed capital must be sufficient for the proposed business and must be fully paid before registration. Foreign-invested capital must follow the approved investment-remittance procedure; no general statutory escrow account is required after verification.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Republic of China (Taiwan) - Tax Administration through the National Taxation Bureaus; official electronic portal is the MOF Tax Portal at https://www.tax.nat.gov.tw, with the Electronic Invoice Integration Service Platform at https://www.einvoice.nat.gov.tw serving as the statutory e-invoice exchange infrastructure.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity or a Taiwan-resident JV with non-resident shareholders, the statutory Tax ID (Unified Business Number) is obtained by completing tax registration at the local National Taxation Bureau after company registration has been certified by the Ministry of Economic Affairs. The procedure is: (1) submit the incorporation documents, the approved articles of association, the foreign investor identification or parent company certificate, the internal revenue registration application, and proof of the domestic business premises; (2) receive the eight-digit Unified Business Number as the combined corporate income tax and VAT file number; (3) where the entity is a non-resident enterprise without a fixed place of business, apply for a non-resident profit-seeking enterprise tax file number with the National Taxation Bureau before making the first Taiwan tax filing or refund claim. No foreign-entity check-the-box or elective classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NT$80,000 average monthly taxable sales; businesses whose average monthly sales exceed NT$80,000 must register as business tax taxpayers. If average monthly sales exceed NT$200,000, the business must issue uniform invoices and is a standard 5% VAT payer. Small businesses between NT$80,000 and NT$200,000 average monthly sales are subject to a special 1% business tax and cannot claim input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Articles 7 and 8 of the Business Tax Act is available where the taxpayer proves actual export of goods from Taiwan customs territory, documented by a valid Customs export declaration, or where Taiwanese services (including qualifying offshore digital services) are provided to nonresident persons, consumed or performed exclusively outside Taiwan, and paid for in foreign currency. The zero-rated sale must be recorded on a zero-rated uniform invoice and matching foreign exchange receipt evidence must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export clearance serial number) issued by the Customs Administration, Ministry of Finance
  • Marine Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical carriage out of Taiwan
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bank-issued Foreign Exchange Proceeds Settlement Certificate or Foreign Currency Receipt Confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Standard VAT payers in Taiwan must issue and transmit electronic uniform invoices under the mandatory e-invoice scheme. Invoices must be created through an MOF-certified Turnkey transmission software or an approved service provider, and the invoice and recipient identification data must be uploaded to the Ministry of Finance Electronic Invoice Integration Service Platform within 48 hours of issuance. The system performs automatic fiscal reconciliation and assigns a random encrypted digital invoice number for anti-fraud verification. Failure to transmit electronically, use of uncertified invoice software, or late transmission can result in disallowance of input VAT deductions and statutory penalties.

On audit, the National Taxation Bureau will claw back the 5% VAT, assess late interest, and impose a delinquency penalty of up to five times the amount of tax evaded or at least NT$30,000 under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act if the exporter cannot present the Customs export declaration, transport documents, and foreign exchange settlement evidence. Fabrication of banking or customs records can expose the taxpayer to criminal tax evasion liability and related fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan dollar (TWD) is not subject to a general requirement to convert all current-account receipts or payments into TWD. Authorized banks process foreign-exchange transactions subject to applicable declaration, documentation, and reporting requirements. Current-account payments, including trade and bona fide service payments, are generally remittable against supporting documents. Capital-account transactions, including foreign investment, capital contributions, loans, securities investments, and repatriation of investment proceeds, may require prior approval, registration, or reporting depending on investor status, transaction type, and applicable sector restrictions. Dividends and liquidation proceeds are not automatically remittable: the company should establish distributable profits, satisfy tax and corporate-law requirements, and provide bank and investment-registration evidence. Mainland Chinese investment into Taiwan is subject to distinct approval and restriction rules and must not be treated as ordinary foreign investment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan generally does not impose a universal statutory deadline to surrender export proceeds or a mandatory conversion ratio requiring exporters to convert foreign-currency receipts into TWD. Receipts remain subject to authorized-bank processing, applicable foreign-exchange declarations and reporting thresholds, and documentary checks. The bank should classify each receipt correctly and retain the export, invoice, contract, and shipping evidence required for the transaction. Confirm current CBC rules and applicable thresholds with the servicing bank before execution; rules differ for mainland China and must be assessed separately if the entity is incorporated or operating there.

Central bank supervision: For the specified Taiwan jurisdiction, the competent monetary authority is the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC); the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) supervises financial institutions and relevant financial activities. The payload also identifies China as the sovereign country and describes a Sino-foreign equity joint venture. Those identifiers do not establish that the entity is a Taiwan company or that it may operate in Taiwan. Confirm the place of incorporation, investor nationality, and intended place of business before applying either jurisdiction's rules. Mainland China and Taiwan have separate legal, banking, and foreign-investment regimes.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Final return due within 5 months after the close of the fiscal year (calendar-year companies: 31 May). A provisional self-assessed tax payment is required by 30 September of the current year. A CPA-audited extension to 30 June is available for large enterprises.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where annual related-party transaction amounts exceed NT$100 million (threshold adjusted annually by MOF); Master File and Country-by-Country Report required when the consolidated global revenue of the ultimate parent group exceeds NT$27 billion.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan has no separate Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the Tax Collection Act and Income Tax Act (Articles 43-1, 43-2) embody an economic-substance and substance-over-form standard. The GP entity must demonstrate a fixed place of business in Taiwan, at least two full-time employees, active board and investment-committee meetings in Taiwan, and independent decision-making. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-reporting

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of up to 3x the tax evaded; criminal fine of up to NT$60 million and imprisonment up to 5 years under Income Tax Act Article 110 and Tax Collection Act Article 41; late-payment surcharge of 1% per two days up to 15%.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign returns known to be false are joint tortfeasors; the court may pierce the veil and order personal civil restitution of the evaded tax plus interest, issue a travel ban under Tax Collection Act Article 24, freeze personal bank accounts, and impose a sentence of imprisonment of 1 to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Income is fully reassessed at an arm's-length profit; operating expenses are disallowed; tax benefit credits are reversed; a deemed dividend is assessed to the non-resident parent with 21% WHT; the company registration may be struck off under the Company Act.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil under Company Act Article 154, making controlling directors and shareholders personally and jointly liable for the company's unpaid tax debts; directors may face travel bans and an order to liquidate personal assets.
AML / KYC Failure of the Venture Capital GP

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Money Laundering Control Act of NT$500,000 to NT$5,000,000 per violation; mandatory registration suspension or revocation by the Financial Supervisory Commission; criminal liability for intentional non-disclosure carries imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of NT$10 million.

Director personal liability: AML compliance is a personal statutory obligation; directors and the designated compliance officer are subject to personal fines, disqualification from holding positions in financial services, asset freezes, and travel restrictions pending investigation.
Misappropriation of Investor Capital / Breach of Fiduciary Duty

Statutory sanction: Civil liability for full restitution and disgorgement of profits; under Criminal Code Articles 335 and 342, embezzlement and aggravated breach of trust are punishable by imprisonment of up to 10 years; the FSC may impose a permanent ban on fund management activities.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable to LPs for damages; the court may order personal asset attachments, issue an injunction against leaving the jurisdiction, and upon criminal conviction enforce imprisonment plus a travel ban.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital exists under the current Company Act for a limited liability company. The entire subscribed capital must nonetheless be paid in full at incorporation, remitted from the foreign shareholder through approved banking channels, deposited in a temporary capital account or escrow account established in the company name in Taiwan, and verified by a certified public accountant's capital verification report filed with MOEA. The escrowed funds cannot be withdrawn or released for operating purposes until the MOEA registration certificate is issued. The proposed capital must be adequate for initial working capital, cleanroom construction and medical device manufacturing compliance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Taxation Administration, Ministry of Finance (Taiwan), acting through its regional tax administrations; online electronic portal: the Ministry of Finance Tax e-Service portal and the E-Invoice Service Platform for electronic uniform invoice transmission and VAT filing.

E-government filing portal

For the Taiwan WFOE (foreign-owned limited liability company): (1) obtain foreign investment approval from the Investment Commission, Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) under the Foreign Investment Act; (2) complete company incorporation and obtain the 8-digit Uniform Business Number (UBI) from MOEA, which is the same number used as the tax ID/VAT ID; (3) within the statutory period after commencement of business, file tax registration with the competent regional National Taxation Administration using the business/enterprise tax registration procedure available through the MOF Tax e-Service portal, and appoint a Taiwan-based tax representative and registered place of business; (4) register as a Uniform Invoice user and, if required, enroll in the electronic invoice service through a certified service provider or the MOF E-Invoice platform. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a Taiwan fixed place of business that merely earns Taiwan-source income should obtain a withholding tax identification number through the withholding statement registration process; however, that number is not a VAT registration ID and does not permit input VAT credit recovery.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the Taiwan WFOE is a separate tax-resident profit-seeking enterprise subject to corporate income tax on its worldwide income, and its foreign shareholder is separately subject to Taiwan-source dividend withholding or treaty relief, if applicable. — For the Taiwan WFOE (foreign-owned limited liability company): (1) obtain foreign investment approval from the Investment Commission, Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) under the Foreign Investment Act; (2) complete company incorporation and obtain the 8-digit Uniform Business Number (UBI) from MOEA, which is the same number used as the tax ID/VAT ID; (3) within the statutory period after commencement of business, file tax registration with the competent regional National Taxation Administration using the business/enterprise tax registration procedure available through the MOF Tax e-Service portal, and appoint a Taiwan-based tax representative and registered place of business; (4) register as a Uniform Invoice user and, if required, enroll in the electronic invoice service through a certified service provider or the MOF E-Invoice platform. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Taiwan WFOE is a separate tax-resident profit-seeking enterprise subject to corporate income tax on its worldwide income, and its foreign shareholder is separately subject to Taiwan-source dividend withholding or treaty relief, if applicable.

Available election forms

None; Taiwan does not have a U.S.-style check-the-box election or a hybrid-mismatch entity election. The Anti-Tax Avoidance Rules and general anti-avoidance doctrines may recharacterize transactions, but a WFOE cannot elect pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NT$200,000 average monthly taxable sales for goods and NT$100,000 for services, as prescribed under the Business Tax Act and Ministry of Finance threshold directives; once exceeded, the business is a compulsory VAT-registered Uniform Invoice taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, sales of goods physically exported from Taiwan are zero-rated for VAT; for a Class III medical device stent manufacturer, the zero-rate applies when the goods are sold to a foreign buyer and the export is evidenced by a Customs export declaration. Services used or consumed wholly outside Taiwan and for which proceeds are received in foreign currency may also qualify as zero-rated exports if the statutory supporting documents are kept.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated export sales invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or packers receipt for consolidated shipments)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange settlement certificate showing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Letter of Credit, SWIFT wire, or export contract and payment statement

Registered businesses must issue Uniform Invoices for each taxable sale. Where electronic Uniform Invoices (e-Invoices) are used, the invoice data is transmitted digitally to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Service Platform through certified e-invoice service providers or the MOF turnkey gateway, and is reconciled with the bi-monthly Business Tax return due by the 15th day after each two-month filing period. There is no per-transaction real-time fiscal stamp for every general VAT taxpayer, but the E-Invoice Platform performs automated validation, storage and anti-fraud verification; invoice serial numbers and digital signatures must be valid, and any abnormality or misuse of e-invoice numbers is subject to immediate blocking, rectification orders and penalties.

The National Taxation Administration cross-checks zero-rated export turnover against the Customs Administration export declarations, E-Invoice Platform records and inward foreign exchange receipts. Where zero-rating is not substantiated by the mandatory customs and FX documentation, the output VAT is clawed back at 5% from the original reporting period, late-payment surcharge is assessed, and a statutory penalty of up to five times the evaded tax may be imposed under the Business Tax Act; fraudulent e-invoice or false export documentation may also trigger criminal liability under the Tax Collection Act and suspension of the business registration. The tax authority may re-open completed periods for fraud and concealment within the extended assessment period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed, non-fully-convertible currency regime with a fundamental legal bifurcation between the current account (经常项目) and the capital account (资本项目). Current account (goods, services, profit remittance, interest, royalties, and travel) is functionally convertible for bona fide transactions; the RMB is freely settleable for import of raw materials (e.g., nitinol alloy tubing, cobalt-chromium sheet stock, bioresorbable poly-L-lactic acid (PLLA) polymer resin, sirolimus/everolimus/paclitaxel drug substances, PTFE hydrophilic coatings) and export proceeds from finished coronary drug-eluting stents, subject to SAFE authenticity verification (真实性审核) that requires the presenting bank to match the submitted commercial invoice, customs declaration, transport documents, and purchase/sales contract. No pre-approval from SAFE is required for current-account items, but each transaction must be reported within 5 working days through the bank's Administration of Foreign Exchange for Trade in Goods system (货物贸易外汇监测系统) and the International Transactions Reporting System (收支申报). Capital account (FDI capital injection, cross-border loans, equity acquisition of domestic enterprises, outbound investment, securities investment, and real-estate investments) remains heavily restricted and requires: (i) SAFE FDI registration (外汇登记), (ii) opening of a capital account (资本金账户) at a designated bank, (iii) external-debt registration (外债登记) if any cross-border loan facility is used, and (iv) compliance with the Foreign Investment Negative List (外商投资准入特别管理措施（负面清单）2024 version) to ensure the medical-device manufacturing scope is in a permitted category; Class III vascular intervention devices are not on the negative list, hence 100% Taiwan-owned capital is permissible. Profit and dividend remittance to the Taiwan parent is permitted on the current account, but the bank and SAFE will demand (i) a valid audit report of the WFOE's statutory financial statements issued by a Chinese CPA firm, (ii) the shareholders' resolution for dividend distribution approved by the board, (iii) a certificate of tax settlement including corporate income tax (CIT) payment and the 10% dividend withholding tax under the Cross-Strait Taxation Agreement on Avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Tax Evasion on Income Tax (海峽兩岸避免雙重課稅及加強稅務合作協議);. if the Taiwan shareholder is a natural person or is deemed a Taiwan tax resident with a certificate of residence, the reduced 5% dividend withholding rate may apply on application; otherwise 10% applies; (iv) a SAFE Form I (基础信息登记表) and Form III (境外汇出申请书) signed by the legal representative; and (v) the bank's assessment that profits arose from operations rather than from capital revaluation or unrealized gains. Principal repayment out of the WFOE's registered capital is not permitted; any capital reduction or deregistration requires SAFE approval and a public notice through the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (信用公示系统) with a 45-day creditor-opposition window, and the Taiwan parent must redeem its interest at fair market valuation with FDI deregistration; funds can only leave after a formal reduction of registered capital is recorded with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and tax clearance from the tax authorities is obtained. In practice, FBME cross-border RMB settlement is also available under the Cross-border RMB Business measures (跨境人民币结算业务管理暂行办法, PBOC 2009) for both current and capital account items, with the advantage of bypassing some FX conversion constraints; the WFOE should evaluate a dual-currency treasury (USD and RMB) to maximize remittance efficiency. Overall hurdle severity: MEDIUM-HIGH for capital account; LOW-MEDIUM for current account; the company must designate a treasury manager to ensure every incoming/outgoing current-account item is matched to an underlying bona fide transaction and every capital-account item is pre-registered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China formally abolished mandatory export proceeds surrender (强制结汇) for the corporate sector in October 2012 under SAFE Circular Huifa [2012] No. 38 (国家外汇管理局关于优化货物贸易外汇管理方式的通知), and the rule was reconfirmed under SAFE Huifa [2020] No. 14 (国家外汇管理局关于进一步推进外汇管理改革完善真实合规性审核的通知). The CN_WFOE_LTD is therefore entitled to retain 100% of its export proceeds from coronary drug-eluting stents in a foreign currency operational account (经常项目外汇账户) at a designated bank, and has the right — not the obligation — to voluntarily settle into CNY at the market rate. However, SAFE imposes mandatory administrative duties on the exporter: (1) Declaration and Registration: all exports must be reported to the China Customs through the 'Single Window' (单一窗口) before departure, and each consignment's foreign-currency receipt must be linked to the customs declaration number through the Trade Monitoring System (货物贸易外汇监测系统); actual collection must be registered within 30 calendar days after the customs declaration date (出口收汇日期核销期). (2) Export Proceeds Cascade: when wires from overseas distributors (e.g., US/EU hospital procurement frameworks, Taiwan-licensed distributers, Southeast Asian importers) arrive in the WFOE's settlement account, the bank will perform an automatic matching with the customs declaration record; no separate 'surrender' is required, but the company must maintain an export proceeds verification logbook and produce it on SAFE audit at least every three years; medical device exports (HS 9021.39, vascular stents; HS 9018.39 for related catheters) do not require prior SAFE approval, but they do require the export license endorsement under the PRC Export Control Law (出口管制法) where applicable; Class III devices must also bear the NMPA registration certificate number on the customs declaration; exports to Taiwan are treated as cross-strait trade and must conform to the ECFA early-harvest lists for product tariffs and the cross-strait Goods Trade Agreement procedures where applicable. (3) Reconciliation and Tolerance Bands: if actual FX received deviates from the export declaration amount by more than 5% of the FOB value, or if the timing of receipt exceeds 90 days after export without a bank-approved extension, the WFOE will be reclassified by SAFE to (i) Class B (B类), subjecting it to 100% document checking for one year and mandatory on-site entry-exit verification for every single transaction; or (ii) Class C (C类), which effectively imposes prior-verified payment restrictions and may require a full compliance review by the FX administration. (4) Conversion: voluntary settlement proceeds from the operational FX account are freely usable for CNY operating expenditure, including payroll, raw-material purchasing, utility, and tax payments, but cannot be placed into a capital-account item unless settled through the capital account rules. (5) Annual SAFE Verification: annually, the WFOE must complete the SAFE 'Foreign Exchange Revenue and Expenditure for Trade' annual verification (外汇收支申报年度報告) and ensure the bank's 'Unit of Foreign Exchange Revenue and Expenditure Goods Trade' (货物贸易外汇收支企业名录) listing is current; revocation from the Directory would suspend all export settlement for 1–3 months. Additional rules under the capital-account deference regime: if the WFOE receives a remittance that exceeds RMB 500,000 per transaction and it fails to clear within 10 working days of the customs declaration, the bank will flag it; if the company holds foreign currency in excess of its verified annual business volume (based on the previous year's declarations), it must either settle it or apply for a quota increase. Effective compliance with surrender-related monitoring is a material precondition for the successful opening and continuous operation of the corporate bank accounts and for the company's ability to access the expedited FX 'green channel' offered to High-tier medical exporters.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC / 中国人民银行) is the sovereign central bank of the People's Republic of China, exercising authority over monetary policy, financial stability, and systemic liquidity. SAFE operates through regional branches (e.g., Beijing branch, Shanghai head-office service window) and has delegated registration and verification authority to designated designated banks (外汇指定银行), subject to ex post facto audit on a risk-tier basis. For the Taiwan-investor leg, the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC / 中央銀行, Taipei) administers all New Taiwan Dollar (TWD) monetary and FX policy, including TWD/CNY clearing under the Cross-Strait Currency Clearing Collaboration Framework Memorandum (两岸货币清算合作备忘录) signed on 31 August 2012. Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW as a primary onshore treasury rail; MEDIUM as an ancillary offshore collection/settlement overlay for the WFOE's overseas distributors, with strict legal constraints. The China regulatory framework does not recognize offshore digital EMI licenses (e.g., UK FCA or US MSB or Singapore MAS Major Payment Institution) as valid authorization to conduct payment services in mainland China. Under the PBOC Administrative Measures for the Payment Services Provided by Non-Financial Institutions (非金融机构支付服务管理办法, PBOC Decree [2010] No. 2, as amended), the CFCA (China's non-bank payment license) is the sole gateway for a private entity to process RMB payments; the WFOE cannot use an offshore EMI portal (Airwallex, Revolut Business, Payoneer, WorldFirst, Currenxie, PingPong's cross-border card) to initiate onshore RMB debits, receive export proceeds in CNY, or settle trade invoices with domestic suppliers, because the onshore bank receiving the funds must match the SAFE trade declarations to the actual FX flow, and any layering through an unlicensed intermediary would be considered an unauthorized payment channel (非法支付渠道) under Article 11 of the Non-financial Institution Payment Measures and Article 221 of the Criminal Law concerning illegal business activities (非法经营罪). As a supplementary instrument, the WFOE may use an offshore EMI platform to collect USD/EUR/GBP payments from foreign hospital or distributor buyers into a virtual multi-currency account, and then sweep the aggregate balance into the onshore BOC or CMB Operational FX Account via the FX remittance (汇入汇款) rail, with each individual invoice documented on SAFE's trade-monitoring portal before the sweep; this pattern must preserve entity-of-record integrity (i.e., the bank account in the WFOE's own name at the EMI is permissible as a collection account, but not as an onshore settlement account). The Fintech EMIs' remote onboarding convenience is counterbalanced by the High_Risk_Special_License underwriting standard imposed by Chinese banks on Class III medical device manufacturers: the onshore bank will refuse to accept a 'trust-me-on-PDF' dossier and will require an in-person interview and physical inspection regardless of the parent's EMI history. Additionally, the Taiwan parent may, for its own cross-border RMB collection from mainland customers, use the cross-border RMB payment system via CTBC or First Commercial Bank (Taiwan), but the China WFOE cannot rely on any Taiwan-regulated EMI (e.g., 街口支付 or TNG) for its mainland outbound payments. Conclusion: the WFOE's primary clearing rail must be a licensed Chinese Tier-1 bank; offshore EMIs may serve only as a front-end receivable collection gateway for remote overseas clients, and even then only where the WFOE confirms full regulatory documentation, maintains a formal outsourcing agreement, and obtains the bank's prior written acknowledgment that it will not process any RMB conversion or settlement activity.

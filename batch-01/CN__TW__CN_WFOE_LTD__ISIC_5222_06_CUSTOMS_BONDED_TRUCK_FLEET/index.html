Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital applies to a non-public limited liability company under Taiwan law. The MOEA and the Investment Commission require that the subscribed capital be fully paid into a Taiwan bank account and shown in a bank capital certificate. For mainland-funded investors the capital must be remitted in cash; no escrow account is mandated, but capital cannot be withdrawn prior to registration except for lawful incorporation expenses.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for all occupied premises (fire alarm, extinguishers, and escape routes), structural load report for the freight parking shed or warehouse floor, and workplace occupational clearances under the Occupational Safety and Health Act; bonded fleet must maintain valid vehicle safety inspection certificates and GPS-sealed cargo compartment seals.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (SAT) of the People's Republic of China, with e-filing through the Electronic Tax Bureau portal. Where the bonded feeder activity creates a fixed place of business or branch in Taiwan, the Ministry of Finance Taxation Administration of Taiwan and the Integrated Tax Filing Portal apply as secondary competent authorities; cross-strait income allocation and treaty relief are governed by the Cross-Strait Double Taxation Agreement.

E-government filing portal

Incorporate the WFOE at the Administration for Market Regulation and obtain the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number. File the tax registration particulars within 30 days through the multi-license integration data exchange; submit the VAT general taxpayer election where planned annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5 million; procure the road freight operating license and customs bonded-fleet registration with GPS electronic sealing; activate electronic tax filing credentials and digital invoice quota; and, if operative, register a Taiwan branch taxpayer ID at the local National Taxation Bureau before commencing feeder services.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law at the standard 25% rate; no pass-through regime exists. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law at the standard 25% rate; no pass-through regime exists. Dividend distributions to the Taiwan shareholder attract withholding tax at 10%, reduced to 5% where the Taiwan corporate beneficial owner holds at least 25% of the WFOE equity for 12 months or more.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Chinese law. Available statutory elections include: (i) small-scale taxpayer status at the 3% statutory levy (temporarily reduced to 1%) against general VAT taxpayer status at the RMB 5 million revenue boundary; (ii) deemed profit-rate assessment for related-party cross-border transportation margins; (iii) explicit zero-rating election for international and cross-strait road feeder services under VAT Pilot Circular 36; and (iv) treaty-rate elections for dividends, interest, and royalties flowing to the Taiwan shareholder.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

9%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of cumulative annual taxable sales in the PRC triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. For a Taiwan branch, Business Tax registration is triggered where average monthly taxable turnover exceeds TWD 80,000 for goods or TWD 40,000 for services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT applies to (i) goods physically carried out of PRC customs territory by the GPS-sealed bonded fleet, and (ii) PRC-sourced international road transportation and airport-feeder services connected to international air waybills in transit. Eligibility requires a valid export/bonded registration, electronic seal records showing departure from customs jurisdiction, and receipt of countervalue in foreign currency. Taiwan branch export feeder income is concurrently zero-rated in Taiwan under the Business Tax Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration with bonded transit endorsement
  • GPS electronic seal manifest and customs transport supervision docket
  • Bonded truck route ledger with trailer seal issuance records
  • Air waybill (master or house) for the connecting international airport feeder leg
  • Commercial invoice, transportation service contract, and vehicle/driver compliance certificates
  • Inbound foreign exchange receipt certificate from the Taiwan or third-country counterparty

Mandatory issuance of Fully Digitalized E-Invoices under Golden Tax Phase IV. Each bonded road transport invoice must be transmitted in real time to the SAT fiscal clearing platform via the Electronic Tax Bureau API, with automatic invoice-number stamping, dynamic quota verification, and real-time fiscal clearance before lawful use. A Taiwan branch must file through the Taiwan Integrated Tax Portal under the mandatory uniform electronic invoice system.

The SAT export refund engine cross-references every zero-rating claim against customs bonded-movement data, GPS seal logs, and foreign-exchange settlement records. Violations trigger refund clawback with late interest, administrative fines of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount under Tax Administration Law Article 63, fines of 100% to 500% for fraudulent refund claims under Article 66, revocation of export tax-refund qualification for up to three years, and criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 204 with imprisonment up to life for large-scale fraud. Taiwan-side fraudulent zero-rating is sanctioned under the Tax Evasion and Penalty Act with fines up to five times the evaded tax and possible suspension of business.

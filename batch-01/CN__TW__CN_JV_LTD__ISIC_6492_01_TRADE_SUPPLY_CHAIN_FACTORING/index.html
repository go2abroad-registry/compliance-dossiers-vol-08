Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No universal minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the Taiwan Company Act for a Company Limited by Shares. However, all subscribed capital must be fully paid in cash by the shareholders before registration; the funds must be originated from each shareholder's external bank account and remitted to a temporary capital account in the Company's name. The funds are de facto blocked until the Ministry of Economic Affairs has granted the company registration and the certified public accountant capital verification report has been filed. No government escrow account is required; bank remittance records and a CPA certificate of capital verification serve as the regulatory evidence. For the factoring license, the Financial Supervisory Commission will separately assess prudential capital adequacy and may impose additional capital conditions before licensing.

Funding & unlock path: The Company must obtain the fire safety inspection or fire safety equipment certificate for the commercial office premises before occupancy, as required under the Fire Services Act and the Regulations on the Installation and Maintenance of Fire Safety Equipment. The local fire department will verify fire alarms, escape routes, emergency lighting, and extinguishers. For a low-risk financial office, no industrial fire permit or high-hazard structure clearance is required; however, the legal building certificate and the landlord safety documents must be retained in the corporate register. Under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, the employer must complete the mandatory workplace self-inspection for office ergonomics, electrical safety, and emergency evacuation, and maintain records of designated labor health and safety staff.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Tax Administration of Taiwan, acting through the relevant Regional National Taxation Bureau; the supporting online electronic portal is the Ministry of Finance electronic taxation service portal, supplemented by the E-Invoice Consolidation Service platform for indirect tax filings and invoice transmission.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident equity joint venture must first complete the statutory incorporation or investment approval process administered by the competent authority for foreign investment, then file a tax registration application with the Regional National Taxation Bureau before commencing business. The Bureau issues a tax registration certificate that records the statutory Uniform Invoice Number, which is the eight-digit enterprise tax identification number used for VAT/business tax, income tax withholding, and customs declarations. For a non-resident enterprise without a fixed place of business but providing cross-border services or digital services into Taiwan, the enterprise must apply for a Ministry of Finance-assigned Tax Identification Number directly through the electronic tax portal, register for business tax on qualifying electronic services, and file periodic VAT/business tax returns through the designated online channel.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

None. Taiwan does not maintain a check-the-box election or an elective hybrid-mismatch classification. A limited joint-venture company is treated as a taxable entity at the entity level under the Income Tax Act, subject to the ordinary corporate income tax regime; financing and factoring arrangements remain subject to arm's-length pricing and thin-capitalisation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary threshold exempts an enterprise from the obligation to file tax registration; every enterprise selling taxable goods or services in Taiwan must register before commencing business. Full VAT-taxable status requiring Uniform Invoice issuance is mandatory once average monthly taxable sales reach NT$200,000; smaller traders below that threshold may be taxed at the 1% small-scale enterprise rate and may be exempt from Uniform Invoice issuance until the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 7 of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act for goods physically exported from Taiwan, for services rendered in Taiwan that are directly connected with and necessary for the export of goods, and for qualifying services supplied to non-resident recipients and consumed outside Taiwan. For cross-border supply-chain factoring, the relevant service fees can be zero-rated only if the underlying receivable is genuinely attributable to an export transaction, the service is performed for a non-resident counterparty, and the documentary evidence demonstrates that the service is not consumed in Taiwan. Domestic or onshore factoring services remain taxable at 5% unless the statutory export-service conditions are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) issued by the Customs Administration evidencing physical export from Taiwan
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing shipment from Taiwan to the foreign buyer or end customer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate or Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a trade-settlement currency
  • Underlying sales or service contract identifying the non-resident counterparty
  • Evidence connecting the cross-border factoring or trade-finance service to the qualifying export transaction
  • Proof of repatriation of foreign exchange proceeds through authorised financial channels

Taiwan does not operate a real-time customs-style VAT clearance with digital stamping. Instead, the statutory system is the Uniform Invoice system. VAT-registered enterprises must issue electronic Uniform Invoices through the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Consolidation Service, transmit invoice records in the prescribed electronic format through the statutory Turnkey interface, and upload e-invoice data within the legally prescribed filing cycle. Anti-fraud integrity is achieved through centrally controlled invoice numbering, batch transmission, cross-matching of sales and purchase invoices, and audit controls exercised by the National Taxation Bureau.

If the taxpayer cannot produce the prescribed export documentation or the foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period, the National Taxation Bureau will reverse the zero rating, reassess VAT at 5% on the gross consideration, and impose overdue interest and late-payment penalties. Where zero-rating is supported by forged export declarations, artificial offshore arrangements, circular fund flows, or otherwise fraudulent documentation, the matter is treated as tax evasion under the Business Tax Act and the Tax Collection Act. The competent tax authority may retroactively claw back the tax concession, levy fines proportionate to the evaded tax at the statutory penalty multiples, and refer the case for criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The jurisdiction is the People's Republic of China (mainland China), not Taiwan, as the composite key indicates country_iso CN and jurisdiction_id TW, but the sovereign_country is China. Given the explicit 'sovereign_country' field with ISO CN, the applicable foreign exchange regime is the PRC's managed floating exchange rate system administered by SAFE under the PBC. Current account transactions are generally freely convertible subject to genuine trade and service background verification, requiring banks to perform 'three-in-one' checks (trade authenticity, customs data, and foreign exchange matching). Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment equity injections, shareholder loans, cross-border guarantees, and factoring-related cross-border receivables purchases, require stringent approval or filing with SAFE and the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) for outbound investment if applicable. Profit remittance for foreign-invested enterprises is permitted after audited annual financial statements, tax clearance, and verification of prior capital injection via the foreign exchange capital settlement system. Surrender rules historically required mandatory conversion of export proceeds, but as of the current regime, enterprises may retain foreign currency in designated bank accounts up to a cap based on the prior year's current account receipts; any excess must be sold to banks. However, trade-in-services and current account payments are settled directly with supporting documents. For factoring transactions, cross-border assignment of accounts receivable from mainland exporters to a Taiwan-related factor (or vice versa) requires compliance with SAFE's regulations on cross-border receivable financing, including reporting via the ASOne system, and may require a special 'cross-border factoring business file number'. Nondeliverable forwards (NDFs) are permitted only for qualified corporate clients with real trade background, while onshore RMB forwards and swaps are available through the interbank market. The renminbi is not fully convertible on the capital account, and any cross-border RMB settlement for factoring must obtain SAFE filing if it involves capital items. Overall, the regime mandates rigorous documentation, timing thresholds, and bank-level pre-approval, with no discretionary access to offshore fintech EMIs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE regulations, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into the domestic foreign exchange settlement account within the statutory time limit of 180 days from the date of actual export receipt, or the date of customs declaration if earlier, but the enterprise may apply for extension under genuine circumstances. There is no blanket mandatory conversion ratio since 2012; however, enterprises are required to settle or retain proceeds subject to the 'foreign exchange settlement account cap' (an upper limit computed as the current account receipts over the past 12 months times a risk-weighted coefficient). Current account foreign currency receipts must be verified for authenticity via the trade monitoring system, and any retention beyond the cap must be converted into RMB. For factoing and supply chain transactions, the underlying trade must be traceable through commercial invoices, customs declarations (if goods move across the border), and transport documents. The surrender obligation is effectively mandatory only to the extent of the cap, but any conversion must be executed through a designated domestic Tier-1 bank.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

FINANCIAL REALITY CHECK: Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex) are legally barred from rendering principal banking or domestic FX settlement services to mainland China-registered entities because they hold no banking license from the PBC or SAFE. Their viability rating is LOW and legally prohibited. Such EMIs cannot open remote RMB or foreign currency settlement accounts with Chinese enterprise legal names, cannot process capital injections, and cannot obtain the required SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate. They can only serve as limited auxiliary payment tools for certain inbound/outbound trade flows if they partner with a mainland-regulated bank as the underlying clearing institution, but the corporate treasury must maintain all primary accounts at a Chinese Tier-1 bank. Any use of unregulated EMIs would trigger immediate KYC red flags and may cause the cancellation of the factoring license. Therefore, the sole viable route is a fully licensed Tier-1 domestic commercial bank with a designated foreign exchange business branch.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, inbound capital from the Taiwan shareholder into the mainland bank must be structured as foreign direct investment (FDI) equity injection or shareholder loan. If equity, the Taiwan entity opens an offshore remittance of USD or RMB to the JV's Capital Settlement Account (资本金账户) at a Tier-1 Chinese bank. The remittance SWIFT MT103 must include the Purpose Code (e.g., '121010' for foreign investment acting party funds) and the comment line must reference the SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate (FDI certificate) number. Before wiring, the JV must complete the FDI QR code registration via the local Administration for Market Regulation and SAFE. The bank must separately receive the payment notice and match it to the registration. Funds in the capital settlement account cannot be transferred to another account for domestic use until the bank has verified the capital narrative and performed a 'willing settlement' (capable of conversion to RMB) according to SAFE rules. If the injection is via shareholder loan, it must comply with the proportional debt-to-equity ratio (not exceeding 2:1) and be registered with SAFE's cross-border financing macro-prudential regulation. The JV should also open a separate Net-Profit Account for future dividend remittance. For injection where the parent is in Taiwan, banks will additionally require a notarized certificate of incorporation and a cross-strait direct remittance approval if the Taiwanese side remits USD via Taoyuan airport bank. All inbound funds must first pass through the bank's pre-verification system, and the bank is required to report the transaction to SAFE within five working days.Outbound payments for trade supplies from China to Taiwan or other regions should be settled using SWIFT MT103 wires for standard open-account payment, or through commercial documentary letters of credit (L/C) for structured trade. Factoring disbursements to mainland suppliers (when the JV acts as factor) are domestic RMB remittances only, requiring the bank to debit the JV's RMB settlement account and credit the supplier's account with the invoice reference. For cross-border reverse factoring where a Taiwanese importer finances its mainland suppliers, the Taiwan importer's bank initiates an MT103 to the JV's account in China; on settlement the JV releases funds to local suppliers via instant clearing (CIPS or bank-account-to-bank-account intraday). For trade settlement in USD, the JV must designate a track that avoids the use of correspondent banks under OFAC sanctions; all USD wires must be cleared via New York if the banking counterparty has US access, but for Taiwan transactions a direct RMB route via CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System) is preferred to bypass currency risk. The JV will use the bank's trade finance portal to submit export/import documents for authenticity. For documentary collections, the bank will handle the shipping documents and effect payment through SWIFT MT400/MT202. All outbound FX payments require supporting documentation (contract, invoice, customs declaration) and must be submitted to the bank before the payment date; the bank then verifies and sends the MT103 with the correct purpose code (e.g., '121030' for trade in goods). If the JV lessens its tail risk, it can also use bank payment obligation (BPO) through SWIFT. The bank will charge a per-transaction fee and the JV must maintain minimum balances for the clearing lines.Onshore RMB/USD forward contracts (up to 5 years) with a licensed Chinese commercial bank, requiring a signed ISDA/CSA or local derivatives agreement and margin collateral fully cash-backed or through bank guarantees. These forwards must be tied to genuine trade exposures from the factoring book and evidenced by underlying invoices.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for offshore USD/TWD or USD/RMB exposure only for qualified entities with approved cross-border trade background; the NDF is settled in RMB or USD net difference based on the fixing rate at the PBOC or Taipei Forex Inc., but only via the bank's offshore branch if the JV has an offshore account. Onshore mainland banks are constrained from executing NDFs for local enterprises; the JV must route NDFs through the Taiwan parent's treasury or a special offshore vehicle.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for long-term shareholder loans converted into RMB or USD, limited to matched funding from the parent and approved by SAFE for capital account hedging. The swap's notional cannot exceed the underlying foreign-currency debt, and the cash flows must be settled on an actual-exchange basis (not differential) for the initial principal unless the bank obtains a special SAFE approval.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: FINANCIAL REALITY CHECK: Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex) are legally barred from rendering principal banking or domestic FX settlement services to mainland China-registered entities because they hold no banking license from the PBC or SAFE. Their viability rating is LOW and legally prohibited. Such EMIs cannot open remote RMB or foreign currency settlement accounts with Chinese enterprise legal names, cannot process capital injections, and cannot obtain the required SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate. They can only serve as limited auxiliary payment tools for certain inbound/outbound trade flows if they partner with a mainland-regulated bank as the underlying clearing institution, but the corporate treasury must maintain all primary accounts at a Chinese Tier-1 bank. Any use of unregulated EMIs would trigger immediate KYC red flags and may cause the cancellation of the factoring license. Therefore, the sole viable route is a fully licensed Tier-1 domestic commercial bank with a designated foreign exchange business branch.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$ 50 million of cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory preparation of a Taiwan Local File; NT$ 200 million of total related-party transactions triggers a contemporaneous transfer-pricing report and summary disclosure; Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting are mandatory when the group's consolidated revenue exceeds NT$ 27 billion, with cross-border related-party transactions over NT$ 20 billion

Economic substance scope: Taiwan applies the substance-over-form doctrine under Article 12-6 of the Tax Collection Act and anti-avoidance measures in Article 43-1 and Articles 43-3/43-4 of the Income Tax Act. For a factoring entity, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are: (i) credit underwriting and receivables due diligence; (ii) execution of the factoring or reverse-factoring contract; (iii) cash collection, ledger monitoring and default management; and (iv) management of the credit risk. CIGA must be performed in Taiwan by local staff with genuine decision-making authority. A Sino-Taiwanese JV acting as a mere booking centre for a mainland Chinese parent will be treated as not having economic substance, and the National Taxation Bureau may reattribute all Taiwan-source income to the actual management place, reject intercompany deductions, or impose a deemed assessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion and Understatement of Factoring Income

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Income Tax Act, a civil penalty of up to three times the understated tax is imposed. Under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act, willful tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to NT$ 6 million; the National Taxation Bureau may also levy monthly delinquency surcharges and impose compulsory demand for unpaid taxes and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve fraudulent invoicing or movable fund diversion are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid taxes, interest and penalties under Article 24 of the Tax Collection Act. The corporate veil is pierced and personal bank accounts and real estate may be frozen/seized; individual directors are blacklisted by the National Taxation Bureau and prohibited from leaving the territory until taxes are settled, and may be sentenced to imprisonment for up to five years for criminal tax evasion.
Shell Company and Artificial Cross-Strait Profit Shifting / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The National Taxation Bureau invokes Article 43-1 and Article 43-4 of the Income Tax Act to disregard the Taiwanese JV, attribute the whole profit to the actual place of management (mainland China), and impose a reassessment plus interest. Article 12-6 of the Tax Collection Act denies any tax advantage from a sham arrangement. Civil penalty for the underpayment may be up to 100% to 300% of the corrected tax, and a resolution to dissolve the entity may be issued by the MOEA.

Director personal liability: Under the alter-ego doctrine, courts will pierce the corporate veil under Company Act Articles 154 and 367 and render the directors personally liable for the JV's tax debts. The tax authority may levy on directors' personal assets, obtain a court order for entity disregards, blacklist directors from serving on any board for three years, ban them from international travel, and prosecute them for criminal tax fraud, resulting in imprisonment of up to five years.
Anti-Money Laundering / Unlicensed Cross-Border Factoring

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 15 and 16 of the Money Laundering Control Act, the conduct is punishable by imprisonment for six months to five years and a fine of up to NT$ 10 million. Unlicensed factoring violates Article 18 of the Financial Regulation and Development Act, attracting an administrative fine of NT$ 2 million to NT$ 20 million and revocation of the company registration; the Central Bank may also fine the company for unapproved cross-border payment settlement transactions.

Director personal liability: The limited-liability shield is removed for directors who knowingly conceal money laundering or conduct unlicensed financial business. Personal assets, including bank accounts and real property, are subject to criminal forfeiture; directors are blacklisted from serving as directors or officers of financial-capital type companies for life, barred from government procurement and foreign-exchange privileges, and may be denied exit from Taiwan. Criminal custodial penalties reach up to five years, with additional fines equal to the portion of laundered assets attributable to the director's acts.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Taiwan generally imposes no universal statutory minimum paid-in capital for an ordinary company limited by shares, but capital must be sufficient for the proposed business and must be fully subscribed and paid before registration. Capital verification by a Taiwan certified public accountant is required. Foreign-invested capital must be remitted through an approved inward-remittance process and recorded in the company's designated account. A SPAC or listed vehicle remains subject to FSC, TWSE, broker-sponsor, underwriting, escrow, and minimum financial requirements that may exceed ordinary incorporation requirements. The approximate electronic company-registration fee is NT$700; paper filing, name reservation, certification, translation, CPA, investment approval, and securities-market fees are additional.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Taiwan Ministry of Finance (MOF), administered operationally by the relevant National Taxation Bureau (NTB) and its local branch office. Corporate registration is generally coordinated through the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) one-stop company registration system. Tax and business tax services are available through the MOF eTax platform and the relevant NTB electronic filing systems.

E-government filing portal

A Sino-foreign equity joint venture established in Taiwan must first complete company incorporation or foreign-invested company registration with the MOEA or the competent local authority, obtain approval for foreign investment where applicable from the Department of Investment Promotion under the MOEA, establish its registered office and bank arrangements, and apply for business tax registration with the competent NTB before commencing taxable business activities. The NTB issues an eight-digit Unified Business Number (UBN), which functions as the entity's principal tax identification and business tax registration number. The application normally requires the company approval or registration documents, articles of incorporation, responsible person's identification, registered office evidence, capital and shareholder information, foreign-investment approval documents where applicable, business premises documentation, and a power of attorney for an agent where used. A foreign company conducting business through a Taiwan branch follows the branch-registration and business-tax-registration process and receives a Taiwan tax identification number. Separate registration may be required for withholding tax, customs importer/exporter status, payroll obligations, electronic invoices, and regulated securities or financial activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not provide a United States-style check-the-box election for a Taiwan company or equity joint venture. A Taiwan company is generally subject to Taiwan profit-seeking enterprise income tax at entity level, with dividends potentially subject to separate shareholder-level taxation or withholding. A branch is taxed on Taiwan-source profits under branch rules and generally does not constitute a separate legal person. There is no general statutory hybrid-mismatch election that converts a Taiwan limited company into a transparent partnership for Taiwan tax purposes. Available statutory choices are limited and fact-dependent, including qualifying income-tax incentives, approved tax holidays, industry-specific investment incentives, foreign tax credit claims subject to statutory limits, head-office expense allocation methods for branches, and, where legally available, profit-seeking enterprise income tax filing methods. A SPAC, acquisition vehicle, or investment company does not obtain pass-through treatment merely because it is formed for a special purpose. Conducting securities, investment advisory, trust, escrow, payment, or other regulated financial activities may require separate approval from the Financial Supervisory Commission or another competent authority and may affect the applicable tax and reporting profile.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Business tax registration is generally required before commencing business operations and is not subject to a general annual turnover threshold for ordinary businesses. Certain small businesses and specified exempt or special-category businesses may be assessed under separate rules; a commonly applied small-business threshold is monthly sales of approximately NT$200,000 for qualification for small-business assessment treatment, but that threshold does not create a general exemption from all registration, invoicing, or tax obligations. A Taiwan JV carrying on taxable business, including taxable services, investment-related services, or other commercial activities, should register irrespective of expected turnover unless a specific statutory exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT may generally apply to goods exported from Taiwan, goods sold to export-related businesses in qualifying transactions, and services supplied to foreign customers or used outside Taiwan where the statutory conditions and documentary evidence requirements are satisfied. For exported goods, the taxpayer must establish that the goods physically left Taiwan and that the export transaction corresponds to the VAT invoice and accounting records. For services, the taxpayer must establish the foreign customer's identity, the contractual scope, the place and beneficiary of actual use, payment evidence, and that the service qualifies as an offshore or other statutorily zero-rated service rather than a service performed or consumed in Taiwan. Zero rating is not automatic for a foreign-currency transaction, an offshore contract, an investment transaction, or a payment received from a non-Taiwan account. Input VAT refunds may be claimed by qualifying businesses, including exporters, but require reconciliation of input invoices, customs records, output transactions, and payment documentation. A SPAC acquisition, listing, underwriting, advisory, trust, escrow, or securities transaction should be separately analyzed because many financial services may be exempt rather than zero-rated, and exempt activity can restrict input VAT recovery.

  • Commercial invoice and packing list matched to the export declaration
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or other carrier transport document evidencing shipment from Taiwan
  • Sales contract, purchase order, service agreement, or other transaction document identifying the foreign customer and commercial purpose
  • Proof of delivery or carrier export confirmation where required by the NTB
  • Foreign-exchange receipt evidence, bank credit advice, remittance record, or other acceptable proof of collection where required for the relevant transaction
  • VAT sales invoice, zero-rated invoice, credit note, and electronic invoice transmission records
  • For qualifying offshore services, evidence of the foreign customer's status, service performance location, service-use location, deliverables, correspondence, personnel or system logs, and confirmation that the service was not used in Taiwan

Taiwan electronic invoices are issued under MOF electronic-invoice regulations through an approved electronic-invoice platform, the MOF e-Invoice Platform, or an approved software and certificate arrangement. Electronic invoices must contain the prescribed seller, purchaser, transaction, tax, invoice-number, and authentication data and must be transmitted to the MOF platform or otherwise reported through the prescribed electronic channel within the applicable statutory period, commonly within two days after issuance for business-to-business electronic invoices, subject to transaction-specific rules and approved arrangements. Taiwan does not generally operate a universal pre-issuance real-time VAT clearance model equivalent to jurisdictions requiring invoice-by-invoice government approval before delivery. Digital signatures, approved certificates, electronic invoice formats, buyer-consent or system requirements, cancellation and allowance documentation, and retention controls apply. Businesses must retain invoice records and supporting books for the statutory period, generally at least five years, and must reconcile electronic invoices with VAT returns, customs declarations, accounting records, and payment evidence.

Taiwan does not impose one universal 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for every export or offshore-service transaction. The 180-day field is retained as an internal compliance control and must be validated against the transaction type, Central Bank foreign-exchange rules, banking documentation, contract terms, and any applicable investment or export-control requirements. The NTB may deny or revoke zero-rating where export movement, offshore use, customer identity, payment, invoice, or service-location evidence is incomplete or inconsistent. Denied zero rating may be reassessed as taxable at the applicable VAT rate, with recovery of improperly refunded input VAT, interest, and applicable penalties. Failure to issue, retain, report, or correctly transmit invoices may result in administrative fines and repeated-violation sanctions under the Value-Added and Non-Value-Added Business Tax Act and related regulations. False declarations, fabricated customs or bank documents, concealed sales, circular payments, or fraudulent refund claims may trigger tax evasion penalties, criminal liability, customs penalties, disgorgement, and assessments covering prior audit periods subject to statutory limitation rules. The taxpayer should preserve a transaction-level audit file and obtain advance written guidance from the competent NTB for material or novel offshore-service and financial-transaction classifications.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China generally permits current-account foreign-exchange transactions, including bona fide trade payments and qualifying profit remittances, subject to bank review, authenticity checks, tax evidence, and applicable SAFE requirements. Capital-account transactions remain regulated; foreign-invested capital contributions, cross-border loans, equity transfers, and capital repatriations must follow applicable registration, account-use, and transaction-documentation rules. A dividend remittance typically requires distributable profits under audited financial statements, completion of loss and reserve allocations as applicable, tax compliance evidence, and bank review. No assumption should be made that a SPAC's fundraising proceeds, acquisition funding, or securities-related receipts qualify as ordinary trade flows. Taiwan operates under a separate foreign-exchange framework; its rules must be assessed independently if the entity or account is in Taiwan.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For mainland China, there is generally no universal statutory requirement to convert all export proceeds into renminbi or to surrender them at a fixed conversion ratio. Export proceeds remain subject to applicable foreign-exchange administration, bank authenticity and consistency checks, trade-document verification, and current reporting or monitoring requirements. Receipt timing and permitted account treatment depend on the transaction, the company's registration status, and current SAFE and bank rules; confirm the applicable rules with the handling bank before shipment and collection. This statement does not determine Taiwan export-proceeds requirements.

Central bank supervision: The payload identifies both China (CN) as the sovereign country and Taiwan (TW) as the jurisdiction, while describing a Sino-foreign equity joint venture. These identifiers do not establish a single applicable banking or foreign-exchange regime. For a mainland China-incorporated entity, the principal authorities are the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). For a Taiwan-incorporated entity, the relevant monetary authority is the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC); mainland China rules should not be applied to it. Confirm the place of incorporation, registered office, and account location before opening accounts or executing transfers.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% corporate income tax on Taiwan-source taxable income. An additional 5% tax generally applies to undistributed current-year earnings retained after the statutory distribution period.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% withholding tax generally applies to dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a nonresident company or individual. Dividends to Taiwan-resident individual shareholders are generally subject to the domestic imputation and withholding rules applicable to the shareholder. A 5% additional tax may apply to undistributed earnings at company level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year company, the annual corporate income tax return is generally due from 1 May through 31 May of the following year. A provisional return for the first six months is generally due during 1 September through 30 September. A company using a non-calendar fiscal year generally files within the prescribed period following the end of the relevant fiscal year. Tax payable is generally due by the applicable filing deadline.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year company, the provisional return for January through June is generally filed from 1 September through 30 September of the same year, with payment by the prescribed deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan transfer-pricing documentation is transaction- and taxpayer-specific. A commonly applied Local File documentation exemption threshold is annual net revenue below NTD 300,000,000 or aggregate annual related-party transaction amounts below NTD 200,000,000, subject to statutory exceptions and transaction categories. A Master File is generally relevant where the Taiwan entity belongs to a multinational enterprise group with consolidated revenue of at least NTD 7,500,000,000 and the applicable related-party conditions are met. Country-by-Country Reporting generally applies at the group level where consolidated revenue reaches NTD 27,000,000,000. Thresholds must be confirmed against the rules applicable to the relevant fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a single standalone economic-substance regime identical to the UAE ESR framework. Substance is nevertheless relevant under Taiwan transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, controlled foreign company, general anti-avoidance, withholding-tax, permanent-establishment, business-tax, and company-law principles. A Taiwan SPAC vehicle should maintain genuine governance, directors' decision-making, accounting records, bank and escrow controls, personnel or appropriately documented operational capability, commercial purpose, transaction oversight, and evidence that functions, assets, and risks match reported profits. Pure conduit arrangements, circular funding, nominee directors, artificial interest or royalty flows, and treaty shopping may be recharacterized or denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and false reporting

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid tax, penalties that may be calculated by reference to the tax evaded, late-payment surcharges, interest, assessment based on available information, and possible criminal prosecution under Taiwan tax laws. Criminal consequences depend on the statute, amount, intent, and evidence.

Director personal liability: The responsible person, director, manager, accountant, or other participant may face administrative fines, civil restitution, joint liability where legally applicable, disqualification or regulatory action, and criminal imprisonment. Corporate limited liability does not protect intentional personal misconduct.
Unlicensed financial, securities, investment, or trust activity

Statutory sanction: Orders to cease operations, cancellation or suspension of business, disgorgement, administrative fines, investor compensation, transaction invalidation risks, and criminal penalties under the Securities and Exchange Act, Banking Act, Trust Enterprise Act, Company Act, or other applicable laws.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face criminal imprisonment, fines, civil compensation, removal, regulatory bans, and liability for investor losses. Travel restrictions may be imposed in enforcement or criminal proceedings where statutory conditions are satisfied.
AML, counter-terrorist financing, and beneficial-ownership failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, compliance orders, account or transaction restrictions, suspicious-transaction investigation, asset freezing or confiscation, and criminal penalties for money laundering or terrorist financing. Penalties depend on the entity's regulated status and the underlying offense.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, officers, and facilitators may face criminal imprisonment, fines, civil asset recovery, disqualification, and regulatory enforcement. A corporate structure does not shield knowing participation, willful blindness, or obstruction.
Abuse of escrow, investor funds, or redemption proceeds

Statutory sanction: Restitution, disgorgement, civil damages, regulatory sanctions, fines, suspension or revocation of approvals, and potential criminal prosecution for breach of trust, fraud, embezzlement, or securities violations.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable for restitution and investor losses and may face criminal imprisonment, fines, asset seizure, director disqualification, and restrictions on leaving Taiwan during proceedings.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's-length income, denial of deductions, additional tax, withholding-tax assessment, penalties, interest, transfer-pricing documentation penalties, and possible general anti-avoidance action.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face civil tax liability where statutory conditions apply, fines for false declarations, professional sanctions, and criminal exposure if the arrangement involves intentional evasion, fraud, or forged documentation.
Improper treaty or withholding-tax relief

Statutory sanction: Withholding-tax assessment against the payer, collection of unpaid tax, interest, penalties, denial of treaty benefits, and potential liability for inaccurate withholding filings.

Director personal liability: The responsible person and withholding agent may face statutory fines, civil liability for unpaid amounts where applicable, and criminal exposure for deliberate concealment or falsification.
False corporate records and sham capitalization

Statutory sanction: Company-law penalties, capital verification challenges, registration cancellation or rectification, tax reassessment, creditor claims, disgorgement, and criminal prosecution for fraud or false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors, founders, accountants, and responsible persons may incur civil liability to the company, shareholders, and creditors and may face imprisonment, fines, disqualification, and asset recovery.
Failure to maintain books, records, and tax documentation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, estimated assessment, denial of deductions or credits, loss of tax incentives or loss carryforwards, audit extension, and adverse evidentiary presumptions.

Director personal liability: The responsible person and accounting personnel may face fines, professional discipline, civil liability for resulting tax losses, and criminal exposure if records were destroyed or falsified.
Cross-border foreign-exchange and capital-control breach

Statutory sanction: Foreign-exchange administrative fines, forced repatriation, transaction cancellation, account restrictions, tax reassessment, and criminal exposure in cases involving fraud, laundering, or prohibited transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal fines, restitution, regulatory sanctions, criminal investigation, asset restraint, and travel restrictions where legally ordered.
Sanctions, export-control, or restricted-party exposure

Statutory sanction: Transaction blocking, asset freezing, administrative fines, license denial, forfeiture, criminal prosecution, and loss of market or listing approvals under applicable Taiwan and cross-border laws.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance personnel may face criminal imprisonment, fines, asset confiscation, disqualification, civil claims, and travel restrictions. Ignorance is not a defense where due diligence obligations were breached.
Fraudulent acquisition disclosure or market manipulation

Statutory sanction: Securities penalties, disgorgement, investor compensation, trading bans, listing sanctions, administrative fines, and criminal imprisonment for insider trading, market manipulation, fraud, or false disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors, sponsors, underwriters, advisers, and officers may be personally liable for investor losses and face imprisonment, fines, disqualification, asset seizure, and travel restrictions.

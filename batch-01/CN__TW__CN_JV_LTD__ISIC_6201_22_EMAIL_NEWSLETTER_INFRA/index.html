E-government filing portal

Foreign non-resident entities must apply for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and Unified Business Number (UBI) by submitting a registration application to the National Taxation Bureau. Required documents include: certificate of incorporation, passport of the responsible person, and proof of local office or appointment of a tax agent. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are eligible for zero-rating under the Business Tax Act. For services, the service must be provided to a foreign buyer, used entirely outside Taiwan, and payment must be received in foreign currency. The service must not be considered as used within Taiwan. For email infrastructure services, the platform must demonstrate that the service is delivered to overseas clients and that the revenue is derived from foreign sources.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)
  • Service Contract
  • Export Invoice

Taiwan mandates the use of electronic invoices (e-Invoice) for all registered businesses. Invoices must be transmitted in real-time to the Ministry of Finance's e-Invoice platform via API integration. The system requires digital stamping and anti-fraud validation for each invoice issued. Non-compliance may result in penalties.

Taiwan's tax authorities conduct rigorous audits of zero-rated export claims. If documentation is insufficient or fraudulent, the tax authority may retroactively assess VAT at 5% plus a penalty of up to 5 times the tax amount. Criminal liability may apply for deliberate tax evasion, including imprisonment. Additionally, the entity may be barred from future zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan operates a managed float and a partially convertible New Taiwan Dollar (TWD). Current account transactions, such as trade in goods and services, are generally freely convertible and can be executed through authorized foreign exchange banks without prior central bank approval, provided the transaction is supported by underlying commercial documentation. Capital account transactions, especially inbound direct investment from mainland China, are subject to a strict prior-approval regime under the Cross-Strait Act and the Regulations on Land or Foreign Investment Approval. The Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) must pre-approve the equity capital injection upstream, and the authorized bank must verify the approval before allowing the remittance to be credited to a capital account. Profit remittance to mainland Chinese shareholders is permitted only after the annual financial statements have been audited and filed with the Taiwan tax authority; the remittance application must be accompanied by the audited profit distribution resolution, the applicable withholding tax certificate, and proof that no unpaid corporate income tax liabilities remain. A minimum tax retention period and a capital verification report further complicate immediate distribution. Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory surrender to TWD, but must be repatriated to Taiwan within 180 days of the shipment date and can be maintained in a Foreign Currency Deposit Unit (FCDU) account. However, for a mainland-owned joint venture, any conversion of export proceeds into TWD or onward remittance requires the bank to screen the ultimate beneficiary, and payments from mainland China in RMB or other convertible currencies must be channeled through an approved cross-strait clearing mechanism, adding settlement latency and documentary intensity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be collected and repatriated to Taiwan within 180 days from the date of shipment; there is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio into TWD, but all proceeds must be reported to the Central Bank through an authorized domestic foreign exchange bank, either by being deposited in a Foreign Currency Deposit Unit account or sold for TWD on an arms-length basis.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (commonly known as Central Bank of China, CBC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise or Revolut Business have a Low public viability for this Taiwan-based joint venture. Taiwan's Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) requires any entity providing payments, remittance, or deposit-taking services to hold either a banking license or an electronic payment institution license under the Act on Electronic Payment Institutions. Offshore EMIs are not licensed in Taiwan and cannot open a local TWD settlement account or provide compliant cross-strait wire routing; they also cannot issue a capital verification certificate acceptable to the Investment Commission or a Taiwan CPA. Their use is therefore limited to supporting non-core foreign currency collections outside Taiwan, but even that would trigger redundancy and reconciliation risk. The correct route is a fully-licensed local tier-1 commercial bank, preferably Bank of Taiwan, acting as the principal operational and clearing facility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection from mainland China to the Taiwan JV must be routed from the mainland Chinese shareholder’s foreign exchange account through an authorized Taiwaese foreign exchange bank using SWIFT MT103, with the foreign exchange purpose code clearly designated as Direct Investment in Equity (code 320 or the domestic CBC equivalent). Prior written approval from the Investment Commission, Ministry of Economic Affairs, must be obtained for the issuance of new shares. Upon receipt, the funds are credited to a dedicated Capital Escrow Account, and a Taiwan CPA must then issue a capital verification certificate confirming the actual receipt and registered capital increase. The verification certificate and proof of the foreign exchange settlement must be submitted to the central bank and the corporate registry before the funds can be released for working capital, operating expenses, or trade payments. Third-country triangulation or payment through an offshore private intermediate vehicle is strictly disallowed and will cause a capital-account penalty and a bank compliance escalation.Trade and operational disbursements for email infrastructure suppliers will be executed across three primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for individual software license invoices, cloud hosting charges, and tax payments; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit for high-value equipment or long-term software subscription contracts exceeding USD 100,000 per transaction; and (3) Documentary Collections for recurring vendor payments with known counterparties where a confirmed LC is not needed. All foreign-currency outbound payments must be matched to the underlying purchase order, invoice, customs declaration or service contract, and the authorized bank will require an FX purpose code and a written remittance instruction signed by the two statutory directors.

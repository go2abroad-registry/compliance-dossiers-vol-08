Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Taiwan has no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a company limited by shares or for a low-risk wholesale/retail WFOE; however, for a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, the Investment Commission, MOEA, will assess the business plan and require a paid-up capital that is reasonable and sufficient for the approved scope, including TikTok Shop retail operations. The capital must be remitted from outside Taiwan into a bank account opened in the corporate name, converted into New Taiwan dollars or retained as approved foreign currency, and verified by a bank-issued capital certificate before incorporation registration is completed. There is no mandatory blocked government escrow account; the funds must remain in the company's capital deposit account and may not be withdrawn or disbursed until the Department of Commercial Affairs has issued the Company Registration Certificate. For mainland Chinese investors, the Investment Commission applies additional review criteria under the Act Governing Relations between Peoples of the Taiwan Area and the Mainland Area and may impose a higher capitalization requirement for sensitive business lines, although no fixed statutory minimum exists.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for a pure online-retail company with no physical premises beyond a registered office. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (国家税务总局; STA). Operational portals: nationwide Electronic Taxpayer system (电子税务局, https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn), 12366 Taxpayer Service Platform, and China International Trade Single Window (中国国际贸易单一窗口) for export and customs filings. Local administration is handled by provincial and municipal tax service bureaus. The WFOE's VAT invoicing is monitored through the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV (金税四期) system.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the WFOE is automatically issued a Unified Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码, USCC), which serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number for all PRC tax purposes. Tax registration is completed through the integrated 'multi-certificate, one entity' procedure; no separate tax registration certificate is issued. The WFOE must then: (i) complete real-name authentication for the legal representative and financial officers; (ii) activate its Electronic Taxpayer account; (iii) complete taxpayer-basic data including banking and accounting details; (iv) apply for invoice quotas and fully digitalized electronic invoice (数电票) activation; (v) obtain a Customs Registration Code (海关收发货人登记编号) via the Customs Single Window for export declarations and export tax refunds; and (vi) register as a Trade Foreign Exchange Receipts/Payments Enterprise (货物贸易外汇收支企业名录) with its designated bank for export proceeds. Foreign shareholders do not need a separate PRC TIN unless they independently derive PRC-source income or create a permanent establishment in China.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise formed under PRC Company Law is tax-resident in the PRC by place of incorporation and is subject to Enterprise Income Tax on worldwide income at the standard 25% rate. It is not a pass-through entity, and no check-the-box or look-through election is available. For VAT purposes it must register; annual taxable sales above CNY 5,000,000 in a 12-month period generally require general VAT taxpayer status, while below that amount it may elect small-scale taxpayer status, which does not alter entity-level income taxation.

Available election forms

PRC law does not provide a US-style check-the-box entity classification election. The material elections available to a PRC WFOE are: (i) VAT taxpayer status election between small-scale taxpayer status (simplified levy, currently reduced to 1% through the applicable transition period; statutory rate 3%) and general VAT taxpayer status where annual taxable sales do not exceed CNY 5,000,000; (ii) export VAT treatment election between 'exempt-and-refundable' treatment and 'exempt-without-refund' treatment for exported goods, including cross-border e-commerce retail exports; (iii) selection of the applicable customs supervision code for cross-border e-commerce export, such as 9610, 9710, 9810 or 1210; and (iv) transfer-pricing documentation and treaty-relief elections for cross-border related-party payments. No hybrid-mismatch or transparent-entity election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No revenue threshold for initial VAT registration; all WFOEs must tax-register upon incorporation. The general VAT taxpayer registration threshold is CNY 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales in a rolling 12-month period. Export registration with the Customs Single Window and cross-border e-commerce export filing have no turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from mainland China's customs territory are zero-rated for VAT. A general VAT taxpayer that makes an export sale may claim refund of attributable input VAT provided the transaction is genuine, the goods were actually shipped out of mainland China, export proceeds are collected in foreign exchange, and supporting documentation is complete and internally consistent. Cross-border e-commerce retail export under Customs supervision code 9610, including sales to Taiwan through TikTok Shop, may use 'exempt-without-refund' treatment where the exporter has no valid input VAT invoice for the goods, or 'exempt-and-refund' treatment where goods were purchased with VAT special invoices and the exporter is a general VAT taxpayer. Export sales to Taiwan qualify where the goods actually leave the mainland China customs territory; digital and offshore platform services require separate analysis under the cross-border taxable services rules of Circular 36/2016.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口货物报关单) or Cross-Border E-Commerce Retail Export Manifest (跨境电子商务零售出口申报清单/9610 list)
  • Cross-border e-commerce order data, platform transaction records, and TikTok Shop settlement reports
  • Payment data including cross-border payment platform settlement reports and Export Proceeds Collection Certificate (银行收汇凭证/涉外收入申报单/Inward Remittance Certificate)
  • Logistics documentation: Bill of Lading, air waybill, or courier/express delivery manifest showing the goods departed mainland China and were delivered to the Taiwan consignee
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • VAT special invoice or purchase invoice of the exported goods where input VAT refund is claimed
  • China Customs export reconciliation and Electronic Port (电子口岸) records
  • Foreign exchange collection proof from the designated bank, including the Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement/foreign exchange memo, when export refund is claimed

China mandates the use of fully digitalized electronic VAT invoices (数电票) issued through the STA-controlled National VAT Invoice Management System. Invoices are validated and cleared in real time, bear a dynamic QR code, an electronic seal, and a unique platform invoice number, and are delivered through the Electronic Taxpayer system or enterprise APIs connected to the STA platform. Paper and electronic VAT special invoices are also transmitted through the Golden Tax System; no input VAT credit or export refund can be claimed in respect of an invoice not uploaded and verified by the tax authority. Cross-border e-commerce retail exports require transmission of order, payment, and logistics data through the Customs Single Window and the cross-border e-commerce retail export declaration system, with customs clearance data automatically reconciled to tax records.

Export refund files are subject to multi-dimensional cross-matching by the STA Golden Tax Phase IV and Customs Single Window: customs declaration, invoice flow, goods flow, foreign-exchange/payment flow, and logistics flow must satisfy 'four-flow consistency'. False export declarations or refund fraud trigger: (i) denial or clawback of the VAT refund; (ii) late-payment interest at 0.05% per day; (iii) administrative penalties under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law and Customs Law, generally from 0.5 to 5 times the tax underpaid or refund fraudulently obtained; (iv) customs penalties for false declaration; and (v) criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 204 for fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds, punishable by fines, confiscation of property, and imprisonment up to life sentence where the amount is especially large. Tax authorities may retroactively recover the clawed-back tax for up to ten years in fraud cases and may permanently disqualify the WFOE from future export zero-rating or revoke its Customs Registration Code.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi is generally convertible for bona fide current-account transactions, including permitted trade payments, service payments, salary payments, taxes, and documented profit distributions. Capital-account transactions remain regulated and are subject to registration, bank review, approved purposes, foreign-investment rules, debt limits, and applicable macro-prudential controls. A WFOE may generally remit dividends, liquidation proceeds, and other permitted returns of capital after completion of statutory audit, tax filing and payment, reserve allocations, board or shareholder approval, and supporting-document review by the handling bank. Banks commonly require evidence of distributable profits, audited financial statements, tax clearance or tax filing records, resolution approving the distribution, and confirmation that registered capital, intercompany loans, and other foreign-exchange registrations are compliant. Payments to or from Taiwan may also be subject to cross-Strait investment, trade, banking, prohibited-counterparty, sanctions-screening, and reporting restrictions. The entity must not assume that a Taiwan-based shareholder, payment provider, or settlement account can bypass mainland foreign-exchange, customs, tax, or payment rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mainland China does not generally impose a universal mandatory surrender ratio or an automatic fixed-period conversion requirement for all export proceeds. Exporters may generally retain foreign currency in permitted foreign-exchange accounts and may settle foreign currency with a bank when commercially required, subject to genuine-transaction review and applicable account limits. Goods-trade foreign-exchange receipts remain subject to customs, tax, banking, and foreign-exchange monitoring, including consistency among contracts, invoices, customs declarations, logistics records, and payment data. Banks may require collection of export proceeds within the contractual and regulatory period and may investigate overdue, abnormal, advance, related-party, third-party, split, or mismatched receipts. If the WFOE is only a domestic or platform retailer and does not act as exporter of record, export-proceeds rules generally apply to the actual exporter and settlement party rather than automatically to the WFOE. Any Taiwan-side exporter or investor must separately comply with Taiwan foreign-exchange reporting and Mainland-area transaction restrictions. Mandatory conversion ratios, if introduced for a specific pilot, account type, sector, or enforcement action, must be confirmed with the designated bank and current SAFE rules before implementation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China and State Administration of Foreign Exchange for mainland China; Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan), Financial Supervisory Commission, and Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs for Taiwan-side capital, banking, and outbound-investment controls. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange is the principal mainland foreign-exchange administration authority, while the People's Bank of China supervises the monetary and payment-system framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium for collection, treasury support, and permitted business payments; Low as a substitute for a mainland corporate bank. An offshore electronic-money institution, including Wise or a similar provider, generally cannot open a mainland RMB corporate deposit account, perform unrestricted domestic payment clearing, hold regulated customer deposits in China, receive or convert foreign-investment capital as a substitute for a designated bank, or independently determine the legality of cross-border trade payments. Use is viable only where the provider accepts the WFOE, the payer and beneficiary are correctly identified, the transaction has a genuine underlying purpose, the payment route is permitted, and the provider's licensing and geographic terms cover China-related transactions. TikTok Shop payouts must be contracted, invoiced, and settled through an account and payment route accepted by the platform and compliant with mainland tax, foreign-exchange, data, and bank requirements. Funds should not be routed through personal accounts, undisclosed third parties, nominee accounts, or accounts inconsistent with the WFOE's registered business model.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% Taiwan corporate income tax on taxable income. A separate 5% undistributed earnings tax may apply to qualifying retained post-tax earnings not distributed or otherwise utilized.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends paid by a Taiwan company to a nonresident shareholder, subject to applicable tax treaty or cross-strait relief. Taiwan does not have a conventional comprehensive income tax treaty with Mainland China; Taiwan-China tax treatment is governed by cross-strait legislation and applicable administrative rules rather than a standard bilateral DTA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The provisional return is generally due during 1 September through 30 September of the current year. If the deadline falls on a non-business day, the deadline is extended to the next business day. A company using a non-calendar fiscal year generally files within five months after fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan generally requires a Local File where annual revenue is at least NT$300 million or where controlled transactions exceed prescribed categories and thresholds, commonly NT$200 million for purchases or sales of tangible goods and NT$100 million for other controlled transactions, subject to current regulations and transaction aggregation rules. A Master File is generally required where the Taiwan entity belongs to a multinational enterprise group meeting the prescribed consolidated-revenue threshold, commonly NT$3 billion, together with applicable Taiwan filing conditions. Country-by-Country reporting generally applies to qualifying ultimate parent or surrogate-parent groups with consolidated revenue of at least NT$27 billion.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a single UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime applicable to all entities. Substance is nevertheless enforced through corporate income tax, transfer-pricing, controlled-foreign-company, beneficial-ownership, anti-avoidance, permanent-establishment, and business-purpose rules. The Taiwan entity must demonstrate real functions, personnel, premises, decision-making, assets, risks, inventory control, customer activity, and commercially supportable pricing. A company that merely receives TikTok Shop proceeds while strategic decisions, procurement, inventory, marketing, and risk control remain outside Taiwan may face income reallocation, denial of deductions, withholding reassessment, permanent-establishment findings, or general anti-avoidance challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Jurisdictional misclassification and unauthorized operating structure

Statutory sanction: Possible denial of business registration, tax registration, deductions, and input tax credits; assessment of unpaid corporate income tax and business tax; administrative fines under tax, company, foreign-investment, labor, customs, and foreign-exchange rules; and orders to cease or regularize operations.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face administrative penalties, tax collection proceedings, civil liability for unpaid taxes and creditor losses, and criminal exposure where conduct involves intentional evasion, false registration, document falsification, or unauthorized business activity. Travel restrictions or imprisonment are not automatic and require statutory grounds and competent-authority or judicial action.
Corporate income tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, back tax, interest, administrative penalties, and potential criminal prosecution under the Taiwan Tax Collection Act. Deliberate evasion by fraud or other unlawful means may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a criminal fine of up to NT$10 million, subject to the applicable provision and court determination.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons and persons who directly participated may incur criminal liability, civil restitution, tax recovery, and director disqualification consequences. Company status does not shield individuals who ordered, approved, or knowingly implemented the evasion.
Business tax and false invoice violations

Statutory sanction: Business tax assessment, interest, penalties that may be calculated by reference to the evaded tax, denial of input credits, invoice restrictions, and potential criminal liability for aggravated or fraudulent conduct under the Business Tax Act and related criminal provisions.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons, accounting personnel, and transaction participants may be jointly exposed to administrative and criminal proceedings where they knowingly created or used false documents. Civil recovery may include unpaid tax, penalties, unjust enrichment, and damages.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustment to arm's-length income, denial of deductions, withholding reassessment, interest, documentation penalties, and possible penalties for inaccurate or incomplete disclosures. Taiwan may use available information, comparables, customs data, platform data, and economic substance evidence.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face civil liability to the company or shareholders for approving non-commercial transactions and may face criminal exposure if transfer-pricing entries are part of deliberate tax fraud, false accounting, money laundering, or asset diversion.
Illegal profit repatriation and withholding failure

Statutory sanction: Withholding-tax reassessment, payer liability for tax not withheld, interest, penalties, denial of deductions, foreign-exchange or company-law sanctions, and possible restrictions on future remittances. Improper distributions may be recoverable from recipients and responsible directors.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unlawful distributions, breach of fiduciary duties, unpaid withholding tax, and company losses. Criminal consequences may arise for falsified approvals, concealed distributions, asset misappropriation, or intentional tax evasion.
Customs, import valuation, and inventory diversion

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessment, duties and business tax, administrative fines, seizure or forfeiture, import restrictions, and criminal prosecution for smuggling or fraudulent declarations where statutory thresholds and intent are established.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons and customs declarants may face civil liability, administrative sanctions, and criminal liability. Directors may be pursued where they authorized or knowingly tolerated the conduct.
Anti-money laundering and prohibited payment flows

Statutory sanction: Account blocking, transaction rejection, enhanced due diligence, reporting to competent authorities, confiscation, AML administrative penalties, and criminal money-laundering liability. Taiwan's Money Laundering Control Act may impose imprisonment of up to seven years and criminal fines up to NT$50 million for qualifying money-laundering conduct, subject to statutory elements and amendments.

Director personal liability: Individuals who know, facilitate, arrange, or benefit from laundering may face imprisonment, fines, confiscation, civil restitution, and asset freezing. Directors may also face travel restrictions where legally ordered; such restrictions are not automatic.
False books, fabricated contracts, and accounting obstruction

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of deductions, estimated assessment, administrative penalties, evidence-preservation orders, and possible criminal liability under tax, accounting, company, forgery, or obstruction provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors, responsible persons, accountants, and employees may be personally prosecuted where they participated in falsification or obstruction. Civil claims may include tax losses, investigation costs, penalties, and shareholder or creditor damages.
Unlawful dividend or capital distribution

Statutory sanction: Recipient recovery, director liability for restoration of distributed amounts, tax reassessment, penalties, and potential company-law or insolvency-related criminal consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly or personally liable for restitution and damages. Imprisonment or travel restrictions require a separate statutory offense, judicial process, and applicable evidence; they do not arise solely from an ordinary defective dividend.
Permanent establishment and unregistered local presence

Statutory sanction: Attribution of Taiwan-source profits, corporate income tax and business tax assessments, withholding obligations, interest, penalties, and possible registration or licensing enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face civil liability for tax and regulatory losses and criminal exposure only where the underlying conduct independently constitutes intentional evasion, fraud, falsification, or another offense.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under Chinese company law and SAMR registration rules, a WFOE must maintain a registered address within the incorporated city that serves as the statutory domicile for service of process, taxation, and regulatory correspondence. A purely virtual office or commercial mailbox is not accepted as a registered address because SAMR requires actual premises and leases for a business term exceeding one year, and the tax authority practices office-inspection procedures. The municipal tax and market supervision bureaus will conduct unannounced site visits, verify the presence of compliant business operations, and reject shell arrangements for trading companies under ISIC 4631 because commodity trading involves continuous documentary and physical trade substance. While a shared office space with dedicated lease, desk, and signage may count, a mail-forwarding-only virtual address will not satisfy the statutory registered office mandate and will increase bank account-opening rejection risk under PBOC-KYC rules.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Taiwan Company Act imposes no statutory minimum paid-in capital for a wholly foreign-owned limited company. However, under the Foreign Investment Act and Central Bank foreign exchange rules, the foreign investor must remit the subscribed capital into a blocked investment escrow account maintained at a licensed domestic bank in Taiwan, obtain a certified public accountant capital verification report, and may not release or disburse the escrowed funds until the foreign investment approval is recorded and the Ministry of Economic Affairs issues the Company Registration Certificate.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), enforced by local district/county State Tax Bureaus; taxpayer services and registration are managed through the national Electronic Tax Bureau portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the Business License constitutes the primary taxpayer identification number. Within 30 days after issuance of the Business License, the WFOE must file tax registration through the Electronic Tax Bureau using the 'Tax Registration Form (Basic Information)' with the competent local tax authority. The tax authority will assign the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) equal to the USCC, determine the VAT registration status (general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer), and record the entity in the STA national database. Foreign non-resident entities without a Chinese permanent establishment may obtain a non-resident tax reference number for withholding and filing purposes through the tax authority, but the WFOE is a Chinese tax resident and therefore follows the standard SAMR/STA registration pathway.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must complete VAT registration upon incorporation. The mandatory threshold for General VAT taxpayer classification is annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000; below this, small-scale taxpayer status applies at 3% (or 1% under certain reductions), but voluntary registration as a general taxpayer is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT-registered WFOE may claim 0% VAT on export sales of VHP raw cane sugar where (1) the goods are physically exported from Chinese customs territory to Taiwan or another overseas destination, (2) the exporter files a valid China Customs Export Goods Declaration and the goods are released, (3) the export sale is evidenced by export contracts, invoices, and transport documents, and (4) the foreign currency proceeds are repatriated to China through a licensed bank within the statutory period. The export transaction must qualify as a genuine sale to a foreign buyer; transactions to bonded zones or China-state subsidiaries qualify only under prescribed procedures.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration (Customs Export Declaration Form)
  • Bill of Lading or other original ocean transport document
  • Export commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract and any amendments
  • Customs-registered export VAT zero-rated sales invoice
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt voucher and cross-border receipt declaration evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Export VAT refund (exemption, credit and refund) declaration schedule if claiming input-VAT crediting

China mandates issuance of VAT invoices through the National Electronic Invoice and 'Golden Tax Phase IV' digital supervision system. VAT general taxpayers must use tax control management systems or the fully digital electronic invoice ('Digital e-Fapiao') platform to issue invoices; sales data, invoice summaries, and aggregate special-invoice registers are transmitted to the STA platform in near-real-time. Mandatory anti-fraud controls include unique invoice codes, digital risk classification, real-time matching against customs export declarations and foreign exchange settlement reports, and suspension of invoice issuance for anomalous taxpayers.

Export VAT zero-rating claims are subject to joint administrative review by the STA and China Customs. Where the exporter fails to substantiate physical export or foreign-exchange repatriation, the tax authority will deny the zero-rating, require repayment of any refunded input VAT, impose late-payment surcharge (0.05% daily) and a monetary penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export-refund schemes constitute criminal offences and may result in criminal fines, confiscation of property, and imprisonment under the Criminal Law; the exporter is also blacklisted under the Joint Punishment System for Tax Dishonesty.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Renminbi is fully convertible for current account transactions under the People's Republic of China's Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement; trade in goods, services, and profit remittances linked to bona fide trade may be settled freely with supporting documents. Capital account items, in contrast, remain subject to a rigorous licensing and registration regime administered by SAFE. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise may lawfully receive foreign currency registered capital only after completing SAFE foreign direct investment information registration and opening a Foreign Currency Capital Account; once converted into Renminbi under the voluntary settlement system, the funds are traced to the prescribed negative list and must not be deployed into equities or entrusted lending. Profit repatriation by dividend distribution is permitted annually, but only after the statutory audit, the filing of the enterprise income tax return, and the satisfaction of any outstanding tax liabilities; the remittance itself is processed against audited financial statements, a board dividend resolution, and the SAFE dividend remittance form. Surrender rules have been substantially liberalized since 2012, yet all export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange account within the statutory timeline, with no mandatory conversion ratio.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For merchandise export proceeds, including VHP raw cane sugar shipments, the exporter must repatriate all foreign currency earnings into a domestic foreign exchange settlement account within the statutory time limit, generally 180 days from the date of customs export declaration, or within the contractually agreed settlement term where shorter. Since the 2012 reform abolishing mandatory surrender requirements, there is no compelled conversion ratio: the WFOE may retain the full amount in a foreign currency settlement account under SAFE quota monitoring or convert all or part into Renminbi at the market rate. Each transaction is verified against customs declaration data, commercial contracts, and shipping documents in the SAFE cross-border receipts and payments platform; enterprises approved under the trade foreign exchange facilitation pilot may enjoy delegated verification by their Tier-1 bank with abbreviated document submissions.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, the statutory foreign exchange control authority operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the national central bank; for Taiwan-denominated clearing and New Taiwan Dollar flows, parallel oversight is exercised by the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) and the Financial Supervisory Commission (Taiwan), with cross-strait Renminbi clearing channelled through Bank of China Taipei Branch.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Legal feasibility is fundamentally negative for onshore treasury use. Offshore digital financial technology EMIs, including Airwallex, Revolut Business, and Wise Business, are neither licensed deposit-taking institutions nor SAFE-designated foreign exchange banks in the People's Republic of China. Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and the PBOC Administrative Measures for the Payment Services of Non-Financial Institutions, no unlicensed digital EMI can lawfully open a domestic Renminbi settlement account, receive export proceeds on behalf of a mainland WFOE, operate a Foreign Currency Capital Account, or execute the SAFE capital verification and trade verification workflow. An offshore EMI may at most act as a pre-funded outbound payment corridor for incidental non-regulatory expenses, but relying on it for capital injection or trade settlement would create uncorrectable gaps in the audit trail, expose the enterprise to SAFE penalties for evasion of foreign exchange control, and invalidate the capital verification report required for registered capital compliance. The WFOE therefore must route all regulated flows through licensed Tier-1 domestic commercial banks; offshore EMIs are viable only as supplementary payment utilities for a separate offshore entity, never for the onshore WFOE.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All foreign direct investment equity contributions must enter through a dedicated Foreign Currency Capital Account established with a licensed Tier-1 commercial bank and registered with SAFE. The non-resident shareholder originates a SWIFT MT103 remittance with the SAFE capital account payment purpose code corresponding to registered capital injection for a foreign-invested enterprise, stating the full legal name of the WFOE and the purpose 'equity contribution to registered capital' in the remittance narrative. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the SAFE Foreign Direct Investment information registration record, confirms the remitting account is not a high-risk sanctioned entity, and credits the funds to the capital account. The WFOE then applies the SAFE voluntary foreign exchange capital settlement system to convert the capital into Renminbi, transferring into a domestic basic deposit account subject to the negative-list use restrictions, which prohibit deployment into securities, entrusted loans, or real estate beyond approved scope. A licensed Chinese certified public accountant subsequently issues the capital verification report confirming paid-in capital, which is filed with the State Administration for Market Regulation and SAFE.Outbound trade settlements for imported raw cane sugar will ride on three primary clearing rails. First, SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfers for open-account payments to sugar exporters, cleared through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) for Renminbi-denominated legs and through CHIPS or correspondent banking for United States dollar legs. Second, irrevocable letters of credit, typically revolving, issued by the onshore WFOE bank and advised through the seller's confirming bank in Brazil, Thailand, or Taiwan; presentation of original bills of lading, SGS inspection certificates, and commercial invoices triggers negotiation and reimbursement under UCP 600. Third, documentary collections, both documents against payment and documents against acceptance, for established counterparties with proven clearance history. For Taiwan-based counterparties, Renminbi payment legs are cleared via Bank of China Taipei Branch under the cross-strait currency clearing arrangement, while New Taiwan Dollar legs require clearing through the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) banking network. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Legal feasibility is fundamentally negative for onshore treasury use. Offshore digital financial technology EMIs, including Airwallex, Revolut Business, and Wise Business, are neither licensed deposit-taking institutions nor SAFE-designated foreign exchange banks in the People's Republic of China. Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and the PBOC Administrative Measures for the Payment Services of Non-Financial Institutions, no unlicensed digital EMI can lawfully open a domestic Renminbi settlement account, receive export proceeds on behalf of a mainland WFOE, operate a Foreign Currency Capital Account, or execute the SAFE capital verification and trade verification workflow. An offshore EMI may at most act as a pre-funded outbound payment corridor for incidental non-regulatory expenses, but relying on it for capital injection or trade settlement would create uncorrectable gaps in the audit trail, expose the enterprise to SAFE penalties for evasion of foreign exchange control, and invalidate the capital verification report required for registered capital compliance. The WFOE therefore must route all regulated flows through licensed Tier-1 domestic commercial banks; offshore EMIs are viable only as supplementary payment utilities for a separate offshore entity, never for the onshore WFOE.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Taiwan does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a limited company. The incorporators must however declare a reasonable issued capital amount, and the full subscribed capital must be deposited in a dedicated bank account in the company's name. A capital verification certificate issued by the bank must accompany the registration application. There is no blocked escrow account rule.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance (MOF), Taxation Agency, through the National Taxation Bureau of the Ministry of Finance; online electronic portal: MOF e-Tax Portal (etax.nat.gov.tw) and the MOF Unified Invoice Platform (e-invoice.nat.gov.tw)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a taxable presence in Taiwan, the entity must first appoint a resident tax agent and register the profit-seeking enterprise with the National Taxation Bureau. The procedure is: (1) submit a foreign company registration certificate duly notarized at a Taiwan consulate or by a local notary, together with the articles of incorporation and a board resolution appointing the Taiwan tax agent; (2) complete the national enterprise registration application through the MOF e-Tax Portal; (3) obtain the Tax Identification Number (TIN/VAT ID), which functions as the single business tax number; (4) file the separate Business Tax registration application for VAT purposes before commencing taxable sales; (5) for foreign digital or cross-border service suppliers without a fixed place of business, register through the MOF online system specifically for foreign enterprises and appoint a tax representative to handle VAT filings and invoicing obligations; (6) receive the Tax Registration Certificate and Uniform Invoice registration approval. All submissions must use the statutory forms embedded in the online portal and require the TIN for subsequent monthly or quarterly VAT returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan operates a managed floating NTD exchange-rate regime with foreign exchange controls administered by the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan). Current-account flows, including cross-border payroll, contractor disbursements, trade settlement, and service remuneration, are generally freely convertible through authorized foreign-exchange banks, subject only to declaration thresholds. Capital-account flows are more guarded: inbound equity subscriptions require advance approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs, and the bank must see the approval letter before crediting the capital escrow account. The NTD is not a fully deliverable offshore currency for pure third-party speculation; all corporate NTD conversions must be documented with a genuine trade, investment, or profit-remittance purpose. Export proceeds must be repatriated into Taiwan within ten days from collection, but can remain in a Foreign Currency Deposit Unit account without mandatory NTD conversion, so the effective surrender ratio is zero on FCDU-deposited receipts. Profit remittances by a foreign shareholder are permitted once the JV's annual accounts are audited, tax returns filed, and no outstanding Central Bank declaration violation is pending. In practice, the regime is current-account liberalized and capital-account guarded, with the licensed Taiwan bank acting as the single clearing node for every inbound and outbound transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected and repatriated into Taiwan within ten (10) days from the date of collection. The exporter may either convert the full 100% into NTD with an authorized foreign-exchange bank or deposit the proceeds into a Foreign Currency Deposit Unit (FCDU) account. The mandatory NTD surrender ratio is zero per cent for proceeds placed in the FCDU account before the deadline; however, repatriation into Taiwan and the lodgment of the export collection record with the bank/customs framework remain absolute statutory requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan), Foreign Exchange Department

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low legal viability as a primary treasury or clearing rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise do not hold an authorized foreign-exchange bank license in Taiwan and are barred from opening a domestic NTD corporate clearing account or from executing the statutory foreign-exchange declaration on behalf of the JV. They may be used only as a supplementary payout API for non-Taiwan contractor remuneration after the source funds have first cleared through a licensed Taiwan bank; the EMI must not be named as the beneficiary bank on the capital escrow or tax-related settlement account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The JV's capital injection must be preceded by final inward investment approval from the Taiwan Investment Commission. The Mainland parent then sends a SWIFT MT103 wire in convertible foreign currency to the JV's capital escrow account at Bank of Taiwan, CTBC, or Mega, using a valid declaration purpose code for direct investment. The receiving bank will record the remittance, issue a foreign-exchange certificate, and allow NTD conversion only after the CPA's capital verification report has been filed with the authorities. Every wire should carry the JV's approved identification number in the beneficiary reference field to match the approval certificate.Local NTD payroll should be run through the domestic clearing system from the operational payroll account, while foreign contractor payments and trade settlements are executed by SWIFT MT103 from an FCDU account, supported by the underlying service contract, invoice, and tax withholding certificate. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% flat profit-seeking enterprise income tax; plus a 5% retained-earnings surtax on undistributed current-year surplus, making the effective corporate-layer burden 24% before shareholder-level dividend withholding tax

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed to a Taiwan non-resident corporate or individual shareholder; the 5% retained-earnings surtax applies at the corporate level before distribution, so the combined corporate tax and WHT burden on distributed profits is approximately 41% without treaty relief

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, file between 1 May and 31 May of the following tax year; for an approved special fiscal year, file within five months after the close of the fiscal year; provisional tax prepayment for calendar-year entities is due by 30 September

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: TWD 100,000,000 in aggregate annual related-party transaction revenue triggers the mandatory local transfer pricing report and master file; related-party transaction disclosure is triggered at TWD 50,000,000; a group with consolidated revenue exceeding TWD 27,000,000,000 must additionally file a Country-by-Country Report and CbC notification

Economic substance scope: Taiwan is not a zero-tax offshore jurisdiction and does not apply OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations as a series; instead, the tax authority relies on the general anti-avoidance rule in Tax Collection Act Article 12-1, the substance-over-form doctrine in income tax determination, and the economic-activity standards embedded in DTA permanent-establishment and beneficial-ownership rules; the JV must prove that its core income-generating activities, including employment contract execution, payroll computation, statutory labor insurance administration, contractor verification, legal compliance, and bank account management, are actually performed in Taiwan; a Taiwanese EOR entity that lacks employees, office premises, decision-making autonomy, risk assumption, and operating substance will be treated as a shell by the Taiwan National Taxation Administration, its related-party deductions will be recharacterized, and treaty benefits will be denied to the beneficial-owner test

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Fraudulent tax evasion through shell payroll pass-through and offshore revenue diversion

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Act Article 41: any taxpayer who evades tax by fraud or improper means is liable to imprisonment up to five years and a fine up to TWD 10 million; Income Tax Act Article 110 imposes an additional understatement penalty of up to three times the undercharged tax; the evaded tax, late-payment interest, and audit additions are all collected with civil enforcement priority over general creditors

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false invoices, concealed offshore accounts, or false tax returns are jointly and severally liable with the company under Company Act Article 23 and Article 154 when the corporate form is abused; the National Taxation Administration may attach personal assets of directors under the tax collection procedure, apply to a court for a travel ban and blacklisting under Tax Collection Act Article 24 when unpaid tax exceeds TWD 1 million, and transfer the file to criminal prosecutors, who may impose up to five years imprisonment on the responsible director
Failure to withhold and remit non-resident withholding tax on outbound dividends, interest, and royalties

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 114 requires the withholding agent to pay the under-withheld tax and subjects the withholding agent to a fine up to three times the amount of the tax not withheld; Article 88 imposes the withholding obligation on the payer at the time of payment; late remittance triggers interest and additional penalties; repeated intentional violation is criminalized under Tax Collection Act Article 42 for withholding agents who fail or fraudulently certify that withholding was performed

Director personal liability: The director who authorized the wire transfers is personally liable for the unpaid WHT as a responsible person of the withholding agent; the tax authority may seize the director's assets and freeze bank accounts; the director may be blacklisted and barred from leaving Taiwan until the outstanding WHT is secured; a responsible person who participates in false certificate issuance or incomplete withholding declarations can be prosecuted and sentenced to imprisonment for up to five years under the criminal articles of the Tax Collection Act
Transfer-pricing manipulation, non-existent local file documentation, and lack of substance in a treaty-claimed EOR platform

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 43-1 authorizes the tax authority to recharacterize the transaction at arm's length and to increase taxable income by the artificially shifted amount; the transfer pricing assessment rules impose an additional penalty for failure to prepare, submit, or retain the local file and master file; Article 110 adds an understatement penalty up to three times the tax undercharged; a deliberate scheme with false documentation triggers Tax Collection Act Article 41 criminal prosecution and also invalidates the treaty claim, exposing the company to the full statutory 21% dividend or 20% royalty/interest WHT with interest and fines

Director personal liability: Directors who approve and sign transfer-pricing documentation that is knowingly false are personally liable for the resulting civil tax debt under Company Act Article 23; the director may be ordered to restore diverted assets to the Taiwanese entity to satisfy tax arrears; Taiwan customs and tax authorities will blacklist the responsible person and issue a departure ban under Tax Collection Act Article 24; criminal proceedings may result in imprisonment up to five years and confiscation of personal property held in Taiwan, including offshore beneficial interests, for the sanctioned tax evasion and fraudulent treaty abuse

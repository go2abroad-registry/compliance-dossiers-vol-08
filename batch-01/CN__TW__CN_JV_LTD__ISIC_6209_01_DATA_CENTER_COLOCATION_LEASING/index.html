A virtual office or commercial mailbox used as the sole registered address is likely to be rejected by tax authorities, SAMR, and banks for the data center colocation industry. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Taxation Administration, Ministry of Finance, Taiwan, acting through the five National Taxation Bureaus (Taipei, Kaohsiung, Northern, Central, Southern) and local tax authorities; online official portals: MOF Tax Entry Portal (etax.nat.gov.tw) for tax registrations and filings, and the e-Invoice Integration Service Platform (einvoice.nat.gov.tw) for electronic uniform invoice clearance and validation.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested limited company (the relevant Taiwan implementation of a Sino-foreign equity joint venture), the TIN/VAT ID procurement procedure is: 1. The foreign investors obtain investment approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) under the Foreign Investment Act. 2. The organizer reserves the company name and business scope with MOEA through the Company Registration Information System. 3. The company files incorporation with MOEA under the Company Act; MOEA issues the incorporation certificate and the eight-digit Unified Business Number (UBIN), which is the sole statutory Taiwan taxpayer identification number and VAT registration number for the entity. 4. The company must complete the tax-file registration (tax registration) with the National Taxation Bureau having jurisdiction over the registered business office before commencing taxable activity; the application must include the MOEA incorporation certificate, the entity constitution, foreign investment approval, business premise lease or ownership evidence, and the appointment of a legal representative. 5. The National Taxation Bureau opens the profit-seeking enterprise tax file and confirms the Business Tax and uniform-invoice obligations; the UBIN is simultaneously used for Business Tax, income tax withholding, and customs matters. 6. The company then registers on the e-Invoice Integration Service Platform for electronic uniform invoice issuance and selects the permitted e-invoice scheme. 7. A foreign non-resident enterprise that has no locally incorporated company and no Taiwan fixed place of business does not receive a UBIN; such an enterprise must apply to the National Taxation Bureau for a non-resident enterprise tax-file number for withholding tax purposes before earning Taiwan-source income and, if it makes taxable cross-border digital-service sales in Taiwan, must comply with the Ministry of Finance cross-border e-service registration scheme.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity election; a locally incorporated limited company is automatically a profit-seeking enterprise taxed at the entity level. No pass-through classification is available for a corporate joint venture, and no consolidated group income tax election is available for a single legal entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For incorporated companies, no turnover threshold applies: tax registration is mandatory from incorporation and commencement of business. For unincorporated businesses, the simplified registration threshold is TWD 80,000 per month in sales of goods or TWD 40,000 per month in sales of services. The statutory VAT headline rate is 5% on taxable gross receipts.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act, goods exported from Taiwan customs territory and services sold for export are zero-rated. For the data-center colocation and bare-metal hosting scope, zero-rating is available only where the colocation service transaction is demonstrably provided to a foreign enterprise, the service is contractually accepted and used wholly outside Taiwan, the servers and equipment are owned by or under the control of the foreign customer, the consideration is denominated in foreign currency and fully repatriated, and no taxable event is attributable to a domestic Taiwan user. If the rack-space leasing, power, cooling, and physical security services are executed at the Taiwan data center, the Ministry of Finance and National Taxation Bureau will generally treat the service as rendered in Taiwan and subject to 5% business tax rather than zero-rated, unless an advance ruling or dual-criteria export service analysis supports offshore use.

  • Customs export declaration form (export entry) filed by the exporter with Taiwan Customs, showing the goods or equipment physically leaving Taiwan
  • Commercial invoice issued by the JV to the foreign customer
  • Packing list
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or international cargo receipt evidencing cross-border transport
  • Inward remittance advice or foreign exchange settlement certificate issued by an authorized bank in Taiwan proving receipt of the export proceeds
  • Executed colocation/hosting service agreement identifying the foreign buyer, offshore service-use location, payment in foreign currency, and assignment of server asset ownership or control

All businesses must issue uniform invoices for every taxable sale. Electronic uniform invoices must be transmitted to the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice Integration Service Platform using the approved standard XML/JSON API schema after each issuance; the platform performs format validation and records the invoice number. Transmission must occur within the time windows prescribed by the Electronic Invoice Implementation Standards, generally within 48 hours for consumer-facing B2C e-invoices and within 7 days for B2B e-invoices. The API connection requires a government-approved web-server certificate and identity credentials issued by the platform. Taiwan does not apply per-invoice continuous government clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping in the Latin American sense; instead, anti-fraud control is exercised through the platform's central registration of invoice codes and random encrypted serial numbers printed as QR codes on e-invoice records.

Customs and National Taxation Bureau examiners may audit the export declaration, bill of lading, service contract, bank settlement records, and e-invoice history. Failure to substantiate zero-rating with the complete documentation set causes the transaction to be reclassified as domestic taxable supply; the taxpayer must pay the 5% business tax, with statutory interest, and the output tax is clawed back retrospectively. Under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act, an underpayment or fraudulent use of false invoices to obtain zero-rating is punishable by a surcharge of five to fifteen times the amount of tax evaded in addition to collection of the tax. Intentional evasion may be referred for criminal prosecution under the Tax Collection Act, and the assessment period is extended in cases of fraud or omission, allowing tax authorities to reopen filed returns and claw back export zero-rating benefits for prior years.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends paid by a Chinese-resident enterprise to its non-resident corporate shareholder under the Enterprise Income Tax Law Art. 3 and 37; a 5% preferential rate applies if the foreign shareholder is a qualifying resident of a treaty/jurisdiction with a 5% reduced dividend withholding provision (e.g., Hong Kong SAR or Singapore under certain conditions), subject to the holding period and beneficial ownership requirements. Taiwan is treated as a domestic jurisdiction for many cross-strait matters in practice, but the cross-strait tax treaty (海峡两岸避免双重征税及加强税务合作协议) is limited in scope and has no statutory dividend withholding reduction to 5%; therefore the standard 10% rate applies for repatriation to Taiwanese shareholders unless the shareholder operates through a Hong Kong/Singapore intermediate holding company that meets the treaty's principal purpose and substance tests. The Taiwan shareholder may also be taxed in Taiwan on the dividend, with a foreign tax credit for the 10% Chinese WHT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous Local File threshold: annual related-party transactions exceeding RMB 10 million for tangible property purchases/sales, or exceeding RMB 40 million in total for all other related-party transactions (including services, interest, and royalties); Master File threshold: annual controlled transactions exceeding RMB 1 billion in aggregate or the entity is a member of a group whose annual related-party revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion and that is one of the largest 100 worldwide; Special Factor File (特殊事项文档) required for intangible property transactions exceeding RMB 10 million. The Taiwan–China JV is presumed to have cross-border related-party transactions (management fees, technical service fees, colocation revenue from affiliated Taiwan data center clients) and must perform an annual related-party transaction disclosure (年度关联业务往来报告表, Form A/J/S) with the EIT return, including the Local File disclosure if the thresholds are met.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as codified in Taiwan’s Anti-Hybrid Mismatch and controlled foreign corporation rules are not directly applicable to the PRC-jurisdiction JV; however, the Chinese tax administration applies the multifactor substance analysis under SAT Public Notice No. 7 (2015) and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in EIT Law Art. 47: the JV must demonstrate real business purpose, actual management and decision-making in China, sufficient employees (headcount), tangible data center assets (servers, racks, power infrastructure), and risk-bearing capacity. If the Taiwan shareholder receives passive income (dividends, royalties, interest) without economic substance, the Chinese payer may deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the income, and impose 10% WHT plus late surcharges; the Taiwanese shareholder may also be subject to Taiwan’s CFC rules if the JV is classified as a CFC and its income is predominantly passive under Taiwan's Income Tax Act Art. 43-4.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (偷税) and Fraudulent Tax Refund

Statutory sanction: SAT will assess back taxes, impose a surcharge of 0.05% per day on the unpaid amount, and impose a penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax; criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Art. 201 (tax evasion) where the amount is over RMB 100,000 and constitutes 10% or more of the total taxable income, punishable by up to 7 years imprisonment and unlimited fines; conviction triggers de facto corporate debarment from public procurement and cross-border bank credit

Director personal liability: Under EIT Law Art. 49 and SAT anti-evasion practice, directors and de facto controllers (including the Taiwan-appointed director) can be jointly and severally liable for restitution of the evaded tax if they authorized, signed, or knowingly benefited from the false filing; travel bans (限制出境) under the Tax Collection Law Art. 44 and 52 can be imposed by SAT for unpaid tax above RMB 20,000; imprisonment up to 7 years, plus prohibition from serving as a director of any PRC company for up to 5 years after criminal conviction
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: SAT may re-assess taxable income under the arm’s length principle and impose a transfer pricing penalty of 5% to 20% of the under-assessed tax (Public Notice No. 16, 2018) if no contemporaneous documentation was prepared; with documentation, only late payment interest applies (base rate plus 2 percentage points); back-adjustments for up to 10 years

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign inflated TP documentation or false related-party transaction schedules face joint and several liability for the re-assessed tax if the entity becomes insolvent; they are also subject to personal fines up to RMB 500,000 under the criminal law for providing false financial statements; travel bans and non-liability release are not available until the reassessment is paid
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance and Tax Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the PPT and beneficial ownership rules, recharacterization of income with full 10% WHT, additional 50% to 3 times WHT penalty for failure to withhold (public notice 372, 2016); Taiwan’s taxpayer may be imputed CFC income under Taiwan Income Tax Act Art. 43-4, triggering Taiwan EIT at 20% with credit for Chinese WHT; the PRC JV may be deemed non-tax-compliant leading to a tax compliance classification downgrade (失信) with public credit blacklisting

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell JV (including the Taiwan nominator) are jointly liable for uncollected withholding tax if the JV failed to withhold dividends/royalties; they can be prohibited from exiting Chinese mainland territory under Art. 6 of the Exit-Entry Administration Law, and may face criminal liability under Art. 205 if the shell is used for invoice fraud; Taiwan CFC regime can attribute undistributed earnings to the individual Taiwan controlling shareholder subject to a 5% surcharge for failure to report
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Foreign Exchange Control Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law (2023), a corporate fine of up to RMB 5 million for serious non-compliance and suspension of business; under the Provisional Regulations on Foreign Exchange Control Art. 39, a fine of 30% to 5 times the foreign exchange amount for illegal cross-border transfer; SAFE may blacklist the JV and revoke its settlement eligibility

Director personal liability: Directors who personally direct the offshore cash routing face personal criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law Art. 190 (foreign exchange evasion) and Art. 191 (money laundering), with imprisonment of 5 to 10 years and personal fines of up to RMB 5 million; in addition, under the Tax Collection Law Art. 44, the SAT can restrict exit and revoke passports; bank account freeze orders against directors’ personal Taiwan and China accounts are available to enforce restitution
False Invoicing (虚开增值税发票) and VAT Fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT penalty of up to 50% of the tax amount plus daily surcharge; criminal conviction for false invoice issuance under PRC Criminal Law Art. 205 imposes a fine of RMB 50,000 to 500,000 on the entity and imprisonment of the responsible individuals for up to 15 years; the tax authority concurrently revokes qualification for simplified tax treatment and denies credit for all connected invoices

Director personal liability: The legal representative, financial director, and Taiwan-nominated senior manager who sign or authorize invoice issuance are individually subject to criminal liability even where the entity is dissolved (Art. 205, Criminal Law); they are barred from leaving China, and their personal assets are attachable for VAT restitution under civil restitution rules

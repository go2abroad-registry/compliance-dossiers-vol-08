Rejected. For a WFOE engaged in ISIC 5222 offshore supply vessel leasing, municipal tax authorities, maritime safety authorities, and banks will reject a virtual office or commercial mailbox because charter party administration, vessel flag and insurance documentation, crew records, and statutory books must be maintained at a physical operational office. Under PRC Foreign Investment Law and Taiwan maritime and shipping authority practice, a registered office must be a real address where legal process can be served and where directors, executives, or a lawful statutory representative can receive notices. Use of a virtual office in this Medium_Risk_Enhanced industry will trigger tax registration denial, bank account refusal, and commercial substance deficiency findings.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under the Company Act; however, the subscribed capital in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid in cash into a designated Taiwan bank account established in the name of the company preparatory office. For a foreign investment WFOE, the amount approved by the Investment Commission must be remitted from abroad and the paid-in capital must be evidenced by a certified public accountant capital verification report. No blocked escrow account is required by general incorporation law, but funds supporting the registration must remain deposited in the Taiwan bank account until the registration certificate is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection certificate and civil defense clearance for the shore office, stores area, and any dock-side support premises; annual fire safety equipment inspection report; structural and electrical load certificate for any marine stores shed or fuel storage area; Occupational Safety and Health Act compliance inspection for maintenance and cargo-handling areas; and port facility security clearance issued for the vessel and shore interface.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under the Company Act; however, the subscribed capital in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid in cash into a designated Taiwan bank account established in the name of the company preparatory office. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taiwan - National Taxation Bureau (regional competent tax office) and the Tax Entry Portal at https://etax.nat.gov.tw; e-invoice data is handled through the Ministry of Finance Integrated E-Invoice Platform at https://www.einvoice.nat.gov.tw.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity intending to conduct taxable business in Taiwan through a fixed place of business, branch, or permanent establishment must first complete foreign-company commercial registration/approval with the Ministry of Economic Affairs. The entity must then apply to the competent National Taxation Bureau for tax registration before business commencement or within 15 days of approval/commencement, submitting the foreign-company registration certificate, approved branch/charter documents, authorized tax agent, registered address proof, and business scope. Upon approval, the Bureau issues an 8-digit Unified Business Number (UBIN), which serves as the VAT registration number and withholding tax account identification. A foreign enterprise without a fixed place of business in Taiwan but deriving Taiwan-source income is not normally VAT-registered; the Taiwan payor performs Income Tax Act withholding and the foreign entity may appoint a tax agent for filings. Online registration and post-registration filing are available through the Tax Entry Portal after credential issuance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign non-resident entity intending to conduct taxable business in Taiwan through a fixed place of business, branch, or permanent establishment must first complete foreign-company commercial registration/approval with the Ministry of Economic Affairs. The entity must then apply to the competent National Taxation Bureau for tax registration before business commencement or within 15 days of approval/commencement, submitting the foreign-company registration certificate, approved branch/charter documents, authorized tax agent, registered address proof, and business scope. Upon approval, the Bureau issues an 8-digit Unified Business Number (UBIN), which serves as the VAT registration number and withholding tax account identification. A foreign enterprise without a fixed place of business in Taiwan but deriving Taiwan-source income is not normally VAT-registered; the Taiwan payor performs Income Tax Act withholding and the foreign entity may appoint a tax agent for filings. Online registration and post-registration filing are available through the Tax Entry Portal after credential issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity classification elections; tax character is determined under the Income Tax Act and the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for mandatory standard VAT registration; any enterprise with a fixed place of business or permanent establishment must register before starting business. Domestic small-scale vendors with monthly average sales below NT$80,000 may qualify for a small-scale exemption from Uniform Invoice and standard VAT requirements, but offshore vessel-leasing and cross-border marine chartering operations are not eligible for that small-scale concession.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Taiwan, goods supplied to foreign vessels engaged in international transportation, and services supplied to foreign enterprises or overseas consumers that are not used within Taiwan and are paid in foreign exchange qualify for zero-rating. The offshore supply vessel leasing and chartering business qualifies where the offshore platform support vessel is chartered for international or Taiwan-external offshore operations and charter hire is paid in foreign currency by a foreign charterer, with the service not consumed or beneficially enjoyed within Taiwan. Zero-rating preserves the exporter's entitlement to input VAT credit or refund.

  • Customs Export Declaration issued by Taiwan Customs (customs export report with export validation)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or vessel charter-party/sea waybill evidencing the offshore supply or international voyage
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Slip or Export Proceeds Remittance Certificate issued by an authorized bank evidencing repatriation of foreign-currency charter hire
  • Commercial invoice and charter agreement in foreign currency evidencing the cross-border maritime supply

Taiwan mandates the Uniform Invoice system for VAT-registered businesses. Taxpayers must issue Uniform Invoices for taxable sales, and electronic invoices must be transmitted to the Ministry of Finance Integrated E-Invoice Platform through the E-Invoice Turnkey Service with prescribed upload deadlines and cross-track reconciliation. Designated/e-invoice users must use government-specified electronic transmission formats and API mechanisms, include serial-number and QR/error-control markings, and comply with anti-fraud controls covering invoice uniqueness, void/recording, and taxpayer account authentication. Zero-rated export and offshore supply sales are evidenced by zero-rated Uniform Invoices supported by customs and foreign-exchange documentation.

Zero-rating claims are subject to strict documentary audit by the National Taxation Bureau. If the customs export declaration, bill of lading, foreign-exchange settlement evidence, or charter documentation is missing, inconsistent, or not repatriated within the applicable deadline, the zero-rate is disallowed, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 5%, additional business tax is assessed, and late-payment interest and statutory penalties apply, including clawback of any previously refunded input VAT. Fraudulent, fictitious, or forged export/charter documentation invokes enhanced assessments, criminal liability under the Tax Collection Act, and heavier statutory penalties on the underpaid tax with possible extension of the retrospective assessment window.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Taiwan limited company after the 2018 Company Act amendment; the sole shareholder must subscribe the full declared capital (NT$16,000,000) and remit it into a Taiwan bank account prior to filing. No statutory escrow account is mandated, but the Investment Commission requires the inward remittance receipt/capital verification certificate and restricts withdrawal of funds for any purpose outside the approved business scope.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains current-account convertibility for trade in services, which includes deep-sea towage hire and maritime salvage awards; all current-account receipts and payments must be supported by a genuine underlying transaction, a commercial contract, invoice, or arbitral award, and must settle through SAFE-supervised designated banks. Capital-account items—FDI equity injection, shareholder loans, and liquidation or capital-reduction repatriation—remain subject to prior SAFE filing/registration, real-demand authentication, and negative-list compliance. For a Taiwan-invested WFOE, after-tax distributable profits may be remitted to the Taiwan parent under the current-account 'investment income' head upon presentation of audited financial statements, the corporate income tax final-settlement certificate, the dividend withholding tax certificate, and the bank's SAFE verification; no fixed repatriation cap applies, but the remittance documentation must be complete at the bank counter. Liquidation proceeds, equity-transfer consideration, and repayment of foreign shareholder loans are capital-account flows requiring SAFE registration and case-by-case review, with processing time of several weeks. Inbound salvage awards under Lloyd's Open Form (LOF 2011) and emergency towage fees are classifiable as current-account 'maritime transport and salvage services' income; convertibility to RMB and onward disbursement is administratively feasible when the contract, the salvage award or security agreement, and the P&I club letter of undertaking are produced. Full RMB convertibility for speculative or capital-account purposes is not available; the WFOE must operate strictly through the onshore FX market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished mandatory FX surrender in 2012; there is no statutory conversion ratio for export or service-fee proceeds. The controlling obligation is repatriation plus SAFE verification: the WFOE must bring towage and salvage receivables into its onshore current-account foreign exchange account through a designated bank, and the bank must report the credit to SAFE within five working days of settlement using the trade-in-services verification module. For service-export contracts, the collection period is the contractual credit term; a receipt more than 180 days after the invoice or service-completion date must be flagged to SAFE with a written explanation and debt-collection evidence. For incidental goods exports (e.g., sale of surplus salvage equipment or condemned vessel parts), the goods-export proceeds must enter the SAFE trade-monitoring system within 90 days of the customs export declaration, and extended collection periods must be pre-registered with the local SAFE branch. The WFOE may retain 100% of its FX receipts in the account or convert to RMB at will; any conversion and every subsequent payment from the RMB settlement-pending account must be matched to a genuine business purpose with invoices. RMB-denominated receipts from Taiwan counterparties must be reported through RCPMIS by the settlement bank; Taiwan legal and natural persons are treated under special compatriot investment rules, but treasury receipts are subject to the same trade-verification framework.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, functioning as the FX-control and cross-border payment regulatory authority under the People's Bank of China (PBOC); cross-strait Taiwan investment is additionally coordinated through the MOFCOM Department of Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macao Affairs and the State Council Taiwan Affairs Office, with Taiwan-origin RMB clearing channeled through Bank of China Taipei branch and the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low, with only ancillary application. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, WorldFirst, Payoneer) cannot lawfully replace SAFE-regulated onshore settlement for a China-incorporated WFOE: towage-fee and salvage receivables must be credited to the WFOE's onshore current-account FX account and reported by the designated bank to SAFE. A mainland entity cannot open a general-purpose offshore EMI account without SAFE 'overseas account' approval, and routing Taiwan parent capital injections or client charges through an offshore EMI would breach RCPMIS reporting and SAFE trade-verification rules. For USD-multi-million invoices, P&I club security deposits, and LOF award wires, the EMI rail is operationally and legally non-viable. A Shanghai Free Trade Zone or Hainan FTP-registered WFOE may obtain a Free Trade Account (FT account) at a licensed bank, which offers onshore-offshore flexibility similar to an EMI but with full SAFE supervision; no unlicensed EMI is permitted to hold the WFOE's operating treasury.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for Taiwan parent to WFOE equity injection: (1) complete the Taiwan-investment filing at the local MOFCOM/Taiwan Affairs competent authority and obtain the WFOE business license; (2) complete the FDI FX registration at the designated bank under the direct registration system, using SAFE receipt code '622010' (inbound FDI equity contribution in currency) with counterpart jurisdiction 'TW'; (3) open a distinct FDI foreign exchange capital fund account segregated from current-account FX accounts; (4) the Taiwan parent remits TWD, USD, or RMB via SWIFT MT103 with the remittance narrative stating 'investment contribution' and quoting the SAFE FDI registration number; (5) upon credit, the bank files the receipt with SAFE and classifies the funds strictly as foreign-exchange capital; (6) conversion of capital into RMB operates under the voluntary-settlement regime, with the bank crediting an RMB settlement-pending-payment account; every onward payment from that account must be invoice-backed and business-purpose verified, and is prohibited for onshore securities, speculative equity or trust investments, non-own-use real estate, and on-lending; (7) for RMB remittances from Taiwan, settlement clears through Bank of China Taipei branch/CIPS and must be reported via RCPMIS by the receiving bank. No minimum paid-in capital is imposed by law, but the registered capital amount in the MOFCOM filing must be received within the undertaking period, and any injection exceeding the registered capital requires separate SAFE approval. Under LOF 2011, the salvage award wire is treated not as capital but as current-account service income; the crediting bank requires the signed LOF agreement and the arbitral award to complete trade verification.Standard clearing rails for the WFOE's payment obligations: (a) ordinary maritime operating payments (bunkers, port dues, crew wages, subcontract towage, P&I premiums, and repairs) are effected by SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the current-account FX account; for each service payment exceeding USD 50,000 or the RMB equivalent, the bank requires the outward-services tax filing voucher from the tax authority and withholds applicable corporate income tax and withholding tax at source; (b) cross-border RMB payments to Taiwan suppliers and sub-tug operators are cleared via CIPS or Taiwan agent banks and reported through RCPMIS; (c) large capital procurement (e.g., construction of a new escort tug, heavy salvaging gear, submersible pumps) should be routed through an irrevocable sight commercial letter of credit issued by the WFOE's bank (SWIFT MT700/MT720) and confirmed by the overseas beneficiary's bank to eliminate prepayment risk; (d) disbursements to overseas co-salvors and tug beneficiaries secured under a LOF escrow are released only upon presentation of the Lloyd's award, a P&I club release instruction, or a jointly signed disbursement authority; the bank records the escrow debit instruction and attaches the supporting release document; (e) all outbound payments must match the negative list and be recorded in the annual SAFE/BOP filing, with the bank reporting each cross-border debit to SAFE within five working days of payment.Deliverable onshore CNY foreign exchange forwards (forward settlement and sale of foreign exchange) for current-account exposures such as USD/EUR towage receivables and subcontract payables, executed under a NAFMII FX master agreement with the onboarded bank; Line 15 (Offset of Prior-Year Losses, 弥补以前年度亏损) Tax losses carried forward and utilized in the current year, capped at 5 years from the loss year. Losses of the Chinese WFOE cannot be off-set against income of the Taiwan parent or other group entities; group relief is not available in China. Late filing of the annual return: RMB 2,000 fine, increased to RMB 10,000 for serious cases (Tax Administration Law Article 62); underpayment other than fraud: 0.05% daily surcharge on unpaid tax (Article 32) and a 50%-500% late-filing/underpayment penalty (Articles 63-64); fraudulent underpayment is subject to criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201.
Line 23 (Taxable Income, 应纳税所得额) Total book profit adjusted by increases, decreases, and loss offset, matched to minimum-tolerance taxable income. Relevant deemed income for a towage vessel owned by a related offshore entity and operated by the WFOE may be imputed under substance and control rules. Late filing of the annual return: RMB 2,000 fine, increased to RMB 10,000 for serious cases (Tax Administration Law Article 62); underpayment other than fraud: 0.05% daily surcharge on unpaid tax (Article 32) and a 50%-500% late-filing/underpayment penalty (Articles 63-64); fraudulent underpayment is subject to criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201.
Line 26 (Applicable Tax Rate, 税率) 25% standard rate; 15% for High and New Technology Enterprises; 20% reduced rate (effective 5% for qualifying small low-profit enterprises). The WFOE is a resident enterprise; the rate does not depend on profit repatriation. Late filing of the annual return: RMB 2,000 fine, increased to RMB 10,000 for serious cases (Tax Administration Law Article 62); underpayment other than fraud: 0.05% daily surcharge on unpaid tax (Article 32) and a 50%-500% late-filing/underpayment penalty (Articles 63-64); fraudulent underpayment is subject to criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201.
Line 27 (Income Tax Payable, 应纳所得税额) Taxable income multiplied by the applicable rate before incentives and credits. No tax sparing credit applies under the Cross-Strait Tax Agreement. Late filing of the annual return: RMB 2,000 fine, increased to RMB 10,000 for serious cases (Tax Administration Law Article 62); underpayment other than fraud: 0.05% daily surcharge on unpaid tax (Article 32) and a 50%-500% late-filing/underpayment penalty (Articles 63-64); fraudulent underpayment is subject to criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201.
Line 31 (Tax Reduction and Exemption, 减免所得税额) Incentive amounts: environmental protection equipment deductions, venture capital exemptions, and R&D super-deductions. Towage-specific incentives are not available; claims require pre-filing technology registration documentation. Late filing of the annual return: RMB 2,000 fine, increased to RMB 10,000 for serious cases (Tax Administration Law Article 62); underpayment other than fraud: 0.05% daily surcharge on unpaid tax (Article 32) and a 50%-500% late-filing/underpayment penalty (Articles 63-64); fraudulent underpayment is subject to criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201.
Line 33 (Foreign-Source Income Tax, 境外所得应纳所得税额) Chinese tax attributable to income from towage/salvage operations sourced outside mainland China. Foreign taxes paid are creditable only for resident enterprises with documentary evidence; the Taiwan parent is not taxed currently on withholding unless dividend remitted. Late filing of the annual return: RMB 2,000 fine, increased to RMB 10,000 for serious cases (Tax Administration Law Article 62); underpayment other than fraud: 0.05% daily surcharge on unpaid tax (Article 32) and a 50%-500% late-filing/underpayment penalty (Articles 63-64); fraudulent underpayment is subject to criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201.
Line 34 (Actual Payable, 实际应纳所得税额) Net annual CIT payable after foreign tax credit and prepayments withheld in prior quarters. Any unpaid balance must be settled before an exit ban can be avoided by the legal representative. Late filing of the annual return: RMB 2,000 fine, increased to RMB 10,000 for serious cases (Tax Administration Law Article 62); underpayment other than fraud: 0.05% daily surcharge on unpaid tax (Article 32) and a 50%-500% late-filing/underpayment penalty (Articles 63-64); fraudulent underpayment is subject to criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201.
Line 2 (Cumulative Taxable Income for the Year, 本年累计金额) Cumulative taxable income computed on actual quarterly profits (or prior-year apportionment basis). Accrual of international towage income is required in the quarter when services are performed, regardless of invoice timing. Late quarterly filing: RMB 2,000-10,000 per Article 62; underpayment of quarterly installments: 0.05% daily surcharge and a 50%-500% penalty where the under-declaration is deliberate.
Line 4 (Applicable Tax Rate, 税率) 25%, 15% preferential, or 20% small-profit rate as applicable. Rate must match the expected annual rate; under-withholding triggers surcharge interest. Late quarterly filing: RMB 2,000-10,000 per Article 62; underpayment of quarterly installments: 0.05% daily surcharge and a 50%-500% penalty where the under-declaration is deliberate.
Line 6 (Quarterly Tax Payable, 应补(退)所得税额) Cumulative prepayment computed after deducting prepayments of prior quarters. Non-resident shareholders' withholding is not credited here; the WFOE's own CIT prepayment is separate. Late quarterly filing: RMB 2,000-10,000 per Article 62; underpayment of quarterly installments: 0.05% daily surcharge and a 50%-500% penalty where the under-declaration is deliberate.
Block A (Withholding Agent Particulars, 扣缴义务人信息) WFOE name, unified social credit code, registered address, and competent tax authority. The WFOE acts as withholding agent for the non-resident recipient; bank payment instructions must cross-match the filed return before foreign exchange release. Failure to withhold: 50%-300% penalty on the unwithheld amount (Tax Administration Law Article 69); failure to file the withholding return: RMB 2,000-10,000 fine (Article 62); failure to remit: 0.05% daily surcharge and freezing of bank deposits.
Block B (Non-Resident Payee Particulars, 非居民企业信息) Taiwan parent company name, Taiwan unified business number (tax ID), country of residence, and bank account details. The Taiwan payee must be identified as resident of Taiwan for the Cross-Strait Tax Agreement claim; the Certificate of Tax Residence from Taiwan's Ministry of Finance (居住者证明) must be retained. Failure to withhold: 50%-300% penalty on the unwithheld amount (Tax Administration Law Article 69); failure to file the withholding return: RMB 2,000-10,000 fine (Article 62); failure to remit: 0.05% daily surcharge and freezing of bank deposits.
Block C (Gross Payment Amount, 收入额) Gross dividend amount resolved, gross interest or royalty contract amount before withholding. Amounts reduced to nil after thin-capitalization recharacterization must be reported as deemed dividends with a 10% (or 5%) withholding rate; SAFE wire payment on Lloyd's standard form requires the same declared amount. Failure to withhold: 50%-300% penalty on the unwithheld amount (Tax Administration Law Article 69); failure to file the withholding return: RMB 2,000-10,000 fine (Article 62); failure to remit: 0.05% daily surcharge and freezing of bank deposits.
Block D (Withholding Rate and Exemption, 适用税率及减免) Statutory 10% provisional rate, or 5% under Cross-Strait Tax Agreement Article 10 for qualifying 25%-or-higher direct shareholders; 7% for interest and royalties under the Agreement. Treaty relief is applied by the WFOE after collecting the Taiwan residence certificate and beneficial ownership declaration; the tax authority may deny relief on post-review if the Taiwan entity is a conduit. Failure to withhold: 50%-300% penalty on the unwithheld amount (Tax Administration Law Article 69); failure to file the withholding return: RMB 2,000-10,000 fine (Article 62); failure to remit: 0.05% daily surcharge and freezing of bank deposits.
Block E (Withholding Date and Remittance, 扣缴日期) Date of actual payment or deemed distribution; remittance deadline seven calendar days thereafter. Failure to remit by deadline triggers the daily surcharge and potential foreign exchange control blacklisting. Failure to withhold: 50%-300% penalty on the unwithheld amount (Tax Administration Law Article 69); failure to file the withholding return: RMB 2,000-10,000 fine (Article 62); failure to remit: 0.05% daily surcharge and freezing of bank deposits.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is currently imposed for a Taiwan limited company. The paid-in capital must nevertheless be reasonably sufficient for the declared subscription-box merchandising, warehousing and fulfillment operation. A Mainland China WFOE investor must first obtain Investment Commission approval, remit the approved capital into a Taiwan bank foreign-investment account, and have the capital verified by an independent Taiwanese certified public accountant. Unless the investment approval letter imposes an escrow, restricted withdrawal, or capital repatriation condition, there is no continuing blocked escrow obligation after capital verification; funds may be used for lawful corporate purposes only after company registration is completed.

E-government filing portal

For the CN_WFOE_LTD, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued upon market-registration is the statutory TIN. Procedure: (1) file the WFOE incorporation application with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); (2) after the business licence bearing the USCC is issued, SAMR data are pushed automatically to the STA; (3) within 30 calendar days, complete the enterprise post-registration tax registration and taxpayer accounting filing on the enterprise eTax portal of the local STA branch; (4) for foreign non-resident entities that are not incorporated as WFOEs but do business in China without a fixed place, file a separate Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Registration via the same portal and obtain a non-resident TIN, attaching the foreign certificate of incorporation, power of attorney, board resolution appointing the tax agent/representative, and bank account details; (5) update the tax registration for changes in capital, shareholders, directors and addresses within 15 working days.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods in the ISIC 4791 subscription-box trade qualify for 0% VAT export zero-rating if they are actually declared at China Customs, leave China Customs territory and are delivered to Taiwan, the exporter is a registered VAT general taxpayer, the export sale is settled in foreign exchange, foreign-exchange proceeds are repatriated to China, and the export tax rebate and exemption application is electronically submitted within the statutory declaration period. Qualifying offshore digital services must also be supplied from China and consumed outside China to receive zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (export tax refund copy) evidencing the outward clearance
  • Export Sales Invoice (VAT invoice issued for export and recorded as zero-rated)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (transport document showing goods shipped from China to Taiwan)
  • Foreign Exchange Collection Electronic Record issued by SAFE or the settlement bank as successor to the former Export Collection Verification Form
  • International Sales Contract, commercial invoice and packing list
  • Purchase VAT Special Invoices supporting the input VAT refund calculation

The STA requires fully digitalised electronic invoices under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. VAT invoices, both special and ordinary, must be issued through the national electronic invoice service platform via the official API; invoice data are transmitted in real time to STA servers; each invoice receives a digitally validated anti-forgery tax stamp and is subject to automated anomaly monitoring. No invoice may be issued offline after the digital e-invoicing roll-out.

The STA and General Administration of Customs cross-validate export declarations, e-invoices, logistics waybills and SAFE foreign-exchange collection records. If zero-rating is denied on audit, the VAT refund is clawed back with statutory late-payment interest; a fraud penalty of 50% to five times the tax involved applies under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection; the enterprise may be deprived of export-tax-refund eligibility for up to two years; criminal liability for tax fraud and invoice or customs document forgery may be pursued under China's Criminal Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is not freely convertible in China's capital account. A WFOE is treated as a resident enterprise for FX purposes, and foreign-investor injected capital is classified as capital-account FX, requiring an SAFE-registered FDI relationship at the domestic bank before receipt. Once received, the RMB capital is restricted to the WFOE's approved business purposes under the payment-basis settlement system; each conversion requires a payment application and supporting underlying documentation. Current-account transactions, such as cross-border subscription revenues derived from the export of physical goods, can be converted by banks after genuine transaction verification under SAFE's trade-in-goods administration without prior approval. Dividend or profit remittance to the Taiwan investor is permitted as investment income after annual audit, corporate income tax settlement, and bank verification of the board resolution, audited financial statements, and tax payment certificates. The principal practical blockage is not legal convertibility but operational compliance: late FDI registration, mismatched trade documents, unclear downstream use of converted RMB, or an unreconciled order-to-cash trail will trigger rejection at the bank's AML/compliance stage. Capital-account outflows such as repayment of shareholder loans or cross-border guarantees remain tightly controlled and require separate SAFE filings and bank evidence. For a subscription box business, the operational FX flows are mostly current-account trade flows and should be kept cleanly documented at invoice, customs, logistics, and payment gateway levels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current trade-in-goods reform, export proceeds derived from subscription box sales must be repatriated to a domestic bank-designated foreign exchange current account within 180 days after the export customs declaration; the mandatory surrender ratio is 0%, meaning a WFOE may retain 100% of eligible export proceeds in foreign currency or convert wholly or partially into RMB at its discretion. The retention must be supported by legitimate trade receipts, and the entire lifecycle from customs declaration, logistics movement, receipt of payment, and settlement must be reported into the SAFE trade-in-goods electronic monitoring system. If payment is delayed beyond 180 days, the exporter must submit an extension application through its bank before the deadline, with the commercial contract, collection correspondence, and a bank-accepted commercial reason.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its subordinate State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Low for use as a bank-of-record; Medium only as an ancillary cross-border collection aggregator. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Revolut, Wise, and Airwallex are not licensed by the PBOC to operate as banks or payment institutions in mainland China, and they cannot legally maintain RMB current accounts, receive foreign-direct-investment capital, or issue account numbers that satisfy SAFE's capital-account reporting requirements. Stripe and Authorize.Net are similarly not licensed to onboard a mainland WFOE directly for RMB settlement; they may only serve as front-end B2C payment aggregators for international subscribers when connected to a SAFE-filed cross-border payment institution such as LianLian Pay, PingPong, or Airwallex's cross-border product, which holds a licensed Chinese collection account and settles net proceeds to the WFOE's domestic bank account. The WFOE must never rely on an offshore EMI as its primary treasury rail because no capital verification, dividend remittance, or compliance audit can be credibly performed through an unlicensed foreign account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route is a direct SWIFT MT103 remittance from the Taiwan investor's corporate account, or from a pre-reviewed conduit account, to the WFOE's FDI foreign exchange capital account at a designated domestic bank, using the bank's capital-account purpose code for foreign direct investment equity capital injection together with the WFOE's SAFE FDI registration serial number. TWD is not a freely convertible settlement currency in mainland China, so the practical remittance currencies are USD or offshore RMB via Hong Kong. Upon receipt, the bank credits the FDI foreign exchange capital account and monitors each subsequent RMB conversion under the payment-basis settlement rules; the WFOE must submit the underlying contract, invoice, and payment purpose for every conversion. Immediately after the capital receipt, a qualified Chinese CPA must issue a capital verification report, and the company must update its registration data with the local State Administration for Market Regulation and the bank's SAFE information system if registered capital or shareholder structure is amended.For operational disbursements such as supplier payments, logistics fees, and customs duties, use SWIFT MT103 from the WFOE's domestic bank to the supplier's bank under a current-account goods-trade or service-trade purpose code, attaching the purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, and customs declaration in the bank's trade monitoring system. For recurring high-value imports of raw materials or subscription-box components, issue a sight Commercial Letter of Credit via SWIFT MT700 using the same documentation package. For low-risk repeat suppliers, documentary collections or direct wires after bank credit approval are acceptable. For settlement of global subscription-box sales, use a licensed cross-border payment institution that aggregates customer payments offshore and settles net proceeds into the WFOE's domestic bank account with a lump-sum reference and a transaction-level data file for SAFE reconciliation. All outbound payments must be matched to underlying trade events; no disbursement should be made without economic substance and a complete documentary trail.Onshore RMB foreign exchange forward contracts with approved domestic banks for hedging currency risk arising from committed USD/CNY or HKD/CNY receipts and payments, subject to verified real trade exposure and no speculative open position.Onshore FX swaps between CNY and USD for short-term liquidity and rollover management of trade finance facilities, limited to actual cash-flow cycles and documented trade obligations.Cross-currency swaps between CNY and USD or HKD via domestic banks for long-term foreign-currency cash-flow matching, with SAFE approval where the tenor or notional exposure exceeds the bank's delegated authority.Offshore NDFs are not permissible for the mainland WFOE as a hedge booked on its own balance sheet; any NDF-like exposure must remain in offshore CNH markets through a licensed overseas affiliate and can never be recorded as a domestic bank facility of the WFOE.

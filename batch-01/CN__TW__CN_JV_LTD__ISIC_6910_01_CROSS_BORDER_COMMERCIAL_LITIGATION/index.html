Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is imposed on a limited company under the Taiwan Company Act; the government does not require an escrow account as a condition of company formation. Nevertheless, for entities using foreign investment, the remitted paid-in capital must pass through an escrowed bank account and be verified by a certified public accountant. For legal-services chambers, the Taiwan Bar Association imposes separate solvency and IOLTA trust accounting requirements, but no minimum capital deposit is fixed by law.

Funding & unlock path: Required: fire safety certification from the Taipei City Fire Department under the Fire Services Act, a structural occupancy approval from the building authority, and an occupational safety and health clearance from the Ministry of Labor for the office premises. These certificates must be posted at the registered office, renewed biennially, and kept in the statutory company records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not provide check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch classification election mechanisms. Corporate entities are inherently subject to entity-level taxation under the Income Tax Act, and no pass-through treatment is available for limited companies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory full VAT registration is triggered when average monthly taxable sales exceed NT$200,000 for goods and services. Foreign e-commerce service providers supplying digital or downloadable services to individuals in Taiwan must register for VAT when annual turnover from Taiwan sources exceeds NT$480,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, a zero rate applies to exported goods that physically leave Taiwan customs territory, and to services rendered to foreign enterprises or overseas persons where the service is actually used or consumed outside Taiwan. Cross-border commercial litigation and legal advisory services qualify when the client is a foreign entity, the work is performed for use in foreign proceedings or foreign commercial arbitration, and the benefit of the service occurs outside Taiwan. Offshore digital legal services supplied to non-resident customers and paid for from foreign currencies also qualify if the customer's place of establishment and place of use are wholly outside Taiwan.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration System output certified by Taiwan Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or International Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment from Taiwan
  • Zero-rated Uniform Invoice clearly marked as exported supply
  • Foreign exchange remittance/settlement certificate issued by an authorized foreign exchange bank in Taiwan
  • Original client agreement, statement of work, and communication records evidencing that the legal service was rendered for external consumption outside Taiwan

Registered VAT taxpayers must issue serialized Uniform Invoices for all taxable sales. Electronic Uniform Invoices (Cloud Invoices) must be transmitted to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Platform within 48 hours of issuance using the Turnkey network connection or application programming interface (API). There is no real-time fiscal diary stamping regime, but the 48-hour batch transmission rule, embedded inspection codes, and annual anti-fraud audit controls operate as the statutory fiscal clearance mechanism.

National Taxation Bureau audit procedures require full preservation of all export/zero-rating documents for a minimum of five years. If zero-rating is found to be unsupported, fraudulent, or failing to meet the foreign use and consumption test, the tax authority will claw back the difference between zero-rated and 5% VAT, impose arrears interest, and apply administrative penalties under the Business Tax Act ranging from one to five times the underpaid tax. Deliberate misrepresentation or fictitious export documentation can lead to criminal prosecution for tax evasion, seizure of assets, and disqualification from future zero-rating or expedited refund treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates full current-account convertibility for trade and service cross-border receipts/payments, while capital-account transactions remain subject to SAFE filing, approval, or designated-bank verification. For the Sino-foreign JV, lawful RMB profits and distributable dividends may be converted and remitted abroad after annual statutory audit, corporate income tax settlement and clearance, and confirmation through the capital account information system; no material convertibility hurdle exists beyond standard tax, SAFE, and bank validation of legitimate source and use.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE Trade Foreign Exchange Name List system, there is no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; the JV may retain export foreign exchange in a domestic designated FX settlement account. However, export proceeds must be repatriated to a licensed domestic bank within 180 days from the export declaration date under the prior verification regime, which remains the benchmark commercial expectation for timely receipt. All export proceeds are subject to total-amount trade verification and must be supported by customs declarations, invoices, contracts, and logistics documents; voluntary conversion to RMB is permitted at market rate.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) is the sole regulator for foreign exchange and cross-border payments; PBOC is the monetary authority and central clearing overseer (CNAPS/CIPS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary settlement rail. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not permitted to open resident RMB settlement accounts or execute RMB FX settlements on behalf of the JV without a licensed onshore bank partner and SAFE filing. They may only provide ancillary foreign-currency collection support if backed by a domestic designated bank and compliant with PBOC payment licensing; otherwise legal and regulatory blockers include the unapproved cross-border FX settlement, AML data sovereignty, and CNAPS/CIPS clearing incompatibility.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File and comprehensive transfer pricing report are mandatory when annual aggregate related-party transactions reach or exceed NT$200 million; a Master File is required for multinational groups with annual consolidated revenues above NT$30 billion.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan has no codified general Economic Substance Regulation; however, tax authorities apply the substance-over-form rule, Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act, and OECD BEPS principles. A cross-border commercial litigation JV must maintain a real Taiwan office, qualified legal staff, an IOLTA trust account, board meeting records, case management protocols, and decision-making authority; otherwise tax benefits may be denied and the entity recharacterized as a tax conduit or shell entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to three times the evaded tax plus late surcharges; criminal liability under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act for fraud-based tax evasion, punishable by imprisonment up to five years and/or a fine of up to NT$60,000 in addition to restitution.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or knowingly adopt false returns may have the corporate veil pierced for civil restitution; the National Tax Administration may impose travel bans, freeze personal bank accounts, and refer the case to the prosecutor for incarceration.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of revenue, disallowance of treaty or cross-strait tax benefits, retroactive transfer pricing adjustment, imposition of the 5% retained earnings surcharge on undistributed earnings, and possible forced deregistration of the JV.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for the underpaid tax and penalties when the tax authority concludes that the entity was used as an artificial arrangement; outstanding tax liabilities may trigger travel bans and civil execution against personal assets.
Anti-Money Laundering Violation for Litigation Chambers

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines from NT$500,000 to NT$5 million for compliance failures under the Money Laundering Control Act; willful facilitation of money laundering may subject the firm to imprisonment up to seven years and corporate fines up to NT$50 million.

Director personal liability: The managing director and compliance officer are personally liable under the AML regime, with asset freezes, travel bans, and judicial seizure of individual bank accounts; company directors may be deported or detained when the cross-border settlement involves sanctioned counterparties.
Withholding Tax / Dividend Repatriation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may levy a penalty equal to 20% of the under-withheld tax, or up to three times for intentional evasion; unpaid withholding tax is recovered from the JV together with interest at the postal savings annual rate.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives are deemed statutory withholding agents; the unpaid WHT, penalties, and interest can be collected from directors' personal assets, and a director who knowingly authorized a disguised dividend distribution may face criminal restraint and imprisonment.

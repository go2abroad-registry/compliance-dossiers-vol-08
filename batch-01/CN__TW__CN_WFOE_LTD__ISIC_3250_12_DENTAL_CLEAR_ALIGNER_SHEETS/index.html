Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic tax bureau portal National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, together with the export tax rebate module of the China International Trade Single Window

E-government filing portal

For the Chinese WFOE, the statutory Tax Identification Number is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code obtained at incorporation. The entity registers as a domestic resident taxpayer through the integrated business-tax data exchange: (1) incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation to obtain the Unified Social Credit Code and business license; (2) the local STA office establishes the taxpayer registration file; within 30 calendar days the WFOE must complete supplementary registration in the National Electronic Tax Bureau, submitting Articles of Association, board resolution, office lease, legal representative and financial officer identification, tax accountant appointment, bank account for tax payments, and foreign investor identification; (3) complete real-name authentication and designate the competent tax office; (4) elect VAT status as general or small-scale taxpayer and obtain e-invoicing quota; and (5) for export activity, complete the Customs export registration and export tax rebate filing. A foreign non-resident parent entity without a PRC establishment and with PRC-source income will instead receive a non-resident taxpayer file number via its withholding agent's electronic tax registration; the WFOE as payer withholds applicable income tax on distributed dividends, interest, or royalties at the statutory rate subject to an applicable double-tax treaty claim.

Available election forms

None for check-the-box; the PRC does not permit pass-through partnership-style election for a limited liability WFOE. Available statutory elections are the VAT taxpayer category election, under which a business with annual taxable turnover up to RMB 5,000,000 may elect small-scale taxpayer treatment or voluntarily register as a General VAT taxpayer, the corporate income tax annual prepayment method choice, and the treaty benefit application for withholding tax reduction on outbound dividends, interest, or royalties payable to the foreign shareholder. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every merchant conducting taxable business in the PRC must register for VAT from the date of business commencement; compulsory General VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. A manufacturer may elect General VAT taxpayer status below that threshold. For export sales of clear aligner sheets, the applicable VAT rate is 0%, with recovered input VAT refundable or carried forward under the export tax rebate mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero per cent VAT applies to tangible dental clear aligner sheets and goods that are physically exported from the PRC customs territory and delivered to an overseas or Taiwan territory buyer. The claim must be supported by a genuine cross-border sales contract, a verified customs export declaration, actual shipment under a bill of lading or air waybill, a properly issued export VAT invoice, and timely repatriation of export proceeds through licensed banks. Title and risk must pass to the non-PRC customer on stated Incoterms, and the goods must not be used or consumed in mainland China. Qualifying offshore digital services could be zero-rated only under the cross-border services relief framework, but the relevant commercial model here is goods-export zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form / export Bill of Entry) bearing customs release verification
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing departure from a PRC customs port to the Taiwan destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by the receiving bank, together with bank foreign exchange income declaration and settlement records
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list issued by the WFOE to the non-resident buyer
  • Signed export sales contract or purchase order showing Incoterms, currency, quantities, unit price, and payment terms
  • PRC VAT invoice, ordinarily an electronic invoice, transmitted through the Golden Tax System Phase IV and tied to the export shipment
  • Export tax rebate declaration and electronic acceptance record from the export rebate module of the National Electronic Tax Bureau
  • Customs release notice or export cargo clearance certificate returned by Customs
  • Bank receipt confirming export proceeds repatriation or cross-border RMB settlement through SAFE-supervised channels
  • Certificate of origin and product conformity certificate or medical device documentation required for customs clearance at the Taiwan import side

The PRC mandates the Golden Tax System Phase IV real-time invoice clearance architecture. The WFOE must issue all VAT invoices through the STA digital/e-invoicing platform using a registered tax control identity; invoice data is transmitted item-level to the national tax database, which applies anti-counterfeit digital stamping, validates buyer and seller VAT identifiers, and monitors repeated or cancelled invoice numbers. The full digital invoice, also known as the all-electronic invoice, is the statutory e-invoice mode for general taxpayers; export invoices must be reconcilable with customs export declaration data. Red-letter invoices are required for credit notes and cancellations. No separate commercial customisation of invoices is permitted beyond STA-approved electronic formats.

Export zero-rating claims are monitored by the STA through Golden Tax System Phase IV cross-referencing of invoice, customs, logistics, inventory, and bank foreign exchange data. Where any mandatory substantiation document is missing, inconsistent, backdated, or fraudulent, the claimed 0% rate is disallowed, input VAT refunds and any export tax rebate already paid are clawed back with statutory late-payment interest, and administrative fines of 50% to 500% of the tax amount may be imposed. Fraudulent export declarations, fictitious foreign buyers, forgery of bills of lading, or false FIRC or foreign exchange receipts attract criminal liability for export tax rebate fraud and VAT invoice fraud, together with Customs Law monetary penalties for false declarations and SAFE fines or suspension for un-repatriated or irregularly settled export proceeds. Audit and clawback exposure covers the statutory five-year document retention period, extended to ten years in suspected evasion or fraud, and may be applied invoice-by-invoice or to the whole annual export period.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with full current account convertibility for legitimate trade and service transactions, but retains strict capital account controls. A China-based Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) may freely convert foreign currency for current-account payments such as raw material imports, royalties, and service fees once genuine transaction documentation is presented. However, equity capital injections, shareholder loans, and external debt conversions fall under capital account supervision and require SAFE registration via a designated domestic custodian bank. Statutory profit remittance by a WFOE requires prior tax clearance, audited financial statements compliant with Chinese Accounting Standards, and evidence that the paid-in capital has been fully contributed and verified under the applicable Foreign Investment Negative List. No surrender requirement applies to export proceeds in the sense of mandatory conversion, but all export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into the domestic banking system within 180 days of export declaration; enterprises may retain such receipts in foreign currency settlement accounts up to SAFE-approved quotas or voluntarily convert them. Obstacles arise when the underlying capital chain involves ambiguous upstream beneficial ownership or when profit repatriation is initiated before annual audit closure, as banks will suspend the remit instruction until the compliance file is complete. For greenfield dental aligner manufacturing, reinvestment of retained earnings is easier than shareholder loan servicing, but every outward remittance must map precisely to a recorded SAFE transaction code and be backed by contract, invoice, customs records, or tax vouchers. This dual-layer regime effectively prohibits unregulated fintech bypass and requires all material FX flows to settle through licensed domestic banks with direct interbank market access.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency proceeds from goods trade must be repatriated into China within 180 days of the date of export declaration as recorded by customs. The statutory framework does not currently impose a fixed mandatory conversion ratio; an exporter may retain the proceeds in a foreign currency settlement account within its SAFE-approved quota, use them directly for current-account payments, or surrender them to a domestic licensed bank for RMB conversion at the prevailing market rate. For printed dental clear aligner sheets classified under HS 3920 / ISIC 3250, the same repatriation timeline and voluntary conversion regime apply, but the exporter must preserve shipping bills, customs declarations, and prior export records to support the authenticity of the trade flow during bank post-verification.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or other non-bank payment institutions are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to open onshore RMB accounts, receive capital injections, or settle export proceeds in China. Although they may be used for overseas non-RMB receivables collection, they cannot legally service the WFOE's statutory capital conversion, China customs linkage, or tax-verified outbound trade payments. The viable architecture is to use a licensed domestic tier-1 bank as the settlement spine, while an offshore EMI may only serve as a supplementary collection service basket outside China before funds are swept into the onshore bank via SWIFT MT103 wires with proper purpose codes. Any arrangement involving direct sheltering of WFOE funds on an unauthorised fintech platform will cause immediate SAFE post-verification failure and may suspend the entity's foreign exchange quota.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The parent company must complete foreign direct investment registration with SAFE through the designated onshore bank; upon approval, the bank issues an FDI registration code and opens a capital injection escrow account. The overseas remitter must send an SWIFT MT103 wire referencing the WFOE name, the FDI registration code, and SAFE purpose code 621161 for onshore equity investment, with the narration clause excluding any reference to loans or trade settlement. After receipt, the domestic bank performs capital verification and submittal into SAFE's Capital Project Information System, after which the funds are converted to RMB under the investor's discretion; no conversion is permitted before the tax anti-avoidance screening and negative-list compliance check are completed.

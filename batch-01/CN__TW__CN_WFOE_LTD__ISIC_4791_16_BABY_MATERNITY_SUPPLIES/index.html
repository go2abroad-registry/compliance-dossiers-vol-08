Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA), Commercial Development Administration, Companies Registration Division; electronic filing is conducted through the MOEA Company and Business One-Stop Online Registration System.

Governing statute

Republic of China (Taiwan) Company Act, Articles 129-132, 154, 161, 172, 387 and 393; Statute for Investment by Foreign Nationals, Articles 4 and 7; Criterion Governing the Approval of Investment by Mainland Area Investors (where ultimate parent is a mainland China entity); Regulation on Company Registration Fees; Commodity Inspection Act, Article 6; Building Act, Article 73; Fire Services Act, Articles 10-13; Occupational Safety and Health Act, Articles 5-6.

Formation / constitutional filings

Company Incorporation Registration Application (Company Incorporation Registration Application Form) together with the attested Articles of Incorporation, supporting capital verification documents, foreign-investment approval letter, and corporate seal card.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Taiwan company limited by shares under the Company Act; however, the foreign investor must obtain prior approval from the MOEA Investment Commission and remit the full approved amount into a New Taiwan Dollar deposit account held in the company's name at a licensed Taiwan bank. The remittance must be verified by a Taiwan-certified public accountant in a capital verification report before incorporation registration. If the ultimate investor is a mainland China entity, the investment must additionally satisfy the positive-list review and quota rules under the Act Governing Relations between Peoples of the Taiwan Area and the Mainland Area and its approval criterion.

Funding & unlock path: The office and warehouse must pass a fire-safety inspection under the Fire Services Act, including installation of fire extinguishers, emergency lighting, and exit signage, and the annual submission of the fire-safety equipment inspection report. The building must maintain a lawful usage license under the Building Act consistent with the declared 'office/warehouse' use. Under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, the employer must conduct a workplace physical-hazard assessment, appoint a labor-safety and health management officer when headcount reaches 31 or more, maintain first-aid and emergency-response arrangements, and keep the labor inspection records available for municipal inspection.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Taiwan company limited by shares under the Company Act; however, the foreign investor must obtain prior approval from the MOEA Investment Commission and remit the full approved amount into a New Taiwan Dollar deposit account held in the company's name at a licensed Taiwan bank. The remittance must be verified by a Taiwan-certified public accountant in a capital verification report before incorporation registration. If the ultimate investor is a mainland China entity, the investment must additionally satisfy the positive-list review and quota rules under the Act Governing Relations between Peoples of the Taiwan Area and the Mainland Area and its approval criterion.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (国家税务总局). Official portal: https://www.chinatax.gov.cn. Electronic tax filing is administered through the provincial Electronic Tax Administration (电子税务局) portals and the China Tax Service application.

E-government filing portal

After the WFOE obtains its business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), it must complete tax registration within 30 days at the competent local tax bureau of the State Taxation Administration. Required submissions include the business license, articles of association, legal representative identity certificate, registered address proof, bank account opening certificate, and shareholder information. The tax authority issues a Unified Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码), which serves as the sole taxpayer identification number for corporate income tax, VAT, stamp duty, and payroll withholding. The enterprise must separately register VAT taxpayer status (small-scale or general taxpayer), export tax rebate eligibility, and payroll/social insurance withholding registrations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — After the WFOE obtains its business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), it must complete tax registration within 30 days at the competent local tax bureau of the State Taxation Administration. Required submissions include the business license, articles of association, legal representative identity certificate, registered address proof, bank account opening certificate, and shareholder information. The tax authority issues a Unified Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码), which serves as the sole taxpayer identification number for corporate income tax, VAT, stamp duty, and payroll withholding. The enterprise must separately register VAT taxpayer status (small-scale or general taxpayer), export tax rebate eligibility, and payroll/social insurance withholding registrations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not permit check-the-box entity classification or hybrid entity elections for a WFOE; a WFOE is mandatorily a Chinese tax resident subject to enterprise income tax on worldwide income. Available elections affecting tax characterization include: (i) election between small-scale VAT taxpayer and general VAT taxpayer status upon exceeding or approaching the RMB 5 million annual turnover threshold; (ii) election of preferential enterprise income tax treatment applicable to small low-profit enterprises, high-tech enterprises, and key software enterprises; (iii) election to use the cross-border e-commerce retail export customs modes 9610/9710/9810 and the corresponding tax exemption/refund mechanism; and (iv) election of the foreign tax credit method under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules apply mandatorily; there is no elective hybrid regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NT$480,000 per year for foreign sellers providing electronic services to Taiwanese consumers. For a foreign seller of physical goods with no fixed place of business, branch, or warehouse in Taiwan, no Taiwan Business Tax registration is required; Taiwan Customs collects import Business Tax at the 5% rate on each physical consignment at clearance, including low-value express parcels under the official express clearance regime. If the WFOE establishes any fixed place of business, branch, or fulfillment warehouse in Taiwan, Business Tax registration is compulsory from the first day of such operation without any turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Chinese VAT law, Taiwan is a separate customs territory for export purposes. The WFOE may claim the zero-rated export VAT treatment and input VAT refund (export tax rebate) on baby and maternity supplies exported to Taiwan provided that: (i) the WFOE is registered as a VAT general taxpayer and has obtained export tax rebate qualification; (ii) the goods are physically exported from China under an approved customs export declaration code (general trade or cross-border e-commerce retail export mode 9610/9710/9810); (iii) the goods are sold to a Taiwan buyer and actually leave China; (iv) export sales proceeds are collected in China through a licensed bank or designated payment institution, including settled funds originating from Stripe or PayPal; and (v) complete input VAT invoices and supporting payment documents are retained. The cross-border e-commerce retail export mode may instead use the 'exempt and not refund' (免征不退) mechanism when input VAT invoices are incomplete, meaning no VAT is charged at export but no input credit or refund is available.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (出口貨物報關單) with approved export record
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment from China and arrival in Taiwan
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign exchange settlement certificate (银行结汇水单) or equivalent digital payment settlement statement for Stripe/PayPal collections
  • Commercial export invoice and packing list
  • VAT Special Invoices (增值稅專用發票) and input VAT payment vouchers supporting the refund calculation
  • Export tax rebate declaration form and e-tax filing receipt
  • Purchase order, sales contract, or e-commerce order log identifying Taiwan consumers
  • Cross-border e-commerce retail export declaration list and transaction manifests when using 9610/9710/9810 mode

Taiwan registered businesses must issue Uniform Invoices (電子統一發票 or paper Uniform Invoice) for all taxable sales and upload electronic invoice data to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integration Service Platform within 48 hours. For cross-border goods importation, Taiwan Customs requires electronic advance manifest filing and real-time express clearance data transmission by couriers and customs brokers. A foreign seller with no Taiwan fixed place of business is not subject to Taiwan e-invoicing for direct consumer shipments; if the seller gains Taiwan nexus through a branch or warehouse, local sales become subject to e-invoicing, quarterly VAT filing, and full fiscal document retention.

The Chinese tax authorities conduct post-refund audits of export declarations, logistics trails, and foreign exchange repatriation. If an export zero-rating claim is found to be fictitious, mispriced, or non-repatriated within the statutory period, the exporter must repay the full VAT refund plus interest. Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law, fraudulent export refunds attract a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax and retrospective clawback across the preceding five years. Serious violations are prosecuted under Article 204 of the Criminal Law for export tax refund fraud, with penalties including substantial criminal fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment. Repeated or large-scale fraud additionally disqualifies the exporter from simplified cross-border e-commerce export refund procedures and triggers enhanced scrutiny of all related-party transactions.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends distributed by the PRC resident WFOE to its Taiwan-resident corporate shareholder (PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Art. 3(3) and Implementation Regulation Art. 91); the WFOE acts as statutory withholding agent and must remit the tax within 7 days of the distribution resolution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 15 calendar days after each quarter-end: April 15, July 15, October 15, and January 15 of the following year; computed on year-to-date accounting profit under PRC GAAP.

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before May 31 of the year following the calendar tax year (within 5 months after the December 31 fiscal year-end).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filed together with the annual CIT return no later than May 31; no extension is available for this package.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when stated related-party transactions in a single fiscal year exceed RMB 100 million for tangible goods, RMB 40 million for intangible property transfers or other transactions, and RMB 20 million for financial asset transfers; Master File is required when prior-year related-party revenue reaches RMB 2 billion; Country-by-Country Report is required when the consolidated group revenue reaches RMB 5.5 billion (SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42).

Economic substance scope: PRC law has no GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substantive economic presence is enforced through: (1) the beneficial-ownership test in SAT Public Notice 2018 No. 9, which denies treaty withholding relief to letterbox or conduit companies lacking personnel, premises, decision-making functions, and risk absorption; (2) the general anti-avoidance rule under EIT Law Art. 47 permitting recharacterization of arrangements without commercial substance; and (3) the transfer-pricing function, asset, and risk analysis under BEPS 13 requiring that China-located profits correspond to China-located employees and activities. The WFOE must maintain an actual office, operating staff, and board decisions in China to support its profit allocation and treaty claims.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: TCAL Art. 63 penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax plus a 0.05% daily surcharge; criminal liability under Criminal Law Art. 201 where the evaded amount exceeds RMB 50,000 and is more than 10% of the tax payable, with imprisonment up to 7 years and fines; SAT blacklisting and coordinated credit and exit restrictions.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, financial officer, and de facto controller face joint civil restitution of the tax shortfall, freezing of personal assets, exit bans, and criminal detention; the corporate veil is pierced where shareholder and company funds are commingled.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization under the GAAR (EIT Law Art. 47), supplementary withholding tax to 10%, interest on under-withheld amounts, and a penalty up to 500% of the underpaid tax where the abuse is deliberate; public disclosure as a tax-abuse case.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false beneficial-ownership declarations may be personally assessed for the recovered tax and face criminal exposure for aiding tax evasion; travel bans and asset preservation orders apply to the controlling shareholders.
Fraudulent VAT Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Art. 205 imposes imprisonment from 3 years to life and confiscation of property for false issuance of special VAT invoices; the tax authority imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax involved, revokes VAT qualifications, and publishes the violation.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, financial controller, and the staff member who arranged the false invoices are jointly and severally liable for restitution and face primary criminal prosecution; exit bans and mutual legal assistance exposure apply to fugitive directors.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Special tax adjustment reallocating income, deemed interest at the PBOC benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points on the underpaid tax, back-assessment over 10 years, and a penalty up to 500% of the underpaid tax where documentation is absent or false.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed inaccurate transfer-pricing returns or stripped the WFOE of assets before assessment can be personally liable for the recovered taxes; no statutory cap shields directors in fraud cases.
AML and Foreign Exchange Violation

Statutory sanction: Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations Art. 39 impose a penalty of up to 30% of the illegal foreign exchange amount plus confiscation of illegal gains; criminal money laundering under Criminal Law Art. 191 carries up to 10 years of imprisonment and fines where funds are disguised or layered.

Director personal liability: Controlling shareholders and finance directors who instructed the under-reporting face personal travel bans, asset freezes, director disqualification, and criminal detention; cross-border asset recovery is pursued through mutual legal assistance channels.

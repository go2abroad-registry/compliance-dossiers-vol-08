Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, R.O.C. (Taiwan) — 財政部; administered through local National Taxation Bureaus and the online e-tax portal (https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw).

E-government filing portal

For a wholly foreign-owned Taiwan subsidiary: (1) file company incorporation with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) One-Stop Business Registration; (2) the MOEA forwards the registration to the local National Taxation Bureau; (3) the Bureau automatically assigns a Taiwan Unified Business Number (統一編號/tax ID); (4) the WFOE completes business tax registration and enrolls in the MOF E-Invoice Platform; (5) a foreign parent making direct cross-border e-commerce sales to Taiwan individuals must separately apply for cross-border e-commerce tax registration at the tax portal once annual Taiwan-source sales exceed NT$480,000.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a wholly foreign-owned Taiwan subsidiary: (1) file company incorporation with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) One-Stop Business Registration; (2) the MOEA forwards the registration to the local National Taxation Bureau; (3) the Bureau automatically assigns a Taiwan Unified Business Number (統一編號/tax ID); (4) the WFOE completes business tax registration and enrolls in the MOF E-Invoice Platform; (5) a foreign parent making direct cross-border e-commerce sales to Taiwan individuals must separately apply for cross-border e-commerce tax registration at the tax portal once annual Taiwan-source sales exceed NT$480,000.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections are available under Taiwan tax law; a Taiwan WFOE is mandatorily taxed at entity level. The only relevant elective mechanisms are the small-scale business operator deemed-tax regime (for very low turnover) and the simplified reverse-charge/self-assessment election for non-resident cross-border e-commerce suppliers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Resident businesses: mandatory tax registration with no turnover exemption before commencing business. Non-resident cross-border e-commerce suppliers of electronic services: NT$480,000 annual sales threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) goods actually exported from Taiwan, evidenced by customs export declarations and shipping documents, and (ii) services performed in Taiwan for overseas customers that are used outside Taiwan, with evidence of foreign-currency receipt or equivalent substantiation. The WFOE must maintain complete export order records, customs clearance evidence, shipping documents, and foreign-currency receipt evidence to claim the 0% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口報單)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank receipt
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List

Taiwan mandates Uniform Invoices. Electronic invoices must be transmitted to the MOF E-Invoice Platform (電子發票整合服務平台) within the prescribed timeframe, generally by the bi-monthly VAT filing deadline; no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is required. API-based electronic invoicing through certified service providers is mandatory for high-volume WFOE operations.

The National Taxation Bureau conducts document-by-document review of zero-rating claims. Failure to substantiate the export within the statutory time frame results in retroactive assessment of the standard 5% business tax, plus interest and a penalty of up to three times the underpaid tax under Business Tax Act Article 51. Fraudulent claims may attract criminal tax evasion charges, and improperly received refunds are clawed back for the full statutory assessment period of five years, extending to seven years for willful evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a partial-convertibility framework. Current-account items such as export receipts, ordinary trade settlement, transportation, royalties, and after-tax profit or dividend remittances are generally convertible after documentary genuineness review and reporting. Capital-account items, including FDI equity injections, shareholder loans, liquidation proceeds, and cross-border guarantees, remain subject to SAFE registration, designated capital/foreign-debt account controls, and applicable quotas or approval conditions. Profit remittance hurdles for the WFOE include audited annual financial statements, corporate income tax settlement clearance, board resolution declaring the dividend, verification that registered capital has been duly contributed, and confirmation that all SAFE reporting and foreign-currency account reconciliation are current. The RMB is convertible for bona fide trade and investment-backed operating needs, but not for speculative or unauthorized cross-border capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE goods-trade administration, export proceeds must be repatriated into the WFOE's domestic foreign-currency current-account or an SAFE-approved offshore account. The statutory matching timeline follows the earlier of the contractual settlement maturity or the customs export declaration period, generally not exceeding 90 days after exportation, with advance export receipts requiring actual export within 90 days unless an extension is filed and approved. There is no mandatory fixed-percentage conversion of export receipts into CNY; exporters may retain foreign currency in approved deposit balances. However, every collection must be reconciled to an electronic customs declaration, commercial invoice, packing list, and transport document via SAFE's Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Unreconciled or overdue export receipts can trigger enterprise status downgrading from Class A to Class B/C and result in blocked settlements.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with delegated foreign-exchange administration and cross-border payment monitoring performed by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary settlement channel, but Low as a primary legal-grade banking venue. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, or neobanks generally cannot hold RMB demand deposits or issue local Chinese account numbers to the WFOE without a PRC payment institution license. They can, however, be used through licensed payment partners for inbound collection of small-value crowdfunding proceeds from Kickstarter, Stripe, or PayPal, and for outbound payments to non-Chinese suppliers, provided the underlying flows are fully reported to SAFE. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity injections must be layered through SAFE's FDI framework. Step 1: complete the FDI foreign-exchange registration for the WFOE at the designated domestic bank and obtain the FDI verification reference. Step 2: open a Capital Contribution Foreign-Currency or RMB Special Capital Account exclusively for the WFOE; no equity funds may be paid into an ordinary operating account without the capital-account code. Step 3: instruct the Taiwan investor to send a standard SWIFT MT103 from a licensed Taiwan bank, with beneficiary 'CN_WFOE_LTD', beneficiary bank equal to the WFOE's onshore branch, a purpose string such as 'FDI paid-in capital - equity injection under the Articles of Association', and the remitter reference containing the SAFE FDI registration number and the WFOE's unified social credit code. Step 4: upon receipt, the onshore bank verifies the SAFE registration, account title, purpose string, amount, and beneficiary identity; converts or retains FX as instructed; records the transaction in the cross-border BOP direct-reporting system; and releases funds only after the full compliance file is verified. Verification documents include the Foreign Investment Filing Certificate, board resolution approving the capital schedule, articles of association, passport or registration certificate of the investor, bank reference letters, and audited parent financial statements.Outbound trade settlements are executed from the WFOE's current account through three primary clearing rails. First, SWIFT MT103 or MT202 COVER for high-value direct remittances to overseas raw-material suppliers, with the SWIFT purpose field referencing the underlying import contract and the beneficiary identical to the payee on the invoice and transport documents. Second, irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit opened by the WFOE's PRC bank in favor of foreign suppliers, governed by UCP600, with settlement against presentation of commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and certificate of origin. Third, CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System) RMB direct settlement where the supplier accepts CNY, which removes SWIFT intermediation and accelerates same-day settlement. All outbound disbursements are reported to SAFE through the BOP direct-reporting system and matched with import trade declarations, customs records, and FX purchase/sale contracts before the bank releases funds.Onshore deliverable CNY FX forwards against USD, EUR, JPY, and HKD, supported by verified real trade receivables or payablesFX swaps between CNY and convertible foreign currencies for covering trade-working-capital maturity mismatchesCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS) for long-term foreign-currency funding related to the WFOE's capital structure or asset investmentPlain vanilla FX options on a current-account or investment-background underlying, requiring SAFE-qualified derivatives documentation and bank approval recordsOffshore non-deliverable CNY forwards (NDFs) executed through the Taiwan parent or an offshore treasury center solely as a hedge against the consolidated USD/TWD exposure, with no onshore delivery of CNY to non-resident accounts absent SAFE approval

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$50 million

Economic substance scope: Taiwan applies a robust economic-substance principle through Article 12-1 of the Taxpayer Rights Protection Act and the general anti-avoidance rules in Articles 43-3 and 43-4 of the Income Tax Act. A Taiwanese WFOE will be recognized as the true profit earner only where it has business premises, employees, actual decision-making authority, and assumes meaningful operational risks. Pure paper entities without substantive activity may be disregarded, and their income reattributed to the actual principal, exposing the enterprise to reassessment and severe penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Intentional Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Income Tax Act, a false return attracts an additional tax penalty of up to 2.5 times the underpaid tax; daily interest accrues from the original due date; criminal tax-fraud prosecution under the Tax Evasion and Tax Investigation Act may result in prison of up to five years and fines up to NT$6 million.

Director personal liability: Through veil-piercing principles, directors can be ordered to make restitution of the underpaid tax plus interest; tax authorities may apply travel bans and exit controls under the Tax Collection Act; responsible officers exposing to imprisonment and personal asset seizure when the evasion is habitual or organized.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority can disregard the legal entity and reallocate the taxable income directly to the beneficial owner, levying 20% corporate income tax, 21% dividend WHT, and an understatement penalty under Article 110, together with interest; sham arrangements are also subject to a civil fine of NT$15,000 to NT$300,000 under the Taxpayer Rights Protection Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintain or sign incomplete substance records may be jointly liable for the reassessed tax deficiency; personal assets may be attached if the sham structure caused irretrievable tax loss, and the director may be barred from leaving Taiwan until the deficiency is settled.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Under Taiwan's Transfer Pricing Rules and Article 43-2 of the Income Tax Act, the tax authority may make an upward adjustment to Taiwan taxable income, levy the resultant 20% corporate income tax with a shortfall penalty up to 2.5 times the deficiency, and impose civil interest from the tax's original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing documents or direct transactions without legitimate commercial rationale may be held personally accountable for the adjusted deficiency if the corporate form is found fraudulent, and could face criminal liability for document forgery under the Criminal Code, carrying imprisonment of up to five years.
AML / Trade Sanctions / Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Violations of Taiwan's Money Laundering Control Act and the Weapons of Mass Destruction Anti-Proliferation Act subject the enterprise to criminal fines of up to NT$100 million and imprisonment of up to seven years; the tax authority may also deny expense deductions for unlawful payments and impose a 10% disgorgement surcharge on the affected income.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who execute or authorize such transactions risk criminal prosecution, personal forfeiture orders, entry bans, and, in cross-border cases, issuance of international arrest warrants through mutual legal-assistance channels.

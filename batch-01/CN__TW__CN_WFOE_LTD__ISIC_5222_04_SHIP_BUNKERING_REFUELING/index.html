A virtual office, commercial mailbox, or agent-only address is not permissible for a PRC WFOE engaged in marine fuel bunkering. SAMR requires a substantive physical registered address in the registration locality; the State Taxation Administration requires an actual place of business for invoice control, tax registration, and on-site inspections; and commercial banks will reject a virtual office for corporate account opening because PBOC customer due diligence rules demand physical verification of business premises. A serviced office with a genuine lease, on-site staff, and record access may be accepted, but a virtual office product per se is not.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Taiwan limited company under the Company Act. However, the Investment Commission of MOEA, when approving a 100% foreign-owned company, will assess capital adequacy and requires the subscribed capital to be remitted and deposited in a blocked Taiwan bank escrow account designated for the company before registration. The escrow funds can be released only after the Company Registration Certificate is issued and the capital verification report by a Taiwan certified public accountant is filed with the registry.

Funding & unlock path: Before operation, the facility must obtain a Fire Safety Certificate and Civil Defense Dangerous Goods Storage Permit from the local Fire Bureau; pass structural load and seismic inspections of tanks, bunker piers and pipe supports conducted by a Taiwan laboratory accredited engineering firm; and secure the Occupational Safety and Health Inspection Office clearance under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, covering evacuation drills, fire alarm and gas detection, personal protective equipment, hot-work permits, confined-space entry procedures, storage tank gas-freeing, and a statutory emergency response plan approved by the port fire brigade.

E-government filing portal

The CN_WFOE_LTD foreign-invested limited liability company is first incorporated under the Taiwan Company Act. The foreign investor applies for investment approval under the Statute for Investment by Foreign Nationals with the Investment Commission, Ministry of Economic Affairs, unless an exemption applies. Company registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs Central Registration Office triggers the issuance of the statutory eight-digit Unified Business Number (UBN), which is the tax identification number (TIN) used for all Taiwan tax filings. Before commencing business, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the local National Taxation Bureau, register as a value-added business tax taxpayer, register its uniform-invoice scheme, and register as a withholding agent for employee and non-resident payments. Standard VAT / GST rate

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NTD 80,000 average monthly taxable sales for goods and NTD 40,000 for services; mandatory VAT registration is required above these thresholds, and businesses with average monthly sales exceeding NTD 200,000 must additionally adopt the uniform-invoice issuance system.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered Taiwan VAT business may claim 0% zero rating for exported goods, services directly related to exports, and goods or services supplied to international transportation. For marine bunkering and lubricant replenishment, zero rating is available where the fuel or lubricant is delivered under Customs supervision to an outbound ocean-going vessel engaged in international trade, the vessel departs Taiwan for a foreign port, the supply is evidenced by a Customs export declaration and bunker delivery documentation, and the purchase consideration is received in foreign exchange from a non-Taiwan buyer or the vessel operator. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied to foreign enterprises are capable of zero rating when they are exported, used outside Taiwan, and supported by foreign-currency remittance evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) confirming bunker fuel/lubricant quantity and date of export release
  • Bill of Lading, sea waybill, or port/Customs departure clearance proving the vessel's outbound international voyage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by an authorized Taiwan bank evidencing receipt of foreign-currency payment from the non-resident shipowner, charterer, or buyer
  • Bunker delivery note or fuel supply report with IMO vessel identification, grade, quantity, and supplier details
  • Zero-rated Uniform Invoice issued and declared in accordance with the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act

Taiwan mandates Uniform Invoices for registered business tax suppliers. Under the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice scheme, qualifying suppliers must transmit e-invoice data to the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice platform through the government Turnkey API service; each e-invoice is assigned a unique invoice number and random anti-fraud code by the platform. The platform performs automated cross-validation between invoice issuance, VAT monthly return filing, and payment records; there is no physical fiscal stamping requirement, but API-based transmission, retention, and periodic declaration are mandatory for participating e-invoice issuers.

The National Taxation Bureau will audit the end-to-end link among the Customs export declaration, bill of lading/voyage evidence, bunker delivery note, zero-rated invoice, and the inward foreign exchange record. Missing or inconsistent export substantiation causes the zero rate to be reversed and the tax authority will assess the underpaid VAT at 5%, add overdue interest, impose a surcharge of up to five times the tax evaded under the Business Tax Act, and may claw back any refunded or claimed input VAT. False or manipulated export documentation can additionally attract Customs penalties and criminal tax-evasion prosecution under the Tax Collection Act, with retrospective reassessment extending to the statutory review period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a strictly managed floating exchange-rate regime. The current account has been liberalized to permit RMB convertibility for bona fide trade and service transactions; a WFOE may freely convert and remit current-account proceeds for imported bunker fuel, port fees, crew costs and marine lubricant replenishment once supporting trade documents and tax certificates are filed with the handling bank. The capital account, however, remains closed: equity capital injections, debt issuance, cross-border loans and repatriation of registered capital require separate SAFE registration, a qualified local custodian structure and often post-payment verification. Profit remittances are permitted only after statutory annual audit and with proof of paid corporate income tax; the distribution itself must be routed through the SAFE-registered capital account or RMB settlement account, and the bank will check the audited financial statements, board resolution, and shareholder approval. In practice, the WFOE must keep an audited Chinese currency balance sheet, file the SAFE annual FDI report, and obtain a capital account payment confirmation before wiring dividends to Taiwan. Certainty of settlement is lower for TWD than USD or RMB, but RMB/USD current-account payments are generally executable at the commercial bank counter.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration, export proceeds from marine fuel sales to international vessels must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange current-account account within 180 days of the customs export declaration date. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero: no stipulated percentage must be sold to the banking system; the enterprise may retain the proceeds in foreign exchange or convert them to RMB at its own election. All receipts must be reported in the SAFE Total Amount Verification system, and the receiving bank must cross-check the declared export value, commodity code, vessel identification data and bunkering evidence against the sales contract.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the onshore CN WFOE; the Taiwan-based investing entity is also subject to the central monetary authority of the Taiwan jurisdiction (commonly cited as the CBC) under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act for outward remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs are not legally viable for the onshore WFOE. PBOC Payment Management Regulations require any entity providing payment or clearing services to a Chinese legal entity to hold a PRC payment institution license; a foreign EMI cannot open a basic deposit account or foreign exchange account for the WFOE, cannot hold segregated client funds for the WFOE, and cannot register as the executing bank for SAFE capital or current-account records. An overseas EMI may only invoice the Taiwan parent offshore, but if used to route WFOE receipts or capital it will fail SAFE receipt and declaration bank-of-record compliance and trigger frozen funds, penalties and possible licence revocation. Viability rating: Low.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan parent must first obtain CBC outward remittance approval or blanket approval, if applicable, and remit through a Taiwan clearing bank by SWIFT MT103. The Chinese WFOE must complete FDI registration with SAFE through the receiving bank and obtain the FDI business registration confirmation before inbound settlement. The MT103 must show the purpose narrative as equity injection to the WFOE, with the SAFE FDI registration number and the correct BOP transaction code from the SAFE Cross-Border Receipts and Payments Classification; the handling bank will map the exact code at account creation. The inward USD or RMB is credited to the WFOE FDI capital account and can be settled only under the payment-after-verification regime after documentation is uploaded to the SAFE or bank system. Capital verification is evidenced by a PRC certified public accountant capital verification report addressed to the company, confirming paid-in registered capital from the Taiwan remittance against the approved registered capital amount.The primary clearing rail for operating and inventory payments is SWIFT MT103 for direct wire disbursements to qualified suppliers, port agents, barge operators and tax authorities, supported by the underlying sales or purchase contracts, invoices, tax certificates and customs or port declarations. For high-value fuel procurement and marine lubricant replenishment, the WFOE should use a commercial letter of credit under MT700 or MT707, with a standby letter of credit under MT760 to secure performance guarantees or vessel supply contracts. Documentary collections under D/P and D/A terms may be used for established counterparties. RMB cross-strait payments should, where available, be cleared through CIPS rather than a plain SWIFT or CHIPS path; USD disbursements should clear through the US correspondent network via the receiving bank. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% single headline rate for profit-seeking enterprises, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2018

21% final withholding tax on dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders out of profits earned in fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2018 (20% statutory rate applies to prior-year profit pools); the Taiwan paying entity must withhold at source and file the withholding return within ten days of payment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transfer pricing documentation (Local File equivalent) is statutorily required for a Taiwan resident enterprise when, in a fiscal year, related-party transactions with a single related party exceed NT$20 million, or aggregate related-party transactions exceed NT$50 million (New Taiwan Dollars). Master File documentation is required only where the Taiwan enterprise is the ultimate parent or surrogate parent of a multinational group filing Country-by-Country reports; CbC reporting applies where the consolidated group revenue exceeds approximately NT$27 billion. Low-risk routine bunker-distribution functions may be exempted from preparing the full report if the taxpayer functions as a limited-risk distributor or terminal operator and satisfies the criteria of the Transfer Pricing Examination Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not maintain a stand-alone regulatory scheme called 'Economic Substance Regulations', but the Substantive Taxation Principle under the Taxpayer Rights Protection Act and the general anti-avoidance rules in the Income Tax Act and the Basic Income Tax Regime allow the tax authority to recharacterise artificial arrangements. Since tax year 2023, Taiwan's CFC rules and effective-management-place doctrine impose a substance requirement on foreign-incorporated entities controlled by Taiwan residents; a Taiwan filing entity performing marine bunkering must demonstrate permanent local substance: licensed storage and barge-custody arrangements, locally resident trading and operations staff, independent board decisions, a substantive bank account receiving invoiced proceeds, and real risk management over credit, fuel-price, and vessel-delivery risks. Absent such substance, the tax authority may reallocate the bunkering profit to the Chinese parent and disregard the Taiwan entity as a conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Intentional Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Deficiency assessment including the 20% income tax and the 5% retained earnings surtax; late-payment surcharge of 1% per two days (up to 15%); a fine of up to twice the evaded tax under Article 110 of the Income Tax Act; criminal prosecution for intentional evasion carries imprisonment of not more than two years and/or a fine of up to NT$150,000.

Director personal liability: The responsible director, CFO, or de facto manager who signed or caused the false filing is personally subject to criminal imprisonment; the tax authority may seize the director's personal assets and impose a restriction on leaving Taiwan (travel ban) where the entity's overdue tax exceeds the statutory threshold under the Tax Collection Act; civil restitution for damage to the corporate estate can be pursued under Article 23 of the Company Act.
Sham Structure and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under the substantive taxation principle; the tax authority will deny the Taiwan entity's income attribution and reallocate the entire margin to the Chinese parent under Article 43-1 (related-party reallocation); the Taiwan entity is assessed at 20% on deemed constructive profits plus 5% surtax on deemed undistributed earnings, followed by a transfer-pricing penalty and a fine of up to twice the underpaid tax; issuance of fabricated VAT invoices exposes the entity to suspension and criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who sign false board minutes, contracts, and invoices face personal criminal liability under the Commercial Accounting Act for falsifying invoices (imprisonment up to five years); the corporate veil may be pierced where the company is used to defraud the tax authority, exposing the beneficial Chinese owner to joint personal and civil liability; responsible persons are also subject to travel bans and asset freezes during the tax investigation.
AML / Sanctions / Payment-Rail Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Control Act, failure to perform CDD or to file an STR is punishable by a fine of NT$500,000 to NT$10,000,000; wilful facilitation of money laundering through the bunker-supply payment chain carries imprisonment of up to two years; the company may be barred from operating the bunkering licence and have its vessels and bunker storage subject to judicial seizure.

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers, the responsible director, and the CFO who knowingly approve sanctioned-vessel payments or conceals STR obligation are individually liable with fines and imprisonment; personal travel restrictions and asset freezes are applied by the prosecutor; a criminal conviction additionally carries disqualification from serving as a director for up to three years and potential civil liability for creditor losses.

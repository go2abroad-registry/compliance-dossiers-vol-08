Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital applies to a PRC WFOE engaged in electronic retail of fast-fashion apparel and footwear under ISIC 4791. The sole Taiwan shareholder must set a registered capital amount in the Articles of Association that is reasonably suited to the business scale. Under the 2024 PRC Company Law, the registered capital must be fully subscribed and paid in within the statutory maximum contribution period, generally five years from establishment, unless a shorter period is adopted in the Articles. No blocked escrow account is mandated; however, the subscribed capital must be remitted from outside mainland China into a designated capital contribution account at a Chinese bank with SAFE foreign-exchange qualification, and the actual paid-in capital must be subsequently reported in the SAFE FDI information system and the SAMR annual report.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no manufacturing or factory premises are involved, so no Civil Defense fire permit or structural load inspection is required. The leased office must nonetheless comply with the PRC Fire Protection Law; the landlord must hold a valid building fire-safety inspection certificate for the premises, and the WFOE should implement workplace fire evacuation procedures, emergency lighting, and occupational safety measures for its office and retail support staff.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), with the taxpayer's provincial e-Tax Bureau electronic portal accessible from the STA official website at www.chinatax.gov.cn. If a separate Taiwan taxable presence is established, the competent authority for that market is the Taxation Administration of the Ministry of Finance, Taiwan.

E-government filing portal

The China-incorporated WFOE obtains its 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the local Administration for Market Regulation in the province of incorporation; the USCC is the sole taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT identification number. The registration record is automatically transmitted to the STA under the integrated one-stop tax registration process, and no separate tax registration certificate is issued. The WFOE must complete post-registration formalities in the e-Tax Bureau within 30 calendar days, including bank account filing, accounting method reporting and VAT taxpayer status confirmation. A foreign non-resident entity without a Chinese establishment does not use the USCC route; its China-source income is reported through a withholding registration using the non-resident entity's identification data and the payer's tax profile.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise for Enterprise Income Tax and is therefore taxed at entity level on worldwide income; for indirect tax purposes it is a VAT taxpayer, initially classified as either a small-scale taxpayer or a general VAT taxpayer.

Available election forms

None for pass-through treatment; a PRC limited liability WFOE cannot elect transparent status. A small-scale VAT taxpayer may elect once to become a general VAT taxpayer if it satisfies the prescribed accounting and filing conditions. China does not provide a check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election for this entity type.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every China-incorporated company must complete tax registration within 30 days after incorporation; there is no turnover exemption from registration. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 within any consecutive 12-month period; below that threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported fast-fashion apparel sold by the WFOE to Taiwan or other foreign customers qualifies for VAT zero-rating when the goods are physically exported from China and cleared by China Customs in the name of the exporting WFOE. The WFOE must be a general VAT taxpayer to claim the input VAT refund; small-scale taxpayers receive simplified export exemption rather than a zero-rated refund.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods declaration) cleared by China Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export shipment
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract or purchase order
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt record evidencing repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days

China mandates invoice issuance through the STA Full Digital Electronic Invoice service platform and tax-controlled invoice equipment for general taxpayers. Invoice data is transmitted electronically to the STA for near real-time validation and cross-matching; each invoice carries a tax authority verification code and QR code for anti-fraud checks. Certified API integrations with approved accounting or enterprise resource planning systems are available for large-scale issuers, but China does not operate a pre-clearance fiscal stamping model.

The STA cross-checks export tax refund claims against customs export declarations, transport documentation, sales contracts and foreign-exchange collection records. If the zero-rating cannot be substantiated, the refund is reversed and late-payment interest is charged. Under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, deliberate underpayment or false claims may result in a fine of 50% to five times the tax involved; fraudulent export refund claims may constitute the criminal offence of fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law, with imprisonment and monetary penalties.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% flat profit-seeking enterprise (corporate) income tax rate under Article 5, Income Tax Act.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed to non-resident corporate shareholders without a fixed business establishment in Taiwan (Articles 88 and 89, Income Tax Act). For resident corporate shareholders, dividend income is included in taxable income with no additional WHT; a 5% retained earnings surtax is imposed on the Taiwan distributing company on undistributed surplus.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar-year enterprises, final annual return due 31 May of the following year. For fiscal-year enterprises, within five months after fiscal year-end (e.g., a 30 June year-end requires filing by 30 November).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Taiwan WFOE must prepare a Local File (transfer pricing report) when aggregate controlled (related-party) transactions exceed TWD 60 million in the fiscal year; a Master File is required when the ultimate parent's global consolidated revenue exceeds TWD 300 million and the Taiwan entity engages in cross-border related-party transactions. The Related-Party Transactions Disclosure (Schedule B-3) must be filed with the annual CIT return even if below the documentation threshold.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan has no standalone ESR but applies substance-over-form and GAAR provisions. To claim treaty benefits, the WFOE must have genuine operating substance: business premises, management and board decisions in Taiwan, employees, and a real commercial function in the e-commerce retail chain. A shell company with no substantial activities (no warehouse, no marketing team, no risk-bearing capacity) will have its intercompany arrangements disregarded; income will be recomputed under the arm's length principle; and dividends or royalties may be recharacterized as taxable distributions to the foreign shareholder, triggering 21% WHT and potential penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act Article 110, Tax Collection Act Article 41)

Statutory sanction: Fine of two to three times the evaded tax (determined by the audit bureau); where willful tax evasion is proven, the responsible person may be criminally fined and imprisoned for up to seven years under the Tax Collection Act; unpaid tax plus daily interest is collected through compulsory enforcement and travel bans.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false tax returns are personally liable for restitution of the evaded tax, statutory fines, and criminal penalties; legal representatives may be prohibited from leaving Taiwan and their personal properties can be seized for unpaid taxes.
Shell Company and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Taiwan tax authority may disregard the WFOE's legal personality under Article 12-1 (GAAR), recharacterize the revenue as attributable to the foreign shareholder, impose transfer pricing adjustment and re-characterize a deemed dividend, and assess a 21% WHT plus interest and penalties of 2–3 times the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors as 'responsible persons' face joint personal liability for the re-assessed tax and penalties; they can be subject to a travel ban, a hold-in-depth hearing, and, under extreme fraudulent circumstances, criminal fraud charges.
Transfer Pricing Paperwork Failure and Advanced Pricing Agreement Absence

Statutory sanction: The tax authority applies the best-method adjustment, disallows the intercompany expenses, and imputes the difference to taxable income; the taxpayer is then subject to an additional tax of 20% plus a penalty of up to 2 times the difference under Article 110; repeated non-compliance can lead to an audit penalty and publication of the taxpayer's name.

Director personal liability: Directors/shareholders who approve disregard of TP rules may be personally liable for the resulting underpaid taxes, and may lose their entitlement to administrative remedies; any attempt to falsely certify the transfer pricing report constitutes perjury in tax procedure and can trigger criminal consequences.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance on Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: Article 114 imposes a penalty of up to 1.5 times the unwithheld amount, plus the tax itself; if the company fails to withhold, the foreign payee remains liable for the tax together with a late-filing surcharge; the tax authority can stop the distribution by freezing the company's bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who knowingly cause the non-withholding are jointly liable with the company for the unpaid WHT and penalties; they may be held personally responsible in insolvency proceedings and face criminal sanctions under the Tax Collection Act.

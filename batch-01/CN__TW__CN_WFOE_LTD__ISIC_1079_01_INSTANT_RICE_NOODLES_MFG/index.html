Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the Taiwan Company Act for a limited company in this manufacturing category. However, a wholly foreign-owned manufacturing entity must maintain a dedicated paid-in capital escrow account with a licensed Taiwan bank. The subscribed capital must be remitted from the foreign shareholder by SWIFT or telegraphic transfer, deposited into the blocked escrow account, and certified by an independent Taiwan Certified Public Accountant. The escrowed funds may not be disbursed for operating purposes until the Department of Commercial Affairs issues the Company Registration Certificate and the unified business number. The registered capital must be reasonably proportionate to the projected factory investment, retort line cost, working capital, and ISIC 1079 food production capacity.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taiwan; administered operationally by the National Taxation Administration and its regional National Taxation Bureaus for business tax and profit-seeking enterprise income tax, and by the Customs Administration for export declaration validation; online portals: Ministry of Finance E-Tax Portal at https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw and Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform at https://www.einvoice.nat.gov.tw

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident investor establishing a Taiwan subsidiary must first obtain investment approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs. The company is then incorporated under the Taiwan Company Act with the Ministry of Economic Affairs commercial registration authority, which issues the Unified Business Number; the Unified Business Number is the statutory Tax Identification Number for all profit-seeking enterprise tax, business tax, and e-invoice filings. Concurrently, or within fifteen days before commencing operations, the entity must file business tax registration with the competent regional National Taxation Bureau under Article 28 of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act. A foreign non-resident enterprise that does not establish a Taiwan entity and has no taxable fixed place of business may obtain a non-resident income tax identification number through its Taiwan withholding agent's withholding tax registration at the same National Taxation Bureau. Supporting documents include the company registration certificate, investment approval letter, board resolution appointing the Taiwan legal representative, and attested proof of the foreign parent entity.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not provide a check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election; an incorporated limited company is mandatorily a resident profit-seeking enterprise taxed at the entity level. A taxpayer may apply for a special fiscal year other than the calendar year with prior approval of the tax authority, and may separately elect applicable investment incentives under industrial promotion statutes, but no entity classification election is available.

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero percent business tax rate may be claimed for goods exported from Taiwan, including processed packaged instant rice noodles, where the goods are actually exported under customs control and the sale is evidenced by an approved Customs export declaration. Zero-rating also covers services that are exported or used wholly outside Taiwan and international transport. The exporter must substantiate that legal ownership passed to the foreign buyer and the goods left Taiwan; the output VAT is zero and any input VAT attributable to export sales remains refundable under the business tax filing system.

  • Customs export declaration approved by the Customs Administration of Taiwan
  • Commercial invoice in foreign currency issued to the overseas buyer
  • Packing list
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing departure from Taiwan
  • Foreign exchange settlement certificate, bank inward remittance record, or inward remittance certificate issued by a Taiwan foreign exchange bank

General VAT taxpayers must issue statutory Uniform Invoices for every taxable sale. Electronic Uniform Invoices to B2B and B2C counterparties must be transmitted to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform; B2B exchanges use platform-to-platform data interchange and B2C invoices are filed by the seller. There is no continuous real-time fiscal clearance or government digital stamping mandate comparable to Latin American models, but the E-Invoice platform applies QR-code authentication, encrypted invoice numbers, and periodic reconciliation; VAT returns are filed bimonthly by the 15th day after each two-month filing period.

If zero-rating is claimed without the complete export declaration, shipping evidence, and foreign-exchange repatriation proof within the 180-day period, the National Taxation Bureau may reclassify the sale as domestic and levy 5 percent business tax on the proceeds, plus overdue interest. Under Article 51 of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act, a deficiency caused by inadequate documentation may attract an additional penalty of two to five times the tax evaded. Where forged shipping documents or fraudulent repatriation or invoice schemes are found, Article 41 of the same Act imposes criminal liability and additional fines. The tax authority may also deny input VAT refunds, retrospectively claw back refunds already paid, and examine prior periods within the statutory limitations period under the Tax Collection Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is not freely convertible for capital-account transactions. A Taiwan-owned Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise operating in mainland China must remit its paid-in capital as foreign direct investment through SAFE-registered capital account channels. Current-account convertibility, however, is substantially liberalized for genuine trade and services, allowing the WFOE to settle trade receipts and pay for imported raw materials, machinery, packaging, and royalties after satisfying bank-level documentation checks. The central regulatory risk lies in the capital account: every capital inflow, transfer, pledge, or foreign debt drawdown requires a filing or approval with SAFE, and the proceeds cannot be diverted to prohibited uses such as equity investments in unrelated entities, entrusted loans to affiliates outside the registered scope, or short-term intercompany financing unless separately authorized. Dividend and profit remittance to Taiwan are permitted after completion of the annual statutory audit, enterprise income tax final settlement, submission of a board resolution, audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and the Foreign Exchange Business Registration form; the remittance is then treated as a current-account payment if the underlying profits derived from actual operations and have been fully taxed. Export earnings are not subject to mandatory surrender, but banks apply real-business-background and macro-prudential scrutiny to sudden surges in cross-strait receipts, requiring the WFOE to evidence trade authenticity through contracts, customs declarations, invoices, and logistics documents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory conversion ratio currently applies: the effective surrender requirement for export receipts is zero, and the WFOE may retain export foreign currency in its domestic deposit account or sell it to a licensed bank at the market rate. The mandatory repatriation rule requires that export proceeds be brought into mainland China through a licensed bank or an approved special offshore account and be reconciled with customs declarations in the SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. The outer statutory surveillance timeline is 180 days from the export customs declaration date for most goods trade; if the proceeds remain uncollected beyond this horizon, the WFOE must file an explanation, provide supporting sales contracts and credit terms, and adjust the declaration where appropriate. Banks will classify overdue or unmatched export receipts as high-risk and may require settlement within 30 days of receipt to mitigate trade-based money laundering flags. The WFOE should instruct customers to quote the exact invoice number, PO number, and WFOE customs registration number in SWIFT remittances to enable straight-through verification.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for current-account and capital-account enforcement in mainland China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Very Low. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Airwallex, Currenxie, and Wise Business cannot act as the primary treasury clearing bank for a PRC WFOE. They are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in mainland China and cannot satisfy SAFE's requirement that cross-border trade in goods, FDI capital injections, and statutory capital verification flows pass through a domestic commercial bank with full customs and invoice documentation. EMI accounts could, at best, serve as peripheral overseas collection gateways for non-trade incidental expenses, but relying on them for the WFOE's core FX operations would break the SAFE registration chain, create untraceable fund flows, and expose the entity to penalties for evasion of foreign exchange controls. Therefore, domestic Tier-1 commercial banks are mandatory for the Stage 4 treasury blueprint; fintech EMIs should be rejected for statutory capital, customs-linked trade payments, dividend remittances, and FX hedging.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Very Low. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Airwallex, Currenxie, and Wise Business cannot act as the primary treasury clearing bank for a PRC WFOE. They are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in mainland China and cannot satisfy SAFE's requirement that cross-border trade in goods, FDI capital injections, and statutory capital verification flows pass through a domestic commercial bank with full customs and invoice documentation. EMI accounts could, at best, serve as peripheral overseas collection gateways for non-trade incidental expenses, but relying on them for the WFOE's core FX operations would break the SAFE registration chain, create untraceable fund flows, and expose the entity to penalties for evasion of foreign exchange controls. Therefore, domestic Tier-1 commercial banks are mandatory for the Stage 4 treasury blueprint; fintech EMIs should be rejected for statutory capital, customs-linked trade payments, dividend remittances, and FX hedging.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Tax-Exempt_Zone not applicable. The WFOE is a resident profit-seeking enterprise (營利事業) with head office in Taiwan and is subject to corporate income tax at 20% on Taiwan-source income and, where applicable, foreign-source income integrated with foreign tax credit; an additional retained-earnings tax of 5% applies to unappropriated surplus where profits are not distributed. The entity is not pass-through eligible; dividends remitted to the foreign parent are subject to non-resident dividend withholding at 21% absent an applicable treaty or cross-strait arrangement. No free-trade-zone income exemption is available for the onshore restaurant and franchise operations.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election or hybrid-entity election exists in Taiwan. Available elections include: (1) the Blue Return election (藍色申報書) with CPA-attested financial statements, preserving the 10-year net operating loss carryforward under Income Tax Act Article 39; (2) the deemed-profits election under Article 25 for qualifying international transport and technical services; (3) Business Innovation Act elections for R&D tax credits, qualified investment expensing, and IP disposal gains; (4) the OECD Pillar Two framework, effective in Taiwan from 2025 for groups exceeding EUR 750 million consolidated revenue, involving a Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax and Income Inclusion Rule that are mandatory rather than elective; (5) anti-hybrid provisions (Income Tax Act Articles 43-3 and 43-4) and CFC rules effective from tax year 2023 which override hybrid instrument outcomes; elections must be filed with the National Taxation Bureau prior to the annual return deadline and, once made, bind subsequent assessment years.

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every legal-entity profit-seeking enterprise, including this WFOE LLC, is mandatorily registered for business tax (稅籍登記) from commencement of business irrespective of turnover. The only statutory de minimis exemption in the Business Tax Act applies to natural-person small-scale operators with average monthly sales below NT$80,000 for goods and NT$40,000 for restaurant services; the threshold is not available to corporate taxpayers. The WFOE's restaurant and franchise operations, with expected monthly sales exceeding NT$200,000 per outlet, are fully liable for 5% VAT and must issue uniform invoices.

The WFOE is obliged to issue statutory Uniform Invoices (統一發票) for all B2B and B2C sales. E-invoice issuance is mandatory for newly registered taxpayers; invoices must be transmitted through MOF-certified software to the E-Invoice Government Integrated Platform within 48 hours for B2B transactions and within 7 calendar days for B2C transactions under the current E-Invoice Implementation Directions. The MOF is phasing in same-day real-time clearance (即時上傳): already required for newly registered entities since 2022 and scheduled for all e-invoice issuers by 2026. Transmission uses HTTPS web-service APIs or the TURNKEY gateway with two-factor certificate-based mutual authentication. There is no physical fiscal stamp; compliance is evidenced by the government-allotted invoice number series (發票字軌) embedded in the invoice QR code and the certified platform upload timestamp. Failure to transmit within the statutory window gives rise to per-invoice administrative fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains RMB convertibility for current-account transactions under IMF Article VIII; capital-account transactions remain subject to SAFE registration, verifiable documentation, and macro-prudential controls. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) may freely settle genuine current-account receipts (sales, royalties, service fees) into RMB through a licensed domestic bank. Profit distribution to the Taiwan shareholder is treated as a controlled current-account remittance: it is permissible only after (i) the annual financial statements are audited, (ii) the corporate income tax and any dividend withholding tax are settled, (iii) a shareholder resolution declares the dividend, and (iv) the bank completes SAFE authenticity review. No prior SAFE approval is required for each profit remittance, but the transaction must be recorded under SAFE's cross-border payment reporting system. The principal obstacles are: unfiled annual FDI information reports, undocumented related-party transactions, outstanding tax/Fines, breach of the capital account negative list, and failure to establish distributable profits. Equity capital injected into the WFOE is irrevocably classified as capital-account funds; principal cannot be repatriated to the Taiwan shareholder except through a liquidating resolution, capital reduction registration, or approved share transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China does not impose fixed mandatory surrender ratios for export proceeds. Exporters may retain 100% of qualifying trade receipts in a domestic current-account foreign-exchange account or convert them to RMB at the onshore market rate through a licensed bank. Under SAFE's Trade in Goods monitoring regime, export proceeds must be repatriated through an authorized banking channel within the contractual payment term; where collection occurs more than 90 days after the export customs declaration date, the WFOE must complete deferred-collection registration in SAFE's monitoring platform before settlement. Non-repatriation of export proceeds without an underlying cross-border credit/loan structure is not permissible and triggers SAFE post-event verification, possible penalties, and downgrading of the enterprise's compliance classification. The WFOE in the malatang self-serve franchise business should be classified as a 'B' enterprise under SAFE's trade-in-goods categorization, meaning standard documentary review applies to every foreign-exchange receipt and payment.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating as the PBOC-affiliated foreign-exchange regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs), such as Airwallex, DBS, or Revolut Business, cannot lawfully serve as the primary onshore account provider for the Chinese WFOE. They are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to conduct domestic RMB clearing, local POS settlement, VAT-linked receipt collection, or SAFE-reportable cross-border payments for a mainland-incorporated company. Their use is limited to supplementary offshore multi-currency treasury vehicles for the Taiwan parent entity, and even that ancillary use requires compliance with Taiwan's foreign-exchange and corporate banking regulations. All onshore operating cash flows, royalty-related remittances, and foreign-exchange transactions must pass through a PRC-licensed commercial bank; routing them through an offshore EMI without a domestic banking relationship would violate SAFE's requirement that cross-border receipts and payments be settled by authorized institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan parent should wire the registered FDI equity capital via SWIFT MT103 directly to the WFOE's dedicated SAFE-registered 'FDI foreign-exchange capital account' at the designated tier-1 Chinese bank. Prior to remittance, the WFOE must obtain the SAFE Foreign-Invested Enterprise Registration certificate (业务登记凭证) and open the capital account under the bank's direct registration mechanism. The wire instruction must clearly designate the transaction type as 'Foreign Direct Investment - Capital Contribution' and use the correct SAFE/BOP transaction nature code for FDI inbound capital formation. The bank will perform authenticity review, anti-money-laundering screening, and capital account income control checks. Upon receipt, the foreign-exchange funds may be settled into RMB through the bank, with proceeds credited to the WFOE's 'RMB capital settlement account' (结汇待支付账户), subject to the negative list. For RMB-denominated inbound investment, the Taiwan parent may channel the funds through Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) using RMB SWIFT-equivalent messaging; in all cases, the bank must record the transaction against the FDI registration and issue the corresponding cross-border receipts declaration. No unregistered inbound remittance may be accepted into an operational account for use as equity capital.Trade settlements for ingredient imports, self-service equipment, and the franchise royalty should run through the following clearing rails: (i) routine royalty and service-fee remittances to the Taiwan parent via SWIFT MT103 under the bank's service trade payment verification, supported by the royalty agreement, tax withholding certificates, and an invoice; (ii) large-scale cross-border procurement of kitchen equipment and bulk packaged ingredients via an at-sight commercial Letter of Credit issued by the mainland bank, with presentation through the Taiwan beneficiary's negotiating bank; (iii) domestic RMB supplier disbursements through CNAPS real-time gross settlement from the WFOE's RMB operational account; and (iv) cross-border RMB payments, where the Taiwan supplier accepts RMB, processed through CIPS with full tax documentation. Every outbound royalty wire must be entered under the international receipts/payments reporting system with the nature code for trademark/franchise license fees; amounts must be consistent with the prior year's sales-based royalty calculation and transfer-pricing documentation. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on gross dividends distributed by a Taiwan profit-seeking enterprise to a non-resident shareholder without a fixed place of business in Taiwan. Resident shareholders are not subject to withholding on distributions, but undistributed earnings are taxed at the 5% retained earnings surtax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year fiscal year, the filing window is 1 May through 31 May of the following year. For a non-calendar fiscal year approved by the tax authority, the return must be filed within five months after fiscal year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing and payment fall between 1 September and 30 September of the current assessment year, based on one-half of the immediately preceding year's taxable income, unless an exemption under Article 67(3) applies when zero or minimal taxable income is expected.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The WFOE must prepare a transfer pricing Local File and supporting comparability analysis when its total Taiwan related-party transaction amount exceeds NT$200 million in a fiscal year, or when the expected annual transaction value with a related party in a low-tax jurisdiction exceeds NT$50 million. A Group Master File is due when the MNE group's consolidated revenue exceeds NT$5 billion; Cross-Border Country-by-Country filing is mandatory when the ultimate parent entity's group revenue exceeds NT$27 billion.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not apply EU-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) verbatim, but the 'substance-over-form' doctrine is enforced through Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act and the anti-tax-haven purpose provisions. A WFOE engaged solely in collecting franchise royalties without local staffing, operational management, or real franchising activity may be disregarded; the tax authority can attribute the income to the actual economic owner and impose penalties. The WFOE must maintain a Taiwan operating office, local employees, board decisions book, bank accounts, and ongoing franchise support records to establish genuine business substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Revenue Concealment

Statutory sanction: Imposition of underpaid tax plus a fine of 2–3 times the concealed amount; daily interest accrual; referral to criminal investigation under the Tax Collection Act, with imprisonment up to 7 years and a fine of up to NT$30 million in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false tax returns are personally liable for the monetary penalty to the extent of the evaded tax; the court may order travel bans and seizure of personal assets; culpable officers face imprisonment and loss of civil rights.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may recharacterize the entire transaction, disallow intercompany deductions, and assess tax directly on the beneficial owner; the company may be deregistered; non-compliance with corporate filing obligations triggers an administrative fine from NT$10,000 to NT$500,000, plus an additional 3x penalty on hidden profits.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the veil where there is an abuse of legal personality; directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax and civil restitution; failure to maintain a valid registered office can lead to automatic dissolution and a separate per-director criminal penalty.
AML Red Flags and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Control Act, conviction carries imprisonment of 3–7 years and a fine of up to NT$50 million if aggravated; repeat corporate offenders may be barred from holding franchise licences; assets are forfeited.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face immediate individual criminal liability; each director can be fined up to NT$10 million and banned from leaving Taiwan; the court may impose a 10-year prohibition on serving as a director of any domestic company.
Transfer Pricing Violation

Statutory sanction: The National Taxation Bureau adjusts taxable income upward to reflect an arm's-length consideration; the adjusted tax may be subject to a 100% penalty under Article 110; interest and daily surcharge accrue; in a serious case involving a tax haven entity, a 200% penalty and criminal referral may follow.

Director personal liability: Chief Executives and Chief Financial Officers who certify false transfer pricing documentation are jointly liable for the underpaid tax if the company fails to collect; repeated willful adjustments result in disqualification from serving as responsible persons and potential travel bans.
Withholding Tax and Repatriation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority collects the missed withholding amount from the WFOE directly, adds a fine equal to 1.5–3 times the withheld amount for deliberate non-compliance, and levies late payment interest; serious cases trigger prosecution of the responsible representative.

Director personal liability: The responsible director is treated as the withholding agent and is personally liable to remit the unpaid withholding; civil tort actions by the government are enforceable against the director's personal estate; travel bans and denial of exit visas are routinely applied.

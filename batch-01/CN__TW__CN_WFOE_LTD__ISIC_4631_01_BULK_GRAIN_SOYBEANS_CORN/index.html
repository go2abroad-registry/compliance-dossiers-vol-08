Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Administration, Ministry of Finance (Taiwan) and its five regional National Taxation Bureaus; online electronic portal: MOF Tax e-Filing System and MOF E-Invoice Integrated Platform

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested Limited Liability Company structured as a Taiwan resident entity: (1) Obtain foreign investment approval from the Investment Commission, Ministry of Economic Affairs. (2) Notarise and file the Articles of Incorporation with the Commercial Development Administration, Ministry of Economic Affairs; the central business registration database will automatically assign a Unified Business Number (UBIN), which serves as the legal Tax Identification Number (TIN) for corporate income tax, business tax (VAT), and customs. (3) Within 15 days after company registration, visit the local National Taxation Bureau to complete the Business Tax registration (sales tax/VAT filing status) and any required income tax withholding registration for employees or foreign payees. (4) For non-resident foreign companies without a physical establishment, a tax proxy or fixed place of business must be appointed and registered under the Non-Resident Income Tax procedure; thereafter, a separate system file number is issued. The UBIN remains constant across all tax types; no separate VAT ID or TIN certificate is necessary beyond the Business Tax Registration Certificate.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No exemption threshold; all revenue-generating enterprises must register from first day of business. TWD 200,000 in average monthly sales is the statutory line above which a business must use the regular Uniform Invoice system and the standard 5% VAT rate; enterprises below this line are still registered as small-scale business taxpayers subject to a simplified 1% business tax (unless they are statutory exempt categories).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods under Article 7 of the Value-Added and Non-Value-Added Business Tax Act when the goods are physically shipped out of Taiwan under a Customs export declaration. The export of bulk soybeans and yellow corn qualifies when the seller is a registered exporter, the cargo leaves Taiwan, and the export is evidenced by prescribed documentation. Qualifying offshore services provided to foreign enterprises in connection with the export trade are also zero-rated. The exporter must claim zero-rating on the periodic VAT return and retain the full evidentiary bundle for substantiation; refunds of input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports are paid after review.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export entry / bill of a mandatory statutory exporter manifest)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill (original or telex release)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or other Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate issued by an authorised bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Under the E-Invoice Implementation Guidelines and Article 32 of the Business Tax Act, registered sellers must issue Uniform Invoices (electronically or paper) and transmit e-invoice data to the MOF E-Invoice Integrated Platform within statutory deadlines: 48 hours for B2C sales at the point of sale and 7 days for B2B invoices (calendar day counting). Sellers must embed a unique e-invoice number and a random anti-fraud validation code on every electronic uniform invoice; the integrated platform provides an API-driven validation service and QR-code verification for consumers. This is not a real-time central government clearance/stamping system; instead, tax authorities perform post-sale reconciliation, risk scoring, and download-based compliance monitoring.

National Taxation Bureaus cross-check export declarations against customs manifests, e-invoice B2B records, shipping documents, and bank foreign-exchange settlement records. Any failure to substantiate the export zero-rating triggers retrospective assessment of the unpaid VAT at 5% plus interest, and a penalty of up to five times the under-declared tax under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act. In cases of fraudulent misrepresentation, the Tax Collection Act authorises additional financial penalties, the freezing of tax refund claims, and criminal liability for tax evasion with imprisonment up to five years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan applies a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which the New Taiwan Dollar is convertible for current-account needs such as import payments for soybeans/corn, freight, insurance, and inspection fees, without prior central bank approval, subject to reporting by licensed designated foreign exchange banks. Capital-account convertibility is a licensed regime rather than a free float. Inward foreign direct investment from mainland China and other foreign jurisdictions requires permission from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs before the remittance is classified as equity capital. Once approved, the WFOE must complete a foreign exchange settlement/declaration form, and the inward funds are booked into a capital escrow account for later verification by a certified public accountant. Outward profit/dividend remittances require the existence of audited retained earnings, paid-in capital proof, tax payment clearance, and the same reporting strictures. There are no foreign exchange surrender requirements for export proceeds, and no mandatory conversion into TWD, but the proceeds must be repatriated within a six-month commercial settlement window. The CBC retains emergency powers to suspend or restrict foreign exchange transactions in the event of severe balance-of-payments dislocation, meaning hedging and multi-currency liquidity planning should always assume positive but regulated convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory settlement deadline for export foreign-exchange receipts is 180 days from the customs export declaration date; if payment is delayed by documentary discrepancies, weather, demurrage, or foreign bank error, an extension application must be filed through the settling designated foreign exchange bank for CBC or BOFT review. Mandatory TWD conversion ratio: 0%. No portion of exported soybean/corn proceeds is required to be surrendered to the central bank or converted into New Taiwan Dollars; the WFOE may maintain a foreign-currency deposit account, convert all or part freely for import settlements, or swap receipts into TWD subject only to the bank's current-account reporting threshold.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) - Foreign Exchange Department, with the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) as the banking conduct and electronic payment regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability. Taiwan's Financial Supervisory Commission requires any institution accepting deposits or executing payments for a Taiwan-resident company to hold a locally granted banking license or electronic payment institution license. An offshore EMI is not licensed by the CBC/FSC and cannot lawfully be used for an escrow account, export proceeds collection, or trade settlement. It may, at best, act as a front-end software layer if the actual account and settlement railing remain at a Taiwan designated bank; otherwise it creates unlicensed deposit-taking risk. The recommended path is direct Tier-1 bank onboarding and SWIFT-based LC/wire clearing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: required when annual related-party tangible-property transfers exceed RMB 200 million, or intangible-property transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or related-party services exceed RMB 100 million. Master File: required when the group's consolidated related-party turnover exceeds RMB 1 billion. CbC report threshold: RMB 5.5 billion consolidated group turnover.

Economic substance scope: China's 'substance over form' doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under CIT Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement No. 9 of 2018 require that a non-resident claimant claiming treaty benefits have actual business operations, employees, assets and risks—not merely paper ownership. While China does not administer offshore ESR codes, an economic-substance deficiency triggers denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of dividends into taxable income, and application of anti-conduit measures. The WFOE must maintain business substance and board/management presence to avoid deemed dividend and GAAR adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declarations

Statutory sanction: CIT Law and Tax Administration Law: deficiency assessment plus late surcharge at 0.05% daily; administrative penalty of 50%–5 times the tax underpaid; criminal penalty under Criminal Law Article 201: imprisonment for up to 7 years and fines for serious evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 (or 10% of tax due).

Director personal liability: Directors and functional managers who directly participate in evasion may be jointly and severally liable for fines and subject to criminal prosecution, disgorgement, and exit restrictions (travel bans) under the exit-reliance mechanism; judicial judgments may pursue personal assets where the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent or abusive conduct.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty/agreement benefits, recharacterization of income, deemed dividend withholding tax at 10%, penalty of 50%–5 times the underpaid tax, and fine up to RMB 500,000 under SAT Notice 9.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution for the disallowed tax and penalties; if falsification of residency or tax evasion is proven, travel bans and criminal accountability may arise.
AML and Cross-Border Payment Red Flags

Statutory sanction: AML Law fines (RMB 200,000–5 million) for failure to perform customer due diligence or suspicious transaction reporting; freezing of assets; revocation of licenses; referral to criminal authorities for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Personal fines up to RMB 500,000, up to 10 years' imprisonment for wilful money laundering, and prohibition from serving as directors; travel bans imposed by enforcement authorities.
Failure to File and Underpayment of WHT on Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent penalty: a late surcharge of 0.05% per day; a penalty of 50%–3 times the amount not withheld/paid (Tax Administration Law Article 69); if the payer fails to withhold, it remains liable for recovery.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for the withholding function are personally liable for the uncollected amount and can be subject to asset preservation orders, travel restrictions and criminal liability if intentional.

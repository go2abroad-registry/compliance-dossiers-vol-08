Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the Taiwan Company Act there is no fixed statutory minimum paid-in capital for a company limited by shares. However, the FSC, acting under the Insurance Act and captive reinsurance regulations, will impose a prudential minimum capital requirement and, where required, a guarantee deposit based on the scale and risk profile of the proposed captive reinsurance program. All initial paid-up capital, including any foreign-invested capital, must be remitted to Taiwan and deposited into a blocked escrow bank account for which a bank capital verification certificate must be issued. The blocked funds may not be disbursed until the FSC operating license is granted and all conditions of the FSC establishment authorization have been satisfied. Any foreign investment must also be reported to the Central Bank and approved under the investment approval framework.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident owner must first obtain foreign investment approval from the Investment Commission, Ministry of Economic Affairs, and incorporate the Taiwan entity or branch with the Department of Commerce. The Taiwan tax identification number is the Unified Business Number (UBN), which also functions as the VAT registration number. The UBN application is filed with the competent National Taxation Bureau before business commencement, using the online Business Tax Registration service or by written application at the local tax office. The filing package must include: authenticated certificate of incorporation and articles of incorporation of the foreign shareholder; foreign-investment approval letter; board resolution appointing the Taiwan responsible person; a registered-office address proof and lease contract; the Ministry of Economic Affairs company or branch registration certificate; and, where applicable, the Insurance Act license for captive reinsurance operations. The National Taxation Bureau verifies the application, assigns the eight-digit UBN, and issues the business-tax registration certificate. A Taiwan subsidiary receives a new UBN; a branch is registered under the same UBN as the head office. Non-resident enterprises without a Taiwan branch and supplying services into Taiwan must also complete VAT registration and obtain a UBN through a tax agent before collecting Taiwanese business tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, a 0% rate applies to goods physically exported from Taiwan and to services supplied to foreign enterprises or individuals where the services are used outside Taiwan and the consideration is in foreign currency. A captive reinsurance SPV qualifies for 0% only where the ceding insurer is a foreign (non-Taiwan) entity, the reinsured risks are located outside Taiwan, the reinsurance contract is performed offshore in the sense that no insurance or reinsurance service is consumed inside Taiwan, and the premium and claims are settled in foreign currency through a Taiwanese foreign-exchange bank account. Domestic risks ceded by a Taiwan insurer are domestic taxable supplies at 5%.

  • Customs Export Declaration processed by the Directorate General of Customs, Ministry of Finance (for goods movements), including the export e-reference number, container and cargo details, and confirmed export port or airport.
  • Commercial invoice and packing list supporting the invoice value declared to customs.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or express consignment note) showing physical movement from Taiwan customs territory to the overseas buyer.
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate or the bank settlement voucher issued by a designated foreign-exchange bank proving actual repatriation of export proceeds into a Taiwan foreign-exchange account.
  • For captive reinsurance service exports: the signed reinsurance treaty or contract, ceding bordereaux, actuarial premium calculation, proof that the underlying risk is located outside Taiwan, and the foreign-exchange receipt record for premiums.

Taiwan mandates uniform invoice issuance. VAT taxpayers must adopt electronic invoices and transmit the e-invoice XML data to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform through the XML TurnKey interface or direct API integration. Retail (B2C) e-invoice data must be uploaded generally within 48 hours of issuance; business-to-business (B2B) e-invoice data is transmitted before the quarterly business-tax filing deadline and matched to the counterparty account. Taiwan does not use physical fiscal stamping; anti-fraud control relies on mandatory QR codes, sequential invoice numbering, random-code authentication, and duplicate or abnormal-sales-pattern detection by the tax authority. Failure to integrate or transmit e-invoices is treated as failure to issue a uniform invoice and is subject to a separate fine.

The National Taxation Bureau may reopen the seven-year statutory assessment period. If the 0% export claim is not supported by the customs declaration, lading documents, contract, and foreign-exchange receipt, the revenue is reclassified as domestic taxable and assessed at 5% VAT, with overdue surcharge and late-filing penalties. False documents, fake reinsurance risk, circular foreign-exchange remittances, or misrepresented offshore risks are treated as tax evasion under the Business Tax Act Article 51, which may subject the taxpayer to a fine of up to five times the tax evaded, plus criminal referral for responsible persons under the Tax Collection Act; the VAT registration may also be cancelled and unreported periods adjusted.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with current account convertibility and partial capital account liberalisation. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture engaged in captive reinsurance, ordinary operating receipts (premiums, investment income, claims reimbursements) are treated as current account items and may be freely converted upon submission of authentic underlying documentation. Profit remittances and dividend distributions to the Taiwan-based or other foreign shareholders are permissible after corporate tax clearance, provided that audited financial statements and board-resolved dividend declarations are filed with the settlement bank. Capital account movements, including foreign direct investment capital injection and external borrowings, remain subject to SAFE registration, special-purpose account usage, and real-transaction verification. The captive reinsurance SPV, as a licensed financial institution, must obtain prior SAFE approval for any outward remittance of reinsurance premiums, claims payments, or statutory capital deductions beyond the regular current account framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE Circular on Optimising Foreign Exchange Administration (汇发〔2019〕28号) and the Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Guidelines, China no longer imposes mandatory conversion (surrender) of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency earnings in designated current account settlement accounts or convert them into RMB at will. Export receipts must nevertheless be repatriated to China through a domestic banking channel within 90 days of the customs declaration date, unless an extension is registered in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. For the specific captive reinsurance business, premium and claim collections from overseas are classified under service trade and must be reported under the Service Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Framework, with no mandatory conversion ratio but with full documentary verification of reinsurance treaties, bordereaux, and settlement statements.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Wise Fit: High

Low - Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, MultiPass) have no legal capacity to open domestic Chinese bank accounts or provide local IBANs denominated in CNY. Under PRC foreign exchange regulations, all corporate foreign currency receipts and payments must route through SAFE-licensed domestic banks. Offshore EMIs cannot act as the primary clearing rail for the captive reinsurance SPV because they do not fall within SAFE's supervisory framework and cannot obtain the necessary capital account or current account reporting codes. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan-based shareholder or any non-PRC foreign investor will inject paid-in capital via SWIFT MT103/202 to the captive SPV's dedicated FDI Capital Account at a SAFE-licensed domestic bank. The remitting bank must include the following wire purpose code in field 70 (Remittance Information): '701010 - 外方以现汇投入的资本金' (Foreign investor cash capital contribution). Before receiving the wire, the SPV must have completed SAFE Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration through the receiving bank, obtained the FDI business registration certificate, and furnished the bank with the unique FDI verification code. The receiving bank verifies the remitter name, transaction narration, and substance of the underlying contract and business licence. Once credited, the capital may be retained in foreign currency or settled into RMB at the prevailing exchange rate. Any conversion and use of the RMB proceeds must comply with the negative list and the operational scope stipulated in the business licence. Actual payment from the capital account to third parties requires submission of payment instruction with supporting vouchers (purchase order, construction contract, reinsurance reserve funding agreement) to the bank for authenticity review.For outward disbursements out of the SPV's current account, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers under the transaction's underlying commercial or reinsurance contract. The remitting bank executes the transfer only after examination of the relevant contract, invoice, and, where applicable, customs declaration. For reinsurance claims and premium cessions, the SPV will present a settlement statement or bordereau together with the reinsurance treaty. If the market custom requires documentary credit, a commercial letter of credit (L/C) may be established using the domestic bank's issuance facility, with SWIFT MT700 series messages for negotiation. For high-value or scheduled reinsurance settlements, the SPV may combine multiple transfers into a single bulk payment instruction, but each underlying claim must be enumerated in the remittance detail. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low - Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, MultiPass) have no legal capacity to open domestic Chinese bank accounts or provide local IBANs denominated in CNY. Under PRC foreign exchange regulations, all corporate foreign currency receipts and payments must route through SAFE-licensed domestic banks. Offshore EMIs cannot act as the primary clearing rail for the captive reinsurance SPV because they do not fall within SAFE's supervisory framework and cannot obtain the necessary capital account or current account reporting codes. They may only serve as supplementary global treasury concentration tools for non-China settled jurisdictions, subject to the entity's internal treasury policy and without touching the China onshore banking layer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$200,000,000 in annual related-party transactions (aggregate sales, purchases, services, fees, and interest) triggers a mandatory contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Report (Local File). For intangibles alone, a NT$100,000,000 threshold applies. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when the ultimate MNE group’s consolidated annual revenue exceeds NT$50,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the GAAR of Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act and the FSC insurance licensing framework jointly require a captive reinsurance SPV to maintain real commercial substance. The SPV must operate from a physical Taiwan office, employ locally-based underwriters and claims personnel, maintain arm’s-length risk-based capitalisation, and ensure that all board decisions and policy assumptions are made in Taiwan. A paper SPV that does not bear actual insurance risk, lacks adequate records, or serves as a pure booking vehicle for a multinational group will be disregarded for tax purposes, with its income recharacterised in the hands of the ultimate insured/parent group.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Underreporting of TX Reinsurance Income

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Income Tax Act, the taxpayer is ordered to make good the tax shortfall and is fined two to three times the underpaid tax. Tax Collection Act Article 41 imposes criminal liability in cases of intentional evasion, resulting in imprisonment for up to five years, a fine, and irrevocable seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: The responsible director and finance officers are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax if the JV is found to be a sham; travel bans and asset freezes are issued under Article 25 of the Tax Collection Act; criminal directors face loss of liberty.
Shell SPV / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority applies Article 43-1 GAAR to recompute income as if the arrangement were not contractual; deficiency penalty up to three times evaded tax; FSC revocation of the captive insurer’s license under the Insurance Act; unlawful operation is punishable by administrative fines and the dissolution of the company.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for corporate dissolution costs, restitution of improperly paid dividends, and regulatory penalties; criminal liability can arise if the shell company was used to defraud creditors or tax authorities, and directors may be disqualified from all financial industry roles for up to five years.
AML / CFT / Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Control Act, administrative fines up to NT$10,000,000 may be imposed for each serious compliance breach; criminal money laundering is punishable by imprisonment for one to seven years and a fine of up to NT$30,000,000; the Financial Supervisory Commission can suspend the entity’s authorised business.

Director personal liability: The compliance director and each board member who knowingly effected or acquiesced in the laundering structure may be prosecuted criminally, fined personally, stripped of their directorship, and banned from holding any appointment in an insurance, bank, or capital-market enterprise.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The National Taxation Bureau may adjust the captive SPV’s taxable income using a deemed arm’s-length pricing method; the underpaid tax is subject to a two to three times deficiency penalty. An additional separate fine of NT$30,000 to NT$100,000 per document is levied for each missing schedule. Persistent incompliance is treated as intentional evasion under Article 41.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed inaccurate TP documentation or knowingly disregarded documentation requirements can be jointly liable for the company’s tax deficiency; where insolvency results, a civil suit may succeed against their personal assets, and a travel restriction order may be issued until the assessment is settled.

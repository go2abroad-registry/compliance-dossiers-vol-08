The paid-in capital must be deposited in a Taiwan New Taiwan Dollar bank account and remitted through foreign exchange procedures; a certified public accountant capital verification certificate is required only if the paid-in capital exceeds the MOEA threshold. No escrow or blocked capital account requirement applies to this low-risk digital publishing entity.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic tax filings and VAT administration are conducted through the nationwide Electronic Tax Service Platform (e-Tax) and the STA e-invoice service system.

E-government filing portal

The Taiwan-resident investor incorporates the WFOE in mainland China and applies to the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) for a Business License containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the legal person's statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). The company's legal representative and financial officer must then, within 30 calendar days of the Business License issuance date, log into the Electronic Tax Service Platform and complete the tax registration by submitting the business license, articles of association, investor resolutions, registered address lease agreement, bank open account certificate, and passport/company registration certificate of the non-resident shareholder. Upon approval, the tax authority issues the tax registration record and activates the VAT taxpayer profile (small-scale or general). A non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China must separately complete non-resident registration for withholding tax and VAT reporting using the STA-issued Tax Identification Number for non-residents.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A general VAT taxpayer may claim the 0% export VAT rate where: (i) physical goods are exported beyond mainland China's customs territory with a customs export declaration; or (ii) qualifying digital services, including hyper-casual mobile game publishing, online platform advertising, and software-enabled content, are supplied to a non-PRC customer and consumed entirely outside mainland China, with the contract, server logs, and payment records confirming off-shore consumption. The privilege requires the exporter to be a registered VAT general taxpayer, to actually receive foreign exchange from outside mainland China, and to maintain a complete statutory documentation file substantiating the cross-border supply.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration Form (Customs Export Declaration)
  • Carrier-issued Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment outside mainland China
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List issued by the exporting WFOE
  • Bank Export Proceeds Receipt Voucher or Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate issued by a State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)-designated bank
  • Cross-Border Service Contract, server/usage logs, and evidence of payment by the overseas counterparty for zero-rated digital services

China enforces a real-time fiscal clearance regime under the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the all-electronic Fapiao platform. All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-approved tax control UKey or the national e-invoice service platform, transmitted electronically to the tax authority in near real time, digitally signed with a QR code encryption stamp, and reconciled with customs declarations, bank fund flows, and contract records under the invoice-flow-fund matching audit mechanism.

The tax authorities may retrospectively disallow export zero-rating if the statutory documentation is incomplete, inauthentic, or inconsistent with bank fund flows, in which case VAT is clawed back at the applicable rate (13% for goods, 6% for services), late surcharges accrue at 0.05% per day, and an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid tax may be imposed under the PRC Tax Administration Law. Deliberate false export declarations constitute tax fraud and criminal tax evasion, resulting in cancellation of export tax rebate qualifications, inclusion on the tax blacklist, seizure of refundable input VAT, and exposure to criminal prosecution for the responsible personnel.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The jurisdiction relevant to this WFOE treasury blueprint is the People's Republic of China, whose currency is the Renminbi (RMB). Mainland China operates under a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict foreign exchange controls administered by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). The current account is generally convertible subject to genuine trade and service transaction verification, while the capital account remains heavily regulated. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in the software and digital gaming publishing sector must ensure that all inbound equity injections are properly recorded under the Foreign Investment Information Reporting system and routed through a designated Renminbi or Foreign Currency Capital Account. Outbound profit remittances require audited financial statements, a tax clearance certificate, and evidence that the distributable profits were generated from verified operational revenue. For a hyper-casual gaming publisher, significant revenue may arrive as cross-border digital advertising payouts from platforms such as AdMob and AppLovin, which are classified as services under current account. These service receipts may require lodging with a domestic bank under a service trade contract, supporting invoices, and proof of digital platform earnings. SAFE will screen such receipts for beneficial ownership and the genuineness of the underlying digital advertising activity. Surrender requirements historically applied to export proceeds but have been abolished for current account receipts since 2012; however banks will still verify the economic substance of service export revenue to curb disguised capital flows. Profit repatriation is permitted once corporate income tax and statutory reserves are satisfied, but the burden of proof is substantial. Any delay or mismatch in documentation can trigger a Suspicious Transaction Report and freeze the payout. Therefore, the treasury strategy must prioritize documentation automation, bank relationship custody, and rigorous reconciliation between platform payout statements and underlying game publishing contracts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As of the current SAFE framework, mandatory export proceeds surrender requirements have been effectively abolished for current account receipts. The WFOE is allowed to retain export service proceeds in foreign currency or convert them into Renminbi at its chosen bank. However, banks are obliged to perform the 'reasonable authenticity and consistency' review under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations. For digital advertising and game publishing service revenue, the entity must submit the digital platform payout statement, executed service agreements, and a breakdown of user platform geography within five working days if requested. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; conversion is at the discretion of the entity. The more relevant statutory timeline is the six-month period for capital account settlement of foreign currency capital, and the requirement to use the Foreign Capital to Foreign Debt Conversion System for any capital injection payment. For current account advertising receipts, the bank will typically credit and permit conversion within the same business day once the transaction is matched against the annual service trade quota. The WFOE must also update the tax filing for the corresponding offshore digital platform fees, as under-remittance can trigger a transfer pricing audit.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Non-feasible for core settlement in the CN jurisdiction. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital-only banks are not licensed to hold RMB current accounts or provide foreign exchange conversion services directly to mainland WFOE entities. The WFOE is required to maintain an onshore banking relationship with a SAFE-designated commercial bank for all capital account receipts, injection verification, profit repatriation and service trade payables. Offshore EMIs can only be used as auxiliary collection nodes for low-value foreign advertising revenue if supported by a licensed payment institution in China, but this introduces a higher AML risk profile and is discouraged. The compliance committee should reject any EMI-centric treasury design.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan shareholder must remit the WFOE registered capital from its domestic Taiwanese NT dollar account through an authorized Taiwanese remitting bank, converting to USD or directly to RMB via the China Foreign Exchange Trade System rate. The remittance must state the purpose code as 'Investment / Registered Capital – Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise capital injection' and include the WFOE Foreign Investment Code (FDI code) in the remittance instruction. The inbound wire should arrive at the Bank of China capital escrow account using SWIFT MT103, with supporting equity transfer agreement or board resolution proving ownership. Within fifteen calendar days of receipt, the bank must settle the foreign currency into RMB using the FDI Foreign Exchange Settlement system and verify the transaction against the annual foreign investment information report. The WFOE must also complete tax registration with the local tax authority and apply for a capital verification report issued by a Chinese certified public accountant if the banks or future lenders require it. No borrowing, third-party 27 letters of credit transfer, or set-off against unrelated trade receivables is permitted on the capital account before settlement.For outbound disbursements related to international game publishing operations and advertising supply, the WFOE should use domestic and cross-border clearing rails in the following order of preference: SWIFT MT103 for supplier payments and international licensing fees, Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) only for tangible physical equipment acquisition where the corporate banking relationship provides full documentary support, and Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) where the counterparty is a recurring trusted vendor and the transaction value is material. For recurring ad network payouts, the entity should contract with a SAFE-registered third-party payment institution for bulk payments into overseas developer accounts, but must retain a segregated audit trail showing the corresponding advertising revenue invoice and tax deduction certificates. All outbound remittances must be tagged with the applicable SAFE payment purpose code, such as code 122010 for royalties and license fees or code 121010 for software export services. The bank will require the underlying contract, invoice, proof of service delivery in the form of downloadable server access logs, and withholding tax payment voucher, where applicable, before clearing the cross-border Instruction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current-account transactions for goods and services are freely convertible through designative Chinese commercial banks. Capital-account items, including paid-in capital injection, shareholder loans, and profit repatriation, are subject to SAFE registration, prior approval, or post-event filing. Profit and dividend remittance from the WFOE requires audited annual financial statements, corporate income tax clearance, and a valid SAFE FDI registration. Cross-strait remittances to Taiwan are treated as capital flows under the Cross-Strait Financial Cooperation Agreement, requiring additional evidentiary documentation and source-of-funds confirmation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be repatriated to a Chinese designated foreign exchange account within a period consistent with trade settlement practice and reported through the SAFE Goods Trade Monitoring System. There is no mandatory selling-to-RMB obligation; however, each export transaction requires reconciliation against customs export declarations (报关单) within 90 days of the export date. Receipts not matched within the statutory verification window become subject to enhanced audit and monetary penalty.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a stand-alone solution; ACCEPTABLE only as an ancillary payment corridor. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Skyee, WorldFirst) without a PRC banking license cannot hold statutory capital, receive SAFE-registered FDI, or settle government L/C proceeds. Funds collected through such platforms must be routed into a licensed Chinese commercial bank account. Legal constraints: absence of a mainland deposit-taking license, lack of SAFE connectivity for cross-border balance-of-payments reporting, and prohibition on direct RMB conversion by non-bank institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol: the Taiwan parent initiates an SWIFT MT103 credit to the WFOE's foreign exchange capital account held at a designated Chinese Tier1 bank. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state the WFOE's FDI Business Registration Certificate Number, the purpose 'Investment – Paid-in Capital', and a reference to the SAFE FDI Registration Code. The Chinese bank verifies (a) the credit amount against the remaining approved registered capital, (b) the authenticity of the underlying FDI Registration, and (c) the corporate resolution authorizing the capital injection. Upon receipt, the bank files a cross-border balance-of-payments report with SAFE and allocates the funds strictly in accordance with the permitted capital-use purposes.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (a) SWIFT MT103 direct outward remittances for paying import suppliers, using purpose code 'General Trade Import' with matched customs import declarations; (b) Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) opened by the WFOE's Chinese bank for bulk purchases of electronic components and subassemblies; (c) For export receipts under government LCs, confirmation and negotiation through the Chinese bank's trade services desk using SWIFT MT710/MT720, with optional forfaiting or discounting of L/C proceeds. All outbound disbursements require underlying contracts, invoices, and customs declarations to satisfy SAFE's trade monitoring requirements.Onshore deliverable foreign-exchange forwards (CNY vs USD/EUR/JPY) transacted with Chinese Tier1 banks under real trade-background and approved exposure limitsOffshore non-deliverable forwards (CNY NDF) executed through the Taiwan parent or an authorized offshore broker, subject to documented hedge economic substance and prohibition on offshore speculative settlementOnshore cross-currency swaps between CNY and USD/EUR for hedging principal and interest of cross-border shareholder loans

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$100 million in aggregate annual related-party transaction value. A lower threshold may apply for transactions with related parties in designated low-tax jurisdictions or for intangible-related transactions.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation, but applies substance-over-form and the place-of-effective-management test. A Taiwan WFOE used as a booking pass-through without local premises, employees, inventory risk, or board control is subject to profit reattribution, denial of treaty benefits, and CFC rules. The entity must maintain substantial manufacturing activity, management decisions, and payroll in Taiwan to survive audit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the full underpaid tax, a penalty of 2 to 3 times the underpaid amount, and criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act and the Criminal Code. Fines may reach NT$10 million, and imprisonment of up to seven years may be ordered for intentional evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or approve fraudulent filings may be personally assessed for the unpaid corporate tax through veil-piercing under the Company Act; travel bans and asset seizure are available; criminal imprisonment applies to responsible officers.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may re-characterize the legal entity, attribute all relevant profits to Taiwan, apply the 20% corporate tax plus a 5% retained-earnings surtax, and impose a 2x penalty on the adjusted underpayment. Benefits of any DTA may be retroactively denied.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to pay the corporate tax, penalty, and interest if the corporate form was a sham; the tax authority can impose civil restitution, travel bans, and, in egregious cases, prosecution for fraud or money laundering.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Control Act, fines up to NT$100 million and imprisonment up to ten years for willful violations. The tax authority may also assess a 20% to 100% evasion penalty on the underlying unreported income.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face personal criminal liability; assets, including offshore accounts, may be frozen; seizure and confiscation orders may be executed against personal estates.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: A transfer-pricing adjustment will increase taxable income and create a 20% to 40% penalty on the additional tax. Failure to file required TP documentation without reasonable cause also triggers a fine of NT$5,000 to NT$100,000 and precludes the taxpayer from invoking the transfer-pricing defense.

Director personal liability: Officers who sign or certify false TP documentation may be jointly liable for the penalty; repeated violations can result in professional disqualification, travel restrictions, and personal civil liability for the tax deficit.

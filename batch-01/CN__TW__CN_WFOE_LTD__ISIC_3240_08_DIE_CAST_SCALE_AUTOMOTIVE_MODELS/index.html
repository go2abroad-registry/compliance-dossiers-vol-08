None under the Taiwan Company Act for a general wholly foreign-owned limited company; however, the shareholder must pay the full subscription amount of the issued shares into a blocked capital escrow bank account before registration, and a licensed certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report confirming receipt of paid-in capital before the MOEA registration filing is accepted. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero percent VAT applies to die-cast zinc alloy scale automotive models physically exported from the customs territory of the People's Republic of China, including cross-strait trade to Taiwan, provided the exporter is a VAT general taxpayer; the goods are produced or purchased in China with verified input VAT; the electronic customs export declaration matches the commercial invoice, packing list, and bank settlement records; and export proceeds are repatriated to a Chinese banking account. The exemption-rebate method applies to direct exports and the exemption-first-rebate-after method applies to commissioned export; failure to depart customs territory or to repatriate proceeds within the statutory deadline converts the zero rating into taxable output with interest and penalties.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Customs Manifest)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export shipment
  • Cross-Border Receipts Declaration from the settlement bank (export foreign exchange receipt)
  • Export tax rebate declaration records with matching input VAT invoice verification

Mandatory digitalized electronic VAT invoicing through the All-Electronic Invoice Service Platform; every invoice is cleared in real time by the Golden Tax Phase IV system with a unique digital invoice number, cryptographic tax-control stamp, and full XML data transmission through taxpayer ERP/API integration or the web portal; paper tax-control-device invoices remain subject to anti-counterfeit code printing and automated daily reporting.

Export zero-rating and rebate claims are subject to automated cross-matching of customs declarations, VAT invoice data, and foreign exchange settlement records. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day deadline displaces the zero-rating, requiring output VAT reversal with interest from the export date. Fraudulent or unverified claims trigger recovery of the refunded tax, fines of 50 percent to five times the amount involved, suspension or revocation of export rebate eligibility, inclusion in the major tax violation blacklist, retroactive assessments without a statute of limitation for fraud, and criminal liability for export rebate fraud exceeding RMB 100,000 with imprisonment up to life.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland China foreign-exchange framework is best characterized as asymmetric convertibility. Current-account receipts and payments such as export sales, freight, commissions, and dividends are generally convertible, subject to genuine transaction documentation, bank due diligence, and SAFE reporting. Capital-account transactions remain strictly regulated: a WFOE can receive inbound equity only after completing FDI registration with SAFE through its domestic bank, and the proceeds must be deposited into a specifically identified capital foreign-exchange account. Once settled into RMB, funds must be deployed within the WFOE's operational scope; prohibited usages include equity investments in non-operating financial assets, unlicensed lending, and real-estate purchases beyond permissible premises. For profit remittance to the overseas parent, the dividend is classified as current-account investment income and is routinely processed without a separate SAFE quota, but the domestic bank will require an audited statutory financial statement, corporate income tax filing proof, a board declaration on distributable profits, and beneficial-owner tax-residency documentation; 10% withholding may apply subject to the applicable Cross-Strait tax treaty arrangement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE goods-trade administration framework, no statutory mandatory conversion ratio applies to export foreign-currency proceeds. The WFOE must repatriate export proceeds into China within the standard 180-day settlement cycle from the date of customs release, except where approved trade-credit extensions or deferred-payment arrangements are registered with the bank and SAFE. The proceeds may be retained in a current-account foreign-exchange export proceeds account or voluntarily converted to RMB at the bank's listed exchange rate. SAFE reconciles every receipt against electronic customs declarations, export invoices, and shipping documents; mismatches must be documented with contracts, bills of lading, letters of credit, and adjustment declarations to avoid trade-surveillance penalties.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) monetary and regulatory umbrella

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

In-depth feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury use. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut, Payoneer, or Airwallex are not licensed as mainland Chinese deposit-taking institutions and may not open RMB corporate accounts, clear capital-account transactions, or issue SAFE-compliant foreign-exchange statements for a mainland WFOE. Offshore EMIs are legally barred from servicing the entity as a regulated bank or treasury settlement rail. They may function only as secondary payment aggregators for low-value export receivables, provided funds are ultimately swept into a licensed domestic bank and reported under SAFE goods-trade rules. Capital injections and outbound trade disbursements must be executed through a tier-one domestic commercial bank.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Statutory audit applies to every PRC company regardless of turnover, balance-sheet size, or headcount; the trigger is a zero-threshold qualitative mandate under Article 164 of the PRC Company Law and Article 20 of the PRC Accounting Law. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, the audit obligation is additionally mandatorily required by the foreign investment annual reporting regime. Further quantitative trigger points requiring expanded audit and attestation procedures are: (i) related-party transactions in aggregate exceeding RMB 100 million (approximately USD 14 million) during the year, which require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation; (ii) total assets or annual revenue exceeding RMB 200 million (approximately USD 28 million), classifying the entity as a large-scale taxpayer with elevated tax audit scrutiny; and (iii) 300 or more employees, triggering social insurance, statistical, and employment compliance audit procedures. None of these thresholds exempts the WFOE from the core independent certified CPA audit obligation.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends remitted by the WFOE to a non-resident shareholder absent treaty relief; under the Cross-Strait Agreement, the dividend WHT is reduced to 5% where the Taiwan resident beneficial owner directly holds at least 25% of the WFOE's capital and satisfies all substantive conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For a Chinese WFOE, the local file must be prepared when annual related-party transactions reach any of the following thresholds: tangible asset transfers at or above RMB 200 million; financial asset/loan transactions at or above RMB 100 million; intangible asset transfers at or above RMB 100 million; or other related-party transactions at or above RMB 40 million. Master file preparation is triggered when the group's consolidated annual related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion or more, or when the group meets the special conditions under SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a UAE-style ESR regime, but the tax authorities apply substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, and general anti-avoidance rules under CIT Law Article 47. A Taiwan shareholder claiming treaty benefits must prove real economic substance and perform core income-generating activities in Taiwan; a shell or letter-box company without employees, premises, decision-making, and business functions will be denied treaty relief and the statutory 10% WHT will be applied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through False Related-Party Invoicing

Statutory sanction: PRC Criminal Law Article 201 imposes criminal liability for tax evasion; Tax Collection and Administration Law Articles 63 and 64 require collection of the evaded tax, a civil penalty of 50%-500% of the evaded amount, and daily late-payment interest of 0.05%.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: directors who participated in the false invoicing are jointly and severally liable for the underpaid CIT and penalties. They face travel bans and exit restrictions under the tax enforcement regime, civil restitution for the WFOE's loss, and, where the evasion is large, imprisonment of up to 7 years with criminal fines.
Shell Company and Treaty Benefit Abuse

Statutory sanction: The SAT denies the treaty benefit under CIT Law Article 47 and the Cross-Strait Agreement beneficial ownership rules, recharacterizes the dividend as paid to the true economic owner, imposes the statutory 10% dividend WHT plus interest from the original due date, and assesses penalties for under-withholding under Tax Collection and Administration Law Articles 62-64.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false beneficial ownership declarations and authorized the reduced withholding are jointly and severally liable for the WHT shortfall and civil restitution. The court may disregard the shell under current Chinese Company Law abuse-of-corporate-form provisions, impose travel bans and asset freezes, and expose directors to criminal tax evasion liability under Criminal Law Article 201 because the false treaty declaration caused a significant underpayment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 41, 44, and 48 and SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42 and [2017] No. 6 authorize a primary transfer pricing adjustment, additional deemed interest at the RMB benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points for missing documentation, late-payment surcharge, and a 50%-500% penalty where the failure involves willful concealment or false records.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally failed to establish arm's length pricing or who destroyed evidence of intercompany arrangements are held personally liable for the resulting CIT and TP interest. Asset preservation orders, exit restrictions, and tax blacklisting may be applied; if the TP adjustment reveals fraudulent concealment, the responsible directors face personal civil restitution and up to 7 years imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 201.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — CN_JV_LTD, being incorporated in Taiwan, is a resident profit-seeking enterprise taxed on worldwide income at 20% CIT (Income Tax Act Art. 3), plus a 5% undistributed-retained-earnings surtax under Art. 66-9 where profits are not distributed; the integrated imputation credit system applies to resident shareholders. Non-resident shareholders are subject to statutory dividend withholding at 21% (or the applicable non-resident rate; no comprehensive income tax treaty is in force, including for the mainland-China shareholder). No pass-through or partnership classification is available by election.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists; entity classification follows the legal form. Relevant elections for an M&A boutique: (i) affiliated-group consolidated return election under Income Tax Act Art. 39-1 (parent holding ≥90% of the subsidiary); (ii) tax-deferred reorganization and loss-carries election under the Business Mergers and Acquisitions Act; (iii) functional-currency election for bookkeeping with NT$ tax translation; (iv) advance pricing agreement or individual letter ruling application for hybrid instrument transfer-pricing; (v) foreign income tax credit election for offshore-source success fees. Hybrid-mismatch and CFC/PEM provisions (Income Tax Act Art. 43-3/43-4) are mandatory anti-avoidance rules, not elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration before commencing business (Business Tax Act Art. 28); no threshold applies to a corporation. Small-scale operators with average monthly turnover below NT$80,000 (≈US$2,500) may apply for exemption from registration; monthly turnover of NT$80,000–NT$200,000 is taxed at a special 1% rate (Art. 13); the standard 5% rate applies above NT$200,000 and to all professional service firms.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Business Tax Act Art. 7(3), export of advisory services qualifies for 0% VAT where (a) the services are rendered to a foreign enterprise without a fixed place of business in Taiwan (or are performed abroad), (b) the deliverable is actually used outside Taiwan, and (c) the consideration is received in foreign currency. Success fees, retainers and due-diligence fees charged by the M&A boutique to non-Taiwan clients (e.g., mainland-China parent or offshore acquisition vehicles), with the engagement report used abroad, are zero-rated; input VAT on local costs becomes refundable. Services used in Taiwan or rendered to a Taiwan branch of a foreign company are taxable at 5%. Foreign-currency receipt must be effected within 180 days (extendable to 270 days with the handling bank's approval or 365 days with Central Bank consent).

Electronic Uniform Invoices (電子統一發票) are mandatory for all registered operators. Invoices must be transmitted to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform (財政部電子發票整合服務平台) within 48 hours of issuance via the Turnkey (加值中心) integration API or a certified value-added service center; the platform performs cross-matching of invoice serial numbers, customs declarations and foreign-currency receipt data. Each e-invoice carries an 8-digit serial number plus a 2-digit random authentication code (digital stamp); B2B/B2C clearance is near-real-time, and zero-rated export invoices are flagged for refund audit.

Zero-rated export claims are a priority audit target: the National Taxation Bureau automatically cross-checks e-invoice data against customs records and foreign-exchange receipts. Where substantiation fails (e.g., no foreign-currency collection, services used in Taiwan, fabricated offshore clients), the supply is retroactively reclassified as 5% taxable: underpaid VAT plus interest is collected, and a penalty of 5 to 10 times the underpaid tax is imposed (Business Tax Act Art. 51; Tax Collection Act Art. 44). Forged contracts or backdated remittances trigger criminal liability under Tax Collection Act Art. 41 (up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of up to NT$5,000,000); the assessment period extends from 5 to 7 years for fraudulent evasion, and customs smuggling liability may attach where goods are involved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CNY is fully convertible for current-account transactions (trade in services including M&A advisory success fees) under SAFE's verification-based regime, but strictly controlled on the capital account. Profit remittance and dividends by a Sino-foreign equity joint venture require annual audit, tax clearance, board resolution on profit distribution, and bank verification under SAFE Circular 16/2019. Equity capital injection, shareholder loans, and repayment of overseas borrowings require prior SAFE registration (FDI registration or Foreign Debt registration). There is no free convertibility for speculative or non-trade capital flows; cross-border M&A advisory fees from non-resident clients are treated as service trade revenue and enjoy current-account convertibility once authentic commercial documents, invoices, contracts, and proof of service completion are submitted to the designated bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE rules, export proceeds from cross-border services rendered by the M&A boutique must be repatriated to a licensed bank in China. Mandatory forced conversion was abolished in 2012; therefore enterprises may retain 100% of qualified export/service proceeds in domestic foreign-currency accounts or voluntarily convert into RMB at market rates. The enterprise must go through 'repatriation and verification' within 30 days after the contractual payment due date or receipt, at the bank where the service trade receipt is booked, presenting the service contract, commercial invoice, proof of service performance, and a SAFE service-trade payment/receipt application. Failure to repatriate within the statutory period triggers SAFE penalty and removal from the 'A-level enterprise' convenience regime. The same rules apply to success fees charged to overseas targets or acquirers in cross-border M&A engagements, with no differentiated conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) — State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium, with significant legal constraints. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, and neobank-style Payment and Electronic Money Institutions regulated in Hong Kong, Singapore, or the UK) are viable for collecting or disbursing small-ticket cross-border success-fee wires from foreign clients or paying transaction bonus milestones to overseas counterparties, but they cannot lawfully open the mandatory RMB Basic Deposit Account inside mainland China. Under PBOC licensing rules, such EMIs lack a deposit-taking banking license and cannot hold client funds as bank deposits; they operate as payment facilitators or foreign-exchange agents. For the M&A advisory boutique, an offshore EMI account may be used as an ancillary collections rail for non-China counterparties, but full treasury control requires a licensed onshore domestic bank relationship; custody of deal escrow funds, regulatory-significant subscription payments, or SAFE-monitored capital flows must remain in a tier-1 commercial bank. Legal caution: China's cybersecurity, data-residency, and cross-border data transfer rules limit the use of fully offshore financial dashboards and user interfaces by a JV whose servers or records sit within PRC territory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing begins with the Shanghai/Shenzhen/Beijing local SAFE office registration of the Sino-foreign equity joint venture's FDI quota and capital account. The Taiwan shareholder or its designated offshore entity then wires the agreed equity injection through SWIFT MT103 from its own bank account to the JV's designated Capital Escrow Account at the Chinese tier-1 bank. SAFE reconciliation requires the remitter name and account number to match the registered shareholder; the payment purpose message must be tagged with SAFE purpose code 'JINJIN' / 'FDI Equity Injection' and quote the valid FDI registration certificate number, if required by the receiving bank. The bank will verify the source-of-funds evidence, board resolution, the original executed joint venture contract (with notarization), and the unified social credit code certificate before crediting the account. Any capital payment in kind (e.g., technology or equipment) must be declared to customs and appraised; cash wiring is accepted for monetary subscription only. If the capital amount exceeds the registered FDI quota, a separate SAFE capital-account adjustment application is mandatory before remittance; otherwise, the surplus will be rejected or suspended for anti-money-laundering review.For distribution of M&A transaction-related success fees, monthly retainers, or reimbursement of overseas professional expenses from the JV's operation account: SWIFT MT103 is the primary clearing rail, sent through the JV's domestic bank to the beneficiary's account in Taiwan or any other jurisdiction. For cross-border RMB settlement, the bank uses the CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System) rail with the MT103 message mapped to the e-CIP standard; the payment purpose must be classified as 'service trade remuneration' or 'M&A advisory fee' under the correct SAFE purpose code. Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) are permissible only when the payment is embedded in a documentary transaction, e.g., where a cross-border acquisition agreement requires the escrow of fees through an L/C guarantee; a pure advisory success fee normally cannot be routed through an L/C without an underlying physical trade document, thus commercial L/Cs are assessed as Low usability for this boutique. For outbound disbursements to INR, JPY, USD, or EUR, the domestic bank applies the daily FX rate and debits the corresponding RMB balance; no separate foreign-currency account is required. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium, with significant legal constraints. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, and neobank-style Payment and Electronic Money Institutions regulated in Hong Kong, Singapore, or the UK) are viable for collecting or disbursing small-ticket cross-border success-fee wires from foreign clients or paying transaction bonus milestones to overseas counterparties, but they cannot lawfully open the mandatory RMB Basic Deposit Account inside mainland China. Under PBOC licensing rules, such EMIs lack a deposit-taking banking license and cannot hold client funds as bank deposits; they operate as payment facilitators or foreign-exchange agents. For the M&A advisory boutique, an offshore EMI account may be used as an ancillary collections rail for non-China counterparties, but full treasury control requires a licensed onshore domestic bank relationship; custody of deal escrow funds, regulatory-significant subscription payments, or SAFE-monitored capital flows must remain in a tier-1 commercial bank. Legal caution: China's cybersecurity, data-residency, and cross-border data transfer rules limit the use of fully offshore financial dashboards and user interfaces by a JV whose servers or records sit within PRC territory.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA; 国家税务总局) — official portal www.chinatax.gov.cn; day-to-day registration, invoice and filing operations are executed through the province-level Electronic Tax Bureau (电子税务局) consolidated on the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau platform (全国统一规范电子税务局); customs-export data matching and refund declaration run through China Electronic Port (中国电子口岸/出口退税申报系统).

E-government filing portal

The CN WFOE obtains its Unified Social Credit Code (USCC, 统一社会信用代码) from the provincial Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) at incorporation. Within 30 calendar days after the business license is issued, the entity must complete initial tax registration confirmation (登记信息确认) via the Electronic Tax Bureau; the USCC automatically becomes the taxpayer identification number (TIN) used for Enterprise Income Tax (EIT), VAT, Individual Income Tax (IIT) payroll withholding, stamp duty and social insurance. A foreign investor that contracts directly in China without a business license must first obtain a temporary tax registration (临时税务登记) from the local STA office. The WFOE then files the VAT payer-status determination (general vs. small-scale), IIT withholding registration, payroll/social insurance linkage and electronic invoice quota calibration before issuing any invoice.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The PRC WFOE is a resident enterprise (居民企业) under Article 2 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law (2007, as amended) and is taxed on worldwide income at the headline 25.0% CIT rate; territorial scope is global, with unilateral foreign tax credit relief (EIT Law Art. 23) available for Taiwan-source and other foreign taxes because the Cross-Strait Tax Agreement (signed 25 Aug 2015) is not yet in force. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The PRC WFOE is a resident enterprise (居民企业) under Article 2 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law (2007, as amended) and is taxed on worldwide income at the headline 25.0% CIT rate; territorial scope is global, with unilateral foreign tax credit relief (EIT Law Art. 23) available for Taiwan-source and other foreign taxes because the Cross-Strait Tax Agreement (signed 25 Aug 2015) is not yet in force. The Taiwan investor is non-resident for PRC tax purposes; dividends and interest remitted from the WFOE are subject to 10.0% PRC withholding (reducible only if the cross-strait agreement is implemented or a local preferential policy applies; no treaty benefits are currently available). Tax-Exempt_Zone aspects: integrated bonded zones (综合保税区) treat goods entering the zone as exported (VAT exempt/zero-rated until the goods leave the zone into the PRC domestic market), and the Hainan Free Trade Port offers a 15% CIT rate for qualifying encouraged industries — relevant if the fulfillment/kitting operation is relocated into such zones.

Available election forms

PRC law contains no check-the-box election: legal classification follows SAMR registration. The following statutory elections are relevant: (1) VAT payer-status election — an entity with annual taxable sales of RMB 5,000,000 or less may elect simplified small-scale registration (3% levy, temporarily reduced to 1% through 31 Dec 2027); above that threshold, general-taxpayer status is mandatory with full input-credit refunds; (2) deemed-resident election (actual management place test) may pull an offshore entity into PRC CIT; (3) small low-profit enterprise (小型微利企业) election lowers the effective CIT rate to 5% for annual taxable income up to RMB 3,000,000 subject to employee/asset caps; (4) high-and-new-technology enterprise certification (15% CIT) is available for qualifying technology-enabled fulfillment software; (5) no fiscal-unity/grouping election exists for CIT; (6) foreign tax credit may be elected on a per-country (direct and indirect) basis; (7) export-refund method is statutory, not elective: manufacturing-related exporters use the 免抵退税 method, trading exporters use the 免退税 method, and bonded-zone operators use the entry-into-zone deemed-export rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general-taxpayer registration applies once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 (net of VAT); taxpayers at or below this threshold may register as small-scale taxpayers (levy method: 3%, temporarily 1% until 31 Dec 2027); monthly sales of RMB 100,000 or less for small-scale taxpayers are VAT-exempt. Registration must be filed in the tax district of the operational warehouse/fulfillment address within 30 days of the trigger event; any VAT liability generates monthly/quarterly filing obligations via the Electronic Tax Bureau.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A general VAT taxpayer may apply the export zero rate (免征增值税并退还进项税额) where goods are physically exported through PRC customs under a lawful customs declaration, the WFOE holds title/control over the goods, the sale is made to an overseas buyer, and proceeds are collected in foreign currency or RMB from the buyer. For ISIC 5210 kitting/packaging: value-added processing incorporated into the exported goods price is refundable to the extent the final invoice classifies the supply as goods; standalone warehousing, handling and logistics services are domestic services at 6% VAT unless they qualify as zero-rated or VAT-exempt offshore/export services under the cross-border service rules (财税〔2016〕36号文 Annex IV, e.g., international freight forwarding, storage of goods located outside China, or services consumed entirely outside China). E-commerce export retail may use the exemption-without-refund regime for qualifying small parcels (cross-border e-commerce comprehensive pilot zones). Input VAT attributable to domestic services and overheads is apportioned and may be non-refundable.

China mandates Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices (数电票) under the Golden Tax Phase IV system (金税四期). General taxpayers issue exclusively through the National Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform (电子发票服务平台): invoices are transmitted in real time to the STA, carry a unique 20-digit electronic invoice number, are cleared against the taxpayer's VAT ledger before offsets are computed, and are verified on the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform (全国增值税发票查验平台). There is no physical fiscal stamp, but the platform acts as a real-time clearance engine; API integration (电子税务局接口) is mandatory for high-volume issuers, and abnormal invoices (异常扣税凭证), red-letter reversal invoices (红字发票) and cross-zone issuance are flagged automatically for risk control.

Export refund claims are risk-classified by the STA (Class I to IV); Class III and IV exporters are refunded only after manual verification. On fraud or error, the STA retroactively clawbacks the refunded VAT plus daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day from the refund date, and imposes an administrative penalty of 100%–500% of the defrauded amount under Article 66 of the Tax Collection Administration Law (2001, as amended). Criminal liability attaches under Article 204 of the Criminal Law (骗取出口退税罪) where the false refund exceeds RMB 50,000: up to 5 years imprisonment for substantial amounts, 5–10 years for large amounts (≥ RMB 500,000), and 10 years to life imprisonment with a fine of 1–5× the defrauded amount or confiscation of property for especially large amounts (≥ RMB 2.5 million). The Golden Tax IV abnormal-refund blocking mechanism triggers cross-agency review by STA, customs and SAFE whenever invoice, customs, FX settlement or logistics data do not fully reconcile; repeat offenders face suspension of export-refund eligibility for up to 3 years and placement on the serious-tax-violation public list with associated customs AEO sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is fully convertible under the current account for bona fide trade in goods and services, including cross-border payments arising from warehouse and fulfillment services. A Chinese WFOE can convert and remit trade-related receipts through designated banks upon submission of underlying contracts, invoices, tax evidence, and customs records. The capital account remains restricted: FDI capital injections, foreign debt, and external conversions require SAFE registration and the use of specially designated capital settlement accounts. Profit remittance is permitted only after annual audit, accrual of statutory reserves, and payment or withholding of income tax; banks must obtain evidence of the final tax clearance, board resolution, and audited financials prior to transferring funds to a foreign shareholder. For cross-strait flows involving Taiwan, the transactions are treated as foreign-exchange dealings; the New Taiwan Dollar is not a freely convertible onshore currency, and settlements must be denominated in CNY, USD, or another convertible currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender ratio for export proceeds under current SAFE rules. A WFOE may retain 100 percent of goods- and service-trade export proceeds in a foreign-currency account or settle them into RMB at will. However, all export receipts must be repatriated to a domestic account within the commercial payment term agreed with the buyer, normally not exceeding 180 days, and must be entered into SAFE's Trade Collection, Verification, and Monitoring System without unreconciled arrears. For processing and kitting arrangements, the WFOE must demonstrate that the value of exported finished goods corresponds to the declared processing fees and the imported components; any overdue export collections from Taiwan counterparties must be reported to the local branch of SAFE, with supporting evidence of collection efforts, contract terms, and customs declarations.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) serving as the delegated authority for foreign-exchange supervision, approval, and registration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary cross-border payment and treasury-optimization flows, Low for serving as the sole corporate treasury account. Offshore EMIs such as Airwallex are not licensed PRC deposit-taking institutions and cannot hold a WFOE's SAFE-registered capital settlement account or satisfy the statutory corporate account requirements. They may be used to collect international marketplace settlements, pay foreign suppliers, and execute FX conversion through licensed partners, but the WFOE must maintain a domestic bank account for tax payments, salaries, and SAFE verification. Legal constraints require full transparency of the underlying trade documents and the use of a PBOC-licensed payment institution in the settlement chain for onshore RMB activity.

The receiving bank will verify the SAFE Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate, enter the transaction into SAFE's digital registration system, and then credit the foreign-currency capital account. Subsequent conversion into RMB for lawful operational purposes must comply with the posted capital-account usage restrictions; funds may not be used for equity investment, borrowing, securities investment, or real-estate acquisition outside the permitted scope. For Taiwan-sourced investment, payment should be wired in CNY, USD, or another freely convertible currency; TWD-denominated remittances cannot be credited onshore without prior conversion through the Taiwanese bank and a suitable clearing route.Routine operating expenditures are cleared through the WFOE's RMB basic account using standard CNY transfers. For foreign-currency disbursements, including payments to Taiwan-based suppliers, subcontractors, or logistics partners, the WFOE will direct its bank to execute a SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer out of the designated foreign-currency operating account. For cross-border trade with Taiwan, the WFOE may alternatively use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) to settle invoiced amounts in CNY, thereby reducing currency conversion costs. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary cross-border payment and treasury-optimization flows, Low for serving as the sole corporate treasury account. Offshore EMIs such as Airwallex are not licensed PRC deposit-taking institutions and cannot hold a WFOE's SAFE-registered capital settlement account or satisfy the statutory corporate account requirements. They may be used to collect international marketplace settlements, pay foreign suppliers, and execute FX conversion through licensed partners, but the WFOE must maintain a domestic bank account for tax payments, salaries, and SAFE verification. Legal constraints require full transparency of the underlying trade documents and the use of a PBOC-licensed payment institution in the settlement chain for onshore RMB activity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous TP documentation (Local File/Master File) is mandatory where prior-year related-party transactions reach any of these RMB thresholds (SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42): tangible-asset transfers ≥ RMB 400 million; intangible-asset transfers ≥ RMB 100 million; financial-asset transfers ≥ RMB 100 million; related-party services ≥ RMB 100 million; Master File is required where prior-year aggregate related-party revenue exceeds RMB 2 billion. In addition, an annual related-party transaction reporting form (including G100000 and GB/TW schedules) must be attached to the annual EIT return regardless of the amounts.

Economic substance scope: mainland China does not apply the offshore ESR (Economic Substance Regulations) regime, but enforces the substance-over-form doctrine through: (i) general anti-avoidance rule (EIT Law Art. 47; Implementation Regulation Arts. 120–123) allowing re-characterization of any structure without reasonable commercial purpose; (ii) beneficial-ownership test for treaty relief (SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9, seven settled factors incl. actual operations, employment, decision-making and risk-bearing); (iii) place-of-effective-management classification of Chinese-tax-resident status; and (iv) the Special Tax Investigation and Adjustment rules requiring attribution of profits to functions performed, assets utilized and risks assumed. The WFOE must demonstrate physical premises, operational staff, actual kitting/fulfillment activity and bank/accounting records to preserve treaty benefits and resist a GAAR adjustment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fictitious Invoicing (虚开发票)

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Arts. 63/66: fine of 50%–500% of the evaded tax; Criminal Law Art. 201 (tax evasion): up to 7 years' imprisonment plus fines for the entity's responsible persons where the evaded amount is huge; Criminal Law Art. 205 (fictitious VAT invoices): up to life imprisonment and confiscation for particularly large amounts; deregistration of VAT qualifications and reputational debarment from e-commerce export programs.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, financial officer and actual de facto controllers face criminal liability (criminal fines and imprisonment); civil restitution to the tax authority for the state's loss; administrative exit bans under Tax Administration Law Art. 44 and Exit-Entry Administration Law Art. 12 (travel ban); disqualification as officers; in case of unit crime, the enterprise is fined and the 'directly responsible personnel' are prosecuted in person.
Shell Company / Letterbox Entity with No Substantive Operation

Statutory sanction: PRC Company Law (2023, effective 1 July 2024) Art. 23: piercing the veil where shareholder abuse of independence causes severe corporate damage or creditors' harm — joint and several personal liability; GAAR re-characterization under EIT Law Art. 47 with supplementary tax and compensatory interest at the PBOC benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points; market supervision deregistration and revoking of the WFOE license; TP 'top-up' adjustments to the Taiwan parent.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and de facto controllers jointly and unlimitedly liable to the tax authority and trade creditors; director liability for false financial reporting; added exit restrictions where the entity owes tax; criminal exposure if the shell is used to defraud tax refunds or cross-border settlement systems.
Anti-Money Laundering and Illegal Fund Settlement

Statutory sanction: PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law (effective 1 January 2025): institution-level fines up to RMB 5 million, alongside confiscation of illegal gains and suspension/license revocation; Criminal Law Art. 191 (money laundering): up to 10 years' imprisonment plus fines for individuals; failure to perform KYC/Odds reporting responsibilities triggers supervisory penalties and 'blacklist' designation.

Director personal liability: Senior management and compliance officers face personal administrative fines and prohibition orders; directors who knowingly participate are criminally liable with confiscation; issuance of asset-freeze orders and cross-border travel bans; the corporate veil may be pierced to pursue controlling shareholders for consequential damages.
Transfer Pricing Non-Disclosure and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: TP adjustments with compensatory interest at the PBOC benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points from the filing deadline (Implementation Regulation Art. 48); penalty for non-compliance with documentation: RMB 2,000–10,000 administratively, presumption of adjustment, and re-characterization of the transactions; exchange-of-information cross-checks under the Cross-Strait Agreement; in cases of fraudulent pricing, application of the 50%–500% evasion fine.

Director personal liability: Where fraudulent pricing is found, the directors responsible for signing and certifying the tax returns are exposed to criminal proceedings; the tax authority may seek civil recovery of the tax shortfall from the shareholders receiving the benefit; exit bans and property preservations apply pending payment; management may be disqualified from serving as directors of other Chinese enterprises.

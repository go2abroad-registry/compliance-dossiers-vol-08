Virtual offices and commercial mailboxes are not permissible for WFOE registration in China. The SAMR requires a physical, rentable office address that is verifiable and must be used for actual business operations. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under the Taiwan Company Act for a limited company; the share capital must be reasonably sufficient for the stated business scope. Before incorporation, the foreign founder must deposit the subscribed paid-up capital into a temporary escrow bank account opened in Taiwan under the proposed company name. The escrow bank must issue a capital balance certificate, and the capital remains blocked or escrowed until the Ministry of Economic Affairs issues the Certificate of Incorporation. Thereafter the escrow account is released to the company's authorized officers.

Funding & unlock path: No Civil Defense fire permit is required for office-only wholesale operations. Before occupying the registered office, obtain a fire safety compliance inspection or certificate from the local fire department if the floor area or occupancy classification so requires. File a workplace safety notification with the municipal labor inspection office and comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act by maintaining emergency evacuation plans, first aid measures, and safety data sheets for any small sample quantities of lithium carbonate. If the company later establishes a warehouse for battery materials, a separate Fire Safety Management Plan, hazardous goods storage approval, and Civil Defense-type fire permit must be obtained before operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box entity classification election and no standalone entity classification election is available for a limited company. The WFOE entity style is classified as a resident profit-seeking enterprise and taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. There is no hybrid-mismatch election; any hybrid mismatch adjustment would be applied under the anti-avoidance provisions of the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General taxable businesses have no turnover-based exemption threshold and must register before starting Taiwan business activity. A special TWD 480,000 annual Taiwan-source turnover threshold applies only to foreign electronic-service suppliers; once exceeded, the foreign seller must register for Taiwan business tax through the fiscal representative regime. Small-scale domestic businesses with average monthly sales below TWD 80,000 may qualify for simplified taxation, but this simplified status does not apply to a regular limited company carrying out battery-metal arbitrage.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 8 of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act, sales of goods that are actually exported from Taiwan and customs-cleared are zero-rated. Qualifying services rendered in Taiwan that are used outside Taiwan, or services connected with exported goods, are zero-rated only if supported by a written contract and foreign-exchange receipt evidencing that the consideration is paid by a foreign buyer. Mere procurement or arbitrage does not qualify unless physical export from Taiwan is completed and the same registered taxpayer appears as the exporter on the Commercial Invoice, the Customs Export Declaration, and the beneficiary of the foreign-exchange receipt. Creditable input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports may be offset or refunded after the supporting document package is reviewed. Importation of battery-grade lithium carbonate into Taiwan is not zero-rated; it is subject to 5% import business tax and customs duties, with input VAT recovery available to registered taxpayers under normal VAT principles.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) approved by Taiwan Customs through the Ministry of Finance Customs Administration Single Window
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving actual cross-border movement of the lithium carbonate powder
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the goods, quantity, unit value, and export destination
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate or bank inward-remittance settlement record, equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), showing the export proceeds were collected from the overseas buyer
  • Export sales contract, purchase order, or pro forma invoice linking the shipment and the foreign-exchange receipt to the zero-rated sale

Taiwan does not use a real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping model. Sellers of taxable goods or services must issue uniform invoices under Article 32 of the Business Tax Act. When E-Invoices are used, the seller must transmit invoice data to the Ministry of Finance Electronic Invoice Integration Service Platform within 48 hours through the E-Invoice Turnkey service or a certified E-Invoice API service provider. The platform controls invoice numbering, random-check code generation, and data validation; the National Taxation Administration matches seller and buyer data to detect under-reporting, late filing, and fraudulent invoices. Any failure to issue, transmit, or correctly report E-Invoice data is subject to the administrative penalties prescribed in the Business Tax Act.

The National Taxation Administration may audit export zero-rating claims retrospectively, ordinarily for five years, but longer where fraud or material misreporting is suspected. If the required Customs Export Declaration, transport document, Commercial Invoice, and foreign-exchange receipt are not complete or do not tie to the same counterparty, the zero-rated treatment is clawed back and output VAT is reassessed at the standard 5% rate with late interest under the Tax Collection Act. Administrative penalties under the Business Tax Act may be imposed up to five times the underpaid tax, and materially false export documentation, forged customs filings, or fraudulent refund claims may lead to criminal liability under the tax evasion provisions of the Tax Collection Act and the penalty provisions of the Customs Act. Failure to repatriate and settle export foreign exchange within 180 days without the approval of Taiwan's central bank also exposes the entity to fines and enforcement measures under the Management of Foreign Exchange Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is not freely convertible on the capital account. Current account trade items, such as import settlement for battery-grade lithium carbonate, commissions, freight, and related trade expenses, are substantially convertible after verification of genuine underlying transactional documents through a licensed PRC bank. Capital account inflows, including the WFOE's paid-in share capital, require SAFE-registered FDI project status, a bank-recorded foreign investment code, and strict compliance with the actual-use principle. Inbound equity funds may only enter through a designated FDI foreign exchange capital account and must be settled into RMB for verified business purposes; they cannot be used for equity investment, intercompany lending, securities purchases, or FX speculation. Profit remittance to the Taiwan parent is permitted after the annual statutory audit, final tax clearance, board resolution, audited financial statements, and SAFE/CRS-related reporting, but the bank must confirm no unpaid taxes, no unresolved SAFE penalty, and a clean trade verification classification. Retained earnings may be remitted in the original FDI currency through the current account channel, subject to genuineness checks and documentary support.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China has no fixed statutory percentage of export proceeds that must be converted into RMB; the original mandatory surrender regime was replaced by repatriation and retention controls. All goods-trade export revenues must be repatriated into China through an eligible bank account within the normal trade settlement cycle, and SAFE's trade monitoring system generates exception reports for any receivable outstanding beyond 180 days from the customs export declaration date unless an offshore retention has been separately approved. The bank verifies the receipt against the customs declaration, contract, and commercial invoice through the SAFE-linked goods-trade monitoring system. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may retain export receipts in USD or another foreign currency or voluntarily sell them to the settlement bank. Non-repatriation, prolonged outstanding balances, or mismatches between goods flow and funds flow can result in a downgrade of the enterprise verification classification and tighter documentary scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); the Taiwan parent's outbound investment approval rests with the Taiwan Ministry of Economic Affairs Investment Commission, but the WFOE's onshore FX regime is exclusively governed by PBOC and SAFE.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable for primary onboarding. Offshore digital EMIs licensed in Hong Kong, Singapore, the UK, or elsewhere are not licensed by PBOC and cannot provide SAFE-compliant RMB settlement accounts to a PRC-incorporated WFOE. They cannot perform FDI capital registration, cannot receive funds into a statutory capital account with the required foreign exchange purpose code, and cannot verify trade-in-goods documentation under SAFE rules. At most, an offshore EMI could support ancillary multi-currency receivables for the Taiwan parent outside mainland China, but such a rail cannot be used to bypass the WFOE's onshore banking obligations. Legal rating: restricted/unavailable for the WFOE treasury mandate.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan parent must first complete any applicable Taiwan MOEA Investment Commission outbound filing, and the WFOE must complete SAFE FDI registration through a licensed PRC bank to obtain the FDI enterprise code. The inbound remittance should be sent as a SWIFT MT103 with the payment purpose identified as FDI capital injection, referencing the WFOE's registration code and unique identifier. Funds must be credited only to the registered FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account and must not be commingled with trade receipts or operating collections. Each conversion from foreign exchange into RMB requires a bank-verified actual-use capital settlement declaration, and the resulting RMB must be applied within the WFOE's licensed business scope. A CPA capital verification report should be prepared for statutory filing and annual audit purposes, and any subsequent movement to operational accounts must be routed through the bank's SAFE-integrated settlement system.Payments to overseas lithium carbonate suppliers should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 for open-account settlement or pre-payment, through commercial letters of credit (MT700/MT707) for large documentary purchases, and through documentary collections (DP/DA) for established suppliers. The instructing bank must verify the import contract, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and customs declaration, and match the payment against the applicable SAFE goods-trade foreign-exchange transaction code and the enterprise verification classification. If the supplier accepts RMB, the payment should be routed through CIPS or cross-border RMB settlement to minimize FX conversion and the retained earnings exposure. Where goods enter through a bonded zone or free-trade area, import duty and VAT settlement should be handled separately in compliance with tax regulations.Onshore deliverable FX forward contracts with notional and maturity matched to signed lithium carbonate purchase contracts, executed through a licensed PRC bank under the SAFE real-demand principle.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) where there is an unambiguous underlying foreign-currency payables or trade-financing exposure linked to the battery metals procurement cycle.FX swaps between RMB and USD/EUR with commercial trade flows as the underlying transaction and no speculative open position.Premium-bearing FX option strategies bought by the WFOE for downside protection; selling uncovered option structures is restricted and not permitted for ordinary treasury hedging.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for a PRC WFOE because SAFE requires a genuine deliverable or physical settlement trade posture; any synthetic NDF-only arrangement would violate the actual-transaction principle and is not bankable onshore.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable for primary onboarding. Offshore digital EMIs licensed in Hong Kong, Singapore, the UK, or elsewhere are not licensed by PBOC and cannot provide SAFE-compliant RMB settlement accounts to a PRC-incorporated WFOE. They cannot perform FDI capital registration, cannot receive funds into a statutory capital account with the required foreign exchange purpose code, and cannot verify trade-in-goods documentation under SAFE rules. At most, an offshore EMI could support ancillary multi-currency receivables for the Taiwan parent outside mainland China, but such a rail cannot be used to bypass the WFOE's onshore banking obligations. Legal rating: restricted/unavailable for the WFOE treasury mandate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Annual related-party transactions in a single year exceeding RMB 100 million for related-party tangible property transactions (goods), or RMB 40 million for related-party financial transactions (interest), or RMB 10 million for other related-party transactions (services, intangibles, royalties) trigger mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; entities with related-party controlled transactions exceeding RMB 100 million in total and with revenue above the threshold must also file Form A109000 and prepare contemporaneous documentation within the statutory period (before May 31 of the following year).

Economic substance scope: China applies the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) under Article 47 of the EIT Law and the 'beneficial owner' / substance-over-form doctrine under SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9; the WFOE must have personnel, offices, decision-making functions, and inventory risk in China; a mere procurement-conduit or paper entity with no CIGA will be recharacterized, its related-party invoices disregarded, and the income reallocated to the Taiwan principal under the substance-over-form doctrine; the Economic Substance requirement is integrated with the BEPS Action Plan and the China Anti-Avoidance framework, though Taiwan's separate ESR regime is not directly applicable to a China WFOE.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Shell Company / General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: Article 41 and Article 47 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law empower the tax authority to make transfer pricing adjustments and recharacterize the whole arrangement; Article 63 of the TACL imposes a fine of 50%–500% of the underpaid tax and late surcharge; the immediate assessment may include 100% of the adjusted tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Under the PRC Company Law and the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil, the sole shareholder of a one-person WFOE bears unlimited joint liability for the company's tax debts if the shareholder fails to prove the corporate assets are separate from personal assets; the legal representative ('法定代表') may be blacklisted and subject to a travel ban (exit restriction) under the 'Cooperative Punishment Memorandum for Tax-Related Offenses' if the unpaid tax exceeds the threshold; criminal liability for tax evasion carries up to 7 years' imprisonment, and where fraudulent fake invoices (虚开增值税专用发票) are used, the legal representative may be imprisoned for up to 10 years or life, with seizure of personal assets for restitution.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Deficiency and Wrong Arm's Length Method

Statutory sanction: Articles 44 and 48 of the EIT Law and SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 / 2017 No. 6: the tax authority may use unspecified methods (e.g. hidden reserves or profit split) to adjust taxable income, apply a 5-year look-back period, impose a 5-year onward adjustment on subsequent years, and levy late interest; failure to produce documentation on demand after notice leads to a presumption of underpayment and a fine under Article 44 of TACL of up to RMB 50,000 plus a surcharge; the adjusted tax amount may be subject to a 50% penalty if deemed willful.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, financial director, and tax agent may be jointly and severally liable for the assessed TP adjustment if they signed inaccurate contemporaneous documentation knowing it to be false; the court may pierce the veil where personal use of company funds triggered the adjustment or where the under-capitalized WFOE is operated as an instrument of the Taiwan parent; directors may face disqualification from serving as legal representatives of any enterprise in China and a travel ban until the assessment is settled.
Withholding Tax Evasion and DTA/FATCA/CRS Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Article 37 of the EIT Law requires withholding; under Article 69 of TACL the withholding agent who fails to withhold is liable for a fine equal to 50%–300% of the unwithheld tax; if the WFOE failed to remit withheld tax within the statutory time limit, a daily late surcharge of 0.05% applies; failure to report under the Cross-Border Information Reporting and CRS also triggers administrative penalties of RMB 10,000–100,000 per instance; the non-resident shareholder may additionally be assessed directly and blacklisted.

Director personal liability: Directors who instructed payment without WHT are personally accountable under Article 20 of TACL as the persons in charge of the withholding agent, with joint liability for the fine and tax; the legal representative's personal assets may be frozen, bank accounts in China may be placed under control, and an exit ban will be issued until the full tax, penalty, and surcharge are paid; criminal liability for evasion of withheld tax under Article 203 of the PRC Criminal Law exposes the director to imprisonment for up to 7 years for failing to pay the withheld tax after obtaining it, plus restitution of the misappropriated amounts.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the Taiwan Company Act, there is no mandatory minimum paid-in capital for a limited liability company as a general matter. However, because the entity is an Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under ISIC 6619, the FSC imposes a special minimum paid-in capital requirement for licensed EMI operations, currently fixed at NT$100 million or such higher amount as the FSC prescribes in the establishment permission. This capital must be fully subscribed and fully paid in cash before the FSC operating license is granted. The funds must be placed in a blocked escrow account at a Taiwan bank approved by the FSC, and no portion may be released or withdrawn without the prior written consent of the FSC. A Taiwan-licensed certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report, and the escrow certificate and verification report must accompany the MOEA registration filing.

No industrial Civil Defense fire permit, structural load certificate, or chemical storage approval is applicable because no physical manufacturing, chemical processing, or warehouse operations are authorized under the FSC license.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taiwan (Republic of China), acting through its regional National Taxation Bureaus; online tax services are delivered via the 'Ministry of Finance Tax Filing and Payment Service Network' (tax.nat.gov.tw) and the 'Integrated Service Platform for Electronic Invoices' (einvoice.nat.gov.tw).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must first complete incorporation under the Company Act of Taiwan with the Ministry of Economic Affairs and obtain its company registration certificate. Step 2: No later than 15 days after the date of commencement of business, the company must file the statutory Business Tax Registration Application (Application for Establishment Registration as a Taxable Business Person) with the local National Taxation Bureau where the registered head office is located. Step 3: Supporting documentation must include the incorporation certificate, memorandum and articles of association, legal representative's identification, lease or ownership title of the Taiwan business premises, and the bank account opening certificate for the Taiwan operating account. Step 4: Upon successful verification, the National Taxation Bureau will issue a Tax Registration Certificate and assign the eight-digit Uniform Invoice Number as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN); the same number is used for Business Tax, profit-seeking enterprise income tax, and customs declarations. Step 5: The company must then register with the e-invoicing platform and apply for a Uniform Invoice issuance entitlement. Step 6: For the foreign non-resident parent or any non-resident service supplier with no Taiwan branch but deriving Taiwan-sourced taxable revenue, a separate Foreign Enterprise Tax Registration must be filed at the same Bureau, with an appointed Taiwan tax agent, a legalized/apostilled certificate of foreign registration, and a Taiwanese bank account for payment purposes; this non-resident TIN is used for reverse-charge Business Tax returns and withholding tax statements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for registration; every enterprise engaged in Taiwan sales of goods or services must register before commencement. Monthly turnover above NT$200,000 in the prescribed preceding period triggers the mandatory issuance of Uniform Invoices and standard monthly VAT filing; turnover at or below NT$200,000 may be treated under the small-scale business taxpayer regime assessed at the special rate of 1% for goods sellers or 2% for service providers, as prescribed by the Ministry of Finance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Articles 7 and 8 of the Value-Added and Non-Value-Added Business Tax Act of Taiwan, 0% VAT applies to (i) physical export of goods with customs clearance, (ii) export services, including qualifying offshore digital services and fintech payment processing, supplied to foreign recipients where the service is performed outside Taiwan or is not used within Taiwan, (iii) sales to foreign embassies and consulates, and (iv) international transport. For an electronic money institution issuing virtual IBANs and managing digital wallet reserves, zero-rating is available only if the service recipient is a foreign enterprise or individual, the service is consumed outside Taiwan, the consideration is paid in a foreign currency from abroad, and the proceeds are repatriated to and settled in a Taiwanese bank account.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic clearing-line record generated by the Taiwan Customs 'CPT Export Declaration System')
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (negotiable transport document issued to the Taiwan exporter incorporating the export declaration number)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (in English or bilingual; quantities, unit prices, ISIC/HS classification, and total amount must reconcile with the customs export declaration)
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by an authorized foreign-exchange bank in Taiwan, evidencing that the export proceeds were received onshore within the prescribed repatriation window

Taiwan statutory e-invoicing is mandatory for all Business Tax taxpayers liable to issue Uniform Invoices. The taxpayer must connect to the Ministry of Finance's 'Integrated Service Platform for Electronic Invoices' via the Uniform Invoice Turnkey API (the statutory XML/EDI interface) or, for over-the-counter retail activity, use the Cloud E-Invoice interface. Cloud e-invoices are cleared and timestamped in real time at issuance; B2B Turnkey invoices must be submitted within 48 hours of issue. The platform performs automatic line-item cross-checking with monthly VAT filings and applies a random-number anti-fraud stamp to each invoice. Failure to clear electronically renders the invoice invalid for input tax credit deduction and exposes the issuer to administrative penalties under the Business Tax Act.

The National Taxation Bureau will audit zero-rating claims by matching the Customs Export Declaration, the bill of lading, the commercial invoice, and the bank's FIRC. If the foreign exchange is not received within the statutory 180-day deadline or the export justification fails, the claimed zero rate is reversed and the outstanding 5% Business Tax is assessed, plus late-payment interest. For fraudulent misrepresentations, Article 51 of the Business Tax Act imposes a fine of 5 to 15 times the evaded tax and may suspend the taxpayer's invoice-issuing rights; fabricating export documents can attract criminal liability under the Tax Collection Law and the Customs Anti-Smuggling Act. Assessments can be reopened within seven years where the statutory documentation bundle is incomplete or inconsistent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan dollar is generally convertible for bona fide current-account transactions, including trade payments, service fees, salaries, taxes, dividends, and other documented operating payments. Capital-account transactions, inward investment, outward investment, intercompany loans, guarantees, acquisitions, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds are subject to the Foreign Exchange Control Act, CBC reporting or approval requirements, bank documentary review, and applicable company-law or investment-review procedures. A foreign-invested Taiwan company may generally remit dividends after audited financial statements, shareholder approval, tax clearance or withholding compliance, and satisfaction of capital-maintenance and creditor-protection requirements. Principal and interest on approved or properly documented shareholder loans may be remitted subject to loan registration, tax withholding, transfer-pricing support, and bank review. There is no general prohibition on profit repatriation, but banks may delay or reject transfers lacking audited accounts, board or shareholder resolutions, tax evidence, beneficial-owner information, or a clear source-and-purpose trail. An electronic-payment or stored-value business may face additional safeguarding, segregation, customer-funds, liquidity, and regulatory-capital restrictions that limit the use of customer funds for dividend or treasury purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan does not generally impose a universal mandatory surrender ratio or a fixed statutory conversion deadline requiring exporters to convert all export receipts into New Taiwan dollars. Export receipts may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts or sold through authorized banks, subject to documentary evidence, transaction-purpose classification, anti-money-laundering review, and CBC reporting requirements. The exporter should retain customs declarations, invoices, bills of lading, contracts, collection records, and bank advices. Foreign-exchange settlement must be processed through authorized banks, and annual or transaction-based limits, declarations, or CBC approval may apply depending on the resident status, transaction type, cumulative amount, and whether the transaction is a capital-account item. A Taiwan company should not assume that export proceeds can be netted against imports, intercompany charges, or shareholder payments without bank confirmation and supporting documentation. If the relevant exporter is a Mainland China entity, PRC SAFE rules, including trade-receipt verification and any applicable conversion or repatriation requirements, must be assessed separately.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), with the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) as the principal financial-sector and electronic-payment regulator. The Taiwan jurisdiction is treated separately from the Mainland China foreign-exchange regime; Mainland China SAFE rules apply only to a Mainland China entity, Mainland bank account, or Mainland transaction leg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium for a licensed Taiwan operator and Low for an offshore digital EMI acting directly in Taiwan. Taiwan electronic payment institutions and electronic stored-value institutions must obtain the relevant FSC authorization and comply with the Electronic Payment Institutions Act, implementing regulations, AML and counter-terrorist-financing requirements, customer-fund safeguarding, operational-risk, information-security, outsourcing, reporting, and consumer-protection rules. A virtual IBAN or wallet product does not independently create a right to conduct Taiwan-regulated payment, stored-value, remittance, foreign-exchange, or deposit-taking activities. An offshore EMI may provide services only within the scope permitted by its home licence and Taiwan law; marketing to Taiwan residents, maintaining local customer balances, onboarding Taiwan merchants, or routing Taiwan payment flows may trigger Taiwan licensing, local-presence, agent, data, tax, and AML requirements. SEPA Credit Transfer is not a domestic Taiwan clearing rail and must be accessed through a correspondent bank or an appropriately licensed payment institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% corporate income tax on taxable income; an additional 5% tax applies to undistributed current-year earnings, subject to statutory exclusions and reinvestment rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a non-resident individual or foreign enterprise, subject to applicable statutory relief. Dividends paid to Taiwan-resident shareholders are generally subject to domestic withholding or imputation rules according to shareholder status.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year company, the annual return and tax payment are generally due from 1 May through 31 May of the following year. For a non-calendar fiscal year, filing is generally due within five months after fiscal year-end. Provisional filing for a calendar-year company is generally due from 1 September through 30 September and is based on the preceding year's assessed tax or current-period computation, subject to statutory exemptions.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, file and pay during 1 September through 30 September. The provisional amount is generally 50% of the preceding year's assessed corporate income tax, or an amount computed under the current-period statutory method where elected or required.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For payments to non-residents, withholding is generally paid within ten days after the withholding event, subject to the specific statutory payment rule. Annual withholding statements are generally issued and filed by the statutory January deadline following the calendar year.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unlicensed electronic-money, payment, or stored-value activity

Statutory sanction: Regulatory orders, business suspension or revocation, disgorgement, administrative fines under applicable banking, electronic-payment, money-laundering-control, and financial-supervision laws, and possible criminal prosecution. Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment, tax-evasion penalties, interest, compulsory collection, and criminal sanctions under the Income Tax Act. Serious intentional evasion can result in imprisonment and substantial criminal fines, with exact exposure depending on amount, method, repetition, and cooperation.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors, managers, accountants, and agents may be prosecuted personally, ordered to provide civil restitution, subjected to asset seizure and travel restrictions where authorized, and imprisoned. Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unpaid withholding tax from the withholding agent, interest, administrative penalties, statement-filing penalties, and potential criminal exposure for intentional fraudulent assistance.

Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Taiwan Ministry of Finance, administered through the competent National Taxation Bureau and its branch offices; principal electronic tax portal: Ministry of Finance eTax Portal (etax.nat.gov.tw). Electronic invoice administration is supported through the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice Platform.

        
E-government filing portal
The proposed entity must first be legally registered in Taiwan with the Ministry of Economic Affairs or the competent company-registration authority. A foreign investor must complete foreign-investment approval where applicable, reserve or register the company name and business scope, establish the registered office, appoint responsible persons, and provide corporate constitutional documents, ownership information, identification documents, powers of attorney, and certified Chinese translations where required. After company registration, the entity must apply for tax registration with the competent National Taxation Bureau before commencing taxable business. The authority issues an eight-digit Unified Business Number (統一編號), which functions as the principal corporate tax identifier and VAT/business-tax identifier. The entity must separately register or activate electronic invoice capability, obtain or arrange an approved electronic-invoice interface, and register relevant bank and customs information. A foreign company carrying on business through a Taiwan branch follows a branch-registration process and receives a Unified Business Number; a foreign company without a Taiwan fixed place of business may require withholding, VAT agent, or specific tax registration depending on the transaction. The payload describes a China WFOE but identifies Taiwan as the tax jurisdiction; a mainland China WFOE is not a Taiwan legal form and cannot use Taiwan registration merely because the parent country is China.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
Taiwan has no general United States-style check-the-box election and no elective pass-through classification for an ordinary Taiwan limited company. A Taiwan subsidiary is taxed as a separate resident company on Taiwan-taxable income and is generally subject to Taiwan corporate income tax. A foreign company branch is taxed under branch and permanent-establishment rules rather than through an elective transparent classification. Partnerships and certain contractual arrangements may have different allocation or withholding consequences, but they do not provide a general elective hybrid-mismatch regime. Taiwan does not provide a broad statutory election allowing an ordinary company to disregard its separate legal identity for income-tax purposes. Anti-avoidance, transfer-pricing, controlled-foreign-company, beneficial-ownership, and hybrid-instrument rules may override the legal form.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
5%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
An ordinary Taiwan company must apply for business-tax registration before commencing taxable business; there is no general turnover threshold that safely exempts an ordinary company from registration. NT$200,000 of monthly sales is the principal small-business threshold relevant to special assessment and small-business treatment, but it should not be treated as a general registration exemption for a company conducting organized commercial operations. Foreign enterprises with a Taiwan fixed place of business, agency, warehouse, personnel, or other taxable nexus may be required to register or appoint a tax agent. VAT returns are generally filed bimonthly, and taxable sales, zero-rated sales, input tax, and supporting documents must be reconciled for each filing period.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods exported from Taiwan may generally qualify for 0% VAT when the export is genuine, the goods leave Taiwan under customs control, the exporter is the seller or properly documented principal, and the export declaration and commercial records support the transaction. Services may qualify for zero-rating when supplied to a foreign customer and used outside Taiwan, subject to the statutory service category, place-of-use analysis, contractual evidence, payment evidence, and proof that the service is not effectively consumed in Taiwan. Offshore trading and re-invoicing do not automatically qualify for zero-rating: the taxpayer must establish the actual supply chain, contractual principal, title and risk transfer, customs status, invoice flow, consideration, and absence of a Taiwan domestic sale. Input VAT refunds or credits require valid purchase invoices, customs records, payment evidence, proper VAT returns, and reconciliation of output and input tax. Related-party re-invoicing must be supported by transfer-pricing and substance documentation and cannot create zero-rating solely through invoice routing.

Taiwan electronic invoices are administered through the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice Platform or approved service providers. Businesses subject to electronic-invoice requirements must issue compliant electronic invoices, transmit required invoice data to the tax authority platform within the prescribed statutory or technical time limits, preserve original electronic records, and maintain buyer, tax, amount, item, and authentication data. Taiwan does not operate a universal Latin-American-style real-time pre-clearance model for every invoice: issuance and platform transmission requirements depend on taxpayer category, transaction type, and applicable implementation rules. B2B transactions may use standardized electronic invoice files, certificates, approved interfaces, and buyer transmission procedures. A fiscal stamp is not generally applied to VAT invoices; electronic authentication, platform transmission, serial-number controls, and audit trails provide the principal digital controls. Paper invoices remain permissible only where legally allowed or where an applicable exception applies.

      
The taxpayer must retain customs, commercial, banking, accounting, contractual, logistics, and electronic-invoice records sufficient to prove the export and the foreign use of services. The National Taxation Bureau may deny zero-rating or input VAT refunds where the export is fictitious, the goods did not leave Taiwan, the service was used in Taiwan, the invoice chain is inconsistent, payment evidence is absent, related-party pricing lacks substance, or the customs and accounting records do not reconcile. Disallowed zero-rating may result in assessment of the unpaid 5% VAT, statutory interest, late-payment surcharges, repayment or clawback of refunds, administrative fines for inaccurate or omitted reporting, invoice-related penalties, and potential criminal exposure for tax evasion, false documents, or fraudulent refund claims. The six-month, approximately 180-day foreign-exchange collection or settlement period is a compliance benchmark for export proceeds subject to applicable foreign-exchange rules; it is not a universal rule that independently converts every offshore service into a taxable or non-taxable transaction. Failure to collect, declare, or properly substantiate proceeds may trigger banking, foreign-exchange, customs, VAT, and income-tax scrutiny.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Taiwan Companies Act for a limited liability company. However, for a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, the foreign investor must subscribe and pay the initial capital into a Taiwan bank account before the incorporation application is submitted, with a Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate and a bank balance/passbook certificate used as capital verification evidence. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online filing through the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax Bureau) platform accessible via www.chinatax.gov.cn and, for VAT invoicing, the STA National Electronic Invoice Service Platform

E-government filing portal

The PRC-incorporated WFOE obtains a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation at incorporation; the USCC is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all PRC taxes. Within 30 days of business license issuance, the WFOE must complete initial tax registration with its competent county-level STA tax bureau through the E-Tax Bureau portal or in-person counter, submitting the SAMR business license, articles of association, legal representative identification, registered address proof, bank account details, and appointed tax administrator information. After registration, the tax bureau records the taxpayer, applies the entity-level corporate income tax classification and VAT status, and activates the electronic invoice and tax-control account. Foreign non-resident shareholders do not obtain a separate Chinese TIN solely as equity holders; a non-resident enterprise earning Chinese-source income without a permanent establishment is handled through the WFOE's withholding registration and withholding payment vouchers. For export activities, the WFOE must also complete the import/export enterprise customs registration with China Customs, receiving a ten-digit Customs Registration Code linked to the USCC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The PRC requires tax registration for every incorporated enterprise regardless of turnover; the statutory economic turnover threshold that triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer status (as opposed to simplified small-scale taxpayer treatment) is RMB 5,000,000 (CNY 5 million) in annual taxable sales for wholesale enterprises. The WFOE's cement clinker wholesale and exporting activity is expected to exceed this threshold, requiring general VAT taxpayer registration and application of the standard VAT rate on domestic taxable supplies while export sales are zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT law, goods physically exported from China by a resident general VAT taxpayer are zero-rated when sold to an overseas buyer and leave PRC customs territory under customs supervision. The WFOE will not charge the 13% output VAT on the export sale of bulk Portland cement clinker to Taiwan and may claim a refund of creditable input VAT attributable to that export. The zero-rating claim is substantiated by the statutory customs export declaration, ocean bill of lading evidencing carriage from a PRC port to Taiwan, commercial documentation, and collection of export proceeds from the Taiwan buyer via the WFOE's settlement bank. Qualifying offshore digital services are outside the business scope; only the goods leg qualifies for zero-rating.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration approved by China Customs, bearing the customs declaration number, commercial invoice values, description of goods as Portland cement clinker, and port of export
  • Ocean or port-to-port Bill of Lading issued by the carrier evidencing shipment from the PRC export port to the Taiwan discharge port
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract between CN_WFOE_LTD and the Taiwan buyer
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt record or export proceeds collection note showing receipt of payment through SWIFT LC or TT wire transfer from Taiwan
  • Export VAT refund/exemption electronic declaration filed through the E-Tax Bureau STA export rebate module linked to the customs export declaration

Mandatory use of fully digitalized electronic invoices (Digital E-Invoices) issued through the STA National Electronic Invoice Service Platform or provincial E-Tax Bureau interfaces. VAT invoices must contain the national uniform invoice format, the seller and buyer Unified Social Credit Codes, and an STA-issued tax control code; invoice issuance data is transmitted in real time to the STA for cross-verification with China Customs export declarations. High-volume taxpayers may integrate through system-to-system/API channels. Paper invoices remain permitted only where digitally authorized but must be produced through an STA tax-control UKey and reported to the STA. Anti-fraud validation is performed through the national VAT invoice inspection and digital tax-control stamping system.

The STA and China Customs jointly cross-match export declarations, export VAT refund claims, logistics data, and the 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation record. If the zero-rated export lacks required documentation, fails to evidence actual export, misses the FX repatriation deadline, or is found fraudulent, the STA will claw back the VAT refund or zero-rating benefit, charge late payment surcharge from the refund date, and impose administrative penalties from 50% to 500% of the tax involved. Deliberate misdeclaration or counterfeit documentation triggers criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund under the PRC Criminal Law, punishable by imprisonment up to life imprisonment and confiscation of property.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

New Taiwan Dollar is fully convertible for trade and current account transactions, subject to a foreign-exchange declaration through an Authorized Foreign Exchange Bank for each remittance above US$500,000. Capital account transactions, including direct equity investment, inter-company loans, and profit repatriation, are convertible but subject to CBC monitoring and documentation; for a mainland-China parent, the Taiwan leg additionally requires prior approval from the Investment Commission, MOEA. There is no surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds, and exporters may retain and operate foreign-currency deposit accounts freely. Profit remittance from the Taiwan subsidiary requires audited financial statements, board and shareholder resolutions, tax clearance, and a valid foreign-exchange declaration proving the original inward investment. Practical hurdles are not convertibility itself but documentary complexity: banks demand the MOEA approval letter, a credible source-of-wealth file, and a clear capital verification trail before executing any significant cross-border payment. TWD liquidity is deep in onshore money markets, but banks will monitor same-day mega wires for AML and impose intraday limits until the account relationship matures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters must collect foreign currency through an Authorized Foreign Exchange Bank, file a Foreign Exchange Declaration (Form EX) for each transaction, and retain underlying trade documentation, including commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and customs export declaration, for verification. The proceeds may remain 100 percent in a foreign-currency deposit account or be converted to TWD at the discretion of the exporter; no forced conversion timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), Foreign Exchange Department; for the mainland China outbound leg, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China is the co-regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

LOW. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, and similar non-bank payment providers are legally barred from servicing corporate entities in Taiwan as primary multi-currency accounts because the Taiwan Banking Act limits deposit-taking and foreign-exchange business to licensed banks. There is no EMI passporting regime in Taiwan; a non-bank company accepting customer deposits or executing payments without a banking license commits a regulatory offense. Only FSC-licensed digital banks (LINE Bank, Rakuten International Commercial Bank, Next Commercial Bank) can offer limited digital onboarding, but they still must comply with the same CBC foreign-exchange declaration and capital controls, and none currently provides the full suite of commercial LC, capital escrow, and cross-strait RMB settlement required for a bulk-cement wholesale treasury operation. Therefore, offshore EMIs should be used only for marginal travel or payroll expenses, never as the primary clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Before any wire, the mainland parent must complete NDRC and SAFE outbound investment registrations and obtain a Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate for Outbound Investment. The Taiwan entity must hold an Investment Commission (MOEA) approval letter naming the exact capital amount and the permissible paid-in capital schedule. The parent then instructs its bank to remit via SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary Capital Escrow Account at an Authorized Foreign Exchange Bank in Taiwan, with the beneficiary account title matching the approved investment and the CBC remittance purpose code Direct Investment - Equity Contribution. The receiving bank will generate an Inward Remittance Notification and a Foreign Exchange Declaration form on behalf of the Taiwan entity. After funds are received, a Taiwan CPA issues a capital verification report confirming paid-in capital, and the entity files the capital increase with the central company registry. No offsetting, netting, or same-day intra-group movement of funds should be executed while the capital is pending verification, and the remittance must be for the exact approved amount to avoid rejection or freezing.Use SWIFT MT103 for small or urgent TT settlements for bulk cement and clinker purchases; use irrevocable commercial letters of credit under UCP600 for large shipments where title documents and vessel-loading evidence are critical; and use documentary collections (DP/DA) for medium-risk counterparties with established payment history. For cross-strait RMB settlement, payments can be cleared through the RMB clearing mechanism operated by Bank of China Taipei Branch, while USD and TWD settlement continue through SWIFT. Every disbursement must be supported by the commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, certificate of origin, inspection or weight certificate, and a true trade-purpose code; the bank will screen the beneficiary, vessel, and underlying transaction against sanctions lists before release of funds.Deliverable outright forward contracts for USD/TWD and RMB/TWD with genuine trade or investment exposure and tenors matched to projected cash flowsCross-currency swaps between TWD and USD for long-term capital and profit-remittance hedging, provided the underlying exposure is documented and approved by the CBC-authorized bankPlain vanilla FX options on USD/TWD and RMB/TWD for hedging forecasted trade flows, subject to real-exposure verificationNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on offshore currencies other than TWD, such as USD/CNY, for mainland China trade exposures; onshore TWD NDFs are not permitted as a domestic retail hedging instrumentFX hedging instruments are permitted only when paired with underlying trade, equity, or dividend flows; speculative or naked derivative positions are prohibited under CBC practice and internal bank suitability policies

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: LOW. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, and similar non-bank payment providers are legally barred from servicing corporate entities in Taiwan as primary multi-currency accounts because the Taiwan Banking Act limits deposit-taking and foreign-exchange business to licensed banks. There is no EMI passporting regime in Taiwan; a non-bank company accepting customer deposits or executing payments without a banking license commits a regulatory offense. Only FSC-licensed digital banks (LINE Bank, Rakuten International Commercial Bank, Next Commercial Bank) can offer limited digital onboarding, but they still must comply with the same CBC foreign-exchange declaration and capital controls, and none currently provides the full suite of commercial LC, capital escrow, and cross-strait RMB settlement required for a bulk-cement wholesale treasury operation. Therefore, offshore EMIs should be used only for marginal travel or payroll expenses, never as the primary clearing rail.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taiwan (National Taxation Administration); online business tax registration via General Tax Alternatives Platform (GTP) and e-invoice service at https://www.einvoice.nat.gov.tw and https://www.mof.gov.tw

E-government filing portal

The foreign-invested Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must obtain a Taiwan Uniform Business Number (UBN or BAN) by applying to the Taipei National Taxation Administration or the regional National Taxation Bureau after incorporation registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA). Required documents include the incorporated charter, foreign investment approval under the Foreign Investment Act, business address proof, director/shareholder list, and bank account opening certificate. For a newly established subsidiary, the UBN is issued on-line through the bicameral 'Commercial Registration and Tax Registration Joint Application' process; for a foreign branch or unregistered foreign legal person, application is made on Form 8902 with the local National Taxation Bureau. The UBN is the unified tax ID for both corporate income tax and business VAT purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not offer a check-the-box pass-through election for limited companies; profit-seeking enterprises are taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. The JV may elect among corporate forms (limited company vs. company limited by shares) at incorporation, and may apply for particular tax preference regimes (e.g., preferential investment allowances, research and development credits, and the foreign-sourced income exemption) under the Statute for Upscaling Industrial Innovation. No hybrid mismatch election is available; partnerships and sole proprietors are pass-through taxpayers only.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NT$40,000 average monthly taxable sales for service businesses (the relevant threshold for BPO/call-center services); goods businesses are generally required to register at NT$80,000 monthly sales. Social security and certain specified service categories may be exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act, zero-rating applies to export goods and to services supplied to foreign enterprises or foreign purchasers provided the services are consumed or effectively used outside Taiwan. For an offshore BPO call center, zero-rating is available if the multilingual helpdesk and outbound telesales services are performed in Taiwan but the contractual counterparty is abroad, payment is received in foreign currency, and the service deliverables/premium phone numbers/promotional activities are not utilized by a Taiwan domestic establishment.

  • Customs Electronic Export Declaration (N5200/N5201) for any physical exports
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Service Agreement or Contract
  • Unified Invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Bank Advance or Central Bank Foreign Exchange transaction record)
  • Proof of actual offshore service performance (call logs, CRM records, CSD records)

Taiwan has a mandatory electronic invoice (e-invoice) regime for B2B and most B2C transactions; the registered business must adopt Uniform Invoice sequence Numbering, encrypt and transmit the full e-invoice dataset to the Ministry of Finance's e-invoice Turnkey system within 48 hours of issuance (real-time clearance for designated industries uses the e-invoice service API). Digital stamping is applied through the Ministry's assigned invoice prefix and synthetic identification; there is no physical fiscal box, but registered taxpayers must use approved transmitting software and maintain the e-invoice response code for audit tracking.

The National Taxation Bureau may deny zero-rating or assess VAT at 5% plus daily interest and a shortfall penalty of up to twice the under-collected tax where export documentation is missing, FX is not repatriated within the deadline, or the taxpayer fails to prove offshore use; deliberate fraud involving false export documentation or sham offshore transactions triggers criminal tax evasion liability under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act with imprisonment and additional civil penalties, and the tax authority may retroactively claw back zero-rated input tax credits for the relevant period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China observes full current-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment and foreign-currency loans, remain subject to case-by-case SAFE registration and verification. Profit remittance by a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture requires completion of annual statutory audit, full settlement of applicable corporate income tax, and submission of the Tax Clearance Certificate and audited financial statements to the designated foreign-exchange bank. Undistributed profits may be freely repatriated after tax payment, without additional prudence or quota restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular No. 30 (2012) and prevailing regulations, current-account receipts, including service-trade export proceeds from BPO call-center operations, must be repatriated into China within 180 calendar days from the transaction or receipt date. No mandatory conversion ratio applies to service-trade proceeds; enterprises may either retain the foreign currency in a designated current-account Foreign Exchange Account or voluntarily convert into RMB at any time. Mandatory conversion ratios are effectively 0% for service exports, unlike certain goods-export sectors where periodic adjustments may be issued under macro-prudential policy.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise B2B, Airwallex, Currenxie) are rated LOW for primary corporate settlement accounts and capital-escrow functions, as they are not licensed deposit-taking banks in China and cannot satisfy SAFE capital-repatriation or FDI registration obligations. Their viability is MEDIUM-to-LOW as ancillary liquidity rails for inbound client payments or outbound subcontractor disbursements, contingent on integration with a licensed onshore Chinese clearing bank and strict respect for per-transaction FX limits. Direct receipt of export service proceeds into an offshore EMI and later repatriation is generally non-compliant with SAFE's mandatory repatriation rule.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign-direct-investment capital for the JV must be routed through a designated SAFE-approved 'Capital Account' (外汇资本金账户) at an onshore bank. The sending overseas bank should transmit a SWIFT MT103 wire with a clear payment narrative: 'FDI capital injection under JV contract [Reference No.] – SAFE FIRS registration [Code].' The Chinese receiving bank will verify the SAFE Foreign-Investment Information Reporting System (FIRS) record and issue a wire-voucher allowing conversion to RMB under the CNY purchase purpose code '1100' (Foreign Direct Investment). Funds cannot be used for prohibited sectors and must be applied toward the registered business purpose, subject to SAFE post-verification.Outbound disbursements for BPO operational spending (e.g., subcontractor fees, cloud telephony software licensing, international consulting) are cleared via SWIFT MT103 for direct-bank transfers or through a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) where a tangible goods component exists. RMB-denominated disbursements to entities in participating countries use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) with SWIFT-compatible narrative. All outbound payment applications must attach the underlying service contract, invoice, and tax registration documents; payments are tagged with SAFE current-account purpose codes (e.g., '2101' for service charges, '2200' for telecommunication and BPO services). Annual settlement return must be filed within 5 months after the fiscal year end; for a calendar-year taxpayer the deadline is 31 May of the following year. Provisional tax prepayment for the current fiscal year is generally due in September.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$200,000,000

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not maintain an offshore Economic Substance Regulations regime. However, treaty benefit claims and transfer-pricing positions require real economic substance; a JV that lacks employees, premises, management decisions, telecommunications infrastructure, and risk absorption in Taiwan will be treated as a shell, denied DTA benefits, and exposed to recharacterization under anti-abuse and CFC rules. A remote BPO call center must demonstrate substantive Taiwan-based operations despite using cross-border digital service delivery.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Understatement of Income

Statutory sanction: Additional tax of up to 3 times the evaded amount under Article 110 of the Income Tax Act and the Tax Collection Act, plus monthly late-payment interest and administrative fines; criminal prosecution may follow.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and representatives face imprisonment up to five years or detention, fines of NT$60,000 to NT$600,000, and personal asset collection after corporate veil piercing.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make arm's-length adjustments, reconstruct taxable income, deny deductions, and levy an additional tax penalty of 0.5 to 1 times the adjusted tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false related-party declarations are jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax and may face criminal liability for intentional false documentation.
Shell Company / Abuse of DTA Benefits

Statutory sanction: Beneficial ownership denial, recapture of the full 21% dividend withholding tax, back taxes, penalties, disallowance of interest and royalty deductions, and refusal of treaty relief.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced; directors become personally liable for unpaid withholding taxes and civil restitution to creditors, and travel bans may be imposed for tax arrears exceeding NT$1,000,000.
AML / Illegal Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: The operation may be reclassified as an illegal payment conduit; tax authorities may apply penalty assessments, and money laundering offenses carry corporate fines, confiscation of proceeds, and regulatory sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors or compliance officers who direct or acquiesce to split payments, nominee transfers, or missing AML documentation face criminal responsibility, imprisonment up to seven years, and loss of professional standing.

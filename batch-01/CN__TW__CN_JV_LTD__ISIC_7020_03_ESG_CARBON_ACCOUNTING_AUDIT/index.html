Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance (Taiwan), Taxation Administration, together with the Regional National Taxation Bureau having territorial jurisdiction over the JV's registered office; online taxpayer portal at https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the Sino-Foreign Equity JV incorporation with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) Commercial and Industrial Registration; the company registration system allocates an 8-digit Unified Business Number (統一編號/UBI) at incorporation. Step 2: Prior to the actual commencement of business, submit the Profit-Seeking Enterprise Tax Registration Form (營利事業設立登記申請書) to the local National Taxation Bureau, attaching the company registration certificate, MOEA investment approval, Articles of Incorporation, responsible person identification, business premises title or lease, and bank account evidence. Step 3: The National Taxation Bureau enrolls the JV as a business taxpayer, records the VAT/Business Tax status, and approves the Uniform Invoice serial-number range. Step 4: For foreign shareholders, the JV also files withholding tax registration (扣繳單位設立登記) for payments subject to withholding. Step 5: Update tax registration within the statutory period after any change in name, business scope, responsible person, or premises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The domestic Sino-Foreign Equity JV Ltd is a ROC tax-resident profit-seeking enterprise; it is neither a pass-through nor a disregarded entity. It files Profit-Seeking Enterprise Income Tax on worldwide net income at a flat entity rate of 20% and is subject to the Alternative Minimum Tax where applicable. A branch of a foreign enterprise is taxed only on Taiwan-source income if the head office is outside Taiwan.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch entity election is available under Taiwan tax law. A Taiwan-incorporated JV has no mechanism to elect transparent pass-through treatment. Group consolidation is not recognized; each company is a standalone taxpayer. A foreign enterprise may choose to operate through a branch rather than a subsidiary, but that choice is made through corporate documentation and does not constitute a tax entity classification election. Certain taxpayers meeting the small-scale threshold may elect simplified gross-receipts business tax treatment, and exporters may elect periodic input VAT refund procedures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

TWD 0 for domestic businesses: tax registration is mandatory before commencement of a taxable activity with no economic turnover exemption. Small-scale businesses with average monthly taxable sales below TWD 200,000 may be subject to a simplified 1% (goods) or 2% (services) gross-receipts business tax. Non-resident suppliers of cross-border electronic or offshore services to Taiwan become registrable VAT taxpayers when annual taxable sales exceed TWD 480,000; below that threshold, imported services are generally subject to reverse-charge Business Tax at 5%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Business Tax Act Article 7 for goods physically exported from Taiwan and for services sold to foreign enterprises or persons where the service is used or consumed outside Taiwan or is necessary for export. For ESG carbon footprint auditing, offset brokerage and carbon advisory services to be zero-rated, the service recipient must be a non-Taiwan enterprise, the engagement must evidence offshore performance or offshore use, and the payment must be received in foreign currency from outside Taiwan; services rendered physically in Taiwan or benefiting Taiwan-based operations do not qualify and must be taxed at 5%. The registered JV must issue a zero-rated Uniform Invoice, report the export sales in the VAT return, and substantiate any input VAT refund claim with export or offshore-service documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口報單) with customs serial number and exporter Unified Business Number for goods; for offshore services, a signed engagement agreement with the foreign recipient and statement of offshore delivery in lieu of a customs declaration
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated tax invoice
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international freight carrier evidence showing physical departure from Taiwan for goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate (銀行水單/匯入匯款通知書) showing remittance from the non-Taiwan customer
  • Carbon audit service work papers, GHG verification evidence, deliverable acceptance report, and proof of actual use of the service outside Taiwan
  • Export sales ledger and reconciliation file enabling tie-back to the relevant VAT return and customs data

Taiwan mandates Uniform Invoices (統一發票) for registered VAT businesses. Electronic invoices must be transmitted to the eGUI Integrated Service Platform (電子發票整合服務平台) using Turnkey software or approved APIs; Uniform Invoice numbers and random codes are system-generated from eGUI. There is no per-invoice cryptographic real-time clearance, but invoice data must be uploaded within prescribed windows (generally 48 hours for B2C invoices and 7 days for B2B invoices) and retained on the platform for statutory periods; void and blank invoice controls also require same-channel reporting. Export invoices must carry a zero-rated tax code and be retained in the invoice book with supporting export documentation.

The National Taxation Bureau scrutinizes zero-rating claims through post-return VAT audits, cross-checking customs declarations, foreign exchange settlement certificates, and bank remittance records. If zero-rating is claimed without actual export or with fabricated foreign exchange receipts, the tax authority reassesses the sales as taxable at 5%, with daily interest, and imposes a Business Tax Act Article 51 penalty of up to five times the underpaid tax; fraudulent export documents may trigger penal tax evasion proceedings under the Tax Collection Act, denial of refund, revocation of invoice registration, and, for goods-related false declarations, Customs Act penalties. Claims are retroactively reviewable for five years from filing, or seven years in cases involving fraudulent concealment, and refunds already paid are clawed back with mandatory interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan dollar is not fully freely convertible. Current-account transactions are generally permitted through authorized banks, subject to genuine-trade documentation, foreign-exchange declarations, anti-money-laundering controls, and applicable transaction thresholds. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity, shareholder loans, outbound investment, guarantees, and certain intercompany financing, require registration, approval, or reporting depending on the transaction and investor status. A foreign-invested Taiwan company may generally remit dividends, liquidation proceeds, interest, and principal after satisfaction of tax, corporate-law, audited-financial-statement, foreign-investment-registration, and bank-documentation requirements. Dividend remittance normally requires evidence of distributable profits, shareholder approval, tax filing or withholding, and relevant investment registration. A mainland-China investor or mainland-controlled structure is subject to materially higher legal risk: investment, ownership, management participation, funding, technology transfer, and related-party transactions may require prior approval or may be prohibited. The proposed ESG carbon-accounting and offset-brokerage activities should be screened separately for regulated professional services, environmental-commodity, securities, futures, payment, and brokerage implications even though ordinary advisory services may not require a general statutory business license.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan does not generally impose a universal statutory requirement that exporters surrender or convert a fixed percentage of export proceeds into New Taiwan dollars. Export proceeds may generally be received in foreign currency or converted through an authorized foreign-exchange bank. The exporter must maintain commercial evidence, including contracts, invoices, shipping or service-delivery records, customs or electronic export records where applicable, bank remittance data, and transaction-purpose information. Foreign-exchange declarations and Central Bank reporting apply when statutory thresholds, transaction types, or annual cumulative limits are exceeded; the applicable threshold must be confirmed with the servicing bank at execution because Central Bank rules and administrative limits may change. Banks may require receipt of proceeds through an authorized bank, reconciliation of outstanding export advances, and explanations for delayed receipt, third-party payment, netting, factoring, or offset arrangements. No fixed conversion ratio should be assumed. Proceeds related to carbon credits, environmental attributes, brokerage commissions, or advisory services should be classified accurately because they may not qualify as ordinary merchandise exports and may attract enhanced source-of-funds and transaction-purpose review.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan), principally through its Department of Foreign Exchange. The principal framework includes the Foreign Exchange Control Act, the Regulations Governing Foreign Exchange Business of Banking Enterprises, and related Central Bank circulars. Investment approval and foreign-investment registration may additionally involve Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs, Department of Investment Promotion. For a mainland-China-linked investor, the Act Governing Relations between the People of the Taiwan Area and the Mainland Area and related regulations impose additional approval, ownership, funding, and transaction restrictions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment initiation and limited operational collection; Low for replacing a Taiwan-authorized corporate bank account. An offshore electronic-money institution cannot automatically accept deposits, operate payment accounts, provide regulated remittance services, or conduct foreign-exchange business in Taiwan without the required Taiwan licensing, local-partner structure, and regulatory permissions. Offshore EMI balances may be treated by Taiwan banks as third-party funds or higher-risk payment flows. The company should use a Taiwan-authorized bank for capital injection, payroll, taxes, dividends, and material customer receipts. Any payment intermediary must be screened for licensing, beneficial ownership, safeguarding, sanctions, correspondent-bank access, data-transfer controls, transaction limits, and prohibition on undisclosed nested payment activity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Taiwan taxpayer must prepare a transfer pricing report (Local File) if related-party transactions exceed NT$20 million in a year or the company meets the total revenue threshold set by the Ministry of Finance (NT$300 million for the Master File threshold; a Master File is required if the group's annual consolidated revenue exceeds NT$30 billion). In addition, a cross-border related-party service fee exceeding NT$2 million must be supported by a cost/benefit analysis and a written contract.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) codification like the UAE or BVI, but the substance-over-form doctrine is embedded in the Income Tax Act (Articles 43-1, 43-3 and 43-4): Article 43-1 allows revenue authorities to recharacterize transactions with no business purpose; Article 43-3 imposes mandatory transfer pricing disclosure and sets document penalties; Article 43-4 (CFC rules) attributes profits of a low-tax foreign subsidiary to the Taiwanese controlling shareholder when the CFC's effective tax rate is below 14% and it fails the substance test (active business test is not sufficient; the CFC must have substantive establishment in the registered jurisdiction). For a Sino-Foreign JV performing ESG carbon auditing and offset brokerage, the tax authority will scrutinize whether the Taiwan entity has real office premises, full-time qualified professionals (carbon auditors, ESG analysts), a board structure that makes strategic decisions in Taiwan, and contracts executed and invoiced from Taiwan. Pure profit-engineering without local substance triggers the four fatal regimes: deemed capital contribution recharacterization, AMT inclusion, penalty for misreporting, and criminal liability for fraudulent tax evasion.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Collection Act (稅捐稽徵法) Articles 41 and 47, tax evasion with intent or by fraudulent means triggers a penalty of up to NT$2 million and imprisonment for up to seven years; in addition, the underpaid tax is subject to a penalty of 20% to 100% of the evaded amount, plus daily interest. A false invoice under the Business Tax Act carries a penalty of 5% of the transaction amount per violation, not less than NT$16,000 and not more than NT$1 million.

Director personal liability: Directors of the JV can be personally liable for the company's unpaid taxes if they fail to discharge statutory filing duties or cause fraudulent returns to be filed. Under Article 47 of the Tax Collection Act, the responsible person (representative director, general manager, or actual controlling shareholder) is subject to criminal imprisonment even when the company is a legal person. Civilly, a director who approves a dividend distribution while the company is insolvent or without considering the unpaid tax liability may be liable to creditors or the state under the Company Act and the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil; travel bans, denial of visa applications, and seizure of personal assets are administrative enforcement tools under the Tax Collection Act (Article 24).
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust the taxpayer's income to an arm's-length amount, resulting in additional tax, plus a penalty of 2 times the underpaid tax amount if the taxpayer failed to disclose the related-party transactions correctly (Article 43-3 of the Income Tax Act); in serious manipulation cases, a 30% surcharge on underpaid tax may be imposed, and the CFC rules require the Taiwanese shareholder to include the low-tax foreign company's profits pro rata in current-year income.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the related-party transaction schedule knowing that it contains misrepresentations or who approve a cross-border payment without due diligence can be jointly and severally liable for the additional tax and penalty. A director found to have conspired to shift profits to a related shell company may face criminal charges of document forgery and breach of trust under the Criminal Code (Articles 210 and 342), with imprisonment up to five years for breach of trust; an exit or travel ban may be issued against the director during the tax investigation (Article 24(3) of the Tax Collection Act).
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act, the tax authority is empowered to recharacterize and allocate the entire profit to the entity that has actual business substance and economic functions, moving taxable income from Taiwan to the real management jurisdiction; if that jurisdiction is not effectively taxing, the AMT 12% tax applies (or the CFC inclusion rule if a low-tax subsidiary is involved). The 5% undistributed-surplus surcharge may also apply; further, misuse of cross-strait treaty relief results in unilateral denial of the 10% WHT rate and retroactive 21% withholding plus interest.

Director personal liability: A nominee director who knowingly operates a shell JV can be pursued under the Money Laundering Control Act (洗錢防制法) if funds passing through the JV are the proceeds of tax offenses and are routed via offshore bank accounts, even if the predicate offense was committed in another jurisdiction. Directors could be stripped of their corporate veil protection, ordered to repay the tax deficiency plus penalties jointly and severally, restricted from leaving Taiwan without court or tax authority permission, and subject to criminal imprisonment of up to seven years for tax fraud and up to five years for money laundering; directors' personal bank accounts can be frozen under the Money Laundering Control Act enforcement rules.
AML and High-Risk Sanctions-Related Counterparty

Statutory sanction: The Money Laundering Control Act (Articles 7, 9, and 18) imposes a fine of up to NT$5 million for failure to maintain AML mechanisms for a low-risk business; for willful concealment of suspicious transactions, imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to NT$50 million; wire transfer reporting thresholds apply to any transfer equal to or above NT$1 million executed through Swift or wire rails if the counterparty is a sanctioned jurisdiction. The capital market regulator can also revoke the company's registration or license for ESG services if it is a member of a professional body.

Director personal liability: Under Article 8 of the Money Laundering Control Act, a director who instructs or permits non-compliant wire transfers without performing a sanctions screen is subject to a criminal penalty of up to three years; if the director provides false or incomplete customer due diligence records, the prison term is raised to five years and the fine to NT$7 million. Directors can be held civilly liable to the state for any confiscated proceeds; an arrest warrant can be issued for transnational money laundering offenses, and a MoU-based cross-strait law enforcement request can trigger personal asset freezing in Mainland China for the corporate director who is a Chinese citizen.
Undistributed Earnings and Dividend Tax Abuse

Statutory sanction: Failure to withhold dividend WHT triggers a penalty of 1.5 times the unwithheld amount (Article 114 of the Income Tax Act); failure to pay the withheld tax on time attracts a daily interest penalty and a surcharge of 10% to 20%; undistributed retained earnings over the distributable threshold are subject to a 5% tax under Article 66-9; if the dividend was artificially structured to avoid WHT, the tax authority may disregard the intermediary, impute the dividend to the Chinese parent, and reassess the 21% withholding (with the reduced treaty rate if the parent obtains TRC, but the intermediary's existence alone may invalidate treaty relief and trigger a 20% penalty on the gross amount).

Director personal liability: The responsible person who knowingly signs a false dividend distribution resolution or gives an instruction to avoid WHT is jointly liable for the withholding tax and penalties under Article 114; such a director may be debarred from acting as a director of any company in Taiwan for up to five years; in cases involving coercive threats or fraud to use offshore nominees to hold dividend rights, imprisonment up to five years applies. Chinese citizens serving as directors of the JV face extradition risk where an MOJ mutual legal assistance request is made for a serious economic crime.

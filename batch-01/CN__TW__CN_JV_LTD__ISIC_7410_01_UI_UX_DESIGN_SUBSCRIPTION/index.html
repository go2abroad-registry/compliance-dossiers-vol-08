E-government filing portal

Under the integrated multi-license reform (多证合一), the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码) printed on the business license is automatically seeded by the Market Regulation Administration to the STA and constitutes the statutory taxpayer identification number; no separate TIN certificate is issued. A non-resident foreign enterprise without a Chinese establishment that earns China-source income obtains a withholding tax ID through its withholding agent or via temporary/non-resident registration under STA Announcement 2019 No. 17.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; pass-through not eligible. The JV is a PRC resident enterprise (居民企业) subject to Enterprise Income Tax on worldwide income at the 25% statutory rate; territorial scope is residence-based (worldwide income) for residents and source-based for non-residents. The entity is presumed to be in a general tax zone with no exempt-zone status unless established in a designated free-trade program.

Available election forms

No check-the-box entity classification exists in the PRC. Available elections: (i) small-scale VAT taxpayer status where annual taxable sales do not exceed RMB 5,000,000 (3% collection rate, temporarily reduced to 1% through December 31, 2027) versus general taxpayer status (6% output VAT with input VAT credit); (ii) voluntary general taxpayer election by small-scale taxpayers with adequate accounting; (iii) election between zero-rating and VAT exemption for eligible cross-border design services; (iv) small-and-low-profit enterprise EIT relief (5% effective rate on taxable income up to RMB 3,000,000, with up to 300 employees and RMB 50,000,000 total assets, through 2027); (v) foreign tax credit method selection; and (vi) CFC and hybrid arrangement disclosures. Cross-strait relief follows the 2015 Cross-Strait Tax Agreement, which provides an ordinary foreign tax credit and mutual agreement procedure but no dividend, interest, or royalty withholding rate reductions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan Dollar (TWD) operates under a CBC-managed floating regime. Current-account flows (cross-border service income, subscription revenue, investment income/dividends) are freely convertible, subject to a statutory declaration system executed through Authorized Banks (DBUs); enterprise remittances above the statutory per-transaction threshold (currently USD 5 million) require ex-ante CBC approval, and enterprises must file the prescribed foreign-exchange declaration sheet. Capital-account flows (equity injection, shareholder loans, capital repatriation) require a prior permit or CBC approval, notably the Foreign Investment Approval under the Statute for Investment by Foreign Nationals. Profit remittance is classified as current-account investment income and requires the filing of tax-payment certificates, financially audited statements, and board minutes; the practical hurdle is the statutory 21% withholding tax on dividends to non-resident corporate shareholders (treaty relief subject to a DTA, not available between Taiwan and mainland China). RMB-denominated capital-account remittances remain restricted and must be converted or routed via USD where permitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan has eliminated mandatory surrender ratios for export earnings. Exporters and service exporters must collect foreign-currency proceeds within 180 days from the date of the export declaration or service delivery, channel the collections through an Authorized Bank (DBU), and may retain 100% of proceeds in a foreign-currency deposit account, convert to TWD at the valuation-date CBC reference rate (or a bank's negotiated rate), or hold them in an OBU. Settlement is supported by commercial invoices, contracts, and the Customs Export Declaration; the bank reports the transaction into the CBC's foreign-exchange position and declaration system. Unrepatriated proceeds exceeding the statutory window require CBC-recognized justifications (e.g., foreign working-capital needs) and are subject to heightened ex-post verification.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan), i.e. the Central Bank of China (中央銀行, CBC), which exercises foreign-exchange administration under the Management of Foreign Exchange Act (管理外匯條例); banking and fintech supervision is vested in the Financial Supervisory Commission (金融監督管理委員會, FSC).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) the non-mainland foreign investor obtains a Foreign Investment Approval (外國人投資核准函) from the Investment Commission prior to remittance; (2) the capital wire is sent via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'INVESTMENT' / 'Capital Injection', citing the approval number and Taiwan company registry; (3) the receiving Authorized Bank performs the CBC capital-account classification, files the foreign-exchange declaration, and credits the funds to the Bank of Taiwan capital escrow/trust account; (4) after CPA capital verification and tax-declaration record issuance, the funds are released to the DBU operational treasury. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% corporate income tax on Taiwan-source taxable income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% withholding tax on dividends or profit distributions paid by a Taiwan company to a nonresident shareholder, subject to applicable special rules and any effective treaty relief

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year company, the return and final tax payment are generally due from 1 May through 31 May of the following year, being the fifth month after fiscal year-end. A non-calendar fiscal year generally files within the corresponding fifth month after its fiscal year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year company, provisional filing and payment are generally due from 1 September through 30 September. The provisional amount is generally based on the preceding year's tax liability or an approved actual-income calculation method.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The business tax return is generally due by the 15th day of the month following the relevant two-month period, subject to statutory extensions or special filing rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan Local File documentation is generally triggered where annual net sales plus non-operating income reach at least NT$300 million, or where related-party transaction thresholds are met, commonly including NT$200 million for certain purchase or sales transactions or NT$100 million for certain other transaction categories. A Master File is generally required for a Taiwan entity belonging to a multinational enterprise group with consolidated revenue of at least NT$3 billion, subject to detailed statutory conditions and exemptions. Thresholds must be tested by transaction type and fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime for ordinary design businesses. Nevertheless, tax authorities apply substance-over-form, beneficial-ownership, anti-avoidance, transfer-pricing, controlled foreign company, place-of-effective-management, and business-purpose principles. The Taiwan entity should maintain local personnel or appropriately documented outsourced functions, decision-making records, premises or operational infrastructure where commercially appropriate, service-delivery evidence, bank and accounting records, and contemporaneous intercompany agreements. A shell entity receiving income without corresponding functions, assets, risks, or commercial decision-making is exposed to recharacterization, denial of deductions, withholding reassessment, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment, tax arrears, late-payment surcharges, administrative penalties, and criminal prosecution for intentional tax evasion. Criminal consequences may include imprisonment and criminal fines under applicable Taiwan tax statutes.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons and directors may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, civil restitution of tax losses, asset preservation, compulsory collection, and restrictions arising from enforcement. Corporate limited liability does not protect intentional personal participation in fraud.
False Transfer Pricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Tax authority transfer-pricing adjustment, denial of deductions, additional corporate income tax, withholding tax reassessment, interest, documentation penalties, and possible assessment under general anti-avoidance rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for losses caused by deliberate mispricing or breach of fiduciary duties and may face criminal exposure where the conduct involves fraudulent accounting, tax evasion, or asset concealment.
Improper Dividend, Royalty, or Interest Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Payer liability for unpaid withholding tax, interest, surcharges, administrative penalties, denial of treaty relief, and reassessment of the payment's legal character. Illegal distributions may also create company-law liabilities.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may be jointly liable for unlawful distributions, unpaid withholding, damages to the company or creditors, and criminal consequences where records or payment descriptions are knowingly falsified.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of expenses, reallocation of income, beneficial-ownership challenge, withholding tax reassessment, anti-avoidance adjustments, VAT consequences, and possible dissolution or enforcement consequences for unlawful operation.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil damages, personal liability for unpaid taxes or creditor harm, asset preservation, and criminal liability if the entity is used for fraud, tax evasion, money laundering, or concealment of beneficial ownership.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial-Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines, transaction restrictions, account suspension, suspicious transaction reporting, confiscation or forfeiture proceedings, and criminal penalties under Taiwan anti-money-laundering legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, accounting personnel, and beneficial owners may face imprisonment, criminal fines, confiscation, travel restrictions, and civil liability where they knowingly facilitate laundering or concealment.
VAT and Invoice Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: VAT assessment, denial of input credits, fixed and percentage-based administrative fines, suspension or restriction of business operations in serious cases, and criminal exposure for fraudulent invoices.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face personal fines, civil liability for tax losses, and criminal prosecution where invoice manipulation or deliberate false reporting is established.
Unlawful Capital Withdrawal or Related-Party Asset Diversion

Statutory sanction: Repayment claims, company-law penalties, tax reassessment, denial of deductions, insolvency or creditor claims, and potential criminal liability for breach of trust or fraudulent conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore assets, compensate the company and creditors, and may face imprisonment, criminal fines, asset seizure, or travel restrictions where the conduct is intentional and unlawful.
Books, Records, and Audit Failure

Statutory sanction: Estimated assessment, denial of deductions, administrative fines, adverse audit presumptions, tax reassessment, and possible criminal consequences for intentional falsification or destruction.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face civil damages, personal administrative penalties, and criminal liability for deliberate record falsification or obstruction of tax inspection.
Unlicensed or Misclassified Business Activity

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, business suspension, order to cease the activity, tax reassessment, and additional sector-specific penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face personal fines, civil liability, and criminal exposure where unauthorized operation causes public, consumer, financial, or tax harm.

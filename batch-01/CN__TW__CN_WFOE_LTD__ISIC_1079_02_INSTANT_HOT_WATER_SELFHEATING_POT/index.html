Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and its provincial/municipal tax offices; electronic compliance is performed through the STA National Electronic Tax Service (e-Tax) portal, with customs export records handled through the General Administration of Customs of China (GACC) and the China International Trade Single Window.

        
E-government filing portal
For a PRC-resident Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) at incorporation is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for all tax purposes. Step 1: Complete company name pre-registration and submit incorporation documents under the Foreign Investment Law. Step 2: Obtain the business license bearing the 18-character USCC. Step 3: The AMR automatically transmits registration data to the STA, creating the tax registration record; the WFOE must log in to the e-Tax portal to verify its tax profile, legal representative, financial officer, accounting method, bank accounts, and VAT taxpayer type. Step 4: Determine VAT status: annual taxable sales at or below RMB 5,000,000 may remain small-scale taxpayers; sales above RMB 5,000,000 require mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration. Step 5: If exports are planned, register the export tax rebate profile and export business eligibility in the e-Tax export tax rebate module, and link it to the China International Trade Single Window and SAFE foreign exchange receipt records. For a Taiwan-resident non-resident shareholder without a permanent establishment in China, no separate PRC TIN is issued solely for shareholding; if China-source income is derived, the WFOE as withholding agent must establish a non-resident withholding tax account in the e-Tax portal and withhold in accordance with PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law and the Cross-Strait Agreement on Avoidance of Double Taxation and Enhancement of Tax Administrative Cooperation.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The PRC maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime and applies a differentiated convertibility regime. Current-account items, including legitimate trade in goods, services, royalties, and dividends, are broadly convertible on a true-and-valid-transaction basis: banks verify the underlying documents, then process settlement without SAFE pre-approval. Capital-account items remain controlled, including inbound FDI equity contributions, external debt, overseas listing proceeds, and treasury outflows. A WFOE must register each foreign-investment round with the market regulator and SAFE through the bank; the registered capital can be paid under the foreign-investment and company-law schedule, and the resulting CNY cannot be used for prohibited purposes. Profit remittances by a WFOE are current-account transactions, but the bank must confirm the prior-year audited financial statements, tax filing and receipt of withholding taxes, and a shareholders' dividend resolution. Any foreign-exchange purchase for dividends is processed against those papers. This creates practical friction but no statutory approval hurdle. The strictest controls apply to cross-border loans, guarantees, and securities investments, all requiring registration or quota. Therefore treasury planning must route operational receipts through current-account bank verification, preserve documentation, and use only licensed onshore banks.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Statutory timeline: export proceeds must be repatriated to the exporter's domestic current-account FX account within 180 days of the underlying customs declaration unless SAFE approval is obtained for a longer collection period. Mandatory conversion ratio: there is currently zero mandatory surrender ratio; China's post-2012 trade FX reform removed forced conversion. Exporters may retain the foreign currency in a SAFE-regulated current-account FX quota, convert to CNY at their own discretion, or deploy it for permitted payments and hedging.

      

      
Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope. The JV is a Taiwan-resident profit-seeking enterprise (營利事業) under Income Tax Act (所得稅法) Article 3; corporate income tax is imposed at the entity level at 20% on worldwide net income, plus a 12% Alternative Minimum Tax on basic income exceeding the NT$500,000 exemption under the Income Basic Tax Act (所得基本稅額條例), and a 5% surtax on unappropriated retained earnings under Income Tax Act Article 66-9. Pass-through treatment is not available for stock or limited companies. Non-resident shareholders, including mainland-China investors, are subject to 21% withholding tax on dividends, which is a final tax. The JV, being resident, is taxed on worldwide income with a per-country foreign tax credit for income taxes paid in mainland China under Income Tax Act Article 3. No special tax-exempt zone status applies; free trade zone or offshore value-added center classification would not alter business tax treatment of digital metaverse services.

Available election forms

Taiwan does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; entity-level taxation is mandatory for incorporated enterprises. Available statutory elections include: (i) Income Tax Act Article 39 — 10-year net operating loss carryforward election, conditional on transparent bookkeeping and continuity of the same business; (ii) Income Tax Act Article 66-9 — retained earnings planning election: the 5% unappropriated-earnings surtax can be avoided by distributing current-year profits in the following year; (iii) Statute for Industrial Innovation Article 10 — elective research and development and workforce-training tax credits, generally up to 15–20% of qualifying expenditure and capped at 30% of the annual corporate income tax liability; (iv) Income Tax Act Article 3 — foreign tax credit election with a per-country limitation, allowing credit for mainland-China and other foreign corporate income taxes against Taiwan corporate income tax; (v) Income Basic Tax Act — offset of regular income tax against the minimum tax base; (vi) Business Tax Act Articles 7 and 39 — election to claim zero-rating for offshore services and to refund excess input VAT; (vii) no hybrid mismatch or branch-loss recapture election is recognized; hybrid mismatches are re-characterized under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act Article 43-1) and related-party interest limitation rules (Article 43-2).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration for all Taiwan-incorporated businesses from the commencement of business under Business Tax Act Article 28; no monetary threshold applies to the JV. For unincorporated small-scale traders, simplified registration and the 1% scheme apply when average monthly sales do not exceed NT$80,000 for goods or NT$40,000 for services. Offshore electronic service providers supplying digital services into Taiwan must register when annual taxable turnover exceeds NT$480,000. The JV, as a resident company selling virtual-concert tickets to both domestic and offshore audiences, must register and charge the standard 5% rate on all Taiwan-consumed supplies, while offshore-consumed supplies may be zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Business Tax Act Article 7, subparagraphs 2 and 3 — zero-rating applies to services provided for use entirely outside Taiwan and to services rendered in connection with exported goods, on the destination principle. For the virtual-concert and metaverse line, the JV may claim 0% where: (a) the digital ticketing platform, streaming nodes, and audience consumption occur outside Taiwan; (b) the contracting purchaser is a non-resident enterprise or offshore individual; (c) the consideration is received in foreign currency or digital assets remitted from outside Taiwan; and (d) the service is not physically or virtually consumed within Taiwan. MOF interpretive rulings classify electronic entertainment and digital performance services as zero-rated export services when the above conditions are evidenced. If any portion of the concert is consumed by audience members located in Taiwan or tickets are resold in Taiwan, the corresponding share is standard-rated at 5% and must be apportioned; failure to apportion constitutes under-collection of business tax.

Real-time fiscal clearance operates via the MOF Turnkey API (Turnkey 傳輸), under which invoices are digitally signed with the enterprise X.509 certificate, encrypted at message level (AES-256), and transmitted to the National Taxation Bureau in JSON/XML format. The platform assigns a unique digital stamp and QR code for consumer lottery reconciliation. Upload windows: B2B e-invoices within 48 hours of issuance; B2C e-invoices within 7 days; cloud-based e-invoices and high-volume virtual-event ticketing systems must transmit on a real-time or near-real-time basis under the MOF phased schedule. Failure to issue or transmit e-invoices triggers the Business Tax Act Article 48 penalties, suspension of the e-invoice quota, and, in cases of revenue concealment, Article 51 evasion penalties of 5 to 8 times the evaded tax. Cryptocurrency-denominated sales are converted to TWD at the spot rate on the transaction date for invoicing, with full token-flow identification records retained.

Export zero-rating refund claims are subject to risk-based selection by the National Taxation Bureau and the Customs Administration, with heightened scrutiny for recurring refunds, related-party offshore buyers, and token-based payments. Refunds are automatically suspended where documentation is incomplete or inconsistent. Erroneous or fraudulent refunds are retroactively clawed back with statutory interest, and the 7-year assessment window under Tax Collection Act Article 21 applies to fraud. Administrative penalties: Business Tax Act Article 51 — 5 to 8 times the evaded tax for false zero-rating claims or false refunds; Article 48 — additional record-keeping fines; Customs Act penalties for false export declarations, including fines up to NT$5,000,000 and confiscation of goods where physical exports are implicated. Criminal exposure: Tax Collection Act Article 41 — imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines for fraudulent refund claims; Criminal Code forgery provisions where invoices or banking documents are falsified. Hybrid or artificial offshore structuring designed to manufacture zero-rating eligibility is re-characterized under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act Article 43-1), and USDC flows through unlicensed intermediaries trigger the Money Laundering Control Act reporting duties and potential forfeiture.

21% for dividends paid to a non-resident enterprise without a Taiwan permanent establishment; branch profit remittance to the foreign head office is not subject to withholding but is already taxed at the 20% corporate income tax rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NTD 200,000,000 aggregate related-party transactions; NTD 50,000,000 for cross-border related-party intangible licensing and service fee transactions; Country-by-Country Reporting applies where the MNE group consolidated revenue exceeds NTD 27,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not formally apply the offshore Economic Substance Regulations used in zero-tax jurisdictions; however, under Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act, the judicial substance-over-form doctrine, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, tax authorities may disregard an entity or transaction lacking genuine commercial purpose, actual employees, assets, and risk management. For the virtual concert metaverse JV, the Taiwan entity must demonstrate locally exercised management, operational control, contract execution, and risk assumption; otherwise profits may be re-attributed to the Taiwan permanent establishment based on the place of effective management.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Unreported Ticket Revenue and Hidden Crypto Settlement

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to three times the understated tax, interest on the underpayment, possible business suspension, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion; a conviction may result in imprisonment of up to seven years and a fine of up to NTD 10,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors are 'statutory responsible persons' and may serve the imprisonment sentence personally where corporate tax evasion is proven; they may also face civil restitution to the company, disqualification orders, and travel restrictions while tax arrears are pending.
Shell Company or Sham Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the arrangement by the Taiwan tax authority, disallowance of losses, additional tax at 20%, penalties up to three times the underpaid tax, and potential cancellation of tax registration and incentives.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and beneficial owners can be treated as actual taxpayers, may incur aiding-and-abetting tax evasion liability with imprisonment up to three years, and may be named in a tax evasion indictment with asset freezing and exit bans.
AML / Cryptocurrency Payment Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines and order to cease business under the Money Laundering Control Act; criminal money laundering liability for proceeds exceeding statutory thresholds, with imprisonment of up to seven years, forfeiture of crypto assets, and civil fines.

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers and directors can face criminal liability for money laundering, personal asset seizure and forfeiture, travel bans, and debarment from managing legal entities in Taiwan.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Excessive Related-Party Deductions

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment by the Taiwan tax authority to an arm's-length basis, disallowance of excess deductions, additional 20% tax on the adjustment, penalties up to three times the underpayment, and separate transfer pricing documentation penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize fabricated or non-substantiated related-party arrangements may be held personally liable for false or misleading tax filings, subject to criminal punishment for knowingly presenting false books, and may be ordered by the court to compensate the company for the tax penalty paid.
DTA / Cross-Strait Relief and Beneficial Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recovery of the full statutory withholding tax, interest, penalties up to three times the avoided tax, and cross-border information exchange leading to simultaneous tax examinations.

Director personal liability: The beneficial owner behind the shell may be identified as the true taxpayer and held personally responsible for the unpaid withholding; directors can face exit restrictions, asset attachment, and imprisonment for intentional use of forged or false tax residence documentation.

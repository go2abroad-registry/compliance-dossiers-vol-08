Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Taiwan Ministry of Finance, administered operationally by the relevant National Taxation Bureau; principal electronic services include the Ministry of Finance eTax Portal and the e-Invoice Platform. Company establishment and business registration are coordinated with the Ministry of Economic Affairs and the competent local government authority.

E-government filing portal

A Taiwan company must complete company registration and business registration before commencing taxable operations. The competent National Taxation Bureau assigns an eight-digit Unified Business Number, which functions as the enterprise tax identification number and is used for business tax, corporate income tax, withholding, customs, banking, and government filings. The standard process is: (1) reserve or confirm the company name and business scope; (2) complete foreign-investment approval where applicable; (3) contribute and verify capital where required; (4) complete company registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs or the competent local authority; (5) register the business premises, responsible person, business activities, and accounting information for tax purposes with the competent National Taxation Bureau; (6) obtain the Unified Business Number and business tax registration status; (7) register for electronic invoicing and relevant tax filing systems; and (8) separately obtain customs importer/exporter registration and foreign-exchange banking arrangements where cross-border transactions require them. A foreign company without a Taiwan subsidiary generally registers a Taiwan branch, representative office, or other permitted presence rather than obtaining a Taiwan corporate TIN solely by application from abroad. A representative office generally cannot conduct ordinary revenue-generating business.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Taiwan limited company or Sino-foreign equity joint venture is generally taxed as a separate legal person. Taiwan does not provide a general United States-style check-the-box election for companies, and no general hybrid-mismatch election converts a company into a pass-through entity. Partnerships and certain contractual arrangements may have different allocation and filing consequences, but they do not alter the classification of this limited-liability joint venture. The company must apply Taiwan corporate income tax, business tax, withholding, transfer-pricing, and other applicable rules based on its legal form and activities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic turnover threshold eliminates business-tax registration for an enterprise conducting taxable business in Taiwan. A Taiwan company must generally complete business registration before commencing operations. The small-business and small-scale-business rules, including the monthly sales threshold applicable for specified exemption or simplified assessment purposes, do not constitute a general safe harbor for a commercial company carrying on ordinary taxable activities. For this financial-market news aggregation and syndication business, registration should be completed regardless of expected turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Taiwan may generally qualify for a 0% business-tax rate where exportation is evidenced by customs records and the transaction satisfies the Business Tax Act requirements. Services may qualify where the service is supplied to a foreign customer and is used outside Taiwan, or otherwise satisfies the statutory definition of exported services. For a Financial_Newswire_API model, the company must analyze the actual place of service use, customer status, contracting entity, access location, data delivery, payment trail, and any Taiwan-based benefit. A foreign customer, foreign-currency invoicing, or receipt of payment outside Taiwan is not alone conclusive. Input business tax attributable to qualifying zero-rated transactions may generally be credited or refunded subject to statutory filing, invoice, customs, payment, and substantiation requirements. Domestic services, services used in Taiwan, and mixed-use arrangements may be taxable at 5% or require allocation.

  • Commercial invoice, contract, purchase order, or other transaction document identifying the supplier, foreign customer, description, consideration, and export terms
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or other transport evidence, where goods are exported
  • Proof of exportation, customs release, and shipment matching the relevant invoice
  • For offshore services, service agreement, statement of work, delivery or access records, customer location evidence, technical logs where relevant, and documentation showing that the service was used outside Taiwan

Taiwan uses the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice Platform and approved value-added centers for electronic invoice issuance, transmission, allocation, cancellation, and reporting. Businesses subject to the uniform invoice system must issue compliant uniform invoices and retain transaction records. Taiwan does not generally require universal real-time pre-clearance of every business-to-business invoice in the manner of certain clearance jurisdictions. The applicable invoice type, buyer identification number, electronic transmission method, timing, and reporting frequency depend on customer type, transaction type, and business-tax status. Export zero-rated sales must be separately identified and supported by customs or service-export evidence. API or gateway integration may be required when the business uses an approved electronic-invoice provider or direct platform integration. Digital invoice data must be retained for the statutory accounting and tax record-retention period.

Taiwan tax authorities may deny or reverse zero-rating where export, foreign-customer, offshore-use, payment, invoice, or service-location evidence is incomplete or inconsistent. The resulting business tax, unpaid tax interest, late-payment charges, administrative penalties, disallowed input-tax credits, and refund clawback may be assessed retrospectively, together with penalties for incorrect invoices, inaccurate returns, failure to maintain records, or failure to cooperate with an audit. Deliberate fabrication of customs records, invoices, payment evidence, or service-use evidence may create tax-evasion exposure, including criminal consequences under applicable Taiwan tax laws. Export proceeds are generally subject to Taiwan foreign-exchange control rules and the commonly applicable six-calendar-month period, approximately 180 days, unless an authorized extension or a transaction-specific exception applies. The 180-day period is not a universal business-tax zero-rating condition for every service transaction; the company must separately verify the current Central Bank foreign-exchange requirements, bank procedures, permitted retention arrangements, and any applicable extension.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including cross-border service fees, subscription revenues, licensing fees, and lawful profit remittance, after the designated foreign-exchange bank performs transaction-authenticity verification. Capital-account convertibility remains controlled. FDI capital injections from the Taiwan investor are permitted only after SAFE foreign-exchange registration is completed; the remitted funds must be deposited in a foreign-exchange capital fund account and may be settled to RMB only under the settlement-by-agreement model with bank compliance checks. Profit remittance to Taiwan is treated as current-account investment income and requires a board-approved profit distribution resolution, audited PRC statutory financial statements, proof of enterprise income tax and withholding tax settlement, and a SAFE declaration. Key hurdles include: (i) no remittance before the registered capital is fully paid and the SAFE FDI credibility review is satisfied; (ii) the designated bank must verify the commercial reality of the JV's profit and the underlying newswire service revenue; (iii) Taiwan-side remittance rules require the receiving Taiwan bank to document the inward remittance purpose; and (iv) capital-account balances not lawfully settled cannot be remitted except through a formal capital reduction or liquidation process.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE does not currently impose a fixed mandatory surrender ratio for export proceeds. The JV may retain lawful current-account foreign-currency revenue in a foreign-exchange settlement account or sell it to a bank. Nonetheless, all export proceeds from the digital subscription/API newswire service must be repatriated to China through the banking system and cannot be retained offshore indefinitely. For goods-export flows, the standard administrative expectation is repatriation within 180 days of customs declaration unless extended; for service-export revenue, proceeds should be collected by the contractual payment due date and no later than the timeframe recognized by SAFE's entity-monitoring platform. Late collection or non-repatriation will trigger SAFE compliance scrutiny and may suspend the enterprise's cross-border collection capacity.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with regulatory interface for Taiwan-origin remittance flows through the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC) and the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC). For the onshore Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, SAFE is the operative FX authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary onshore treasury rail; Medium as an auxiliary global collection processor. A PRC-incorporated EJV cannot maintain its principal operating account with an offshore digital EMI because such entities are not licensed by the PBOC. Stripe may be used to collect card-based API subscription fees only where a lawful collection structure exists: either the EJV settles through a licensed PRC acquiring bank and receives RMB onshore, or the Taiwan/offshore parent owns the Stripe merchant account and subsequently remits funds to the PRC JV under a documented service-trade or FDI purpose. Legal constraints include the PBOC Payment Services Administrative Measures, SAFE cross-border payment-institution filing requirements, and the prohibition on foreign payment institutions clearing RMB onshore without a licensed PRC clearing bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan investor must instruct its Taiwan remitting bank to send a SWIFT MT103 wire to the JV's designated foreign-exchange capital account at the onshore bank. The wire instruction must quote the SAFE foreign-exchange registration code, the JV's FDI file number, and the balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment equity injection. The receiving bank verifies the remitter name, beneficiary name, amount, and purpose against the SAFE Foreign Exchange Administrative Platform, after which the funds are credited and may be settled to RMB into a restricted settlement account. No anonymous or unrelated third-party capital injection is permitted, and the JV must preserve the original Taiwan bank instruction and SAFE registration certificate for bank audits.Primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for operational remittances to overseas vendors, licensors, data-service providers, and tax authorities; bank-to-bank MT202 cover wires are used where correspondent routing requires separate cover. For goods-based trade, a commercial documentary letter of credit may be issued under SWIFT MT700 and settled through MT103/MT202, but an ISIC 6391 digital newswire API business will predominantly rely on direct service-trade MT103 wires. CIPS and CNAPS may be used for RMB-denominated outbound payments where the overseas beneficiary maintains a cross-border RMB account.

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is imposed for the corporate vehicle under the Taiwan Company Act. However, for the Trust Enterprise License required to administer irrevocable offshore spendthrift trusts, the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) requires a minimum paid-in capital of New Taiwan Dollars 500,000,000 (approximately USD 15.6 million), fully subscribed, fully paid up and certified by a Taiwan certified public accountant before license issuance. Available election forms

None. Neither the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law nor the Taiwan Income Tax Act offers an elective pass-through check-the-box mechanism comparable to IRC Section 7701. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

China: all enterprises must complete VAT registration from the first day of business; mandatory general taxpayer status applies when annual VAT taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000 (RMB 5 million), with voluntary general taxpayer registration available below that threshold. Taiwan: business tax registration is mandatory before commencement of business, and the monthly sales threshold used to distinguish small-scale business persons from standard business persons is TWD 200,000; monthly turnover below that threshold may be taxed under the small-scale business-person special regime, but registration is still required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

PRC: 0% VAT applies to goods physically exported from China; 0% or exemption applies to qualifying services consumed entirely outside China, including cross-border fiduciary or trust administration services where the recipient is an overseas trust, the service is not used in China, and consideration is received in foreign exchange from outside China. Taiwan: zero rating applies to goods exported abroad and services rendered to overseas customers, including trust administration fees for an offshore discretionary asset-protection trust, where the underlying trust assets and beneficiary community are outside Taiwan. In both economies, zero-rating is conditional on contemporaneous export records, contractual documentation, and foreign-currency receipts.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued by China Customs or Taiwan Customs as applicable
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or equivalent multimodal transport document evidencing physical export movement
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration entity, description and duty or tax value
  • Foreign exchange receipt verification voucher or Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued SWIFT credit advice showing repatriation of export proceeds to the exporting entity
  • Trust administration fee contract and statement of services qualifying as a zero-rated supply for cross-border fiduciary services

PRC: the Golden Tax Project Phase IV fully digitalises the invoice lifecycle; all VAT invoices must be issued through the China Electronic Taxation System or online invoice service platform, transmitted digitally to the STA in near-real time, and subject to anti-fraud system inspection before the customer can use the invoice for credit. Uniform digital invoices, including special e-invoices, are reconciled against reported VAT returns. Taiwan: the Uniform Invoice system is administered through the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Platform; every business person must issue Uniform Invoices for each taxable sale, and e-invoice data must be transmitted to the National Taxation Administration within the statutory reporting period; the system enforces mandatory sequential numbers, encrypted cloud-stamp, and real-time VAT control.

PRC: false export refund claims and VAT invoice fraud trigger clawback of the zero-rating benefit plus a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of unpaid tax and a penalty of 50% to five times the tax under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection; criminal liability for export tax refund fraud and issuance or use of fraudulent special VAT invoices may be imposed under Articles 201, 204 and 205 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. Taiwan: if zero-rating substantiation fails or fraudulent documents are presented, the National Taxation Administration recomputes the VAT or Business Tax, imposes back-tax with interest and penal multipliers under the Business Tax Act and the Tax Collection Act, and may pursue responsible-person imprisonment for organising tax evasion. Ten-year retroactive assessment periods apply in fraud cases under the Tax Collection Act; export zero-rating documents must be preserved for at least five years in normal cases and for the full period during which tax assessments remain open.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

In the PRC, RMB is fully convertible on current-account items such as trade, services, and profit remittances, subject to bank verification and SAFE reporting. Capital-account items, including FDI capital contributions, foreign debt, and trust-related capital injections, remain registration- or approval-gated and cannot be settled without an underlying SAFE record. Profit remittance by a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is treated as a current-account transfer but requires audited financial statements, bank records of prior capital verification, corporate income tax completion certificates, and a SAFE-registered profit distribution resolution. In Taiwan, the New Taiwan Dollar is not a fully convertible international currency; current-account settlements are liberalized but each transaction must be declared through the CBC Foreign Exchange Declaration System, and capital-account transfers above statutory thresholds require CBC or the Designated Foreign Exchange Bank's prior approval. Cross-strait RMB remittances are cleared only through the Bank of China Taipei branch and are subject to cross-strait banking regulations and CBC memorandum rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

PRC: Export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange account and reconciled against customs declaration data in the SAFE electronic system; there is no mandatory surrender ratio and exporters may either retain the foreign currency or settle it into RMB through a licensed bank. Taiwan: Exporters are required under the CBC Direction Governing Foreign Exchange Declaration and the Foreign Trade Act to collect export proceeds and remit them inward within the generally applicable statutory period of 180 days after shipment unless an extension is granted by the CBC or a Designated Foreign Exchange Bank; no fixed mandatory conversion ratio applies, but all receipts must be recorded in the Foreign Exchange Declaration System.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and People's Bank of China (PBOC) for PRC-side foreign exchange and monetary control; Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC) for Taiwan-side foreign exchange administration and banking supervision. All cross-strait wire traffic must satisfy both the SAFE International Receipts and Payments Reporting System and the CBC Foreign Exchange Declaration regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as the primary treasury rail; Medium as an ancillary collection/invoicing rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Wise Business are not licensed banks under PRC law and cannot maintain SAFE-compliant capital accounts, access CIPS directly, or provide lawful settlement of FDI capital injections. In Taiwan, an EMI may operate only if licensed as an Electronic Payment Institution under the Act Governing Electronic Payment Institutions and must keep client funds in custodial bank accounts; it cannot transact on behalf of a PRC fiduciary trust without crossing banking-permit boundaries. Accordingly, EMIs are acceptable only for pre-repatriation trade receivables or funds held outside mainland China; all onshore clearing and capital flows must pass through a licensed domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the Taiwan investor's capital injection requires the following sequence: (1) the Taiwan investor submits a Foreign Exchange Declaration to the CBC or its Designated Foreign Exchange Bank and obtains a receipt number; (2) the remittance is sent by SWIFT MT103 in USD or CNY to the JV's named FDI capital settlement account at Bank of China or ICBC; (3) the MT103 narrative must state the registered capital contribution, the JV name, the valid SAFE FDI registration code, and the China-side foreign investment filing reference, using SAFE transaction purpose code 622014 or the bank-assigned equivalent for FDI equity capital; (4) the receiving bank verifies the SAFE digital foreign exchange registration before crediting the capital escrow account; (5) conversion of the foreign currency into RMB is performed only after the JV submits a board resolution authorizing settlement and a capital payment confirmation; (6) CIPS or a China-bank internal clearing route is used for RMB-denominated credits so that the transaction is visible to PBOC's cross-border payment surveillance system.Outbound trade and trust disbursements must clear through licensed correspondent banking rails only. For ordinary trade payments to Taiwan suppliers, the JV should remit via SWIFT MT103 through a PRC Designated Foreign Exchange Bank using SAFE transaction purpose code 121010 for merchandise trade or the appropriate services code, with the beneficiary bank in Taiwan receiving through the Bank of China Taipei branch or a Taiwan correspondent bank. For letter-of-credit operations, the JV's PRC issuing bank should open a commercial documentary letter of credit under UCP600 using the SWIFT MT700 series; the Taiwan beneficiary's bank may confirm the L/C and negotiate documents against conforming presentations. For fiduciary trust distributions, the JV must instruct the bank with a SWIFT MT103 that references the trust deed, the beneficiary identification, and the distribution resolution; any FX conversion between CNY, USD, and TWD must occur within the licensed bank's current or capital-account framework, never through an unregulated EMI or crypto rail. Taiwan-side outward remittances are subject to the CBC Foreign Exchange Declaration and, where the amount exceeds the CBC threshold, prior approval is required; all disbursements must be supported by underlying transaction documentation retained for at least five years.Deliverable FX forwards (including rolling forwards) for CNY/USD and CNY/TWD exposures arising from real trade or FDI capital flows, transacted with a SAFE-designated bank under the 'real demand' principle and without speculative open positions.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) restricted to offshore CNH/TWD markets for the Taiwan parent or the overseas trust entity; PRC onshore JV entities may generally not access NDF books for RMB speculation, save for SAFE-approved foreign-debt hedging transactions.Cross-currency swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for matching long-term foreign-currency debt, FDI capital service obligations, or trust income streams, subject to SAFE transaction registration and verification of the underlying exposure.FX swaps (spot-forward combinations) for liquidity and cash-flow matching on trade receivables and payables, executed with the JV's principal relationship bank and recorded in the SAFE reporting system.Plain vanilla FX options and option combinations (zero-cost collars with deliverable forwards) permissible where the bank has a cross-border derivatives license and the underlying position is documentary, in full compliance with PRC and Taiwan derivative suitability rules.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low as the primary treasury rail; Medium as an ancillary collection/invoicing rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Wise Business are not licensed banks under PRC law and cannot maintain SAFE-compliant capital accounts, access CIPS directly, or provide lawful settlement of FDI capital injections. In Taiwan, an EMI may operate only if licensed as an Electronic Payment Institution under the Act Governing Electronic Payment Institutions and must keep client funds in custodial bank accounts; it cannot transact on behalf of a PRC fiduciary trust without crossing banking-permit boundaries. Accordingly, EMIs are acceptable only for pre-repatriation trade receivables or funds held outside mainland China; all onshore clearing and capital flows must pass through a licensed domestic bank.

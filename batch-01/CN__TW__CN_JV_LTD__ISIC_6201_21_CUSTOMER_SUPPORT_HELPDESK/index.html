Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally not permissible for a joint venture company engaged in helpdesk SaaS customer support operations in Taiwan. The Ministry of Economic Affairs and the National Taxation Administration require a physical registered office in Taiwan capable of receiving legal process and maintaining statutory records. Taiwan Company Act, including Articles 17-18 on company name and business scope, Articles 20-22 on incorporation registration, Articles 98-113 on limited companies, and Articles 156-169 where a company limited by shares is selected; Foreign Investment Act and its implementing regulations apply to non-resident investment approval. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Taiwan generally has no statutory minimum paid-in capital for an ordinary limited company. The subscribed capital must be sufficient for the company to conduct its business, and a Taiwan-certified public accountant must verify the paid-in capital before incorporation registration. No general escrow account is mandated; capital must be paid into the company-designated account and evidenced by bank records and the accountant's verification report. Foreign investors must comply with approved investment remittance procedures.

Employers must comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act, workplace risk assessment, emergency procedures, and employee insurance requirements.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taxation Administration (財政部賦稅署) and regional National Taxation Bureaus (國稅局); online portal at https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw

E-government filing portal

For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture incorporated in Taiwan, the company must complete Ministry of Economic Affairs commercial registration, obtain a MOEA approved registration document, then apply to the National Taxation Bureau of the local district for profit-seeking enterprise registration and issuance of a statutory Tax ID Number (營利事業統一編號). Foreign enterprises without a fixed place of business but supplying cross-border digital services must register as tax-paying business operators and obtain a Tax ID Number through the Ministry of Finance electronic services portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not operate a check-the-box regime. A company ordinarily cannot elect pass-through status, but may elect among certain accounting-period alignment, consolidated filing for affiliated companies under Article 39 of the Income Tax Act, or investment tax credit and incentive regimes under the Statute for Industrial Innovation. Hybrid mismatch elections are limited; Taiwan follows its domestic anti-abuse and controlled-foreign-company regimes rather than offering hybrid entity classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic physical and digital businesses: no monetary threshold, registration is mandatory from the first regular selling or service activity. Nonresident foreign electronic service suppliers providing B2C digital services to Taiwanese consumers: mandatory VAT registration if annual taxable digital service turnover exceeds NT$480,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero percent VAT applies to export of goods leaving Taiwan under Customs export declaration, zero-rated services rendered outside Taiwan and used by the foreign purchaser, and certain internationally sold services such as cloud-based helpdesk support where the place of service usage is outside Taiwan. The Taiwanese supplier must be a registered VAT business, maintain auditable service-rendering evidence, and issue an export service invoice supported by contract, delivery and payment evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口報單) or equivalent electronic customs record
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / transport evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank receipt evidencing collection from overseas customer

Taiwan mandates the issuance of e-invoices (電子發票) for most B2C and many B2B transactions, with real-time transmission to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integration Service Platform. Registered businesses must use an approved e-invoice scheme, including the Turnkey system or an API integration, transmit invoice data to the fiscal platform in near-real time, and embed the statutory automated carrier (cipher) and encryption key. The digital stamping takes the form of the Ministry of Finance cloud issuance confirmation and carrier-code electronization; failure to comply attracts surcharges and transaction disqualifications that may disallow zero-rating.

The Taiwan tax authority may retrospectively disallow zero-rated treatment where export evidence is incomplete, false or fabricated, or where export proceeds are not repatriated within the applicable forex collection period (generally within 180 days for merchandise exports, with shorter periods for certain service exports). Remedies include reassessment at the standard VAT rate, late interest, civil delinquency penalties up to five times the evaded tax under Business Tax Act Article 51, and criminal liability for tax evasion, including imprisonment, criminal fines, and confiscation of fraudulent refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is fully convertible for genuine current-account transactions (goods, services, profit/dividend remittance, and current transfers) without prior SAFE approval. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity contribution from the Taiwan shareholder, require FDI registration and bank verification. Dividend/profit remittances by the JV to Taiwan shareholders are current-account income transactions and are permitted after verification of the audited financial report, corporate income tax payment certificate, board resolution, and SAFE service-trade declaration framework; no separate convertibility license is required. Taiwan-sourced investment is treated under PRC Foreign Investment Law and the Taiwan Compatriots Investment Protection Law, subject to the negative list and applicable cross-strait investment rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China has eliminated mandatory export-proceeds surrender and mandatory conversion ratios; since the 2012 reform, the JV may retain export/foreign-exchange receipts in its designated current-account or settle them into RMB at market rate. Nevertheless, services-trade proceeds must be repatriated to the JV's onshore bank and reported on SAFE's platform within 5 working days after the bank has credited the transaction, matched against the underlying SaaS contract, invoice, and the International Balance of Payments transaction code. Long-delayed receivables (typically in excess of 180 days beyond contractual due date) may be treated as abnormal data points in SAFE's total-amount monitoring and trigger a compliance review.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) — the PRC central bank and delegated FX regulator under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations (2008 Revision).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Conditional/Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) are not licensed banks in mainland China and cannot act as the JV's principal bank or hold its capital funds. They may be used only as technical payment gateways for collecting overseas SaaS subscription receivables (Stripe Billing, PayPal), with all funds swept through a PBOC-registered cross-border payment institution into a SAFE-designated domestic bank account. Substantive treasury accounts or liquidity concentration with offshore EMIs is not legally viable for the PRC JV because SAFE and PBOC require cross-border current-account and capital-account flows to be settled through the onshore banking system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan shareholder transfers equity capital by SWIFT MT103 from a Taiwanese bank in TWD, USD, or CNY to the JV's FDI Capital Fund Foreign Exchange Account at the PRC designated bank. The wire instruction must include the bank's SWIFT/BIC, beneficiary legal name, exact account number, and the SAFE Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration reference; the remittance purpose is coded as inward direct investment equity (SAFE Balance of Payments code family 6210/6220 as mapped by the receiving bank). Upon receipt, the bank verifies the amount and source against the FDI Credential and joint-venture contract; funds may then be converted into RMB under the PRC 'willing settlement' (意愿结汇) rules using the bank's capital-item payment verification system. The JV must maintain the original bank receipt, SWIFT MT103, certificate of foreign exchange registration, Taiwan Compatriots Investment Certificate or foreign investment certificate as applicable, board resolution on capital calls, and tax/audit filings for the verification file.Operating disbursements for the SaaS/helpdesk business (software license fees, cloud infrastructure, subcontractors, marketing) are cleared through the JV's operational account using SWIFT MT103 (ordinary customer transfer), SWIFT MT202 (bank-to-bank transfer for liquidity positioning), or the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) for CNY payments. Payments for imported servers or telecommunications equipment can be supported by a commercial letter of credit (MT700) if the supplier demands documentary credit; for services, open-account MT103 settlement is the common rail.

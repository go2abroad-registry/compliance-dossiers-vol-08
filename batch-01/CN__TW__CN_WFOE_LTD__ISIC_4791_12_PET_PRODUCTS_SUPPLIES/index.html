Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Ministry of Finance of the Republic of China (Taiwan), administered through the competent National Taxation Bureau of the place where the business is established; electronic services are available through the Ministry of Finance eTax Portal. The supplied structure contains a jurisdictional inconsistency: a mainland-China Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise is not a Taiwan entity form. A Taiwan operation would generally require a Taiwan company, branch, representative office, or other locally registrable business form.

        
E-government filing portal
A Taiwan company or branch must first complete business registration with the competent authority, normally through the Ministry of Economic Affairs or the relevant municipal or county authority, and then apply for tax registration with the competent National Taxation Bureau. The authority issues or confirms the business tax identification number used for VAT filings and invoices. Required materials generally include the registration certificate, articles or establishment documents, responsible-person identification, registered-office evidence, authorized-agent documents, bank information where requested, and business-scope evidence. A foreign company without a Taiwan fixed place of business generally does not obtain a Taiwan VAT number merely because it sells remotely into Taiwan; tax registration, withholding, customs, platform, or other obligations may nevertheless arise depending on the transaction structure. A mainland-China WFOE cannot directly use the Taiwan registration process without establishing a legally recognized Taiwan presence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
Taiwan does not provide a United States-style check-the-box election for ordinary companies. A Taiwan-incorporated company is generally subject to entity-level corporate income tax, while distributions may create shareholder-level tax consequences. Branches of foreign companies are generally taxed on Taiwan-source or Taiwan-attributable income under branch rules. There is no general statutory election that converts an ordinary Taiwan company into a pass-through entity for Taiwan tax purposes. Related-party and hybrid-instrument outcomes are determined under Taiwan corporate income tax, transfer-pricing, anti-avoidance, beneficial-ownership, and international tax rules.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
5%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
For ordinary business-tax registration, the commonly applicable threshold is monthly sales of NT$80,000 for goods businesses and NT$40,000 for service businesses, subject to statutory exemptions, special regimes, and administrative changes. For the stated electronic retailing of pet supplies, the goods threshold of NT$80,000 per month is the relevant baseline. A business with a fixed place of business generally must register when the applicable threshold and business-registration conditions are met. Import VAT is generally collected at importation regardless of the domestic registration threshold.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Exports of goods that are actually exported from Taiwan may generally qualify for 0% business tax when supported by customs export records and other prescribed evidence. The exporter should retain the export declaration, commercial invoice, packing list, transport documentation, proof of shipment or delivery outside Taiwan, sales and purchase records, and evidence linking the exported goods to the relevant tax period. Services may qualify for zero-rating only where the statutory conditions for services supplied for use outside Taiwan or other qualifying international services are satisfied; a foreign customer or foreign-currency payment alone is insufficient. Imported pet products are subject to import customs formalities and import business tax, and domestic resale is generally subject to Taiwan business tax. Input-tax refunds or credits require valid compliant invoices and substantiation that the inputs relate to taxable or zero-rated supplies.

Businesses subject to Taiwan business tax generally issue government-formatted uniform invoices and submit business-tax returns, normally bimonthly. Electronic invoices must comply with Ministry of Finance specifications and may be issued, transmitted, stored, and uploaded through approved electronic-invoice systems or the government platform. Taiwan generally operates a post-issuance electronic-invoice reporting and verification framework rather than a universal Latin-American-style real-time pre-clearance model for every invoice. Digital invoice data must contain required seller, buyer, transaction, tax, amount, and invoice-number information. B2C electronic commerce may use approved electronic invoices and platform-mediated delivery mechanisms. Export transactions require records supporting export status and zero-rating.

      
There is no general Taiwan statutory rule requiring all export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days. Foreign-exchange reporting, declaration, and banking requirements may apply under the Foreign Exchange Control Act and related regulations, depending on the amount, transaction type, resident status, and payment route. A zero-rating claim may be denied or reversed if customs evidence is missing, the goods were not actually exported, the transaction is fictitious, the invoice is incorrect, or the supply was used in Taiwan. Disallowed input credits and refunds may be assessed retrospectively with tax, interest, and applicable penalties. False invoices, fabricated export evidence, tax evasion, concealment, or fraudulent refund claims may result in administrative fines, surcharge, recovery of improperly refunded tax, and criminal liability under applicable Taiwan tax and criminal laws. The precise penalty depends on the nature, amount, intent, repetition, disclosure, and cooperation involved.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The PRC maintains a bifurcated convertibility regime. Current-account items, including cross-border trade in goods, e-commerce platform payouts, logistics fees, and after-tax profit remittances by a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), are generally freely convertible at the market rate, subject to bank verification of authenticity and consistency. Capital-account items, including registered capital injections, shareholder loans, equity transfers, and cross-border debt, remain controlled by SAFE and require registration, approval, or quota supervision. Profit/dividend remittance by the CN_WFOE_LTD to its Taiwan shareholder is eligible for current-account treatment only after the following are supplied to the designated bank: audited statutory financial statements, a lawfully adopted profit-distribution resolution, proof of enterprise income tax payment, and, where applicable, evidence of withholding tax at the rate under the Cross-Strait tax arrangement or the default 10% non-resident withholding rate. The practical hurdles to profit remittance are: (i) verification of distributable profits after statutory surplus reserves; (ii) consistency of the tax-filing amount with the audited balance sheet; (iii) absence of adverse SAFE monitoring tags for earlier trade mismatches; and (iv) anti-round-tripping checks where the ultimate ownership has PRC-resident nexus. Capital-account inflows such as equity capital enjoy no blanket convertibility; every use of the injected funds through RMB settlement is reviewed against the investment purpose and the SAFE filing.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under SAFE's trade-in-goods foreign-exchange administration, export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic bank account within 90 days from the date of export customs declaration unless an extension has been registered in SAFE's monitoring system. Repatriation can be made in the invoiced foreign currency or in another convertible currency, and the receiving bank will validate the proceeds against the export declaration and the enterprise's trade-monitoring list. Since the 2012 trade-FX reform, there is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the CN_WFOE_LTD may retain up to 100% of export receipts in a foreign-currency settlement account and may convert to RMB only where actual business use requires settlement. However, any variance beyond the SAFE-prescribed tolerance (generally 5% or US$50,000 equivalent) between actual export collections and the monitor-list expectation will trigger an escalation requiring a written discrepancy defence, underlying order documentation, customs records, and platform payout statements. For cross-border e-commerce export, the exporter may consolidate declarations under the 9610, 9710, or 9810 customs codes and must ensure the PayPal/Stripe settlement ledger, logistics tracking, and SAFE trade-declaration data are reconciled on at least a monthly cycle.

      

      
Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China as the operative foreign-exchange regulator

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Stripe
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Offshore digital EMI and payment-facilitator accounts (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Payoneer, Intergiro) are not a substitute for a SAFE-banked corporate account for CN_WFOE_LTD. They may be used as e-commerce payout aggregation rails for Stripe/PayPal revenues, but the WFOE must flow such proceeds onward to a domestic bank account under the goods-trade repatriation timeline. Legal constraints: (i) most offshore EMIs require an offshore-incorporated entity (Hong Kong, Singapore, UK) and will not open the account directly for a PRC WFOE; (ii) funds retained overseas beyond the SAFE repatriation deadline constitute a trade-FX compliance violation; (iii) the final receiving bank will require the underlying EMI settlement ledger, customer transaction logs, and customs declaration trail; and (iv) EMTs with banking-as-a-service arrangements are not classified as 'domestic settlement accounts' by PBOC/SAFE and cannot receive capital-account injections. The recommended architecture is to use Stripe/PayPal purely as merchant collection gateways, with daily or weekly settlement into a Hong Kong or Taiwan PSA account, and thereafter a SWIFT MT103 credit transfer into the Bank of China/China Merchants Bank settlement account under the goods-trade purpose code.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        For compliant injection of registered capital from the Taiwan shareholder into the CN_WFOE_LTD, the following SWIFT/clearance protocol must be observed. First, the WFOE must complete SAFE FDI registration and open a capital-contribution foreign-exchange account (资本金账户) at its designated bank. Second, the Taiwanese shareholder must send a direct bank-to-bank SWIFT MT103 wire from its Taiwan corporate bank account—never through an offshore EMI or third-party pooling account—with the remitter name exactly matching the shareholder shown in the FIE registration. Third, the payment instruction must contain the SAFE balance-of-payments transaction code 601 (foreign direct investment equity capital injection) for a new capital injection, or code 603 for acquisition of existing equity, together with the SAFE FDI registration serial number and the purpose narrative 'Investment Capital Contribution'. Fourth, the receiving bank will verify against the registered paid-in capital, conduct an anti-money-laundering sanctions screen, and complete the 'International Income/Payment Declaration for Domestic Entities' (涉外收入申报单) on the WFOE's behalf. Fifth, once credited to the capital account, the funds cannot be used for current-account expenses until the WFOE submits the underlying business contract, invoice, and a 'Capital Account FX Settlement Application' to convert into RMB or to transfer to a domestic RMB settlement account. Shareholder loans must be separately registered as cross-border debt with SAFE and cannot be routed through the capital contribution account.Outbound trade disbursements for the CN_WFOE_LTD (e.g., settlement with pet-product factories, payment of Taiwan-located raw-material suppliers, cross-border logistics fees, platform commissions, and overseas refunds) should be cleared through the following rails. (i) SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) from the WFOE's foreign-exchange settlement account to the overseas beneficiary bank, with the correct SAFE transaction code reflecting the true nature of each payment (physical goods, freight, platform commission, refund); the bank will require invoice/P.O., customs declaration (for goods), transport or service contract, and tax certificate where the payment is tax-relevant. (ii) Commercial letters of credit (sight or usance) issued by the mainland bank in favour of the Taiwan or third-country supplier, particularly where procurement volumes justify documentary protection. (iii) Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) in CNY for suppliers willing to settle in renminbi, reducing USD correspondent charges and shortening clearing time. (iv) For PayPal/Stripe refund and chargeback flows, the platform settlement may be netted abroad, but each net remittance into mainland China must be accompanied by an order-log and shipment-evidence package; separate refund wires from the domestic FX account to the customer's card network may be used where the platform cannot net. All outbound payments above SAFE's monitoring threshold require a 'Payment Instruction and Use Declaration' and can be rejected if the trade data show a mismatch between contract date, invoice amount, logistics event, and payment sequence.Deliverable CNY/USD foreign-exchange forwards (远期购售汇) with real-trade underlying and notional exposure capped by the WFOE's committed export settlement receipts.Foreign-exchange swaps (外汇掉期) to manage short-term RMB/USD liquidity mismatches without creating open positions.Cross-currency swaps (交叉货币利率互换) for long-dated foreign-currency obligations or to synthetically hedge a USD-denominated shareholder loan into RMB exposure.FX options and option combinations (collars, forwards-with-opton, zero-premium structures) where the WFOE does not sell uncovered options; the strategy must be supported by a current treasury-risk policy and SAFE/PSA verification.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible as a standalone onshore instrument for the WFOE; if the Taiwan parent treasury books a Hong Kong NDF on the WFOE's exposure, the onshore WFOE should mirror it with a deliverable forward or option to maintain hedge documentation and avoid SAFE open-position violation.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Offshore digital EMI and payment-facilitator accounts (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Payoneer, Intergiro) are not a substitute for a SAFE-banked corporate account for CN_WFOE_LTD. They may be used as e-commerce payout aggregation rails for Stripe/PayPal revenues, but the WFOE must flow such proceeds onward to a domestic bank account under the goods-trade repatriation timeline. Legal constraints: (i) most offshore EMIs require an offshore-incorporated entity (Hong Kong, Singapore, UK) and will not open the account directly for a PRC WFOE; (ii) funds retained overseas beyond the SAFE repatriation deadline constitute a trade-FX compliance violation; (iii) the final receiving bank will require the underlying EMI settlement ledger, customer transaction logs, and customs declaration trail; and (iv) EMTs with banking-as-a-service arrangements are not classified as 'domestic settlement accounts' by PBOC/SAFE and cannot receive capital-account injections. The recommended architecture is to use Stripe/PayPal purely as merchant collection gateways, with daily or weekly settlement into a Hong Kong or Taiwan PSA account, and thereafter a SWIFT MT103 credit transfer into the Bank of China/China Merchants Bank settlement account under the goods-trade purpose code.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Taiwan Company Act does not impose a minimum authorized paid-in capital for a limited liability company. The amount stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid up and deposited in a bank account; a temporary capital verification deposit is required before registration. The funds may be released after the company receives its company registration certificate and unified business tax number. There is no statutory blocked-escrow requirement except a foreign investment approval letter may condition the capital remittance on evidence of the bank deposit.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online administration through the China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal of the relevant provincial tax service.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the local STA office within 30 days after the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the business license. The SAMR-issued Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) serves as the legal tax identification number (TIN). Submission includes foreign investment filing, articles of association, registered address proof, legal representative identification, and bank account details. The WFOE must then register for VAT with the STA office and, if annual taxable turnover is expected to exceed RMB 5,000,000, apply for General VAT taxpayer status. For export activities, the WFOE must complete export tax refund registration and customs registration in the China International Trade Single Window to obtain an exporter code.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must complete tax registration regardless of turnover; mandatory General VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are eligible for the 0% VAT rate under the PRC VAT legal framework. The WFOE must be a General VAT taxpayer, the goods must physically leave the Chinese customs territory under a valid customs export declaration, a genuine offshore sale to the buyer must be documented, and export proceeds must be received and verified in accordance with SAFE rules. Cross-border e-commerce exports must be made through an eligible platform and appear in customs statistical records.

  • China Customs Export Declaration (Declaration Form for Exported Goods)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export Proceeds Verification Certificate / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)
  • Export Sales Contract
  • Customs-verified Cross-Border E-Commerce Declaration (where applicable)

China operates the Golden Tax System Phase IV requiring fully digitalized electronic invoices (FDEI). VAT invoices must be issued through STA-approved systems, transmitted in real time to the tax authority via system-to-system API, and secured by cryptographic anti-fraud digital stamps and the required tax control key.

Zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective STA review. If documentation is false, incomplete, or proceeds are not repatriated within the deadline, the claimed VAT refund will be clawed back, the supply will be reclassified as taxable at the standard VAT rate, a daily late-payment surcharge will apply, and administrative penalties of 50% to five times the unpaid tax may be imposed. Fraudulent export refund applications may also trigger criminal liability, customs disqualification, and loss of future zero-rating privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is current-account convertible: for genuine goods and service transactions, a WFOE may convert trade-related FX receipts and make payments through SAFE-authorized banks by submitting the customs declaration, invoice, contract and transaction documents. Capital account convertibility remains heavily controlled: inbound FDI equity injection must be registered under the SAFE FDI registration system, deposited into a dedicated FDI foreign exchange capital account, converted to RMB only through bank-supervised settlement, and each capital RMB payment must be backed by an actual operating expenditure (procurement, salary, rent, tax, royalties). Dividends to the Taiwan parent are treated as profit remittance under the current account and require audited financial statements, tax clearance, a board distribution resolution and bank verification; the bank will not process dividend remittance if the WFOE has unreconciled cross-strait tax or debt obligations. Export proceeds must be repatriated to China and retained in a foreign currency account or sold to a designated bank; there is no mandatory conversion ratio under the current trade monitoring framework, but overdue export receipts beyond 90 days must be explained and reported to SAFE. Overall, liquidity planning must assume PBOC/SAFE documentation lead times and real-demand verification on every FX conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into the WFOE's domestic foreign-exchange current account within the trade payment term evidenced by the customs declaration and contract; any receipts falling outside the 90-day monitoring window must be reported through the bank's trade foreign exchange monitoring system as advance/trailing receipts. The mandatory surrender ratio is zero: a WFOE may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency or sell them to a designated FX bank at the benchmark spot rate, but every RMB conversion must be covered by the original customs declaration, commercial invoice, packing list, logistics documents and the SAFE trade digital record.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan investor should remit in USD or another freely convertible currency by SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's dedicated FDI foreign exchange capital account at a PBOC-licensed bank. Before remittance, the WFOE must complete SAFE FDI registration and open the capital account using its unified social credit code and foreign investment information report. The MT103 narrative must carry the SAFE foreign exchange purpose code for foreign direct investment equity contribution (non-resident direct investment paid-in capital) and reference the WFOE entity name and FDI registration record reference number. Upon credit, a Chinese certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report; the funds may then be settled into RMB under the bank's payment-purpose settlement process, with each RMB payment linked to genuine operating transactions (purchase of goods, payroll, rent, tax, third-party logistics). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard corporate income tax rate under Article 4 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% (on gross dividends distributed by a Chinese resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise shareholder, unless reduced by a tax treaty that China has signed. Taiwan is not a party to any effective DTA with mainland China, so the 10% statutory rate applies.)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly: within 15 days after the end of March, June, September, and December; Annual: by May 31 of the following year; the tax year is the Gregorian calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation required if any single related-party transaction amount crosses: RMB 20 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 10 million for intangible asset transfers (royalties); RMB 10 million for financial asset transfers; or RMB 40 million aggregate for other related-party transactions (e.g., services). Master File required if aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. The thresholds are defined in SAT Bulletin No. 42 (2016).

Economic substance scope: China does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime similar to Cayman/BVI; however, under the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) of Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the recharacterization principle, the tax authority may disregard transactions or entities lacking economic substance. The company must demonstrate that it has real operations, employees, office space, and core income-generating activities (CIGA) in China for e-commerce, logistics, and customer support; payments to offshore affiliates for routine services should be supported by evidence of actual services performed. For a Taiwanese ultimate parent, the China WFOE must maintain a full set of operational records to avoid a challenge that it is a shell entity artificially transferring profits out of China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law (TCAL), any person who fabricates, alters, conceals, or destroys accounting books or uses false invoices to underpay tax shall be ordered to pay the amount arbitrarily underpaid plus 50%-5 times the unpaid tax as a fine. Under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, tax evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and more than 10% of the payable amount constitutes a criminal offense, punishable with imprisonment up to seven years plus fines.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where directors knowingly directed or deliberately allowed the false invoicing and underreporting. Directors can be held personally joint and several for the unpaid tax and penalties. In criminal cases, a responsible director faces imprisonment for up to 7 years, criminal fines, confiscation of personal assets, and a ban on serving as a director in any PRC-registered company for a designated period. The tax authority may also place the director on the 'Illegal Taxpayer Blacklist' (Tax Blacklist) triggering travel bans and denial of exit from China for up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Entry Authority under Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law has the power to adjust taxable profits. Under Article 122 of the Implementation Rules, an adjustment will be subject to interest computed at the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points per annum until the additional tax is paid. In severe cases, the inability to substantiate the transaction may be treated as fraudulent tax evasion, triggering fines up to 5 times the tax shortfall and criminal exposure under the Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the related-party agreements or knowingly sign false documentation can be held civilly liable for the tax and interest paid by the company. If the transfer pricing scheme is found to be a sham to evade tax, the director may face criminal charges for attempted tax evasion (imprisonment up to 7 years), personal fines, and disqualification as a director. Additionally, the tax authority can apply to have the director’s assets attached to satisfy unpaid tax liabilities.
Money Laundering and Foreign Exchange Violations

Statutory sanction: Under Article 191 of the Criminal Law of the PRC, money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of up to 10 years, with fines of up to 20% of the laundered amount and confiscation of criminal proceeds. Under the Anti-Money Laundering Law of the PRC, administrative fines of up to RMB 5 million can be imposed on the institution, and the responsible senior management may be fined up to RMB 500,000 individually. Violations of the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations may also lead to confiscation of illegal gains and fines up to 30% of the amount involved.

Director personal liability: Directors who facilitate, authorize, or fail to prevent the laundering scheme face personal criminal responsibility. Under Article 191, a director can be sentenced to imprisonment for up to 10 years, forfeiture of personal assets, and an order to pay restitution to the state for the tax evaded. The court may issue a travel ban and a no-exit order preventing the director from leaving China pending investigation. In addition, the director is disqualified from holding positions in financial institutions or being a legal representative of any Chinese company, and personal assets—including real estate and bank accounts—may be frozen and seized.

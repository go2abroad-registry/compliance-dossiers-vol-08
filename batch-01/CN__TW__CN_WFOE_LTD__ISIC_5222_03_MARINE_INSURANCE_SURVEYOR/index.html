Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital applies to a company limited by shares under the Taiwan Company Act following the 2018 amendment. No blocked escrow account is required. The subscribed capital must be fully paid into a corporate bank account in Taiwan before registration and verified by a Certified Public Accountant capital verification report. If capital is remitted from outside the Taiwan jurisdiction, the remittance must be reported through a designated foreign exchange bank and evidenced by a Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate, retained for regulatory inspection.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Fire Services Act is required for the registered office and any survey staging area; this includes a building public safety inspection certificate, fire extinguishers, emergency lighting, evacuation route signage, an evacuation plan, and fire drill records. Civil Defense clearance involves verifying that the premises comply with the public safety inspection standards and that responsible personnel are trained in emergency response. Occupational clearance requires a workplace risk assessment under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including personal protective equipment for surveyors, safe work procedures for vessel access, cargo hatch inspection, and confined-space entry, plus accident reporting to the labor authority within eight hours of any serious workplace incident. If the company does not conduct manufacturing, chemical processing, assembly, or warehousing, no industrial plant license is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Taiwan (R.O.C.) Ministry of Finance, Taxation Administration, enforced through the regional National Taxation Bureaus (Taipei, Northern, Central, Southern, Kaohsiung). The statutory online electronic portal is the Ministry of Finance e-Tax Service Portal at https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw, together with the Ministry of Finance Business Tax e-Declaration System for periodic VAT filings.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) Limited Company in Taiwan must first secure foreign investment approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs. After the company is registered with Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs, the entity automatically receives an eight-digit Unified Business Number (UBIN), which serves as the universal national tax identification number for corporate income tax, value-added business tax, and customs declarations. Before commencing business, or within 30 days after company registration if business has commenced, the WFOE must file a Business Entity Tax Registration Application with the National Taxation Bureau in whose jurisdiction the registered address or fixed place of business is located. The submission bundle must include the company registration certificate, articles of incorporation, foreign investor and shareholder documentation, board and resident manager appointment evidence, lease agreement or proof of business premise, and a letter of authorization for local fiscal agents. The tax office enters the UBIN into the tax database and activates the business tax account. If the foreign entity has no fixed place of business in Taiwan but performs taxable marine survey services inside Taiwan, it must appoint a fiscal representative and apply for registration through the same regional National Taxation Bureau before supplying any Taiwan-sourced services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election, and there is no general hybrid-mismatch elective mechanism under the Income Tax Act or the Business Tax Act. A Taiwan-incorporated WFOE limited company is automatically classified as a resident profit-seeking enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide income, and it is mandatorily registered as a value-added taxpayer for business tax purposes. No pass-through classification or consolidated tax filing election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold applies for mandatory business tax registration; every business entity with a fixed place of business or engaging in taxable activities in Taiwan must register before commencement. Small-scale enterprises whose average monthly taxable sales do not exceed New Taiwan Dollars 200,000 may elect the simplified special business tax regime at a lower effective rate instead of the standard VAT computation, but any WFOE providing marine insurance survey services to commercial underwriters is ordinarily treated as a full VAT business entity required to issue Uniform Invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of Taiwan's Business Tax Act, a zero percent VAT rate applies to goods that are actually exported outside Taiwan, and to services supplied to a foreign enterprise, foreign individual, or foreign institution where the service is used or consumed outside Taiwan. For the marine insurance survey business covering vessel hull and cargo damage claims, zero-rating is eligible when the survey report, damage assessment, inspection certificate, and claim-adjustment deliverable are provided to a foreign marine underwriter or overseas policyholder and the economic benefit is realized outside Taiwan. The service must be genuinely exported rather than consumed in Taiwan; inspection or surveying performed on a vessel or cargo within Taiwan operational waters for a domestic insurer's Taiwan claims file remains subject to the standard 5% rate. Qualifying offshore digital services are also zero-rated if electronically supplied and used outside Taiwan, but the core services in this business remain physical marine survey activities that must meet the export-of-services documentation test.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) for goods supplied directly in connection with an export transaction
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued by the carrier evidencing movement of goods from Taiwan to a foreign destination
  • Full executed marine survey and claims inspection contract specifying the foreign underwriter or overseas insured party and the Taiwan exporter or service provider
  • Commercial invoice and tax Uniform Invoice (where applicable) addressed to the foreign counterparty
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Receipt or Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by an authorized foreign-exchange bank evidencing receipt of foreign-currency settlement for the zero-rated export service
  • Complete marine survey report, photographic evidence, damage assessment certificate, and photocopies of vessel or cargo documents demonstrating the deliverable was used by the foreign beneficiary

Taiwan mandates the Uniform Invoice system for all VAT-registered business entities. The WFOE must procure serially numbered Uniform Invoices from the National Taxation Bureau and issue a Uniform Invoice for every taxable sale of goods or services. For business-to-business and business-to-consumer sales where electronic Uniform Invoices are used, the taxpayer must adopt the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice platform and transmit XML invoice data through the statutory Government Turnkey Server or certified API channel, generally without extension, within the filing cycle. The e-Invoice platform applies QR-code security features, a unique invoice number, and central data storage for anti-fraud cross-checking. Hard-copy Uniform Invoices do not require a separate digital stamp at issuance, but the registered invoice series must be reconciled with all issued, voided, and unused invoices in the VAT periodic filing.

The National Taxation Bureau applies strict audit scrutiny to zero-rated export services by comparing the export declaration, bill of lading, survey contract, commercial invoice, foreign-currency payment evidence, and banking remittance records. The taxpayer must retain the full export documentation bundle for at least five years from the filing date, and foreign-currency proceeds should be repatriated and evidenced within the 180-day statutory substantiation window. If the taxpayer claims zero-rating without genuine export documentation, or if the documentation is found to be fraudulent, forged, or commercially implausible, the tax authority will assess the underpaid business tax at 5%, impose additional interest from the original due date, and apply under the Business Tax Act a penalty of one to five times the amount of the tax evaded. Where deliberate fraudulent invoices or forged customs documents are used, the responsible person may be subject to criminal liability, imprisonment, and further fines under the Taiwan Tax Evasion and Criminal Law framework, and the tax authority may order the suspension of the taxpayer's Uniform Invoice purchasing privilege until compliance is restored.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime. RMB is freely convertible for most current account transactions, but the capital account remains highly regulated. A WFOE must obtain SAFE registration and open a designated FDI capital account; capital received from the Taiwan-based parent can only be converted with an underlying negative-list check and funds must be used for the declared business purpose. Profit remittance to shareholders requires audited financial statements, board resolution, enterprise income tax return and tax payment proof; the bank will scrutinize whether the WFOE has completed annual industrial and commercial filing and foreign investment information reporting. Service trade income from marine surveying is a current account credit and enjoys relatively free convertibility, provided the contract, invoice and survey deliverables are authentic. However, any remittance to tax havens or payments not matching the service description will be blocked under SAFE truthfulness and compliance review. Cross-border foreign debt, equity transfers, liquidation proceeds and any form of offshore borrowing require separate quota or registration. In practice, converting dividends or returning capital to the Taiwan parent is the most difficult liquidity action and must be supported by a full documentary chain and tax settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For exported goods, the exporter must normally bring the foreign-currency proceeds back to a domestic designated account within the agreed trade credit period and no later than 120 days after export customs declaration unless approved under a different collection method; if the proceeds are not received, the WFOE must report to SAFE's Goods Trade Monitoring System. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio under the current SAFE framework; the exporter may retain 100% of the foreign currency in a current-account settlement account and convert to RMB voluntarily. For marine insurance survey services exported under trade-in-services, there is no surrender requirement, but the receipt must be declared on the Service Trade International Revenue and Expenditure Declaration Form and must be matched with the survey contract and delivery evidence.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to open domestic deposit accounts, RMB clearing records, FDI capital-account collection services or statutory capital verification reports for an onshore mainland China WFOE. They are legally barred from serving as the primary treasury rail for this entity. An EMI can be used only as a secondary gate for incidental overseas supplier payments if routed through a licensed partner bank, but it cannot replace a tier-one domestic commercial bank for SAFE filing, foreign exchange conversion, tax withholding, or capital account compliance. WFOE treasury operations must maintain their core clearing accounts with an onshore bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan shareholder must remit the equity injection via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's SAFE-registered FDI foreign exchange capital account at a designated domestic tier-one bank. The remittance references must include the SAFE FDI registration code and the bank-designated FX purpose code for foreign-invested enterprise equity injection. After receipt, the bank reports the cross-border receipt to SAFE and the WFOE's certified public accountant performs statutory capital verification. Only after the capital verification report is issued can the funds be settled into RMB and transferred to the operational RMB account for approved expenses. Wise and other EMI rails are not acceptable for FDI capital injection or statutory capital verification.For marine surveying operational payments, SWIFT MT103 is the primary rail for direct wire disbursements to overseas subcontractors, professional indemnity insurers and equipment suppliers. For large procurement of specialized survey equipment, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued by the domestic bank is the appropriate rail, supported by invoice, transport document and quality certificate. For contested claim settlements, documentary collections may be used with condition documents such as survey reports and independent adjuster certificates. All outbound disbursements require underlying contract, invoice, tax clearance and the Service Trade International Revenue and Expenditure Declaration Form; a standalone Wise transfer cannot satisfy SAFE reporting and tax dossier requirements.Onshore CNY/USD deliverable forward contracts executed with a domestic commercial bank, supported by genuine current account exposures such as marine survey service fees receivable and operational payables.FX swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps with a domestic bank, limited to existing SAFE-registered foreign debt or long-term trade exposure.Offshore non-deliverable forwards in CNH may be used by the Taiwan parent or offshore affiliates, but the onshore WFOE may not enter speculative NDF positions; onshore hedging must be delivered and bank-approved.FX options are permissible only when approved by the bank's treasury risk committee and matched with confirmed marine insurance claim settlement inflows.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to open domestic deposit accounts, RMB clearing records, FDI capital-account collection services or statutory capital verification reports for an onshore mainland China WFOE. They are legally barred from serving as the primary treasury rail for this entity. An EMI can be used only as a secondary gate for incidental overseas supplier payments if routed through a licensed partner bank, but it cannot replace a tier-one domestic commercial bank for SAFE filing, foreign exchange conversion, tax withholding, or capital account compliance. WFOE treasury operations must maintain their core clearing accounts with an onshore bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File must be prepared if the aggregate of all related-party transactions (including marine survey service fees, reinsurance premium sharing, and claims commissions) exceeds NT$200 million in a given year. A Master File is mandatory for multinational groups with annual consolidated revenue exceeding NT$2 billion. The Country-by-Country Report is required when group annual consolidated revenue exceeds NT$27 billion.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan has not enacted a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute applicable to all entities. Instead, the Income Tax Act, the Company Act, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) impose a 'real substance' requirement in practice. The tax authority disregards a letterbox company that lacks a physical office, employee payroll, bank account, and substantive decision-making in Taiwan. The doctrine is applied both in the transfer pricing context (denial of treaty benefits for no business purpose) and in corporate registration (where the tax authority may re-attribute the income to the foreign parent).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act Article 110)

Statutory sanction: A fine of up to 3 times the underpaid tax amount and a daily surcharge of 0.05% for delayed payment. Deliberate non-filing with tax due over NT$120,000 carries imprisonment for a maximum of 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign falsified returns or order concealed offshore transactions are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties, and interest. The Taiwan tax authority may impose a travel ban (控制出境) and invoke criminal liability against the 'responsible person' (代表人), resulting in imprisonment. Corporate veil lifting under Company Act Article 139 applies when the entity is used to mask fraud.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority re-characterizes the entity as a transparent conduit, disallows all intercompany expenses, and imposes a 5% surcharge on the re-determined taxable income plus monthly interest. In egregious cases the registration itself is revoked by the Commercial Ministry.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and the actual beneficial owner may be held personally liable for the unpaid tax through the application of the 'piercing the corporate veil' doctrine, and may be disqualified from acting as a director in Taiwan for up to 3 years. If the shell structure is used to conceal AML risks, criminal imprisonment up to 2 years applies.
AML and Sanctions Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Control Act, fines of NT$500,000 to NT$5,000,000 and suspension of the company's business licence for repeat or serious violations; failure to maintain transaction records results in a fine of up to NT$1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to imprisonment for up to 2 years and a fine of up to NT$5,000,000 if they intentionally or through gross negligence fail to implement an AML program. The corporate veil is pierced where officers funnelled proceeds to a foreign parent; the court may attach personal bank accounts and impose a 5-year travel ban.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The tax authority makes a primary adjustment, increases taxable income to reflect an arm's-length price, and applies a 5% surcharge on the underpaid tax (maximum 30% of the deficiency). A secondary adjustment may deem a 20% withholding tax on constructive dividends if the correction is not remitted.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or implement non-arm's-length fees may be sued by the company for breach of fiduciary duties, and the tax authority may issue a deficiency assessment against the controlling foreign shareholder as a secondary taxpayer. In aggravated cases, a criminal referral for tax fraud is made under Article 110.

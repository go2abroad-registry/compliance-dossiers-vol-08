Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic portal: e-Tax Bureau (https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) established in mainland China, the entity must first obtain a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) during incorporation. The USCC serves as the sole Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all tax purposes. Subsequently, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the local STA office within 30 days of obtaining the business license, either online via the e-Tax Bureau or in person. The registration process requires submission of the business license, articles of association, legal representative identification, and proof of registered address. Upon completion, the entity is automatically enrolled for Corporate Income Tax (CIT), Value-Added Tax (VAT), and other applicable taxes. For foreign non-resident entities without a permanent establishment, a separate tax registration for withholding purposes is required, but this does not apply to the WFOE itself, which is a resident entity.

Mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below this threshold, small-scale taxpayer status applies with a 3% collection rate (or 1% for certain qualifying small-scale taxpayers).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (including Chinese Baijiu liquor) are eligible for a 0% VAT rate provided the exporter is a general VAT taxpayer, the goods are physically exported from mainland China to a foreign destination (including Taiwan), and the exporter has collected all required export documentation. The zero-rating applies to the export sale, and the exporter may claim a refund of input VAT attributable to the exported goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for air freight)
  • Export Proceeds Collection Certificate (formerly Inward Remittance Certificate)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Export Contract

All VAT invoices must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's Golden Tax System (GTS) using the official e-invoice platform. Invoices are transmitted in real-time to the STA's central database, and each invoice is assigned a unique anti-fraud code and digital stamp. The system enforces mandatory data validation, including buyer and seller USCCs, product descriptions, and tax rates. No separate physical clearance is required, but the electronic data must match the customs export declaration for zero-rated exports.

The STA conducts rigorous audits of export zero-rating claims. If documentation is incomplete, fraudulent, or if export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, the tax authority will disallow the zero-rating, require repayment of any VAT refund received, and impose a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day. In cases of deliberate fraud, penalties range from 50% to 500% of the tax evaded, and criminal liability may be pursued under the Criminal Law for tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a bifurcated convertibility regime. Current-account transactions, including the export of Chinese baijiu liquor, are generally convertible upon presentation of a bona fide underlying trade contract, customs declaration, commercial invoice, packing list, and transport documents. However, the New Taiwan Dollar is not a freely convertible settlement currency, so export invoicing to Taiwan is normally denominated in US dollars, RMB, or another acceptable major currency. Capital-account transactions remain strictly regulated: shareholder loans, paid-in capital injections, and profit repatriations require prior SAFE registration, FDI capital verification, audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and documented use of funds. For a WFOE, foreign registered capital must be injected through a dedicated capital account and can be converted into RMB under the voluntary settlement policy strictly for working-capital purposes. Profit remittance to the overseas parent is permitted only after annual statutory audit, corporate income tax settlement, and submission of a board resolution, with applicable dividend withholding tax paid. Export trade proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account within the statutory period and are continuously monitored through SAFE's cross-border receipt and payment verification system. Overall, convertibility is liberal for genuine trade but procedurally robust, and there is no free-floating convertibility for speculative or unrelated capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; the exporter may voluntarily convert 0% to 100% of foreign-currency receipts held in an export settlement account. However, the full amount of export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic SAFE-supervised trade settlement account within 90 calendar days from the date of customs export declaration. If collection extends beyond 90 days due to commercial terms, the WFOE must submit a deferred collection report through the banking channel to SAFE. Banks validate trade authenticity against the customs declaration number, invoice, and logistics evidence before any crediting or settlement.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and largely prohibited for primary banking. PRC foreign-exchange law requires all cross-border trade receipts and expenditures to be settled through designated domestic banks registered with SAFE. Offshore fintech EMIs, such as global digital wallet providers or electronic money institutions without a mainland China banking license, cannot act as the primary settlement rail for a Chinese WFOE. They may provide ancillary payment collection tools outside China, but the funds must ultimately be repatriated through a licensed domestic bank under a verified trade background. Attempts to use unlicensed EMIs for export proceeds repatriation would trigger AML red flags and could result in SAFE penalties, denial of customs verification, and freezing of assets. Therefore, the WFOE must bank with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank; fintech EMI usage is limited only to short-term foreign marketing or client-side convenience and never as a substitute for regulated cross-border clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound FDI, the WFOE must first obtain the SAFE Foreign Investment Registration Certificate from the local administration. Shareholders' capital transfers are wired from the overseas parent's bank to the WFOE's capital verification account at a designated Tier-1 Chinese bank using SWIFT MT103 with the purpose code 'Foreign Direct Investment – Registered Capital'. The remittance statement must cite the SAFE registration number. After receipt, the bank performs capital verification, records the transaction in the SAFE system, and upon presentation of the enterprise operating license and board resolution, the funds may be converted into RMB under the voluntary settlement policy. The RMB proceeds must be held in a designated enterprise account and strictly used for operating expenses, procurement of baijiu inventory, and permissible working capital. Any change in investment structure requires an amendment to SAFE registration before further flows. For export trade, inbound payment from Taiwan buyers is routed to the WFOE's export settlement account using SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'Foreign Trade Receipt – Export of Goods', accompanied by the relevant customs declaration and proof of shipping.Outbound funds from the WFOE primarily consist of supplier payments for raw materials, bottling services, freight and insurance, packaging materials, and certain cross-border taxes or fees. Corporate-to-corporate payments are executed via SWIFT MT103 with a proper transaction purpose code and supporting trade documents. For larger and recurring import transactions, the WFOE may open irrevocable documented commercial letters of credit (L/Cs) at the same Tier-1 bank for the benefit of domestic suppliers, guaranteeing payment against transport and quality certificates. Documentary collections under the ICC Uniform Rules are acceptable for smaller payments with known counterparties. All outbound disbursements must pass through SAFE's cross-border monitoring system; no cash withdrawals, nonsensical split payments, or settlements through unlicensed third parties are permitted. For Taiwan-bound export proceeds, the WFOE may also use documents against acceptance or payment with a confirming bank if the Taiwan buyer requires trade finance. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Enterprise Income Tax rate on worldwide income for a PRC-resident Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, unless a lower preferential rate applies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% of gross dividends paid by the PRC-resident WFOE to a non-resident enterprise shareholder, including a Taiwan-registered corporate shareholder, subject to any reduction under the applicable cross-strait tax arrangement or a recognized tax treaty provision.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 5 months after the fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year WFOE, the deadline is 31 May of the following year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 15 days after the end of each quarter; for a calendar-year WFOE, the deadlines are 15 April, 15 July, 15 October and 15 January of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File under SAT Public Announcement 2016 No. 42 arises for the WFOE if annual related-party transactions exceed any of: RMB 200 million for tangible asset buy/sell transactions; RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers or licenses; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; or RMB 40 million for all other related-party transactions. A Master File is required when the global ultimate parent of the group has consolidated revenue above RMB 5.5 billion in the prior fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime as seen in certain offshore financial centres. Instead, China enforces substance-over-form, the general anti-avoidance rule under EIT Law Article 47, beneficial ownership requirements, controlled foreign corporation rules and related-party transaction documentation to disregard artificial structures. A mainland WFOE and its Taiwan intermediate holding company must demonstrate actual offices, employees, risk control, board decision-making and real commercial functions to avoid recharacterization of profits and denial of treaty-type relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filing

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may reassess tax due with a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax; criminal conviction for tax evasion under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 can lead to up to 7 years' imprisonment and fines.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives, financial officers and beneficial shareholders who directed or concealed the evasion may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and civil damages; courts may order travel bans, exit restrictions, asset freezes and imprisonment.
Export Tax Rebate and Customs Fraud

Statutory sanction: Repayment of all fraudulently obtained export tax refunds, administrative penalties of 1 to 5 times the refund amount, customs penalties and forfeiture; criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 204 can include imprisonment up to life and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible staff may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, travel bans, confiscation of personal assets and disqualification from serving as corporate officers; the corporate veil will be pierced where illicit intent and personal control are proved.
Shell Company / Conduit Structure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard the artificial arrangement under general anti-avoidance rules, recharacterize income, deny treaty or cross-strait relief, assess the resident entity at 25%, apply 10% dividend withholding tax and impose penalties and daily surcharges; the controlled foreign corporation rules can currently tax undistributed profits in the hands of mainland controlling shareholders.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors who created or operated the conduit may be jointly liable for restored taxes; courts may issue travel bans, freeze bank accounts and impose criminal sanctions for fraudulent avoidance.
AML and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: PRC AML Law and Criminal Law Article 191 authorize account freezing, asset confiscation, regulatory fines, customs penalties and imprisonment for money laundering; criminal penalties may be aggravated when cross-border trade-based laundering is proved.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners, compliance officers and responsible directors can be prosecuted personally, with exposure to imprisonment, confiscation of personal property, travel bans and prohibition from re-entering mainland or Taiwan financial sectors.
Transfer Pricing Non-Arm's-Length and Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income, impose a daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge, apply a transfer pricing penalty where documentation is false or incomplete, and assess a withholding penalty of 50% to 3 times the tax not withheld under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69; retrospective adjustments can cover 5 to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who sign false transfer pricing disclosures or knowingly fail to withhold face personal enforcement, civil damages, travel bans and, in egregious cases, criminal liability for fraud or tax evasion.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Taiwan Companies Act for a general trading and brokerage company. However, the subscribers must fully subscribe and pay the stated capital before registration. The paid-in capital must be deposited into a dedicated paid-in capital settlement account at a Taiwanese bank, and a bank-issued certificate of paid-in capital must be filed with the Registrar. The capital may not be returned, withdrawn, or transferred out of the account prior to issuance of the Company Registration Certificate. The Investment Commission may impose a higher paid-in capital threshold or a proof-of-funds requirement at the time of foreign-investment approval.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); primary digital portal is the National Unified Standard e-Tax Platform at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn with identity authentication at https://tpass.chinatax.gov.cn; export-related records are exchanged with the General Administration of Customs (GACC) through the China International Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign parent incorporates the WFOE as a limited liability company with the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) under the Foreign Investment Law, obtaining the Business License containing the statutory 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Step 2: Under the 'one license, one code' and 'multi-license integration' reforms, the USCC is automatically recognized by the STA as the enterprise taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT registration number; no separate tax registration application is required for the entity. Step 3: Within 30 days of license issuance, the WFOE must complete tax-registration information confirmation on the e-Tax platform, file its bank account particulars, submit its accounting system and bookkeeping filings, apply for invoice quota, and confirm general VAT taxpayer status once annual taxable sales reach or exceed the statutory threshold. Step 4: Where the WFOE pays dividends, interest, royalties, or fees to non-resident shareholders or counterparties, it must register as a withholding agent; the WFOE itself is a PRC tax resident, so no separate branch or permanent-establishment registration applies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current account: fully convertible in practice for goods, services, and routine profit remittances since China's acceptance of IMF Article VIII obligations (effective December 1996). A WFOE may wire distributable dividends to its Taiwan shareholder after: (i) the annual statutory audit, (ii) final settlement of Corporate Income Tax via the annual reconciliation return (汇算清缴) with payment of any tax due, and (iii) submission to the remitting bank of the audited balance sheet, income statement, board dividend resolution, and the tax-payment certificate (完税证明). The bank applies an authenticity review only; there is no pre-approval quota and no SAFE license step. Capital account: restricted. Registered-capital injections require prior SAFE FDI registration; the imported capital can then be used for genuine operating purposes under the 'willful settlement' (意愿结汇) policy, but converted RMB earmarked for future use must remain in a dedicated settlement-pending account (结汇待支付账户). Shareholder loans, buyback of equity, and dissolution-remittance are separate capital items requiring either SAFE registration or approval. Cross-strait RMB: RMB remittances between the WFOE and Taiwan counterparties are permitted via the cross-strait RMB clearing mechanism without special quota, provided the underlying trade or investment documents evidence genuine transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Repatriation: all export proceeds from granular elemental sulfur (HS 2503.00) must be credited to the WFOE's current-account foreign-exchange account at a licensed SAFE bank onshore; offshore retention requires SAFE case-by-case approval. Collection timeline: proceeds should be collected within 90 calendar days from the date of the customs export declaration; collections beyond 90 days must be reported as delayed collection (延期收款) in the SAFE Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring Platform within 30 days of actual receipt, and systematic delays will trigger an aggregate trade-inspection (总量核查) downgrade. Conversion: since July 2012 the mandatory surrender ratio is zero - the WFOE may retain 100% of export receipts in USD or convert to RMB at its discretion by onshore spot settlement; the SAFE 2019 reform (Public Notice [2019] No.28) further removed the former foreign-exchange account retention ceiling; A-class trade-status enterprises are nevertheless expected to demonstrate an annual export-receipt collection coverage ratio generally at or above 90% by customs value, and invoicing, customs declarations, and receipts must be reconcilable in the monitoring platform.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), a statutory sub-ministry authority executing the foreign-exchange administration powers of the People's Bank of China (PBOC). For the Taiwan leg, the PBOC also coordinates with the Bank of China Taipei Branch, which acts as the designated RMB clearing bank for Taiwan under the Cross-Strait Financial Cooperation Agreement of 2009, and with the Taiwan Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) for inter-bank correspondence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File thresholds generally include annual related-party tangible-asset transactions exceeding RMB 200 million, financial-asset transactions exceeding RMB 100 million, intangible-asset transactions exceeding RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceeding RMB 40 million. A Master File is generally required where the China entity belongs to a multinational group with consolidated annual revenue of at least RMB 5 billion and specified cross-border related-party transactions or other prescribed conditions exist. Thresholds and exemptions must be tested for the relevant fiscal year and transaction category.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate a separate regime universally titled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is enforced through the Enterprise Income Tax Law, beneficial-ownership rules, general anti-avoidance rules, permanent-establishment principles, controlled foreign company rules, thin-capitalization rules, treaty anti-abuse standards, and transfer-pricing provisions. A company must demonstrate real functions, personnel, premises, assets, decision-making, commercial purpose, and risk control. A conduit company lacking substantive ownership or business activity may be denied treaty benefits and may face tax reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Jurisdictional misclassification and unauthorized business scope

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, loss of preferential treatment, administrative fines, business-scope correction orders, suspension, license revocation, and possible confiscation of unlawful income. Transactions may also create customs, VAT, foreign-exchange, and regulatory liabilities.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face civil liability for losses, administrative sanctions, restrictions on serving as responsible persons, and criminal liability where the conduct involves fraud, illegal business operations, smuggling, or intentional tax evasion. Personal travel restrictions may be imposed through judicial enforcement for unpaid obligations.
Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid tax, 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge, and administrative fines generally ranging from 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. Serious cases may be referred for criminal prosecution under the Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors, legal representatives, finance managers, and other directly responsible persons may face fines and imprisonment. Tax crimes can result in civil restitution, asset preservation, restrictions on consumption, and travel restrictions.
False or fraudulent VAT invoices

Statutory sanction: Input-tax denial, tax recovery, confiscation, substantial fines, invoice-related sanctions, and criminal penalties that may include long-term imprisonment depending on the amount and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, finance officers, and employees who knowingly participate may be criminally liable independently of the company's limited liability. Civil restitution, asset seizure, professional restrictions, and travel controls may apply.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Special tax adjustment, additional tax, late-payment interest calculated under the special adjustment rules, possible deemed withholding, and penalties for failure to provide reliable Local File, Master File, or related-party reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors may face administrative and civil consequences for deliberate misstatement or breach of fiduciary duties. Criminal exposure arises where transfer-pricing manipulation is part of tax evasion, invoice fraud, bribery, or fraudulent foreign-exchange activity.
Beneficial-ownership and treaty-abuse failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, withholding at the full domestic rate, recovery of underwithheld tax, late-payment surcharge, and anti-avoidance reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors and tax officers may face liability for knowingly submitting false residence or beneficial-ownership evidence. Fraudulent documentation may lead to criminal prosecution and enforcement against personal assets.
Illegal profit repatriation and foreign-exchange violations

Statutory sanction: Dividend reversal, tax recovery, foreign-exchange penalties, confiscation of unlawful gains, bank-account restrictions, and possible criminal liability for serious illegal foreign-exchange operations.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore improperly distributed funds and compensate the company or creditors. Serious cases may result in imprisonment, travel restrictions, disqualification from corporate office, and enforcement against personal assets.
Anti-money laundering and trade-based money laundering

Statutory sanction: Transaction rejection, enhanced due diligence, account freezing, suspicious-transaction reporting, administrative penalties, confiscation, and criminal prosecution for money laundering or predicate offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance personnel may face criminal liability if they knowingly facilitate laundering or conceal criminal proceeds. Civil restitution, asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment may apply.
Customs, commodity-origin, sanctions, and controlled-trade violations

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessment, confiscation, fines, cargo seizure, import or export suspension, license consequences, and criminal prosecution for smuggling or other customs offenses.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives and responsible officers may incur criminal liability for intentional customs fraud or sanctions evasion, including imprisonment, fines, asset confiscation, civil restitution, and travel restrictions.
Permanent establishment and unauthorized offshore activity

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax, VAT, withholding, interest, penalties, and possible retroactive tax registration or establishment recognition.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for unpaid corporate taxes caused by deliberate avoidance and personal sanctions where the conduct involves concealment, false filings, or obstruction of tax inspection.
Failure to preserve accounting and tax evidence

Statutory sanction: Expense disallowance, deemed-profit assessment, denial of treaty relief, administrative fines, adverse evidentiary presumptions, and tax adjustment based on available information.

Director personal liability: Directors and finance officers may be sanctioned for obstruction, destruction of records, or intentional non-compliance. Criminal liability may arise where document destruction conceals tax evasion, fraud, or money laundering.

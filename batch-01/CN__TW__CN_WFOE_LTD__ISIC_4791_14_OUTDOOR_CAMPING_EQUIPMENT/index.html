Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through local provincial and municipal tax service bureaus; the online electronic portal is the National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax portal) administered by the STA.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident investor establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise in Mainland China, the WFOE must: (1) obtain notarized, apostilled or legalized foreign investor documentation and board resolutions; (2) complete foreign investment filing or negative-list review with the Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) and business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), obtaining the Business License and the Unified Social Credit Code, which functions as the statutory taxpayer identification number for tax, customs, and social insurance; (3) within 30 days of Business License issuance, complete tax registration through the National Electronic Tax Bureau portal by submitting the articles of association, lease or address certificate, investor identity documents, and bank account information; (4) complete VAT taxpayer status election, whether small-scale taxpayer or general taxpayer, based on projected turnover; (5) register separately with the local tax authority for urban construction tax, education surcharges, payroll withholding, and stamp duty; (6) register with the General Administration of Customs for export procedures and with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for foreign-exchange receipts; (7) bind the e-Tax account and receive the electronic tax registration certificate. No separate TIN certificate is issued beyond the Unified Social Credit Code.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Chinese VAT general taxpayer may claim zero-rate, being exemption with credit or refund, for outdoor camping equipment exported from the Mainland customs territory, including deliveries to Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macau, provided: the goods are physically exported and cleared by Mainland customs under an export declaration; the exporter maintains complete input VAT documentation; the goods are not on the export prohibition or restriction list; export sales are evidenced by commercial contracts, customs declarations, and logistics documents; and export proceeds are repatriated and settled in accordance with SAFE requirements. Cross-border e-commerce retail export goods may also qualify when order, payment, and logistics data are transmitted electronically through the China International Single Window. Qualifying offshore and digital services supplied to overseas recipients and wholly consumed outside Mainland China may also be zero-rated or exempt under the cross-border services negative list.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (export goods declaration submitted through the China International Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the Mainland customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or SAFE banking foreign-exchange receipt record evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export sales contract or customer purchase order
  • VAT invoice (export version) or cross-border e-commerce export declaration data in the customs unified system

Mandatory digital invoicing under the Golden Tax Phase IV framework: VAT invoices must be issued through the STA's Electronic Tax Bureau platform or certified API integrations, and invoice data must be transmitted in real time to the STA. Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices carry encrypted tax-control codes and digital signatures that serve as the anti-fraud stamp; no manual physical stamping is required once the invoice is issued and cleared through the national electronic invoice system.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to joint audit by the STA, General Administration of Customs, and SAFE, using Golden Tax IV data matching, customs declaration comparison, logistics trail verification, and foreign-exchange archive checks. Missing or inconsistent substantiation results in denial or clawback of VAT exemption or refund amounts plus statutory late-payment surcharges and interest. Fraudulent claims, including forged customs declarations, false bills of lading, phantom exporters, circular foreign-exchange arrangements, or fake invoices, attract administrative fines, tax-credit blacklisting and heightened risk-monitoring status, revocation of VAT refund qualifications, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent export tax-refund claims under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed foreign exchange system under PBOC/SAFE. The current account has attained factual convertibility: cross-border trade in goods and services, including export collections and payment of trade-related fees, can be settled through designated banks against verifiable commercial documents. The capital account remains tightly controlled, and every cross-border capital event — inbound direct investment, foreign borrowings, profit repatriation, share transfer, or liquidation — must be registered and approved or recorded by SAFE. For a Taiwan-owned WFOE, foreign exchange capital remitted into the registered capital account cannot be used at will; it must be converted into RMB only within the scope permitted by the FDI settlement rules and cannot be used for working capital outside the declaration. Profit remittance by the WFOE to the Taiwan parent requires audited financial statements, tax payment certification, and a SAFE-controlled foreign exchange payment, but there is no quarantine surcharge. Export receipts are no longer subject to mandatory surrender; however, they must be repatriated into China and reported to SAFE through the goods trade monitoring system. Offshore NDF arrangements for CNY are not an onshore corporate solution, and all hedging must be genuine-demand based at licensed domestic banks. This dual-track structure necessitates treasury planning that segregates current account flows from capital account flows at every transaction level.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to China and credited to the enterprise's current account foreign exchange settlement account within the normal trade settlement cycle; there is no statutory mandatory conversion percentage, and the WFOE may retain 100% of export receipts in foreign currency or voluntarily convert them into RMB through a designated forex bank. The bank must report each receipt or payment to SAFE under the trade-in-goods monitoring system within 30 days, presenting the customs declaration, invoice, and logistic evidence supporting the underlying transaction.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low for offshore digital EMI platforms. A mainland China WFOE is not eligible to open a primary settlement account with overseas fintech EMIs such as U.K., Singapore, or Hong Kong e-money institutions, because those entities are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to operate in mainland China and cannot deliver onshore CNY clearing or compliant SAFE reporting. Stripe does not directly onboard mainland China incorporated entities, while PayPal China holds a non-bank payment license and may be used only as a downstream e-commerce collection channel; the final corporate treasury and FX clearing must still sit with a licensed domestic bank. Fintech EMIs should therefore be positioned only as supplementary merchant collection aggregators, never as the regulated banking rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan parent must obtain the applicable investment approval and the China WFOE must complete SAFE FDI registration before any funds are sent. The efficient clearing route is a single SWIFT remittance in USD or HKD from the Taiwan shareholder to the WFOE's designated FDI foreign exchange capital account at a Tier-1 bank, with the purpose coded as direct investment equity capital injection. Upon receipt, the bank matches the remittance to the SAFE FDI record and capital verification reference; funds can be converted into RMB through settlement against the capital account, and a China-certified public accountant issues the capital verification report. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for offshore digital EMI platforms. A mainland China WFOE is not eligible to open a primary settlement account with overseas fintech EMIs such as U.K., Singapore, or Hong Kong e-money institutions, because those entities are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to operate in mainland China and cannot deliver onshore CNY clearing or compliant SAFE reporting. Stripe does not directly onboard mainland China incorporated entities, while PayPal China holds a non-bank payment license and may be used only as a downstream e-commerce collection channel; the final corporate treasury and FX clearing must still sit with a licensed domestic bank. Fintech EMIs should therefore be positioned only as supplementary merchant collection aggregators, never as the regulated banking rail.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a wholly foreign-owned limited liability company in Taiwan, the procedure to obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) is as follows: (1) Incorporate the company with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) by filing the Articles of Incorporation and obtaining a Company Registration Certificate. (2) Apply for a Business Registration Certificate from the local National Taxation Bureau, which issues a Uniform Business Number (UBIN) that serves as the TIN. (3) Register for Business Tax (VAT) if the expected monthly sales exceed NT$200,000. (4) For foreign investors, obtain prior approval from the Investment Commission of MOEA under the Statute for Investment by Foreign Nationals. The entire process typically takes 2-3 weeks and requires submission of the parent company's incorporation documents, a power of attorney, and proof of capital remittance.

Taiwan mandates the use of electronic invoices (e-invoices) for businesses with annual sales exceeding NT$30 million. The system requires real-time transmission of invoice data to the Ministry of Finance's e-invoice platform via certified software. There is no real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping, but the e-invoice system integrates with the government's Uniform Invoice Regulations, and non-compliance can result in penalties.

If zero-rating is claimed without proper documentation or if the foreign exchange is not repatriated within 180 days, the tax authority may impose a penalty of up to 5 times the tax amount, plus interest. Retrospective clawback of zero-rated input tax credits may also apply, and fraudulent claims can lead to criminal prosecution under the Tax Collection Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China's currency convertibility regime is bifurcated between current account and capital account. Current account receipts and payments for trade and services are freely convertible for genuine transactions upon submission of supporting documents such as contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and shipping documents to a designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account items, including direct investment, remain under a registration and approval system administered by SAFE. For this Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, the inward FDI equity injection from the Taiwanese shareholder constitutes a capital account inflow that must be registered with SAFE, placed in a dedicated capital account or later settled to CNY only for approved business purposes, and supported by a capital verification report issued by a Chinese certified public accountant. Profit remittance to Taiwan is treated as a current account income item and is not subject to any annual quota, provided the WFOE has filed audited financial statements, settled corporate income tax, and withheld the statutory dividend withholding tax (generally 10% in the absence of tax treaty relief). The Taiwanese investor must separately obtain outbound investment approval from Taiwan's Investment Commission (MOEAIC) and the remittance must pass through a Taiwanese bank that applies Taiwan's cross-border reporting thresholds. The RMB remains a managed float currency and is not freely tradable offshore for speculative purposes; any hedging instrument must be supported by an identified underlying commercial or investment exposure. In summary, the WFOE faces moderate convertibility friction in the form of documentary verification and registration timelines, but there are clear, executable procedures for FDI deployment, operational foreign exchange purchase/sale, and dividend repatriation, which makes the overall regime administratively navigable for a bona fide Ro-Ro shipping enterprise.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current account management framework, service trade export proceeds, including ocean freight and Ro-Ro vessel transport income, must be repatriated into China and credited to a domestic foreign exchange account within 180 days after the regulatory reporting of the export receipt. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0% because the former forced surrender regime was abolished in 2012; the WFOE may freely choose to sell the foreign exchange to a designated bank for CNY or retain it in an approved foreign exchange settlement account for future current account payments. However, 100% of the export foreign currency receipts must be brought onshore, cannot be left offshore, and must be recorded in the cross-border receipt verification platform. Any use of retained proceeds to settle imports, pay shipping agent fees, or fund other approved current account expenses must be supported by verified commercial documents and reported to the supervising bank.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, exercising delegated authority from the People's Bank of China (PBOC); the Taiwan remittance leg is supervised by the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Legal viability for offshore digital EMI service providers (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut, Currencycloud) to act as the primary banking channel for a mainland WFOE is low. These fintechs are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in China, cannot maintain SAFE foreign exchange registrations, cannot open onshore RMB settlement accounts, and are not permitted to provide capital verification services for FDI injections. Their role is restricted to ancillary multi-currency collection in offshore accounts or prepaid card functionality, which is inadequate for satisfying China's treasury, PBOC, and SAFE compliance obligations. Domestic Chinese payment institutions such as Alipay and WeChat Pay also lack the full corporate banking deposit and cross-border FX settlement license required for a WFOE under SAFE monitoring. Therefore, the WFOE must maintain its primary operational and capital accounts with a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwanese shareholder must first secure an outbound investment permit from Taiwan's Investment Commission (MOEAIC) for its mainland China investment. The remittance is then executed via SWIFT MT103 from a Taiwanese bank to the WFOE's designated USD or TWD capital account held with a Tier-1 Chinese bank. The remittance instruction must specify the SAFE FDI purpose code 608 and be accompanied by the investment certificate, the board resolution, and the signed capital injection agreement. The receiving Chinese bank will verify the funds and coordinate the SAFE FDI registration, after which the WFOE obtains a foreign exchange registration IC card. The capital verification report is prepared by a Chinese CPA and filed to the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and SAFE within the statutory period. Where the injection exceeds USD 5 million, the bank may request an internal cross-border compliance approval from its head office, but the route remains fully executable for a bona fide shipping enterprise.For operational trade settlements, the WFOE will use SWIFT MT103 for direct foreign currency payments to overseas charterers, port agents, fuel suppliers, and marine insurers, always supported by the underlying commercial contract and invoice. For large procurement of vehicles, vessels, or emergency spare parts, the WFOE can issue or receive documentary Commercial Letters of Credit via SWIFT MT700, with the documents representing the bill of lading and insurance certificate. Documentary collections (bank collection) are appropriate for freight receivables where the exporter and importer prefer a low-cost payment against documents rather than a letter of credit. All outbound foreign currency payments must be submitted through the bank's SAFE trade verification system, and the bank will require the contract, invoice, customs declaration (if goods), and a written explanation of the business rationale. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% PRC Enterprise Income Tax (CIT) standard rate on net taxable income; a reduced 15% rate is not available unless the WFOE separately obtains a qualifying high-technology or encouraged-industry certification, which is generally outside the ordinary Ro-Ro vessel operation profile.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% PRC withholding tax on the gross dividend paid by the WFOE, a resident enterprise, to its Taiwan non-resident shareholder; the WFOE is the statutory withholding agent and must remit the withheld tax to the STA within 15 days after the dividend is declared or paid, whichever is earlier.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a calendar fiscal year, the annual CIT return must be filed and the final CIT liability paid no later than May 31 of the following year; quarterly prepayments are due by April 15, July 15, October 15, and January 15 following the close of the relevant quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under STA Public Notice [2016] No. 42, contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation thresholds are transaction-type based: controlled tangible property transactions above RMB 200 million per fiscal year, controlled financial transactions above RMB 100 million, controlled intangible asset transactions above RMB 100 million, controlled service transactions above RMB 100 million, and other controlled transactions above the statutory threshold; an annual related-party transaction declaration is embedded in the CIT filing package and must comprehensively identify all Taiwan-affiliated charter, loan, service, royalty, and vehicle-handling arrangements.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the STA applies a robust substantive-beneficial-ownership doctrine under SAT Announcement No. 60 (2015) and Circular 30 (2012) when granting treaty and cross-strait benefits; the WFOE must maintain actual functional premises, employees, board decisions, voyage negotiation, charter control, risk assumption, and cash management in the PRC; for the Taiwan shareholder to be the beneficial owner of dividends, interest, and royalties, its Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in Taiwan and it must not be a mere conduit passing income to a third-party resident; a management control failure shifts substance to the Taiwan parent and creates a risk that the tax authorities recharacterise the WFOE as a sham, deny treaty relief, and impose severe penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through related-party transfer pricing manipulation and failure to file contemporaneous TP documentation

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 44, Tax Collection Administration Law Articles 62 and 63, and STA Public Notice No. 42; the tax authority can make a gross transfer pricing adjustment, disallow non-deductible payments, impose interest and daily late surcharges, and impose fines of 50% to 100% of underpaid tax; where the annual tax evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and exceeds 10% of the tax payable, criminal tax evasion liability arises under Criminal Law Article 201 with fines and up to seven years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, chief financial officer, and directors who knowingly sign false tax filings or instruct concealment of related-party transactions may have the corporate veil pierced under PRC Company Law principles, making personal assets available for tax restitution; they face travel restrictions and blacklisting under the tax enforcement regime, prohibition from leaving China under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 44, and criminal exposure to up to seven years in prison, additional fines, and confiscation of illicit gains.
Shell Company and abusive beneficial ownership used to obtain reduced Cross-Strait withholding or treaty benefits

Statutory sanction: The STA is empowered to deny treaty and cross-strait benefits under Announcement No. 60 (2015) and Circular 30, recharacterising the transaction and imposing the 10% statutory withholding together with interest, late surcharges, and penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax; the payer is jointly liable for the under-withheld amount, and false declarations constitute tax fraud under the Tax Collection Administration Law with monetary penalties and possible criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders who establish or operate a sham Taiwan entity are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and damages to the WFOE, personal bank accounts and assets may be attached through civil restitution, the legal representative is placed on the serious tax non-compliant taxpayer blacklist and subject to exit bans, and deliberate tax evasion through sham ownership exposes responsible individuals to up to seven years imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 201, with or without forfeiture of diverted profits.
Thin capitalization and disguised equity financing through excessive related-party interest

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 46 and the joint MOF/STA thin-capitalisation rules disallow the interest paid on excessive related-party debt; the disallowed interest is recharacterised as a constructive dividend, causing 10% withholding tax plus VAT and surcharges, late payment surcharges, and potential transfer pricing penalties; the WFOE must correct all controlled transaction schedules and pay the additional CIT and withholding taxes with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who approve or execute artificial debt structures for the purpose of tax avoidance are civilly liable to restore corporate losses, their personal assets may be reached by the tax authorities in egregious cases, the legal representative is subject to blacklisting and travel bans until tax liabilities are settled, and criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 can result in imprisonment of not less than three years and up to seven years, together with fines and confiscation of the avoided-tax benefit where the evasion is found to be deliberate and substantial.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is generally convertible on the current account for genuine trade and service transactions, with banks performing document-based verification under SAFE's post-supervision system, but the currency remains largely non-convertible on the capital account without specific regulatory approvals. A Taiwan-invested WFOE must first complete SAFE's foreign direct investment registration to obtain a Foreign Exchange Business Registration and Investment Certificate; only then can the offshore shareholder remit capital into the entity's FDI capital settlement account. Inward capital must be settled under the Negative List framework and cannot be used for unapproved domestic equity investments, speculative purposes, or non-operating outflows. Profit and dividend remittances are treated as current account outward transfers only after the WFOE demonstrates audited retained earnings, payment of corporate income tax, and a genuine dividend resolution; banks typically require the annual tax filing, auditor report, and a non-resident witholding tax clearance certificate. Remittances are also governed by the Regulation on Foreign Exchange Administration, and each transfer must carry the appropriate purpose code and be supported by underlying contracts and invoices. There is no central-bank approval requirement for routine genuine profit repatriation, but any mismatch between declared purpose and actual payment, round-tripping red flags, or use of shell companies will trigger enhanced due diligence and possible suspension of the bank's facilitation role. Overall, the current account is open and freely convertible on a documentary basis, while the capital account is gated; the operative hurdle for the WFOE is documentary compliance rather than an explicit quantitative profit remittance quota.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's goods trade foreign exchange monitoring rules, export proceeds in foreign currency must be received through a domestic designated forex bank and reported to the Customs and SAFE reporting systems. Where no abnormal monitoring category applies, export receipts should be repatriated to China within 90 days from the date of customs clearance. The exporter may elect either to retain the proceeds in a domestic current-account foreign exchange account or to convert them into RMB, with no statutory mandatory percentage conversion ratio. If conversion is elected, the bank applies the prevailing market rate; if retention is elected, the retained foreign currency may only be used for genuine current-account payments and working capital needs. SAFE may assign a trade-inspection category of B or C to exporters showing abnormal collection patterns, which removes simplification privileges and requires full documentary exhaustion for every transaction.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

NOT VIABLE as a primary Chinese treasury rail. Under the PRC Licensing Measures for Payment Institutions and SAFE's cross-border forex rules, an offshore digital EMI without a Chinese payment institution license cannot legally open a domestic RMB account for a WFOE, cannot accept instructions from the FDI capital account, and cannot execute corporate forex conversion. Such EMIs may support ancillary merchant collection for non-regulated cross-border flows, but the WFOE's statutory foreign exchange, capital verification, export proceeds, and profit remittance transactions must be processed through a licensed domestic commercial bank. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be routed from the Taiwan shareholder's bank account through a direct Swift MT103 to the WFOE's FDI Foreign Currency Capital Account at a Chinese tier-1 bank, referencing the SAFE FDI registration certificate number and the correct capital-account purpose code for foreign direct investment. The WFOE must present the Foreign Investment Certificate, SAFE FDI Foreign Exchange Registration, board resolution, and executed capital contribution agreement before the bank will credit the account. After receipt, the bank conducts capital verification through the electronic FDI registration platform and issues a capital verification statement. Once verified, the capital can be settled into RMB only through the designated RMB capital expenditure account, and the resulting funds must be applied strictly within the approved business scope and Negative List limitations. Use of an omnibus or pooled account is prohibited for FDI capital injection; all transfers must carry a unique end-to-end transaction reference.The WFOE may settle genuine import transactions for surplus commercial aircraft parts using SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments and urgent disbursements once the customs declaration, invoice, and forex purpose code have been uploaded to the bank. For large OEM or high-value rotable purchases, a Commercial Letter of Credit at sight is recommended because it provides conditional security of payment and a fully trade-documented clearing rail. Documentary Collections under D/P or D/A terms are permissible and can be used for trusted repeat counterparties, but they carry higher settlement timing risk and require purchase order, inspection certificate, and transport document matching. Under the PRC Licensing Measures for Payment Institutions and SAFE's cross-border forex rules, an offshore digital EMI without a Chinese payment institution license cannot legally open a domestic RMB account for a WFOE, cannot accept instructions from the FDI capital account, and cannot execute corporate forex conversion. Such EMIs may support ancillary merchant collection for non-regulated cross-border flows, but the WFOE's statutory foreign exchange, capital verification, export proceeds, and profit remittance transactions must be processed through a licensed domestic commercial bank. Feasibility rating: Low; use only as a supplementary collection tool when the EMI is backed by a Chinese licensed clearing bank and never for capital account, export surrender, or SAFE-reportable flows.

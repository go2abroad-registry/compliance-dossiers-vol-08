Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Taiwan's tax authorities (Taiwan National Tax Administration) and banks typically reject a virtual office or commercial mailbox for a company engaged in e-commerce and online retail when the company is required to maintain a physical presence for VAT registration, inventory storage and tax audit purposes. For a WFOE engaged in online retail of 3D printers and consumables, a virtual office may be allowed only if the company can demonstrate an actual business address, a dedicated phone line, and the presence of records, premises and staff during business hours. However, to obtain a bank reference letter and open a corporate account, a physical leased office address in Taiwan is strongly recommended. The risk of rejection by the Taiwan Drug Administration (if applicable) is low; but the National Taxation Bureau requires a fixed place of business for business tax (VAT) registration. A commercial mailbox service that offers no physical office space will not satisfy the 'place of business' requirement.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Taiwan company limited by shares engaged in ISIC 4791 online retailing. The subscribed capital, however, must be genuine, fully paid in cash or approved assets, deposited into a Taiwan bank account in the company's name, and verified by a Taiwan-licensed CPA before the Department of Commerce will accept the registration application. No central escrow or blocked-account requirement applies to non-restricted businesses; the only restriction is that the paid-in capital may not be withdrawn or loaned back to shareholders before incorporation, because Article 9 of the Company Act penalizes false capital declarations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the national electronic tax service platform (China Electronic Tax Bureau e-Tax portal).

E-government filing portal

The WFOE is domesticated as a Chinese resident enterprise. Step 1: the non-resident corporate sponsor incorporates the WFOE as a limited liability company through the Market Regulation Administration, and receives a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Step 2: within 30 days of license issuance, the WFOE completes tax registration at the local STA branch; the USCC is converted into the statutory taxpayer identification number and VAT registration number. Step 3: the WFOE applies for general VAT taxpayer status where annual taxable sales equal or exceed RMB 5,000,000, and opens its tax account in the e-Tax portal. Step 4: for export online retail, the WFOE registers for export tax refund/exemption status and links its customs registration code and bank settlement account to the STA export tax system. Non-resident entities with no permanent establishment in China can obtain a separate withholding taxpayer ID under source taxation procedures, but the WFOE itself is a resident taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory from commencement of business; the compulsory general taxpayer threshold is annual taxable sales of RMB 5,000,000, with small-scale taxpayer simplified VAT at 1% and monthly turnover exemption of RMB 100,000. Export e-commerce WFOEs must register regardless of turnover to claim zero-rating.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods actually exported from mainland China to Taiwan and other non-mainland jurisdictions qualify for 0% VAT export zero-rating under the Chinese VAT law, provided the goods are subject to a valid customs export declaration, legal ownership passes to the overseas buyer, and consideration is received through a licensed foreign exchange bank in accordance with SAFE regulations. Desktop 3D printers and resin filament consumables sold online under ISIC 4791 qualify as tangible exported goods; digital offshore services would face a separate qualification test.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (Chinese customs export declaration)
  • Commercial invoice and certified packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued to the Taiwan/overseas buyer
  • Cross-border e-commerce export manifest including order list, payment list and logistics list
  • Bank export proceeds collection voucher issued by a SAFE-designated bank
  • Zero-rated VAT digital e-invoice and export tax refund application record

China mandates the issuance of fully digitalised electronic invoices through the STA electronic invoice service platform. The Golden Tax System Phase IV performs real-time central invoice clearance, assigns a national anti-fraud two-dimensional stamp, and automatically reconciles invoice output with customs export data before the buyer is invoiced.

Export zero-rating is an auditable preference. The exporter must retain the customs, logistics, commercial and foreign-exchange documentation for at least five years. If the STA or customs authority establishes that no export occurred, the export proceeds were not repatriated and verified within 180 days, or the documentation is forged, the zero-rating is cancelled and the VAT becomes payable with a daily late surcharge of 0.05% and an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the refunded VAT. Fraudulent export refunds with false declarations or fabricated foreign-exchange vouchers are criminally prosecutable as tax fraud or smuggling under Chinese law, with severe fines and imprisonment for serious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates under a managed floating exchange-rate regime with conditional RMB convertibility. Current-account items, including trade-related collections, service payments, and ordinary business remittances, are generally convertible once the WFOE presents the required commercial documents, tax certificates, and SAFE transaction declarations through a licensed foreign-exchange bank. Capital-account items, particularly inward FDI equity injections, shareholder loans, and any cross-border repayment obligations, remain subject to a strict registration-and-verification framework administered by SAFE. The Taiwan-invested WFOE must complete SAFE FDI foreign-exchange registration before any inward remittance, open a dedicated foreign-currency capital account with a designated domestic bank, and settle each capital tranche only after the bank verifies the underlying commercial rationale and investment conformity. Dividend repatriation to the Taiwanese shareholder is permitted only after annual statutory audits, corporate income tax final settlement, and a board-approved profit-distribution resolution; the remitting bank will independently require proof of tax clearance, accumulated-loss analysis, and the appropriate dividend-remittance purpose code. Full free convertibility does not exist. RMB converted through capital-account settlement cannot be diverted to prohibited industries, domestic equity investments, or unapproved onshore lending. The practical remittance hurdles include strict underlying-document matching, repetitive documentary checks for every outward payment, and continuing beneficial-ownership surveillance intended to prevent shell-company structures and trade-based money laundering.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds arising from 3D printer and resin-filament cross-border e-commerce sales must be repatriated into China within 90 days from the export customs declaration date. There is no mandatory conversion percentage in the current post-quota regime; the WFOE may retain 0% to 100% of the foreign currency in a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account or convert ratably into RMB through the designated bank. All receipts must be reported through SAFE's cross-border receipt/payment and trade-verification system, with the underlying order, logistics, customs, and invoice data available for bank reconciliation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign-exchange authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Direct onboarding of an offshore fintech EMI as the WFOE's primary treasury bank is legally unviable in mainland China. PBOC and SAFE reserve licensed banking, foreign-exchange settlement, and capital-account servicing for duly licensed Chinese financial institutions. The WFOE cannot lawfully maintain an onshore operating account or execute SAFE-regulated FX transactions through Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, or similar digital EMI platforms. Such firms may appear only as collection-side payment aggregators feeding export e-commerce proceeds into a licensed domestic settlement bank through a PBOC-licensed cross-border payment institution, and they must not handle FDI capital injection, dividend remittance, payroll clearing, or import settlement. The treasury spine should therefore remain with Bank of China, ICBC, or CCB, while Shopify Payments and Stripe payouts can be channelled to the bank account only through compliant aggregator arrangements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan shareholder must remit the registered capital from its own audited bank account through SWIFT MT103 with the remitter name matching the approved investor, the beneficiary as the WFOE, and the SAFE-mapped FDI purpose code for inbound direct-investment paid-in capital. The WFOE must complete FDI foreign-exchange registration with the receiving domestic bank before the wire arrives. Funds are credited to a dedicated foreign-currency capital account; for each settlement tranche the bank verifies the board call-for-capital resolution, the capital contribution schedule, a Chinese CPA capital verification report where required, and a non-prohibited-use undertaking. After conversion, the RMB is swept to the basic operational account for inventory, logistics, and marketplace expense disbursements, and every use must stay within the business scope of the WFOE.Use SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments to pre-vetted resin, filament, and hardware suppliers, always with import contracts and customs declarations mapped to the SAFE trade purpose code. Use commercial letters of credit for large-volume 3D printer parts and resin master batches where the supplier requires bank payment undertaking, with documents checked against Incoterms. Use documentary collections (DP/DA) for repeat suppliers in established banking jurisdictions. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is mandatory under SAT Public Notice 2016 No.42 when annual related-party tangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million, intangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, financial-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or related-party service transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File documentation is mandatory where aggregate annual related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion. Even if thresholds are not met, the WFOE should maintain a basic transfer pricing file whenever cross-border sales, royalties, or management fees are paid to the Taiwan parent.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not presently apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) or Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) as a separate statutory code. However, economic substance is enforced through PRC EIT Law Article 41 (arm's-length principle), Article 47 (General Anti-Avoidance Rule), SAT Public Notice 2009 No.601 (beneficial ownership), and the OECD BEPS minimum standards. For a Taiwan-owned WFOE conducting online retail, the CIGA equivalent requires the entity to perform the actual core functions that generate revenue: inventory procurement, warehousing, order fulfillment, pricing, marketing, customer service, payment settlement oversight, and after-sales support. If those activities are performed by the Taiwan parent or by a shell arrangement without WFOE employees, assets, or decision-making power, the Chinese tax authorities may disregard the WFOE's legal form, reattribute the profits to the economic substance, deny treaty/arrangement benefits, and impose special tax adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Concealment of Cross-Border Online Sales Revenue

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law Article 63: the tax authority will collect the unpaid tax and impose a fine of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid. Daily late-payment surcharge continues at 0.05% under Article 32 until full payment. If the amount evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and is more than 10% of the total tax payable, criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 applies: up to 3 years' imprisonment and a fine; where the amount evaded exceeds RMB 500,000 and exceeds 30% of the tax payable, imprisonment of 3 to 7 years applies.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced under PRC Company Law where the WFOE's capital is insufficient, assets are commingled with the Taiwan parent's, or the company is a mere sham. Directors who signed false tax filings may be personally liable for the unpaid tax to the extent the misused funds are traced to them. Personal bank accounts may be frozen, personal assets attached, the legal representative placed on the tax blacklist and high-consumption restriction list, and an exit ban imposed preventing travel outside China. Criminal responsibility attaches to the legal representative, CFO, and the directly responsible financial officers who knowingly prepared or signed the false return.
Abusive Transfer Pricing and Shell-Conduit Structure

Statutory sanction: PRC EIT Law Articles 41 and 47 authorize the tax bureau to adjust the WFOE's taxable income and recharacterize the transaction to reflect the arm's-length result. Under EIT Law Article 48, the tax authority may impose interest on the underpayment of tax for the years of adjustment, including a special adjustment interest rate. The adjusted amount remains subject to the late-payment rules. If the fabricated intercompany contracts are found to be false bookkeeping or sham transactions, Tax Administration Law Article 63 imposes the 50%-500% evasion penalty; if criminal intent is proved, Criminal Law Article 201 applies with imprisonment and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or signed retroactive, fictitious, or abusive intercompany agreements may be personally liable to the company and creditors for causing financial harm and to the state for the tax shortfall. The supervising tax authority can seek attachment of the directors' personal assets and joint liability under the corporate veil-piercing theory of PRC Company Law. A travel ban and 'dishonest person' blacklisting will apply to the legal representative. Where fraudulent invoices or forged contracts are used, the directors face criminal prosecution for false invoice documentation under PRC Criminal Law and tax evasion under Article 201.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance and False DTA Claim

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law Article 69: the WFOE as withholding agent will be ordered to withhold the unpaid amount and will be fined 50% to 300% of the amount that should have been withheld. The tax authority may also assess the Taiwan shareholder directly for the unpaid tax, together with late-payment surcharge. If the false DTA claim constitutes fraudulent understatement of Chinese-sourced dividend income, the 50%-500% tax evasion penalty under Tax Administration Law Article 63 applies; if the evaded tax reaches the Criminal Law Article 201 threshold, criminal liability is imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed the dividend resolution and knowingly failed to withhold can be held personally and severally liable for the unremitted withholding tax and penalties. If the dividend was used to strip assets from the WFOE and frustrate tax collection, the court may disregard limited liability and order the directors to repay the funds from personal assets. Exit bans and blacklisting apply, and directors who forged the Taiwan TRC or beneficial-ownership statements face criminal charges for forging official certificates under PRC Criminal Law Article 280, punishable by up to 3 years' imprisonment, and for tax evasion under Article 201, with maximum imprisonment of 7 years for major amounts.

None - Taiwan repealed the statutory minimum paid-in capital requirement in 2009; however, for a mainland-China-invested company, the subscribed capital must be fully remitted from outside Taiwan through approved banking channels and exchanged into New Taiwan Dollars in a preparatory bank account before the incorporation registration is certified. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax), with export/customs filings made through the China International Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

The China WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise and is not treated as a foreign non-resident. It obtains its tax identification number automatically at incorporation: the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) is the TIN, after completing the one-time taxpayer information registration with the local STA office within 30 days of the SAMR business-license issuance. The steps are: (1) incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation and obtain the USCC; (2) register for taxpayer status with the competent STA tax service hall or e-Tax portal, supplying the business license, articles of association, equity structure, accounting/tax filing information, and bank account certificate; (3) receive the Tax Registration Confirmation and activate an e-Tax account; (4) if the WFOE will export goods, file the Export Tax Refund/Exemption Registration through e-Tax or the International Trade Single Window; (5) for any foreign (including Taiwan) non-resident entity with China-source income but no establishment, that foreign entity must separately complete Non-resident Enterprise Tax Registration (or withholding registration) with the payer's STA office to obtain its non-resident TIN before payments are remitted. A pure export sale from the WFOE to Taiwan does not itself trigger Taiwan national tax registration for the WFOE unless the WFOE later creates a Taiwan permanent establishment or physical branch.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (PRC resident enterprise; taxed under Corporate Income Tax on worldwide income at the statutory 25% rate; no pass-through, partnership, or disregarded-entity treatment for a limited-liability WFOE).

Available election forms

None comparable to US check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. The PRC tax system imposes entity-level taxation by default. The only elective status available is the VAT general-taxpayer election for newly formed enterprises below the RMB 5,000,000 compulsory threshold, and the small low-profit enterprise CIT preferential rate is a statutory classification rather than a free election. Export tax-refund methodology is dictated by the entity's production or trading activity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory for every legally established WFOE from the date of incorporation; there is no turnover exemption for a PRC resident enterprise. The RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable-sales threshold is the mandatory boundary for General vs Small-scale VAT taxpayer status: annual taxable sales above RMB 5,000,000 require General taxpayer status; below it, the WFOE may still elect General status, which is practically necessary to claim input-VAT credits and export tax refunds. Dried goji berries/herbal botanicals that qualify as primary agricultural products are eligible for the reduced 9% VAT rate on domestic sales, but export sales are zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A PRC WFOE exporting dried goji berries/herbal botanicals from mainland China customs territory to Taiwan is eligible for the 0% VAT export rate under the Export Tax Rebate regime only if: (1) the WFOE is the exporter of record on the customs export declaration, not merely an agent; (2) the goods physically leave mainland China and are cleared into Taiwan as a separate customs territory; (3) the WFOE is a VAT General taxpayer and has valid VAT special input invoices; (4) the export sale is supported by a genuine commercial

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA's VAT Invoice System integrated with the Golden Tax Phase IV platform, using the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau. Fully digitalized e-invoices carry a unique invoice number and digital signature and are uploaded to the STA in real time; software integrators use STA-approved APIs, and all invoice issuance must occur in the e-Tax account or through STA-certified service providers. No physical fiscal stamp is applied; the anti-fraud control is the real-time issuance, authentication, and cross-matching of invoice data to customs declarations, export logistics, and foreign-exchange receipts through the International Trade Single Window data-sharing mechanism. Export invoices are issued as 0% export-rated e-invoices.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime with comprehensive current-account convertibility but strict capital-account controls. For a China-domiciled WFOE, inbound FDI equity subscriptions enter through the capital account and are registered with SAFE under the Foreign Investment Information Reporting System. These funds must be credited to a capital settlement account, settled into RMB through a bank-conducted capital settlement procedure, and ring-fenced for lawful business operations only. Current-account receipts from exporting dried goji berries and herbal botanicals are convertible into RMB after trade verification, allowing the exporter to retain foreign exchange or voluntarily settle at a designated bank; no mandatory surrender ratio is imposed. Profit repatriation to a Taiwan parent is feasible but only after year-end audit completion, tax settlement, submission of audited financial statements and a board resolution to the remitting bank; the outbound dividend is then processed as a current-account item. Onshore hedging remains tightly regulated, requiring genuine trade background, and access to offshore NDF markets is limited. The overall assessment is therefore a semi-convertible current-account system with capital-project liberation reserved for qualifying FDI and trade flows, and with compliance dependence on SAFE registration, bank verification, and demonstrable economic substance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from the dried goji berry and herbal wholesale business must be repatriated into China generally within 180 days of the customs export declaration, either by returning the foreign currency to a designated foreign-currency settlement account or by converting it to RMB at a licensed bank. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; SAFE allows exporters to retain 100% of export receipts in foreign currency or to settle 100% into RMB at their discretion. All proceeds must nevertheless be declared on the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System, and the bank must verify the supporting customs declaration, commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading. Offshore retention beyond the 180-day window is permitted only with a valid SAFE offshore retention filing or prior approval for specific trade structures.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); cross-strait settlement clearing coordination is effectuated through the Bank of Taiwan Taipei branch under the Cross-Strait Currency Settlement Agreement, while the People's Bank of China remains the central regulatory authority for all onshore PRC foreign-exchange matters

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low legal feasibility for servicing the WFOE entity. Under the PBOC Payment Licensing Rules and SAFE cross-border payment supervision, domestic non-bank undertakings cannot legally operate conventional EMI services, issue e-money corporate accounts, or launch cross-border payment/collection accounts for a PRC-incorporated WFOE without obtaining a Chinese payment institution license and SAFE trade-monitoring approval. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut Business, or Currencycloud are not licensed to receive equity capital contributions into the WFOE capital account, nor can they validly disburse trade payments while bypassing an onshore commercial bank. They may only serve as auxiliary payment-billing rails when partnered with a licensed Chinese bank or a qualified third-party payment processor like Alipay Global or Tenpay Global, and the underlying settlement must still pass through the domestic Tier-1 bank. The recommended viability rating is 'not substitutable'; the primary account architecture must rest on licensed PRC commercial banks, while EMI rails are limited to low-order receivable aggregation and minor ancillary FX automation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital for the CN WFOE must pass through the SAFE-registered FDI procedure: first the WFOE obtains a Foreign Investment Information Report code from the local foreign-exchange administration; second, the entity opens a dedicated Capital Settlement Account at an authorized Tier-1 commercial bank; third, the Taiwan investor transmits a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wire quoting the SAFE registration code and clearly denoting the purpose as 'equity injection for registered capital under foreign investment'; fourth, the receiving bank verifies the remitter against the registered shareholder list and performs capital conformity checking; fifth, the funds are recorded as foreign-investment capital and may be settled to RMB through the capital settlement process after verifying that the project is not on the foreign investment negative list; sixth, a Chinese certified public accountant issues a capital verification report after injection, and the WFOE files the report with the local administration for market regulation; and seventh, use of the settled RMB is restricted to lawful business operating expenses, capital construction, and working capital, with customs and VAT documentation retained for audit.Outbound trade disbursements for the dried goji berry and herbal wholesale activity are cleared via SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments, commercial letters of credit issued through MT700 with subsequent MT710 amendments and MT202 cover for trade finance settlement, and documentary collections using D/P and D/A instructions through correspondent banks. Each payment must be reconciled against the underlying cargo invoice, packing list, customs declaration, and transport document, and the transaction must be reported through the SAFE Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. For Taiwanese counterparties, cross-strait RMB clearing can be executed through the Bank of China Taipei branch or through the onshore CNH clearing window; USD-denominated settlements follow the normal correspondent-bank chain with the WFOE's designated Chinese bank acting as settlement bank and maintaining the outgoing MT103 audit trail.SAFE-approved deliverable foreign exchange forwards (primarily USD/CNY, HKD/CNY, and EUR/CNY) strictly for hedging current-account trade receivables and payables with full genuine underlying transactionsForeign exchange swaps executed onshore with licensed commercial banks in order to manage short-term currency turnover and cash-flow mismatches arising from goji berry export collection cyclesOffshore CNH nondeliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted only as a genuine hedge of real export receivable exposure through an overseas licensed bank; prohibited as a speculative standalone derivative for a mainland WFOECross-currency interest-rate swaps restricted to covering long-term shareholder loans or cross-border debt arrangements that are SAFE-registered and recorded under the macro-prudential foreign debt administration frameworkSAFE-approved structured forwards or vanilla currency options with mandatory trade underlying, knock-out or no-knock-out clauses, traded solely through designated PRC commercial banks and never through unlicensed digital platforms

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low legal feasibility for servicing the WFOE entity. Under the PBOC Payment Licensing Rules and SAFE cross-border payment supervision, domestic non-bank undertakings cannot legally operate conventional EMI services, issue e-money corporate accounts, or launch cross-border payment/collection accounts for a PRC-incorporated WFOE without obtaining a Chinese payment institution license and SAFE trade-monitoring approval. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut Business, or Currencycloud are not licensed to receive equity capital contributions into the WFOE capital account, nor can they validly disburse trade payments while bypassing an onshore commercial bank. They may only serve as auxiliary payment-billing rails when partnered with a licensed Chinese bank or a qualified third-party payment processor like Alipay Global or Tenpay Global, and the underlying settlement must still pass through the domestic Tier-1 bank. The recommended viability rating is 'not substitutable'; the primary account architecture must rest on licensed PRC commercial banks, while EMI rails are limited to low-order receivable aggregation and minor ancillary FX automation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% of net taxable income (Article 5, Income Tax Act; effective for fiscal years beginning 2018 and thereafter).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed to non-resident corporate shareholders, including mainland-China shareholders, under Art. 73 (Income Tax Act) and Art. 73-1 for retained-earnings distributions. Such dividends are subject to final withholding and no further income-tax filing is required for the recipient.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing period runs from 1 May to 31 May of the year immediately following the taxable year; for a fiscal year ending 31 December, the deadline is 31 May of the succeeding calendar year. Extensions may be granted to 31 August upon application.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar-year taxpayers: first prepayment by 30 June, second prepayment by 30 November; each installment equals 50% of the prior year's income tax liability unless an alternative estimate is elected.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared where annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed NT$100,000,000; Master File is required when the group's annual global revenue exceeds NT$3,000,000,000 and cross-border intercompany transactions exceed NT$200,000,000; Country-by-Country Report applies when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds NT$27,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not directly apply the EU Economic Substances Requirements (ESR), but its income tax law incorporates the Economic Substance Doctrine through Art. 43-1 of the Income Tax Act: transactions undertaken without commercial purpose or whose economic substance is the mere shifting of profits to a low-tax jurisdiction may be recharacterized. The tax authority applies a pragmatic substance analysis to shell WFOEs, requiring real office premises, employees, strategic-decision making, business risk management, local bank accounts, and audited financials. Taiwan's CFC rules (effective 2023) also impute the undistributed earnings of controlled foreign entities in low-tax jurisdictions to their Taiwanese shareholders, further reinforcing the need for substantive local activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Revenue Understatement

Statutory sanction: An understatement penalty of 1 to 3 times the amount of hidden tax under Article 110 of the Income Tax Act; additional criminal fines up to NT$500,000 under Article 36 of the Tax Collection and Enforcement Act; and imprisonment up to five years for intentional tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; courts can issue travel bans to prevent departure from Taiwan and impose personal asset attachment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority recharacterizes the entire profit as Taiwan-source business income under Article 43-1 and imposes the 20% corporate tax plus a 1x to 3x penalty; the Ministry of Economic Affairs may also revoke the WFOE's business license for exercising the company in a way that violates public order.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally reassessed under a veil-piercing theory if they knowingly allowed the sham structure; they are exposed to criminal sanctions for fraud, administrative revocation of employment visas, and court-imposed injunctions restricting access to Taiwan.
Failure to Withhold Withholding Tax on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: A fine of 20% of the unwithheld tax is imposed on the WFOE as the withholding agent (Article 114 of the Income Tax Act); if the withholding cannot be collected from the non-resident, the WFOE is liable for the full amount of tax.

Director personal liability: The responsible director is personally liable for the unwithheld tax plus penalties, and may be subjected to administrative execution against personal property; repeated non-compliance can trigger a travel ban and criminal prosecution.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: A monetary penalty of up to NT$50,000 for failure to produce documentation; the tax authority may also disregard the incomplete documentation and perform a discretionary assessment, imposing the 20% tax on an adjusted income plus penalties of up to 5% of the entire transaction value.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally responsible for ensuring accurate and complete TP documentation under the company law duties of care; knowing non-compliance may result in personal fines and a ban on holding company directorships in Taiwan.

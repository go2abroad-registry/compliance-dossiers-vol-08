Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Taiwan has no general statutory minimum paid-in capital for an ordinary company limited by shares. Capital must be sufficient for the proposed business and establishment costs. No general statutory escrow account is required, but the subscribed capital must be remitted to the company-designated Taiwan bank account and verified by a Taiwan certified public accountant before or during registration. Foreign investors must obtain foreign investment approval before capital remittance where the investment falls within the Foreign Investment Act.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain building-use and occupancy confirmation, fire safety equipment approval and periodic inspection from the local fire department, and structural or load verification for machinery and storage areas where required. Implement occupational safety controls under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including laser guarding, interlocks, ventilation, emergency stops, electrical safety, combustible-dust and fire-risk controls, personal protective equipment, training, chemical and waste inventories, and accident reporting. Obtain hazardous-work, pressure-equipment, lifting-equipment, or other specialized inspections if the installed equipment triggers those requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan has no general United States-style check-the-box election and no broad elective hybrid-mismatch classification regime. A Taiwan limited company is generally subject to Taiwan profit-seeking enterprise income tax on its Taiwan taxable income, while a Taiwan branch of a foreign company is taxed under branch rules. Business tax treatment is determined by statutory classification, including ordinary VAT treatment, small-business assessment treatment where legally available, and zero-rating for qualifying transactions; these are registrations or statutory treatments rather than freely elective entity classifications. Taiwan controlled-foreign-company and transfer-pricing rules may apply to qualifying Taiwan tax residents and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For small-business assessment purposes, the commonly applicable monthly sales thresholds are approximately NT$80,000 for goods businesses and NT$40,000 for service businesses. These thresholds do not provide a general exemption from registration where a business is carrying on taxable commercial activity, imports goods, operates through a taxable establishment, or is otherwise required to register. Ordinary VAT taxpayers generally register before commencing business and file bi-monthly business-tax returns.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualifying exports of goods may be zero-rated where the goods are exported from Taiwan under customs control and the export is supported by the customs declaration and related commercial records. The export transaction must be attributable to the Taiwan VAT registrant and must not be a domestic sale disguised as an export. Services may qualify for zero-rating where they are supplied to a foreign customer, used outside Taiwan, and satisfy the applicable statutory conditions; services performed or consumed in Taiwan generally do not qualify merely because the customer is foreign. The taxpayer must report the zero-rated sales in the VAT return and retain commercial contracts, purchase orders, invoices, shipping records, customs evidence, proof of foreign customer status, payment records, foreign-exchange settlement evidence, and evidence of offshore use for services. Input VAT refunds require valid input invoices or customs duty documents, proper accounting records, and reconciliation of the claimed input tax with the zero-rated export turnover.

  • Taiwan Customs Export Declaration or equivalent electronic customs record showing the Taiwan exporter, goods, value, destination, and export status
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Sales contract, purchase order, or other transaction agreement
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other transport document
  • Proof of export completion, including customs release or shipment confirmation where required
  • Foreign-exchange settlement or inward-remittance evidence, bank collection record, or legally acceptable alternative payment evidence
  • Input VAT invoices and import VAT payment documents supporting any refund claim
  • For offshore services: service agreement, foreign customer identification, deliverables, evidence of offshore use, invoice, and payment trail

Taiwan uses a government-supervised electronic invoice system rather than a universal Latin-American-style real-time pre-clearance model. Businesses required to issue electronic invoices must use the MOF e-Invoice Platform or an approved service provider, maintain approved electronic certificate credentials, generate compliant invoice numbers and formats, transmit invoice and deduction information through the prescribed system or API, and retain electronic records. Electronic invoices must contain statutory seller, buyer, transaction, tax, amount, date, and identification information. Certain B2B and B2C categories are subject to electronic-invoicing requirements and implementation schedules. Customs export declarations are processed through customs systems separately from VAT invoicing. Failure to issue, transmit, preserve, or correct electronic invoices can lead to tax assessments and administrative penalties.

The taxpayer must be able to reconcile the zero-rated invoice, VAT return, customs declaration, accounting ledger, shipping evidence, contract, and payment or foreign-exchange record. The National Taxation Bureau may deny zero-rating, reclassify the transaction as taxable domestic turnover, assess the unpaid 5% business tax, impose statutory interest, and recover or claw back improperly refunded input VAT. False export declarations, fabricated shipping documents, sham offshore-service claims, circular transactions, undisclosed domestic use, or fictitious payment evidence may trigger administrative penalties under the Business Tax Act and related tax laws; tax-evasion penalties can be calculated by reference to the tax evaded and may reach multiples of the evaded tax. Serious cases may also create criminal exposure under Taiwan tax, customs, document-fraud, or money-laundering laws. Export proceeds are generally subject to Taiwan foreign-exchange settlement and reporting requirements, with a commonly applicable six-month period equivalent to 180 days; extensions, alternative settlement methods, and sector-specific exceptions must be verified with the Central Bank and the taxpayer's authorized bank.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a partial convertibility regime. Current account transactions, including profit remittances, are handled under a verifiable authenticity framework; they can be converted through banks without pre-approval, provided the underlying transaction documents, audited retained earnings, tax clearance and economic substance evidence are supplied. The capital account remains highly controlled: foreign direct investment injection, shareholder loans, capital reduction and exit proceeds require SAFE registration through a designated bank and are generally convertible only after compliance verification and full tax clearance. Taiwan-sourced investment is treated as foreign investment under the mainland's negative-list regime and receives no special profit-repatriation quota beyond audited distributable reserves.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China has abolished mandatory surrender ratios. Exporters may freely retain export receipts in foreign currency or settle 100% into RMB. All export proceeds must be credited to a domestic trade foreign exchange account within the monitored trade receipt period, generally within 90 days from the date of export customs declaration. Advance receipts must be matched to actual export within 30 days or registered in the SAFE trade monitoring system. RMB settlement occurs at the bank's listed rate after verification of the customs declaration, commercial invoice, and transport documents.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the monetary authority, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the specialized FX regulator.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE must first obtain its business license and then open a basic RMB account and a foreign exchange settlement account at a licensed domestic bank. The bank files the Foreign Investment Information Report and completes the SAFE FDI Registration. The Taiwanese investor then sends a SWIFT MT103 remittance with purpose code 'FDI Capital Contribution' and the unique Foreign Investment Credential Number. The bank verifies the capital account and performs conversion into RMB through the Capital Settlement module, recording the receipt to qualified foreign capital status for subsequent profit remittance or equity transfer purposes.Trade settlements are executed through SWIFT MT103 instructions for telegraphic transfers, supported by commercial invoices, purchase orders, customs declarations and the trade foreign exchange monitoring code issued by the bank. For larger transactions or at the seller's request, the WFOE may issue a commercial letter of credit through SWIFT MT700, governed by UCP600; documents are presented and paid via MT756.

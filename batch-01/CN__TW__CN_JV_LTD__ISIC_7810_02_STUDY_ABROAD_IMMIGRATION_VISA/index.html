Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for general companies in Taiwan; however, for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the Investment Commission of MOEA requires a capital adequacy assessment. The capital must be fully subscribed and paid-in within the period specified in the investment approval. No escrow account is mandated, but funds must be remitted from abroad and deposited in a local bank account, with a capital verification report issued by a certified public accountant.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taxation Administration (Taiwan), acting through the National Taxation Bureau and local tax offices; online electronic portal: the Ministry of Finance Tax Registry and e-Filing Platform (mof.gov.tw) and the e-Invoice Cloud Service (einvoice.nat.gov.tw)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity forming a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture in Taiwan must first obtain investment approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs, then apply for business registration with the National Taxation Bureau to receive a statutory Unified Business Number/Tax Identification Number (TIN). The application requires the approved Articles of Incorporation, foreign company certification, domestic address or leased premise proof, responsible-person identity documents, and the investment approval letter. After the TIN is issued, the JV must complete VAT business registration under the Business Tax Act before commencing taxable sales. If the foreign entity has no fixed place of business in Taiwan, the TIN and VAT registration application must be filed through a qualified withholding agent or duly appointed domestic tax representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign non-resident entity forming a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture in Taiwan must first obtain investment approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs, then apply for business registration with the National Taxation Bureau to receive a statutory Unified Business Number/Tax Identification Number (TIN). The application requires the approved Articles of Incorporation, foreign company certification, domestic address or leased premise proof, responsible-person identity documents, and the investment approval letter. After the TIN is issued, the JV must complete VAT business registration under the Business Tax Act before commencing taxable sales. If the foreign entity has no fixed place of business in Taiwan, the TIN and VAT registration application must be filed through a qualified withholding agent or duly appointed domestic tax representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for corporate entity classification; Taiwan does not offer check-the-box pass-through elections for a limited company. The only relevant administrative election is application for small-scale enterprise treatment under the Business Tax Act when average monthly sales revenue is below NT$200,000, which subjects qualified enterprises to a 1% business tax rate instead of standard VAT, but the entity remains an entity-level taxpayer for income tax; hybrid mismatch elections are not separately available in Taiwan.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general VAT registration exemption; all enterprises selling goods or services in Taiwan are required to register. However, businesses with average monthly revenue below NT$200,000 may qualify for simplified lump-sum business-tax treatment at 1% for value-added enterprises or 4% for non-value-added enterprises; once average monthly turnover reaches or exceeds NT$200,000, the enterprise is subject to mandatory full VAT registration, issuance of uniform invoices, and normal VAT compliance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Business Tax Act, zero-rated export treatment applies to: (1) goods exported from Taiwan under customs control; (2) services directly related to exported goods or international transport; and (3) services rendered by a Taiwanese enterprise for an offshore party where the entire service is used or supplied outside Taiwan and the consideration is received in foreign currency. For the study-abroad and immigration visa consultancy services of the JV, offshore client services must be contractually supplied to non-residents, performed for destinations outside Taiwan, and invoiced and collected in foreign currency to qualify for the 0% VAT rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued by Taiwan Customs
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical export of any goods
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Foreign exchange inward remittance certificate or Foreign Exchange Settlement Water issued by a designated bank showing receipt of the foreign currency proceeds
  • Bilingual or English-language service contract or agreement identigying the offshore customer, service scope, and payment terms
  • Uniform Invoice or e-Invoice in the export series issued to the foreign buyer where required

Taiwan mandates the Uniform Invoice System, including electronic Uniform Invoices (e-Invoices) under the Ministry of Finance integration service. Registered VAT taxpayers, particularly B2B and high-volume B2C businesses, must integrate with the e-Invoice platform through certified turnkey software or API using tax-entity digital certificates; e-invoice data, invoice numbers, and carrier information must be uploaded to the Ministry of Finance platform for recording. Real-time clearance is not imposed for every invoice, but Taiwan enforces platform-based digital stamping through the unique Uniform Invoice number, seller identifier, encryption mechanism, and anti-fraud controls such as the GUI authentication code and carrier validation.

Taiwan tax authorities may deny zero-rating, reassess the uncollected 5% VAT, impose penalty surcharges and interest, and issue fines of up to five times the amount of tax due when mandatory export documentation is missing, false, or inconsistent. Where foreign-exchange repatriation does not occur within 180 days from the export or service supply date and no valid exception exists, the zero-rating claim is presumed unsubstantiated and subject to retrospective clawback, additional penalties, and enhanced audit scrutiny, including potential anti-money-laundering reporting under Taiwan financial regulations.

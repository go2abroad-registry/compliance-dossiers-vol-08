Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. The Taiwan Company Act imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a limited company. However, for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the foreign shareholder (including PRC investor) must remit the subscribed capital into a Temporary Foreign Investor Capital Account (外國投資人投資帳戶) at a designated bank, obtain a Certificate of Foreign Exchange Settlement (資金轉換證明), and retain the bank's verification letter for submission to the Investment Commission and the registry authority. No escrow account blocking rule applies; the funds are understood to be available for immediate corporate use upon completion of the registration and issuance of the company seal.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable – the company is not a manufacturing or chemical facility; therefore no Civil Defense Fire Grant Permit (消防防護計畫) is required. However, as an employer of office workers, the company must obtain a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate from the local Fire Department under the Fire Services Act, which mandates annual inspection of the office premises, fire extinguishers, escape exits, and a fire prevention plan for any building with a floor area exceeding 600 square meters. The company must also comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act by submitting an occupational safety plan to the Ministry of Labor for approval if the number of employees exceeds 100; a simple self-check is sufficient for a low-risk digital office operation. In addition, a structural load inspection is not needed as no heavy machinery is installed. The company shall ensure smoke detectors and a first aid kit are in place and that the legal representative is aware of emergency evacuation procedures.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File an investment application with the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs for the foreign equity interest in the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. Step 2: Upon approval, incorporate the Taiwan entity under the Company Act through the MOEA Department of Commerce or the authorized special municipality commercial registration system. Step 3: The integrated company and tax registration system automatically issues the eight-digit Unified Business Number (UBI), which is the combined corporate income tax ID and VAT registration number. Step 4: Complete Business Tax Registration with the local National Taxation Bureau before operations commence, as required by the Value-Added and Non-Value-Added Business Tax Act. Step 5: Apply for the Uniform Invoice Purchase Certificate and, if e-invoicing is used, register for the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integration Service Platform with the Uniform Invoice Turnkey certificate. Step 6: For a pure non-resident enterprise without a Taiwan branch, there is no routine VAT registration for business-to-business export income; a Taiwan-source income tax registration is established through the withholding tax system. If the non-resident supplies electronic services directly to Taiwan individual consumers and breaches the NTD 480,000 annual turnover threshold, it must appoint a fiscal representative and register for Business Tax through the MOF online registration channel to obtain a UBI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan applies statutory company-law classification rather than check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. A foreign-invested limited liability company is always a profit-seeking enterprise subject to entity-level income tax and VAT; no pass-through election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for domestic Taiwan VAT; registration is mandatory before commencing any taxable business. For foreign cross-border electronic-service suppliers to Taiwan individuals, the registration threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding NTD 480,000; domestic small-scale businesses with monthly taxable sales below NTD 200,000 may be assessed under the simplified small-profit enterprise regime at 1% instead of issuing uniform invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the Value-Added and Non-Value-Added Business Tax Act where goods are physically exported from Taiwan or where services, including offshore CAD/BIM and structural drafting services, are rendered to a foreign enterprise, the consideration is paid in foreign currency or otherwise evidenced as an export transaction, and the service is delivered for use outside Taiwan. A Taiwan-registered exporter claiming 0% must show the contractual overseas customer, proof of actual delivery or service performance, receipt of export consideration from outside Taiwan, and retention of the supporting export documents. Digital services intended for local consumption by Taiwan individual consumers are outside the export zero-rating rule and remain subject to 5% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs export entry/export form issued by the Ministry of Finance Customs Administration)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill covering physical export of goods
  • Commercial contract, purchase order, or statement of work identifying the overseas customer and the CAD/BIM deliverable
  • Service completion report, acceptance certificate, or delivery log evidencing that the service is used outside Taiwan
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange settlement voucher evidencing actual receipt of payment
  • Cross-border wire transfer proof, SWIFT MT103, or bank credit advice matching the invoice amount
  • Tax invoice or e-invoice with 0% VAT rate clearly indicated and aligned to the export documentation
  • Foreign exchange entry record filed with the designated foreign-exchange bank showing timely repatriation

Taiwan mandates Uniform Invoices for VAT businesses. Registered businesses must use the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integration Service Platform and transmit e-invoice records through the Uniform Invoice Turnkey channel using the assigned certificate, with near-real-time submission at issuance. The platform provides tax-authority-side anti-fraud controls: unique eight-digit uniform number, random e-invoice code, encrypted QR code, and central cross-validation on the MOF platform. Taiwan does not require a fiscal processor or physical digital stamping; the anti-fraud structure is the central e-invoice integration and lottery validation system.

The exporter must retain every export and foreign-exchange document for inspection. The tax authority will match the customs export record, e-invoice, contract, bank MT103, and FIRC. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the 5% business tax is reassessed, interest is added, and the underpayment penalty applies under the Business Tax Act on top of the clawback. In cases involving false export declarations, forged foreign-exchange certificates, or document packages created to disguise domestic consumption, the Ministry of Finance may revoke the registration, impose the maximum aggravating penalty, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution under the Tax Collection Act.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Taiwan jurisdiction operates under a managed floating exchange rate regime administered by the central bank. The New Taiwan Dollar is freely convertible for current-account payments, including trade settlements and profit remittances, provided the underlying transaction is supported by authentic invoices, contracts, customs records, and tax certificates. Capital-account conversion, however, remains selectively controlled: direct investment inflows require approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs, and the introduction of capital from Mainland China carries heightened scrutiny under Taiwanese regulations governing mainland investment. Once the investment is approved, paid-in capital must be routed through a designated foreign-currency deposit account, converted to NT dollars by a local bank, and subjected to a certified public accountant capital-verification report before the company can pass the remittance purpose test. Profit remittance is allowed on an annual basis after audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement, and legal reserve requirements; banks will require the tax certificate, audit report, shareholders' resolution, and proof that the remittance is distribution of after-tax profit. There is no comprehensive surrender requirement for export proceeds, but exporters must repatriate and present the export declaration to their designated bank within the statutory period.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Control Act, export proceeds from goods or services must be collected and repatriated to Taiwan within five years of the export declaration date or service completion date, unless an extension is granted by the Central Bank. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%, meaning exporters may keep the proceeds in an authorized foreign currency deposit account or sell them through a designated bank into NT dollars; however, the underlying export declaration and invoice must be processed through a licensed bank for foreign exchange transaction reporting.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) - Foreign Exchange Department

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Taiwan's Foreign Exchange Control Act reserves designated foreign exchange operations to domestic licensed banks. Offshore digital EMIs are not regulated banking entities in Taiwan and cannot hold NT-dollar settlement accounts, issue local clearing codes, or act as a main treasury rail. The JV may use an offshore EMI such as Wise only as an ancillary low-value expense mechanism in jurisdictions where that EMI holds a local license; inbound capital subscription and outbound corporate service disbursements must be cleared through a Taiwan-designated bank or the EMI's licensed banking partner with full AML visibility. Therefore, any treasury blueprint treating Wise as the primary clearing rail is rejected under local law.

For construction material imports, use a commercial letter of credit issued via MT700 with full shipping document requirements. All outbound trade disbursements should be cleared through Bank of Taiwan or Mega International Commercial Bank to maintain formatting and compliance continuity.Deliverable USD/TWD forward contracts with tenors up to 12 months, supported by underlying commercial invoices or purchase orders and executed with a Taiwan licensed designated bank.Cross-currency swaps and currency options on USD/TWD for genuine hedging of long-term capital-linked debt or trade flows, subject to central bank transaction reporting thresholds.Non-deliverable forwards are not permissible for the domestic Taiwan resident entity under normal hedging rules; speculative NDF positions are prohibited.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Taiwan's Foreign Exchange Control Act reserves designated foreign exchange operations to domestic licensed banks. Offshore digital EMIs are not regulated banking entities in Taiwan and cannot hold NT-dollar settlement accounts, issue local clearing codes, or act as a main treasury rail. The JV may use an offshore EMI such as Wise only as an ancillary low-value expense mechanism in jurisdictions where that EMI holds a local license; inbound capital subscription and outbound corporate service disbursements must be cleared through a Taiwan-designated bank or the EMI's licensed banking partner with full AML visibility. Therefore, any treasury blueprint treating Wise as the primary clearing rail is rejected under local law.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local file and master file are mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed NT$50 million (approximately US$1.6 million), or when intercompany intangible property transactions exceed NT$20 million. The same thresholds trigger the annual related-party disclosure schedule.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan has not enacted full OECD Economic Substance Regulations, but the substance-over-form doctrine applies. For cross-strait treaty relief, the Taiwan tax authority will deny treaty benefits to a mainland Chinese resident that lacks commercial substance, such as an intermediate holding company or shell JV. The Taiwan entity must ensure that the CN_JV_LTD performs substantive decision-making and operational functions for its equity interest, employing local staff, premises, and management in China. For Taiwan's own entity, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must include actual engineering management, risk assumption, and strategic CAD/BIM design decisions. Pure pass-through invoicing without local employees, offices, and contract execution capacity is treated as a red flag for sham filings.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion by false or understated invoices and artificial profit shifting through intercompany CAD/BIM service fees

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Tax Collection Act and Articles 101-103 of the Income Tax Act, understated tax attracts an additional penalty of 2 to 3 times the amount of tax evaded, plus late interest. Making false invoices in violation of Article 32 leads to fines of up to NT$1.1 million and suspension.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false tax returns may be personally civilly liable for the tax shortfall if corporate assets are insufficient. Criminal liability: imprisonment of up to 5 years under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act for fraudulent tax evasion. Travel bans and property liens may be imposed by the National Tax Administration. In egregious cross-border conduit arrangements, directors of both the Taiwan and Chinese entities may be jointly pursued for unjust enriched amounts.
Shell enterprise in the mainland Chinese parent without economic substance utilized to obtain treaty withholding tax relief

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits by the Taiwan tax authority under the principal purpose test and general anti-avoidance rule under Article 12-1 of the Tax Collection Act. The full 21% WHT is re-imposed, plus a penalty of up to 15% of the underpaid withholding tax, and additional 1% monthly surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused the use of a sham entity may be personally liable for restitution of the tax underpaid to the Taiwanese Treasury. In addition, under the enterprise crime theory or joint tort liability, directors can be sued for damages. A blacklisting order can freeze their Taiwan entry visas and asset dealings. Criminal liability for forgery of the Chinese company's records can result in imprisonment up to 7 years under Articles 210 and 215 of the Taiwan Criminal Code.
Failure to disclose controlled cross-border related-party transactions and absence of contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation

Statutory sanction: Under Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act and the Taiwan Transfer Pricing Assessment Rules, the tax authority may impute arm's length income and impose a penalty of 2 to 3 times the understated tax amount. Failure to provide documentary evidence results in the rejection of deductions and a documented penalty of up to NT$300,000 for failure to retain records.

Director personal liability: Directors face direct risk of personal civil liability if they signed the incomplete tax return knowing of omitted transactions. Repeated violations constitute intent to evade tax and can lead to personal criminal imprisonment up to 5 years. Directors may also be disqualified as officers and have travel restrictions imposed. If the fabricated transfer pricing study is used to defraud the authorities, fraud charges under general criminal law apply with liability for compensating the tax authority's costs.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the Companies Act of Taiwan, there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a general company limited by shares; however, the subscribed capital must be fully paid and verified by a Taiwan licensed certified public accountant before incorporation registration. No blocked escrow account rule applies for a foreign-invested company. For a wholly foreign-owned enterprise, the foreign shareholder must first obtain the Investment Commission, MOEA Foreign Investment Approval, then remit the approved subscribed capital into the company bank account in Taiwan. The remitted capital must be applied toward the approved investment plan, and any subsequent reduction or change in the invested capital requires separate approval or amendment filing with the Investment Commission.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - for a pure online retail and consignment operation, there is no mandatory civil defense fire permit, structural load inspection or factory safety clearance. However, the company must comply with the Fire Services Act and Occupational Safety and Health Act for its leased office, including maintaining portable fire extinguishers, emergency exits and basic workplace safety arrangements; if the company later opens a physical warehouse or fulfillment center, it must obtain a building use permit, fire safety equipment inspection and civil defense clearance from the local fire authority before occupancy.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; the electronic tax filing and registration portal is China Tax Service (e-tax bureau platform, accessed through the State Taxation Administration official website chinatax.gov.cn and provincial e-tax endpoints).

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) limited liability company established in China, the foreign sponsor first completes enterprise establishment registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), obtaining an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The USCC is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number under Chinese tax law; no separate TIN is issued. Tax registration is effected automatically through the SAMR registration data-sharing system, but the WFOE must log into the China Tax Service e-tax portal within 30 days of establishment to complete the tax information confirmation, registration of bank deposit account details, accounting software/fiscal system filing, legal representative biometric verification, and VAT taxpayer qualification election. If the projected annual taxable turnover exceeds the mandatory VAT general taxpayer threshold, the entity must apply for general VAT taxpayer status, acquire the Tax UKey fiscal control device, and enroll in the special VAT invoice issuance system. The tax registration certificate has been abolished; the USCC is the sole tax identifier for income tax, VAT, customs duty, and export tax rebate filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not operate a check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election regime. A China-incorporated WFOE is automatically treated as a resident enterprise taxpayer subject to entity-level Corporate Income Tax on worldwide profits, and as a VAT taxpayer under the general or simplified collection method. The electable threshold is limited to small-scale VAT taxpayer status when annual turnover is below RMB 5,000,000, or to the simplified second-hand goods VAT collection method where applicable; no entity status classification election is available.

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

China zero-rates VAT on goods that genuinely leave the customs territory of China under an export customs declaration and are sold to a foreign buyer. For a WFOE e-commerce reseller to qualify, the sales must be supported by an export contract, a completed China Customs export declaration, the physical departure and arrival of goods evidenced by transport documents, and the repatriation of foreign exchange proceeds in accordance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) regulations. Alternatively, goods sold through cross-border e-commerce platforms using the 9610/9710 regulatory mode may be zero-rated provided the declared commodity, buyer, payment flow, and logistics data match to the satisfaction of customs and tax authorities. The taxpayer must be a general VAT taxpayer, maintain separate export transaction ledgers, and file the export tax rebate declaration within the statutory period. Secondhand luxury goods exported without prior input VAT credit are generally not eligible for a VAT rebate, although zero-rating still removes the 13 percent output VAT on exported secondhand goods.

  • China Customs Export Declaration (the export goods customs declaration form, including the scheduled departure date, destination country, commodity HS classification, customs value, and the official customs release stamp or electronic clearance date)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export shipment from China and arrival at the destination, with issuer, vessel/flight number, port of loading, port of discharge, and cargo description consistent with the customs export declaration
  • Commercial Invoice and Sales Contract between the WFOE and the overseas buyer, including the exact cross-border purchase price and Incoterms
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Settlement/Collection Certificate proving the receipt of export-receipt foreign exchange or RMB cross-border settlement proceeds, generated by the receiving bank under SAFE trade verification procedures
  • Packing list, order ledger, cross-border e-commerce order initiation records, payment transaction receipts from the PSP, and the export tax rebate declaration recapitulation (often referred to as the export rebate shopping list/reconciliation sheet)
  • Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation evidencing the repatriation of funds within 180 days from customs clearance

VAT invoicing in China is governed by the State Taxation Administration's nationwide VAT invoice management system. All fapiao (tax invoices) must be issued through certified tax control equipment, including the Tax UKey and online e-tax clearance platforms. Special VAT invoices used for input tax credit, as well as ordinary VAT invoices, are transmitted in near real time to the STA and embedded with a unique encrypted anti-counterfeiting tax control code. A mandatory invoice data standardization protocol requires barcode/QR code stamping, encrypted digital signature, and daily batch reconciliation with the e-tax bureau. Fully digitalized electronic invoices (full-digital e-invoices) are accepted without OFD file format limitations. Cross-border e-commerce exports cleared under Customs supervision codes 9610 and 9710 require simultaneous synchronization of e-invoice issuance and customs declaration data, with digital ledger retention for the export tax rebate review.

China Customs and the State Taxation Administration conduct sample-based and risk-targeted post-clearance audits of all export zero-rating and export VAT rebate claims. A false export declaration, forged bill of lading, fraudulent foreign exchange receipt, or mismatch between customs declaration and declared export revenue will void the zero-rating entitlement and trigger immediate clawback of the rebate, assessment of unpaid VAT, late-payment interest accrual, and a penalty under Article 63 of the Chinese Tax Collection and Administration Law of between 50 percent and 500 percent of the tax evaded. Concurrently, the Customs Authority may impose export smuggling penalties, revoke the issuer's Class A customs credit status, and include the enterprise record in the joint disciplinary blacklist. If the fraudulent export-rebate activity constitutes the Criminal Law offense of fraudulently obtaining export tax rebates, the responsible entity and its legal representative face criminal fines and custodial sentence risks. Re-patronage of an already-invalid exemption, failure to submit supplementary documentation, or failure to repatriate foreign exchange within 180 days also triggers automatic zero-rating revocation and retroactive interest recovery.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The composite WFOE is a PRC-incorporated wholly foreign-owned enterprise whose ultimate treasury oversight spans both mainland China and Taiwan. On the mainland, the RMB is fully convertible on the current account for trade in goods, services, and dividend remittances, but remains strictly regulated on the capital account. Inward equity capital from the Taiwanese shareholder must be registered with SAFE as foreign direct investment, held in a designated FDI foreign-exchange capital account, and settled into RMB only after the bank examines the underlying business purpose. Capital account transfers, including equity repurchases, intercompany loans, and offshore guarantees, require separate SAFE registration, filing, or approval. Profit or dividend remittances by the WFOE are treated as current-account investment income and can be paid out post-audit and post-withholding tax; the remitting bank will demand proof of tax payment, audited financial statements, board resolutions, and the FDI registration code. No automatic conversion exists for the capital account; funds received as capital cannot be used freely for equity investment, on-lending, or securities without approval. In Taiwan, the CBC operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with no surrender duty on current-account earnings, but the Ministry of Economic Affairs Investment Commission regulates investment flows into mainland China, and the CBC monitors large remittances. The practical effect for the WFOE treasury is that any cross-border cash movement must carry a statutory purpose code, be backed by an underlying commercial or investment contract, and be processed through SAFE/CBC-licensed banks rather than offshore payment intermediaries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

PRC (SAFE): For goods sold by the mainland WFOE, export-foreign-currency proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign-exchange account generally within 90 days from the export customs declaration. There is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio; after repatriation the exporter may retain the FX in a current-account settlement account or settle it into RMB at a designated bank. Taiwan (CBC): Exporters must repatriate foreign-currency proceeds to Taiwan within 180 days of shipment, and may either retain them in a foreign-currency deposit account or convert into NT dollars at an authorized bank; the CBC may require a temporary full or partial conversion only in a balance-of-payments emergency.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) of the People's Republic of China, and the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC) as the dual supervisory authorities for cross-strait foreign exchange regulation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Mainland China does not permit offshore EMI institutions to provide local IBANs, virtual bank accounts, or payment-as-a-bank services to a WFOE; the PBOC and SAFE require all foreign-exchange revenue and payments to be settled through licensed commercial banks and licensed cross-border payment institutions. Stripe and Escrow_Pay may be used only as merchant payment gateways that sweep funds to the WFOE's designated bank account; they cannot operate as the primary treasury cash or capital account. In Taiwan, offshore EMIs face the same treatment under the Electronic Payment Institutions Act. The overall feasibility for the WFOE is therefore Low as a stand-alone treasury rail; EMIs can add value only as customer-facing collection tools with a licensed in-country partner and full AML/KYC data transmission to the bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwanese shareholder must first obtain regional investment approval from Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs Investment Commission for mainland investment, and the WFOE must complete SAFE FDI registration with its mainland bank to obtain a Foreign Exchange Business Registration (FDI) code. The injection is wired via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's FDI capital account in the named beneficiary bank; the SWIFT field 50K must identify the direct investor, field 59 must reference the FDI capital account, and the remittance information field must quote the FDI registration code, investment contract number, and the appropriate purpose code for FDI equity injection. The bank will complete currency verification and a Chinese CPA will issue a capital verification report. The funds cannot be used for non-operating items; conversion into RMB and disbursement must follow the bank's examination of supporting invoices, contracts, and the approved business scope. For the Taiwan outbound leg, the remittance is classified under the Regulations on Investment in Mainland China and must be reported to the CBC through the remitting bank.For operational payables such as inventory purchases, appraiser fees, logistics costs, and consignor settlements, use SWIFT MT103 on a transaction-by-transaction basis when speed and traceability are required. For high-value luxury inventory purchases and larger consignments, use irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) to condition payment upon presentation of shipping, inspection, and title documents, or use Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) when counterparty risk is moderate. All disbursements must reference the underlying contract, commercial invoice, and customs entry in the payment purpose code field. For cross-border RMB settlement, route through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) where the Taiwanese counterparty's bank participates. Aggregate settlement flows from Stripe and Escrow_Pay should be swept under a licensed cross-border merchant payment agreement into the onshore current-account settlement account so that the bank can reconcile gross transaction data with net remittances.CNY/USD deliverable forward purchase and sale contracts with SAFE-licensed onshore banks, restricted to hedging actual current-account and approved capital-account exposures.CNY/USD cross-currency swaps for matching foreign-exchange and interest-rate risk on FDI-related loans and intercompany funding, with tenors aligned to the underlying facility.USD/NT$ deliverable forwards executed with Taiwan authorized banks for the Taiwan-side operating entity's USD receipts and NT$ payables.FX options on current-account flows, permissible only where the notional and tenor do not exceed the underlying exposure and the contract is entered for hedging rather than speculation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted onshore for the mainland WFOE's RMB exposures; NDFs may be considered only by the Taiwan offshore group entity through CBC-licensed banks for hedging CNY exposure that is otherwise non-deliverable in Taiwan.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Mainland China does not permit offshore EMI institutions to provide local IBANs, virtual bank accounts, or payment-as-a-bank services to a WFOE; the PBOC and SAFE require all foreign-exchange revenue and payments to be settled through licensed commercial banks and licensed cross-border payment institutions. Stripe and Escrow_Pay may be used only as merchant payment gateways that sweep funds to the WFOE's designated bank account; they cannot operate as the primary treasury cash or capital account. In Taiwan, offshore EMIs face the same treatment under the Electronic Payment Institutions Act. The overall feasibility for the WFOE is therefore Low as a stand-alone treasury rail; EMIs can add value only as customer-facing collection tools with a licensed in-country partner and full AML/KYC data transmission to the bank.

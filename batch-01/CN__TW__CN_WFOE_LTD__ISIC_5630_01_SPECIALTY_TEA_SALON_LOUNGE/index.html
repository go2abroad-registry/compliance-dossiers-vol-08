Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Taiwan's Income Tax Act. All limited companies are taxed at the entity level. A small-scale enterprise may be taxed under a deemed assessment regime if monthly sales remain below the VAT registration threshold, but such treatment is mandated by turnover and not an elective classification. No pass-through election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NT$200,000 average monthly sales; businesses below this may be classified as small-scale taxpayers without mandatory invoice issuance, but VAT registration is required once any one month exceeds the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to (i) physical export of goods where the goods leave Taiwan under a customs export declaration, including cross-border e-commerce sales of loose-leaf tea; and (ii) taxable services rendered to foreign enterprises or used entirely overseas, supported by invoices issued in foreign currency and remittance evidence. Pure in-person salon services provided to tourists inside Taiwan are domestic supplies and remain subject to 5% VAT.

Electronic Uniform Invoicing (EUI) is mandatory for all VAT-registered enterprises. B2B invoices must be uploaded to the MOF E-Invoice Platform within 48 hours of issuance; B2C invoices must be uploaded within 7 days. Integration uses certified API service providers; no real-time transactional fiscal stamping is required, but each invoice must bear a government-allocated serial number and be traceable in the platform.

Export zero-rating claims are cross-verified against customs export records, foreign exchange settlement data, and the e-invoice platform. Criminal fraud penalties under the Tax Collection Act include imprisonment and fines. Refund clawbacks are retrospectively applied within the 7-year statutory limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible for current-account items, including cross-border trade in tea goods, salon services, dividends and royalties, provided the remitting bank verifies the real-transaction background and reports the transaction to SAFE. Capital-account items remain regulated. A WFOE receiving equity capital must complete the AMR Foreign Investment Information Report and obtain a SAFE capital-project registration through its designated bank before the Taiwan investor can fund the FDI capital account. Profit-remittance hurdles include the annual statutory audit, allocation of the 10% statutory surplus reserve until that reserve equals 50% of registered capital, payment of enterprise income tax, and a board resolution approving the dividend. Dividend withholding for a Taiwan corporate shareholder is 10% absent a ratified cross-strait tax arrangement, and only decreases if the Cross-Strait Double Taxation Agreement is implemented by the tax authorities of both sides.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China's mandatory export-proceeds surrender regime was repealed in 2012; no statutory conversion ratio or surrender timeline currently applies. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in a foreign-exchange settlement account or settled into RMB voluntarily. Nevertheless, proceeds must be repatriated to China within the credit terms of the underlying export contract, and a deferred-collection report is mandatory where payment is not expected within 90 days of the customs export declaration. SAFE's monitoring system assigns A-class, B-class and C-class status; B- and C-class enterprises lose the fast-track verification and voluntary-settlement options.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), the central bank, is the supreme monetary authority; the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), administratively managed by PBOC, is the designated authority for exchange control, current- and capital-account surveillance, and the trade-monitoring system. The National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) supervises banks and payment institutions on licensing, KYC, and anti-money-laundering enforcement jointly with PBOC.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% profit-seeking enterprise income tax rate; plus a 5% retained earnings surcharge on undistributed surplus where applicable and a 12% minimum tax under the Income Basic Tax Act for most profit-seeking enterprises.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on gross dividends paid to a non-resident profit-seeking enterprise or non-resident individual shareholder; resident shareholders are not subject to dividend withholding but the undistributed earnings surcharge of 5% may apply at corporate level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$200 million in aggregate annual related-party transactions triggers the mandatory Local File; NT$300 million triggers a Master File; country-by-country reporting applies when group consolidated revenue exceeds NT$27 billion. Even below these thresholds, the WFOE must maintain a transfer pricing study if the tax authority issues a documentation notice.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not apply a GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but anti-avoidance is enforced through the substance-over-form doctrine under the Tax Collection Act and the general anti-avoidance provisions of the Income Basic Tax Act. The specialty tea salon WFOE must maintain real local staff, physical tea and ceramic inventory, local pricing discretion, board minutes, contractual risk and bank accounts to avoid being recharacterized as a shell or nominee of the mainland shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False or Fictitious Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Collection Act, fraudulent evasion may result in criminal imprisonment of up to five years, criminal fines, a penal surcharge of one to three times the underpaid tax, and deemed assessment where books and records are unavailable or unreliable.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false invoices, suppress cash sales or direct deceptive bookkeeping are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties. The tax authority may freeze bank accounts, seize assets, restrict travel against the legal representative, and criminal conviction may result in director disqualification and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing / Thin-Capitalization Misstatement

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may recharacterize intercompany transactions under Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act, impose deemed arm's length income, deny deductions for undocumented related-party charges, and assess a transfer pricing penalty surcharge of up to 1.5 times the additional tax due.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve knowingly false transfer pricing documentation or direct the filing of falsified comparability analyses can be held civilly liable in shareholder or creditor actions. In aggravated cases, the corporate veil is pierced, exposing beneficial owners to restitutionary liability, travel restrictions and criminal prosecution for fraudulent declarations.
AML / Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The competent authority may deny treaty benefits and reimpose the full 21% dividend withholding and 20% royalty withholding rates, assess the 12% alternative minimum tax on overstated no-tax profits, and refer suspicious transactions to the Financial Intelligence Unit for money-laundering investigation under the Money Laundering Control Act.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate form is abused, courts may disregard limited liability and hold shareholders and directors personally responsible for corporate tax debts and AML penalties.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an ordinary bakery WFOE because the Foreign Investment Negative List does not restrict pastry manufacturing, central kitchen operation or franchise business. The registered capital amount and contribution schedule are fixed in the Articles of Association and the foreign investor is obligated to contribute within the agreed period. There is no blocked escrow-account requirement for this business category; however, a Taiwan-resident investor must remit the subscribed capital through the foreign exchange capital account opened with a designated bank and obtain the SAFE recording before the funds are available for operating expenses.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no monetary exemption from VAT registration for enterprises; all PRC legal entities must complete tax registration. VAT general taxpayer status becomes mandatory when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold, the WFOE may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it elects general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods manufactured or traded by the WFOE may be zero-rated under PRC VAT when they are physically exported from the PRC customs territory to an overseas buyer, including a Taiwan import recipient under cross-strait customs processing. The commodities must pass through customs control and be released on an Export Customs Declaration; the exporter must hold general taxpayer status, sell to an overseas party, receive the export proceeds in foreign exchange, and submit a complete electronic export declaration to the STA within the relevant tax return period. Qualifying offshore services, such as master franchise royalties and central kitchen consulting supplied to Taiwan franchisees, may be zero-rated if the services are rendered outside China, consumed outside China, and supported by a cross-border service contract

All VAT invoices must be created on the national Unified Electronic Invoice Platform administered by the STA. Fully digitalized electronic invoices carry a unique invoice code and anti-counterfeit tax-control number; issuance data are transmitted to the tax authority in near-real time. Enterprises using ERP must integrate through the official e-invoice API or use the e-Tax portal; third-party or extemporaneous paper invoices without tax-control data are not accepted for input VAT deduction or export refund. For export invoices, the invoice data are automatically matched with customs export declarations through the national single window and the STA export tax refund system.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates an asymmetric foreign exchange control regime. Current account items are generally convertible for trade, services, and direct investment-related payments once underlying commercial documents are verified by a designated domestic bank. Capital account items, however, remain subject to registration, approval, or quantitative and qualitative restrictions. For this Taiwanese-owned WFOE operating a bakery and pastry franchise chain, the practical implication is that equity capital injections, shareholder loans, dividends, and returns on liquidation cannot flow freely across the border without satisfying SAFE's Foreign Investment Business Filing, as well as the domestic bank's end-use authenticity review. Export and import proceeds are classified under trade-in-goods monitoring, and enterprises must repatriate export receipts to China but may retain them in foreign currency accounts or convert to RMB at market-determined rates. The principal profit remittance hurdles are the need for an audited financial statement proving statutory after-tax profit, full payment of withholding income tax on dividends, a board resolution declaring the dividend, and a SAFE-compliant service/current account payment application. In practice, the bank will require the contract, invoice, tax payment certificate, and any foreign exchange registration before effecting the dividend wire to Taiwan. The system is functional but heavily compliance-driven, and any mismatch in purpose codes or beneficiary naming will trigger suspension.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For general trade in goods, export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated into China through a designated domestic bank within the SAFE trade monitoring window, typically 90 days after export shipment; no mandatory conversion ratio is imposed, meaning 0% of the proceeds need to be surrendered into RMB. The WFOE may hold the funds in its foreign exchange settlement account or convert them as needed for business operations, but it must file the relevant export customs declaration, bill of lading, invoice, and collection documents through the bank's trade verification system.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBoC), with SAFE branches at provincial and municipal levels exercising delegated foreign exchange registration, verification, and balance-of-payments reporting authority for all foreign-invested enterprises in mainland China.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, Airwallex, or similar are not legally authorized to maintain or service RMB-denominated corporate accounts for a mainland China WFOE. Under PBoC and SAFE rules, all inbound RMB settlement for foreign-invested enterprises must be handled by licensed domestic banks or licensed non-bank payment institutions that have obtained a payment business license. Offshore EMIs do not hold such licenses and cannot provide the required SAFE registration, capital verification, or trade authenticity review. Viability is therefore low and such providers should be used only as auxiliary payment tools for non-resident treasury purposes, never as the primary corporate banking rail for the WFOE.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan parent should wire funds via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated FDI capital account at a Chinese Tier-1 bank, using the exact beneficiary name matching the SAFE registration and bank account records. The remittance purpose code must be the foreign direct investment capital injection code, and the bank must receive the board resolution and investment contract before crediting. After receipt, the WFOE must apply to the bank for 'settlement under current receipt' with the operation-principle review; funds must be injected into operating expenses or fixed asset purchases within the allowed fashion and cannot be used for equity investments or securities. The bank will issue a capital verification report or the WFOE will appoint a certified public accountant for the FI capital verification statement.For franchise royalties and service fees to Taiwan, use SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers with the service trade payment application and tax withholding certificate. For bulk imported raw ingredients or kitchen machinery, use a commercial letter of credit (L/C) issued by the WFOE's Chinese bank in favor of the Taiwan supplier, or a documentary collection (D/P or D/A) when the relationship is mature. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, Airwallex, or similar are not legally authorized to maintain or service RMB-denominated corporate accounts for a mainland China WFOE. Under PBoC and SAFE rules, all inbound RMB settlement for foreign-invested enterprises must be handled by licensed domestic banks or licensed non-bank payment institutions that have obtained a payment business license. Offshore EMIs do not hold such licenses and cannot provide the required SAFE registration, capital verification, or trade authenticity review. Viability is therefore low and such providers should be used only as auxiliary payment tools for non-resident treasury purposes, never as the primary corporate banking rail for the WFOE.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For Mainland China Local File: generally related-party tangible-asset transactions exceeding RMB 200 million, financial-asset transactions exceeding RMB 100 million, intangible-asset transactions exceeding RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceeding RMB 40 million in the relevant tax year. A Master File is generally required where the multinational group has consolidated annual revenue of at least RMB 1 billion and the Chinese entity has cross-border related-party transactions, or where other statutory conditions apply. A Special Issue File may be required for cost-sharing arrangements or thin-capitalization matters.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not operate a standalone regime identical to the EU or certain offshore Economic Substance Regulations. Substance is nevertheless enforced through general anti-avoidance rules, beneficial ownership requirements, special tax adjustments, transfer pricing rules, permanent-establishment principles, controlled foreign company rules, thin-capitalization rules, and anti-treaty-shopping principles. A Taiwan or offshore franchisor should demonstrate real personnel, decision-making, IP ownership or control, risk assumption, expenditure, and operational capacity proportionate to the royalties and service fees received.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Jurisdictional Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment surcharges, administrative fines, disallowance of deductions and incentives, withholding recovery, and possible deregistration or licensing consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives may face administrative sanctions, personal tax-law penalties where legally applicable, restrictions on acting for non-compliant entities, civil restitution, and criminal exposure for intentional false filing or evasion. Travel restrictions are fact-specific and are not automatic.
Tax Evasion and False Filing

Statutory sanction: Recovery of tax, late-payment surcharges, fines generally ranging from 50% to five times the evaded tax for tax evasion, and criminal prosecution where statutory criminal thresholds are met.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors, legal representatives, finance officers, and other directly responsible persons may face criminal fines and imprisonment under the Criminal Law, together with civil restitution and enforcement restrictions.
Invoice and VAT Fraud

Statutory sanction: Input VAT denial, tax recovery, fines, confiscation, administrative detention or criminal prosecution depending on conduct and amount; serious cases may carry substantial imprisonment terms.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face criminal liability, fines, civil recovery, asset seizure, and restrictions on serving as legal representatives. Travel bans may arise during enforcement but are not an automatic tax penalty.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Special tax adjustment, recovery of additional tax, interest calculated under the applicable special-adjustment rules, loss of treaty or incentive benefits, and penalties for documentation or reporting failures.

Director personal liability: Directors generally face corporate rather than automatic personal tax liability, but may incur civil liability for breach of duty and personal administrative or criminal liability where manipulation involves fraud, fabricated documents, bribery, or intentional evasion.
Beneficial Ownership and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or arrangement relief, withholding at the domestic rate, recovery of tax and interest, anti-avoidance adjustment, and penalties for inaccurate withholding filings.

Director personal liability: Personal liability may arise for knowingly false declarations, fraud, document fabrication, or complicity. Civil restitution and enforcement restrictions are possible; imprisonment depends on criminal findings.
Undistributed-Profit and Illegal Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Invalid or recoverable distribution, tax withholding reassessment, foreign-exchange compliance penalties, bank reporting escalation, and possible shareholder repayment obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors may owe civil restitution to the company and creditors and may be liable for breach of fiduciary or capital-maintenance duties. Criminal liability requires additional unlawful conduct.
Unlicensed Food and Franchise Operations

Statutory sanction: Orders to correct or cease operations, confiscation, fines, license suspension or revocation, product recall, consumer compensation, and possible criminal liability for serious food-safety violations.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives and responsible managers may face personal fines, employment or market-access restrictions, civil compensation, and imprisonment for serious food-safety, public-health, or fraud offenses.
Anti-Money Laundering and Suspicious Payments

Statutory sanction: Account restrictions, transaction suspension, reporting failures penalties, confiscation, administrative fines, and criminal prosecution for money laundering or related offenses.

Director personal liability: Responsible individuals may face personal fines, criminal imprisonment, confiscation, civil restitution, and enforcement or travel restrictions depending on the offense and court order.
Customs and Import Understatement

Statutory sanction: Recovery of duty and import VAT, customs fines, confiscation, intensified customs supervision, and criminal prosecution for smuggling or serious customs fraud.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers and agents may face personal fines, criminal liability, civil recovery, asset seizure, and travel restrictions imposed under applicable enforcement procedures.
Permanent Establishment and Local Tax Leakage

Statutory sanction: Assessment of enterprise income tax, VAT, withholding tax, interest, and penalties on attributable profits, together with registration and filing obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors may face corporate-law and tax-law consequences for intentional concealment. Personal imprisonment is not automatic and requires proof of a separate criminal offense.
Bookkeeping, Record Retention, and Data Integrity Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, deemed-profit assessment, denial of deductions and credits, inability to defend transfer pricing, and adverse tax adjustments.

Director personal liability: The company and responsible accounting personnel may be sanctioned. Directors may incur civil or administrative liability where the failure is deliberate, materially prejudicial, or connected with evasion.

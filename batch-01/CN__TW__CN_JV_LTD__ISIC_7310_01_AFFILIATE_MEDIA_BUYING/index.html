Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a general digital advertising and joint venture company under the Taiwan Companies Act. However, the registered capital must be fully subscribed in the Articles of Incorporation, and each shareholder's contribution must be deposited into a segregated corporate capital account at a licensed bank in Taiwan and verified by the bank before registration. No governmental blocked escrow account is required. The capital may be used as working capital after registration, but it may not be returned to shareholders before a lawful distribution.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable at the industrial plant level. For the ordinary registered office, the company must maintain compliance with the Fire Services Act, including fire extinguishers, escape routes, and a fire safety management plan if required by the building occupancy permit, and must comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act on workplace safety; no civil defense license is otherwise mandated.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taiwan – National Taxation Administration, acting through local National Tax Bureaus; online electronic portals: Taxation E-Platform (https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw) and E-Invoice Integration Service Platform (https://www.einvoice.nat.gov.tw).

E-government filing portal

Because the investing party is a mainland Chinese entity, the first mandatory step is to obtain inbound investment permission from the Investment Commission, Ministry of Economic Affairs, Taiwan, under the Regulations Governing the Permission of Investment in Taiwan from the Mainland Area. After approval, the Taiwan-incorporated joint venture must complete company registration under the Taiwan Company Act through the Ministry of Economic Affairs one-stop company registration system, which assigns the eight-digit Unified Business Number used as the tax identification number (TIN). Before commencing business, the company must file the Business Tax Registration Application with the competent local National Tax Bureau, attaching the company registration certificate, investment approval document, lease or place-of-business proof, and authorized representative identification. The National Tax Bureau then confirms the entity as a VAT-registered profit-seeking enterprise and issues the business tax registration certificate. Foreign non-resident shareholders are not issued separate Taiwan TINs for the local entity; payments of Taiwan-source income to such shareholders are subject to withholding by the Taiwan entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Business tax registration is generally required before commencing business; the statutory exemption threshold below which a small business may remain unregistered is average monthly sales of NT$80,000 or less for goods and NT$40,000 or less for services. Once monthly sales exceed these thresholds, mandatory business tax registration is triggered. Additionally, a business with monthly sales below NT$200,000 may be treated as a small-scale business operator assessed at the 1% business tax rate instead of the standard 5% VAT, while monthly sales at or above NT$200,000 generally require mandatory Uniform Invoice issuance and full VAT accounting.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Article 7 of the Taiwan Business Tax Act applies to goods physically exported from Taiwan under customs control and to services rendered in Taiwan that are actually used outside Taiwan, including cross-border digital advertising, affiliate media-buying, and lead-generation services performed for overseas clients. To claim 0% VAT, the taxpayer must hold a genuine and complete export or offshore-service file proving shipment or overseas use, and the foreign exchange proceeds must be collected through authorized banking channels.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Export Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Foreign Exchange Transaction Declaration and bank inward remittance settlement certificate or equivalent bank foreign exchange receipt evidence
  • Export Service Contract and proof of overseas utilization for offshore services

Taiwan requires VAT-registered businesses to issue Uniform Invoices for all taxable sales. Electronic Uniform Invoices must be transmitted to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integration Service Platform through the Turnkey application programming interface or the e-service portal, where invoice data is archived and assigned a validation number. This is a post-issuance secure transmission mandate rather than a real-time fiscal pre-clearance stamping system. Mandatory cloud-based e-invoices apply to designated e-commerce, retail-chain, and high-volume digital-commerce sectors, and consumer-facing e-invoices must carry QR-code digital validation. Failure to transmit e-invoice data may result in Uniform Invoice penalties and suspension of tax benefits.

The National Taxation Administration may retrospectively reassess unsubstantiated or fraudulent zero-export claims as domestic taxable sales at the 5% VAT rate. Under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act, the taxpayer is liable for the underpaid tax plus a fine of not less than one time and not more than five times the additional tax amount. If the claim is supported by forged customs declarations, fictitious foreign exchange repatriation, or false service contracts, criminal liability may arise under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act, with imprisonment for up to five years, and the applicable tax assessment period is extended to seven years for tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a bifurcated convertibility regime. Current-account items, including services-trade inflows and outflows for online advertising and affiliate media buying, settlement of dividends, and payment of platform fees, are fully convertible and may be settled directly at a domestic bank under SAFE post-verification without prior approval. Capital-account items, including FDI capital injection, equity transfers between the Taiwan investor and the joint venture, external borrowing, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds, require prior SAFE registration or approval. TWD is not freely convertible inside mainland China; receipts from Taiwan are normally settled in USD or CNH. Dividend remittance to the Taiwan corporate investor is permitted only after audited financial statements are filed, corporate income tax is settled, withholding at 10% is paid, and the FIE annual report is submitted. Cross-border online advertising service fees are treated as services trade and are outside the capital-account quota, but each remittance must carry a bona fide underlying contract and platform delivery evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory surrender of foreign exchange was abolished in 2012; the present rule is mandated repatriation with retained use. The joint venture must repatriate all export or services-trade proceeds, including affiliate commissions and advertising arbitrage revenue for overseas platforms, into its domestic settlement account under SAFE's Trade in Goods and Trade in Services Foreign Exchange Administration Measures. The statutory expectation is that proceeds enter China within the verification window of the relevant customs declaration or service invoice, and in practice no later than 180 days from the issuance of the underlying invoice; amounts outstanding beyond 90 days are flagged in the SAFE Total Amount Monitoring System and beyond 180 days trigger an explicit written explanation, bank lodging, and potential administrative penalty. Conversion of retained foreign currency into RMB is at the enterprise's commercial discretion, with no forced conversion ratio, and requires a bank-declared purpose with supporting documents.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) in coordination with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with cross-strait RMB clearing routed through the Bank of China Taipei branch and supervised on the Taiwan side by the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) and the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for the China-domiciled joint venture. Offshore digital EMIs such as Mercury, Wise, and Currencycloud cannot onboard a China-incorporated entity because they hold no Chinese banking licence and SAFE prohibits a resident enterprise from maintaining undisclosed offshore bank balances. Wise Business does not support China-incorporated companies for USD or TWD receiving accounts. The practical alternative is a Taiwan or Hong Kong affiliate vehicle collecting platform payouts and executing EMI-based disbursements, while the China entity receives its earned margin via a licensed onshore settlement channel. Within China, licensed cross-border payment institutions registered with SAFE, such as LianLian, PingPong, and Payoneer through its licensed China partner, are the permissible fintech-style rails for affiliate revenue settlement; a direct foreign EMI account for the China entity is structurally disfavoured and treated as a round-tripping violation if it is used to hide onshore receipt obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan or Hong Kong investor remits USD via SWIFT MT103, or CNH via CIPS, to the joint venture's FDI Capital Account at the Chinese custodian bank. The remittance is identified as direct investment by a foreign investor in the equity of a foreign-invested enterprise, referencing the SAFE FDI registration notice and the FIE business licence. The bank validates the instruction against the Capital Project Information System, books the funds, and converts them to RMB for permitted working-capital purposes such as rent, payroll, and advertising inventory procurement. No minimum statutory paid-in capital applies under the Foreign Investment Law 2020; the contribution schedule agreed in the joint venture contract governs. The capital injection itself is not subject to income tax, but a 10% withholding tax applies on any later dividend distribution to the Taiwan investor. Current-account receivables from Taiwan affiliates are routed to the USD settlement account under the SAFE services-trade purpose code for advertising, supported by the relevant contract and invoice retained for five years.Payments to overseas advertising platforms such as Google, Meta, TikTok, and X, and to Taiwan media vendors, are cleared via SWIFT MT103 for USD or TWD-converted-USD, and via CIPS for CNH-denominated payments to Taiwan counterparties using Bank of China Taipei as the RMB clearing counterparty. Every payment must be declared under the SAFE services-trade purpose code for advertising and market-research services, with the platform invoice, media-delivery proof such as impression logs, and the corresponding service agreement lodged at the bank. Same-day TWD disbursement to Taiwan publishers is not directly available onshore; treasury batches TWD through a USD side-rail with FX conversion at the Taiwan intermediary. The paying bank requires evidence of the withholding tax paid on VAT and enterprise income tax for cross-border services before the remittance is released. Letters of credit are not standard in performance-based media buying; the dominant rails are MT103 wires for vendor settlements and contract-referenced platform remittances.Onshore CNY/USD FX forward contracts and FX swaps with real services-trade or capital-injection exposure, entered under a bank-approved hedging line and SAFE derivative qualification under the real-demand principle.FX options, both purchased and written, conducted with the settlement bank under the SAFE-approved derivatives framework, including zero-cost collars, subject to the bank's suitability and realisation test.Cross-currency interest rate swaps solely for long-term FDI debt or registered equity situations, requiring prior SAFE capital-account approval.Explicitly excluded: offshore CNY non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for onshore entities.

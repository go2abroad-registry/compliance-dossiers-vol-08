Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the Taiwan Company Act for an ordinary limited company. However, the foreign shareholder must fully subscribe and pay for all issued shares, deposit the paid-in capital into a dedicated bank capital verification account, and obtain a certified public accountant capital verification report before the establishment registration is filed. The deposited funds remain escrowed and may not be withdrawn for operations until the Ministry of Economic Affairs issues the company registration certificate. The foreign investment approval letter will state the approved remitted capital amount, and the company cannot reduce that amount without Investment Commission consent.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a branch or permanent establishment in Taiwan, the procedure to obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN, known as the Unified Business Number) is as follows: (1) Secure approval from the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) for branch incorporation, filing the required application and supporting documents. (2) Upon MOEA approval, register with the local National Taxation Bureau (NTB) within 15 days, submitting the application form, MOEA approval letter, lease agreement for the Taiwan office, and identification documents of the responsible person. (3) The NTB will issue a Unified Business Number (UBI) and a Tax Registration Certificate. (4) If the entity has no physical presence but engages in taxable activities, it must appoint a tax agent and file a registration application with the NTB. (5) For VAT purposes, the entity must also register for the Uniform Invoice system and obtain a Uniform Invoice number.

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated if the goods are physically exported from Taiwan or services are provided to foreign clients and the consideration is received in foreign currency. The exporter must provide evidence of export and foreign exchange receipt to claim the 0% VAT rate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a managed floating RMB regime where current-account receipts and payments are generally convertible, while capital-account transactions remain tightly administered by SAFE. The WFOE’s paid-in equity contribution from its Taiwanese parent is classified as foreign direct investment under the capital account. Therefore, before any inward remittance, the WFOE must complete FDI foreign-exchange registration with SAFE or through the receiving designated bank, open a dedicated capital foreign-exchange account, and obtain an FDI registration certificate. Once capital is settled into RMB, the funds enter a settlement-to-be-paid RMB account and may only be deployed for the declared genuine business purpose, such as construction or lease of temperature-controlled storage, purchase of refrigeration equipment, operational expenses, or salary payments; using capital funds for prohibited investments, entrusted loans, or secondary market speculation is non-compliant. Export receipts from cold-storage and refrigerated logistics are current-account income, so they do not require SAFE pre-approval, but every receipt must be reported under the Trade in Foreign Exchange Monitoring System and matched with customs declarations, warehouse contracts, and shipping documents. Profit remittance to the Taiwanese parent is a current-account dividend, yet practical hurdles include audited financial statements showing distributable profits, a board shareholder resolution, tax-clearance confirmations and withholding tax receipts, plus bank-level AML due diligence; these documentary requirements can stall large one-off remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE’s current trade reform, there is no mandatory foreign exchange surrender ratio and no compulsory conversion of export proceeds into RMB. Export receipts from cold-storage warehousing services must be repatriated to China and received in the WFOE’s designated current-account foreign exchange bank account, and must be reported under the Trade in Foreign Exchange Monitoring System within the payment term shown in the commercial contract; deferred collection beyond 90 days from the date of export declaration requires the WFOE to be registered as a deferred-receipt trade enterprise or requires SAFE or bank approval. The WFOE may retain up to 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency deposit accounts, or sell them to the bank at the prevailing market rate; conversion is an enterprise choice, not a statutory requirement. Any prolonged failure to repatriate or any discrepancy between customs records, logistics bills, and bank payment value will be automatically flagged and may disqualify the WFOE from future trade facilitation status.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and virtual-account providers have low viability for this China-domiciled WFOE. Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, only banks designated by SAFE may act as designated foreign exchange banks to lawfully conduct settlement and sale of foreign exchange, handle receipt and payment of trade and investment transactions, and report BOP or SAFE data. Offshore EMI licenses such as UK FCA, Singapore MAS, or Hong Kong MSO or VASP confer no authority to open a domestic RMB basic deposit account, maintain an FDI capital account, or perform SAFE registration and capital verification for the WFOE. An EMI can at most provide multi-currency collection and payment front-ends by partnering with a licensed Chinese bank that remains the legal settlement bank, executes KYC and AML controls, and issues all customs and SAFE reporting; even then, the FDI capital injection route and profit remittances should not be pushed through the EMI because bank account ownership and audit trail must rest with the Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route the Taiwanese parent’s remittance via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE’s sole FDI capital-account bank chosen at offshore registration. Prior to remittance, the WFOE must complete SAFE FDI foreign exchange registration, obtain the Foreign-Invested Enterprise foreign exchange registration certificate, and open a Capital Account for FDI equity. The remittance should be annotated with the FDI registration number and the purpose phrase Equity capital injection into CN_WFOE_LTD. The receiving bank will code the transaction under the SAFE Balance-of-Payments purpose classification for direct investment equity capital and will immediately pre-verify the remitter name against the registered foreign investor. Upon receipt, funds are held in the capital account; when used, the bank converts RMB under the settlement-to-be-paid mechanism and monitors the use of proceeds against the validated business plan; a registered Chinese CPA then issues a capital verification report confirming that the full stated registered capital is paid in and reconciles it with the bank’s returned records.For outbound payments from the WFOE to foreign suppliers of refrigeration equipment, spare parts, logistics carriers, and technology licensors, the standard rails are: first, SWIFT MT103 direct telegraphic transfer for low-value and standard delivery terms; second, commercial letters of credit at sight or usance for larger capital goods, governed by UCP600 and issued under the importing bank’s credit approval; and third, documentary collections such as D/P or D/A for established supplier relationships. Every payment must be matched to a valid trade declaration, including the import customs declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, insurance certificate, and, for service trade payments, the tax bureau’s cross-border service trade registration or withholding certificate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million for tangible property transactions, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, and RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions in a fiscal year trigger the mandatory Local File; the Master File is required where aggregate annual related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion or more. All cross-border intercompany charges, including cold-storage service fees, royalties, interest, and management fees, must be supported by contemporaneous documentation even below the thresholds if their quantum or method is abnormal.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the EIT Law GAAR, the beneficial-ownership provisions, and the OECD BEPS minimum standards require the WFOE to have genuine economic substance to secure treaty and domestic-treatment benefits. For temperature-controlled cold storage, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include maintaining temperature integrity, warehouse security and insurance, inventory receipts and dispatches, cold-chain traceability, energy management, customer contracting, and management of spoilage risk. The WFOE must employ local staff, control physical assets, bear operational risk, and make real commercial decisions in China; a passive shell holding a warehouse lease will be disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer pricing / tax evasion through falsely low intercompany storage fees

Statutory sanction: The tax authority recharacterises the consideration and imposes a transfer-pricing adjustment under EIT Law Articles 41 and 48; the shortfall is taxed at 25%; late-interest begins from the date underpaid; a transfer-pricing penalty or a 50%-500% tax-evasion penalty under TAL Article 63 may be imposed; Criminal Law Article 201 applies if the conduct involves fraudulent returns causing significant underpayment.

Director personal liability: The director and financial controller who signed the false return are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and penalties if corporate assets were commingled or fraudulently transferred; courts may pierce the veil under PRC Company Law Article 20; the tax authority may freeze bank accounts, seize cold-storage equipment, blacklist the entity for tax dishonesty, and notify immigration to ban the legal representative from leaving China.
Shell-company / treaty abuse by a Taiwan conduit shareholder

Statutory sanction: The competent tax authority denies treaty benefits under EIT Law Article 26 and the beneficial-ownership rules in SAT Announcements 2009 No. 2 and 2015 No. 60; the difference between the 5% and 10% WHT is recovered with late interest; GAAR under EIT Law Article 47 allows a redetermination of the business purpose, and penalties reach 50%-500% of the underpaid tax for aggravated concealment.

Director personal liability: If the WFOE's directors allow it to be used as a conduit, they face civil liability for restitution of unwarranted treaty refunds; personal assets can be attached where the company's separate legal personality is abused; criminal liability arises under Criminal Law Article 201 (tax evasion) and Article 162 (false liquidation/evasion of debts); exit bans and social-credit blacklisting apply to the responsible director.
Withholding tax failure and unauthorised profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is held liable under TAL Articles 26 and 69; if the tax is not withheld or remitted, the tax authority collects the overdue tax from the WFOE and imposes a penalty of 50%-300% of the unwithheld amount; the underpaid withholding tax bears 0.05% daily late interest; deliberate non-remittance is criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the WFOE to distribute profits without withholding are personally liable to the extent of assets transferred; the corporate veil is pierced under Company Law Article 20 if the dividend was paid while the WFOE was insolvent; responsible persons face civil damages, confiscation of personal assets, travel restrictions, and imprisonment of up to seven years for large-scale evasion.

Competent corporate registry

Department of Commercial Services, Ministry of Economic Affairs, Taiwan (R.O.C.) (經濟部商業司), with mandatory pre-approval by the Investment Commission (投資審議委員會) for foreign and Mainland-China investment.

Governing statute

Taiwan (R.O.C.) Companies Act (公司法), Articles 1, 128-130, 133-138, 156, 387; Foreign Investment Act (外國人投資條例), Articles 3, 6-8; Regulations Governing Investment in Taiwan by People of the Mainland Area (大陸地區人民來臺投資許可辦法) applicable to PRC-origin shareholders; additional scrutiny under the Investment Commission review rules.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is imposed under the Taiwan Companies Act; however, the registered capital must be fully subscribed and actually paid in, certified by a R.O.C.-licensed CPA capital verification report (資本額查核報告書). The Investment Commission must approve the total investment amount before remittance; funds are remitted through a designated foreign exchange bank into the Company's capital account. No separate escrow account is statutorily required, but the bank remittance certificate serves as the escrow-equivalent proof for capital verification.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: fire-protection equipment installation and annual inspection under Fire Services Act Articles 6, 10, and 13; maintain an emergency escape plan and, if more than 100 employees, establish a fire-safety management committee. Building: verify that the certificate of occupancy permits office use (Group G-2) and obtain any required change-of-use authorisation from the municipal Construction Bureau. Occupational: comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act — conduct ergonomics and electrical-safety hazard identification, provide first-aid equipment, register workplace risk assessments, and report any occupational accidents within the statutory 8-hour notification window.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Taiwan Ministry of Finance, administered locally by the competent National Taxation Bureau; electronic tax services are provided through the Ministry of Finance eTax Portal and the integrated company-registration and business-registration systems.

E-government filing portal

A Sino-foreign equity joint venture must first complete entity establishment and business registration with the competent Taiwan company-registration authority, including foreign investment approval where applicable. The entity must then register its business-tax status with the competent National Taxation Bureau before commencing taxable business activities. The tax authority issues or confirms the eight-digit Unified Business Number (統一編號), which functions as the entity's statutory tax and business identification number. Typical supporting documents include the approved articles of incorporation, company registration certificate, foreign investor approval documents, responsible-person identification, lease or premises evidence, business-scope description, bank-account information where requested, and an authorized-agent power of attorney. A foreign enterprise without a Taiwan company may require branch, representative-office, or other approved operating registration before obtaining a tax identity, depending on the activities performed. Changes to responsible persons, registered address, business scope, capital, or tax status must be reported within the applicable statutory period.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not provide a general United States-style check-the-box election for companies. A Taiwan limited company or Sino-foreign equity joint venture is generally taxed as a separate legal person. Pass-through treatment is not available merely by election. Certain transparent or special regimes may arise from the legal form and governing trust, partnership, securities, or investment statutes, but an employee stock ownership trust must be analyzed separately from the operating joint venture. Taiwan generally has no broad hybrid-mismatch election that converts an ordinary company into a pass-through entity. Tax incentives, investment credits, and approved special regimes require satisfaction of their specific statutory conditions and do not change the entity's default legal tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The small-business exemption thresholds are generally NT$80,000 of monthly sales for goods and NT$40,000 of monthly sales for services. Businesses exceeding the applicable threshold, or otherwise carrying on taxable business requiring registration, must complete business-tax registration. The threshold does not eliminate registration or other tax obligations where a specific statutory registration requirement applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Taiwan may generally qualify for 0% business tax when the export is properly declared through customs and the taxpayer can establish that the goods physically left Taiwan. Qualifying export-related transactions may include goods sold for export, goods exported by the seller, and other transactions specifically recognized under the Business Tax Act and related regulations. Services may qualify for zero rating only where they fall within the statutory categories, such as services supplied to foreign customers and used outside Taiwan or other expressly recognized cross-border services. The taxpayer must establish the customer, contractual scope, place and use of the service, consideration, payment trail, and documentary evidence supporting offshore use. Zero rating does not automatically apply merely because the customer is non-Taiwanese or payment is received in foreign currency. Input business tax refunds require valid tax invoices or approved documents, reconciliation to books and tax returns, and satisfaction of the statutory refund conditions. Unsupported, incorrectly classified, or subsequently cancelled exports may be reassessed at the applicable business-tax rate.

  • Taiwan Customs export declaration or electronically filed export declaration bearing the relevant customs acceptance or release information
  • Commercial invoice and packing list, where applicable
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or other transport document evidencing shipment from Taiwan
  • Sales contract, purchase order, or equivalent export transaction record
  • Proof of export, customs release, or other evidence that the goods physically departed Taiwan
  • Electronic invoice or other legally prescribed sales document, with zero-rate coding where required
  • For offshore services: service agreement, customer and beneficiary evidence, deliverables, timesheets or service records where relevant, and evidence that the service was used outside Taiwan
  • Bank remittance advice, inward-remittance record, or other payment evidence where required to substantiate consideration; this is not a universal substitute for customs export evidence and is not independently sufficient for every zero-rated transaction
  • Import or export permits, certificates of origin, inspection records, or sector-specific approvals where required by the goods or transaction

Taiwan uses an electronic-invoice platform and standardized electronic-invoice formats administered under Ministry of Finance rules. Covered business entities must issue, transmit, retain, and report electronic invoices in accordance with the applicable category-specific timetable and technical requirements. Electronic invoices are generally uploaded or transmitted through the Ministry of Finance platform or an approved electronic-invoice service provider; Taiwan does not operate a universal Latin American-style real-time pre-clearance model for every transaction. Business-to-business transactions, export transactions, allowance documents, cancellation documents, and financial or system-generated invoices may have different issuance and transmission requirements. Digital signatures, qualified certificates, authorized system access, invoice numbering, XML or prescribed-format data, audit trails, and retention controls may be required. The entity should verify current implementation dates and exemptions with the competent National Taxation Bureau before commencing invoicing.

Taiwan does not impose a universal 180-day foreign-exchange-repatriation rule that independently determines business-tax zero-rating for every export transaction. The 180-day value should therefore be treated as a compliance-control parameter requiring confirmation against the transaction type, banking rules, contract terms, and current Central Bank requirements. Failure to collect or document export proceeds may trigger banking, foreign-exchange, customs, or tax review, but does not automatically establish taxable treatment without analysis of the underlying transaction. During a tax audit, the National Taxation Bureau may require customs records, transport evidence, contracts, invoices, payment records, service-use evidence, and reconciliation to accounting books and VAT returns. If zero-rating is denied, the authority may assess the applicable 5% business tax, interest, late-payment charges, and statutory penalties; input-tax refunds may be clawed back and previously refunded amounts recovered. False invoices, fabricated export records, concealment of taxable sales, or intentional tax evasion may additionally result in administrative fines, suspension or cancellation of invoice privileges, customs consequences, and criminal liability under applicable Taiwan tax and criminal laws. Voluntary correction before audit, prompt repayment, complete records, and documented internal controls may reduce exposure but do not eliminate statutory liabilities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CNY is freely convertible on the current account (goods, services, dividends, interest) since China's IMF Article VIII commitment, subject to bank-conducted authenticity and legality review under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations (国务院令第532号, as amended) and the SAFE Trade in Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Capital account items (FDI equity injection, cross-border ESOP sponsor loans, equity transfers of trust-held shares, external debt) remain registration-based: FDI capital must obtain SAFE registration before receipt, be credited to the dedicated Capital Escrow Account (外汇资本金账户), and may only be settled under the proceeds-commitment / voluntary settlement regime (支付命令函, 意愿结汇 per 汇发〔2016〕16号). Dividend and profit remittance by the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is classified as current account and requires the Outward Payment Tax Filing Certificate (对外支付税务备案表), audited financial statements, and a board profit-distribution resolution; the Taiwanese shareholder's dividend benefits from a treaty-reduced 5% withholding if its direct shareholding is 25% or more and substance conditions are met, otherwise the domestic 10% withholding applies. TWD (新台币) is not a freely convertible listed currency on the China interbank market; Taiwan-side capital injection is therefore typically denominated in USD or CNY, with TWD conversion executed via USD on the offshore leg.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory export-proceeds surrender and fixed statutory conversion ratios were abolished by SAFE Announcement No.1 of 2012. Export receipts must still be repatriated into mainland China and monitored through the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System (货物贸易外汇监测系统); A-class enterprises may settle at the bank counter without case-by-case SAFE approval, while B/C-class enterprises face documentary examination for each transaction. Amounts outstanding beyond the contractually agreed collection period (customs practice 90 days per the Single Window norm) combined with abnormal overdue-collection ratios can trigger a downgrade from A to B/C classification. There is no statutory minimum percentage of export proceeds that must be converted into CNY; the JV may retain foreign currency in its current-account FX account or settle at will. ESOP-related cross-border sale proceeds are capital-account receipts and require separate SAFE registration under employee equity incentive FX rules (汇发〔2012〕7号), not the trade-monitoring surrender regime.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (中国人民银行, PBoC) as the central bank, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (国家外汇管理局, SAFE) exercising delegated FX administration under State Council authorization. Cross-strait capital flows involving the Taiwan jurisdiction are additionally governed by the Law on the Protection of Investment by Taiwanese Compatriots (台湾同胞投资保护法) and the 2015 Cross-Strait Avoidance of Double Taxation and Strengthening of Tax Cooperation Agreement.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% for dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders (including Mainland Chinese corporate/individual investors), effective from the 2018 tax reform. Prior years were 20%. The rate is final and is levied on the gross dividend. No reduced domestic rate applies to a Mainland China investor because no cross-strait DTA is in force.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory local file/master file preparation and TP report are triggered when the aggregate annual related-party transaction amount exceeds NT$100 million (approximately USD 3.3 million). An SME exemption may apply to enterprises with related-party transactions below NT$100 million and total revenue below NT$100 million, but the disclosure schedule must still be attached. Country-by-country reporting applies to MNE groups with parent-group consolidated revenue exceeding NT$10 billion (approximately USD 330 million).

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not have a general Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) statute akin to the UAE, but the Income Tax Act Articles 12-1 and 12-3 embody a broad general anti-avoidance rule and judicial substance-over-form principles. For an ESOP trust administration JV, the enterprise must demonstrate real business substance in Taiwan: physical office, full-time administrative and compliance personnel, board minutes, independent accounts, and documented decision-making functions. A JV operating as a shell with no Taiwan employees or office, or merely channelling dividends derived from mainland-linked treasury shares, will be recharacterized, deductions denied, profits allocated to the beneficial parent, and potentially the investment approval revoked.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Underreporting Taiwan-Source Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Income Tax Act, a penalty is imposed on the amount of underpaid tax, ranging from 0.5 to three times the understated tax. Criminal tax evasion under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act carries imprisonment for up to five years and/or a criminal fine.

Director personal liability: The responsible director and finance officer are jointly and severally liable for unpaid company taxes if they caused or allowed asset concealment (Tax Collection Act Art. 6). The National Taxation Bureau may apply to the court for a travel ban, and the director’s personal assets may be attached to satisfy the tax shortfall.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Investment Commission (MOEA) may revoke the mainland investment approval under the Statute for Investment by Mainland China and order divestment. The National Taxation Bureau may disregard the JV’s legal personality, allocate its income to the Real Beneficial Owner, and assess tax at 21% withholding plus 20% CIT with a penalty of up to three times the deficiency.

Director personal liability: Directors and nominal shareholders may be ordered to return all distributed funds to the company (Company Law Art. 23). Civil restitution claims, administrative debarment from serving as directors, and incapability to hold a passport may be imposed; criminal banking or securities charges can result in imprisonment if the structure qualifies as a financial fraud or false disclosure offence.
AML / Money Laundering via ESOP Trust

Statutory sanction: The Money Laundering Control Act (as amended in 2018) imposes administrative fines of NT$1 million to NT$10 million on trust service companies and their directors for non-compliance with internal control and reporting obligations (Art. 18). If the activity is willful, the responsible person is subject to imprisonment of six months to five years, and the JV is subject to corporate criminal fines (Art. 20).

Director personal liability: Directors, trustees, and AML compliance officers can be prosecuted individually and face disgorgement of all fees plus personal monetary fines. The court may order freezing of bank accounts, seizure of assets, and prohibition on leaving Taiwan; the JV license or business scope certificate may be cancelled by the competent authority.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will adjust the taxable income to the arm’s-length result under the Taiwan Transfer Pricing Regulations. The adjusted tax is assessed at 20% with interest; a penalty of up to three times the underpaid tax applies under Income Tax Act Article 110. Failure to prepare the mandatory TP report within the statutory period also triggers a separate penalty of NT$50,000 to NT$100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or cause false TP documentation may bear civil liability for resulting tax penalties and may be referred to the tax prosecutor for criminal fraud. If the TP file is deemed fraudulent, the officer can be sentenced to imprisonment for up to five years and be personally responsible for restitution of the tax loss.
Unlawful Dividend Distribution / Capital Maintenance Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Company Law, distributions of dividends must be made only from the company’s annual surplus. Directors are liable to restore the full amount of unlawful distributions; administrative fines of NT$100,000 to NT$2 million may be imposed by the competent authority (Company Law Art. 89).

Director personal liability: Each director and supervisor who approved or executed the distribution bears personal and joint liability to creditors and shareholders for the amount illegally distributed. Bankruptcy proceedings and disqualification from business management may result; in severe cases with intent to defraud creditors, criminal liability under Company Law Article 23 and the Criminal Code (fraud) applies, carrying imprisonment.

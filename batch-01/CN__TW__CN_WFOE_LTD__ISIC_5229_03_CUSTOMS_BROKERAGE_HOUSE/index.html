Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is required for a Taiwan WFOE limited liability company under the Companies Act, provided the subscribed capital is real, sufficient for the approved business scope, and fully paid before filing. The capital must be deposited in a dedicated corporate bank account in the company's name and certified by a Taiwan CPA; it is not a blocked escrow account, but funds may not be withdrawn or pledged before the incorporation registration is completed. For the customs brokerage license, the company must demonstrate paid-in capital of at least NT$5,000,000 (about USD 156,000), and it is operationally recommended to meet this threshold at incorporation to avoid a later capital increase.

Funding & unlock path: The office premises must obtain a building use license and a fire safety equipment inspection certificate from the local fire department under the Fire Services Act. The customs brokerage operation must be housed in a structurally safe, non-residential premises and must maintain an emergency evacuation plan. The responsible customs broker must hold an effective professional customs license and be employed by the company. If the company later operates a bonded warehouse, it must also complete an Occupational Safety and Health Administration workplace hazard assessment and maintain material safety data sheets for any stored regulated goods.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating the national electronic tax filing and compliance portal known as the Electronic Tax Service Platform; for the present China-Taiwan cross-border customs brokerage engagement, Taiwan is treated as the export destination and does not create a China-side permanent establishment in Taiwan.

E-government filing portal

Upon formation of the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) as a Chinese resident limited liability company, the Administration for Market Regulation issues a business license containing the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code, which automatically constitutes the enterprise's statutory Tax Identification Number for all PRC tax purposes under the integrated licensing and tax registration system. No separate TIN procurement is required for the WFOE itself. For a foreign non-resident enterprise that owns the WFOE and receives dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees from China, the non-resident must apply for a non-resident tax identification number at the competent STA bureau and settle its Chinese-source income tax through the withholding agent, using the applicable Double Tax Agreement or Cross-Strait taxation arrangement documentation for any reduced rate claim. All tax registrations, filings, VAT invoice issuance, and export tax refund procedures are administered through the Electronic Tax Service Platform and the China International Trade Single Window.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold for basic VAT registration of a Chinese WFOE; the WFOE must register for VAT upon business license issuance and Unified Social Credit Code assignment. Under Article 28 of the VAT transitional rules, a mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold applies at annual taxable turnover of RMB 5,000,000. Below that amount, the WFOE may be treated as a small-scale taxpayer, but because the entity is a foreign-invested enterprise engaged in customs brokerage and international trade facilitation, it is advisable to elect General VAT Taxpayer status to claim input VAT credits and issue tax-specialized invoices. Customs brokerage services are standard-rated at 6% VAT, while the national standard headline VAT rate for taxable sales of goods is 13%; export of qualifying goods is zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT law, exported goods are zero-rated, meaning the input VAT attributable to the exported goods can remain deductible or refundable through the export tax refund process. A qualifying export requires a customs export declaration that matches the sales contract, an export invoice, a proper bill of lading or airway bill, and full foreign exchange repatriation through a duly licensed bank in China. For cross-border services, zero-rating or exemption is available only where the service recipient is an overseas party, the service is consumed entirely outside China, and payment is received in foreign currency; a customs brokerage service that is performed domestically for the convenience of an exporter is generally treated as a domestic taxable service subject to VAT at 6%, not as zero-rated, unless the entire service is supplied to and consumed by an overseas counterparty in a qualifying cross-border services category.

  • Commercial Invoice and Export Sales Contract
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Customs Release Record and Export Tax Rebate Verification Data
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Receipt Voucher from the SAFE Trade Payment and Collection Monitoring System

China operates a centralized digital tax-control compliance environment under the Golden Tax System Phase IV and the national fully digitalized electronic invoice (su dian piao) service platform. VAT special invoices and ordinary invoices must be issued through certified tax-control equipment or the electronic tax service platform, with encrypted invoice datasets transmitted to the STA in near real time for validation. Customs brokers are required to issue lawful VAT invoices for their brokerage fees and cannot use blank or self-styled receipts. The national fully digital electronic invoice system supports automated receipt, XML data capture, and invoice clearing via the taxpayer's digital account; any invoice issued outside the legitimate STA platform is non-compliant and ineligible for input VAT deduction or export refund substantiation.

The State Taxation Administration may retroactively deny or claw back any VAT zero-rating or export tax refund where the underlying customs declaration is false, the goods are not actually exported, foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day deadline, or the documentation bundle is incomplete or inconsistent with supporting logistics records. Under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and the Criminal Law, the taxpayer is liable to repay all refunded tax, pay a late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day of the underpaid tax, and may be fined from 50% to 500% of the fraudulent refund amount; shipments may also be subject to Customs Law penalties including forfeiture of goods, suspension or revocation of the customs broker license, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent export tax refund behavior.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland China-based WFOE operates under a current account that is broadly convertible for trade and services, but the capital account remains heavily administered by SAFE. RMB is conditionally convertible for bona fide business transactions; every cross-border flow must be supported by authenticity documentation and a registered purpose code. Inbound FDI capital into the WFOE requires SAFE FDI registration, a designated foreign exchange capital account, and a post-injection capital verification and settlement process. Converted RMB cannot be used for purposes outside the WFOE's business scope, share equity investments, securities, speculative wealth management, or repayment of domestic RMB loans unless expressly permitted. Export proceeds must be repatriated through a licensed bank into the trade verification monitoring system; there is no blanket fixed mandatory conversion ratio, but receipts must either be settled into RMB or retained in a permissible foreign currency account with full trade documentation. Profit remittance to the Taiwan shareholder is treated as current account dividend outflow and requires audited financial statements, a lawful distribution resolution, tax clearance/withholding tax voucher, and proof that the capital settlement complied with the negative list. Taiwan-side CBC rules also apply to the outward remittance from Taiwan, requiring cross-strait investment approval and bank-level declaration. Overall, convertibility is operational for genuine trade and investment, but any attempt to use the WFOE as a round-tripping or treasury-pass-through vehicle will trigger immediate bank and SAFE rejection.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods export, SAFE requires the WFOE to repatriate foreign currency export proceeds to a designated bank account and complete the goods trade verification/write-off process within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration; extensions require SAFE registered justification. The mandatory conversion ratio is not a fixed statutory percentage, but all proceeds must be returned to China and either settled into RMB through a licensed bank or held in a SAFE-approved foreign exchange current account, with underlying customs declarations, invoices, contracts, and logistics evidence supporting the receipt. Unjustified offshore retention is an AML and FX violation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the mainland CN WFOE; Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC) for Taiwan-side remittance and clearing reporting

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and legally unsuitable for primary onboarding. Mainland China does not permit offshore digital EMI platforms to hold WFOE operating, capital, or foreign exchange accounts, and they cannot perform SAFE registration, capital verification, conversion, or cross-strait remittance supervision. An offshore EMI may only sit as a restricted corporate card or disbursement utility in very narrow cases, but any RMB-denominated treasury flow must clear through a licensed onshore commercial bank. Taiwan-side clearing similarly requires an FSC-licensed bank; a fintech EMI cannot act as the legal counterparty for foreign exchange or MT103 rail settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: TWD 200,000,000 in annual related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File preparation; group-level Master File and country-by-country reporting thresholds apply separately to multinational groups above the Taiwan consolidated revenue thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the substance-over-form and beneficial ownership doctrines require that the WFOE perform core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Taiwan, including physical office presence, qualified customs declaration personnel, managerial decision-making, contract execution, and bonded warehouse oversight. A letterbox entity with no local substance is exposed to tax recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, and license revocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through non-filing or underreporting

Statutory sanction: Under Taiwan Income Tax Act Article 110, the National Taxation Bureau assesses the omitted tax plus a fine of up to three times the underpaid amount; late-filing surcharges under Article 108 also apply. Criminal prosecution may be initiated under Tax Collection Act Article 41.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause false returns may have the corporate veil pierced if the WFOE is found to be a sham; they face personal civil liability for unpaid tax and interest, asset attachment, travel ban and blacklisting in the tax enforcement register, and criminal imprisonment of up to five years.
Transfer pricing manipulation and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 43-1 authorizes reattribution of income at arm's length, with penalties up to three times the underpaid tax under Article 110. Recharacterized distributions attract 21% dividend withholding plus interest and surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable where they directed the creation of false transfer pricing documentation; civil restitution of the full tax shortfall is enforced against personal assets; criminal conviction can impose imprisonment, and serious unpaid assessments trigger departure bans and Ministry of Finance blacklisting.
Shell company abuse, beneficial ownership misstatement, and AML non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may deny all treaty and preferential tax benefits, impose full statutory WHT of 21% on dividends and 20% on royalties and interest, assess penalties of up to three times the evaded tax, and coordinate with the Customs Administration to suspend or revoke the customs brokerage license. AML violations trigger separate monetary penalties and criminal liability under Taiwan's Money Laundering Control Act.

Director personal liability: If the corporate veil is pierced for fraud or sham, directors and beneficial owners are personally liable for all unpaid corporate tax and penalties; travel bans and blacklisting are imposed under the Tax Collection Act enforcement powers; intentional false disclosure or money laundering can result in imprisonment for directors and controlling persons.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Taiwan Companies Act imposes no general minimum paid-in capital for a Company Limited by Shares. However, because the entity will operate as a private employment service institution engaged in executive search and headhunting, the Ministry of Labor requires minimum paid-in capital of NTD 5,000,000 for domestic placement and NTD 10,000,000 if the institution also recruits or dispatches foreign workers. The capital must be fully subscribed in cash by the shareholders, deposited in a dedicated capital account opened in the exact company name, and verified by a certified public accountant. No statutory blocked escrow account is mandatory, but the capital may not be depleted or withdrawn before the Private Employment Service Institution Establishment Permit is issued and the required security deposit is posted.

Funding & unlock path: The office premises must obtain fire safety equipment inspection and occupancy clearance from the local fire department under the Fire Services Act before staff take occupancy, including the installation of fire extinguishers, emergency lighting, and an appropriate fire-evacuation plan. Because the entity has no manufacturing, chemical, assembly, or warehousing operations, no Civil Defense hazardous-materials license, no compressed-gas permit, and no industrial site operation license are required. The employer must also complete labor occupational safety and health facility reporting, appoint a designated workplace safety person, enroll all employees in labor insurance, and maintain a compliant indoor workplace environment before commencing headhunting operations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture incorporated in Taiwan must first complete company registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) under the Company Act. Upon approval, MOEA automatically forwards the incorporation record to the relevant National Taxation Bureau, which assigns the statutory 8-digit Uniform Invoice Number (TIN/統一編號). The company then files tax registration (profit-seeking enterprise income tax registration and Business Tax registration) via the e-Tax Portal or at the local National Taxation Bureau service counter. A foreign entity without a fixed place of business in Taiwan, but acting as an overseas supplier or receiving remittances, must apply for a separate Foreign Enterprise Tax ID using the Non-Resident Enterprise withholding procedure. All applications require the company's MOEA registration certificate, articles of incorporation, foreign investment approval from MOEA Investment Commission (where applicable), legal representative identification, and the company chop/seal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elective classification regime. The JV is a legal person and profit-seeking enterprise taxed at 20% corporate income tax on worldwide income. No consolidated/group filing election is available for an unconsolidated JV. The only administratively relevant classification election is the small-scale business VAT election under the Business Tax Act if average monthly sales are below the threshold, but a licensed executive-search agency issuing retainers is generally required to use uniform invoices and is treated as a general VAT taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.05%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

New Taiwan Dollar (TWD) 200,000 average monthly taxable sales. Businesses with regular monthly sales above TWD 200,000 must register as general VAT taxpayers, issue Uniform Invoices, file monthly Business Tax returns, and charge 5% VAT. Businesses below the threshold may be classified as small-scale enterprises and taxed at 1% quarterly without input VAT credit; however, a cross-border executive-search company structured as a general profit-seeking enterprise is expected to obtain full VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Articles 7 and 8 of the Business Tax Act, zero-rating applies to the export of goods and to services supplied to foreign enterprises or foreign individuals where the services are used or consumed outside Taiwan. For executive-search/headhunting services, zero-rating is claimable if (1) the contractual buyer is a non-resident foreign enterprise with no fixed place of business in Taiwan; (2) the search mandate, candidate placement, and value created occur outside Taiwan; (3) the executive-selection service is not rendered into the Taiwan domestic market; 4) the consideration is paid in foreign currency and remitted into Taiwan. If the JV provides headhunting services to a Taiwan domestic client, or to hunt for Taiwanese executives for a Taiwan operating entity, the service is considered used in Taiwan and is subject to 5% VAT, not zero rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (for physical export goods; for cross-border services, the service export listing and zero-rated sales schedule in the monthly Business Tax return serve as the service export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (for physical export shipments; in executive-search services, the retained-search engagement contract, placement report, and evidence of candidate hiring/delivery to the foreign client substitute for the Bill of Lading)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign-currency receipt proof from an authorized foreign-exchange bank in Taiwan evidencing collection of the fee from the foreign client)
  • Foreign registration certificate or foreign identity document of the overseas client (to prove non-resident status and absence of a Taiwan fixed place of business)
  • Executed professional service agreement and search/placement performance records (supporting the offshore service delivery and zero-rating nexus)

Taiwan mandates the Uniform Invoice (統一發票) system for all general VAT taxpayers. Electronic Uniform Invoices must be issued through a tax-approved e-invoice system and transmitted to the MOF E-Invoice Platform using the Turnkey API/TLS XML connection, normally within 48 hours after issuance. There is no real-time 'fiscal clearance' stamping by the tax authority as understood in Latin American regimes. Instead, the platform assigns a QR code and 8-digit random number for B2C invoices, while B2B e-invoices require eligibility confirmation by the buyer. All e-invoice records must be archived in the platform before the monthly Business Tax return is filed.

National Taxation Bureaus electronically cross-check export zero-rating claims against customs declarations, e-invoice platform data, bank remittance records, foreign-client tax status, and corporate income tax returns. A fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claim triggers reassessment of the 5% VAT shortfall, together with late interest and administrative penalties up to five times the evaded tax under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act. The tax authority can reopen and claw back falsely zero-rated transactions for the statutory seven-year examination period. Where forged remittance certificates or false e-invoice export records are used, criminal tax-evasion liability under the Tax Collection Act applies, with imprisonment of up to five years and possible suspension of e-invoice and export-privilege status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan dollar is generally convertible for bona fide current-account transactions, including payment for services, salaries, professional fees, imports, and approved business expenses. Taiwan does not impose a general universal capital-account prohibition on corporations, but capital remittances, foreign investment, dividend distributions, intercompany loans, debt repayment, equity reductions, and liquidation proceeds require documentary support and may require MOEAIC, CBC, tax, corporate-law, or banking procedures. Dividend and profit remittances normally require approved financial statements, shareholder or board resolutions where applicable, evidence of tax filing or tax payment, and proof of distributable profits. A foreign investor must maintain consistency between the approved investment amount, registered capital, ownership records, and the remitted amount. If the investor is a Mainland China person or entity, additional Cross-Strait approval, prohibited-sector, ownership, beneficial-owner, and remittance restrictions must be reviewed before funding or distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan has no general statutory rule requiring every exporter or service provider to convert a fixed percentage of foreign-currency receipts into New Taiwan dollars within a universal fixed period. A company may generally retain foreign-currency proceeds in an appropriately documented foreign-currency account, subject to bank reporting, transaction-purpose verification, accounting, tax, and anti-money-laundering requirements. Conversion or settlement is processed through authorized foreign-exchange banks using commercial evidence such as service agreements, invoices, contracts, payment records, and foreign-exchange declarations where required. Executive-search and recruitment fees are service receipts rather than physical-goods export proceeds; the company should maintain contracts, invoices, client identity, service-delivery evidence, withholding-tax analysis, and bank remittance records. Thresholds, reporting forms, and documentary requirements can vary by transaction type and may be updated by the CBC; the bank's current compliance instructions must be confirmed before execution.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), acting under the Foreign Exchange Control Act; the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) regulates banking, payment services, and financial institutions; the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEAIC) administers foreign investment approvals and registrations. Cross-Strait investment and remittance restrictions may additionally apply under the Act Governing Relations between the People of the Taiwan Area and the Mainland Area.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium for supplementary collections and payments; Low as a substitute for the Taiwan operating bank. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment platform cannot automatically open a Taiwan corporate bank account, hold regulated client money in Taiwan, perform unrestricted foreign-exchange conversion, or replace an authorized foreign-exchange bank. Wise Business or a comparable provider may be usable only where the provider accepts the entity, the relevant currencies, the payment purpose, and the source-country corridor. Taiwan banks may reject or delay transfers from fintech pooled accounts because the remitter, beneficial owner, payment purpose, and underlying payer are not transparent. Use a local bank as the principal treasury account and obtain prior written confirmation of permitted inbound client receipts, outbound payroll, refunds, intercompany transfers, and foreign-currency retention.

Route capital through an authorized Taiwan foreign-exchange bank into the designated capital or foreign-investment account in the exact legal name of the Taiwan entity. Before remittance, align the approved investment amount, investor name, ownership percentage, currency, capital purpose, and beneficiary bank details. The receiving bank should verify the remitter, ultimate beneficial owner, purpose, sanctions status, and relationship to the beneficiary before crediting or converting funds. After receipt, complete any capital verification, company-registration update, tax, accounting, and foreign-investment reporting steps required by Taiwan law. For Mainland China-origin funds, obtain applicable Cross-Strait authorization before remittance and use only a permitted financial institution and route.For ordinary overseas service, software, recruitment, advertising, professional, and vendor payments, use the Taiwan company's operating account and an authorized foreign-exchange bank. The bank may transmit a SWIFT MT103 or its ISO 20022 successor equivalent after reviewing the invoice, contract, beneficiary details, tax treatment, sanctions screening, and payment purpose. For substantial or higher-risk engagements, use a documentary or standby commercial letter of credit only where commercially justified; the issuing bank, advising bank, nominated bank, documents, Incoterms where relevant, and discrepancy rules must be specified.

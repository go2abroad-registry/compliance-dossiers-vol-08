Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a PRC WFOE engaged in online retail of consumer electronics, a pure virtual office or commercial mailbox-only address is generally rejected by SAMR, the municipal tax bureau, and the People's Bank of China for industry-standard banking account opening and invoicing. Local registration authorities generally require a fixed physical office, real lease agreement, and ability to receive regulatory premises inspection. A virtual office that lacks substantive presence should never be used as the sole registered office because PBOC and SAMR view shell arrangement as unacceptable. The WFOE is advised to lease at least 15-30 square meters of genuine shared or dedicated office space, demonstrate core management activities on site or through verifiable staffing arrangements, and keep all documentary proof, including lease, evidencing the actual domestic operational footprint.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is prescribed under the Taiwan Companies Act for a limited liability company. However, under the Foreign Investment Act, the foreign investor must remit the capital amount expressly approved by the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs into a Taiwan foreign-exchange bank account. The bank issues a foreign direct investment remittance certificate, and a certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report before the incorporation filing is accepted. The funds are not held in a blocked escrow account, but they may not be withdrawn or returned to the investor prior to issuance of the Company Registration Certificate unless a lawful capital reduction has been separately approved.

E-government filing portal

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is not fully convertible on the capital account; current account receipts and payments are generally liberalized after genuineness and consistency checks, whereas capital account flows, including inbound foreign direct investment, external debt, and equity injections, require SAFE registration and bank-level verification. A Taiwanese shareholder funding a PRC WFOE must first establish an FDI foreign exchange capital account in a designated domestic bank and report the remittance under SAFE's direct investment system. Converted RMB from capital settlement may be disbursed only for bona fide operating purposes, such as salaries, rent, inventory purchases, and overheads, and cannot be used for securities investment, entrusted loans, or real estate outside the approved business scope. Export proceeds from online retail of personal electronics under ISIC 4791 must be repatriated into a domestic current account and either retained as foreign exchange or voluntarily settled into RMB; SAFE's trade monitoring system automatically tracks collection and matching against customs declarations. Dividend remittance to the Taiwanese parent is technically processed through the current account, but banks require audited financial statements, board resolutions, proof of after-tax earnings, and tax clearance certificates. Profit repatriation is therefore feasible but subject to documentary scrutiny, and capital account convertibility can be tightened by macroprudential measures at any time.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds derived from consumer electronics e-commerce must be repatriated into the WFOE's Chinese bank account within the statutory collection period for trade receivables, generally not later than 90 days from the export date under open account terms, or earlier if the sales contract or payment terms specify a shorter deadline. There is currently no fixed mandatory foreign exchange surrender percentage for current account export proceeds; the WFOE may either retain the foreign currency in a settlement account or convert it into RMB at the prevailing interbank rate through the designated domestic bank. SAFE's post-verification system will flag overdue non-repatriation on the 91st day, and the bank will require a written explanation, customs declaration records, logistics evidence, and the underlying commercial contracts to clear the alert.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and its local branches, as the competent foreign exchange administration authority for a PRC-incorporated Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and neo-banks are legally barred from acting as the primary treasury or deposit-taking institution for a PRC WFOE. The PRC Commercial Banking Law and SAFE regulations require all foreign exchange capital accounts, RMB basic deposit accounts, and trade settlement flows to be maintained at a licensed domestic bank. EMIs such as Stripe, PayPal, Airwallex, or Wise may be used only as external payment gateways for online checkout, but all settlement funds must be swept into the onshore designated bank account under the statutory foreign exchange declaration framework. Using an offshore EMI to bypass SAFE registration, capital account segregation, or trade monitoring would constitute illegal cross-border financial activity and expose the WFOE to penalties. The feasibility rating for offshore EMI treasury usage is therefore very low, while the viability of using EMIs as ancillary online payment collection rails is conditional on prior registration with the domestic bank and periodic transaction reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity funding from the Taiwanese shareholder must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 into the WFOE's designated FDI foreign exchange capital account at a domestic Tier-1 bank. The bank will apply the SAFE-approved foreign direct investment capital receipt purpose code for equity contribution and require the presentation of the WFOE's SAFE Business Registration Certificate before booking the transaction. The remitter must match the shareholder name recorded in the FDI filing; third-party remittances are not accepted. Upon receipt, the bank registers the inflow in the Capital Account Information System, and the WFOE may then settle the foreign currency into RMB at the prevailing exchange rate for permitted operating purposes only. Any converted RMB must be disbursed through the bank's supervision system and documented with invoices or contracts. A Chinese CPA-issued capital verification report will be required by the market regulator or the bank if the registered capital is increased or the shareholding structure is amended.Routine supplier payments for inventory, logistics, and marketing services are cleared through onshore-originated SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers, supported by invoices, purchase orders, and customs declarations. For higher-value or contract-governed electronics procurement, the WFOE may use confirmed irrevocable commercial letters of credit or documentary collections through the domestic bank's trade finance desk; the beneficiary must present a compliant set of documents including a commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, packing list, and inspection certificate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs and neo-banks are legally barred from acting as the primary treasury or deposit-taking institution for a PRC WFOE. The PRC Commercial Banking Law and SAFE regulations require all foreign exchange capital accounts, RMB basic deposit accounts, and trade settlement flows to be maintained at a licensed domestic bank. EMIs such as Stripe, PayPal, Airwallex, or Wise may be used only as external payment gateways for online checkout, but all settlement funds must be swept into the onshore designated bank account under the statutory foreign exchange declaration framework. Using an offshore EMI to bypass SAFE registration, capital account segregation, or trade monitoring would constitute illegal cross-border financial activity and expose the WFOE to penalties. The feasibility rating for offshore EMI treasury usage is therefore very low, while the viability of using EMIs as ancillary online payment collection rails is conditional on prior registration with the domestic bank and periodic transaction reporting.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: In Taiwan, the mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) is required when the total related-party transaction amount exceeds NT$100 million (approximately USD 3.3 million) for a single year or NT$50 million for a related-party intangible transaction. A Country-by-Country Report is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding NT$27 billion.

Economic substance scope: The Taiwan tax authority applies the substance-over-form doctrine under Article 12-1 of the Tax Collection Act and the Income Tax Act; transactions lacking economic substance and business purpose may be recharacterized. For entities claiming treaty benefits, the Payor and Beneficial Owner Test requires the overseas parent to demonstrate substantive business operations in its jurisdiction, including employees, premises, and decision-making over the Taiwanese WFOE. Taiwan's free trade zone enterprises claiming preferential tax status must prove core income-generating activities (warehousing, logistics, sales contract negotiation, inventory management) are performed within the zone. For intercompany e-commerce retail operations, a mere invoice booking entity without control over risks and inventory will be deemed a shepherd/conduit, and profits will be reallocated to the principal. Advanced Pricing Agreement (APA) and mutual agreement procedure (MAP) under the Cross-Strait Agreement are available for prospective certainty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Underreporting Cross-Border E-Commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Tax Collection Act, a tax evasion penalty of up to 20% of the tax amount under-reported for negligent errors, and a penalty of up to 2 to 3 times the evaded tax for intentional concealment. Additionally, under Article 71 of the Income Tax Act, failure to file a return may result in a surcharge of up to 20% of the assessed tax plus a separate fine under the Tax Collection Act. Interest on unpaid tax accrues at prescribed rates.

Director personal liability: Civil liability: Directors and the responsible person are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax, penalties, and interest if the evasion results from their intentional or negligent instructions. The National Taxation Bureau can apply for a court order to freeze personal bank accounts and place a lien on personal property. Criminal liability: Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act imposes imprisonment for up to five years, detention, and/or a fine of up to NT$6 million for willful evasion. Directors may also be subject to criminal liability for forgery of corporate and tax documents, and a travel ban may be issued preventing them from leaving Taiwan until the tax debt is settled.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance in Taiwanese Entity

Statutory sanction: Article 12-1 of the Tax Collection Act permits the tax authority to recharacterize transactions lacking economic substance and impose tax on the actual economic owner. The Taiwan court has upheld the assessment of income taxes on the parent as having a permanent establishment in Taiwan; the WFOE's status is ignored for tax purposes, and the parent's Taiwan-source income is taxed at 20% corporate rate plus a 21% dividend WHT if remitted. Civil fines up to NT$3 million for failure to maintain accounting records or for fictitious bookkeeping under the Business Entity Accounting Act.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the resulting additional tax and penalties if they signed misleading financial statements. The absence of economic substance in a foreign parent entity (e.g., China parent) may also trigger the piercing of the limited liability veil under Taiwan Company Law Article 154, where a director who misleads creditors or evades tax may be required to compensate the company and creditors. Criminal provisions: Knowingly creating false business records or using a falsely invoiced shell to claim input VAT and expenses constitutes a criminal Tax Collection Act violation, punishable by imprisonment for up to seven years and a fine of up to NT$10 million. The court may issue a Marital Property Execution order against the director's personal property.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering and False Withholding / Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under Article 43-2 of the Income Tax Act, the tax authority may disregard the transaction, impute a reasonable profit, and impose a penalty of up to 30% of the underpaid tax. The failure to withhold and remit WHT under Article 114(1) of the Tax Collection Act attracts a penalty equal to 50% of the under-withheld amount and a separate fine. Under Taiwan's Money Laundering Control Act, transactions involving criminal proceeds or intentionally submitted false financial reports for AML purposes may lead to a fine up to NT$5 million and imprisonment up to seven years. The tax authority may also issue an additional 5% retention surtax on undistributed earnings if the disguised fee is not reclassified as dividend.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate sham intercompany agreements to evade WHT are personally liable for withheld amounts under Article 89 of the Tax Collection Act. Civil: The tax authority may pierce the corporate veil pursuant to the theory of abuse of legal personality under the Civil Code, forcing directors to surrender personal assets. Blacklisting: The Implementation Rules of the Tax Collection Act permit the National Taxation Bureau to seek a court order imposing a travel ban on the responsible director for unpaid tax exceeding a certain threshold. Criminal: Intentional tax evasion and falsification of tax returns is a crime under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act, punishable by up to five years' imprisonment; if the scheme involves cross-border money laundering, the Money Laundering Control Act imposes an additional sentence of up to seven years, and directors may be subject to seizure of overseas real estate and bank accounts through mutual legal assistance.

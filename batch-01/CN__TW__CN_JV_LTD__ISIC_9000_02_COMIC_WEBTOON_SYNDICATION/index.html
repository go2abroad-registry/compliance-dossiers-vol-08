Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act, exported goods departing Taiwan customs territory and services provided for use outside Taiwan or in connection with exported goods qualify for zero-rated VAT. Digital webtoon subscriptions and multilingual chapter unlocking services sold to overseas readers qualify as service exports if the services are not consumed within Taiwan and the supplier retains evidence of foreign consumption, payment from abroad, and outward service jurisdiction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Form) issued by Customs Administration
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill or Combined Transport Document
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Foreign exchange earnings certificate or Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)
  • Digital service export contract and subscription plan documentation evidencing overseas consumers
  • Payment gateway settlement statements from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing identifying non-Taiwan user jurisdictions

Taiwan mandates the issuance of Uniform Invoices. E-invoicing must use the Ministry of Finance integrated e-invoice platform, and eligible businesses integrate through the E-Invoice Turnkey API to upload invoice data. There is no real-time tax authority pre-clearance stamping for standard B2C cloud invoices, but cloud invoices carry anti-fraud QR codes and randomized check numbers, and transaction data is subject to fiscal retention and automated post-issue verification.

If the exporter fails to retain or produce the required customs, logistics, invoice, and FX repatriation documentation, the tax authority may deny zero-rating and retroactively assess 5% VAT on the exported sales for up to seven years, with interest from the original due date. Penalties of up to five times the underpaid tax may be imposed for confirmed fraudulent zero-rating claims, and false foreign exchange declaration violations may trigger separate administrative fines under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan runs a managed-floating, market-clearing foreign exchange system. The New Taiwan dollar is freely convertible for current-account trade and service payments and for approved capital-account movements. A Taiwan-resident entity may receive, hold, and sell foreign currency without surrender to the state; conversion is carried out with designated foreign-exchange banks at quoted market rates. Remittances above the Central Bank's reporting threshold require a Foreign Exchange Transaction Report and, for direct-investment capital, a prior approval from the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) Investment Commission. Profit distributions and royalty remittances must be supported by audited financial statements and withholding-tax certificates; the Taiwan-side convertibility hurdle is moderate, not license-based. The principal non-convertibility exposure in this CN/TW structure is on the mainland side: the Chinese JV shareholder must complete NDRC, MOFCOM and SAFE ODI registration before funding the Taiwan entity. Without that statutory evidence, the Taiwan bank will decline the inbound wire as a suspected capital-smuggling transaction. There are no domestic limitations on holding USD or repatriating declared dividends once tax clearance and beneficial-ownership certification are produced.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan imposes no mandatory surrender of export foreign-exchange earnings and no required conversion ratio to New Taiwan dollars. Export receipts can be retained in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account, offset against approved import payments, or sold to a designated bank at market rate. The bank that receives the settlement must verify the customs export declaration, the purchase order and the invoice; for a single settlement exceeding US$100,000, the Central Bank's Reporting System requires a Foreign Exchange Settlement/Declaration form completed by the company. For mandatory reporting, banks must file the transaction with the Central Bank on the standard schedule, normally by the fifth business day after the month of settlement. No statutory timeline requires the exporter to convert within a set number of days; the only filing deadline is the bank's monthly reporting cutoff.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), Foreign Exchange Department

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: 1.5/10 - Not viable as a primary corporate treasury rail. Taiwan's Banking Act, Foreign Exchange Control Act and FSC licensing framework require any deposit-taking or foreign-exchange service to be provided by a licensed bank or a regulated offshore banking unit. Offshore digital EMIs are legally barred from opening a domestic Taiwan corporate account, issuing a local account number, or conducting B2B clearing on behalf of the JV. The only acceptable function is as a supplemental collection or billing conduit for consumer platforms such as Apple IAP or Google Play, with funds automatically swept to a licensed Tier-1 bank account.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on the gross amount of any dividend paid by a Taiwan resident enterprise to a non-resident shareholder (per Article 88 of the Income Tax Act, rate effective since 2018). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$200,000,000 (approximately US$6.5 million) in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions in a fiscal year, or NT$100,000,000 (approximately US$3.3 million) in domestic related-party transactions, triggers mandatory preparation of a Transfer Pricing Local File and Master File (per the Assessment Rules on Profit-seeking Enterprise Income Tax and Transfer Pricing). A Taiwan subsidiary is also required to file a country-by-country report (CbCR) if its foreign parent group has group revenue exceeding NT$27 billion and the Taiwan entity alone books NT$50 million or more of related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute resembling OECD/Caribbean ESR. However, the Taiwan tax authority applies the double-rollover anti-avoidance rule under Article 43-4 of the Income Tax Act, the Controlled Foreign Corporation (CFC) rule (effective January 2023), and the 'place of actual management' (PAM) doctrine to recharacterize a Taiwan-registered entity as a non-resident if substantive decisions and CIGA (e.g., product development, contract negotiation, board meetings and R&D staff leadership) take place outside Taiwan, especially in mainland China. For ISIC 9000 (digital webtoon publishing), the CIGA are: (1) content acquisition, negotiation and licensing agreements; (2) digital platform development, server management, and customer support in Taiwan; and (3) strategic direction of app-store sales distributions. If the JV is a mere mailbox company receiving Apple/Google royalty flows and passing them to the PRC parent, the Taiwan tax authority will either (a) treat the JV as a non-resident enterprise and assess a 20% WHT on all Taiwan-source income, or (b) invoke the CFC rules to attribute the retained profits to the PRC parent for PRC tax purposes, while denying Taiwan-resident tax filing benefits. Therefore, the JV must maintain physical office space, at least one full-time Taiwan-resident senior manager, local board-meeting minutes, and a payroll schedule showing Taiwanese employees performing the core CIGA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return Filing

Statutory sanction: Article 110 of the Tax Collection Act: a fine of up to five times the amount of tax evaded; Article 41 of the Tax Evasion Act: criminal fine up to NT$5,000,000 and imprisonment for a term of not more than five years where tax is concealed through unlawful means. In addition, under Article 35 of the Business Tax Act, fraudulent VAT/income tax collection can lead to a fine of 100% to 300% of the evaded amount. The JV's tax return may be reassessed within a 7-year limitation period for factual errors and within 10 years for cases not filed at all (or fraudulent).

Director personal liability: Non-resident (PRC/Taiwan) directors who sign misleading financial statements may be jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax corpus, interest, and penalties. The court can issue an order to pierce the corporate veil where the director used the JV as a ‘mere instrumentality’ to evade tax, attaching personal assets of the directors. In addition, the National Taxation Bureau can apply for a travel-ban order against the directors under the Tax Collection Enforcement Act, preventing departure from Taiwan until the tax debt is settled. Criminal conviction for tax fraud carries up to five years' imprisonment plus a fine; professional directors may be disqualified from serving as officers of any Taiwanese company under the Company Act for ten years.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance (CIGA Misdeclaration)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 10 of the Income Tax Act and the Board of Taxation's interpretative ruling on the ‘central place of management and control,’ the Taiwan tax authority may treat the JV as a non-resident enterprise and tax all Taiwan-source digital-content income at the 20% WHT rate (gross) without deductions. The false statement of residence and business activity triggers the anti-abuse provision of Article 43-4 (investment income attribution) and Article 43-1 (unreasonable related-party allocation) allowing the authority to disallow the cost and expense allocation and recharacterize the licensing fee as a constructive dividend. The Company Act imposes an administrative fine of NT$1,000,000 to NT$5,000,000 for filing false registration documents, and the Business Registration law may order the dissolution of the shell-company.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly declare a shell company as a Taiwan resident operator are personally subject to piercing of the corporate veil, rendering their personal assets (including PRC-held properties) liable for corporate tax debt. The tax authority may further blacklist (list as a major conscientious delinquent) the director and the JV, causing a ban on luxury transactions and a freeze on all Taiwan bank accounts. Under the Money Laundering Control Act, if the shell company is used to pass funds through Apple/Google IAP for the benefit of undisclosed shareholders, the directors face criminal liability of up to two years' imprisonment, a fine of up to NT$5,000,000, and the forfeiture of the illicit pathway.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Withholding Tax Violations on Outbound Royalties/Interest

Statutory sanction: Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act allows the tax authority to adjust the transaction back to arm's length; the adjustment triggers a shortfall tax rate of 20% plus a TP adjustment penalty of 100% of the understated tax under Article 110(1) of the Tax Collection Act. Failure to maintain TP documentation pursuant to the Assessment Rules is punishable by an administrative fine of up to NT$500,000. For the withholding violation, Articles 114-1 and 114-2 of the Income Tax Act impose a penalty of twice the amount that should have been withheld, but in any event not less than NT$10,000. If the non-resident payee has no TRC and the JV relies on the parent's claim of ‘license-free’, the entire payment is reclassified as a taxable distribution subject to the 21% WHT and the same penalty.

Director personal liability: As the withholding agent, the JV's responsible director is personally liable for the full amount of the under-withheld 20% or 10% WHT and penalties if the JV's assets are insufficient. The tax authority can file a criminal complaint under Article 35 of the Tax Collection Act for failure to account for and pay tax withheld from another person, which is a felony carrying a criminal fine from NT$100,000 to NT$5,000,000 and imprisonment for up to five years. A director who issues a false beneficial-ownership declaration or who signs the intercompany contract knowing the royalty rate to be inflated (e.g., a 60% net margin to the PRC parent) will be jointly and severally liable for the entire TP adjustment amount. The director is also subject to a travel ban and admission to the tax delinquent blacklist until the debt is resolved.

Taiwan's Company Act requires a registered office within Taiwan, but does not absolutely outlaw a virtual office. However, the tax authority and Taiwan banks generally reject a mere commercial mailbox for an ISIC 7020 consultancy because they require demonstrable physical business presence, local substantive employees, and operational capacity. A virtual office is permissible only if it includes a dedicated registered agent, a physical meeting room, a workable mailbox, and a lease of at least one year with evidence of utility usage; even then, the financial institution may impose enhanced due diligence and request documentary proof of actual business operations during the initial account opening phase.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested profit-seeking enterprise such as CN_JV_LTD: (1) obtain foreign-investment approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs; (2) complete the profit-seeking enterprise registration and company registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs and the local municipal/county authority; (3) file the prescribed tax registration application with the National Taxation Bureau where the business office is situated, attaching the approval certificate, certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, proof of business address, and identification and seal of the responsible person (and, if required, the local tax representative); (4) receive the Unified Business Number, the statutory tax identification number for income tax, business tax, uniform-invoice procurement, and customs declaration linkage; (5) elect electronic submission and e-invoice transmission on the Integrated Tax Portal after issuance of the Unified Business Number. For a foreign non-resident entity without a fixed place of business but making taxable Taiwanese-source sales or cross-border e-commerce sales, the foreign entity must register with the National Taxation Bureau itself or through an appointed local tax representative, using its foreign incorporation evidence, power of attorney, and a statement identifying the tax agent; the registration becomes effective when taxable Taiwanese-source revenue starts. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for resident profit-seeking enterprises; every taxable business must complete business-tax registration before commencing operations. Small-scale businesses with monthly taxable sales at or below NT$200,000 remain registered but pay the reduced 1% non-value-added business tax. A foreign non-resident supplier of cross-border electronic services selling more than NT$480,000 per year to Taiwan individuals must register for business tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, 0% VAT treatment applies to: (1) goods physically exported from Taiwan with customs clearance; (2) services sold by a Taiwan business to a wholly foreign client where the services are used or enjoyed outside Taiwan; (3) international transport and cross-border ancillary services; and (4) qualifying offshore digital or consultancy services. For an ISIC 7020 overseas market-entry consultancy, the service contract, feasibility and regulatory-licensing reports, the client's overseas business address, and foreign-currency invoices must evidence that the benefit of the consulting service lies outside Taiwan. The exporter then shows no output business tax on the transaction and claims credits for input business tax on related purchases; any residual input-credit refund is applied through the monthly/quarterly Business Tax return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) approved by Taiwan Customs Administration
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or international airway bill showing goods left Taiwan customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Certificate of Foreign Exchange Settlement issued by an authorized foreign-exchange bank
  • For services: signed engagement contract, market-entry feasibility report, and payment records establishing the foreign client's offshore use
  • Foreign-exchange repatriation statement showing export/service proceeds were collected by the Taiwan entity

Taiwan mandates the issuance of Uniform Invoices for taxable sales. Electronic Uniform Invoices (cloud invoices) are issued through a Ministry-of-Finance-certified turnkey system or software integrator, using the statutory XML/JSON schema, digital certificates, and TLS transmission; invoice data is transmitted to the Ministry of Finance Cloud Invoice Database for validation, duplicate-issue detection, and anti-fraud monitoring. Printed electronic invoices must bear the 2D/QR barcode and random anti-counterfeit number. This is not a real-time government pre-clearance regime; however, invoice issuance, allowance, voidance, and monthly/quarterly Business Tax return declarations are automatically reconciled, and discrepancies trigger notices and penalties under the Business Tax Act.

The National Taxation Bureau reconciles Business Tax returns against customs export records, foreign-exchange settlement statements, and Cloud Invoice Database data. If the zero-rating claim is not substantiated, the gross consideration is assessed at 5% business tax, overdue interest is added, and administrative fines are imposed under Articles 51 and 52 of the Business Tax Act. Known forgery of customs documents or foreign-exchange certificates, sham offshore structures, or deliberate mischaracterization of domestic consultancy services as foreign use triggers criminal liability under Article 41, retroactive reassessment within the seven-year statutory assessment period, loss of refund entitlement, and seizure of assets/inventory where evasion is established. Export proceeds that are not repatriated within 180 days, absent central-bank approval for an extension, will normally cause the zero-rated export status to be denied.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan dollar is generally convertible for current-account transactions. Taiwan does not operate a general exchange-control regime requiring prior approval for ordinary trade receipts, service payments, dividends, interest, or other legitimate current-account remittances, subject to bank reporting, documentary verification, anti-money-laundering controls, tax compliance, and applicable foreign-exchange declaration thresholds. Capital-account transactions are more regulated. Corporate investments, outward investments, foreign borrowing, guarantees, securities transactions, and transactions involving mainland China may require prior approval, registration, declaration, or compliance with sectoral and cross-Strait restrictions. Profit remittance should be supported by audited or approved financial statements, shareholder or board resolutions, evidence of distributable profits, withholding-tax filings or tax clearance, and proof of the underlying investment. A China-based Sino-foreign equity joint venture must separately comply with Mainland China foreign-exchange, tax, dividend-distribution, and cross-border payment rules. Direct or indirect payments involving Taiwan and Mainland China require enhanced bank review under applicable cross-Strait regulations and may be prohibited where the underlying activity, counterparty, route, or beneficial ownership is restricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan has no general statutory rule requiring exporters to surrender all export proceeds to the Central Bank or convert them into New Taiwan dollars at a fixed ratio. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted through authorized foreign-exchange banks. Banks require customs or commercial documentation, invoices, contracts, shipping records, and transaction-purpose information, and report or process qualifying foreign-exchange transactions under Central Bank rules. No universal export-proceeds conversion deadline or mandatory conversion percentage should be represented without confirming the current bank-specific and transaction-specific rule. For Mainland China operations, export receipts and foreign-exchange settlement are governed separately by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange and related Mainland rules; the applicable entity, export location, customs declaration, account type, and payment purpose determine repatriation, settlement, and documentation requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan), together with the Ministry of Economic Affairs, Investment Commission, and the Banking Bureau of the Financial Supervisory Commission for applicable investment, banking, and cross-Strait controls

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium for supplemental collections and payments; low as the sole treasury bank. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment institution, including Wise Business where the entity and use case are accepted, does not replace a Taiwan-authorized bank account, a regulated capital account, or any required Mainland China foreign-exchange channel. Availability depends on entity incorporation location, directors, beneficial owners, business activity, currencies, payment corridors, sanctions screening, and platform policy. Wise B2B should be treated as a payment service rather than a guaranteed bank-wire substitute. It may not support capital injections, shareholder funding, escrow, restricted cross-Strait settlements, regulated investment flows, cash deposits, documentary trade finance, or all Taiwan-dollar functions. Use a licensed Taiwan bank for registered capital, tax payments, payroll, dividend remittances, foreign-exchange declarations, and high-value or sensitive transactions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% corporate income tax on taxable income; an additional 5% surtax generally applies to undistributed earnings of a profit-seeking enterprise retained without an approved business purpose.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a nonresident individual, foreign company, or mainland Chinese enterprise, subject to applicable special rules and any legally available relief. Dividends paid to a Taiwan-resident corporate shareholder are generally subject to Taiwan's intercorporate dividend rules rather than nonresident withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan local-file documentation is generally required where the taxpayer's annual net operating revenue and non-operating income reaches at least NT$300 million or the annual aggregate amount of controlled transactions reaches at least NT$200 million, subject to detailed exemptions and transaction-category rules. A master file is generally required where the Taiwan entity belongs to a multinational enterprise group meeting the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold, commonly NT$3 billion, together with the prescribed cross-border related-party conditions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies at the group level where consolidated revenue reaches NT$27 billion, subject to filing and notification rules. Exact thresholds and exemptions must be confirmed for the relevant fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan applies substance-over-form, anti-avoidance, transfer-pricing, controlled foreign company, beneficial-ownership, and business-purpose principles. Taiwan does not operate a single offshore Economic Substance Regulations regime identical to the Cayman Islands or British Virgin Islands regimes; however, an entity must demonstrate real personnel, premises, decision-making, functions, assets, risks, records, and commercial purpose. A Taiwan consultancy invoicing the Chinese parent should evidence local staff, management control, client work, deliverables, market activity, and risk assumption. Artificial arrangements may be recharacterized, denied deductions, subjected to withholding, or treated as dividend or other taxable distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional assessed tax, interest, administrative penalties that may reach twice the underpaid tax, invoice penalties, compulsory collection, and criminal prosecution for intentional tax evasion. Criminal penalties can include imprisonment and fines under Taiwan tax and criminal laws.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face criminal liability, fines, civil restitution, tax collection against legally reachable assets, disqualification consequences, and travel or exit restrictions where legally ordered. Corporate limited liability does not protect intentional personal misconduct.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may adjust taxable income to an arm's-length amount, deny deductions, impose additional corporate income tax, assess withholding tax, apply interest and penalties, and require local-file, master-file, or audit documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may incur civil liability for losses caused by knowingly approving non-arm's-length transactions, false records, or unlawful distributions; criminal exposure arises where conduct constitutes tax fraud, forgery, breach of trust, or other offenses.
Illegal profit repatriation and withholding failure

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent liability for the unpaid tax, collection of tax from the Taiwan company, interest, penalties, denial of deductions, and possible additional 5% undistributed-earnings surtax.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may be jointly exposed under tax collection, company, and criminal laws for deliberate withholding failures, unlawful distributions, misuse of corporate assets, or false accounting.
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or domestic tax benefits, recharacterization of income, transfer-pricing adjustments, withholding assessments, invoice penalties, beneficial-ownership challenges, and possible anti-avoidance reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil damages, breach-of-duty claims, personal tax or criminal exposure for fraudulent arrangements, and regulatory restrictions. Travel bans or imprisonment are not automatic and require statutory grounds and competent-authority or judicial process.
AML, sanctions, and suspicious cross-border payments

Statutory sanction: Transaction rejection, account suspension, suspicious-transaction reporting, asset freezing or seizure, administrative fines, and criminal penalties under Taiwan anti-money-laundering and sanctions-related laws.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and employees may face personal fines, criminal prosecution, asset confiscation, civil liability, and restrictions on leaving Taiwan where authorized by law. Corporate form does not shield knowing participation or deliberate failure to report.
Unlawful mainland-Taiwan investment or remittance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, orders to rectify or unwind the transaction, confiscation or forced repatriation, remittance restrictions, license or registration consequences, and criminal liability for serious or intentional violations.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may be personally liable for unlawful approvals, concealment, or unauthorized transfers and may face fines, criminal prosecution, asset measures, and legally ordered travel restrictions.
Unlawful corporate distributions and insolvency misconduct

Statutory sanction: Repayment or restitution of the distribution, director liability for corporate losses, creditor claims, accounting penalties, tax reassessment, and criminal liability for breach of trust or unlawful distribution.

Director personal liability: Directors may face joint civil restitution, creditor damages, disqualification, asset execution, and imprisonment where the conduct satisfies criminal elements. Travel bans are case-specific and require lawful authority.

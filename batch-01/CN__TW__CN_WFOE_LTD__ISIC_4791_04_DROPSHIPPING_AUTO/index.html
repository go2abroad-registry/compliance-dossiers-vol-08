Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taiwan (財政部) and its subordinate regional National Taxation Bureaus (各地區國稅局), including the National Taxation Bureaus of Taipei, Northern, Central, Southern, and Kaohsiung areas, depending on the company's registered office. The principal online portals are the Ministry of Finance website (https://www.mof.gov.tw), the E-Tax Integrated Portal (https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw), and the e-Invoice Integrated Service Platform (https://www.einvoice.nat.gov.tw).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested Taiwan WFOE (limited liability company) first obtains foreign investment approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs under the Foreign Investment Act and incorporates with the Ministry of Economic Affairs through the Company Registration One-Stop Service. The incorporation process yields the company registration certificate and a statutory eight-digit Unified Business Number (統一編號), which functions as the corporate tax identification number, the business tax (VAT) registration number, and the e-invoice identifier. The WFOE must then complete profit-seeking enterprise income tax registration and business tax registration with the competent local National Taxation Bureau before commencing business, filing the company registration certificate, articles of incorporation, the responsible person's identification, corporate seal, and registered office address. The National Taxation Bureau will issue a tax registration certificate and approve the purchase and use of Uniform Invoices (統一發票). Subsequently, the WFOE obtains a company digital certificate (工商憑證) and enrolls in the e-Invoice Turnkey transmission service for electronic filing and electronic invoice issuance. No minimum paid-in capital is required under the current Company Act, and the entire registration procedure is typically completed within 15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not operate a check-the-box entity classification election; an incorporated WFOE limited company is mandatorily taxed at the entity level as a profit-seeking enterprise with worldwide income taxation under the Income Tax Act. Available statutory elections relevant to the cross-border automated dropshipping structure include: (i) election of a non-calendar fiscal year subject to approval by the National Taxation Bureau; (ii) election under the Foreign Investment Act route which governs foreign-investor registration and may affect tax-treaty benefits; (iii) election to file joint consolidated income tax returns for relation-enterprise groups under the Income Tax Act if strict affiliated-group criteria are met; (iv) election to register voluntarily as a full value-added Business Tax taxpayer for a small-scale business otherwise taxed on deemed sales at the special 1% rate; and (v) election to transmit e-invoices directly through the Turnkey channel or indirectly through an accredited e-invoice value-added center. Hybrid mismatch and CFC/PEM rules are not elective; they apply by statute and require self-assessment and disclosure where controlled foreign corporations or place-of-effective-management re-characterization is triggered.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Full VAT registration with Uniform Invoice (統一發票) obligations is mandatory when a Taiwan-resident profit-seeking enterprise has average monthly taxable sales of NT$200,000 or more (approximately NT$2.4 million annualized). Businesses below this threshold are generally treated as small-scale enterprises and assessed on tax-authority-determined deemed sales at a special 1% business tax rate, with no entitlement to input VAT credits. Separately, a foreign electronic-service supplier without a Taiwan fixed place of business becomes registrable and must collect 5% Business Tax on online services supplied to Taiwan individuals once annual taxable sales exceed NT$480,000. All Taiwan WFOEs, including those in the automated dropshipping industry, must complete business tax registration before commencing business regardless of turnover volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Value-Added and Non-Value-Added Business Tax Act, the 0% Business Tax rate applies to: (i) the export of goods from the Taiwan customs territory; (ii) services provided in Taiwan and used outside Taiwan, or services rendered and used wholly outside Taiwan; and (iii) certain sales of goods within duty-free areas and between foreign buyers in triangular export arrangements. For the automated dropshipping business model, zero-rating eligibility requires the Taiwan WFOE to evidence either: (a) for goods physically exported from Taiwan to the foreign end-customer, that the WFOE appears as the exporter/consignor on the Customs Export Declaration and holds transport documentation showing delivery abroad; or (b) for goods shipped directly from a third-country supplier (for example, mainland China) to the foreign end-customer without entering Taiwan, that the transaction is a triangular export under which the WFOE zero-rates only the cross-border merchandising service or commission income, supported by the supplier's export declarations, cross-border transport evidence, and the WFOE's receipt of foreign currency proceeds. Excess input VAT credits attributable to such zero-rated exports may be refunded on a bi-monthly filing basis, subject to tax-authority verification against customs EDI records, e-invoice data, foreign-exchange settlement data, and retained documentary evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口報單) naming the Taiwan WFOE as exporter/seller and evidencing departure from Taiwan customs territory
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List expressly indicating 0% Business Tax treatment
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier shipment receipt (for example, DHL, UPS, FedEx, EMS) evidencing physical delivery to the foreign consignee
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate (外匯收入水單) or bank settlement certificate showing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Sales Contract or Purchase Order from the foreign buyer establishing the commercial terms of the export sale
  • For triangular/offshore dropshipment: the third-country supplier's export declaration, cross-border transport records, and shipment tracking evidencing direct delivery from the third-country warehouse to the foreign end-customer without entry into Taiwan

Taiwan mandates Uniform Invoices (統一發票) for all standard VAT taxpayers. Electronic Uniform Invoices (電子發票) must be transmitted to the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice Integrated Service Platform (EISP) through the direct Turnkey channel or an accredited e-invoice value-added center, using XML file formats and a company digital certificate (工商憑證). Under the current administrative framework, B2B e-invoice data is generally required to be uploaded to the EISP within 48 hours of issuance, while B2C e-invoice data may be uploaded within the bi-monthly filing period. The EISP performs automated validation, rejects non-conforming records, and records platform receipt codes. There is no pre-issuance fiscal-stamping or real-time clearance requirement equivalent to the Italian SDI or similar Latin American fiscal control regimes, but retail sales to consumers must carry a QR code and participate in the Uniform Invoice lottery system. Marketplace and dropshipping platforms operating in Taiwan may be recognized as e-invoice transmitters for their sellers and are then required to provide API integration for real-time or periodic invoice data upload to the EISP.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to intensive audit verification against Taiwan Customs EDI export declarations, e-Invoice Integrated Service Platform records, transport waybills, and the Central Bank foreign-exchange settlement database. Where a zero-rating claim is unsupported, overstated, or fraudulent, the National Taxation Bureau will retrospectively reverse the 0% rate, assess 5% Business Tax on the full export consideration, and impose a delinquency surcharge of up to 15% plus statutory interest. Under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act, the tax authority may levy an evasion penalty of up to three times the underpaid tax. The general tax assessment period is five years for filed returns and seven years for unfiled returns, permitting significant retroactive clawback. Fraudulent export zero-rating involving forged customs documents or fabricated foreign-exchange remittance certificates is additionally subject to criminal tax evasion liability under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act, carrying imprisonment of up to five years, and the responsible person, including the WFOE director, may be held personally liable for civil penalties and surcharges. In egregious cases, the tax authority may suspend the taxpayer's Uniform Invoice purchase entitlement and e-invoice transmission privileges while the investigation is pending.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (CNY) is not fully freely convertible under the capital account. Current-account transactions, including cross-border e-commerce sales of goods and associated logistics and payment service fees, operate under a verified-positivity framework that permits conversion via designated foreign-exchange (FX) banks subject to SAFE record-filing and documentary verification. Profit remittance by a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is treated as a capital-account current-item outbound transfer: it requires prior audited financial statements, corporate income tax (CIT) settlement and withholding certificates, and verification that the enterprise has completed statutory foreign-exchange registration (FDI registration), annual multi-department reporting, and SAFE capital-project information reporting. WFOEs are prohibited from direct conversion of registered capital into RMB for purposes other than bona fide business use under the 2014 Circular on Further Improving the Administration of Foreign Exchange in Capital Projects and related SAFE operational rules. Capital-account items, including foreign debt and equity transfers, require separate SAFE approvals or registrations. For an automated dropshipping WFOE with no physical goods inventory in China, the key remittance hurdle is evidencing that underlying sales transactions represent genuine current-account trade in goods or services, supported by customs declarations where applicable, online platform sales reports, logistics delivery records, payment provider settlement statements, and complete invoice documentation, in order to satisfy the bank's 'real trade background' verification and SAFE's anti-fraud and anti-money-laundering checks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE framework, China has abolished the mandatory full surrender of export proceeds in foreign currency. Export receipts in FX may be retained by the WFOE in its designated FX settlement account at a domestic bank, or converted into RMB voluntarily. However, all export proceeds for goods must be repatriated to China within the statutory time limit: the general requirement is that export proceeds must be received and settled through the domestic banking system no later than 180 days from the date of export declaration, unless a longer period is approved by SAFE or the relevant goods are eligible for a deferred payment arrangement under SAFE regulations. There is no mandatory conversion ratio in 2025; the enterprise may keep FX proceeds in an interest-bearing current account or convert to RMB through a domestic bank at the market-based central parity rate with a daily spread determined by the PBOC. Nevertheless, the WFOE must complete the electronic 'trade in goods' declaration (货物贸易外汇收支名录登记) under SAFE's goods-trade foreign-exchange administration, and each receipt from an overseas buyer or an online payment intermediary such as Stripe or PayPal remitting the proceeds must be matched against the corresponding customs declaration or postal/express shipment record. Proceeds from cross-border e-commerce retail sales may be settled under the simplified 'cross-border e-commerce retail export' procedures where the total value per shipment is below a stated threshold (typically USD 5,000 or equivalent), requiring a qualified third-party payment institution or the bank to handle the settlement and maintain transaction-level records. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day window, or failure to report the credit to SAFE, may result in administrative penalties, mandatory correction orders, inclusion in the foreign-exchange violation list, and suspension of the bank's processing of future transactions.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium (legally restricted). Foreign fintech or electronic money institutions (EMIs) incorporated outside mainland China are not permitted to open deposit accounts with Chinese commercial banks in their own right without a licensed Chinese banking or payment institution entity. A non-resident EMI cannot provide a physical or fully digital settlement account in CNY inside mainland China unless it operates through a licensed Chinese third-party payment institution (e.g., Alipay, WeChat Pay, or a licensed cross-border payment processor) under PBOC/SAFE rules. Offshore EMI accounts used to collect payments from customers (e.g., Stripe account with a Hong Kong or Singapore entity) may coexist with the mainland WFOE, but the WFOE's own export proceeds must ultimately be repatriated to its mainland bank account via a lawful cross-border payment channel. Using an offshore EMI solely to collect dropshipping revenue outside China, without a compliant underlying economic substance and tax reporting chain, would create permanent establishment and transfer pricing risks in Taiwan/China and fail the SAFE real-trade-background test. Practical viable structure: (i) an overseas payment intermediary licensed by the country of the sales platform (e.g., Stripe, PayPal) collects the end-customer funds; (ii) the intermediary, or the WFOE's mainland partner/PingPong, WorldFirst, or LianLian pay-type licensed service provider, settles the funds to the WFOE's domestic settlement account under a SAFE-filed cross-border e-commerce receivable collection scheme; (iii) the WFOE maintains quarterly reconciliation between platform payouts and its own accounting records. Foreign EMIs cannot directly clear RMB funds in CIPS/CHIPS or SWIFT for mainland corporations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route for a Taiwan parent to inject capital into a mainland WFOE is as follows: (1) register the FDI project with the local counterpart of the Ministry of Commerce (外商投资信息报告) and obtain the SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration for FDI; (2) open a RMB or FCY capital account (资本金账户) at a designated bank; (3) wire the injection from the Taiwan shareholders' bank account via SWIFT using purpose code 'FDI Equity Injection' (and for the PBOC, the remittance instruction should reference 'Investment in the capital of a resident enterprise' (股权投资), commonly using the e-Code 'TWC' or 'Foreign Direct Investment' as specified by the bank) with a PDF of the FDI registration certificate and the board resolution approving the capital; (4) the receiving bank performs the SAFE 'permitted use' verification; (5) the funds may be credited to the RMB settlement account after conversion under the 'FDI capital project settlement' regime, and the WFOE must use the funds strictly for operating costs, procurement, salaries, rents, and other non-prohibited activities; (6) file the quarterly 'FDI report' with SAFE through the bank, and include the capital injection in the annual multi-department reporting (工商年报). No in-principle approval from SAFE is required because China applies a 'registration-based' system for FDI; however, remittances of more than RMB 5 million (or the bank's internal threshold) will trigger an enhanced review of the source of funds. Wire transfer protocols applicable: use SWIFT MT103, ensure the beneficiary account name exactly matches the WFOE's bank-account name, provide the full legal name and tax identification number of the sender, and include the 8-character FX business code (e.g., 'FDI', '622') determined by the bank; for payment institutions, a special remarks field '跨境电商收结汇' is used when converting sales proceeds, not for capital injections. Verification required: the bank will check the board resolution, the foreign-invested enterprise business license, the SAFE FDI file, and a declaration that the capital is not from a sanctioned jurisdiction. If the Taiwan parent uses an intermediary company (e.g., a Hong Kong vehicle), the FDI route must still show the ultimate investor, and an additional '实际控制人说明' may be required.For outbound disbursements from the WFOE to overseas suppliers (e.g., the dropshipping upstream vendor in the Taiwan region or a third-country manufacturer), the permitted clearing rails are as follows: (i) Direct SWIFT MT103 payment: the WFOE instructs its domestic bank to transfer RMB or FX funds to the supplier's account; the bank requires a verified trade background — an import/export contract or purchase order, a supplier invoice, customs or logistics evidence, and, where the goods are purchased from outside mainland China, an import customs declaration if the goods physically pass through customs; if goods are drop-shipped directly from a third-country factory to an overseas customer and never legally enter mainland China, the payment may be re-characterised as a cross-border service payment or offshore purchase, with the WFOE as the seller of services/merchandise, requiring a service-buying agreement, tax invoice, and possibly a tax withholding by the bank. (ii) Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C): a confirmed, irrevocable L/C issued by the WFOE's bank may be used for higher-value purchases; for dropshipping, it is rarely used due to low unit values and high volumetric frequency, but for bulk replenishment of inventory in an overseas bonded warehouse, an L/C at sight is acceptable. (iii) Banker's acceptance or T/T after goods receipt: the common e-commerce practice is 'T/T against order invoice' within 30 days. All outbound payments are subject to the bank's real-trade-background verification; the bank will check the commodity code, price reasonableness, and recipient jurisdiction. Under SAFE and PBOC rules, the WFOE cannot use its own RMB operating funds to buy FX for an overseas payment unless it has an approved current-account FX purchase quota; in practice the bank processes each application individually. To ensure smooth settlements, the WFOE must maintain a digital repository of supplier agreements, proof-of-delivery, and a transaction list to reconcile the outbound payment with the corresponding sales funds collected from Stripe/PayPal. MT103 fields should include the beneficiary bank's SWIFT BIC, the beneficiary account, and the purpose code 'Trade Payment' as instructed by the bank. The Commercial L/C route requires the WFOE to deposit margin (typically 10%-50%) and the bank will check the WFOE's credit line, history, and KYC profile.Onshore CNY/USD forwards (远期结售汇) with a licensed domestic bank: permitted for hedging genuine current-account FX exposures arising from cross-border e-commerce sales, subject to the bank's assessment of a real trade background and the SAFE rule that the forward must mature within the underlying trade period and be closed out against actual FX cash flows.CNY/USD FX swaps (货币掉期), also known as cross-currency swaps, permissible between a domestic bank and the WFOE for matching cash flows on capital accounts or large trade transactions, subject to bank credit approval and SAFE reporting.Offshore NDF (non-deliverable forward) contracts: theoretically permissible for the Taiwan parent or an offshore treasury centre, but the mainland WFOE cannot directly enter an NDF with an offshore counterparty without having a genuine offshore trade receivable/payable and using an offshore account; many banks caution that onshore WFOEs are better served by participating in the onshore deliverable forward market, because an NDF may be treated as a disguised capital-flow vehicle and violate SAFE Circular 7 of 2017 regarding real-trade-backed FX derivatives.FX options (买入看跌或看涨期权): available from domestic banks for hedging currency risk, but stricter for SMEs with no existing FX positions; the purchase must be real-trade-backed; only plain-vanilla options are permitted, and option strategies that increase leverage may be denied.Cross-border RMB settlement swap agreements (跨境人民币资金池): not a classic hedging instrument but a permissible liquidity tool for a group treasury; if the WFOE is part of a cross-border group, a two-way cross-border RMB cash-pooling arrangement with a domestic permitted bank in the pilot zone may reduce FX conversion needs.Natural hedging through multi-currency accounts: maintaining USD, EUR, or CNY current accounts at the domestic bank and offsetting payables against receivables in the same currency to reduce conversion frequency, permissible without SAFE approval.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium (legally restricted). Foreign fintech or electronic money institutions (EMIs) incorporated outside mainland China are not permitted to open deposit accounts with Chinese commercial banks in their own right without a licensed Chinese banking or payment institution entity. A non-resident EMI cannot provide a physical or fully digital settlement account in CNY inside mainland China unless it operates through a licensed Chinese third-party payment institution (e.g., Alipay, WeChat Pay, or a licensed cross-border payment processor) under PBOC/SAFE rules. Offshore EMI accounts used to collect payments from customers (e.g., Stripe account with a Hong Kong or Singapore entity) may coexist with the mainland WFOE, but the WFOE's own export proceeds must ultimately be repatriated to its mainland bank account via a lawful cross-border payment channel. Using an offshore EMI solely to collect dropshipping revenue outside China, without a compliant underlying economic substance and tax reporting chain, would create permanent establishment and transfer pricing risks in Taiwan/China and fail the SAFE real-trade-background test. Practical viable structure: (i) an overseas payment intermediary licensed by the country of the sales platform (e.g., Stripe, PayPal) collects the end-customer funds; (ii) the intermediary, or the WFOE's mainland partner/PingPong, WorldFirst, or LianLian pay-type licensed service provider, settles the funds to the WFOE's domestic settlement account under a SAFE-filed cross-border e-commerce receivable collection scheme; (iii) the WFOE maintains quarterly reconciliation between platform payouts and its own accounting records. Foreign EMIs cannot directly clear RMB funds in CIPS/CHIPS or SWIFT for mainland corporations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing is performed through the China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal linked from the STA official website (www.chinatax.gov.cn) and operated by the competent in-charge tax authority for the WFOE's registered address.

E-government filing portal

A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise obtains its statutory tax identity automatically under the multi-license integration reform: the 18-digit Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation is the TIN/VAT registration number. The foreign shareholder must complete the following: (1) incorporate the WFOE in China and obtain the business license; (2) within 30 days, file tax registration particulars (VAT, corporate income tax, stamp duty, urban maintenance and education surcharges) in the provincial e-Tax portal; (3) complete taxpayer-type ratification and, if annual taxable turnover will exceed CNY 5,000,000, apply for general VAT taxpayer status; (4) open a Taxpayer Digital Account and bind legal representative and financial officer identity information; (5) submit constitutional documents, registered address proof, lease agreement, passport copies of the foreign shareholders and legal representative, bank account opening certificate and ultimate beneficial ownership declarations; (6) receive the electronic tax registration confirmation. A non-resident seller without a Chinese establishment obtains a non-resident tax identification code through withholding registration or direct non-resident enterprise registration at the in-charge authority; no paper TIN certificate is issued.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All enterprises must obtain tax registration upon business license issuance; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual VAT taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000 (approximately USD 700,000). Below that threshold, the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer unless it elects general status for export refund purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of goods by a general VAT taxpayer qualifies for the zero rate when the goods are physically exported under an electronic customs declaration; the sale is with an overseas buyer; title and risk pass outside mainland China; the goods are not prohibited from export; and export consideration is collected from a non-mainland party in foreign exchange and repatriated to China. Qualifying cross-border services may be zero-rated only if they are included in the statutory list of cross-border taxable services and are consumed entirely outside China. For Taiwan-bound cargo, the transaction must be evidenced by a lawful outward China-Taiwan customs movement record.

  • Export Customs Declaration (electronic customs entry with customs release endorsement)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export movement
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Foreign Exchange Collection Confirmation or bank receipt of inbound remittance (the PRC equivalent of an inward remittance certificate)
  • Export Contract showing incoterms and destination outside mainland China
  • Purchase VAT invoices and inventory records for the exported diagnostic reagents (for input VAT refund substantiation)
  • Export Tax Refund (Exemption) Declaration lodged in the e-Tax export-rebate module

China's Golden Tax Phase IV requires issuance of fully digitalized electronic invoices through the electronic tax bureau and the Taxpayer Digital Account. Invoice data is cleared in real time before use; no physical paper seal is required for all-digital invoices; each invoice is assigned a unique national invoice code and serial number by the STA platform. Approved ERP API integrations or third-party e-invoicing service providers are mandatory for systematic invoice reporting; commodity details (ISIC/HS classification and VAT rate) must be transmitted for diagnostic reagent trading.

The STA and China Customs exchange electronic data to match declarations, logistics and foreign-exchange receipts. If zero-rating or export refund is claimed without a genuine customs export, real commercial contract, or timely foreign exchange repatriation within 180 days, the zero rate is reversed and 13% output VAT plus late-payment interest is assessed. Additional consequences include recovery of any refund received, a fine of up to five times the tax or refund involved under the PRC Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and, for intentional fraud, criminal prosecution for fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law; the WFOE's export tax-rebate status may also be suspended and downgraded to a one-year controlled export-rebate file review.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under Taiwan's foreign exchange legal framework, the CBC administers a managed floating system for the New Taiwan Dollar (TWD/NTD). Current account transactions are effectively freely convertible: trade in goods and services, dividends, and remitted current income may be settled by authorized domestic banks without case-by-case CBC approval, subject only to bank KYC screening and regulatory reporting for large remittances. Capital account items, including direct investment inbound equity contributions, shareholder loans, and property investments, remain subject to prior application and approval by the CBC and the relevant investment commission. The WFOE's initial capital injection from mainland China is treated as a capital account direct investment requiring prior approval by the Taiwan Investment Commission (MOEA) and subsequent CBC foreign-exchange registration. Surrender requirements today are limited: exporters must repatriate the full contract value of export receipts into Taiwan within 180 days from shipment, but they are not compelled to convert to NTD if the proceeds are held in authorized Foreign Currency Deposit Units (FCDUs). Profit remittance obstacles are moderate: a Taiwan company may remit dividends after CPAs audit and tax filing, subject to corporate income tax and dividend withholding, and the remittance must be reported to the CBC via the remitting bank. Any delay or denial typically arises from unresolved beneficial ownership documentation or unverified source of funds rather than from an explicit prohibition on outbound dividends.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export foreign currency receipts must be received by an authorized domestic bank in Taiwan within 180 days from the date of shipment, or from the date of the customs export declaration for temperature-controlled diagnostic reagents, whichever is earlier under the remitting bank's internal policy. 100% of the invoiced export value must be repatriated into Taiwan and credited to a Foreign Currency Deposit Unit (FCDU) account or converted into TWD. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into TWD; the exporter may retain proceeds in foreign currency. If conversion is elected, it must be executed through a CBC-authorized bank at the prevailing reference rate. Failure to meet the 180-day repatriation timeline triggers CBC inquiry, tax scrutiny, and potential foreign exchange regulation penalties.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), Foreign Exchange Department

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud are not licensed as banks in Taiwan and are legally incapable of opening NTD-denominated current, escrow, or FCDU accounts. Taiwan's Foreign Exchange Regulation Act confines foreign-exchange settlement, capital account registration, and export proceeds handling to authorized domestic banks and selected financial institutions. Such EMIs cannot issue CBC-approved settlement codes, perform statutory capital verification, or provide the local branch compliance interviews required by Taiwan's banking AML rules. For this capital-intensive WFOE, an offshore EMI should be used only as an ancillary multi-currency payment collection rail for invoicing in third countries with no NTD flows; it cannot serve as the primary treasury or banking channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route the mainland China equity injection as a single SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the dedicated capital escrow account at Bank of Taiwan under the CBC direct investment purpose code for inward remittances and the MOEA-approved capital investment reference number. The remittance must identify the mainland parent as remitter and the Taiwan WFOE as beneficiary. Upon credit, the bank issues a foreign exchange certificate; the CPA performs statutory capital verification and files the capital verification report with the investment commission. Funds may be converted to TWD or held in the FCDU only after the capital registration is completed. Any shareholder loan above equity must be separately approved and recorded as a capital account remittance to avoid unauthorized borrowing classification.Settlement for imported reagent inventory and supplies should use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers through the authorized Taiwan bank, with CBC current account payment purpose code identifying imports of goods and accompanied by invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, and relevant import permits. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Intercompany transactions exceeding NT$200,000,000 (New Taiwan Dollars) in the aggregate per category trigger mandatory preparation of a Country-by-Country Report, Master File, and Local File. In addition, all related-party transactions exceeding NT$10,000,000 in total amount for financial institutions or NT$50,000,000 for non-financial enterprises require the preparation of the transfer pricing report (Local File) unless the taxpayer meets the simplified exemption. Enterprises with related-party transactions below NT$30,000,000 may file a simplified disclosure form. For this WFOE distributing IVD reagents, if related-party purchases from the China parent exceed NT$50,000,000, the Local File is mandatory and must be filed upon request within 60 days.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan's Income Tax Act Article 43-1 empowers the tax authority to reallocate income and disallow deductions when a taxpayer engaged in related-party transactions deviates from arm's length and shifts profits to a low-tax jurisdiction or to a related party. The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in Taiwan are not formally branded as 'ESR' but operate through the anti-avoidance and substance-over-form doctrines: the WFOE must demonstrate the performance of significant functions, employment of personnel in Taiwan, decision-making authority of local managers, ownership of relevant business risks, and the presence of physical office space. For distribution of diagnostic reagents, the WFOE must show that it performs inventory management, customer credit, regulatory compliance (TFDA import licenses), local logistics, and assumes inventory risk. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for the limited-risk distributor include active sales negotiations, contract execution with Taiwan hospitals and laboratories, acceptance of customer orders, handling of product complaints, and management of accounts receivable. If the China parent purportedly executes all strategic selling activities and the WFOE is a mere commissionaire with no contractual discretionary authority, Taiwan tax authorities may deem the WFOE to have a permanent establishment of the China parent, tax the Taiwan-source trading profit at 20%, and impose penalties. A written Local File must include an industry and functional analysis, comparability analysis, application of the Resale Price Method (preferred for distributors) or TNMM, and a contemporaneous benchmarking study using Taiwan or regional comparables.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Artificial Profit Shifting to China Parent

Statutory sanction: Under Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act, the tax authority is empowered to reallocate the income and impose assessment of a deficiency. The underpaid tax is subject to a penalty of 15% for negligence or up to 100% for willful evasion under Article 110 of the Income Tax Act. Under Article 46 of the Tax Collection Act, false or concealed invoices and accounting records may incur a 5% penalty per invoice, capped at 1,000,000 TWD, and the responsible person may be fined up to 500,000 TWD. If the fraud is detected, interest is compounded at the post office savings rate plus a late penalty of 10% per month.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil under Taiwan Company Act Article 154, holding directors personally liable if the company is used to evade tax or is undercapitalized. The responsible director can be held jointly and severally liable for the full tax deficiency plus penalties when they approved the false transfer pricing and signed the tax returns. Criminal conviction under Article 76 of the Tax Collection Act may result in imprisonment for up to 5 years for willful tax evasion. Directors may further face a travel ban (出境限制) imposed by the National Taxation Bureau for tax debts exceeding 20 million TWD, and personal assets may be frozen, attached, and auctioned. In addition, the Investment Commission may revoke the WFOE's investment approval, causing dissolution.
Failure to Withhold Taiwan-Source Withholding Tax on Outbound Remittances to China Parent

Statutory sanction: Article 88 and Article 114 of the Income Tax Act subject the withholding agent to a penalty of 20% of the un-withheld amount, payable by the WFOE. In addition, the unpaid withholding tax itself is collected from the WFOE, and the responsible person is jointly liable. Under Article 93 of the Tax Collection Act, the tax authority may order the taxpayer to provide security for the tax and impose daily late payment interest of 0.5% per 30 days. If the WFOE failed to file the withholding statement, a fine of 3,000 to 30,000 TWD applies per return. The tax authority may also recharacterize the remittances as constructive dividends, resulting in the 21% dividend WHT and additional penalties.

Director personal liability: The director of the WFOE who approved the money transfer abroad is personally liable for the withholding tax amount and 20% penalty under Income Tax Act Article 114 because it is a non-remittable statutory obligation. The director's personal assets may be seized upon company default under the principle of responsible person liability. If the director willfully fails to withhold and uses offshore accounts, they commit criminal tax evasion, subject to imprisonment of up to 2 years plus a fine of up to 480,000 TWD under Article 118 and Article 76 of the Tax Collection Act. The National Taxation Bureau will issue a travel ban for a responsible person of a company with tax arrears exceeding a statutory threshold, and the China parent's future administrative relations with Taiwan are jeopardized.
Shell Company/Sham Economic Substance and Fake Local Operations (Piercing the Corporate Veil)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 43-1 and the judicial interpretation of the Supreme Administrative Court, the tax authority may disregard the WFOE's separate corporate existence and attribute its Taiwan-source income to the China parent, resulting in a 20% income tax assessment on the entire Taiwan revenue as if earned by a permanent establishment. Additionally, the WFOE may be fined for failing to obtain the required TFDA Medical Devices License under the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act, with penal fines of up to 1,500,000 TWD and forfeiture of counterfeit products. Under the Economic Substance Doctrine, the tax authority may apply Article 66-9 for retained earnings and levy a 5% undistributed surplus tax on the company's unappropriated earnings, and a 10% surcharge for unreasonable accumulation under the AMT statute. The WFOE will also be disallowed from input VAT credit and pay VAT penalties up to 30% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Taiwan courts pierce the corporate veil when a company lacks economic substance: the controlling director and the beneficial shareholder (the China parent) are jointly and severally liable for the company's taxes under Company Act Article 154(2). If the director knowingly signed false financial statements, the Commercial Accounting Act Article 71 imposes imprisonment of up to 10 years and a fine of up to 1,000,000 TWD. In addition, the director faces a travel ban (限制出境) under the Tax Collection Act Article 24, under which the collection authority may apply to the court for detention of the responsible person if there is evidence of intent to evade collection by hiding assets. The China parent, as an overseas shareholder, may have its Taiwan shares or bank balances frozen and its representative denied entry into Taiwan. In severe cases with forged documents, the director is subject to fraud under Criminal Code Articles 210-216 and imprisonment for up to 7 years.

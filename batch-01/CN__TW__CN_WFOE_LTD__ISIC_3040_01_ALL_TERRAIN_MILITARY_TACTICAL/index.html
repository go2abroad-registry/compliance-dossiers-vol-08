Taiwan operates a Uniform Invoice mandate. A standard WFOE must issue sequential Uniform Invoices (hardcopy or cloud-based electronic invoices) for domestic sales. Electronic sales and allowance data must be encrypted, signed, and transmitted through the certified e-Invoice Turnkey network or a Ministry of Finance-approved platform/API to the e-Invoice Exchange Center; the center validates the invoice numbering against the registered invoice range and stores the transaction for audit matching. Although Taiwan does not use pre-transaction fiscal approval/stamping, the e-Invoice cloud system includes a mandatory random-number anti-fraud control and a fixed upload window within the monthly/bimonthly declaration period; failure to upload or any mismatch triggers automatic return notices and late-filing penalties.

      
Zero-rated export transactions are audited by matching customs export declarations, zero-rated sales reports, uniform invoice exception reports, and central bank foreign-exchange records. If foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day statutory benchmark or the export documents are later cancelled/amended, the National Taxation Bureau may reclassify the transaction as domestic taxable supply and assess 5% VAT on the original consideration, plus late-payment interest and administrative penalties. Where deliberate false zero-rating or fraudulent refund is established, the Business Tax Act imposes penalty multiples on the tax evaded, and criminal tax evasion liability may be referred to the prosecutorial authorities.

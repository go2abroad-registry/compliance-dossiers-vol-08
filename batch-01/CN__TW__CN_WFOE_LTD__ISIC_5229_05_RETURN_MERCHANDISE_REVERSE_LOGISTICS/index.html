Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; online services through the National Electronic Tax Bureau portal (https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

A China-incorporated WFOE receives automatic tax registration through government data sharing between the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and the State Taxation Administration. The competent local tax bureau issues a unified taxpayer identification number (TIN) which serves simultaneously for Enterprise Income Tax, VAT, and export tax refund purposes. No separate VAT registration number is required. The WFOE must file a tax information report and activate its taxpayer profile within 30 days of business license issuance. If a foreign entity has not yet formed a permanent establishment in China, it may obtain a withholding-tax TIN through the Overseas Enterprise Tax Registration procedure before claiming any treaty benefits or zero-rated VAT refunds.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income at 25% Enterprise Income Tax; no pass-through eligibility) — A China-incorporated WFOE receives automatic tax registration through government data sharing between the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and the State Taxation Administration. The competent local tax bureau issues a unified taxpayer identification number (TIN) which serves simultaneously for Enterprise Income Tax, VAT, and export tax refund purposes. No separate VAT registration number is required. The WFOE must file a tax information report and activate its taxpayer profile within 30 days of business license issuance. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income at 25% Enterprise Income Tax; no pass-through eligibility)

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available under Chinese tax law. The WFOE is mandatorily classified as a resident enterprise by virtue of incorporation in China. The only relevant election is the voluntary registration by small-scale VAT taxpayers as general VAT taxpayers, which is required for export zero-rating. For income tax purposes no entity classification election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales; exceeding this threshold triggers mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer. Below the threshold the WFOE is automatically classified as a small-scale VAT taxpayer (collection rate 3%, temporarily 1%), but can elect general taxpayer status to export and claim zero-rating. All newly incorporated enterprises receive VAT registration automatically regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from the PRC customs territory qualify for zero-rating, resulting in output VAT at 0% and refund of input VAT. The exporter must be a general VAT taxpayer and must submit a valid customs export declaration. Cross-border services, including international freight forwarding, reverse logistics agency, and offshore processing support services, may qualify for zero-rating if the services are wholly consumed outside China and invoices are issued for foreign-currency collection. Reverse logistics activities physically performed on returned goods inside China, such as inspection, regrading, repair, and repackaging, are generally subject to standard VAT rates unless conducted within a bonded customs supervision zone, in which case applicable bonded policies apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口货物报关单)
  • VAT Special Invoice for the purchased export goods (增值税专用发票)
  • Commercial Export Invoice (出口发票)
  • Packing List (装箱单)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (提单/空运单)
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Receipt or Cross-Border RMB Collection Voucher (出口收汇结汇水单/跨境人民币收汇说明)

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV e-invoicing framework. Full digital electronic invoices (fapiao) are issued via the Electronic Tax Bureau platform or API-connected certified software and are transmitted to the State Taxation Administration in real time. Each invoice carries a unique serial number and a digital electronic seal; no physical fiscal stamping or pre-approval of identical invoice flows is used. The tax authority performs automatic matching between issuing and receiving taxpayers, and VAT filing reconciles output and input invoices with customs and bank data.

Tax authorities cross-check export refund claims against customs declarations, logistics bills, insurance records, bank receipts, and FX settlement data. If an export zero-rating claim is found unsupported, fictitious, or misdescribed, the refunded tax is clawed back along with interest from the refund date, and a penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax evaded is imposed under Article 63 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Criminal liability under Articles 201 or 205 of the Criminal Law may attach to fraudulent VAT refund schemes, leading to fines and imprisonment up to life. Repeated violations trigger disqualification from export tax refund status and adverse credit ranking.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a bifurcated convertibility framework. The current account is fully convertible for bona fide trade and service transactions, including payments for reverse logistics, freight, and quality inspections, subject to documentary verification and the foreign exchange monitoring system. The capital account, including WFOE registered capital injection, external debt, and cross-border equity transfers, remains restricted and requires case-by-case SAFE registration or approval. Profit remittance is permitted in the current account after submission of tax clearance certificates, audited financial statements, and corporate resolutions; no additional convertibility surcharge applies. For reverse logistics operators, returned-goods proceeds are treated as ordinary current-account receipts and must be matched against import/export customs declarations to avoid audit exceptions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China does not mandate a statutory surrender ratio for export proceeds. Under the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Rules (2012, revised), exporters must repatriate all export proceeds to China (mainland) within 90 days of the export declaration date; thereafter, the proceeds may be held in a foreign-currency current-account deposit or voluntarily converted to RMB at the enterprise's bank. Mandatory verification against the Customs Declaration (export) and the Foreign Exchange Receipts and Payment Declaration Form is required. For reverse logistics goods returned offshore or re-exported, the enterprise must maintain an accurate reconciliation of the original export and subsequent return to avoid currency mismatches and customs-valuation discrepancies.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Payoneer, PingPong) have limited viability as a primary banking rail for a mainland WFOE. Legal constraints: they are not licensed as deposit-taking institutions in China and cannot hold RMB or foreign currency settlement accounts on behalf of a WFOE for capital account transactions. Feasibility rating: Low for capital injection and statutory trade verification; Medium as an auxiliary payment-processing layer for cross-border e-commerce receipts, provided the EMI operates under a Payment Institution License issued by PBOC (as a joint-venture/licensed entity) or serves only as a neutral agent passing funds to a licensed onshore bank. For this reverse logistics scope, Airwallex may be used for small-value refunds and disbursements to offshore suppliers, but must be backed by a compliant onshore corporate bank account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership chain involving non-transparent offshore shareholders (e.g., Taiwan-based private owners) and a newly formed WFOE with no prior operating history, triggering heightened due diligence under SAFE and PBOC AML Rules 2023.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership chain involving non-transparent offshore shareholders (e.g., Taiwan-based private owners) and a newly formed WFOE with no prior operating history, triggering heightened due diligence under SAFE and PBOC AML Rules 2023.

Defense evidence: Provide a certified group structure chart, shareholders' register, and UBO declaration (Form RC06/RC07) evidencing natural-person ultimate owners; audit-confirmed source of funds of the registered capital; notarized certificates of incorporation of all intermediate holding entities; and an investment commitment letter from the Taiwan parent company.
The reverse logistics business model (ISIC 5229) involves high volumes of returned merchandise, multiple cross-border counterparties, and potential mismatches between goods flow and payment flow, raising suspicion of trade-based money laundering.

Root cause: The reverse logistics business model (ISIC 5229) involves high volumes of returned merchandise, multiple cross-border counterparties, and potential mismatches between goods flow and payment flow, raising suspicion of trade-based money laundering.

Defense evidence: Submit a complete trade compliance pack containing: master reverse-logistics agreement with offshore clients, sample customs declarations (export and re-import), logistics warehouse receipts, quality-inspection certificates, and a reconciliation ledger matching each batch of returns to the original invoice. Include a written SOP for returned-goods disposal and a 3-year projected cash-flow model to demonstrate economic substance.
Request to open both a capital escrow account and multiple operational accounts at the same bank may trigger internal fraud and lending-risk questioning, especially if the WFOE has no local payroll or tax registration record.

Root cause: Request to open both a capital escrow account and multiple operational accounts at the same bank may trigger internal fraud and lending-risk questioning, especially if the WFOE has no local payroll or tax registration record.

Defense evidence: Present the WFOE's business license with unified social credit code, tax registration certificate, office lease agreement, complete Articles of Association, and evidence of local employees enrolled in social insurance. Root cause: Cross-border refunds to customers or third-party liquidators may be flagged as anomalous outbound fund transfers under the 'Fully Automatic FX Monitoring and Alert System' if the remittance purpose code is inconsistent.

Ensure all refund receipts are supported by original trade contracts, customs declarations of the returned goods, and a written refund policy. Pre-clear large refunds (above USD 50,000 equivalent) with the designated bank relationship manager using a stamped refund justification letter.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE's registered capital must be remitted from the foreign shareholder via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the WFOE's Capital Escrow Account (FDI) at a designated Tier-1 bank. The payment must include the precise purpose/reference code required by SAFE: 'Investment in a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise' (FDI registration number). The remitting bank must transmit both the remitter's and beneficiary's full names and account numbers. Upon receipt, the bank performs authenticity verification against the FDI Certificate (received from MOFCOM or now filed with the market regulator) and the Foreign Exchange Business Registration (FDI) Form issued by SAFE. The entire remittance must be registered in the 'Investment Control' module within 15 days of receipt; failure to do so results in funds being blocked and a cross-border red-flag filing. For Taiwan-sourced capital, the cross-strait remittance protocol applies additionally: the Taiwan financial institution must report the remittance to Taiwan's Central Bank (CBC) and ensure it is originated through an approved banking channel to a mainland Chinese bank.Outbound payments for trade settlements (purchase of returned-goods spare parts, freight, overseas warehouse fees) are executed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the WFOE's operational RMB/foreign-currency account to the overseas beneficiary. For high-value or recurring disbursements, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued by an onshore bank can be used when trading with new counterparties; the LC must be backed by an underlying trade contract and the issuing bank will require margin cover of 20–100% depending on creditworthiness. For no-LC transactions, the bank requires a payment instruction with the correct purpose code and supporting documents (contract, invoice, customs declaration if goods are exported/imported). Batch disbursements to multiple logistics providers can be handled via a single payment file uploaded to the bank's treasury interface, but each underlying trade must be verifiable. The ACH (Large-Value Payment System / HVPS) is used for RMB-denominated onshore transfers; cross-border RMB settlement can be routed via SWIFT using RMB, with the benefit of avoiding FX conversion loss but subject to the bank's RMB cross-border clearing approval under SAFE rules.Onshore CNY forward contracts (deliverable forwards) with a licensed onshore bank, under a rolling real-trade-background documentation requirement (trade contract + customs data) for hedging receivable/payable FX exposure.CNY non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for onshore WFOEs due to SAFE regulations; the only permissible NDF market is offshore (CNH), and onshore parties cannot directly participate unless through an approved cross-border interbank channel.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) between CNY and TWD/USD/HKD for long-term liabilities, requiring proof of underlying current-account exposure and SAFE/National Association of Financial Market Institutional Investors (NAFMII) master agreement registration.FX options (vanilla put/call) with trade-background verification, permissible for hedging import/export cash flows of the reverse logistics operation; premium must be expensed as a financial cost and the option cannot be exercised for speculative gain.Natural hedging instruments such as maintaining multi-currency cash pools at the same bank (CNY, USD, TWD) and matching inbound and outbound flows to reduce residual FX exposure; but currency conversion between retained foreign exchange and RMB is limited to the actual transaction value with a maximum 10% tolerance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (contemporaneous documentation) is mandatory for one fiscal year where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200,000,000 for tangible goods, RMB 100,000,000 for intangible asset transfers, service transactions, or financial transactions. Master File is required when the global consolidated revenue of the group exceeds RMB 5,000,000,000. The related-party transaction reporting schedule (A105050) is compulsory for all WFOEs regardless of threshold and must be filed with the annual CIT return.

Economic substance scope: China applies a General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under CIT Article 47 and the Special Tax Adjustment Regulations (SAT Notice No. 16, 2009). Although no standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) exists, a Taiwanese investor claiming treaty benefits must demonstrate substantial business activity in Taiwan — real employees, premises, decision-making, and risk assumption. A shell investor without substance will be denied treaty reductions and may face recharacterisation of income; the WFOE’s own substance is evaluated to ensure it is not a conduit deriving improper non-resident tax advantages.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (逃税)

Statutory sanction: Civil restitution of tax evaded plus a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount; criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law (Article 201) if the evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the tax due, carrying up to 7 years’ imprisonment and additional fines

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives may be jointly and severally liable for restitution, subjected to personal asset freezing, exit restrictions (travel bans), and, in flagrant cases, criminal detention and imprisonment; corporate veil will be pierced where the company is a mere alter ego of the Taiwanese investor
Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Denial

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty reduction resulting in re-withholding at 10% domestic rate; imposition of GAAR adjustment with a 10-30% surcharge on the re-adjusted tax payable; potential 0.05% daily interest on under-withheld tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for failure to withhold correctly; travel bans and asset seizure for the responsible foreign investor; criminal exposure if the shell arrangement facilitates money laundering
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Mandatory TP adjustment to an arm's length result; assessment of interest at the benchmark loan rate plus 5 percentage points; penalty of 0.05% daily on the underpaid CIT; fine up to RMB 50,000 for missing documentation; criminal referral for deliberate falsification

Director personal liability: Executives who sign false TP declarations or knowingly submit inaccurate documentation may incur civil joint liability, criminal sanctions for invoice and financial misreporting, and exit restrictions pending full payment
AML & Cross-Border Payment Violation

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law, fines up to RMB 5,000,000 for a corporate violator, suspension of business license, revocation of payment licenses; where funds derive from predicate offences, criminal liability for money laundering (imprisonment up to 10 years plus fines)

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined up to RMB 500,000, prohibited from banking management duties, and criminally prosecuted for knowing participation in illicit payment transfers; travel bans apply until the AML investigation concludes

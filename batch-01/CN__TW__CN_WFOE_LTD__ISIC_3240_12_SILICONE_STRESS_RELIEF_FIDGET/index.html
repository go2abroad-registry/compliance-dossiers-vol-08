Competent corporate registry

Company Act of Taiwan (公司法), Revised as of 2023, Articles 1-20 (incorporation and registration), Article 101-110 (limited company capital and shareholders resolution); Statute for Investment by Foreign Investors (外國人投資條例), Articles 7-9 (investment permission and remittance); Regulations on Company Name and Business Scope Review; Standards for Registration, Approval, and Administration of Foreign Investment.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists for a general WFOE, provided that the total paid-in capital stated in the Articles of Incorporation is fully remitted from abroad through a Taiwan foreign-exchange-designated bank and evidenced by a CPA-verified Capital Verification Report and a Deposit Balance Certificate. Capital paid in may not be withdrawn or loaned back to the foreign investor until registration is completed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Taiwan Ministry of Finance (MOF), administered locally by the relevant National Taxation Bureau; principal electronic services include the MOF eTax Portal and the Business Tax Electronic Invoice Platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested limited company must first complete company registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs, Department of Commerce, or the competent local government, and obtain its Unified Business Number (統一編號). It must then complete tax registration with the competent National Taxation Bureau before commencing taxable business activities. The filing package generally includes the company registration certificate, articles of incorporation, responsible-person identification, registered office evidence, investment approval or foreign-investment documentation where applicable, business scope, bank information, and power of attorney for an agent. A foreign enterprise without a Taiwan fixed place of business may apply to the competent tax authority for relevant tax registration or withholding identification based on its Taiwan-source activities. VAT registration and electronic invoice enrollment are separate operational steps and should be completed before issuing taxable invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-invested limited company must first complete company registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs, Department of Commerce, or the competent local government, and obtain its Unified Business Number (統一編號). It must then complete tax registration with the competent National Taxation Bureau before commencing taxable business activities. The filing package generally includes the company registration certificate, articles of incorporation, responsible-person identification, registered office evidence, investment approval or foreign-investment documentation where applicable, business scope, bank information, and power of attorney for an agent. VAT registration and electronic invoice enrollment are separate operational steps and should be completed before issuing taxable invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not generally provide a United States-style check-the-box election for a Taiwan limited company. A company is ordinarily subject to Taiwan corporate income tax on its Taiwan-source income and, where applicable, worldwide income under Taiwan residence rules. Partnerships and certain transparent arrangements may receive different treatment under specific statutes, but a Taiwan limited company cannot elect pass-through treatment merely by filing a classification form. No general hybrid-mismatch election is available; anti-avoidance, transfer-pricing, controlled foreign company, thin-capitalization, and other anti-base-erosion rules apply where their statutory conditions are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A company carrying on business in Taiwan is generally required to register for business tax before commencing operations; there is no general turnover de minimis threshold for a registered company. For small business operators eligible for the special non-VAT assessment regime, the commonly applicable monthly sales thresholds are NTD 80,000 for goods and NTD 40,000 for services, subject to statutory classification and administrative rules. A foreign supplier making electronically supplied services to Taiwan consumers may have a separate nonresident VAT registration threshold of NTD 480,000 in any six-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Taiwan may qualify for a 0% business tax rate when the exporter can establish actual exportation and retain the required customs evidence. Services may qualify where they are supplied to foreign customers and are used outside Taiwan, subject to statutory conditions and evidence of the customer, contractual scope, payment, and offshore use. Domestic sale of goods that are subsequently exported by another party does not automatically qualify unless the statutory export or bonded-transaction conditions are satisfied. Input business tax refunds may be claimed by an eligible business where zero-rated export sales generate excess input tax, subject to filing, documentary substantiation, customs verification, and applicable refund procedures.

  • Customs export declaration or other official electronic export record showing the exporter, goods, value, and export clearance
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order or sales contract, and transport documentation such as bill of lading, airway bill, or equivalent carrier record
  • Proof of delivery or other evidence establishing removal of the goods from Taiwan, where required by the tax authority
  • Bank remittance record, foreign-exchange settlement record, or other payment evidence where relevant; an inward remittance certificate is not universally required for every zero-rated export but may be requested for substantiation
  • For zero-rated offshore services: service agreement, customer identification, service-delivery records, evidence of offshore use, invoices, and payment records
  • VAT return schedules, uniform invoices, accounting ledgers, inventory records, and reconciliation between customs declarations, sales invoices, and reported zero-rated turnover

Taiwan businesses subject to the uniform-invoice system generally issue government-prescribed electronic or paper uniform invoices. Electronic invoices and relevant invoice information must be transmitted to the MOF electronic invoice platform within the applicable statutory period, generally within two days after issuance for ordinary electronic invoices, subject to special rules for particular transaction types. The system uses government-approved invoice formats, assigned invoice numbers, electronic signatures or approved authentication methods, and platform reporting. Taiwan generally operates post-issuance reporting rather than universal real-time transactional clearance. Export invoices and zero-rated sales require appropriate zero-rate coding and supporting transaction records.

The 180-day period should be treated as a transaction-specific compliance control rather than a universal Taiwan VAT rule; Taiwan foreign-exchange, customs, banking, and tax requirements may impose different timing rules depending on the transaction and payment structure. The National Taxation Bureau may deny or reverse zero-rating, disallow related input-tax credits or refunds, assess unpaid business tax, interest, late-payment charges, and penalties, and require amended returns. False customs declarations, fabricated export evidence, sham offshore-service claims, or intentional tax evasion may trigger administrative penalties under the Business Tax Act and Tax Collection Act, including multiples of the tax evaded, assessment of the underlying tax and interest, and potential criminal liability. Refunds obtained through fraud may be clawed back with penalties and may also expose responsible persons and participating agents to criminal investigation. Records should generally be retained for the statutory tax-record retention period and be capable of reconciling customs, accounting, banking, inventory, and electronic-invoice data.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi is generally usable for current-account transactions subject to genuine-trade documentation, bank review, customs and tax consistency, and applicable reporting. Capital-account transactions remain controlled and require registration, filing, quota, or bank-level verification depending on the transaction. Foreign-invested enterprises may receive registered foreign capital and may convert and use capital funds only for permitted purposes. Capital must not be used for prohibited investments, securities trading, real-estate speculation, unauthorized lending, or other restricted purposes. Profit, dividend, interest, royalty, and service-fee remittances generally require board or shareholder approvals, audited or tax-supported financial records, applicable tax filings or withholding, and bank review. Dividend remittance normally follows annual profit determination, loss recovery, statutory reserve requirements, and distribution approval. Related-party service, royalty, interest, and financing payments must satisfy transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, substance, arm's-length, and anti-avoidance requirements. Taiwan is a separate regulatory perimeter: Taiwan-dollar conversion, foreign exchange reporting, and repatriation through Taiwan banks are governed by Taiwan law and cannot be assumed available to the mainland WFOE.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mainland China does not generally impose a universal mandatory conversion ratio requiring all export proceeds to be surrendered into renminbi. Export receipts may generally be retained in foreign currency or converted through an authorized bank, subject to SAFE rules, bank verification, customs and trade-data consistency, and the enterprise's foreign-exchange classification. There is no single generally applicable statutory deadline or fixed surrender percentage for this business model. Export proceeds must nevertheless be collected through compliant bank channels, matched to the underlying transaction, and recorded in accordance with applicable foreign-exchange, accounting, tax, customs, and anti-money-laundering requirements. Banks may require export contracts, invoices, customs declarations, bills of lading, payment evidence, and explanations for delayed, partial, offset, or third-party receipts. Any Taiwan export receipt is subject to Taiwan Central Bank reporting and bank documentation rules rather than mainland SAFE rules.

Central bank supervision: Primary mainland China authorities: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). For any Taiwan-based account, payment, or treasury activity, the competent authorities are the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) and the Financial Supervisory Commission (Taiwan). The China WFOE must principally apply mainland China foreign-exchange, banking, customs, tax, and cross-border payment rules. Taiwan-related activity requires separate Taiwan legal and banking analysis and does not convert a mainland China WFOE into a Taiwan entity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for collection and payment support, but Low as a substitute for the regulated mainland banking stack. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment platform cannot independently provide the mainland WFOE with unrestricted RMB deposit, foreign-exchange conversion, capital-account settlement, or domestic settlement-bank functionality unless the relevant activity is performed through a properly licensed mainland institution or an approved bank partnership. Platform collections require contract, merchant onboarding, beneficial-owner verification, transaction monitoring, payment-purpose consistency, data and cybersecurity review, and settlement into an account held by the correct legal entity. Personal accounts, undisclosed payment aggregators, informal netting, virtual-asset channels, and third-party collection structures should not be used.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor should remit funds from an account held in the investor's own name to the WFOE's approved foreign-capital account through an authorized mainland bank. The remittance instruction should identify the legal entity, capital-contribution purpose, currency, investor, and applicable registration or filing reference. The bank should verify the investment registration, ownership documents, source of funds, remitter identity, and consistency with the registered capital and constitutional documents. The WFOE should not route capital through employees, unrelated affiliates, personal accounts, offshore wallets, or informal netting. After receipt, conversion into RMB should be conducted only for permitted operating purposes and with supporting contracts, invoices, tax documents, and payment approvals. The treasury ledger should separately track foreign capital, operating receipts, intercompany funding, trade receipts, taxes, dividends, and hedging settlements.Supplier and service payments should be made from the WFOE's mainland operational account to the verified contractual counterparty through an authorized bank. For ordinary trade, the bank may process a cross-border telegraphic transfer using SWIFT MT103 or an equivalent bank payment message, supported by contract, invoice, purchase order, customs or logistics evidence where relevant, and tax documentation for services. For higher-value or documentary transactions, use a commercial letter of credit, including issuance, amendment, presentation, discrepancy, and settlement controls under the agreed documentary-credit rules. Payment references should state the accurate commercial purpose and avoid split payments or unexplained third-party settlement. For imports, the bank may require customs, shipping, invoice, and trade-finance documentation. For royalties, interest, service fees, and other non-trade payments, obtain applicable tax review, withholding evidence, related-party support, and bank or SAFE documentation before remittance. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% PRC enterprise income tax for a standard resident enterprise, including a wholly foreign-owned enterprise incorporated in Mainland China.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% PRC withholding tax on dividends paid by a PRC resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise, subject to applicable treaty or cross-Strait arrangement relief, beneficial-owner requirements and anti-abuse rules. Dividends must generally be paid from distributable after-tax profits after statutory reserve requirements and settlement of tax, debt and regulatory obligations.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly prepayment is generally due within 15 days after the end of each quarter. Annual final settlement and payment of balance are generally due within five months after the end of the tax year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the annual return and balance payment are generally due by 31 May of the following year. If a deadline falls on a statutory holiday, the deadline follows the applicable extension rule.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For a PRC resident enterprise, a Local File is generally triggered where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible-asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial-asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible-asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions, subject to statutory exemptions and current implementation rules. A Master File is generally relevant where the enterprise belongs to a group meeting the applicable multinational-group and controlled-transaction thresholds, commonly including annual related-party transactions exceeding RMB 1 billion or other prescribed conditions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to a Chinese tax-resident ultimate parent or designated reporting entity of a multinational group with consolidated revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion, subject to current rules and jurisdictional filing conditions.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate a single regime identical to the UAE Economic Substance Regulations, but substance, beneficial ownership, commercial purpose, actual functions, assets, risks, personnel, decision-making and control are central to transfer-pricing, treaty-relief, controlled-foreign-company, general anti-avoidance and permanent-establishment analysis. A WFOE manufacturing silicone fidget products should maintain real production capability, personnel, premises, machinery, inventory control, quality systems, procurement evidence and management decisions in China. A Taiwan or offshore affiliate with no personnel, decision-making, operational risk or commercial capability may be denied treaty relief, have deductions challenged, or be recharacterized as a conduit or low-substance entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid tax and late-payment surcharge; administrative penalty generally ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax evaded; serious cases may result in criminal prosecution for tax evasion and related offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, finance officers and other responsible persons may face criminal fines and imprisonment where statutory criminal thresholds are met. Personal civil liability may arise for losses caused by unlawful acts. Travel restrictions, credit restrictions and asset enforcement may follow a court judgment or enforcement order; corporate limited liability does not protect deliberate personal misconduct.
VAT and invoice fraud

Statutory sanction: Input-tax denial, recovery of tax, penalties, cancellation of invoice privileges and potential criminal liability for false VAT invoicing, with imprisonment and fines depending on the circumstances and amount involved.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors, legal representatives, finance personnel and actual controllers may face criminal penalties, personal fines, enforcement restrictions and restrictions on departure where legally ordered.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may make a special tax adjustment, impose interest or additional interest, deny deductions, reassess withholding tax and investigate related-party transactions for prior years.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability to the company or shareholders for negligent or intentional breaches of duty. Criminal liability generally requires additional fraudulent or tax-evasion conduct, but personal exposure increases where records were knowingly falsified.
Undisclosed related-party transactions

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties, loss of documentation exemptions, transfer-pricing adjustments, penalty interest and increased audit risk.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers may be fined administratively and may face civil or criminal exposure if omission forms part of a deliberate tax fraud or document-falsification scheme.
Illegal profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: Dividend reversal, tax reassessment, withholding tax, VAT and surcharge exposure, recovery of unlawful distributions and potential foreign-exchange or company-law sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to return unlawful distributions and compensate the company or creditors. Deliberate asset dissipation or fraudulent accounting may result in criminal liability and enforcement restrictions.
Foreign-exchange and cross-border payment violations

Statutory sanction: Foreign-exchange investigation, orders to correct, confiscation of unlawful gains, administrative fines and possible criminal prosecution for illegal business operations, fraud or money laundering.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives, actual controllers and payment approvers may face personal fines, criminal liability, travel restrictions and asset enforcement.
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or cross-Strait relief, denial of deductions, withholding-tax reassessment, general anti-avoidance adjustment, transfer-pricing adjustment and possible deregistration or licensing consequences where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors and actual controllers may face civil liability and, where the structure is used for fraud, tax evasion or asset concealment, criminal prosecution and enforcement restrictions.
Beneficial ownership and treaty-relief abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of relief, collection of the full withholding tax, interest, penalties and anti-avoidance review.

Director personal liability: Knowingly false residence certificates, ownership statements or beneficial-owner records may expose signatories and responsible officers to administrative or criminal liability.
Customs, origin and export-control violations

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessment, duties, import VAT, late charges, administrative fines, seizure and potential criminal liability for smuggling or customs fraud.

Director personal liability: Responsible customs declarants, directors and actual controllers may face fines, criminal prosecution, travel restrictions and personal asset enforcement.
AML, sanctions and suspicious-payment failures

Statutory sanction: Bank account restrictions, transaction rejection, regulatory investigation, confiscation, administrative fines and criminal sanctions for money laundering or sanctions evasion where applicable.

Director personal liability: Actual controllers and approving officers may face criminal liability, personal fines, travel bans or departure restrictions where ordered, and civil liability for resulting losses.
Corporate record and accounting obstruction

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, adverse tax assessment, loss of compliance status, evidence-preservation orders and criminal liability for obstruction, concealment or destruction of accounting materials.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives may be personally fined and prosecuted where their conduct materially obstructs authorities or facilitates tax, customs or financial crimes.
Product compliance and consumer-law exposure

Statutory sanction: Product recalls, confiscation, fines, business suspension, compensation claims and potential criminal liability for serious quality or consumer-safety violations.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible quality officers may face civil compensation, administrative penalties and criminal liability if defective or unsafe products cause serious injury or death.

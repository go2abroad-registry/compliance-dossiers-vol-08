Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (国家税务总局, STA), operating through the National Unified E-Tax Bureau portal (全国统一规范电子税务局, etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and the 12366 tax service hotline. The Shenzhen/Shanghai or local STA branch where the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture laboratory is registered exercises day-to-day administration. All filings, VAT general taxpayer qualification, and e-invoice quotas are managed electronically under the Golden Tax Phase IV (金税四期) system.

E-government filing portal

A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must complete company incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which simultaneously assigns the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码) that functions as the statutory TIN/VAT registration number. Within 30 days of obtaining the business license, the JV completes tax registration (税务登记) via the E-Tax Bureau portal by filing: (1) the SAMR business license; (2) the Foreign Investment Filing Certificate (外商投资信息报告); (3) the Articles of Association and Joint Venture Contract; (4) registered address proof and lease agreement; and (5) legal representative and finance officer identification. Upon completion, STA issues the Taxpayer Qualification Information, activates the VAT taxpayer classification (General or Small-Scale), enables the UKey digital certificate, sets the invoice quota, and binds the enterprise bank account to the Golden Tax system for real-time reconciliation. No separate TIN is required for a domestically incorporated EJV; the JV is a Chinese resident taxpayer in its own right.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not maintain a check-the-box or pass-through regime; all equity joint ventures are classified as C corporation equivalents taxed at entity level under Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) at a 25% headline rate on worldwide income. Available statutory elections relevant to the laboratory: (i) VAT classification election between Small-Scale Taxpayer (simplified 3% levy, subject to annual turnover ≤ RMB 5 million and full compliance) and General Taxpayer (input credit mechanism at 6% for modern technical services); (ii) EIT collection-method election between audit-based assessment (查账征收, the default and mandatory benchmark) and the restricted deemed-assessment method (核定征收); (iii) the 100% super-deduction election for qualifying R&D expenditures (研发费用加计扣除, applicable to laboratory test-method development); (iv) accelerated depreciation / one-time expensing election for research and testing equipment under current accelerated-depreciation rules; (v) the 15% reduced EIT rate election available only where the JV qualifies as an encouraged industry enterprise or an advanced-technology service enterprise within the designated pilot zones; and (vi) the foreign-tax-credit election on notional cross-border service income. Hybrid mismatch elections are unavailable; anti-deferral and transfer-pricing rules apply to cross-border service payments to the Taiwan parent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover for mandatory General VAT taxpayer registration. A small-scale taxpayer with annual sales not exceeding RMB 5,000,000 may elect simplified taxation at 3% (temporarily reduced to 1% during the relief window); monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 (or quarterly RMB 300,000) are wholly VAT-exempt for small-scale taxpayers. Because the JV performs high-value UN38.3 testing contracts and must issue full credit-bearing invoices to corporate clients, it is registered as a General VAT taxpayer at 6% on testing, certification, and electromagnetic compliance fees.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Battery UN38.3 drop and electromagnetic testing is NOT eligible for export zero-rating under the 财税〔2016〕36号 Annex 4 positive list. The zero-rate categories are limited to international transport, offshore R&D, design, software, information-systems, consultancy (certain classes), and integrated-circuit design and testing services 'completely consumed outside China.' Physical battery-safety drop testing executed within a PRC laboratory is a domestic place-of-supply service consumed at the location of the testing bench; the STA negative-list position treats such testing as fully domestically consumed and therefore taxable at 6%, even where the contracting principal is the Taiwan parent, all fees are paid in foreign exchange, and the technical report is transmitted offshore. Zero-rating (0%) or exemption (免税) would be available only in the exceptional structure where the goods being tested are never brought into China and the JV's engineers perform the test protocols at an overseas facility or remotely on samples located abroad; in that case the complete-consumption test is satisfied, the Cross-Border Service Exemption/Zero-Rating Filing is admitted, and input VAT on the related service costs becomes refundable or creditable under the general-taxpayer mechanism. For the standard import-to-China sample flow, no refund path exists and the 6% VAT is the final cost.

Mandatory fully digitalized electronic invoices (全面数字化电子发票, 数电票) are issued through the STA centralized E-Tax Bureau platform under Golden Tax Phase IV; physical paper invoices are phased out. Every invoice is assigned a digitized serial number in real time and cleared against the STA big-data cloud before issuance; the UKey-based identity authentication is compulsory. Invoice amounts, VAT payable, and declared sales are automatically cross-reconciled with bank settlement data, corporate income tax returns, and the foreign-exchange payment records held by banks. Cross-border service fee remittances require the Service Trade and Other Items Foreign Exchange Payment Tax Record Form (服务贸易等项目对外支付税务备案表) to be filed with STA prior to the bank effecting remittance to the Taiwan parent or overseas recipient. Failure to issue a compliant 数电票 or any mismatch between invoice data and bank inflows triggers immediate system alert, taxpayer image downgrade, and invoice-quota suspension.

The STA integrates Golden Tax invoice data, Customs trade data, and bank foreign-exchange receipts; any testing-services zero-rating or exemption claim is audited against the physical location of the test equipment, the customs entry records of the battery samples, and the 180-day FX repatriation trail. Where zero-rating/exemption is successfully claimed and later found ineligible: the full VAT output tax becomes immediately payable (retroactive to the invoice issuance date), daily late-payment surcharges accrue at 0.05% per day, and the administrative penalty ranges from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid pursuant to the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection (Article 63) and the VAT Law implementing rules. The JV is additionally exposed to: (i) loss of the audit-based collection mode with forced deemed-profit taxation; (ii) freeze and reduction of its 数电票 invoice quota; (iii) inclusion in the serious-tax-violation taxpayer blacklist with joint disciplinary sanctions on its legal representative; (iv) clawback of any VAT refunds plus interest in the currency of the refund; and (v) criminal liability for fraudulent export-tax-refund or false-invoice offenses (虚开增值税专用发票, tax fraud) where the misrepresentation is deliberate, carrying penalties up to life imprisonment and fines in addition to the tax clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan maintains a de facto fully convertible current account: receipts and payments for testing services may be freely converted through a DBU after the filing of a CBC foreign-exchange declaration (外匯收支或交易申報書) for each transaction with a TWD equivalent of NT$500,000 or more. The capital account is selectively restricted: a mainland Chinese investor must obtain prior approval from the MOEA Investment Commission (投審會) under the 大陸地區人民來臺投資許可辦法, after which the remitted equity capital can be converted at market rates without separate CBC conversion approval. Profit and dividend repatriation to the PRC parent is permissible after audited financial statements and a valid dividend resolution, subject to withholding tax settlement, the CBC declaration, and confirmation that no unfulfilled investment conditions remain. A practical structural hurdle is the absence of a deep direct interbank TWD/CNY market; settlement is typically triangulated through USD, or through the cross-strait RMB clearing arrangement operated by Bank of China Taipei Branch (中國銀行臺北分行), which prices TWD/RMB on a deliverable basis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

PRC side (parent JV): export proceeds must be repatriated to China (調回境內) within the trade-receipt verification window normally set at 90 days from customs clearance, with deferred-receipt registration extending the window to 180 days and advance receipts arriving 30+ days before shipment requiring registration in the SAFE trade monitoring system; no mandatory conversion applies, so the JV may retain 100% of receipts in its foreign-currency settlement account or convert into CNY at its discretion. Taiwan side (operating subsidiary): under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act and CBC implementing rules, the subsidiary must negotiate service export proceeds by either selling the foreign exchange to a DBU or placing it into a foreign-currency deposit account at a DBU within 180 days from completion of the testing service (invoice date for services); retention beyond 180 days requires a documented application to the CBC. Because laboratory billings are predominantly EUR- and USD-denominated from EU battery and electronics manufacturers, treasury should accumulate balances in the DBU foreign-currency account and execute periodic EUR/USD to TWD spot conversions aligned with payroll and opex cycles.

Central bank supervision: Dual-jurisdiction control: (i) for the PRC parent JV, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange of the People's Republic of China (国家外汇管理局, SAFE) governs capital-account convertibility, outward direct investment registration and cross-border trade verification; (ii) for the Taiwan operating entity, the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (中央銀行, CBC) is the monetary and foreign-exchange authority under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act (管理外匯條例) and supervises all designated foreign-exchange banks (指定外匯銀行, DBU). The Financial Supervisory Commission (金融監督管理委員會, FSC) supplements bank licensing and AML/CFT enforcement in Taiwan.

Box 06 (Tax on Income) Final corporate income tax at 20% after applying exemptions, credits, and carryforwards. The JV self-assesses and pays; foreign shareholders receive dividends subject to separate withholding. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 1% of the assessed tax (maximum 12% of the tax amount), plus interest; failure to file after an official reminder leads to an estimated assessment and an additional penalty up to 3 times the tax under Article 108 of the Income Tax Act.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File (transfer pricing report) is required when annual controlled transactions exceed NT$50 million; Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds NT$30 billion; Country-by-Country Report obligations attach at approximately NT$27 billion of group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan applies a general anti-avoidance rule (Article 12-1 of the Tax Evasion and Penalty Act) and Article 43-1 transfer-pricing recharacterization powers. Although no formal circular-style ESR statute exists, the testing laboratory must maintain a real and substantive presence: physical premises, qualified staff, testing equipment, actual operational decisions in Taiwan, and credible documentation of testing activities. Artificial booking of profits without such substance may lead to recharacterization and penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Falsification of Accounts)

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under Article 41 of the Tax Evasion and Penalty Act: imprisonment up to 5 years, detention, or a fine up to NT$6 million; together with reassessment, delayed-payment interest, and a monthly surcharge of 1% up to 12%.

Director personal liability: Directors, general managers, and accounting officers who knowingly organize or consent to the fraud may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and imposed fines; a travel ban may be requested under Article 24; for intentional concealment, courts may pierce the corporate veil for civil restitution.
Shell Company and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization under Article 12-1 and TP adjustment under Article 43-1; the tax authority may reallocate income to the true economic owner, impose additional tax at 20%, add late-interest, and levy administrative fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who permit the entity to operate as a shell may be personally assessed for constructive dividends; the corporate veil may be pierced for unpaid tax liabilities where the company is found to be a sham, and officers can face imprisonment for tax evasion.
AML / Customer Due Diligence Neglect

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Control Act, administrative fines up to NT$10,000,000, possible suspension or revocation of the laboratory accreditation, and FIU asset-freeze orders.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers who knowingly fail to report or intentionally conceal suspicious transactions face criminal imprisonment (up to 2 years under the regulatory reporting provisions and up to 7 years for active acts of money laundering), personal criminal fines, and travel restrictions.
Withholding Tax Neglect on Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: The payer is held liable for the un-withheld tax amount plus late-payment interest and administrative penalties; penalties may range from 10% to 30% of the unpaid sum, with a maximum fine of NT$1,000,000 in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who approve such payments without adequate due diligence may be personally liable for the withheld tax and penalties; in cases of fraudulent avoidance, criminal liability under the Tax Evasion and Penalty Act may extend to imprisonment.

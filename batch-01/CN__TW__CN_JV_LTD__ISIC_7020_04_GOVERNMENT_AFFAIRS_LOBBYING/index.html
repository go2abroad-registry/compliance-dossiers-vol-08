Procedure: (1) Apply to the Ministry of Economic Affairs, Department of Commercial Affairs for approval of a branch office or a Taiwan-incorporated subsidiary; submit the foreign parent's incorporation certificate, articles of incorporation, board resolution authorising the Taiwan establishment, and a notarised power of attorney, with authentication as required. Available election forms

Taiwan does not recognise US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch flow-through elections. A foreign investor may choose the corporate form of a Taiwan-incorporated subsidiary, which is a Taiwan-resident profit-seeking enterprise taxed on its worldwide income, or a Taiwan branch, which is a non-resident profit-seeking enterprise taxed on Taiwan-source income only; this choice is made through corporate-law registration, not a tax classification election. Other available elections are limited to procedural VAT/invoice matters, such as the use of electronic invoices and the filing of business tax returns under the credit method.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan dollar is generally convertible for bona fide current-account transactions, including trade in services, operating expenses, and approved profit distributions. Taiwan does not operate a general capital-account convertibility regime equivalent to unrestricted offshore movement of capital. Capital inflows, equity funding, shareholder loans, acquisitions, and repatriation of capital may require investment approval, documentary evidence, bank reporting, and transaction-purpose verification. A Taiwan company may distribute dividends only from legally distributable earnings after statutory accounts, tax clearance or withholding compliance, and corporate approvals. Dividends, service fees, interest, royalties, liquidation proceeds, and capital reductions paid to non-residents are subject to applicable Taiwan withholding tax, treaty relief conditions, transfer-pricing support, and authorized-bank processing. A China-linked investor presents an additional eligibility and national-security risk: Mainland Chinese investment, ownership, management participation, beneficial ownership, and service arrangements may be restricted or require prior approval, and a Taiwan bank may decline onboarding even where general foreign-investment rules would otherwise permit the structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be surrendered to the banking system or converted into New Taiwan dollars at a fixed ratio. Export and service-export receipts may generally be retained in foreign currency or converted through an authorized foreign-exchange bank, subject to accurate customs, invoice, contract, and transaction-purpose records. Foreign-exchange declarations or reporting may apply where the transaction is not a routine trade receipt, where the amount exceeds applicable thresholds, or where the transaction falls within CBC reporting rules. Banks may require evidence of the underlying export or service transaction, and suspicious, irregular, related-party, or China-linked receipts may be escalated under AML and cross-strait controls. No mandatory conversion percentage should be assumed; the bank's internal documentary and reporting requirements must be confirmed before implementation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), operating with the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) for banking supervision and the Ministry of Economic Affairs Investment Commission (MOEAIC) for foreign investment approvals. Because the proposed investor is identified as China-linked, the Act Governing Relations between the People of the Taiwan Area and the Mainland Area and its implementing regulations must also be assessed before account opening, capital injection, or remittance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium for routine foreign-currency payments and treasury technology; low for core operating accounts, capital escrow, shareholder funding, or regulated collection of third-party funds. Taiwan does not provide a simple EU-style passportable electronic-money institution regime. Electronic payment institutions and payment processors are regulated under Taiwan financial laws and may be limited in stored-value issuance, payment collection, foreign-exchange activity, remittance, settlement, and third-party funds handling. An offshore digital EMI cannot be assumed to provide a lawful substitute for a Taiwan-authorized bank or permitted payment institution. The JV should use a Taiwan authorized bank for capital injection, payroll, taxes, dividends, trade receipts, and material supplier payments, with any fintech limited to bank-integrated technology services after legal and bank approval. Government-relations and lobbying receipts require enhanced source-of-funds and underlying-service documentation and must not be routed through accounts designed to conceal the payer, beneficial owner, or purpose.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Mainland China parent, shareholder, director, controller, or ultimate beneficial owner associated with a Taiwan entity.

Root cause: Mainland China parent, shareholder, director, controller, or ultimate beneficial owner associated with a Taiwan entity.

Defense evidence: Provide a complete ownership chart from the Taiwan JV through every intermediate entity to each natural-person ultimate beneficial owner; certified constitutional documents; shareholder register; passports and residential evidence; board and shareholder resolutions; evidence of nationality and place of incorporation; MOEAIC or other competent-authority investment approval; cross-strait approval analysis; sanctions and politically exposed person screening; proof that no prohibited Mainland investment, nominee ownership, or undisclosed control exists; and a written legal memorandum addressing the Act Governing Relations between the People of the Taiwan Area and the Mainland Area.
Government-affairs, lobbying, policy-advisory, or foreign-stakeholder engagement activity classified as medium-risk enhanced.

Root cause: Government-affairs, lobbying, policy-advisory, or foreign-stakeholder engagement activity classified as medium-risk enhanced.

Defense evidence: Provide a detailed business plan; statutory license, registration, or filing evidence under the Taiwan Lobbying Act and other applicable professional or sector rules; client and payer list; signed engagement letters; scope-of-work descriptions; fee schedules; invoices; deliverable examples; conflict-of-interest policy; anti-bribery and anti-corruption policy; political-exposure screening; beneficial-owner information for each client; evidence that payments are commercial advisory fees rather than political contributions, facilitation payments, or undisclosed intermediary compensation; and transaction monitoring procedures.
Inbound capital described ambiguously as equity, shareholder loan, service fee, or intercompany settlement.

Root cause: Inbound capital described ambiguously as equity, shareholder loan, service fee, or intercompany settlement.

Defense evidence: Submit the executed joint-venture agreement, articles, capitalization table, investment approval, board resolution, subscription agreement or loan agreement, funds-flow memorandum, remitter account statement, proof of source of funds, tax analysis, transfer-pricing support, bank payment-purpose declaration, and confirmation that the remittance amount matches the approved capital or contractual obligation. Maintain separate accounting and bank references for equity, debt, revenue, reimbursements, and expenses.
Unusual third-party remitter, payment from an offshore account, layered intermediary, or payment inconsistent with the invoiced customer.

Root cause: Unusual third-party remitter, payment from an offshore account, layered intermediary, or payment inconsistent with the invoiced customer.

Defense evidence: Provide a three-party or agency agreement explaining the payment path; full identity and beneficial ownership documents for the remitter, customer, and intermediary; commercial rationale for third-party settlement; invoices and acceptance evidence; bank statements showing the source of funds; sanctions, AML, and adverse-media screening; tax treatment; and a written bank pre-clearance where practicable. Reject unexplained third-party payments and cash settlements.
High-value cross-border service receipts without tangible deliverables or with vague descriptions such as consulting or facilitation.

Root cause: High-value cross-border service receipts without tangible deliverables or with vague descriptions such as consulting or facilitation.

Defense evidence: Maintain the master services agreement, work orders, timesheets, meeting records, reports, correspondence, travel records, deliverables, customer acceptance, invoice, tax invoice, pricing benchmark, transfer-pricing file, and evidence of the personnel and resources performing the work. Use precise payment descriptions and ensure the activity is legally permitted under Taiwan licensing, lobbying, public-affairs, anti-corruption, and foreign-agent restrictions.
Potential sanctions, export-control, dual-use, military, state-owned-enterprise, or politically exposed counterparty exposure.

Root cause: Potential sanctions, export-control, dual-use, military, state-owned-enterprise, or politically exposed counterparty exposure.

Profit remittance, management fee, royalty, interest, or capital reduction to a non-resident shareholder.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% corporate income tax on Taiwan-sourced taxable income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a nonresident individual, foreign company, or other nonresident recipient, subject to applicable exemptions or legally effective treaty relief. Dividends to Taiwan-resident companies are generally governed by separate domestic rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally filed from 1 May through 31 May of the following year for a calendar-year taxpayer. A taxpayer with a non-calendar fiscal year files within the fifth month after fiscal year-end. An extension may be available where statutory conditions are met.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally filed during September for the first six months of a calendar year, subject to the statutory filing window and available exemptions. The provisional payment is credited against annual corporate income tax.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Tax withheld is generally remitted within the statutory period following payment, allocation, or constructive payment, commonly by the tenth day of the following month. Annual withholding statements are generally issued and filed by the statutory January deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan generally requires a Local File for reportable controlled transactions unless an exemption or simplified documentation rule applies. A principal threshold for comprehensive transfer-pricing documentation is annual net revenue and non-operating income of at least NT$3 billion or aggregate cross-border controlled transactions of at least NT$1.5 billion, with transaction-type and other statutory tests also relevant. A Master File is generally relevant where the enterprise meets the NT$3 billion revenue threshold and the applicable aggregate cross-border controlled-transaction threshold. Country-by-Country Reporting may apply to Taiwan constituent entities of multinational enterprise groups meeting the NT$27 billion consolidated revenue threshold. Thresholds and exemptions must be checked for the relevant fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance is nevertheless relevant under transfer-pricing rules, anti-avoidance principles, beneficial-ownership analysis, source-of-income rules, tax treaty access, deductibility, and controlled foreign company rules. The entity should demonstrate Taiwan decision-making, personnel, premises, functions, assets, risks, books, bank activity, and commercial purpose appropriate to its government-relations advisory and lobbying activities. Artificial conduit arrangements, circular payments, nominee directors, and undocumented shareholder services are high-risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unauthorized Mainland China investment or prohibited foreign investment

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, orders to withdraw or correct the investment, suspension or revocation of investment approval, forced disposal of shares, and possible restrictions on future investment. The amount depends on the violated statute and conduct.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face administrative liability, civil damages, disqualification consequences, and criminal liability where false filings, evasion of approval, or prohibited conduct is involved. Travel restrictions or imprisonment are not automatic and require statutory grounds and judicial process.
Bribery and improper influence involving public officials

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Taiwan anti-corruption and Criminal Code provisions; penalties may include imprisonment and fines, forfeiture of illicit benefits, debarment, and corporate administrative consequences. Penalty level depends on the official's status, benefit amount, intent, and transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, employees, agents, and beneficial owners may be prosecuted personally. Corporate authorization, knowledge, acquiescence, or failure to maintain controls may support corporate and individual liability. Civil restitution and forfeiture may apply.
Unregistered or noncompliant lobbying and government-affairs activity

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, orders to correct, public disclosure, suspension or cancellation of registration, and possible referral for criminal investigation where bribery, fraud, or false documents are involved.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons and lobbyists may incur personal administrative or criminal liability for false reports, concealed principals, or prohibited conduct. Corporate veil piercing is fact-specific and is not automatic.
Tax evasion and false accounting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, late-payment interest, administrative fines, denial of deductions or credits, compulsory collection, and criminal prosecution for intentional evasion or fraudulent methods under Taiwan tax laws.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons, accountants, signatories, and participants may face criminal imprisonment and fines, civil restitution, forfeiture, and tax collection against responsible persons where statutory conditions are satisfied. Travel bans may be imposed in appropriate collection or criminal proceedings; they are not an automatic tax consequence.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and undocumented related-party services

Statutory sanction: Taiwan tax authority adjustment to taxable income, denial of deductions, deemed income or withholding adjustments, penalties for inaccurate returns, interest, and possible anti-avoidance review.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may incur liability for intentional false records, fraud, breach of fiduciary duty, or participation in evasion. Ordinary transfer-pricing adjustments alone do not automatically create imprisonment or veil piercing.
Anti-money laundering and suspicious payment activity

Statutory sanction: Suspicious transaction reporting, account freezing, confiscation, administrative fines, business restrictions, and criminal liability for money laundering or predicate offenses. Penalties depend on amount, knowledge, concealment, and participation.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, employees, and agents may be personally prosecuted where they knowingly participate, facilitate, conceal, or fail to perform legally required controls. Assets may be confiscated and civil restitution may be ordered.
Sanctions, export-control, and prohibited-party exposure

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties, license suspension, transaction prohibition, asset blocking, confiscation, and criminal prosecution under applicable Taiwan sanctions, national-security, trade, and export-control laws.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face criminal liability, fines, confiscation, management disqualification, and civil claims. Imprisonment and travel restrictions depend on the specific offense and judicial order.
Corporate governance and fiduciary breach

Statutory sanction: Civil damages, disgorgement, shareholder claims, accounting sanctions, administrative penalties, and criminal breach-of-trust or misappropriation charges where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be jointly liable for damages, restitution, disgorgement, and criminal penalties. Veil piercing requires abuse of the company, commingling, fraud, or another legally recognized basis; it is not presumed from ownership or control alone.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and its electronic tax portal (e-Tax Bureau / 电子税务局); in Taiwan jurisdiction, the competent authority is the National Taxation Bureau (財政部各地區國稅局) under the Ministry of Finance, with the integrated tax declaration portal (電子申報繳稅服務).

        
E-government filing portal
For a WFOE established in mainland China, the foreign investor must first obtain a business license (营业执照) from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), after which the company must register for tax with the competent STA branch within 30 days using the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) as the tax identification number; the WFOE must complete taxpayer registration for corporate income tax, VAT, and other applicable taxes, and obtain the tax registration certificate data via the e-Tax Bureau. For Taiwan-related operability, if the entity maintains a Taiwan branch or fixed place of business, a separate Taiwan tax registration with the National Taxation Bureau is required, using the company's Taiwan Business Registration Number (統一編號).

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a WFOE established in mainland China, the foreign investor must first obtain a business license (营业执照) from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), after which the company must register for tax with the competent STA branch within 30 days using the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) as the tax identification number; the WFOE must complete taxpayer registration for corporate income tax, VAT, and other applicable taxes, and obtain the tax registration certificate data via the e-Tax Bureau. For Taiwan-related operability, if the entity maintains a Taiwan branch or fixed place of business, a separate Taiwan tax registration with the National Taxation Bureau is required, using the company's Taiwan Business Registration Number (統一編號).

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
China does not provide check-the-box elections; corporate income tax is levied at entity level on the WFOE. The WFOE may elect general VAT taxpayer status for input VAT crediting. Taiwan branch profits are subject to Taiwan entity-level taxation with a possible tax credit for Taiwan-sourced taxes against mainland CIT, subject to cross-strait tax rules and anti-avoidance limitations.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
A person engaged in taxable supplies with annual business volume exceeding CNY 5,000,000 is generally required to register as a general VAT taxpayer; below that threshold, simplified taxpayer status applies at 3% collection rate, though port stevedoring services are taxable supplies and a WFOE will normally register as a general taxpayer to claim credits.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
China's export VAT zero-rating applies to exported goods; port stevedoring services supplied in China to non-resident carriers for international shipping or for goods in transit are generally zero-rated if the services are provided in connection with export and paid in foreign currency. Taiwan zero-rating applies to services rendered in Taiwan that are used or enjoyed outside Taiwan, and to services related to international transport.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Taiwan operates a generally liberalized foreign-exchange regime. The New Taiwan dollar is broadly convertible for ordinary trade, service, and other current-account transactions, subject to anti-money-laundering controls, documentary verification, bank reporting, and restrictions applicable to particular counterparties or transactions. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment, capital injections, equity transfers, intercompany financing, guarantees, and certain securities transactions, may require prior approval, registration, reporting, or bank documentation depending on the investor, transaction type, amount, counterparty, and applicable nationality or cross-strait rules. A Taiwan operating company may generally remit dividends, interest, principal, service fees, royalties, and other permitted amounts offshore after satisfying corporate approvals, withholding-tax obligations, accounting support, bank review, and any required investment or foreign-exchange filings. The submitted data contains a jurisdictional inconsistency: the sovereign-country code is CN, while the jurisdiction is TW and the entity is described as a CN WFOE. This blueprint treats Taiwan as the operating jurisdiction and assumes that the entity is lawfully established or registered there. If the entity is instead a Mainland China WFOE, the People's Bank of China and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange regime must be substituted, and Taiwan-specific conclusions must not be used.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Taiwan generally does not impose a universal statutory requirement that export proceeds be surrendered to the CBC or converted into New Taiwan dollars within a fixed period, and no general mandatory conversion ratio applies to ordinary export receipts. Exporters may ordinarily retain foreign-currency proceeds in approved foreign-currency accounts or convert them through authorized banks. Banks must verify the underlying commercial transaction, retain or obtain relevant customs, invoice, bill-of-lading, contract, and settlement evidence, and complete required foreign-exchange reporting. Proceeds involving restricted countries, sanctioned parties, unusual payment structures, related-party settlements, or cross-strait counterparties may be subject to enhanced review, licensing, reporting, or prohibition. The company should reconcile export receivables, customs declarations, bank credits, and invoices and maintain evidence supporting the business purpose and beneficial ownership of each receipt. If the actual entity is in Mainland China, export proceeds, foreign-currency retention, and conversion rules are materially different and must be assessed under SAFE rules, including applicable export-receipt verification and foreign-exchange settlement requirements.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC); the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC), Banking Bureau, supervises banks and relevant financial institutions; the Ministry of Economic Affairs Investment Review Division administers applicable foreign investment registration and approval matters.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taiwan (R.O.C.) – Taxation Administration, operating through regional National Taxation Bureaus; online electronic portal: Tax E-Portal at https://www.tax.nat.gov.tw

E-government filing portal

Foreign non-resident entities establishing a limited company or branch in Taiwan must obtain a Uniform Business Number (UB, the statutory TIN) by filing 'Application for Profit-Seeking Enterprise Tax Registration' with the local National Taxation Bureau within 15 days after company/branch registration. The application (written or via the Tax E-Portal) must include: (a) the foreign incorporation certificate or equivalent legalized official registration document; (b) the articles of incorporation and the Taiwan company registration approval from the Ministry of Economic Affairs Investment Commission (where foreign investment approval applies); (c) lease agreement or ownership certificate for the Taiwan business address; (d) letter of appointment of the Taiwan-based tax manager or certified public accountant, plus the manager's identity document; and (e) a declaration of non-resident ownership status and expected main place of effective management. On completion, the Bureau issues the UB and a taxation seal. A separate simplified tax registration path exists for offshore foreign electronic service providers with no Taiwan fixed place of business: such providers must register online for a Business Tax TIN when Taiwan-source turnover exceeds TWD 480,000 per year, appointing a domestic tax representative and filing business tax returns quarterly.

Available election forms

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For Taiwan-registered profit-seeking enterprises, no monetary threshold exempts registration; registration is mandatory prior to commencing business. Small-scale enterprises with average monthly taxable sales of TWD 200,000 or below may opt for a simplified 1% rate and quarterly filing. For non-resident foreign electronic service providers, mandatory Business Tax registration and collection arise when their annual Taiwan-source digital service sales exceed TWD 480,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rate export treatment is available under Article 8 of the Business Tax Act for (i) goods physically exported from Taiwan customs territory; and (ii) services rendered by a Taiwan business if the services are supplied to non-resident customers and the consideration will be used wholly outside Taiwan, generally evidenced by a foreign-currency settlement contract, an export declaration (for goods) or a service agreement with the foreign recipient, plus foreign exchange repatriation receipts. For an ISIC 7020 AEO certification and security auditing service sold directly to an offshore client, the service must qualify as a cross-border supply that is effectively consumed offshore, must be invoiced to the non-resident party, and the wire-transfer or Wise remittance must be settled in foreign currency to the Taiwan entity.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) bearing Taiwan Customs release stamp/electronic release code
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill / multimodal transport document)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List for goods; for services, the service contract and descriptive work statement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank foreign exchange settlement / receipt advice) proving collection of export consideration in foreign currency
  • Foreign exchange repatriation reconciliation statement substantiating receipt within the statutory 180-day deadline

Taiwan enforces a Uniform Invoice system. VAT-registered entities must issue Uniform Invoices for every B2B and B2C taxable supply; the Ministry of Finance requires business entities (including AEO-certified enterprises) to implement electronic Uniform Invoices and transmit invoice data to the E-Invoice Integration Service platform using the Turnkey API or the approved cloud invoice medium. Transmission to the corporate network occurs in near-real time (by end of the tax filing period); every e-invoice receives a unique lottery number and validation code, enabling anti-fraud reconciliation. Non-compliance with the electronic invoicing mandate is penalized by the National Taxation Bureau under the Business Tax Act.

The National Taxation Bureau will invalidate zero-rating absent a valid Customs Export Declaration or credible foreign exchange settlement proof; the 5% business tax is then assessed on the invoice amount, together with late interest from the issuance date. Under the Business Tax Act, non-compliance involving false invoices, forged bills of lading, or fictitious remittance certificates constitutes tax evasion punishable by a fine of from one to ten times the evaded tax and, under the Tax Collection Act, criminal liability for the responsible persons. The tax authority may reassess the previous five tax years (extended to seven years for fraud) and claw back any VAT refunds already paid without awaiting the exporter's re-filing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the People's Bank of China and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange, the Renminbi is convertible for current account items such as trade payments, service fees, travel, wages, dividends and interest through licensed designated banks without prior SAFE approval, subject to genuine underlying documentation, bank scrutiny and balance-of-payments declaration. Capital account transactions are still heavily controlled. For the Chinese joint venture, inbound equity capital from the Taiwan investor requires approval under the Mainland-Taiwan investment regime, SAFE foreign exchange registration, a dedicated foreign exchange capital settlement account and a post-injection usage restriction requiring funds to be used for bona fide operating purposes within the permitted negative list. There is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds after the 2012 liberalization; exporters may retain foreign currency in a foreign exchange settlement account or convert voluntarily to RMB with the presenting bank. Profit remittance to the Taiwan shareholder is treated as a current account income item but requires an annual audit, tax reservation, withholding certificate, board resolution and seat of effective management review. In practical terms, the entity can trade and settle current account items freely, but every capital account inflow, outflow, capital conversion, dividend declaration and cross-border debt transaction is subject to registration, verification and bank-level compliance clearance. The bank cannot disburse capital account RMB through simple e-wallet or fintech rails; funds must pass through the SAFE-aligned settlement pending payment account system with field-level monitoring of counterparties and usage. Any deviation from this convertibility architecture will create a serious AML and regulatory breach for the designated bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export foreign exchange receipts from goods and services must be repatriated to a PRC domestic bank and reported through SAFE's trade monitoring and direct investment systems. The statutory repatriation timeline is 30 calendar days from the date the foreign exchange collection is received, with approved extension windows of up to 180 days for goods exports under documented deferred payment terms. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into RMB; the exporting entity may retain the proceeds in a domestic foreign currency account or voluntarily convert at the market rate through a designated bank. For the AEO certification and supply chain audit services rendered by the Chinese JV, the service-export payment received from Taiwan must be matched to a signed service contract, tax invoice, work completion report and SAFE service trade declaration before the bank will release the funds.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise are not viable as a primary clearing rail for the PRC joint venture. They are not licensed as banks or electronic payment institutions under PBOC and SAFE and cannot lawfully open RMB settlement accounts, receive capital injections, execute currency conversion on behalf of a mainland entity, or disburse funds into the Chinese CNAPS clearing system. Any use of Wise by the Taiwan shareholder outside China is permissible only as an ancillary remittance tool that ultimately delivers into the JV's specified SWIFT-recognized bank account; however, the main RMB treasury ledger, capital verification flow and corporate expense payments must reside at a licensed domestic commercial bank. Attempting to use Wise for the JV's direct domestic disbursements would generate unlicensed payment business red flags and undermine the AML and tax compliance posture.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Before any remittance, the Taiwan investor must obtain the PRC investment approval certificate, SAFE foreign exchange registration and obtain an FDI business registration code through the local market supervision administration. The Taiwan investor's bank then sends the capital injection by SWIFT MT103 with a genuine direct investment purpose code for inbound FDI equity capital, quoting the Chinese JV's SAFE FDI file number and the approved name of the account-holding bank. Funds must be credited strictly into the JV's foreign exchange capital settlement account at the designated PRC commercial bank. After credit, the bank requires a SAFE declaration, the original remittance advice, the approved feasibility study or capital contribution resolution, and the notarized cross-strait verification evidence. The capital amount cannot be converted to RMB until the bank confirms the underlying transaction meets the negative list and the actual business principle; then funds are moved into a settlement-pending payment account for approved operating payments. A licensed PRC certified public accountant must then issue the statutory capital verification report, which becomes the final evidence for the business registration and tax bureau records. The entire process must avoid any third-party pooling account, fintech EMI intermediary or same-day offshore sweep structure.For service payments such as AEO audit sub-contracting, software licensing, security testing and international consultancy fees, the JV will use SWIFT MT103 transfers through its designated bank with the service trade foreign exchange declaration, original contract, invoice, proof of service delivery, tax withholding certificate and the China BOP declaration form. For physical security equipment or supply chain hardware purchases related to client audit work, the JV may use commercial letters of credit and documentary collections as an alternative to open account settlement. The payment rails must remain inside either SWIFT for foreign currency or CNAPS for RMB; no outbound USD/RMB disbursement may be completed through an offshore EMI or a non-bank wallet. Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under Taiwan Company Act art. 20(2) and MOEA implementing rules, a non-public company is legally required to have its annual financial statements audited and certified by an independent certified public accountant when paid-in capital reaches or exceeds NT$30 million, approximately USD 950,000. CPA audit is also mandatory for all companies whose shares are publicly traded on TWSE or TPEx or otherwise subject to Securities and Futures Bureau oversight; for all banks, insurers, securities, futures, bills-finance and financial holding companies; and for any foreign-invested JV where the Investment Commission imposes an audit condition in the investment approval. In tax filings, the National Taxation Bureau may require a CPA-certified profit-seeking enterprise income tax return where annual total revenue exceeds NT$100 million, approximately USD 3.1 million, even if the JV is below the capital audit threshold. Taiwan has no general statutory headcount audit trigger; headcount affects labor and payroll filings, not the audit duty.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a nonresident corporate shareholder, subject to any applicable statutory exemption or treaty relief. Taiwan and mainland China do not have a comprehensive income tax treaty equivalent to a bilateral DTA; treaty relief should not be assumed for a mainland China shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a calendar-year enterprise, the annual return and final tax payment are generally due from 1 May through 31 May of the following year, being the fifth month after fiscal year-end. A non-calendar fiscal year generally files within the corresponding fifth month after its fiscal year-end. Provisional payment is generally due by the end of the ninth month of the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan Local File documentation is generally required where the enterprise meets applicable thresholds, including annual net operating revenue and non-operating income of at least NT$500 million or annual controlled transaction amounts meeting the prescribed thresholds, commonly including aggregate related-party transactions of at least NT$200 million. A Master File is generally required where annual net operating revenue and non-operating income reach at least NT$3 billion and the prescribed cross-border related-party conditions are satisfied. Country-by-Country Reporting generally applies to a Taiwan constituent entity of an MNE group meeting the NT$27 billion consolidated group revenue threshold, subject to reporting and notification rules.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate an OECD-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime applicable to every entity. Substance is nevertheless relevant under transfer-pricing rules, anti-avoidance principles, beneficial-ownership analysis, permanent-establishment rules, controlled foreign company rules, and general tax assessment powers. The Taiwan JV should maintain real personnel, decision-making, premises, systems, risk control, customer records, audit workpapers, and commercial capacity proportionate to its declared supply-chain audit activities. A company that merely invoices mainland China or foreign customers while material functions and risks are performed offshore is exposed to profit reallocation, withholding, permanent-establishment, and sham-transaction challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, late-payment charges, administrative penalties, compulsory collection, and potential criminal prosecution under Taiwan tax and criminal laws. Tax evasion penalties may be measured by reference to the tax avoided and may include imprisonment for serious or intentional conduct.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face criminal liability, civil restitution for unpaid tax and damages, asset seizure, restrictions on departure or travel in collection and criminal proceedings, and imprisonment where statutory criminal elements are established.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustment to arm's-length income, denial of deductions, additional corporate income tax, withholding tax, interest, documentation penalties, and possible audit expansion to related years and entities.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible managers may incur civil liability for losses caused by deliberate misreporting and may face criminal exposure if the conduct constitutes tax fraud, falsification, breach of trust, or other offenses.
Withholding Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the payer, surcharges, interest, administrative penalties, and potential restrictions on tax clearance or refunds.

Director personal liability: The responsible person and withholding agent may be personally exposed to statutory collection liability and civil claims; intentional concealment may create criminal tax exposure.
Beneficial Ownership and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or statutory relief, withholding at the full domestic rate, recovery of the shortfall, penalties, interest, anti-avoidance assessment, and possible fraud prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors, tax agents, and signatories may face civil restitution, professional sanctions, criminal liability for false documents, and travel restrictions where legally ordered.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions, denial of deductions, transfer-pricing adjustment, permanent-establishment analysis, withholding assessments, penalties, and possible deregistration or licensing consequences if corporate registration information is false.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for creditor and tax losses, breach-of-duty claims, and criminal exposure for fraud, false registration, or breach of trust.
AML, Sanctions, and Beneficial-Owner Screening

Statutory sanction: Transaction rejection, account suspension, suspicious-transaction reporting, regulatory investigation, administrative fines, asset freezing, forfeiture, and criminal sanctions under applicable anti-money-laundering and sanctions laws.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal fines, criminal prosecution, asset restraint, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for knowing participation, facilitation, concealment, or negligent statutory noncompliance where applicable.
Customs and AEO Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessment, duty and tax recovery, administrative fines, seizure, suspension or revocation of customs privileges, loss of AEO eligibility, and criminal customs liability for smuggling or intentional false declarations.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face joint or individual civil liability for customs losses and criminal liability, including imprisonment, where intentional smuggling, document falsification, or evasion is proven.
Unlicensed or Misleading Professional Services

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, prohibition orders, customer claims, contract invalidity or termination, professional disciplinary action, and criminal liability where unauthorized practice or fraud is established.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face civil damages, restitution, disqualification consequences, and criminal prosecution for fraud, false certification, or unauthorized practice.
Permanent Establishment and Unreported Taiwan Activities

Statutory sanction: Retroactive income tax assessment, withholding tax, penalties, interest, registration requirements, and possible enforcement against the Taiwan entity or local agents.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers may incur civil liability for tax and customer losses and criminal exposure where the structure involves deliberate concealment or false filings.
Corporate Governance and Related-Party Abuse

Statutory sanction: Dividend recovery, shareholder and creditor claims, tax reassessment, fines, transaction invalidation, director removal, and damages under Taiwan company law.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for restitution and creditor losses and may face criminal breach-of-trust or misappropriation charges where statutory elements are satisfied.

Applicable AML statute: Taiwan: Money Laundering Control Act (MLCA) as amended, Company Act (Articles 386-1 and 387-1) requiring UBO registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA), and Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) regulations on AML/CFT. Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a Single Family Office (SFO) engaged in private investment holding, a virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally not permissible. Taiwan's tax authorities (National Taxation Bureau) and banking institutions require a physical registered office where records are kept, board meetings can be held, and regulatory inspections can be conducted. The Company Act mandates that a company must have a registered office within Taiwan, and the address must be a physical location where the company's statutory books and seals are maintained. Banks, when opening corporate accounts, will conduct on-site visits to verify the physical presence. A virtual office may be acceptable for administrative purposes but will not satisfy the 'place of effective management' test for tax residency and may trigger enhanced scrutiny. Therefore, a physical office lease is strongly recommended.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance (Taiwan), administered by the National Taxation Bureau having territorial jurisdiction; online business registration and filing via the Ministry of Finance Tax Portal (etax.nat.gov.tw), with company and UBN registration co-administered by the Ministry of Economic Affairs Department of Commercial Affairs and foreign-investment screening by the Investment Commission where applicable.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested or joint-venture entity, the procedure is: (1) obtain foreign-investment approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs where the foreign shareholding triggers approval; (2) incorporate the limited company or branch/representative office under the Company Act in the Ministry of Economic Affairs Department of Commercial Affairs database, thereby obtaining the statutory Unified Business Number (UBN) which serves as the tax/VAT identifier; (3) complete business tax registration with the competent National Taxation Bureau before commencing operations by submitting the incorporation documents, UBN certificate, registered address proof, business scope, and director/shareholder disclosure; (4) for non-resident enterprises without a fixed place of business in Taiwan, apply separately to the National Taxation Bureau for a non-resident taxpayer registration through the Ministry of Finance Tax Portal for withholding-tax and VAT obligations on Taiwanese-source income.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-invested or joint-venture entity, the procedure is: (1) obtain foreign-investment approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs where the foreign shareholding triggers approval; (2) incorporate the limited company or branch/representative office under the Company Act in the Ministry of Economic Affairs Department of Commercial Affairs database, thereby obtaining the statutory Unified Business Number (UBN) which serves as the tax/VAT identifier; (3) complete business tax registration with the competent National Taxation Bureau before commencing operations by submitting the incorporation documents, UBN certificate, registered address proof, business scope, and director/shareholder disclosure; (4) for non-resident enterprises without a fixed place of business in Taiwan, apply separately to the National Taxation Bureau for a non-resident taxpayer registration through the Ministry of Finance Tax Portal for withholding-tax and VAT obligations on Taiwanese-source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not have a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch pass-through election; profit-seeking enterprises are taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act. Group taxation is available only on a statutorily qualified consolidated-loss basis, not as an elective pass-through or hybrid regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for incorporated profit-seeking enterprises: registration is mandatory before commencing business. For small-scale business operators, simplified taxation applies when monthly sales remain below NT$80,000 for goods or NT$40,000 for services, but such threshold does not apply to a limited liability joint-venture family office vehicle registered as a VAT-taxable business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating eligibility under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act covers: (i) goods exported from Taiwan with customs declaration; (ii) goods or services supplied to a foreign buyer and used outside Taiwan; and (iii) qualifying services rendered in Taiwan for offshore customers, including offshore digital or financial back-office services, provided the taxpayer proves the export/supply, retains the contractual and invoice documentation, and evidences collection of consideration in foreign currency or NT dollars from overseas in compliance with Central Bank of China (Taiwan) foreign-exchange regulations.

  • Customs export declaration (Customs Bill of Entry/export manifest) filed with Taiwan Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued by the commercial carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or, for Taiwan bank evidence, the bank-issued foreign-exchange receipt statement evidencing repatriation of export proceeds from a foreign client

VAT-registered businesses must issue uniform business invoices in accordance with the Uniform Invoice Use Regulations. Approved electronic-invoice taxpayers must use Ministry of Finance-certified e-invoicing systems, transmit invoice data through the turnkey API to the Ministry of Finance Electronic Invoice Integration Service Platform, and comply with time-stamped transmission and retention requirements; the platform performs the anti-fraud digital clearance and exchange functions. Failure to transmit on time or issuing non-compliant invoices is penalized under the Business Tax Act.

The National Taxation Bureau may re-examine all zero-rated export transactions and will disallow the 0% rate if the export documents, customs clearance, shipping evidence, or foreign-exchange repatriation proof are incomplete or not received within the required period. Disallowed amounts are subject to retrospective assessment of 5% business tax plus interest; where false documentation, fictitious exports, or fraudulent refund claims are involved, the taxpayer faces a deficiency penalty of up to five times the evaded tax, potential criminal tax-evasion liability for responsible officers, and forfeiture of any VAT refund previously granted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime rather than a fully free float. The New Taiwan Dollar is convertible on current-account transactions, including trade in goods and services, dividends, interest and ordinary remittance, without a prior exchange-control permit, although all settlements must pass through a CBC-authorized bank and be reported under thresholds in the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act. Capital-account flows, including inbound equity investment from a mainland Chinese shareholder into the Taiwan JV, remain controlled and are subject to prior authorization, investment filing, and bank-level FX declaration; they are not freely convertible on demand. Export proceeds do not have to be surrendered to the central bank, but the exporter must evidence collection within statutory timelines and may retain them in foreign currency deposit accounts. Profit remittance can be blocked by unresolved tax clearance, CRS classification mismatches or source-of-wealth deficiencies, but routine dividend distributions with audited financial statements and withholding tax proof are honored. The jurisdiction is therefore best described as partially convertible: liberal on trade and operating liquidity, restrictive and documentary-intensive on capital movement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must complete an Export Transaction Declaration (ETD) through an authorized bank within 10 working days after customs shipment and must collect the foreign exchange proceeds within 180 days from shipment. There is no mandatory surrender ratio under the current liberalized regime: 0% of export receipts must be converted where the company elects to retain a foreign-currency deposit; if converted to NTD, the effective conversion amount is 100% of the claimed remittance at prevailing market rates. All collection and conversion must be reported to the CBC and authorized bank under the Foreign Exchange Transaction Reporting Rules.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan), jointly with the Financial Supervisory Commission for banking and capital-market licensing

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. A pure fintech EMI without a Taiwanese banking license cannot legally open NTD clearing accounts, hold client custody assets, or service the SFO as a primary treasury counterparty. Foreign EMIs may be accepted only as technology or white-label intermediaries routed through a licensed domestic bank; the legal account relationship remains with the FSC-licensed bank and the CBC-regulated depository institution. Any proposal to use an offshore EMI as the operating bank must therefore be rejected for statutory compliance reasons.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection must be routed by SWIFT MT103 or the local equivalent from the remitting bank to the designated Taiwan bank beneficiary account. The remittance instruction must state EQUITY INJECTION FOR CN_JV_LTD and include the receiving bank's settlement code. The bank requires a completed Inward Remittance Application for Equity Investment, the capital subscription resolution, the investment approval or cross-strait filing, and CBC Foreign Exchange Transaction Declaration forms. After settlement, the bank issues a foreign exchange certificate, the company obtains a CPA capital verification report, and the amount is registered with the relevant corporate registry within the statutory timeline.Trade disbursements may be executed through SWIFT MT103 for direct high-value payments, MT700 for commercial letters of credit funding cross-border imports or exports, and documentary collections (MT400/MT202) for lower-value settlement. All NTD legs must clear through the Taiwan domestic interbank system; USD and other foreign-currency legs can be cleared through CHIPS, Fedwire or the correspondent network of the authorized domestic bank. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing and payment due by the 5th month after the end of the fiscal year (usually 31 May for calendar-year taxpayers); extension of one additional month may be applied for before the original due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: New Taiwan Dollar (NTD) 50 million for intercompany transactions; transfer pricing Local File and Master File are mandatory when annual related-party revenue exceeds NT$50 million or overseas related-party transactions exceed NT$20 million.

Economic substance scope: The Taiwan tax authority applies the Economic Substance Doctrine through the anti-avoidance rule in Article 12-1 of the Income Tax Act, disregarding arrangements lacking commercial substance. A Single Family Office is expected to have a minimum number of qualified employees, an office in Taiwan, a substantive business purpose, and a decision-making capability over asset acquisitions and dispositions. If the family office is deemed not to conduct Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Taiwan, its passive investment income may be reclassified as Taiwanese-source business income, attracting a 20% corporate income tax plus surcharge and, where fraud is evident, criminal liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Undeclared Offshore Investment Income

Statutory sanction: Article 110 of the Income Tax Act imposes a fine of up to three times the evaded tax, plus a 20% delinquent tax surcharge. Article 109 of the Tax Evasion Penalty Act prescribes imprisonment for up to five years for willful evasion; evasion exceeding NT$1 million triggers immediate criminal referral and publication on the tax blacklist.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed the concealment are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax as well as penalties; civil proceedings may attach personal assets, including family trust distributions. The director may be subject to a travel ban and entry refusal (blacklisting). If tax evasion is proven beyond a reasonable doubt, the director faces imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years with no suspension if the sum exceeds NT$3 million. The court may also disallow the director from being a director of any Taiwan company for 5 years under the Company Act.
Shell Company / Abuse of Corporate Form

Statutory sanction: Under Article 12-1 of the Income Tax Act, the tax authority may disregard the corporate entity and reallocate income to the actual economic owner, levying a 20% corporate income tax on the diverted income, plus an additional 20% penalty for non-arm's length pricing. The company may be struck off under the Statute for Establishment and Management of the Branches of Foreign Companies if it possesses no substance.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors who knowingly permitted the shell arrangement may be held personally liable for all unpaid corporate tax under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil; asset preservation orders can freeze personal bank accounts and real property. Directors may be banned from traveling abroad (blacklisted) and from holding board positions in any Taiwanese financial institution for 10 years. If the shell is used to evade tax by paper losses, directors may be criminally charged under Article 41 of the Money Laundering Control Act and face imprisonment of up to 7 years.
Anti-Money Laundering Breach for Family Office Investment Flows

Statutory sanction: Article 9 of the Money Laundering Control Act imposes a fine of NT$1 million to NT$10 million on the company and its directors who fail to implement internal control and know-your-customer procedures; repeat offenders face a mandatory shutdown of the licensed activity and revocation of the corporate filing. Violation of Article 5 (incomplete customer due diligence) is punished by an administrative fine up to NT$5 million and a public reprimand.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable to the state for the unreported transaction under the doctrine of knowing participation; personal assets may be seized, and a travel ban is automatically issued when the criminal investigation is initiated. A conviction for money laundering by a family office director carries imprisonment from 6 months to 7 years and a fine up to NT$5 million; the director also faces permanent disqualification from serving as a trustee or investment advisor in Taiwan. The court may order the forfeiture of the entire SFO asset pool if the funds are traced to unlawful activity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Taiwan Ministry of Finance, Taxation Agency; operational competence is delegated to the five National Taxation Bureaus (e.g., National Taxation Bureau of Taipei). Online portals: Ministry of Finance Tax Portal (tax.nat.gov.tw) and the e-Invoice Integration Service Platform (einvoice.nat.gov.tw).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident establishing a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture in Taiwan must first register the company with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) and obtain a Unified Business Number (UBI). After incorporation, the JV must apply for profit-seeking enterprise income tax registration and Value-Added Business Tax registration at the local National Taxation Bureau under Article 28 of the Value-Added and Non-Value-Added Business Tax Act. Required filings include the tax registration application, MOEA business registration certificate or company incorporation certificate, articles of incorporation and JV agreement, identity document of the responsible person, and proof of fixed place of business such as a lease agreement. If the foreign entity is not a local operational JV but sells cross-border electronic services to Taiwan residents without a fixed place of business, it must register for Taiwanese VAT via the Ministry of Finance cross-border e-commerce service platform and receive a foreign-entity VAT registration number; a non-resident without permanent establishment may alternatively obtain a withholding tax account for the Taiwan customer to withhold income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary threshold for a locally incorporated JV; business tax registration is required before any taxable sales commence. Small-scale businesses with monthly taxable turnover below NT$80,000 may be subject to simplified/reduced taxation. A foreign cross-border electronic service provider must register for Taiwan VAT when its annual sales of electronic services to Taiwan resident individuals exceed NT$480,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Value-Added and Non-Value-Added Business Tax Act, a 0% VAT rate applies to goods exported or delivered outside Taiwan, and to exported services such as cross-border PropTech SaaS when the service is performed for a foreign customer and is not consumed in Taiwan. The foreign-currency consideration must be remitted into Taiwan and supporting documentation must demonstrate the offshore consumption and cross-border nature of the service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Guan Qu Bao Dan / export customs declaration) authenticated by Taiwan Customs for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill for physical export shipments
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate or Inward Remittance Water confirming repatriation to Taiwan
  • Executed service agreement and SaaS usage evidence demonstrating the foreign customer and non-Taiwan consumption

Taxable enterprises must issue Uniform Invoices for all taxable sales. B2B and B2C e-invoices must be uploaded to the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice Integration Service Platform; SaaS/PropTech subscription invoices are issued as cloud e-invoices and transmitted electronically. The platform requires API-based transmission and storage of invoice data for tax authority control, audit matching, and anti-fraud validation; false e-invoices are treated as false Uniform Invoices. Taiwan does not apply a real-time fiscal digital stamping clearance system of the mainland China type.

National Taxation Bureaus audit the exported goods or services under the export declaration, Uniform Invoice, and FX settlement trail. If the export zero-rating is not substantiated, or the foreign exchange is not received or repatriated within the required period, the zero-rating is reversed and the 5% VAT is assessed retroactively. Under the Business Tax Act and the Tax Collection Act, false invoices or false export documentation can trigger penalties up to five times the evaded tax, prohibition of refunds, and criminal referral for fraudulent tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan dollar is generally convertible for bona fide trade, services, and other current-account transactions through authorized foreign-exchange banks. Taiwan generally maintains a liberal current-account regime, subject to transaction reporting, documentary support, anti-money-laundering review, and applicable annual aggregate settlement thresholds. Capital-account transactions, including equity investment, intercompany funding, acquisition of Taiwanese assets, dividends, liquidation proceeds, and certain outbound investments, may require prior approval, registration, or supporting filings. A mainland-China investor or beneficial owner may face additional eligibility, ownership, sector, national-security, and Investment Commission restrictions before capital injection, account opening, dividend remittance, or related-party funding. Profit remittance normally requires audited or approved financial statements, tax clearance or evidence of tax payment, shareholder or board approvals, proof of distributable profits, and evidence that the remittance is not a disguised capital withdrawal or prohibited mainland-China transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan does not generally impose a universal mandatory conversion ratio or automatic surrender requirement for export receipts. Export proceeds may ordinarily be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted through an authorized foreign-exchange bank, subject to export documentation, invoice and shipping evidence where applicable, bank reporting, and AML review. No general fixed statutory conversion deadline applies solely because proceeds are export receipts; however, the enterprise must comply with applicable trade-settlement, tax, accounting, foreign-exchange reporting, and banking-documentation requirements. Large or unusual settlements may require Central Bank reporting or approval under applicable aggregate annual settlement thresholds. If the operating entity is legally established in mainland China rather than Taiwan, mainland-China SAFE rules, including export-receipt and foreign-exchange settlement rules, would apply instead and this Taiwan assessment would not control.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (中央銀行); foreign-exchange transactions are also governed by the Ministry of Economic Affairs, the Investment Commission, the Foreign Exchange Control Act, the Regulations Governing Foreign Exchange Business, and applicable cross-Strait investment regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and treasury support; low for replacing a regulated Taiwanese corporate bank. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment platform cannot ordinarily open a Taiwanese bank account, hold New Taiwan dollars, conduct regulated domestic deposit-taking, or execute foreign-exchange business in Taiwan without the required local authorization. Stripe or comparable providers may support card billing and settlement only subject to merchant underwriting, supported-country rules, settlement-currency limits, beneficial-owner verification, tax documentation, chargeback controls, and Taiwan payment-services requirements. ACH Direct is not a universal Taiwan domestic clearing rail; U.S. ACH generally requires a U.S. account, U.S. collection structure, or an authorized payment intermediary. Customer funds should not be commingled with corporate operating funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity capital through a designated capital-injection or investment account at an authorized Taiwan foreign-exchange bank, using the investor's own account and an exact legal-entity name match. The remittance instruction should state the subscription or capital-injection purpose, currency, amount, investor identity, investment approval or registration reference, and beneficiary account. The bank should receive the executed investment agreement, capitalization schedule, corporate resolutions, source-of-funds evidence, ownership chart, and relevant Investment Commission or cross-Strait approvals before or concurrently with settlement. Do not use third-party payers, personal accounts, cash, undisclosed offshore vehicles, or unapproved shareholder loans. The bank should verify sender, beneficial owner, purpose, foreign-exchange reporting classification, sanctions status, and whether the transaction is permissible under Taiwan mainland-China restrictions. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan generally requires transfer-pricing disclosure and supporting documentation for controlled transactions. A taxpayer may qualify for simplified documentation only where applicable revenue and controlled-transaction thresholds are not exceeded; commonly relevant thresholds include annual revenue below NTD 300,000,000 and aggregate controlled transactions below NTD 200,000,000, subject to transaction-specific exceptions. A Local File is generally required when the applicable exemption is unavailable. A Master File is generally relevant where the multinational enterprise group's annual consolidated revenue reaches at least NTD 27,000,000,000 and the Taiwan entity has relevant controlled transactions. Country-by-Country Reporting generally applies at the same NTD 27,000,000,000 consolidated-revenue threshold, subject to reporting-entity and filing rules.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a single UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance is enforced through transfer-pricing rules, anti-avoidance provisions, beneficial-ownership analysis, business-purpose requirements, controlled-foreign-company rules, place-of-effective-management considerations, source rules, and deductibility requirements. A Taiwan entity claiming deductions for related-party SaaS development, management, licensing, financing, or platform support must demonstrate personnel, decision-making, assets, risk control, actual performance, commercial benefit, and arm's-length pricing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and intentional underreporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, statutory administrative penalties, interest, compulsory collection, and criminal exposure under the Income Tax Act and related criminal laws. Serious tax evasion may result in imprisonment of the responsible person and substantial fines, with the exact sanction depending on conduct, tax amount, intent, and prosecution provisions.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons and directors may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, civil liability for losses, restitution of unpaid taxes, asset preservation, restrictions on departure, and enforcement against personal assets where statutory conditions are met. Limited liability does not protect intentional personal misconduct.
False accounting and fraudulent documentation

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, denial of deductions or credits, invoice-related penalties, fines, and potential criminal liability for document fraud, tax fraud, or commercial-accounting violations.

Director personal liability: Directors, responsible persons, finance officers, and cooperating advisers may be jointly exposed to criminal investigation, civil restitution, personal fines, asset seizure, and travel restrictions.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment to an arm's-length result, denial of deductions, additional corporate income tax, withholding tax assessment, interest, penalties, and possible anti-avoidance reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for tax losses and breach of fiduciary duties. Deliberate false documentation or collusion may create criminal exposure for the responsible person and participating officers.
Improper dividend, interest, royalty, or service payment withholding

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the Taiwan payer, interest, withholding penalties, filing penalties, and possible disallowance of the related deduction. The payer may remain liable even where the foreign recipient received the full net payment.

Director personal liability: The responsible person and withholding officers may face administrative and civil liability. Deliberate concealment or false certificates may result in criminal proceedings and restrictions on departure.
Unlawful Mainland Chinese investment or nominee ownership

Statutory sanction: Investment approval violations may lead to fines, orders to dispose of shares, cancellation or revocation of approvals, restrictions on operations, confiscation or corrective measures, and penalties under Taiwan regulations governing Mainland-area investment and cross-strait economic relations.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face personal fines, disqualification or removal consequences, civil damages, criminal exposure for false filings, asset restrictions, and possible travel restrictions. Foreign shareholders may also face enforcement against Taiwan-located assets.
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions, transfer-pricing adjustments, source recharacterization, withholding assessments, anti-avoidance reassessment, interest, penalties, and possible revocation or regulatory action where registration or business activity is fictitious.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unpaid taxes, creditor losses, and misleading filings. Intentional sham arrangements can create criminal exposure, asset preservation, and departure restrictions.
Money laundering and suspicious payment routing

Statutory sanction: Reporting, customer-identification, recordkeeping, account-freezing, administrative, and criminal sanctions under Taiwan anti-money-laundering laws. Serious money-laundering conduct may carry imprisonment and substantial fines, with penalties depending on amount, predicate offense, and intent.

Director personal liability: Directors, responsible persons, compliance officers, and beneficial owners may face criminal liability, confiscation, restitution, asset freezing, travel restrictions, and imprisonment. Corporate limited liability does not shield knowing participants.
Unlicensed or misrepresented business activity

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines, cease-and-desist orders, suspension or revocation of registrations, customer refunds, tax reassessment, and criminal penalties where the relevant sector law provides them.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face personal administrative fines, civil claims, criminal prosecution for unauthorized operations, and restrictions on departure in connection with unpaid penalties or criminal proceedings.
Personal-data and cybersecurity noncompliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, corrective orders, suspension of data processing, customer compensation, cybersecurity sanctions, and potential criminal liability for unlawful disclosure or misuse of personal data.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face civil damages, administrative sanctions, and criminal exposure for intentional or grossly negligent misuse or disclosure of protected information.
Failure to maintain books, records, and audit trail

Statutory sanction: Estimated assessment, denial of deductions, penalties, audit escalation, withholding reassessment, and possible criminal exposure if records are intentionally destroyed or falsified.

Director personal liability: The responsible person and directors may be liable for resulting tax losses, creditor claims, and obstruction-related sanctions. Personal asset enforcement and departure restrictions may apply under tax collection or criminal procedures.

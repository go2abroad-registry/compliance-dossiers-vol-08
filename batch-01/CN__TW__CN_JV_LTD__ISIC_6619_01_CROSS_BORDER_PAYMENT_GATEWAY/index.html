Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a company limited by shares under the Company Act (the former NT$1,000,000 floor was repealed); however, the FSC imposes a separate, materially higher paid-in capital floor at the electronic-payment licensing stage. For incorporation, the subscribed capital must be deposited into a Taiwan-dollar or foreign-currency escrow account opened in the JV's name, verified by a licensed CPA, and the funds remain escrowed (non-withdrawable) until the MOEA issues the incorporation registration certificate.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance of Taiwan (R.O.C.), administered operationally through the relevant National Taxation Bureau; primary portals include the Ministry of Finance eTax Portal and the Ministry of Economic Affairs Company and Business One-Stop Service system.

E-government filing portal

A Taiwan-incorporated Sino-foreign equity joint venture must first complete company registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs or the competent local authority, obtain its Unified Business Number (UBN), and register its business tax status with the competent National Taxation Bureau before commencing taxable business. The UBN generally serves as the enterprise tax identification number and business tax registration number. The application package ordinarily includes the articles of incorporation, company registration approval, foreign investor approval and investment documents where applicable, registered office evidence, responsible-person identification, capital verification or bank evidence where required, business-scope details, and banking information. A foreign company operating through a Taiwan branch follows branch-registration and tax-registration procedures. A nonresident foreign electronic-services provider supplying Taiwan consumers may require separate business tax registration under the foreign electronic-services rules, subject to the applicable sales threshold and transaction characterization. A payment-service provider must separately obtain the required Financial Supervisory Commission authorization or license before conducting regulated payment, acquiring, clearing, remittance, or electronic-payment activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not provide a United States-style check-the-box election for companies. A Taiwan company is generally taxed as a separate legal person on Taiwan-source income and other income within the applicable corporate income tax regime. A foreign enterprise may operate through a subsidiary or branch, but the choice is structural rather than an elective tax classification. Partnerships and other pass-through arrangements may receive entity-specific treatment, but a limited company or equity joint venture is not generally permitted to elect pass-through treatment. No general hybrid-mismatch election permits a company to change from entity-level taxation to transparent taxation. Treaty relief, withholding-tax reductions, approved investment incentives, and qualifying industry incentives are claim-based statutory mechanisms, not entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold permits a Taiwan-incorporated company conducting taxable business to avoid business-tax registration; registration is required before commencement of business. The commonly referenced NT$40,000 monthly threshold concerns certain small-scale businesses and tax-assessment treatment and is not a general exemption from registration for a corporate payment-service provider. Foreign electronic-service providers supplying Taiwan consumers may be subject to a separate NT$600,000 annual sales threshold under the applicable electronic-services business-tax rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated business tax may apply to qualifying exported goods and qualifying services supplied to foreign customers and used outside Taiwan, subject to the Business Tax Act and implementing rules. For a cross-border payment gateway, zero rating is not automatic merely because the customer, settlement bank, or payment rail is outside Taiwan. The taxpayer must establish that the contractual recipient is a foreign customer, the service is exported or utilized outside Taiwan, the transaction is not actually used in Taiwan, and the service is not an exempt financial service or another specially regulated category. Supporting evidence should include contracts, service descriptions, merchant and customer location data, transaction and settlement records, invoices, foreign-currency receipt evidence, bank statements, remittance records, fee calculations, and documentation demonstrating offshore utilization. Zero-rated input business tax may generally be refundable or creditable when the statutory export conditions and documentary requirements are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration, where goods are exported
  • Commercial invoice and packing list, where goods are exported
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent transport evidence, where goods are exported
  • Foreign customer contract, purchase order, or service agreement
  • Tax invoices or e-invoices identifying the zero-rated supply
  • Foreign-exchange receipt, bank remittance advice, or equivalent payment evidence
  • Settlement and transaction reports proving the offshore service recipient, payment flow, and service utilization
  • Evidence of customs export clearance or other proof of export for goods
  • Accounting records reconciling declared export turnover, received consideration, and claimed input-tax refunds

Taiwan businesses subject to business tax generally must issue government-regulated electronic invoices where required by the applicable e-invoice rules and transmit invoice information through the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice Platform or an approved service provider. Required invoice data must use prescribed formats, authentication, numbering, buyer information, tax amounts, and cancellation or allowance procedures. Taiwan does not generally operate a universal real-time VAT clearance model for every invoice; transmission timing depends on the invoice category and applicable regulations. Digital certificates, API or batch-upload connectivity, invoice archival, and electronic-accounting controls may be required. Payment-service providers must also maintain transaction, settlement, customer-identification, and audit records under financial-supervision and anti-money-laundering rules. Payment processing, acquiring, clearing, platform, and financial-intermediation fees must be classified transaction by transaction because particular financial services may be exempt or subject to special business-tax treatment, while ordinary taxable service fees generally bear 5% VAT.

Export proceeds and qualifying offshore-service receipts are generally subject to Taiwan foreign-exchange declaration and settlement requirements, commonly requiring repatriation or settlement within six months, subject to permitted exceptions, approved extensions, transaction structure, and Central Bank rules. A taxpayer claiming zero rating without proving export status, foreign customer status, offshore utilization, or receipt of consideration may have the zero-rated treatment denied, with the applicable 5% business tax assessed retroactively, together with interest, late-payment charges, and related penalties. Improper input-tax refunds may be clawed back. Business Tax Act violations may result in administrative penalties that can be calculated by reference to the tax evaded, including substantial multiples in cases of tax evasion, false invoicing, omitted sales, or fraudulent refund claims. Serious falsification, concealment, forged documentation, or deliberate evasion may create criminal exposure for responsible persons and involved agents. Financial-sector licensing breaches, AML failures, unauthorized payment activity, and inaccurate regulatory reporting may separately trigger Financial Supervisory Commission sanctions, license restrictions, disgorgement, or criminal liability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$200 million in annual controlled related-party transactions combined with NT$300 million revenue triggers the Local File; a Master File is additionally necessary where the global ultimate parent has consolidated revenue in excess of NT$10 billion.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not maintain a separate Economic Substances Regulation regime; nevertheless, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Tax Collection Act Article 12-1) empowers the NTA to recharacterise a JV lacking genuine office, personnel, control and risk-taking as a conduit. In such circumstances the JV can be treated as a non-resident's permanent establishment, and the entire Taiwan-source income is subject to 20% corporate tax and WHT, with penalties.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the Taiwan Company Act, there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a company limited by shares. The JV must, however, subscribe paid-in capital at least equal to the amount approved by the Investment Commission, and the entire approved capital must be fully paid in cash at incorporation unless the Commission permits deferred payment. The remitted funds must be deposited in a newly opened temporary bank account in the name of the Company and certified by the bank before registration. The funds are not held in a blocked escrow account; after incorporation, control of the account passes to the board-authorized signatories. The paid-in capital should be commensurate with the registered business scope and working capital needs of the voice-streaming application; under the Foreign Investment Act, capital may be contributed in cash, machinery, technology or intellectual property, but for an ISIC 6311 service company cash contribution is recommended.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Taiwan Ministry of Finance, including the competent National Taxation Bureau and its local tax office; electronic tax services are provided through the Ministry of Finance eTax Portal. Business registration and company or branch registration are administered through the Taiwan Ministry of Economic Affairs, Department of Commerce and the relevant local authority.

E-government filing portal

The Taiwan jurisdiction identifier in the payload is Taiwan, although the entity label Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is principally a PRC legal-form designation and is not a standard Taiwan company form. A Taiwan operating entity, branch, or subsidiary must first obtain the applicable foreign-investment approval and company, branch, or business registration through the Ministry of Economic Affairs and establish a Taiwan registered office. The entity then files its business registration and tax registration information with the competent National Taxation Bureau. The tax authority issues an eight-digit Unified Business Number, commonly used as the Taiwan business tax number and corporate tax identification number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not generally provide a United States-style check-the-box election for classifying an incorporated company or branch. A Taiwan company is ordinarily subject to Taiwan corporate income tax on its taxable income, while a Taiwan branch of a foreign company is taxed under branch rules. A foreign company may be subject to Taiwan tax based on a Taiwan fixed place of business, agent, source income, or Taiwan-sourced electronic services. Taiwan has no general elective hybrid-mismatch classification mechanism that converts a limited company into a fiscally transparent entity. Treaty applications, withholding-tax relief, tax consolidation or filing-group treatment where legally available, and industry or investment incentives are relief or compliance mechanisms rather than entity-classification elections. Taiwan controlled foreign company rules and place-of-effective-management concepts may affect the tax treatment of offshore related entities, but they do not constitute a check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NT$480,000 of monthly sales is the principal small-business threshold commonly used for compulsory ordinary VAT registration analysis. This threshold does not remove the need for a company or business registration and does not necessarily determine registration obligations for foreign electronic service providers or businesses whose activities fall within mandatory registration rules. A Taiwan company or fixed-place business should register before commencing taxable transactions; foreign electronic service providers supplying Taiwan consumers may have separate registration obligations even without a Taiwan physical establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Taiwan VAT zero-rating may apply to qualifying exports of goods and to qualifying services supplied to foreign purchasers or connected with exports, subject to the statutory conditions and documentary evidence. For a voice-streaming or social application, zero-rating is not automatic merely because the customer uses an overseas payment card, because Apple or Google remits funds from abroad, or because the application is hosted outside Taiwan. The taxpayer should establish that the contractual customer is outside Taiwan, the service is supplied to and used by the foreign customer or otherwise falls within a statutory zero-rated category, the Taiwan entity is the actual supplier, consideration is received through permitted channels, and the transaction is recorded in the accounting and VAT systems. Services consumed by Taiwan users, Taiwan-resident subscribers, or Taiwan business customers generally require Taiwan VAT treatment even if payment is processed offshore. Exported goods require customs export evidence. Export-related services and offshore digital services require contracts, customer-residency evidence, service-delivery records, platform settlement reports, foreign-exchange or payment evidence, and a transaction-level allocation between Taiwan and non-Taiwan users. Input VAT refunds are generally available for qualifying zero-rated exporters to the extent permitted by the VAT rules, but refund claims remain subject to documentary and substantive verification.

  • Customs export declaration and customs release evidence for exported goods; not applicable to a pure digital service unless physical goods are also exported
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, purchase order, or equivalent evidence identifying the foreign purchaser and the exported goods or services
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier export record, or other transport evidence for physical goods, where applicable
  • Evidence of foreign purchaser location, including customer master data, contractual address, business registration, tax residence, and platform account records
  • For digital or offshore services, service agreement, order records, access or delivery logs, user-location evidence, platform merchant-of-record documentation, and settlement statements
  • Bank remittance advice, foreign-exchange receipt, or other permitted payment evidence linking the consideration to the claimed zero-rated transaction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank-issued foreign-exchange evidence where required by the competent tax authority or used to substantiate foreign-currency proceeds
  • VAT sales ledger, electronic invoices or export documentation, credit notes, refund calculations, and reconciliation to general ledger and bank records
  • For Apple IAP or Google Play Billing, platform tax reports, merchant-of-record terms, gross customer billings, refunds, commissions, withholding, net remittances, and evidence identifying the supplier and customer jurisdiction

Taiwan electronic invoices are issued, transmitted, and reported through the Ministry of Finance e-Invoice Platform or an approved service provider. Taxpayers must use the applicable electronic invoice format, assign a compliant invoice number, include the purchaser and transaction data required by the VAT rules, retain electronic records, and transmit the invoice data within the prescribed reporting period. Taiwan generally operates a post-issuance electronic reporting and platform-validation model rather than a universal real-time pre-clearance model for every transaction. B2B invoices ordinarily require the purchaser's Unified Business Number where applicable, while B2C invoices may use a carrier, mobile barcode, or donation code. The business must maintain API or approved-system controls for invoice issuance, cancellation, allowance notes, reconciliation, and audit retrieval. Apple App Store and Google Play billing require transaction-level analysis of the contractual seller, merchant of record, customer location, commission, remittance, invoice responsibility, and whether the Taiwan entity supplies the customer or merely receives platform settlement. Platform statements alone do not automatically establish export zero-rating or eliminate Taiwan VAT obligations.

The 180-day control period should be applied to export-proceeds monitoring where the relevant foreign-exchange or trade-control rule requires receipt or reporting within that period; Taiwan does not impose a universal tax rule that automatically makes every offshore digital-service receipt subject to a single 180-day repatriation deadline. Failure to support zero-rating may result in reassessment of output VAT at the applicable rate, denial or clawback of input-VAT refunds, interest, late-payment charges, and administrative penalties for inaccurate, omitted, or late VAT filings. False invoices, fabricated export documents, sham foreign customers, concealed Taiwan consumption, or intentional misrepresentation may trigger aggravated tax penalties and, depending on the conduct, criminal liability under tax, customs, commercial, foreign-exchange, or criminal laws. The tax authority may audit platform contracts, IP and device-location indicators, subscriber records, app-store reports, payment-processor data, bank remittances, related-party arrangements, and user-level allocation methodologies. A claim should therefore be reversed or amended promptly when export conditions fail, with a complete audit trail documenting the correction.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan dollar is generally convertible for current-account transactions, including bona fide trade in goods and services, subject to bank documentary review, reporting, and applicable anti-money-laundering controls. Capital-account transactions are more regulated. Inbound foreign investment, Mainland Area investment, equity transfers, shareholder loans, guarantees, and certain cross-border securities or intercompany financing may require prior approval, registration, reporting, or supporting documentation. A China-linked shareholder or investor requires separate analysis under the Act Governing Relations between the People of the Taiwan Area and the Mainland Area, related regulations, and Investment Commission practice. Profit remittance is generally feasible after corporate tax, withholding tax, accounting, statutory reserve, corporate approvals, and any required investment registration or approval are completed. Dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, and repayment of shareholder loans must be supported by the relevant contracts, board or shareholder resolutions, tax filings, withholding evidence, transfer-pricing support, and proof of available distributable funds. The proposed entity classification as a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is not a standard Taiwan corporate form and must be reconciled with the actual place of incorporation, shareholder nationality, investment approval status, and applicable cross-Strait restrictions before banking or remittance implementation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan does not generally impose a universal mandatory foreign-exchange surrender ratio requiring all export proceeds to be converted into New Taiwan dollars. Export receipts may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to applicable bank reporting, documentary verification, accounting, tax, customs, and anti-money-laundering requirements. There is no generally applicable statutory rule requiring conversion of all export proceeds within a fixed number of days. Banks may require export declarations, commercial invoices, bills of lading, contracts, payment records, and evidence of the underlying transaction. Any specific industry, special-zone, subsidized-financing, customs, or investment approval condition must be checked separately. If the operating entity is actually established in Mainland China rather than Taiwan, Mainland China foreign-exchange rules apply instead, including SAFE registration, designated foreign-exchange bank review, and potentially different rules for export proceeds, capital contributions, and profit remittance.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), with foreign-exchange administration under the Foreign Exchange Control Act and related CBC regulations. The Financial Supervisory Commission regulates banks, payment institutions, electronic payment institutions, and financial consumer protection. The Ministry of Economic Affairs Investment Commission administers applicable inbound, outbound, and Mainland Area investment approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to Low for core treasury, payroll, local collections, and regulated payment activity. Taiwan does not provide an EU-style passport for offshore electronic-money institutions. An offshore digital EMI may provide corporate payment services only within the scope of its home-country authorization and the bank or correspondent-bank risk policy. It should not accept Taiwan deposits, operate a Taiwan payment account, provide Taiwan-regulated electronic payment services, or conduct local customer funds flows without the required Taiwan authorization or a licensed local partner. Offshore accounts do not replace a Taiwan corporate bank account for capital injection, tax payments, payroll, local vendor settlement, or regulated payment collection. Apple App Store and Google Play settlement should be mapped separately to the merchant-of-record, contractual recipient, withholding-tax position, foreign-exchange reporting, and Taiwan consumer-protection requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use a Taiwan-authorized bank and remit from an account held in the registered investor's legal name. Before funding, complete all applicable Investment Commission, company-registration, capital-verification, and cross-Strait approvals or filings. The remittance instruction should identify the transaction as paid-in capital, capital increase, shareholder loan, or another legally accurate purpose; it must not describe debt as equity or use a generic personal remitter. Provide the bank with the investment approval or registration, subscription or investment agreement, board and shareholder resolutions, capitalization table, source-of-funds evidence, remitter ownership evidence, and beneficiary account details. The receiving bank should confirm the applicable foreign-exchange declaration, account classification, capital verification, and reporting process before receipt. For goods or higher-risk counterparties, use a commercial letter of credit, documentary collection, or controlled open-account process. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to Low for core treasury, payroll, local collections, and regulated payment activity. Taiwan does not provide an EU-style passport for offshore electronic-money institutions. An offshore digital EMI may provide corporate payment services only within the scope of its home-country authorization and the bank or correspondent-bank risk policy. It should not accept Taiwan deposits, operate a Taiwan payment account, provide Taiwan-regulated electronic payment services, or conduct local customer funds flows without the required Taiwan authorization or a licensed local partner. Offshore accounts do not replace a Taiwan corporate bank account for capital injection, tax payments, payroll, local vendor settlement, or regulated payment collection. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% for dividends paid to a non-resident enterprise; 21% for dividends paid to a non-resident individual. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Taiwan resident company must prepare a Transfer Pricing Report (Local File) when the total amount of related-party transactions exceeds NT$200 million (approximately USD 6.6 million). A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent enterprise reaches NT$27 billion (approximately EUR 750 million). A simplified local file may be required when cross-border related-party transactions exceed NT$50 million or domestic related-party transactions exceed NT$100 million.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not enact Gulf-style annual Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Nevertheless, the Income Basic Tax Act and the Income Tax Act Article 43-1 authorise tax authorities to disregard transactions or legal forms that lack economic substance, including interposing shell companies and allocating income to tax haven entities. Thin capitalisation rules limit interest deductions to a debt-to-equity ratio of 3:1. Treaty benefits and cross-strait relief are denied to entities without actual management, employees, office space, and business activities in Taiwan. For a voice streaming joint venture, the entity must demonstrate substantive operational substance covering: Taiwan-based product management, customer transaction review, app store servicing, technical hosting arrangements, and local decision-making records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Concealment of Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act Article 110, a taxpayer filing an understated return is liable for a fine equal to the amount of tax understated, and up to twice the understated tax when concealment or intentional misstatement is established. Under the Tax Collection Act Article 41, intentional tax evasion by fraudulent means leads to imprisonment of not more than 5 years, short-term imprisonment, or a fine of up to NT$1,000,000, plus the evaded tax and interest.

Director personal liability: The responsible director can be held jointly liable for the delinquent tax where the company fails to pay; under Tax Collection Act Article 24, the director's departure from Taiwan may be restricted when tax arrears are material. Criminal imprisonment for intentional evasion attaches to the actual responsible person, and a civil court may pierce the corporate veil where the director concealed revenue for personal benefit.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will adjust related-party income under Income Tax Act Article 43-1 and may recharacterise the arrangement, resulting in additional corporate income tax, a 1% monthly late surcharge up to 20%, and an understatement penalty of up to twice the tax deficiency. Failure to supply TP documentation after an official request may result in a penalty of NT$1,000 to NT$10,000 per item and additional adjustments for intentional non-compliance.

Director personal liability: If the adjusted tax remains unpaid, directors of the Taiwan JV may face collection against personal assets under Tax Collection Act Article 23 and 24-regime including bank levies and travel bans. A director who knowingly constructs artificial transfer prices is criminally liable under tax evasion provisions if the adjustment is classified as intentional tax evasion.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse and AML Offences

Statutory sanction: The jurisdictional tax authority may deny treaty benefits and recharacterise the entity's income as earned by the parent; fictitious deductions are reversed. Under the Money Laundering Control Act, enterprises that fail to establish AML internal controls risk administrative fines of up to NT$5,000,000 and responsible persons are subject to up to 2 years imprisonment if the omission is conducted with wilful negligence. The Central Bank and Financial Supervisory Commission may freeze suspicious account traffic.

Director personal liability: Directors who use the Taiwan JV as a shell to launder subscriber payments or evade taxes are individually accountable for AML violations; corporate veil-piercing can expose personal assets. Taiwanese courts have upheld travel restrictions and asset seizure against responsible persons where the entity operates without real substance and the director's personal conduct enables the abuse.
Withholding Tax Abuses on Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act Article 114, a withholding agent that fails to withhold and remit is liable for the full unpaid tax amount plus a fine of 1 to 3 times the amount improperly shipped. The transaction may be reclassified as a dividend and subject to the 20% withholding tax, the 5% retained-earnings surtax, and interest. Criminal liability under Tax Collection Act Article 42 can reach 3 years' imprisonment for willful failure to withhold.

Director personal liability: Directors of the withholding agent are personally responsible for unpaid withholding tax when they have signed or directed the payments. Civil restitution may be ordered from directors to recover unlawful distributions; travel bans and passport revocation may be requested by the National Taxation Bureau for unremitted withholding obligations above material amounts, and repeated deliberate conduct exposes the responsible person to criminal imprisonment.

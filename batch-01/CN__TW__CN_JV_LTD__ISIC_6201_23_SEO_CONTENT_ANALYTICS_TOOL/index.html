Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Republic of China (Taiwan), administered through the competent local National Taxation Bureau; the principal electronic tax portal is the Ministry of Finance eTax Portal (https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw). Company and foreign-company registration is generally coordinated with the Ministry of Economic Affairs, Department of Commerce, and the local tax bureau.

E-government filing portal

A Taiwan company or Taiwan-registered Sino-foreign equity joint venture must first complete company registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs or the competent local authority, obtain its eight-digit Uniform Business Number (統一編號), and complete tax registration with the competent National Taxation Bureau before commencing taxable business. A foreign company establishing a branch or other fixed business presence must obtain approval and registration under the Company Act, receive a Uniform Business Number, and register for business tax. A foreign enterprise without a Taiwan establishment that supplies cross-border electronic services to Taiwan consumers may be required to register for VAT through the designated online tax-registration process and obtain a tax identification number when the statutory registration threshold is met. Applications generally require constitutional documents, certificate of incorporation, responsible-person identification, Taiwan agent or representative information, registered-address evidence, business scope, bank information where requested, and certified Chinese translations or notarization/legalization where applicable. Registration should be completed before taxable operations or invoice issuance. A separate VAT number is generally not issued in the European Union format; the Uniform Business Number and tax-registration status are the principal Taiwan identifiers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not provide a United States-style check-the-box election for ordinary companies, branches, or equity joint ventures. A Taiwan company is generally subject to profit-seeking enterprise income tax at entity level, with dividends or profit distributions potentially subject to separate shareholder-level consequences. A taxpayer generally cannot elect partnership or transparent treatment merely by filing a tax form. Business-tax treatment is determined by the Value-Added and Non-Value-Added Tax Act, the nature and place of supply, and the taxpayer's registration status. Taiwan has targeted anti-avoidance and controlled-foreign-company rules, but no broad statutory hybrid-mismatch election that converts an ordinary Taiwan limited company into a pass-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For ordinary business-tax registration, the commonly applied monthly sales thresholds are NT$80,000 for sellers of goods and NT$40,000 for providers of services, subject to statutory exclusions, changes in law, and the tax authority's classification. For foreign enterprises supplying electronic services to Taiwan individuals, the relevant registration threshold is generally annual Taiwan-source sales of NT$480,000. A business may be required to register earlier where it has a fixed place of business, taxable operations, or another statutory registration trigger. A Taiwan company or registered branch should not rely on the small-business threshold to postpone registration without confirmation from the competent National Taxation Bureau.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Taiwan business-tax rules, exported goods may qualify for a 0% rate when the goods are exported from Taiwan and the export is supported by customs records. Services may qualify for 0% treatment where they are supplied to foreign customers and are used or enjoyed outside Taiwan, including qualifying offshore services, provided the statutory place-of-use and evidentiary requirements are met. For a MarTech analytics SaaS business, zero rating is not automatic merely because the customer is incorporated abroad: the supplier should establish the customer's foreign status, contractual recipient, service-use location, delivery and access records, absence of Taiwan use or benefit where required, invoice trail, and receipt of consideration through acceptable banking or foreign-exchange evidence. Services supplied to Taiwan customers or used in Taiwan generally remain subject to 5% VAT. VAT refunds or credits for export transactions require properly documented input tax, valid invoices, business-tax filings, and proof supporting the 0% output treatment.

  • Taiwan Customs export declaration or equivalent customs export record for exported goods
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, and Bill of Lading or air waybill where applicable
  • Foreign customer contract, purchase order, service agreement, or platform order record
  • Evidence identifying the foreign customer and the contractual recipient
  • Electronic service delivery, access, usage, or system-log evidence supporting offshore use where relevant
  • Bank remittance advice, foreign-exchange settlement record, or other legally acceptable evidence of receipt of export proceeds
  • Taiwan business-tax invoice and sales ledger showing the 0% transaction
  • Proof of import-country delivery or acceptance for goods, where required by the tax authority
  • Tax return schedules and supporting reconciliation between accounting revenue, customs data, invoices, and bank receipts

Taiwan uses a government-regulated electronic invoice system administered by the Ministry of Finance. Businesses subject to electronic-invoice requirements must issue compliant electronic invoices, use an approved service provider or compatible system, assign compliant invoice numbers, retain transaction data, and transmit invoice information to the Ministry of Finance electronic invoice platform within the applicable statutory timing. Taiwan generally does not operate a universal pre-issuance real-time clearance model equivalent to China's Golden Tax system; transmission and platform validation occur under the electronic-invoice rules. B2B invoices, B2C electronic invoices, carrier or cloud-invoice arrangements, and donation or buyer-identification data have separate technical requirements. Paper invoices remain available in permitted circumstances, but businesses should confirm applicability, transmission deadlines, XML specifications, certificate requirements, and system onboarding with the competent National Taxation Bureau before commencing SaaS sales.

The 180-day period is a commonly applied foreign-exchange remittance benchmark for export proceeds and must be checked against the transaction type, applicable foreign-exchange rules, banking requirements, and any approved extension; it is not a universal VAT rule for every offshore service. The National Taxation Bureau may deny or reverse 0% treatment where export, offshore use, foreign-customer status, or payment evidence is insufficient. The resulting VAT may be assessed retroactively at 5%, together with interest, late-payment charges, and administrative penalties. Incorrect invoices, false customs or remittance evidence, omitted sales, or intentional use of 0% treatment to evade tax may trigger penalties under the Value-Added and Non-Value-Added Tax Act, potentially including fines calculated by reference to the evaded tax, loss of refund or credit entitlement, and criminal exposure for serious tax fraud or document falsification. Records should generally be retained for the statutory tax-preservation period and longer where an audit, refund claim, litigation, or customs matter remains open.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Transfer Pricing Local File is mandatory when annual related-party transaction amounts exceed NT$50 million. A Master File is required when the consolidated group revenue of the multinational group exceeds NT$50 billion. Below the threshold, the taxpayer must complete the related-party disclosure schedule in the annual profit-seeking enterprise income tax return and be prepared to substantiate the arm's length nature of all cross-border transactions.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations code comparable to the UAE; however, the competent tax authority applies the substance-over-form principle, Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act, and beneficial ownership clauses in DTAs to deny treaty benefits or reallocate income where the JV lacks payroll, premises, management functions, or substantive decision-making. Taiwan's CFC rules introduced in 2021 further require an actual active business presence before deferral of income through low-tax offshore vehicles is permitted.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Omitted tax assessment plus a penalty of 0.5 to 3 times the evaded tax; interest accrual; criminal fines and imprisonment up to five years under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally use the corporate form to commit fraud are exposed to veil piercing under Company Act Article 154(2) and become jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax and penalties, with potential travel restrictions under Tax Collection Act Article 24 and criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company / Weak Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reallocation of taxable profits to the true economic owner under Article 43-1, disallowance of intercompany service fee deductions, back-tax assessments, late interest, and administrative fines for false accounting records.

Director personal liability: If the shell arrangement is deemed a sham, directors may be held jointly liable for the full enterprise tax exposure, be subjected to non-departure orders, and suffer personal asset seizure when the corporate veil is pierced.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments increasing taxable income, a penalty of up to 3 times the resulting tax under Article 110, interest computations, and the risk of secondary adjustment on deemed dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign untrue pricing certifications or knowingly direct artificial pricing practices may be personally assessed for negligence or fraud, with personal restitution and criminal exposure under commercial false-document statutes.
AML / Payment Channel Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines under the Money Laundering Control Act ranging from NT$500,000 to NT$10,000,000 per violation, mandatory internal control remediation, and potential suspension of non-banking financial service operations.

Director personal liability: A responsible person who willfully disregards AML obligations may be criminally prosecuted, subjected to substantial personal fines, prohibited from leaving Taiwan during investigation, and held civilly liable for losses arising from enabled financial crime.

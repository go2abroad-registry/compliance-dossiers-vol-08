Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Ministry of Finance, Taiwan (財政部, R.O.C.) — Taxation Administration (賦稅署) in policy/compliance oversight, with territorial tax jurisdiction exercised by the regional National Taxation Bureaus (各地區國稅局). The statutory e-portal is the Ministry of Finance Tax E-Portal (財政部電子稅務入口網), https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw; company incorporation and TIN issuance are interoperable with the MOEA Company Registration One-Stop System (公司及商業一站式線上申請作業, https://gcis.nat.gov.tw).

        
E-government filing portal
For CN_JV_LTD, a Taiwan-registered foreign-invested limited liability company (有限公司/股份有限公司): (1) obtain company-name and business-scope pre-approval (公司名稱暨所營事業預查) from the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) Company Registration portal; (2) file articles of incorporation and register the company with MOEA or the competent municipal commercial authority — the statutory Tax ID Number (統一編號, TIN) is automatically generated during incorporation, so no separate national TIN application is required; (3) complete mandatory business/VAT registration (營業登記) with the local National Taxation Bureau having jurisdiction over the registered office — this activates the profit-seeking enterprise income tax account, business tax account, and the uniform-invoice (統一發票) quota allocation; (4) maintain/activate the E-Invoice (電子發票) enrollment on the MOF E-Invoice Integration Service Platform (電子發票整合服務平台) and apply for the Turnkey transmission credentials if the projected B2B/B2C e-invoice volume meets the designated threshold or upon bureau designation; (5) non-resident shareholders do not require a separate Taiwan TIN for the company's registration, but the JV must register as a withholding agent (扣繳義務人) for withholding on dividends, royalties, and other Taiwan-source payments to its non-resident/offshore shareholders and vendors; foreign juridical persons operating through a Taiwan branch/office obtain their own TIN by filing the foreign profit-seeking enterprise tax-registration application with the local National Taxation Bureau. Mainland-China investors are additionally subject to the Cross-Strait Act (兩岸人民關係條例) investment-permission procedure before tax registration. End-to-end government processing is 3–7 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
Taiwan does not maintain a check-the-box pass-through election; every incorporated company is an autonomous taxable entity subject to profit-seeking enterprise income tax at 20% (plus 5% retained-earnings surtax on undistributed profits). Available statutory elections/regimes relevant to the JV: (i) a foreign profit-seeking enterprise (including offshore parent shareholders) with a fixed place of business (PE) in Taiwan may elect, in the annual final settlement return, to be taxed on actual Taiwanese net income rather than on gross-basis/imputed-profit withholding; (ii) net-operating-loss carryforward election — a Taiwan company may elect to carry forward statutory NOLs for 10 years (Income Tax Act Art. 39; loss-offset under the amended 2018 rule; the surviving company in a qualified merger may elect to absorb the acquired company's unused NOLs subject to statutory deductibility caps); (iii) distribution-vs-retention election — the shareholders' resolution to distribute profits triggers non-resident dividend withholding (21% for foreign corporate shareholders) whereas retained earnings are subject to the 5% undistributed-earnings surtax assessed in the following year; (iv) CFC rules (Income Tax Act Art. 43-3) apply to Taiwan shareholders of controlled offshore entities effective for tax year 2024, with an election to rely on the de-minimis exemption thresholds (net CFC income cap and asset cap); (v) anti-hybrid and anti-reverse-hybrid limitations imposed by post-BEPS Taiwan amendments eliminate deduction/non-inclusion mismatches arising from entity classification or permanent-establishment hybrids; treaty or cross-strait tax arrangement relief, where available, must be asserted by the taxpayer through advance approval or the statutory refund/relief procedure with the Ministry of Finance.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
5%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Mandatory VAT (business tax) registration threshold: average monthly sale of goods exceeding NT$80,000 or average monthly sale of services exceeding NT$40,000 (Business Tax Act and Enforcement Rules). An incorporated company engaged in any taxable business is, however, required to register as a VAT business operator (營業人) at establishment regardless of projected revenue; a JV operating programmatic AdTech/SSP services will register from day one and file business tax returns every two months.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rated export VAT treatment (0%) applies under Business Tax Act Art. 7 to: (i) physical export of goods from Taiwan customs territory; (ii) services performed in Taiwan but used outside Taiwan, and services ancillary to exports; (iii) services rendered to foreign enterprises/institutions/individuals without a Taiwan fixed place of business where benefits are enjoyed offshore — this covers the programmatic advertising/SSP/DSP and data-services model of CN_JV_LTD when sold to offshore advertisers, ad exchanges, or publishers. The zero-rated supply must be invoiced at 0%, and the associated input VAT is creditable and refundable (退稅) via the bi-monthly business tax return and the Art. 39 refund procedure. Substantiation requires showing outbound movement (goods) or offshore use/benefit (services), an overseas recipient, and eventual foreign-exchange remittance into Taiwan.

Taiwan's closed-loop fiscal-invoice system requires every VAT business operator to use tax-authority-allocated uniform-invoice (統一發票) number ranges and to issue invoices for all taxable sales, including zero-rated exports. E-invoicing (電子發票) is adopted through certification and enrollment on the MOF E-Invoice Integration Service Platform (財政部電子發票整合服務平台); designated large-volume and high-risk industries must transmit e-invoice data in (near) real time via the encrypted Turnkey API (Turnkey XML transmission) which performs schema validation and assigns the platform-controlled invoice number — this is Taiwan's functional equivalent of real-time fiscal clearance, without digital stamping. The MOF is progressively mandating real-time e-invoice transmission for newly registered B2B/B2C issuers, and cross-checks invoice lineage, cancellation reconciliation, and AI risk-scoring on inter-company invoice patterns. Non-compliance (failure to transmit, suppression of invoicing, use of unauthorized number ranges) triggers penalties under Business Tax Act arts. 46–51 and possible suspension of business operations.

      
National Taxation Bureaus conduct strict documentary and data-driven audits of zero-rated export claims, cross-matching customs export declarations, shipping manifests, e-invoice data, and central-bank foreign-exchange records; artificial-intelligence risk flags apply to exporters with recurring late remittance, circular or shell-company payment chains, and round-tripping structures. If foreign-currency proceeds are not repatriated to Taiwan within the statutory 180-day deadline (extendable only upon approved application for justified commercial delay), the 0% rate is reversed and 5% business tax is assessed retroactively on the export value, together with late-payment surcharges (滯納金 up to 15%) and daily interest. Fraudulent zero-rating — false export declarations, inflated invoices, fictitious offshore recipients, or sham FX recycling — triggers Business Tax Act Art. 51 penalties of up to five times the evaded tax, suspension of business, criminal prosecution under Tax Collection Act Art. 41 (imprisonment up to five years), and extension of the tax-assessment window to seven years under Tax Collection Act Art. 21, meaning refunded input VAT and non-collected output VAT remain clawback-exposed for that full period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Renminbi is fully convertible for bona fide current-account items, including trade in services and post-tax profit remittances, subject to bank-level verification. Capital-account items such as foreign direct investment equity injections remain controlled and require SAFE foreign investment registration, bank confirmation, and use-of-funds compliance under the Foreign Investment Negative List. Profit distribution to the Taiwan partner is characterised as current-account outbound remittance; the bank requires audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, a valid board resolution authorising dividends, the original JV contract, and a completed cross-border declaration. The practical remittance hurdle is not current-account convertibility but the complete documentary chain and the absence of an OECD-style binding double-tax treaty; the Cross-Strait tax arrangement reduces withholding tax where the beneficial ownership test is satisfied.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
There is no mandatory conversion ratio under SAFE's contemporary foreign exchange monitoring framework; the JV may retain 100% of export/adtech service proceeds in a domestic foreign-currency account or settle all or part into RMB at a designated bank. Statutory discipline is enforced through total-amount monitoring rather than fixed surrender: goods export proceeds must be repatriated within the standard trade settlement window monitored against customs data, and service export proceeds, including programmatic advertising income, must be received by the contractual due date. SAFE's data-driven supervision will downgrade a chronic over-due exporter from Class A to Class B/C, triggering bank-level presentation of underlying contracts, invoices, customs or BOP declarations, and cash-flow justifications; parking receivables in an offshore EMI or a non-approved settlement account in lieu of repatriation is unlawful.

      

      
Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, exercising delegated regulatory authority under the People's Bank of China (PBOC); SAFE branch filing, designated foreign exchange bank supervision, and PBOC anti-money-laundering oversight collectively govern the cross-border treasury operations of the PRC-incorporated Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: Low (1/5) as a primary treasury rail for the PRC JV. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, or Dukascopy cannot lawfully replace a PRC-licensed bank as the operating account provider of a mainland incorporated entity. PBOC and SAFE require all cross-border trade and capital receipts to be settled in a designated domestic foreign-exchange bank or through a licensed PRC non-bank payment institution; using an EMI as an autonomous account holder would create an unlawful interposition and expose the treasury to AML, sanctions, and FX violation risks. The viable model permits an EMI only as an instruction intermediary forwarding settlements to a domestic designated bank, never as the onshore legal account custodian.

Defense evidence: Publisher onboarding and KYB records, agency agreements, contractual chain diagrams, payment aggregation and settlement licences, evidence of economic substance and tax residence of each receiving entity, sanctions and adverse-media screening results, and monthly intercompany or vendor settlement summary explaining the white-labelling, forward contracting, or fee-sharing arrangement.

      

      
      

        Transactions denominated in digital advertising credits, media offset balances, or barter units producing unexplained FX remittances
        
Root cause: Transactions denominated in digital advertising credits, media offset balances, or barter units producing unexplained FX remittances

        
Defense evidence: Contractual provisions defining media credits and settlement-in-kind, independent valuation report for non-cash consideration, audited journal entries converting credits to monetary receivables, SAFE pre-consultation memo or legal opinion, and a written reconciliation explaining why the clearing value appears as a cash FX flow.

      

      
      

        Suspected intercompany netting or treasury concentration between the PRC JV, Taiwan partner, and offshore subsidiaries
        
Root cause: Suspected intercompany netting or treasury concentration between the PRC JV, Taiwan partner, and offshore subsidiaries

        
Defense evidence: Intercompany service or treasury-management agreement, China transfer-pricing documentation file, monthly netting summary with underlying invoices, bank-affirmed approval or confirmation that netting is not circumventing SAFE verification, and copies of all underlying cross-border settlement instructions and tax receipts.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The Taiwan partner's capital injection must be routed by direct SWIFT MT103 from the partner's bank account in Taiwan or the partner's nominated offshore bank to the JV's FDI foreign exchange capital account at a high-viability designated bank such as Bank of China. The wire must name the remitter exactly as the certified shareholder, cite the SAFE-specific foreign investment registration code of the JV, and carry a bank declaration purpose code for direct investment equity capital under the PRC Balance of Payments statistical reporting framework. The receiving bank will verify that the amount does not exceed the registered paid-in capital, that the remitter is not an unrelated third party, and that no portion is disguised as shareholder loan unless the shareholder loan has separate SAFE registration. After settlement, capital funds may be transferred from the FDIFC account to the RMB basic account only after the bank completes the negative-list use review; every expense item must be matched by invoices and payment vouchers. For RMB-denominated cross-strait capital inflows, use the cross-strait RMB clearing mechanism via Bank of China Taipei branch and CIPS, with the wire purpose declared as 'cross-border RMB foreign direct investment capital injection' and verified against the Foreign Investment Information Report.For programmatic advertising inventory payouts, supply-side platform fees, publisher commissions, and software licensing settlements, the primary clearing rail is a direct SWIFT MT103 or SWIFT gpi instruction drawn on the JV's operational foreign-currency account at its designated bank. The payment should be tagged with the relevant service-trade purpose code, typically reflecting advertising, marketing support, or software services depending on the bank's BOP declaration mapping. The bank verification bundle must include the underlying commercial contract, the exact commercial invoice, a performance or settlement report proving the service was rendered, any withholding tax certificate if the payment is subject to PRC source tax, and beneficial ownership confirmation for the recipient. CIPS is the preferred companion rail for RMB outbound settlements to Taiwan and other participating jurisdictions; for higher-value ad-tech transactions to physical-media vendors or hardware suppliers, a Commercial Letter of Credit is permissible only when the payment secures physical goods, and is not appropriate for pure digital inventory disbursements.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards (USD/CNY and EUR/CNY) executed with a SAFE-licensed domestic bank under a genuine current-account or capital-account underlying exposure.Foreign-exchange swaps (FX Spot/Forward swap combinations) used for treasury liquidity conversion between RMB and settlement currencies.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) between CNY and USD for long-term capital protection on FDI debt, intercompany loans, or committed equity contributions.Vanilla foreign-exchange options, including collars, spread options, and participating forwards, structured by a licensed PRC bank with adequate risk-disclosure and underlying trade verification.Offshore or Taiwan-market RMB non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only when booked through an approved offshore treasury vehicle; an onshore PRC bank cannot write RMB NDF contracts. For TWD/CNY exposure, treasury risk should be converted into a USD-leg through deliverable onshore forwards due to the shallow TWD/RMB liquidity.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low (1/5) as a primary treasury rail for the PRC JV. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, or Dukascopy cannot lawfully replace a PRC-licensed bank as the operating account provider of a mainland incorporated entity. PBOC and SAFE require all cross-border trade and capital receipts to be settled in a designated domestic foreign-exchange bank or through a licensed PRC non-bank payment institution; using an EMI as an autonomous account holder would create an unlawful interposition and expose the treasury to AML, sanctions, and FX violation risks. The viable model permits an EMI only as an instruction intermediary forwarding settlements to a domestic designated bank, never as the onshore legal account custodian.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China maintains a regulated partial-convertibility FX regime: current account items (trade in goods and services, dividend remittances, and ordinary operating payments) are generally convertible at banks upon documentary verification, while capital account items (foreign direct investment, cross-border financing, and securities investment) remain subject to SAFE registration, filing, and in some cases prior approval. For this Taiwan-funded WFOE, the B2B MRO e-commerce receipts from overseas buyers fall under the goods trade foreign exchange supervision framework: the company must join the Trade Foreign Exchange Receipts and Payments Online Catalogue as an A-Category enterprise, and banks will settle trade receipts without per-transaction authorization provided the trade background is verifiable through customs declarations, contracts, invoices, and logistics records. Export proceeds enjoy 100 percent retention in foreign currency accounts; no mandatory surrender or conversion obligation exists under current SAFE rules. Profit remittances to the Taiwan shareholder are permitted only after the registered capital is fully injected and verified, the Chinese CPA annual audit is filed, corporate income tax and dividend withholding are settled, and the bank verifies the prior-year audited results; this creates a practical remittance hurdle of two to four weeks of documentation processing. Capital account inflows require a SAFE Foreign Investment registration with the settlement bank before the injection, and the funds must enter a designated capital escrow account; conversion of the capital into CNY is allowed for genuine business operating expenses and is prohibited for speculative securities or entrusted loans under the Foreign Investment Negative List. Overall, CNY convertibility for current-account operating flows is high, while capital-account convertibility is medium-high only when the WFOE sustains a flawless compliance documentation trail.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 2012 SAFE reform and the current Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Guidelines, no mandatory surrender ratio applies: exporters may keep 100 percent of export receipts in their foreign currency current account. The overriding statutory duty is timely repatriation: all export foreign exchange proceeds for goods actually exported from China must be collected into a domestic goods trade FX account within 180 days from the customs export declaration date, extendable to 390 days after a formal filing to the local SAFE branch for valid commercial causes. On receipt, the A-Category enterprise may either retain the foreign currency or settle into CNY at the bank spot rate; the bank reports the transaction under the goods trade balance-of-payments codes and matches it against the customs electronic data. Delinquent collection beyond the statutory window triggers a downgrade to B-Category status, which imposes per-transaction verification and a heavier documentary burden on all subsequent trade receipts and payments.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), which serves as the Central Bank of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as a primary treasury rail. European or Singapore-licensed EMI and neobank platforms cannot open domestic RMB current accounts or obtain CNY clearing membership in mainland China, and they are legally barred by PBOC and SAFE from acting as the receiving bank for SAFE-registered FDI capital injection or from performing capital-account FX settlement. Cross-border e-commerce payment service providers holding a PBOC Payment Business License may provide offshore card collection in USD or EUR, but the ultimate liquidity must be repatriated into the WFOE domestic goods trade FX account under the trade supervision framework. Therefore, the blueprint permits a licensed PSP for card acquiring only and mandates a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank for all capital and trade settlement rails; the overall viability rating for EMIs as account providers is Low and as payment facilitators is Medium.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Before any remittance, the WFOE completes SAFE direct-investment FX registration with the receiving Tier-1 bank and obtains the Foreign Exchange Business Number for the FDI capital escrow account; the Taiwan shareholder then dispatches a SWIFT MT103 to that account, with the payment narrative quoting the WFOE name and the SAFE direct-investment purpose code for equity capital injection. The receiving bank validates the purpose code, credits the escrow account, and permits conversion into CNY only for lawful operating purposes under the Foreign Investment Negative List, uploading the capital account data to the SAFE system. The WFOE simultaneously retains a licensed Chinese CPA to perform statutory capital verification once the funds clear, producing the capital verification report required for updating the registered capital in the State Administration for Market Regulation corporate registry. Any change in the investment schedule, repatriation of excess funds, or a shareholder loan facility must be separately re-registered; using a fintech EMI or a credit-card top-up for capital injection would be rejected by the bank and would constitute a compliance violation.For the B2B MRO export model, outbound liquidity clears through the China goods trade framework: the WFOE must be listed in the Trade FX Collection and Payment Catalogue as an A-Category enterprise at the settlement bank, and each disbursement is matched against the contract, commercial invoice, packing list, and transportation document. Low-value open-account supplier payments to Taiwan or third-country sources of MRO hardware are executed by SWIFT MT103 with settlement in one to two banking days; bulk consignments are transported via a documentary commercial letter of credit (MT700 or MT707) or a documentary collection (DP or DA) when the supplier requests bill-drawee protection; and cross-border Chinese yuan settlement through the CIPS system is available onshore to reduce FX conversion cost for Taiwanese suppliers accepting CNY. Chargebacks and refunds arising from credit-card e-commerce returns must be sourced from the acquiring institution refund rail and re-credited to the original card; if a refund must be made outside that rail, the WFOE issues an MT103 current-account refund with the chargeback reference, platform order number, and original card transaction audit trail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$50 million in aggregate related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File preparation; NT$200 million in aggregate related-party transactions triggers Master File; Country-by-Country Report threshold is group consolidated revenue above NT$27 billion.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan applies the Economic Substance Doctrine through Article 43-4 of the Income Tax Act and the Tax Transparency and Anti-Tax Avoidance Act; a WFOE in the B2B MRO e-commerce sector must show actual management, personnel, logistics infrastructure, and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Taiwan, such as order processing, risk assumption, inventory control, pricing strategy, and procurement negotiation; if the China parent exercises substantial decision-making, the WFOE may be treated as a shell, and its profits will be re-attributed to the resident party; the CIGA analysis must be embedded in the Master File and updated annually.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Tax Collection Act, a penalty of twice the underpaid tax is assessed, in addition to the underpaid tax itself; criminal prosecution under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act (Tax Evasion) carries imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to NT$50 million; an arrest warrant may be issued for absconding taxpayers.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil under Article 25 of the Company Act and Article 23 of the Tax Collection Act, making directors personally and jointly liable for the company's unpaid taxes and penalties; personal assets, bank accounts and private properties are frozen; directors are subject to a departure ban (blacklisting) preventing overseas travel; a criminal conviction imposes imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years, plus disgorgement of evaded assets.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Article 43-4 of the Income Tax Act and the Tax Transparency and Anti-Tax Avoidance Act, the tax authority may re-characterize the entire revenue as directly derived by the China parent and assess tax on a deemed basis; treaty benefits are denied and a retroactive reassessment for up to 10 years is authorized; a penalty of 2 to 4 times the deficient tax is imposed depending on intent.

Director personal liability: Directors are treated as 'beneficial owner' claimants under the General Anti-Avoidance Doctrine; they become jointly and severally liable for the recaptured tax and surtax; the National Tax Administration can issue a restraining order on personal property and a travel restriction; criminal exposure under the Company Act's false reporting provision leads to imprisonment of up to 7 years and a fine of NT$20 million, plus mandatory corporate deregistration.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Under Article 114-1 of the Income Tax Act, a TP adjustment results in a penalty equal to 2x the underpaid tax; failure to prepare a TP report carries a fine of NT$30,000 to NT$100,000; if the TP report is falsified, the penalty escalates to 3x underpaid tax and criminal liability under the Commercial Accounting Act and Securities Crime Act; tax authorities may also impute unrealized profit based on the profit split method.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation commit perjury and falsification of financial records under Article 71 of the Commercial Accounting Act, carrying a sentence of 3 to 10 years imprisonment and a fine of NT$5 million to NT$50 million; they are personally liable for the recaptured tax and incurred penalties; assets, shareholdings and trust investments are subject to freezing; the director may be prohibited from serving as a director of any company in Taiwan for a period of 5 years.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is imposed by the Taiwan Company Act, and no blocked escrow account is mandated for a private equity buyout SPV. The paid-up capital must be subscribed in the Articles of Incorporation and remitted to a bank account opened in Taiwan. A certified public accountant capital verification report is required when paid-in capital exceeds NT$5 million or when the registry expressly requires verification. Foreign-currency capital contributions from offshore shareholders must be routed through a Taiwan foreign-exchange bank and reported to the Taiwan Central Bank for subsequent remittance freedom in respect of dividends, loan repayments, or share disposals.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable to a purely financial SPV. If a physical registered office or service office is occupied, the premises must hold a current fire safety inspection certificate under the Taiwan Fire Services Act and Building Act, and any occupied workplace must comply with the Taiwan Occupational Safety and Health Act, including exit marking, emergency response plans, electrical safety, and general workplace hygiene inspection.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taiwan (R.O.C.) through its Department of Taxation and the competent local National Taxation Bureau for the entity's registered place of business; electronic portals are the E-Tax Portal (https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw) and the E-Invoice Integration Service Platform (https://www.einvoice.nat.gov.tw).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must appoint a tax representative in Taiwan and file the statutory tax-registration application with the competent National Taxation Bureau before commencing activity. The applicant must submit: (i) the foreign certificate of incorporation or equivalent constitutional instrument; (ii) a resolution approving the Taiwan business/registration; (iii) the appointed tax representative's letter of appointment, passport, and resident certificate, together with the representative's Unified Business Number/TIN; (iv) evidence of the Taiwan physical place of business (lease or service-agreement contract) or, for a pure offshore electronic-service supply, a cross-border e-commerce VAT registration declaration; and (v) the standardised application form used by the National Taxation Bureau for tax registration. For non-resident cross-border e-commerce operators, the registration is made under the simplified tax-registration regime for foreign electronic-service suppliers; after documentary review, the National Taxation Bureau issues an 8-digit Unified Business Number (TIN) and tax-registration certificate. If the foreign entity has no fixed place and no trade presence in Taiwan, no general VAT registration is required; VAT withholding obligations may apply on Taiwan-source service payments by the Taiwan payer.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not operate a US-style check-the-box election, does not provide pass-through classification for limited-company SPVs, and has no domestic hybrid-mismatch entity election. A foreign entity with a Taiwan branch is taxed at entity level on Taiwan-source profit; a Taiwan-incorporated JV is taxed as a resident entity. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NT$480,000 per year for non-resident electronic-services suppliers; NT$80,000 per month of goods sales or NT$40,000 per month of services sales as the statutory taxability threshold for domestic/onshore small business. A domestic entity with a fixed place of business must register before commencement of business irrespective of projected turnover where output is not otherwise exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate when they are actually exported from Taiwan: the goods must be sold in the export trade, shipped out of the customs territory, and supported by a customs export declaration. Qualifying offshore services (including electronic and digital services) are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident for use or consumption outside Taiwan and paid in foreign exchange. The zero-rated status must be proven by contemporaneous export, logistics, contractual, and foreign-exchange repatriation records at the time of the VAT periodic filing; input VAT associated with zero-rated exports may be reclaimed through the VAT refund mechanism after statutory scrutiny.

  • Customs Export Declaration as released by the Taiwan Customs Administration
  • Commercial Invoice and Uniform Invoice/Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill/Courier Receipt proving actual export movement
  • Export sales contract or purchase order containing the price and incoterms
  • Bank foreign-exchange settlement certificate or inbound SWIFT/payment record evidencing receipt of the export consideration from the foreign buyer

Electronic Uniform Invoices are mandatory for VAT-registered enterprises that choose to use e-invoices and for designated categories. The issuer must connect via the MOF E-Invoice Integration Service Platform Turnkey service (machine-to-machine API) to upload invoice data; B2C e-invoices are typically transmitted within 48 hours of issuance, and B2B data is filed through the same channel. Taiwan does not operate a real-time continuous-transaction-control (CTC) fiscal clearance model; the anti-fraud mechanism is a platform-validated electronic invoice number, QR code, and random anti-fraud selection code. Paper uniform invoices remain lawful where e-invoicing is not mandated.

The National Taxation Bureau cross-checks export zero-rating against customs release records, bank foreign-exchange receipts, and e-invoice data. If any required export document is missing, the foreign-exchange consideration is not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period, or the export is found to be simulated or misdeclared, the tax authority retrospectively reclassifies the supply as taxable, assesses 5% VAT on the sales consideration, imposes overdue tax interest and late penalties, and may impose a punitive fine of up to five times the evaded tax pursuant to the Business Tax Act. Falsifying export documents or deliberately obtaining a zero-rate can trigger criminal tax-evasion liability under the Tax Collection Act and Criminal Code, including imprisonment; the filing position may also be reopened for audit beyond the ordinary assessment period if fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan's FX regime is an open but managed system. Current-account transactions such as trade payments, service fees, royalties, dividends, and interest can be converted and remitted through CBC-designated foreign exchange banks without central bank approval, provided the remitter submits underlying invoice/contract documentation and a foreign exchange settlement or declaration form. Capital-account flows, including equity injection, fund redemption, M&A consideration, investor loans, and profit repatriation outside standardized exemptions, require CBC filing; where a People's Republic of China entity is an investor, the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) must grant cross-strait investment approval before the inbound remittance is accepted. The New Taiwan Dollar is not fully internationalized and remains subject to rate management by the CBC, which also monitors settlement through the domestic banking units. There is no statutory surrender duty on export receipts; a Taiwan corporation may keep export proceeds in foreign currency or sell them, subject to transaction-backed declarations. Profit remittance is permissible only after audited financial statements, tax filing, withholding settlement, the original capital-verification report, and the bank's CBC remittance coding are in place. For the PRC-side partner, SAFE outbound investment registration and the bank's capital account filing are mandatory before funds can leave China, and any mismatch with Taiwan's MOEA/CBC requirements can freeze the entire clearing chain.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a Taiwan-domiciled exporter, the CBC requires export proceeds to be collected through an authorized designated bank within the statutory period, generally 120 days after shipment unless an extension is approved; no mandatory conversion ratio applies because Taiwan does not impose a surrender regime. The receipt must be reported on the FX settlement/declaration form and may be retained in USD or another permitted currency, or converted into New Taiwan Dollars at market rate. If the Chinese joint-venture partner books export revenue on the PRC side, SAFE's current-account rules require repatriation of those proceeds into China within the applicable filing window and settlement through a designated bank; any offshore retention must be individually approved by SAFE. The SPV itself is not an exporter unless it takes title to goods, in which case these rules bind its treasury.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC) as the onshore Taiwan FX authority, together with the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for any PRC-controlled capital flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Pure fintech EMIs have low viability in Taiwan. Taiwan's Banking Act and CBC regulations reserve multi-currency corporate lending, foreign exchange, and cross-border clearing for licensed banks and designated financial institutions; an unlicensed EMI cannot open a legally effective corporate account or connect directly to CBC settlement systems. Digital-only banks in Taiwan hold commercial banking licenses and may be used, but they do not eliminate the underlying KYC or the requirement for a physical or video-verified board mandate. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex should not be used as the primary treasury rail for the SPV because they lack Taiwan deposit-taking authority and any partner-bank arrangement reintroduces the same Tier-1-bank compliance gate.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder-loan proceeds must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the SPV's capital escrow account at the chosen Tier-1 Taiwan bank. The wire must name the SPV exactly, cite the MOEA Investment Commission approval number or filing reference, state the CBC foreign exchange purpose code for direct investment, and identify the ultimate remitter to avoid incoming-remittance repricing. For a PRC investor, the PRC bank must also cite SAFE ODI/filing and cross-border capital account registration before releasing the RMB or foreign currency. On receipt, the Taiwan bank will file the inward remittance declaration with the CBC, freeze the funds in the capital escrow bucket, and require a Taiwan-licensed CPA to issue a capital verification report tied to the purchase price and the exact NTD/USD exchange rate. The escrow may be released only after the CPA verification, the bank's enhanced KYC committee sign-off, and the corporate authorized-signatory instruction.All trade and operating disbursements should clear through the Tier-1 bank's domestic banking unit or offshore banking unit. Direct cash payments must use SWIFT MT103 with an underlying invoice, contract, or disbursement instruction and the applicable CBC trade purpose code. For physical goods, use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit transmitted by SWIFT MT700/MT707 or, for smaller counterparties without LC appetite, a documentary collection (D/P or D/A) against full set of shipping documents. The SPV may also use a Bank Payment Obligation (BPO) under the Uniform Rules for Bank Payment Obligations to reduce documentary friction with the syndicated lender. RMB payments to PRC suppliers should be routed through the CBC-authorized RMB clearing channel or via the bank's cross-border RMB settlement service, but in all cases the disbursement must have a matching import/export declaration or M&A payment schedule to survive bank compliance review.Onshore deliverable FX forwards in TWD/USD, TWD/CNY, and USD/CNY up to the amount of documented trade, loan, or dividend exposure, with a maximum tenor of 12 months and no cash-settlement delivery unless an underlying invoice or debt repayment is proven.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps through the Tier-1 bank's DBU/OBU, used strictly to align the currency base of the syndicated loan with the SPV's equity and receivable portfolio; swaps must be back-to-back with the funding leg and cannot create a leveraged speculative FX position.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in TWD or CNY may be executed only through a CBC-approved offshore branch or OBU, and only when onshore delivery is legally or commercially impossible; the SPV must submit a board-approved hedging policy, monthly mark-to-market report, and underlying exposure schedule to the bank, and speculative NDF trading is strictly prohibited.

Taiwan's Banking Act and CBC regulations reserve multi-currency corporate lending, foreign exchange, and cross-border clearing for licensed banks and designated financial institutions; an unlicensed EMI cannot open a legally effective corporate account or connect directly to CBC settlement systems. Digital-only banks in Taiwan hold commercial banking licenses and may be used, but they do not eliminate the underlying KYC or the requirement for a physical or video-verified board mandate. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex should not be used as the primary treasury rail for the SPV because they lack Taiwan deposit-taking authority and any partner-bank arrangement reintroduces the same Tier-1-bank compliance gate.

Turnover: A mandatory independent audit by a licensed Taiwan CPA is required: (1) for public companies and companies whose shares are listed or traded on the Taiwan Stock Exchange or Taipei Exchange under the Securities and Exchange Act; (2) for non-public companies whose paid-in capital exceeds TWD 30 million (approximately USD 1.0 million) under Company Act Article 20, as designated by MOEA; (3) for financial institutions, banks, insurers, securities firms, financial holding companies, and other FSC-supervised enterprises regardless of capital size; (4) for foreign-invested companies, including Sino-foreign equity joint ventures, when the Investment Commission approval or foreign investment permit expressly conditions the annual report on certified CPA financial statements; and (5) for enterprises applying for a Blue Return or certain tax incentives that require a CPA-attested financial report.

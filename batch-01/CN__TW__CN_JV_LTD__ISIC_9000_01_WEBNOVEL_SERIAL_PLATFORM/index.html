Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Taxation Administration, Ministry of Finance, Taiwan (R.O.C.) with the online electronic tax registration and filing portal at https://www.tax.nat.gov.tw; e-Invoice cloud services are administered through the E-Invoice Integration Platform at https://www.einvoice.nat.gov.tw

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Taiwan business presence, such as the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, must first complete corporate registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs before obtaining tax identity. The responsible person of the foreign entity must hold a valid resident certificate (ARC or representative office registration) for the Taiwan branch or subsidiary. The entity must submit the application for tax registration to the jurisdiction of the local National Taxation Bureau after business registration, using the standard Business Registration Application for Tax Purpose form together with the authenticated articles of incorporation, the parent company certificate of incorporation, a power of attorney for the local tax agent, lease or ownership documents for the Taiwan operating address, and identification of the responsible person and shareholders. The National Taxation Bureau will issue a single Tax Identification Number (TIN) that serves for both income tax and Business Tax (VAT) purposes, and will activate the Uniform Invoice registration. For a pure digital platform with no physical Taiwan office, a non-resident foreign entity may alternatively satisfy its tax registration obligation by appointing a tax representative and registering for cross-border e-commerce Business Tax under the Online Platform Operator registration mechanism, after which the same TIN is used for VAT on Taiwan-source digital services and withholding obligations on cross-border transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch tax classification elections. A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture incorporated under the Taiwan Company Act is a resident company subject to entity-level income tax. A foreign non-resident entity may choose structurally between operating as a branch (subjecting Taiwan-source profits to business tax and branch profit remittance tax) or as a subsidiary (subjecting distributed dividends to withholding tax), but this is a corporate formation choice, not an elective tax classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no monetary turnover threshold that permits deferral of standard VAT registration; every entity selling taxable goods or services in Taiwan must complete tax registration before commencing business and must issue Uniform Invoices from the first sale. The only statutory concession concerns small-scale enterprises: if average monthly sales are below NT$200,000 and the business does not issue uniform invoices, the National Taxation Bureau may impose the simplified 1% business tax rate in lieu of the standard 5% output VAT. For the webnovel subscription platform operated by the JV, full 5% VAT registration and Uniform Invoice issuance are mandatory from the date of the first Taiwan-source digital service transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 7 of the Taiwan Business Tax Act for exported goods and for services rendered to foreign enterprises, foreign persons, or overseas users provided the services are not used within Taiwan. For the webnovel serial platform, subscription and reading services delivered through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing to non-Taiwan resident readers qualify for zero-rating when the sales revenue is not derived from Taiwan domestic users, when the service is performed entirely offshore or delivered through overseas platform markets, and when the foreign exchange consideration is remitted to the Taiwan operating entity. The exemption from the 5% output tax requires the taxable entity to substantiate offshore use through platform settlement reports, territory-specific app store fiscal reports, subscriber IP country evidence, and the absence of Taiwan-source consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Manifest) submitted via the Taiwan Customs online system for physical exports, where any physical fulfillment or auxiliary merchandise exists
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment of exported goods from Taiwan customs territory to the foreign destination, when applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Central Bank of Taiwan authorized bank Foreign Exchange Settlement Receipt evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the overseas counterparty or app store platform remitter
  • Digital platform settlement statement from Apple App Store Connect and Google Play Console showing gross sales, app store commissions, tax-adjusted proceeds, territory breakdown, and non-Taiwan reader consumption
  • Signed international content licensing or distribution agreement between the Taiwan JV and the offshore platform entity specifying the cross-border digital service nature of the subscription revenue
  • Service delivery evidence including server logs, IP geolocation records, subscriber domicile registration data, and payment gateway country codes demonstrating that the webnovel services were consumed exclusively outside Taiwan
  • Foreign exchange repatriation confirmation from the Taiwan bank showing the remittance was booked as export proceeds and settled to the JV account within the statutory collection period

Taiwan mandates electronic Uniform Invoice clearance for registered businesses. The e-Invoice system requires real-time transmission of invoice data to the Taxation Administration through the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Turnkey (T-K) API or a certified private gateway; invoice numbers and random codes are centrally assigned; each invoice is digitally signed and timestamped before display to the customer. For B2C digital subscription platforms, cloud e-invoices must be issued and transmitted immediately upon the successful charge through Apple In-App Purchase or Google Play Billing, and the platform operator must record the app store platform settlement data in the approved ESS-3 or ESS-10 XML formats for audit and refund purposes. The system also supports anti-counterfeit rapid-validation QR codes, prohibited-bracket controls, and automatic fraud marking on abnormal invoice sequences.

Taiwan tax authorities will disallow zero-rating if the exporter fails to produce the required customs export documents, foreign exchange remittance records, platform settlement statements, or evidence that digital services were actually consumed offshore. Upon audit or investigation, the National Taxation Bureau will assess the uncollected 5% Business Tax, impose overdue interest and late-payment surcharges, and levy administrative penalties under the Business Tax Act, which can reach up to several multiples of the under-collected tax where the erroneous zero-rating is found to be intentional or fraudulent. Use of fabricated foreign exchange receipts, forged customs declarations, fake offshore platform agreements, or circular remittance arrangements may result in criminal referral for tax evasion, seizure of assets, and personal liability of the responsible director and tax agent. The tax authority may also disallow input VAT credits associated with the zero-rated export revenues and claw back any VAT refund previously paid for the period under audit, together with civil penalties for materially misstated VAT returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime for the New Taiwan dollar. Current-account transactions, including subscription income from the webnovel app, cross-border author royalties, cloud-service payments and marketing disbursements, are generally convertible through an authorised FX bank upon submission of a Foreign Exchange Transaction Declaration and the underlying commercial documentation. The TWD is not a fully internationally deliverable reserve currency; offshore convertibility is limited to settlement through designated banks. Capital-account convertibility is restricted: inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, acquisitions, and outward repatriation of capital or dividends require case-by-case approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs and the filing of a prescribed foreign-exchange purpose code. Export proceeds from overseas digital content sales are subject to CBC monitoring; the exporter must either convert the foreign currency into TWD or deposit it in a foreign-currency deposit account at a designated bank, and customs or service-export declarations must be matched to bank receipts. Profit remittance hurdles are material: before any dividend can be sent to the mainland Chinese parent, the JV must complete a Taiwan CPA audit, file corporate income tax returns, obtain tax clearance, show distributable retained earnings under the Company Act, and obtain Investment Commission approval for the outward dividend remittance; the remitting bank will also require a board resolution, the audited financial statements, and proof that no registered capital or asset revaluation surplus is being repatriated instead of earned profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory rule: all export foreign-currency receipts, including revenue from digital content subscriptions treated as service exports, must be received and settled through an FX bank designated by the CBC within 180 days from the date of service provision or invoicing, unless an extension is approved by the CBC before the due date. Mandatory conversion ratio: there is currently no fixed statutory surrender ratio; the JV may retain 100% of eligible export proceeds in a foreign-currency deposit account or convert them to TWD at a licensed bank. However, the CBC may impose a temporary full or partial surrender directive during a balance-of-payments emergency, in which case the JV must sell the specified proportion at the prevailing central-bank-designated exchange rate. The bank will file each transaction with the CBC under the FXRS, and aggregated receipts above the declaration threshold require a detailed Foreign Exchange Transaction Declaration.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), exercising foreign exchange regulation through designated FX banks under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act and the Ministry of Economic Affairs Investment Commission for direct investment approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Negligible. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, Revolut or Payoneer are not licensed by the CBC and cannot open domestic foreign-currency accounts, receive the JV capital injection, issue regulated trade settlement, or convert TWD onshore. Taiwan legal order recognizes only licensed financial institutions as designated FX banks; an offshore EMI may only serve as a supplementary collection conduit if the underlying merchant account is held by a licensed Taiwan bank, and even then the JV treasury must be domiciled in a CBC-designated bank. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% for non-resident corporate shareholders (Article 88 and Article 68 of the Income Tax Act). Under the Cross-Strait Double Taxation Agreement, mainland China corporate shareholders may qualify for a reduced 10% rate, and 5% for direct shareholding of 25% or more, if the beneficial owner requirement is satisfied; however, the Taiwan competent authority has issued a formal interpretation (台財稅字第10804528220號) that the dividend withholding rate for mainland China residents under the Cross-Strait Agreement is aligned with domestic rates at the time of distribution, and unless the mutual agreement attachment is ratified by further administrative exchange, treaty practice has historically applied the 10% / 5% rates only when the Chinese tax authority provides an effective TRC and the application is pre-approved by the competent authority.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For resident corporations, the annual return is due on the last day of the fifth month after the fiscal year-end (e.g., 31 May for calendar-year taxpayers). For non-resident corporations with a fixed place of business or branch, the same fifth-month deadline applies to the branch annual return. For non-resident corporations without a fixed place of business, the tax is settled through final withholding at the source; there is no annual return unless the non-resident elects to file a deemed-deficit return or is required to file a simplified return under Article 41 of the Income Tax Act, with a deadline of 10 days after the transaction or 10 days after the withholding agent pays the proceeds, and always by the statutory filing deadline of the following year for cost or expense deduction claims.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan Transfer Pricing Rules (營利事業所得稅不合常規移轉訂價查核準則) require preparation of a Transfer Pricing Report (TP Report) when the total amount of intercompany transactions with related parties in a fiscal year exceeds NT$200 million, or NT$50 million for transactions involving intangible property or services; but a simplified disclosure is required on the annual CIT return (Form 16) for any related-party transaction exceeding NT$20 million. Even below these thresholds, the tax authority may require contemporaneous documentation if the annual related-party transaction amount exceeds NT$50 million or if the profit ratio is abnormal. For cross-border digital platform operations, the Taiwan JV must maintain Local File and Master File documentation when the ultimate parent's consolidated revenue exceeds NT$30 billion or the Taiwan entity's related-party transactions exceed NT$200 million.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the Caribbean or UAE; however, the substance-over-form doctrine is codified in Article 12-1 of the Tax Collection Act and Article 43-1 of the Income Tax Act. A JV used solely as a flow-through invoicing entity without real employees, office premises, and core business functions would be attacked under the general anti-avoidance rule. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for a digital webnovel platform include content acquisition and editing, platform server operation, user acquisition and subscription billing, payment processing via Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, and risk management. If the CIGA are performed by a mainland China parent or Taiwanese founder outside Taiwan, the Taiwan JV may be deemed a shell and its income reallocated to the actual decision-maker, causing additional tax, penalties, and in egregious cases criminal liability. The Taiwan tax authority also applies the 'place of effective management' test under Article 12-1 and the MOF interpretation to treat a board of directors that meets outside Taiwan or makes key management decisions in mainland China as creating a permanent establishment / actual management place in mainland China, with corresponding implications for Cross-Strait tax status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Profit Declarations (Income Tax Act Article 110)

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 110: understatement of tax due due to fraud or intentional concealment incurs a penalty of up to 3 times the underpaid tax amount. Tax Collection Act Article 41: tax evasion by fraudulent or improper means is punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years, concurrently with a fine of NT$60,000 or above; if the evasion amount exceeds NT$1 million, the court may impose imprisonment for 1 to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Taiwan courts pierce the corporate veil under Article 154 of the Company Act only in cases of severe abuse, but the Tax Collection Act Article 49 and Article 13 of the Income Tax Act impose personal liability on the responsible person (representative director) for unpaid taxes and penalties if the company fails to pay after summary assessment and the responsible person caused the failure through culpable misconduct. Criminal liability attaches personally: directors, CFOs, and individuals who sign the tax return may be sentenced to imprisonment up to 5 years and face asset seizure and civil restitution of the evaded tax. If the director is a Taiwanese resident and actively orchestrates cross-border profit shifting, he or she may also be blacklisted by the Ministry of Justice and the Customs Administration, receiving a travel ban under the Enforcement Act for Tax Collection (Administrative Execution Act) and a court order prohibiting departure for up to 5 years (Article 24 of the Administrative Execution Act).
Shell Company Abuse and Transfer Pricing Fraud (Income Tax Act Article 43-1)

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 43-1 allows the tax authority to reallocate income and disallow excessive related-party deductions; the same article supports penalties under Article 110: up to 3 times the understated tax. Failure to prepare the TP documentation when required by the Regulations Governing Determination of Non-Arm's-Length Transfer Pricing incurs a fine from NT$30,000 to NT$300,000 under Article 9 and may cause the tax authority to adopt an unfavorable deemed-profit assessment. The 12% retained earnings surtax under the Additional Agricultural and Industrial Profits Surtax Act also applies to accumulated undistributed profits of the shell.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer pricing reports or collude with the parent company to fabricate agreements are criminally liable for helping evade tax under Tax Collection Act Article 41, punishable by up to 5 years imprisonment. Civil liability includes joint obligation for the re-assessed tax and penalties of up to 3 times the tax avoided. Where the Taiwan JV is a tool of the mainland China parent's digital platform operation, the Taiwan competent authority may apply the 'substance over form' doctrine to disregard the Taiwanese subsidiary and treat the mainland China parent as having a permanent establishment in Taiwan, leaving directors personally exposed to seizure and blacklisting. The Ministry of Financial Affairs may blacklist the director and CFO for tax delinquency, triggering a travel ban and automatic restriction on real property or bank account transfers.
Cross-Strait Profit Repatriation Without DTA Compliance (Cross-Strait Double Taxation Agreement Violation)

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 114: the withholding agent that fails to deduct tax as required must pay the unwithheld tax for the principal, plus a fine of 1 to 3 times the unpaid amount. Tax Collection Act Article 48-2: for inaccurate withholding returns, a penalty of 5% of the understated amount for every 2 months, up to 15% or NT$90,000 minimum, plus interest at the postal savings rate. If the false fee characterization is discovered, the recharacterized dividend is subject to 21% WHT and penalties under Article 110; the profit-seeking enterprise may also lose the right to claim the cross-strait treaty relief for the relevant tax year and face an upward adjustment of 20% CIT on the disguised distribution.

Director personal liability: The director or principal tax officer who personally signs the false withholding declaration or orders the mischaracterization of dividend remittance is personally liable under Article 114 of the Income Tax Act to the extent he or she is the 'responsible person' of the withholding agent. Criminal exposure arises under Tax Collection Act Article 42 (for withholding agent fraud): imprisonment up to 3 years, or up to 5 years if the amount exceeds NT$300,000. Civil liability: the responsible person is jointly and severally liable with the company for the unpaid tax liability, and the court may order asset restraint. If the delinquent tax exceeds a threshold and the responsible person fails to repay, the Ministry of Justice Administrative Enforcement Agency will list the director and the company on the tax delinquency blacklist, leading to a travel ban, preventing departure from Taiwan, and prohibiting acquisition or disposal of property, bank deposits, and shares. In egregious cases, the prosecutors may institute criminal proceedings concurrently with the civil suit for compensatory restitution to the national treasury.

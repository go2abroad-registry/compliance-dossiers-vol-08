Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (国家税务总局); online service portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (电子税务局) accessible via provincial STI domains; export-tax administration sub-portal: Export Tax Rebate System (出口退税管理系统); customs interface: China International Trade Single Window (中国国际贸易单一窗口).

        
E-government filing portal
Foreign investor incorporates the WFOE at SAMR (State Administration for Market Regulation) and automatically receives the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码), which serves as the statutory TIN; tax registration is completed online via the Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days of incorporation, with supplementary filings for bank deposit account numbers, accounting software details, and VAT general taxpayer status election.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise (居民企业) taxed on worldwide income at 25% CIT; VAT status defaults to general taxpayer (一般纳税人) for manufacturing exporters; no pass-through or flow-through tax treatment is recognized under PRC law. — Foreign investor incorporates the WFOE at SAMR (State Administration for Market Regulation) and automatically receives the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码), which serves as the statutory TIN; tax registration is completed online via the Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days of incorporation, with supplementary filings for bank deposit account numbers, accounting software details, and VAT general taxpayer status election.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation; the WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise (居民企业) taxed on worldwide income at 25% CIT; VAT status defaults to general taxpayer (一般纳税人) for manufacturing exporters; no pass-through or flow-through tax treatment is recognized under PRC law.

        
Available election forms
Elections available: (1) voluntary VAT general taxpayer election before the mandatory RMB 5 million annual sales threshold; (2) simplified VAT small-scale taxpayer election at 3% for limited domestic turnover, although export rebate qualification requires general taxpayer status; (3) CIT prepayment base elections (actual/prior-year/estimated); (4) annual election for the small low-profit enterprise preferential regime subject to statutory thresholds; no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections exist; anti-hybridity is enforced via CIT Art. 46 thin-capitalization rules and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No incorporation-based threshold: VAT registration is mandatory for all WFOEs from legal establishment; the small-scale taxpayer simplified regime applies only when annual taxable sales ≤ RMB 5,000,000, but export zero-rating and the 免抵退 (exemption-credit-refund) method are exclusively available to VAT general taxpayers.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
A production-enterprise WFOE is eligible for the export tax zero-rating and refund (免抵退) regime when: (1) the goods are self-produced frozen dim sum physically exported from mainland China to Taiwan via a seaport/airport; (2) the enterprise holds VAT general taxpayer status; (3) the export customs declaration, bill of lading, and foreign exchange receipt are all completed and internally consistent; (4) the rebate declaration is filed within the statutory window (no later than the following year's April 30 tax filing period); (5) export proceeds are received and verified via a bank settlement slip within 180 days of export customs clearance. Taiwan is recognized as a separate customs territory, so exports to Taiwan are treated as export transactions eligible for the zero VAT rate and full input VAT refund at the statutory export rebate rate (13% for HS 1902/1904/1905 pre-cooked frozen products; the enterprise must verify product-specific rebate codes in the current-year rebate rate schedule; reduced rebate months may apply to certain processed food codes requiring the 10% refund differential classified as a cost).

Fraudulent zero-rating (false customs declarations, fake B/Ls, over-invoicing, matched-pair round-tripping between mainland and Taiwan entities, or unrepatriated proceeds) triggers: (1) retrospective clawback of all VAT refunds with statutory late-payment interest at 0.05% per day; (2) an administrative penalty of 50%–500% of the reclaimed tax under the Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63; (3) a specific export-tax-fraud penalty of 1–5 times the defrauded amount; (4) criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 204 with imprisonment from 5 years to life for aggravated fraud; (5) revocation of VAT general taxpayer status and export rebate qualification with a minimum 3-year disqualification period; (6) inclusion in the joint disciplinary blacklist (失信名单) affecting customs clearance, banking, and cross-border settlement facilities.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Mainland China maintains current account convertibility for trade in goods and services, including profit remittances from a WFOE, which are processed by designated banks upon verification of audited financial statements, tax clearance, and the foreign exchange registration record. Capital account transactions remain subject to prior SAFE registration/approval, mandatory foreign exchange reporting, and negative-list restrictions on foreign investment. Profit remittance is permitted through the current account category, but requires documentary proof of distributable reserves and withholding tax settlement; no mandatory conversion of RMB profits is imposed, yet capital-account FX conversion must strictly adhere to the approved business scope and use-purpose verification under SAFE Circular 19 and subsequent rules.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
China has eliminated mandatory surrender of export proceeds since 2012. Export receipts in foreign currency must be repatriated to mainland China through a designated foreign exchange bank and either retained in the enterprise's foreign currency settlement account or converted into RMB at the enterprise's discretion. SAFE's trade verification system enforces a statutory collection timeline generally set at 180 days from the export customs declaration date for general trade, unless an extension is obtained from SAFE. For a frozen dim sum processing WFOE, export receipts must be matched with the China International Trade Single Window declarations and supplier invoices; no fixed mandatory conversion ratio applies to current account receipts.

      

      
Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Low. Offshore digital EMI and payment institutions are not licensed to open corporate accounts or provide proprietary clearing infrastructure within mainland China. A WFOE cannot lawfully rely on an offshore EMI as its primary treasury or settlement rail. Cross-border fund movements must flow through Chinese licensed banks or approved non-bank cross-border payment institutions acting as processors, with underlying trade or investment documentation available to SAFE and the bank. Offshore EMIs may support ancillary foreign currency receipts only by clearing via a corresponding Chinese bank account, which does not replace the need for a domestic corporate banking relationship.

Defense evidence: Complete corporate chain chart with registry extracts from Taiwan and mainland China; UBO declaration forms per mainland AML rules; certified copies of business registration and investment approval; board resolutions; audited financial statements of the shareholder; source-of-funds declaration supported by bank statements and sale/purchase agreements for the invested capital.

      

      
      

        Capital injection expected from a Taiwan entity whose main business is not clearly linked to food manufacturing.

Defense evidence: Master supply and co-manufacturing agreement between the WFOE and the Taiwan shareholder; food production license (SC license / food production permit) of the mainland factory; build-operate-transfer or equipment purchase contracts; customs declaration records for raw material imports; projected cash flow model of the dim sum production line; bank reference letter from the Taiwan shareholder's bank.

      

      
      

        High-volume related-party cross-border payments without clear commercial substance or order history.
        
Root cause: High-volume related-party cross-border payments without clear commercial substance or order history.

        
Defense evidence: Intercompany services or goods supply agreements reflecting arm's-length pricing; transfer pricing documentation or comparable analysis; detailed demand forecasts and purchase orders for quick-frozen steamed dim sum ingredients; board-approved treasury management policy; historical shipping and customs records; bank statements showing consistent operational cash flows.

      

      
      

        Beneficial owner or director identified as a politically exposed person (PEP).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound FDI capital from the Taiwan shareholder must be remitted through a designated bank in mainland China to the WFOE's foreign exchange capital account (FDI Capital Account) using the SAFE-approved remittance purpose code for foreign direct investment (generally 'Direct investment – Capital injection' / code 622011 or the relevant prevailing SAFE code set). Prior to remittance, the WFOE must complete the foreign investment information report with the Ministry of Commerce/SAMR and obtain the SAFE FX registration via the bank (via the FX Capital Account Registration system). The remitter must be the registered foreign shareholder; third-party remittance is not permitted unless the bank verifies a genuine chain of funds and SAFE reporting. After funds are received, RMB conversion is permitted only for approved uses such as purchase of production equipment, raw materials for dim sum manufacturing, wages, rent, utilities, and other operating expenses within the WFOE's registered business scope. The bank will monitor the use of the converted RMB funds under the negative-list regime and may require filing of capital account use declarations.Outbound trade settlements from the mainland WFOE to Taiwan suppliers or service providers must clear through SWIFT MT103 wires or documentary trade instruments. For raw ingredient imports (e.g., frozen seafood fillings, flour, seasonings), the preferred rail is SWIFT MT103 with payment purpose code reflecting general trade settlement, reconciled against the China International Trade Single Window electronic customs declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, and packing list. For larger equipment purchases or long-dated supply contracts, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (sight L/C) issued by the WFOE's mainland bank in favor of the Taiwan beneficiary is recommended; settlement occurs against presentation of compliant shipping documents. All outward remittances must be matched to the underlying transaction in the bank's trade verification system (e.g., 货贸外汇收支申报), with applicable withholding tax certificates if service imports are involved. Payments to Taiwan may be subject to additional documentary scrutiny under SAFE's cross-border payment consistency verification, especially when the payee is a related party.Onshore RMB foreign exchange forwards (plain vanilla, trade-backed, tenors generally up to 12 months) permitted under SAFE real-demand verificationOnshore RMB cross-currency swaps (fixed-floating or fixed-fixed) executed with a licensed Chinese bank for hedging underlying trade or investment cash flows, subject to business genuineness documentationCFTC/ISDA-documented non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) available to the offshore Taiwan-side counterparty only; mainland WFOE is restricted from direct offshore NDF participation, but may indirectly hedge through the Taiwan parent treasury using back-to-back onshore forwards

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
20% corporate income tax on the taxable income of a Taiwan profit-seeking enterprise.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
21% withholding tax generally applies to dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a non-resident shareholder. The domestic rate may be reduced under an applicable Taiwan income tax agreement or other statutory relief, subject to eligibility and beneficial-ownership requirements.

An enterprise using an approved non-calendar fiscal year generally files within the fifth month after fiscal year-end. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan generally requires a Local File for qualifying cross-border controlled transactions unless an applicable exemption applies, including the threshold under which total annual controlled transactions are below approximately TWD 200 million, subject to transaction-specific rules. A Master File is generally required for a Taiwan enterprise meeting the large-enterprise criteria, commonly including annual net operating revenue of at least TWD 3 billion or aggregate cross-border controlled transactions of at least TWD 1 billion, subject to the current regulations and prescribed exemptions. A Country-by-Country Report may apply to a Taiwan constituent entity of a multinational enterprise group meeting the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold, generally EUR 750 million.

      
Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a single standalone OECD-style Economic Substance Regulation regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant under Taiwan transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, the general anti-avoidance rule, controlled foreign company rules, permanent-establishment principles, treaty anti-abuse standards, and business-purpose doctrines. The Taiwan entity should maintain real employees, premises, production assets, decision-making, inventory control, quality systems, procurement functions, risks, and commercial activity proportionate to its claimed manufacturing profile. A low-risk manufacturer with no personnel, no production control, or no operational capacity may face recharacterization, deduction denial, transfer-pricing adjustment, withholding exposure, or permanent-establishment concerns.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Corporate income tax evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Additional tax, statutory surcharge, interest, assessment of omitted income, and potential criminal liability under Taiwan tax laws. Intentional evasion can result in imprisonment and criminal fines; the applicable sentence depends on the offense, amount, evidence, and judicial findings.

        
Director personal liability: Directors, responsible persons, managers, accountants, and other participants may incur criminal liability where they personally direct, approve, assist, or conceal the conduct. Civil restitution, tax recovery, asset preservation, disqualification consequences, and potential travel restrictions may arise in enforcement proceedings. Piercing of limited liability is not automatic and generally requires personal wrongdoing, fraud, abuse of corporate form, or statutory grounds.

      

      
      

        False invoicing and fraudulent expense deductions
        
Statutory sanction: Disallowance of deductions and input tax credits, additional tax, penalties, interest, invoice-related sanctions, and possible criminal prosecution for false documentation, tax evasion, or fraud.

        
Director personal liability: Responsible persons who authorize or knowingly use false documents may face personal criminal prosecution, civil tax restitution, director or officer liability, and seizure or freezing of assets. Imprisonment is fact-dependent and may apply where the conduct satisfies the relevant criminal offense.

      

      
      

        Transfer-pricing manipulation
        
Statutory sanction: Taiwan tax authorities may make an arm's-length adjustment, deny deductions, impose additional corporate income tax, assess withholding tax, charge interest, and apply documentation or reporting penalties. Corresponding adjustments are not automatic.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are exposed where they knowingly approve non-arm's-length arrangements, false documentation, or deliberate profit diversion. Ordinary disagreement over method selection generally does not create personal criminal liability absent fraud, concealment, or intentional evasion.

      

      
      

        Withholding tax non-compliance
        
Statutory sanction: The Taiwan payer may be assessed the unpaid withholding tax, late-payment interest, surcharges, filing penalties, and possible liability for failure to collect or remit tax. The payer generally cannot cure the exposure merely by asserting that the foreign recipient was contractually responsible.

        
Director personal liability: Responsible persons and withholding agents may incur administrative and criminal exposure for deliberate failure to withhold or remit. Directors may face personal claims where they authorized the payment and tax diversion or acted fraudulently.

      

      
      

        Permanent-establishment or agency risk
        
Statutory sanction: Attribution of additional Taiwan-source income, corporate income tax, withholding tax, penalties, interest, and possible reassessment of intercompany charges. Treaty protection may be denied where the facts satisfy a permanent-establishment or dependent-agent test.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may face civil and administrative exposure for knowingly maintaining inaccurate functional descriptions or tax returns. Criminal liability generally requires intentional concealment, fraud, or evasion.

      

      
      

        Shell company and insufficient economic substance
        
Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions, reallocation of income, treaty-benefit denial, withholding assessments, transfer-pricing adjustments, VAT reassessment, and potential fraud or tax-evasion prosecution where records are fictitious.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face civil restitution, asset tracing, injunctions, and criminal liability if the entity is used to conceal ownership, launder funds, evade tax, or issue false documents. Limited liability does not protect intentional personal misconduct.

      

      
      

        Anti-money laundering and suspicious payment activity
        
Statutory sanction: Transaction suspension or rejection, suspicious transaction reporting, account restrictions, administrative penalties, confiscation, and criminal sanctions under Taiwan anti-money-laundering and criminal laws where money laundering or predicate offenses are established.

        
Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, compliance officers, and employees may be personally liable for knowing participation, assistance, concealment, or willful blindness. Criminal penalties may include imprisonment, fines, asset confiscation, and restrictions on financial activities.

      

      
      

        Food, product, and statutory-license non-compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Product seizure, recall, destruction, production suspension, license cancellation, administrative fines, business closure, civil claims, and criminal prosecution for serious food-safety violations.

        
Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face personal administrative and criminal liability where they knowingly approve unsafe production, conceal contamination, falsify records, or obstruct inspection. Civil compensation may include consumer and commercial losses.

      

      
      

        Customs and import-export fraud
        
Statutory sanction: Additional customs duty, import tax, VAT, penalties, seizure, forfeiture, loss of customs privileges, and possible criminal prosecution for smuggling or fraudulent declarations.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and customs declarants may face personal liability where they knowingly submit false declarations or participate in duty evasion. Asset seizure and criminal restrictions may apply.

      

      
      

        Unauthorized capital withdrawal or disguised profit distribution
        
Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, deemed dividend withholding, corporate law sanctions, restoration of company assets, shareholder repayment obligations, civil damages, and possible criminal liability for breach of trust or misappropriation.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may be required to restore funds, compensate the company or creditors, and face criminal prosecution for breach of fiduciary duty, embezzlement, fraud, or unlawful distribution.

      

      
      

        Failure to maintain books and records
        
Statutory sanction: Bookkeeping penalties, estimated assessment, denial of deductions, loss carryforward denial, additional tax, interest, and enhanced scrutiny of all related-party transactions.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face personal sanctions where record destruction or obstruction is intentional. Criminal exposure increases when poor records conceal tax evasion, money laundering, or false invoicing.

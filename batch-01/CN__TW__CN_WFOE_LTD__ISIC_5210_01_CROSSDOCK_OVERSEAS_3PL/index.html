Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office or commercial mailbox addresses are generally not permissible for a WFOE in China. The Company Law and SAMR regulations require a physical registered address that is suitable for business operations and where legal documents can be served. Tax authorities and banks typically reject virtual office addresses for the purpose of opening corporate bank accounts and obtaining tax registration. For the cross-border e-commerce fulfillment industry, a physical presence is also required to demonstrate commercial substance, especially for customs and logistics licensing. However, a registered office may be a leased commercial space, and the company must maintain a physical presence with at least one employee or a designated representative at that address. The use of a virtual office may trigger a risk of administrative penalties, revocation of the business license, or refusal of bank account opening.

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Department of Commercial Affairs, Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA), Taiwan, acting through the One-Stop Company Registration Online System; investment screening for Mainland investors is conducted by the Investment Commission, MOEA.

Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Taiwan limited company under the current Company Act. The subscribed capital must nevertheless be fully paid in before the company registration is filed, and for a Mainland-funded WFOE the paid-in amount must match the amount approved by the Investment Commission. There is no blocked-escrow account rule; the capital must be deposited into the company's own New Taiwan Dollar account at an authorized foreign exchange bank and kept available for lawful corporate purposes.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Fire Services Act, including biannual/annual reporting and inspection, is required before occupation; building structural load and racking/seismic certificates are required for mezzanines and high-bay racks; occupational safety and health approval is required for forklift operations, dock-leveler safety, evacuation procedure, emergency lighting, fire hydrants, and employee labor/pension insurance registration with the Bureau of Labor Insurance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taxation Administration; electronic portal: National Taxation E-Government Online Services (e-Tax Portal) at https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity (including a Chinese WFOE) must first obtain an Alien Tax ID / Business Registration Number by applying to the National Taxation Bureau of the Ministry of Finance. The application must be filed in person or through a tax attorney/accountant at the appropriate regional National Taxation Bureau office, accompanied by a certified copy of the entity's certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, identification of the responsible person (passport of the legal representative or authorized manager), a Taiwan agent agreement if no place of fixed business exists, and any approved government approval for a foreign company branch. Upon submission, the bureau will issue a 'Tax Identification Number (TIN)' together with a 'Revenue Registration Certificate'. For a foreign company establishing a branch in Taiwan, the TIN is a Unified Business Number (also used as the VAT/Business Tax number) and must be printed on all invoices, receipts, and business documents. If no physical presence is established, a foreign seller may register as a non-resident seller through the Ministry of Finance's online registration system for cross-border e-commerce, providing bank account details, a Taiwan tax representative, and foreign certification documents. The registration must be completed before any taxable supply is rendered in Taiwan.

None; Taiwan does not provide a check-the-box or look-through entity election. A foreign branch or registered foreign entity is taxed as a separate entity at the entity level on Taiwan-source income. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration applies to any enterprise with average monthly sales exceeding NT$200,000 within the preceding six months. For foreign companies selling digital services or goods to Taiwan individuals, an annual turnover threshold of NT$480,000 triggers simplified business tax registration and collection.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment applies to goods actually shipped from Taiwan to destinations outside Taiwan provided the seller retains conclusive evidence that the goods left Taiwan and the sale was made to a foreign buyer or a customs export process was completed. For cross-border e-commerce overseas warehouse fulfillment, the goods may also qualify for zero-rating if they are shipped from a Taiwan physical warehouse to an overseas destination or delivered into a foreign designated carrier for onward international transport. The export must be supported by customs export documentation, a commercial invoice, full freight and shipping records, and proof of receipt of foreign currency. Qualifying offshore digital services are zero-rated only when supplied to a non-Taiwan business recipient or non-resident individual and when the relevant service is actually performed outside Taiwan or exported electronically; supporting service destination and usage evidence must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued by the customs authority
  • Bill of Lading or international airway bill / ocean carrier transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) confirming receipt of payment in foreign currency from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Customs brokered shipping instructions and transport records demonstrating physical departure from Taiwan
  • Overseas warehouse receipt or foreign 3PL delivery confirmation evidencing destination

Taiwan mandates the use of Uniform Invoices for all sales. Modern electronic invoicing is administered through the E-Invoice Platform, requiring registered sellers to provide e-invoice XML data via approved Turnkey software or a government-licensed service provider. There is no real-time pre-clearance or digital anti-fraud stamping; instead, the platform requires periodic transmission of invoice data to the Ministry of Finance within set filing windows. Sellers must integrate their point-of-sale or billing systems with the E-Invoice Turnkey API, use published XML schemas, and maintain secure electronic signature certificates issued by a Taiwanese certificate authority. The system does not require continuous government authorization before issuing each invoice, but periodic audit and matching against VAT returns are enforced, with severe penalties for unregistered invoice issuance or failure to transmit e-invoice data.

Taiwan customs and the National Taxation Bureau jointly scrutinize export zero-rating claims. If the exporter cannot produce the required customs export declaration, shipping documents, or valid foreign currency inward remittance within the statutory deadline, the claimed 0% VAT is disallowed and reassessed at the standard 5% rate. In addition, a penalty surcharge of up to five times the underpaid VAT may be imposed under the Business Tax Act, and where false or forged export documents are used, criminal consequences under the Tax Evasion and Anti-Corruption provisions may apply, including fines or imprisonment. Retrospective assessments are generally allowed for seven years in cases of tax fraud or nontransparent export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible on current account for bona fide international transactions, including cross-border trade settlement, service payments, and profit remittance, once genuine underlying commercial documents are provided to a designated forex bank. Current account convertibility is supported by the SAFE registration mechanism and does not require separate case-by-case SAFE approval for routine operating payments, although the bank must perform post-verification. Capital account transactions remain restricted and controlled; foreign equity capital injected into the WFOE must be booked in a SAFE-registered FDI capital account, and the deployment of capital contribution proceeds into RMB requires a valid capital verification report from a PRC certified public accountant and must align with the registered business scope. Profit repatriation to the Taiwan parent is permitted after compliance with corporate income tax and dividend withholding settlement, but the foreign exchange conversion and outward remittance must be processed through the bank against the annual audited financial statements, board resolution, and tax payment certificates. Overseas warehouse service fees, customs duties, freight and fulfilment disbursements are current account items and can be settled freely once the trade and service background is evidenced. Overall, the regime is administratively monitored but not freely convertible without documentary tracking, and the WFOE must maintain full end-to-end audit trails.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE export proceeds verification regime, foreign currency export proceeds from the cross-border e-commerce overseas warehouse fulfilment model must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange account within 180 days from the customs export declaration date, unless the WFOE has obtained a SAFE-approved deferred payment registration or qualified receivables financing arrangement. There is currently no mandatory percentage surrender ratio; the WFOE may retain 100% of export proceeds in a foreign currency settlement account or voluntarily convert them into RMB at the designated bank's spot rate. Every collection must be matched to the customs declaration, e-commerce platform settlement records, and logistics documents within the SAFE ledger verification cycle.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated FX control authority for the PRC WFOE; Taiwan-side remittance flows are further subject to the foreign exchange regulatory framework of the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) where applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Airwallex and comparable offshore digital EMI providers are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in mainland China and are legally barred from opening domestic RMB basic settlement accounts or serving as SAFE-verified onshore FX clearing banks. They are therefore not viable as the primary treasury rail for the WFOE's domestic capital injection, statutory FX conversion, or export collection. Their permissible role is limited to auxiliary offshore multi-currency collection and disbursement at the overseas warehouse leg, provided all funds are ultimately routed through a licensed Chinese bank and fully reconciled to underlying commercial documents. Use of any offshore EMI without upstream bank delivery would create an unverified current account receipt and heightened SAR risk under PRC AML rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan parent injects equity via SWIFT MT103 into the WFOE's SAFE-registered FDI capital account at a designated Chinese bank. The remittance must name the registered Taiwan investor as remitter, state the purpose as direct investment equity contribution, and be preceded by the PRC FDI registration/recordal and any required cross-strait investor documentation. Upon credit, the receiving bank verifies the remitter against the SAFE FDI information, the WFOE business licence and the approved shareholding structure; a PRC certified public accountant then issues a capital verification report. RMB conversion of capital proceeds is released only for permitted operating uses within the registered business scope, and any change of use or secondary conversion requires additional SAFE or bank filing.For trade settlement and 3PL disbursements, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for direct payments to overseas warehouse operators, freight forwarders, customs brokers and platform sellers. Bulk merchandise procurement can be supported by irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued via MT700 and documentary collections when suppliers require title document control or Banker's acceptance. Cross-border RMB payments may be cleared through CIPS where the counterparty accepts RMB; domestic logistics costs and local fees are settled through local clearing rails using the RMB basic account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local file required if: (a) tangible-asset related-party transfers exceed RMB 200 million in the fiscal year; (b) intangible-asset or financial-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million; (c) other related-party transactions (including services) exceed RMB 40 million. Master file required if the group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds RMB 1.1 billion (under SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42).

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form principles and the 'actual management institution' test to determine tax residency. Under SAT Bulletin 2018 No. 9, a beneficial owner of dividends must have substantive business activities (employees, assets, operations) and cannot be a shell/conduit entity. The WFOE must maintain real economic substance in China (physical office, personnel, decision-making) to avoid being characterized as a shell for treaty shopping. Though China does not have formal ESR rules like the UAE, it enforces economic substance via General Anti-Abuse Rules (GAAR) in Article 47 of the EIT Law and the special tax adjustment provisions, which could recharacterize transactions lacking commercial purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 201 of the Criminal Law of the PRC, tax evasion is punishable by up to 3 years' imprisonment and/or a fine if the amount evaded is between 10% and 30% of the taxable amount or over RMB 50,000; 3 to 7 years for amounts over 30% or over RMB 50,000. Additionally, Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax and late interest at 0.05% daily.

Director personal liability: Under the Doctrine of Piercing the Corporate Veil (Article 20 of the Company Law), directors who intentionally commit tax evasion for the company may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax if they abused the corporate form. Directors face criminal prosecution, asset seizure, travel bans (blacklisting by the tax authority), prohibition from leaving China, and imprisonment as responsible persons.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Article 44 of the EIT Law and Article 121 of the Implementation Regulations, the tax authority may make a transfer pricing adjustment and charge interest (basic rate plus 5 percentage points) for the underpaid tax. Under Article 122, a penalty of up to 50% of the underpaid tax may be imposed for failure to provide documentation without reasonable cause; Article 62 of the Tax Collection and Administrative Punishment Law provides fines of RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 for non-filing.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false TP documentation or directed artificial transfer prices may be held personally liable for resulting tax deficiencies under the 'deemed legal representative' doctrine. The director may be added to the tax authority's blacklist (Joint Punishment List), restricting high-consumption behavior, air/rail travel, and entry/exit; if fraud is proven, criminal liability for tax evasion may extend to imprisonment.
Unlawful Profit Repatriation / WHT Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 69 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, the company (as withholding agent) that fails to withhold is liable for an administrative penalty of 50% to 300% of the un-withheld tax. Article 19 of the EIT Law imposes a 10% WHT on dividends; failure to pay results in late interest at 0.05% daily. Under Article 47 of the EIT Law, the GAAR allows recharacterization of the shell structure, disallowing treaty benefits and imposing tax on the actual shareholder.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign off on dividend distributions without appropriate withholding expose themselves to personal liability for the un-withheld tax to the extent they knowingly approved the transaction. They may face criminal liability under Article 201 for assisting in tax evasion, including imprisonment; civil liability for recovery of the tax by the tax authority; and a personal travel ban/restriction on exit from China (under the Exit-Entry Administration Law).

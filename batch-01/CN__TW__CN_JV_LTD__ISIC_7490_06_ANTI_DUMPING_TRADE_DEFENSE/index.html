Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Taiwan has no general statutory minimum capital for a limited company, except where a special regulated business requires one. The subscribed capital must be fully paid before registration, and a Taiwan-certified public accountant must verify the capital. A foreign investor must obtain investment approval, remit funds through an approved bank, and complete capital verification and investment realization procedures. No general escrow account is required, but funds must remain traceable through the designated bank account.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain landlord and building-management confirmation that the premises are approved for office use. Comply with the Fire Services Act, applicable building and fire codes, evacuation and emergency-planning requirements, and any inspection required by the local fire department. Structural-load certification is generally unnecessary for ordinary office occupancy but may be required for alterations, heavy equipment, archives, or changed use. Comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act, labor insurance, health insurance, workplace risk controls, and mandatory training. No factory occupational clearance is required unless physical industrial operations are added.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The JV is a resident profit-seeking enterprise taxable in Taiwan on its worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate of 20%; undistributed earnings are subject to the 5% retained earnings surtax. It is not a pass-through entity; no partnership flow-through or check-the-box pass-through election is available. Foreign shareholders' dividends are generally subject to withholding tax at source unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty or the Taiwan Offshore Banking Unit regime.

Available election forms

Taiwan does not provide a check-the-box election for entity classification; the JV is mandatorily classified as an entity-level taxpayer under the Income Tax Act and the Business Tax Act. In limited cases, the JV may make statutory elections or invoke relief mechanisms, including: (i) election of the foreign income tax credit for foreign-source income under Article 3 of the Income Tax Act, subject to the creditable limit in Taiwan; (ii) application of double tax treaty benefits, including reduced withholding rates and mutual agreement procedure; (iii) election to file as a regular VAT zero-rated exporter for input VAT refund purposes; and (iv) adoption of the Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) filing posture under the Income Basic Tax Act where applicable. No hybrid mismatch or group consolidation elections are recognized under Taiwan tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The mandatory VAT/GST registration threshold is average monthly taxable sales exceeding NT$200,000 in the preceding six months. A business falling below this threshold may nonetheless register voluntarily. Once registered, the enterprise must issue Uniform Invoices and file quarterly business tax returns.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act. For the JV's trade-remedy defense consulting services (ISIC 7490 / NAICS 541199), zero-rating applies where services are provided to foreign enterprises or individuals and are actually used outside Taiwan, including anti-dumping and trade defense consulting performed from Taiwan for overseas clients. Export of goods similarly qualifies at 0% where customs export formalities are completed. The zero-rating claim is effected by stating the 0% rate in the quarterly Business Tax return and retaining documentary proof of exportation or foreign service use. Unlike tax-exempt sales, zero-rated sales allow recovery of input VAT, and excess input VAT is refundable to the exporter.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口報單)
  • Bill of Lading (提單)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate (出口結匯水單/外匯收入水單)
  • Commercial Invoice (商業發票)
  • Packing List (裝箱單)
  • Sales Contract or Purchase Order (買賣契約書/訂單)
  • Service Performance Evidence for Cross-Border Services (e.g., service report, correspondence, offshore use certification, and foreign client identification)

Taiwan enforces an electronic Uniform Invoice (統一發票) system. VAT-registered enterprises must issue e-invoices through the Ministry of Finance Electronic Invoice Integrated Service Platform (EIIP) and transmit invoice data to the platform within 48 hours of issuance. There is no pre-clearance or real-time digital stamping authorization by the tax authority; compliance is achieved through post-issuance electronic transmission, barcode/QR code coding, and matching of B2B invoice data with buyers' Unified Business Numbers. Export zero-rated invoices must be separately coded and filed as 0% tax rate in the quarterly Business Tax return. The API-based EIIP supports B2B and B2C cloud invoice issuance, electronic storage, buyer system integration, and automatic audit trail generation.

The National Taxation Administration cross-checks zero-rating claims against customs export declarations, banking FX records, and electronic invoice data. Erroneous or fraudulent zero-rating claims cause restoration of the 5% VAT and disallowance of input VAT credits, plus interest. Under the Business Tax Act, tax evasion or misclassification may result in a penalty of up to 5 times the underpaid tax; grossly fraudulent filings may also trigger criminal liability, TIN de-registration, denial of refund privilege, and blacklisting of the responsible legal representative.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current-account transactions (consultancy fees, legal-defense service remittances) are generally freely convertible upon production of genuine transaction documents, under the 1996 IMF Article VIII obligations and the PRC Foreign Exchange Regulations; capital-account items (FDI equity injection, shareholder loans, liquidation remittances) require SAFE or bank-level registration and do not enjoy automatic convertibility. Profit remittance requires an audited financial statement, board profit-distribution resolution, tax assessment/payment certificates, and prior foreign-exchange registration; no general remittance cap is imposed for qualifying reinvestment of RMB profits, but Taiwan-dollar capital repatriation must be supported by a completed asset inspection report (验资报告). The JV's business is a professional-services exporter, so its primary convertibility risk lies in proving the 'real need' of each USD/TWD payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory blanket surrender of service-trade export proceeds was abolished; the JV may retain eligible export/non-trade current-account income in a foreign-currency account or sell it to a designated bank at commercial rates. Statutory compliance obligations under SAFE include (i) filing of the cross-border service-trade contract with the bank; (ii) submission of receipt/payment application and underlying invoices through the bank's Service-Trade FX administration system; and (iii) repatriation of service income within the banking period and no delayed offshore accumulation absent a SAFE-approved offshore account. For trade-remedy consulting, the bank will classify receipts as 'Consultancy and related services' with the appropriate SAFE purpose code (e.g., 4241 or 4242), and no fixed statutory surrender ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Primary authority: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) exercising administration of RMB and foreign-currency accounts for the domestic JV. Ancillary authority: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC) governs Taiwan-dollar settlement and cross-strait fund flows to the extent TW-source funds are routed.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Generally Low for this JV structure. Offshore digital bank/EMI providers (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, TransferWise/Revolut) cannot maintain RMB-denominated onshore accounts or accept SAFE-regulated capital injections; they may only serve as inbound collection gateways for small-value service invoices through licensed partner banks in China. For statutory capital injection, LC operations, or large trade-defense disbursements, a physical mainland bank account remains mandatory. If the JV chooses an EMI corridor, SAFE will still require the ultimate onshore bank settlement record and full underlying contract data, making the marginal compliance benefit negligible except for micro-consulting receivables.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Sino-Foreign Equity JV, the compliant inbound wire is routed via SWIFT MT103 from the Taiwan shareholder's NT$/USD account to the bank-designated FDI Capital-Pledge Account in mainland China. The remittance instruction must carry (i) an accurate Purpose of Payment code, normally '623011 - Capital contribution for establishment of foreign-invested enterprise' or '623012 - Increase in paid-in capital of a foreign-invested enterprise'; (ii) a clear reference to the SAFE FDI registration number (业务编号); and (iii) the beneficiary bank's 12-digit SWIFT BIC. Before disbursement, the bank screens the remitter name against the SAFE investment record, obtains the company passport/credential and FIE business license, and verifies the capital contribution through a capital-account 'foreign-exchange settlement' process. The JV must not use the basic RMB account for inbound foreign-exchange capital; any attempt to send equity proceeds via an ordinary operational remittance will be recalled or blocked.Trade-defense consulting outflows predominately clear via SWIFT MT103 (bank wire) with a custom 'Services / Legal Fees' purpose code (e.g., China SAFE Service-Trade Code 4241/4242), supported by (i) a cross-border service-trade contract; (ii) a tax-withholding certificate or tax-exemption certificate filed with the local State Tax Bureau; and (iii) an electronic service-trade declaration in the bank's cross-border payment system.

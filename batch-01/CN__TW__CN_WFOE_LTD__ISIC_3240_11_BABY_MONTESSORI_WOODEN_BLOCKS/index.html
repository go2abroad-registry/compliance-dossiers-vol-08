Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible for current-account items (trade in goods and services, wages, general remittances) provided that the underlying transactions are genuine and properly documented. Capital-account items (FDI inflow, cross-border corporate borrowing, and outward direct investment) remain subject to SAFE registration, approval, or access to Free Trade Zone discretionary reform measures. Profit remittance to the Taiwan parent shareholder is permitted after tax certification and audited financial statements substantiate the distributable surplus; the remittance is classified as a current-account dividend payment and requires the submission of a Foreign Exchange Registration for Direct Investment (FDI) record and related documentation. The WFOE may not convert RMB into foreign currency for speculative or non-trade purposes; foreign exchange purchases must be for verified trade payments, dividend distributions, or approved capital-account operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds under the current SAFE framework. The statutory obligation is repatriation of export receipts to a domestic foreign-currency account (or direct sale to a designated bank) within 90 days from the date of export; the period is extendable to 180 days for deferred payment arrangements with supporting trade contracts. Repatriated proceeds are entered into the SAFE trade-in-goods monitoring system, verified against the related customs declarations, and may be retained in foreign currency or settled into RMB at the enterprise's discretion. Delayed repatriation without bank notification may result in administrative fines of up to 5% of the undistributed foreign exchange amount and suspension of certain forex operations.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) — the PBOC is the central bank and the SAFE is the statutory foreign exchange control authority.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan parent shareholder must remit capital by SWIFT MT103 (Transaction Type: F/X converted to USD or RMB) to the WFOE’s designated foreign-exchange capital-escrow account at the branch of Bank of China or another SAFE-authorised bank. Field 70 (Details of Remittance) must contain the exact reference 'FDI Investment — CN__TW__CN_WFOE_LTD' and the SAFE purpose code for direct investment ('FDI'). The receiving bank will validate the FDI Control Information (SAFE-registered amount, negative-list compliance) before crediting. Internal approval by the bank’s compliance and anti-money-laundering team is required, and the bank may request the WFOE’s foreign investment registration certificate, the Taiwan shareholder’s board resolution authorising the remittance, and a certified copy of the Taiwan corporate registry. At the point of RMB conversion, the WFOE must present each intended expenditure category (salaries, rent, raw-material payments) to the bank; conversion cannot occur until the underlying current-account impulse is verified.For low-value,same-day or urgent trade payments (e.g., import of beechwood samples, packaging supplies, logistics fees), the WFOE may execute a SWIFT MT103 payment from its operational current-account, with the payment purpose code 'other trade services' and attached commercial invoice, contract, and customs declaration. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) are also available for established counterparties. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% of taxable income for profit-seeking enterprises; Taiwan companies and Taiwan permanent establishments of foreign enterprises are generally taxable on Taiwan-source income, with worldwide-income rules applying to Taiwan-incorporated companies subject to foreign-tax-credit limitations.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% withholding tax on dividends paid by a Taiwan company to a non-resident shareholder, subject to applicable tax treaty or approved cross-strait relief. Taiwan-resident companies generally receive dividends under separate domestic rules, including imputation and investment-income treatment where applicable.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and balance of tax are generally due by May 31 of the following year for a calendar-year taxpayer. A statutory extension may be available for qualifying enterprises with approved accounting or audit arrangements. Provisional filing, where applicable, is generally due by September 30.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally due by the fifteenth day of the month following the end of the relevant two-month VAT period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan generally requires a local transfer-pricing report for a Taiwan enterprise with annual net revenue of at least NT$300 million or related-party transaction amounts reaching applicable prescribed thresholds, commonly NT$200 million in aggregate for specified transaction categories, subject to transaction-level exemptions and current regulations. A master file is generally required where the Taiwan entity is part of a multinational enterprise group and the applicable Taiwan revenue and cross-border related-party transaction thresholds are met, commonly including annual net revenue of at least NT$3 billion and prescribed cross-border related-party transactions. A country-by-country report may apply to a Taiwan constituent entity of an MNE group meeting the OECD consolidated-revenue threshold, generally EUR750 million, subject to Taiwan filing and exchange rules.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not have a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance remains relevant under Taiwan's anti-avoidance rules, transfer-pricing rules, controlled foreign company rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty anti-abuse standards, and the general principle that arrangements lacking genuine commercial purpose may be recharacterized. The entity should maintain Taiwan personnel, decision-making, production capacity, inventory control, premises, banking, quality control, customer and supplier functions, and risk management proportionate to its claimed manufacturing and distribution activities. A Taiwan company controlled by mainland Chinese investors may also face investment-approval, ownership, foreign-exchange, and cross-strait regulatory issues separate from taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Invalid entity classification and foreign-investment authorization

Statutory sanction: Business suspension, mandatory correction, confiscation or fines under applicable company, investment, cross-strait, and tax laws; tax registration and filing may also be challenged.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may incur administrative fines, civil damages, disqualification consequences, and criminal liability where unauthorized investment, false registration, or intentional evasion is established. Travel restrictions or imprisonment are not automatic and require statutory grounds and due process.
Corporate income-tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, interest, administrative fines that may reach multiples of the underpaid tax, tax assessment by estimation, and potential criminal prosecution for deliberate evasion.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face criminal imprisonment and fines under applicable tax statutes, together with civil restitution of unpaid tax and damages. Corporate limited liability does not protect persons who personally participated in the offense.
Fraudulent invoices and VAT abuse

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of input VAT, repayment of refunds, substantial administrative fines, business restrictions, and criminal penalties under the Business Tax Act and related criminal provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors, accountants, invoice issuers, and knowing users may face criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, civil restitution, and asset seizure.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment to an arm's-length amount, additional tax, interest, documentation penalties, denial of deductions, and possible penalty for inaccurate filing.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for tax losses caused by intentional misconduct or gross negligence. Criminal liability generally requires additional evidence of fraud or intentional tax evasion.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unwithheld tax from the payer, interest, administrative penalties, and potential denial of treaty relief.

Director personal liability: The responsible withholding agent may be personally exposed under Taiwan tax rules where the failure was intentional or attributable to statutory responsibility. Criminal exposure depends on fraud, concealment, or other aggravating conduct.
Beneficial ownership and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, withholding at the domestic rate, tax reassessment, interest, penalties, and anti-avoidance recharacterization.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly submit false documents or direct sham arrangements may face civil restitution and criminal liability under tax, fraud, or document offenses.
Customs, origin, and export-control misstatement

Statutory sanction: Customs duty and VAT reassessment, fines, seizure, forfeiture, import or export suspension, and possible prosecution under customs or trade laws.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers may face personal fines, civil liability, and criminal sanctions where declarations were knowingly false or supported by forged records.
Product-safety and licensing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Product recall, destruction or withdrawal, sales prohibition, administrative fines, license or registration consequences, and liability for consumer injury.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face civil compensation, administrative penalties, and criminal liability for dangerous products, serious injury, death, or deliberate concealment.
AML, sanctions, and suspicious-payment violations

Statutory sanction: Transaction rejection, account freezing, reporting obligations, administrative fines, asset confiscation, and criminal penalties under Taiwan anti-money-laundering and financial laws.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face investigation, asset restraint, civil recovery, fines, and imprisonment for knowing money laundering, concealment, or proceeds handling. Travel restrictions may be imposed only by competent authority under applicable procedure.
Payroll, labor, and social-insurance tax failure

Statutory sanction: Back taxes, labor and insurance assessments, interest, administrative fines, employee claims, and possible criminal liability for fraudulent records.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face civil claims, administrative fines, and criminal liability for intentional withholding fraud, document falsification, or serious labor-law violations.
Record destruction and obstruction

Statutory sanction: Separate procedural fines, adverse assessment, loss of deductions or tax credits, compulsory production orders, and obstruction-related criminal sanctions where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, accountants, and agents may incur personal civil and criminal liability. Piercing of limited liability is fact-specific and generally requires fraud, abuse of the entity, unlawful conduct, or personal participation.

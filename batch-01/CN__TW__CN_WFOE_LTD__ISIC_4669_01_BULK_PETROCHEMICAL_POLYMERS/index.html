Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is required under the Taiwan Company Act, but the paid-in capital must be adequate for the stated business scope. The foreign investor must obtain prior Investment Commission approval, remit the subscribed capital into a Taiwan bank account in the name of the proposed WFOE, and cause a Taiwan-certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report under Company Act Article 7. No general escrow or blocked-account mandate is imposed by Taiwan law; however, the remitted capital may not be withdrawn or deployed for operating expenses before incorporation is registered and the capital verification is completed.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office and any warehouse must obtain a lawful building occupancy permit from the municipal government and must comply with the Taiwan Fire Services Act. The warehouse must pass a fire safety inspection covering fire extinguishers, sprinkler density, emergency lighting, exit signs, aisle width, storage height, and fire department access. The operator must submit an annual fire safety equipment maintenance report to the local fire department and implement a fire safety management plan. Under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, the company must perform a workplace hazard assessment, provide first-aid equipment and trained personnel, maintain material safety data sheets for polyolefin dust, prevent dust accumulation, and ensure employees receive safe handling and emergency response training. The company must also participate in local civil defense self-protection arrangements, such as providing building occupant rosters and cooperating with municipal post-disaster inspections, if so required by the competent authority.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (国家税务总局) via the E-Tax Platform (电子税务局) and the electronic tax registration system administered by the STA; cross-border investment entities also interact with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) for establishment filings and subsequently obtain the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC).

E-government filing portal

First incorporate the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise with the local branch of the State Administration for Market Regulation (Market Regulation Administration) to obtain the Business License and the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). Then complete post-incorporation tax registration through the electronic tax authority system (E-Tax Platform) within 30 days of obtaining the Business License. The WFOE must file the Comprehensive Tax Registration Form, submit the SAMR Business License, articles of association, valid passport and residence permit copies of the legal representative, the foreign investor's certificate of incorporation, and proof of registered address. Upon successful processing, the STA issues the tax registration status and creates the electronic taxpayer profile linked to the USCC, enabling monthly VAT, Corporate Income Tax, stamp duty and withholding filings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a WFOE engaged in wholesale of petrochemical polymers, no registration threshold exemption applies because corporate entities are mandatory VAT taxpayers. General taxpayer compulsory status arises when annual sales of goods exceed RMB 5,000,000; since the entity will be registered as a general taxpayer, the standard rate of 13% applies to wholesale supply of petrochemical polymer granules. If small-scale registration were chosen, the threshold is RMB 5,000,000 annual turnover and the simplified collection rate is 3%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The wholesale export of bulk petrochemical polymers (virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules) to Taiwan qualifies for a 0% VAT export zero-rating and VAT refund status if the goods physically leave China customs territory and are exported abroad, including cross-strait exports to Taiwan. The exporter must be a VAT general taxpayer, must complete the official export customs declaration in its own name or via an approved export agency, must issue an ordinary commercial invoice supporting the export, must receive foreign currency payment, and must ensure the export proceeds are repatriated and verified. Qualifying digital services are not in scope because this entity conducts physical goods wholesale; zero-rating also requires that the goods are sold to a non-resident buyer for delivery abroad and that all customs procedures occur before the next VAT filing period, or within the statutory export declaration deadline.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口货物报关单 / Export Bill of Entry)
  • Commercial Invoice for Export
  • Packing List and Weight List
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill evidencing export from mainland China to Taiwan
  • Foreign Exchange Proceeds Verification (出口收汇核销单 / Bank Foreign Exchange Settlement Record)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Receipt of Overseas Remittance confirming payment in USD or other foreign currency from the Taiwan buyer
  • Export VAT refund application form filed via the STA E-Tax Platform
  • Evidence of the export sales ledger in CIF/FOB terms matching the customs declaration value

China operates a mandatory Digital-Invoice (全电发票 / Electronic Invoice) system through the e-invoice platform of the STA, synchronised with the E-Tax Platform. B2B VAT invoices are issued electronically in real-time via official provincial electronic invoice systems, uploaded to the national electronic invoice clearing platform, and validated centrally. The system requires tax U-key authentication, digital stamps and blue/red-letter invoice controls. Since 2022, the nationwide 'Full Digitalised Electronic Invoice' pilot applies to new general taxpayers; taxpayers must use the official e-invoice generation interface, transmit invoice XML/PDF via API to the STA, comply with the electronic invoice number and digital cipher rules, and cannot issue unauthorised paper VAT special invoices. A live fiscal clearance machine is not used in mainland China; instead, real-time validation and central invoice reconciliation occur through the STA's Electronic Invoice Service Platform.

Export zero-rating is closely scrutinised by the State Taxation Administration and the General Administration of Customs. If the exporter fails to substantiate the export declaration, cannot evidence physical exit, does not repatriate foreign exchange within the statutory deadline, or submits falsified invoices or customs declarations, the tax authority may disallow the 0% rate, recover the input VAT refunded, impose supplementary VAT and unpaid tax surcharge (0.05% daily), and charge a penalty ranging from 0.5 to 5 times the evaded tax under the PRC Tax Administration Law. In severe cases involving fraudulent VAT export refunds, a 50% to 500% fine applies and the matter may be referred for criminal liability for tax fraud, with imprisonment up to seven years; the entity may also face GST/CIT adjustment, blacklisting, and revocation of export tax refund credentials.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed-float, partially convertible foreign exchange system. The RMB is fully convertible for current-account transactions supported by genuine trade documents, but capital-account flows are subject to registration, approval, or quota controls. A Taiwan-invested WFOE may receive equity capital only after FDI registration with SAFE; the inward remittance is credited to a dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account, and any conversion into RMB must be made through a licensed bank placing funds into a Settlement-Pending Account with restricted spending purposes verified by contracts and invoices. Profit and dividend repatriation to the Taiwan investor is legal but requires audited financial statements, enterprise income tax and withholding tax payment certificates, and confirmation of the original capital-investment registration. There is no statutory forced conversion of current-account export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency in settlement accounts or convert through a designated bank at market rates. However, capital-account RMB convertibility remains constrained, particularly for foreign-currency outbound guarantees, repayment of external debt, and offshore investments, each of which requires a SAFE-registered underlying transaction. Treasury teams must therefore use licensed commercial banks and maintain a complete transaction dossier; offshore EMIs or informal channels are not acceptable under PRC AML and illegal cross-border payment rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration reform, exporters are no longer subject to a fixed mandatory surrender ratio; 100% of export foreign currency receipts may be retained in a foreign-currency current account or settled into RMB at market rates. The statutory obligation is to repatriate export proceeds through licensed banks into mainland China and to ensure the actual receipt date is consistent with the expected receipt date declared in the Goods Trade Monitoring System. Any export payment collected later than 90 days after the customs export date must be reported as a deferred receipt, and B-class or C-class enterprises face enhanced supervision and restricted payment methods, but no fixed percentage conversion requirement applies.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable as a primary treasury or clearing rail. China's PBOC and SAFE regulatory perimeter does not recognise offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex as licensed banks for domestic corporate account opening, RMB settlement, or foreign exchange clearing. They cannot receive WFOE equity capital, issue capital verification reports, or act as clearing banks for LCs or outward trade payments. At best, they could support immaterial ancillary merchant collections in limited offshore currencies, but all RMB clearing and cross-border flows involving the WFOE must pass through a licensed Chinese commercial bank. Therefore, the recommended approach is to build the entire corporate banking structure on Tier-1 domestic banks and avoid EMIs for compliance-critical flows.

The Taiwan investor must remit the equity contribution via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a licensed mainland bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable as a primary treasury or clearing rail. China's PBOC and SAFE regulatory perimeter does not recognise offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex as licensed banks for domestic corporate account opening, RMB settlement, or foreign exchange clearing. They cannot receive WFOE equity capital, issue capital verification reports, or act as clearing banks for LCs or outward trade payments. At best, they could support immaterial ancillary merchant collections in limited offshore currencies, but all RMB clearing and cross-border flows involving the WFOE must pass through a licensed Chinese commercial bank. Therefore, the recommended approach is to build the entire corporate banking structure on Tier-1 domestic banks and avoid EMIs for compliance-critical flows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million annual aggregate related-party tangible-asset transfers; RMB 100 million annual aggregate related-party intangible-asset transfers; RMB 100 million annual aggregate related-party financial-asset transfers; RMB 40 million annual aggregate related-party service transactions. A Master File is required where the group's related-party revenue reaches RMB 1 billion and other statutory conditions are met.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations registration regime, but the substance doctrine is embedded in Article 47 of the CIT Law, the General Anti-Avoidance Rules, and the beneficial-owner tests under STA Announcement 2018 No. 35. The WFOE must demonstrate real core income-generating activities: procurement, inventory management, marketing, sales contracting, logistics coordination and risk control in China. The Taiwan shareholder claiming 5% dividend relief must itself perform the CIGA related to its shareholding and be the true economic owner of the shares. A letterbox Taiwan company, or a WFOE that merely creates paper invoices while the real trading functions occur outside China, will be denied treaty benefits and may be treated as a sham.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate False CIT Filing

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law: the tax authority recovers the underpaid tax plus late-payment surcharge and imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax. Serious evasion involving substantial amounts is criminal under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law, with fines and imprisonment for persons directly responsible.

Director personal liability: Directors and the statutory legal representative may have the corporate veil pierced under PRC Company Law if they abused the limited liability entity to conceal assets from tax authorities; courts may order civil restitution of diverted funds, freeze bank accounts, and restrict travel. The director in charge of accounting and taxation may be blacklisted as a seriously dishonest taxpayer, subjected to an exit ban, disqualified from serving as director, and sentenced to imprisonment for up to seven years for criminal tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Missing Contemporaneous Documentation

Statutory sanction: Articles 41 and 47 of the PRC CIT Law and STA Announcement 2016 No. 42 allow the tax authority to re-determine taxable income using the arm's length principle, impose interest and late-payment surcharges, and apply penalties for failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation. If the mispricing is characterised as fraud, penalties may equal 50% to 500% of the adjusted tax.

Director personal liability: A director who signed false transfer pricing documentation or instructed the financial team to conceal intercompany transactions may be jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax adjustments where the structure is held to be an abuse; the company may be blacklisted and the director restrained from obtaining tax clearance for outbound travel or asset sales. Serious deliberate mispricing with forged comparables can expose officers to criminal liability for tax evasion or document fraud.
Shell Conduit and Abusive DTA Relief / Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: STA Announcement 2018 No. 35 denies beneficial-owner status and the tax authority disregards the treaty benefit, reimposing the statutory 10% withholding tax on dividends, interest or royalties, together with late-payment interest and penalties. Application of the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the CIT Law can also cause recharacterisation of the financing and service arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors and shareholders who caused incorporation of the shell to evade tax may have the corporate veil pierced, with obligations to refund the preferential tax avoided and to satisfy unpaid corporate liabilities from personal assets. Taiwan and Chinese administrative authorities may place the entity on a tax violation blacklist, ban the legal representative from leaving the jurisdiction, and in cases of fraudulent tax refund or intentional evasion, the responsible individuals may face criminal imprisonment and criminal fines.

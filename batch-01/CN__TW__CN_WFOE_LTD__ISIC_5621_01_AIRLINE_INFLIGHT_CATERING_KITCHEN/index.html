Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible for this WFOE because the registered business is an airport bonded in-flight meal production kitchen requiring a physical, licensed production site, bonded inventory storage, and direct ramp delivery capability. The local SAMR will reject a virtual address for a food-manufacturing entity, the tax authority requires actual business premises for tax registration and invoice control, and Chinese banks will refuse to open corporate settlement accounts without a physical site inspection. A registered office must therefore be a real operating address satisfying food safety, customs supervision, and aviation security requirements.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No fixed statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a limited company under the Company Act of Taiwan. However, the WFOE must have paid-in capital reasonably commensurate with its approved investment amount and the ISIC 5621 inflight catering business scope, and the capital must be remitted into a Taiwan bank capital contribution account, verified by a certified public accountant, and reported on the capital verification report. There is no mandatory blocked escrow account requirement for a standard WFOE; the foreign exchange control regime requires the capital to be used only for the investment purpose approved by the Investment Commission, and any diversion requires prior approval.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, acting through its Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) portal and the Golden Tax Phase IV system for invoice clearance and tax supervision.

E-government filing portal

The CN WFOE obtains its legal status via the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) business license; under the integrated multi-license consolidation registration, SAMR data sharing auto-registers the WFOE with the STA and issues the tax Identification Number (TIN) equal to the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The WFOE must then activate its tax account in the STA e-Tax portal, register financial and legal controllers, set up payroll and social insurance records, implement statutory accounting records, apply for VAT invoice quotas, complete VAT general taxpayer status determination, and register the bonded in-flight catering kitchen with China Customs for export supervision. For foreign non-resident entities without a permanent establishment in China, the statutory route is non-resident enterprise tax registration through the withholding agent or a tax representative before the STA, supported by foreign entity certificate and contractual documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; China does not offer check-the-box pass-through elections or elective hybrid mismatch treatment. The WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide income. A VAT general taxpayer/small-scale taxpayer election is available under the VAT regime but does not change entity-level EIT classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the mandatory threshold for general VAT taxpayer classification; every Chinese WFOE must complete VAT tax registration upon establishment regardless of turnover. Non-resident foreign suppliers making taxable supplies into China may have separate registration or withholding-agent collection obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to goods physically exported from the PRC customs territory where the WFOE is the registered exporter, the goods are customs-declared for export, the export invoices are issued, the sales proceeds are received in foreign currency and repatriated within the statutory deadline, and the export documentation is complete. Inflight catering meals supplied from a bonded airport kitchen to international airlines may qualify for the export zero rate when the meals are loaded onto an outbound international flight under customs supervision and are consumed outside the PRC customs territory; domestic sales remain taxable at the applicable standard rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) filed through the China Customs Single Window for the international outbound movement
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation of the inflight catering cargo from the PRC customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or an equivalent State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) export foreign-exchange receipt record evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Export sales contract with the international airline or foreign purchasing entity
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export cargo manifest
  • Bonded warehouse entry/exit or customs supervision documentation for the airport inflight catering kitchen

China mandates real-time fiscal control via the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform under Golden Tax Phase IV. VAT invoices must be issued from the tax authority-controlled system, uploaded to the STA for near-real-time clearance, and protected by a tax-control digital stamp, anti-counterfeit QR code, and centralized data matching. High-volume B2B issuers, including the inflight catering WFOE, are required to use API-based integration with the platform for automated invoice generation, routing, and reconciliation.

The STA and China Customs jointly audit export zero-rating and refund claims. If the export declaration, transportation documents, invoice trail, bonded supervision records, or foreign-exchange repatriation evidence are missing, inconsistent, or false, the zero rate is denied, the VAT is retrospectively clawed back, and late-payment surcharge is imposed at 0.05% per day. A penalty of 50% to 500% of the under-collected tax may be applied under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection; fraudulent export refund claims may also trigger criminal liability under the Criminal Law, export-invoice restriction, downgrade of tax credit rating, and loss of export-tax-refund eligibility for the WFOE.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains partial capital account convertibility with current account transactions generally open. For WFOEs, profit remittance is permitted post-tax clearance and SAFE verification but subject to forex registration and source-of-funds documentation. Hurdles include SAFE approval thresholds for large outflows and potential delays in repatriation verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: Export receipts must be surrendered to the designated foreign exchange bank within 180 days. Mandatory conversion and surrender of 100% of export proceeds at the bank's prevailing exchange rate, unless exempted for specific trade financing with prior SAFE approval.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Legal constraints: Offshore digital EMIs are restricted for corporate treasury functions under China's AML and FX regulations; local bank accounts with SWIFT connectivity are required for cross-border operations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

For a PRC wholly foreign-owned enterprise, the competent authority is the State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China, administered operationally through the competent local tax service bureau and the national electronic tax bureau portal. The jurisdiction identifier "TW" denotes Taiwan and is legally inconsistent with a PRC WFOE structure. Taiwan is administered separately by the Ministry of Finance, National Taxation Bureau, and local tax offices; a PRC WFOE cannot be registered as a Taiwan entity under PRC WFOE legislation.

E-government filing portal

PRC WFOE procedure: (1) complete market-regulation registration and obtain the business licence; (2) obtain the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code, which functions as the enterprise tax identification number; (3) complete tax-information confirmation and taxpayer registration through the competent local tax bureau or electronic tax bureau; (4) confirm enterprise income tax, value-added tax, customs, foreign-exchange, statistical, social-security, and invoice profiles; (5) elect or qualify as a VAT general taxpayer where appropriate; (6) obtain electronic invoice and tax-control access; (7) register customs and the foreign-trade operator profile if exporting or importing; (8) register the enterprise bank account and foreign-exchange collection arrangements; and (9) maintain tax declarations even during dormant or nil-activity periods. A foreign enterprise without a PRC establishment generally obtains a tax identification record only when required for withholding, treaty relief, tax filing, or a specific PRC tax obligation; it does not obtain a PRC WFOE tax identity merely by being incorporated offshore. A Taiwan entity follows a separate Taiwan business-tax registration process and cannot use a PRC Unified Social Credit Code as its Taiwan tax ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China has no United States-style check-the-box election for a PRC limited company or WFOE. A WFOE is generally a PRC resident enterprise subject to entity-level corporate income tax, ordinarily at 25%, on worldwide income subject to statutory rules and treaty limitations. Available classifications and methods include VAT general-taxpayer treatment or small-scale-taxpayer treatment where statutory conditions are met, ordinary VAT calculation or an applicable simplified method, export VAT exemption or exemption-plus-refund treatment, and statutory preferential regimes such as high-and-new-technology enterprise treatment, small low-profit enterprise treatment, or approved regional incentives where eligibility is satisfied. These are substantive statutory regimes, not elective entity classifications. Hybrid-mismatch elections are not generally available as a broad taxpayer election. The Taiwan identifier in the payload requires separate legal validation before implementation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For PRC VAT purposes, annual taxable sales of RMB 5,000,000 is the principal threshold associated with small-scale taxpayer status and general-taxpayer administration. Tax registration and tax-obligation registration are required independently of that threshold once the enterprise is established and conducts or is legally required to conduct taxable business. The 13% rate generally applies to sales of goods, including qualifying manufactured goods such as drones and components, subject to classification, export, and special-rate rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For PRC goods exports, qualifying exported goods may receive VAT exemption, credit, and refund treatment rather than a universal automatic 0% cash-rate mechanism. Eligibility generally requires a registered exporting taxpayer, lawful export status, qualifying goods, valid customs export declaration data, compliant VAT input documentation, accurate export and VAT filings, and reconciliation of goods, invoices, customs data, logistics, and payment information. The applicable refund rate depends on the goods classification and current export-tax-rebate schedule; the 13% domestic VAT rate does not automatically determine the refund rate. For cross-border services, zero-rating or VAT exemption is available only for specified service categories and conditions, typically requiring an overseas recipient, offshore consumption or use, no prohibited domestic use, qualifying contracts, compliant invoices, payment evidence, and required filing or recordal. Domestic services performed for a foreign customer are not automatically zero-rated. Taiwan business tax applies separate zero-rating rules to qualifying exported goods and services.

  • Commercial invoice, packing list, sales contract, and purchase order
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or other transport document
  • VAT special invoice or other legally compliant input-tax documentation supporting the refund calculation
  • Export VAT exemption, credit, and refund filing records
  • Foreign-exchange collection, settlement, or payment evidence where required by the applicable export-tax-rebate and foreign-exchange rules

  • Product classification, export-control, customs valuation, and origin records where applicable

PRC taxable sales generally require issuance of compliant VAT invoices through the tax-controlled invoicing infrastructure or the fully digital electronic invoice system, as applicable to the taxpayer and locality. Invoices must contain accurate seller, purchaser, tax-identification, goods or service, amount, tax-rate, and tax-amount data. Electronic invoices are transmitted through the tax authority's electronic system; certain taxpayers and transactions are subject to digital validation, risk controls, invoice quotas, and tax-data matching. PRC VAT invoices are not universally subject to a transaction-by-transaction pre-clearance process equivalent to every Latin American clearance model, but the tax authority may impose real-time or near-real-time controls, invoice issuance restrictions, and digital risk verification. Export declarations, VAT invoices, customs records, logistics records, payment records, and foreign-exchange evidence must reconcile. A Taiwan operation would instead follow Taiwan's Ministry of Finance electronic invoice platform, business-tax filing, and e-invoice rules.

There is no universal PRC statutory rule requiring all export proceeds to be repatriated within exactly 180 days. The applicable timing depends on export-tax-rebate rules, foreign-exchange administration, bank review, transaction terms, and the evidence required for the specific filing. Delayed or missing foreign-exchange receipts may suspend, delay, or disallow export-tax-rebate treatment and may require additional explanation or supporting evidence. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating, exemption, credit, or refund claims may result in repayment of improperly obtained refunds or credits, recovery of underpaid VAT and surcharges, statutory late-payment interest, administrative fines, invoice restrictions, adverse taxpayer-risk classification, cancellation or suspension of refund privileges, customs penalties, foreign-exchange reporting consequences, and potential criminal liability for tax fraud, false invoicing, smuggling, or fraudulent customs declarations. Audits commonly test the authenticity of the customer, shipment, transport route, payment, production capacity, inventory movement, customs declaration, invoice chain, and consistency between customs, tax, banking, and accounting data.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Renminbi (CNY) is fully convertible for current-account transactions (goods/services trade and profit remittances) subject to documentary verification: for dividend remittance, the WFOE must complete annual audit, settle corporate income tax, and register the digital Tax/SAFE Form 113B (对外支付税务备案) before the bank debits the outward SWIFT. The capital account remains controlled: FDI capital injections require SAFE FDI registration (FDI外汇登记凭证), and discretionary FX settlement ('意愿结汇') introduced by the 2015 SAFE reform allows conversion of foreign-currency capital without transaction-specific evidence at the time of conversion, provided proceeds comply with the negative list (no securities investment, no entrusted loans to non-affiliates, no non-self-use real estate, no out-of-scope spending). Profit remittance by a Taiwan-owned WFOE is legally permissible without quota but is operationally gated by tax clearance, audit continuity, and the bank's Beneficial Ownership (URBO) file currency verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Compulsory surrender was abolished in October 2012; there is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio, and the WFOE may retain 100% of export receipts in its foreign-currency settlement account. However, repatriation is mandatory: export proceeds must be received in the designated SAFE-monitored settlement account within the contractual payment terms, and banks verify every credit against the Customs Export Declaration (报关单) through the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System (货物贸易外汇监测系统). Receipts (or payments) occurring more than 90 days after the customs event must be reported as delayed receipt/payment via the same system. The WFOE is classified 'A', 'B', or 'C': A-class units enjoy '先支付后核查' (pay-first, verify-later) facilitation; B/C-class units are subject to post-verification release, full documentary pre-approval, and, in the most restrictive C-class posture, mandatory import-payment/export-receipt matching ratios of up to 100% per transaction until upgraded by SAFE.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC / 中国人民银行) is the central monetary authority; the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE / 国家外汇管理局) exercises delegated authority over all foreign exchange current-account, capital-account, and trade-verification matters. Cross-strait RMB clearing operates under the 2013 Cross-Strait Currency Clearing Mechanism, with Bank of China Taipei Branch as the RMB clearing bank in Taiwan and Bank of Communications (Shanghai) as the TWD clearing bank on the mainland.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low Feasibility for offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currenxie, Payoneer) as primary operating treasury vehicles. Legal constraints: a mainland WFOE cannot lawfully hold RMB operating balances or perform onshore FX settlement via an unlicensed offshore EMI; PBOC's Administrative Measures for Payment Services (非金融机构支付服务管理办法) confines forex conversion and settlement to licensed institutions. Offshore EMIs are viable only as ancillary USD/HKD collection vehicles for Stripe/PayPal marketplace payouts, with periodic netting into mainland through current-account SWIFT credits accompanied by customs declarations and the SAFE trade-verification record. For PayPal, the WFOE should leverage PayPal China (贝宝支付, PBOC-licensed under the 2020 foreign-shareholding approval); for Stripe, settlement into mainland bank accounts must route through PBOC/SAFE-licensed cross-border aggregators (e.g., PingPong 杭州乒乓, LianLian 连连银通) which perform market-rate conversion, tax-data transmission, and customs reconciliation on behalf of the WFOE.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are mandatory when related-party transactions exceed NT$500 million (per fiscal year) or when the enterprise transacts with related parties in low-tax jurisdictions exceeding NT$50 million; Country-by-Country Report required when the consolidated group revenue exceeds NT$54 billion. Smaller WFOEs must still prepare contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation when related-party transactions exceed NT$100 million (or NT$50 million with low-tax counterparty).

Economic substance scope: Applicable: the Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) and the Income Basic Tax Act (IBTA) and Transfer Pricing Assessment Rules require the Taiwan-registered WFOE to demonstrate substantive business operations, real economic activity, risk control, and strategic decision-making within Taiwan; shell or letterbox structures without substance risk recharacterization of transactions, denial of treaty benefits, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent CIT Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 110 of the Tax Collection Act: penalty up to 3 times the tax underpaid (or up to 5 times for fraudulent evasion); prosecution under Article 72 of the Tax Collection Act carries imprisonment up to 5 years, detention, and/or fine up to NT$600,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and the responsible person (statutory representative or CFO) may be held jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties where they directly participated or knowingly benefited; travel bans under Article 24 of the Tax Collection Act; criminal imprisonment and asset seizures may apply.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income to arm's length; penalty of 2% on corrected tax amount for TP disclosure violations (up to NT$1 million for non-compliance); for intentional mispricing, a fine up to 5 times the underpaid tax with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved non-arm's length transactions that caused tax deficiency are jointly liable for the deficiency and penalties; intentional collusion with foreign related parties may be treated as tax crime subject to imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Real Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities may disregard the entity or reallocate income to the beneficial owner under controlled foreign company rules and the substantial business doctrine; treaty withholding benefits are denied; the tax administration may impose the higher 21% non-resident withholding rate on income recharacterized as Taiwan-source dividends.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and beneficial owners exposed to criminal liability for false declarations and money laundering; civil liability to the tax authority for all unpaid taxes and penalties; personal travel bans and asset freezes enforced.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering and Payment Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Control Act: administrative fines up to NT$5 million for non-compliance; criminal penalties for money laundering include imprisonment up to 7 years and fines up to NT$5 million; Customs violations may trigger seizure of goods and penalties up to 2 times the value of goods.

Director personal liability: Directors holding compliance authority under the AML regime are personally liable for failure to implement internal controls; criminal sanctions apply to actual participants; civil forfeiture claims attach to their personal assets.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mail-forwarding mailbox is strictly rejected for the registered office of a WFOE engaged in mail-order retail under ISIC 4791 with cash-on-delivery settlement. The PRC Company Law (Article 10) requires a real and continuous domicile, and SAMR registration will not approve an address that is not an exclusive leased or owned commercial premise. The local tax authority (State Taxation Administration) requires a physical site capable of storing accounting records, issuing electronic invoices, and receiving correspondence; a virtual address will trigger a tax registration suspension. Furthermore, commercial banks performing KYC for the cash-on-delivery collection network (including the target payment rails Airwallex and local logistics remittance) will refuse to open a corporate settlement account if the address is solely a virtual office, because the COD business model involves elevated cash-handling risk. Regional pilot programs that allow 'address hosting' (e.g., Shanghai Free Trade Zone) are limited to technology or services companies and do not extend to physical-goods COD retail. Therefore, a leased physical office with a red-seal lease certificate (红本租赁凭证) is mandatory for incorporation and for all subsequent bank and tax filings.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Taiwan WFOE limited liability company under ISIC 4791. The foreign investor must demonstrate adequate capitalization in the business plan reviewed by the Investment Review Commission. Paid-in capital must be remitted from abroad and evidenced by a bank certificate of inward remittance and a deposit balance certificate. No blocked escrow account is statutorily required for this e-commerce business scope.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for pure mail-order retail and cash-on-delivery e-commerce without a warehouse or assembly operation. If the company later handles physical goods, it must obtain a fire safety certificate from the local Fire Bureau (Civil Defense Division), a structural use approval from the municipal Public Works Department, and a workplace safety clearance under the Occupational Safety and Health Act before commencement.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic registration and filing are conducted through the Electronic Tax Authority (E-Tax Bureau) portal at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn, together with the provincial electronic tax landing pages and the STA VAT Invoice Management System.

E-government filing portal

For the resident CN WFOE: (1) complete incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation and obtain the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number; (2) complete tax registration, taxpayer qualification, and tax-scope confirmation through the E-Tax Bureau within 30 calendar days of business license issuance; (3) complete VAT taxpayer type identification (small-scale or general), invoice type calibration, and the export tax refund/export tax exemption filing record before beginning export operations. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment that earns Chinese-source income or supplies taxable goods or services into China, the competent tax authority issues a Non-Resident Enterprise Taxpayer Identification Number through the E-Tax Bureau after the applicant provides the foreign corporate registration certificate, board resolutions, a tax residency certificate from the jurisdiction of residence, a China-nominated agent or withholding agent appointment letter, and documentary evidence of the Chinese-source taxable event.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity classification. China does not operate a check-the-box or comparable pass-through entity election regime for a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise; a WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed at entity level under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. The separate VAT registration election between small-scale taxpayer and general taxpayer status is an indirect-tax registration choice, not an entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual VAT taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; tax registration itself is required within 30 calendar days of business license issuance regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from the Chinese customs territory by a VAT general taxpayer that holds a valid export-tax-refund filing record are eligible for the 0% export VAT rate with refund or credit of input VAT. Qualifying cross-border e-commerce retail exports may use the 9610, 9710, 9810, or 1210 customs supervision regimes and, where applicable, the no-invoice exemption treatment. The exporter must demonstrate actual cross-border dispatch through customs, retain the export sale documentation, and provide evidence that the foreign-currency export proceeds were repatriated to China within the statutory period, including where cash-on-delivery proceeds are collected by local logistics remittance partners and paid through licensed cross-border payment rails such as Airwallex or an equivalent regulated institution.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (customs export declaration / customs Bill of Entry equivalent) issued by China Customs with the customs declaration number
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill or comparable carrier-issued cross-border transport document
  • Export commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export contract or confirmed mail-order/e-commerce order record
  • Export foreign exchange repatriation and collection verification record on the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System, supported by bank inward remittance and foreign exchange settlement records
  • VAT ordinary invoice for the export sale where applicable, and, for trading entities, the input special VAT invoice from the upstream Chinese supplier
  • Cash-on-delivery payment rail foreign exchange reconciliation statement from the licensed cross-border payment provider or local logistics remittance partner
  • Certificate of origin or domestic purchase documentation required by customs or the destination jurisdiction, including preferential trade certificates where applicable for exports to Taiwan

Registered VAT taxpayers must issue invoices through the VAT Invoice Management System / STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform using an approved tax control device or a fully digitalized electronic invoice integration. Fully digitalized electronic invoices are uploaded in near-real time to the national electronic invoice platform with a unique invoice verification number, supporting anti-fraud duplicate-issuance checks. Enterprises with general VAT taxpayer status must use the special VAT invoice system; approved third-party e-invoicing service providers connect through STA-compliant APIs, and the Golden Tax System performs invoice clearance verification, false-invoice monitoring, and retrospective audit controls.

Tax authorities may scrutinize export declarations, transport documents, VAT invoices, and foreign exchange repatriation records through the Golden Tax System risk-monitoring modules and paperless export-tax-refund review. If export zero-rating or refund is found to be fraudulent, unsupported, or based on false documentation, the STA will disallow the zero-rating, claw back the refunded tax, assess late-payment surcharges, impose a penalty of one to five times the defrauded amount under the Law on Administration of Tax Levy and Collection, suspend the taxpayer's export tax refund processing for a statutory period, downgrade customs credit status, and may refer the matter for criminal investigation under PRC criminal and customs law for fraudulent export tax refund.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime for the New Taiwan dollar with the central bank actively smoothing disruptive or one-sided market flows. Current-account convertibility is broadly unrestricted for goods, services and ordinary trade-related remittances, while capital-account transactions require prior regulatory approval or post-remittance statistical filing through a licensed designated foreign-exchange bank. Inward direct capital contributions into a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise must be approved by the Ministry of Economic Affairs Investment Commission and then settled through a local designated foreign-exchange bank under a central-bank foreign-exchange transaction report. Export receipts are freely retainable in approved foreign-currency deposit accounts, but every export transaction must be supported by trade documents and reported for central-bank statistics. Profit remittance to the ultimate parent in mainland China is lawful only after the WFOE files its audited Taiwan statutory financial statements, settles all corporate income and withholding tax liabilities, obtains the annual central-bank remittance validation and submits the foreign-investor capital registration certificate. In practice the dominant hurdles are tax clearance, a clean capital verification record, and proof that each dividend remittance matches distributable reserves; any residual capital-account reduction requires separate Investment Commission approval. Offshore digital or anonymous e-money channels are not recognised for statutory capital, tax clearance or customs purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must collect and settle export foreign-currency proceeds within 180 days from the date of shipment or loading; the proceeds may be retained in an approved foreign-currency deposit account at a designated foreign-exchange bank or converted into New Taiwan dollars at the prevailing market rate. There is no legislated minimum mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter has full discretion to retain 100% of the foreign currency, provided the inward remittance is correctly classified, supported by export documentation and reported to the central bank through the depositary bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Offshore electronic money institutions such as Airwallex are not licensed as banks or electronic payment institutions under Taiwans Financial Supervisory Commission legal framework; they cannot hold statutory corporate deposits, receive capital injection, perform foreign-exchange settlement or issue tax-recognised trade receipts onshore. They may only act as ancillary software/technical introducers to local licensed banks, and they are not a permissible substitute for a designated foreign-exchange bank for WFOE treasury obligations.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Complex beneficial-ownership chain passing through mainland China and Hong Kong with nominee shareholders and opaque tiering

Root cause: Complex beneficial-ownership chain passing through mainland China and Hong Kong with nominee shareholders and opaque tiering

Defense evidence: Direct WFOE parent-structuring diagram, audited group consolidated financial statements, UBO declaration identifying all natural persons owning 25% or more, current share registry with individual shareholder identities, certified English translations of Chinese business licences and registration certificates, source-of-funds documentation for each historical share subscription, and signed bank references from the ultimate parents licensed bank.
Cash-on-Delivery e-commerce model using logistics agents to collect cash, producing fragmented local-currency deposits and possible mingling of third-party collections

Root cause: Cash-on-Delivery e-commerce model using logistics agents to collect cash, producing fragmented local-currency deposits and possible mingling of third-party collections

Defense evidence: Signed logistics cash-collection agency agreement, e-commerce platform order-level daily COD reconciliation reports, cash storage and branch deposit slips, internal cash-handling standard operating procedures, logistics delivery confirmation records, and a CPA-initiated revenue-recognition audit confirming no unrelated third-party funds pass through the WFOEs bank accounts.
Cross-border remittances routed through third-party payment aggregators or foreign logistics firms with trade descriptions inconsistent with ISIC 4791 mail-order retail

Root cause: Cross-border remittances routed through third-party payment aggregators or foreign logistics firms with trade descriptions inconsistent with ISIC 4791 mail-order retail

Defense evidence: Original commercial invoices describing the exact retail merchandise, packing lists, air waybill or bill-of-lading copies, final-mile delivery confirmation, customs export/import declarations, marketplace seller records, and SWIFT MT103 copies with consistent foreign-exchange purpose codes in field 70 matching the underlying online orders and logistics settlement.
Capital injection arriving after incorporation in multiple tranches from various group accounts or without a matching Investment Commission permit and central-bank FX settlement declaration

Root cause: Capital injection arriving after incorporation in multiple tranches from various group accounts or without a matching Investment Commission permit and central-bank FX settlement declaration

Defense evidence: Approved Investment Commission foreign-investor permit, copy of the approved remittance warrant, SWIFT MT103 messages showing the exact named remitter and legal beneficiary WFOE name, inward remittance credit advices, central-bank foreign-exchange transaction settlement report issued by the local designated bank, and a Taiwan CPA capital verification report signed within the statutory period confirming full subscription receipt.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT103 STP from the directly permitted foreign investor to the WFOEs NT-dollar or foreign-currency escrow account at a designated Bank of Taiwan branch. The payment must reference the central-bank foreign-exchange direct-investment purpose code for equity capital, and the remitter name must exactly match the foreign-investor permit holder. Immediately after receipt, the designated bank must be instructed to book the foreign-exchange transaction report and convert or retain the funds according to the investment permit. A Taiwan CPA must then issue the statutory capital verification report, typically within one month of remittance, and the original inward remittance advice, capital verification report and Investment Commission approval must be retained for tax filing, bank annual review and future dividend repatriation.Outbound trade disbursements from the WFOE operational account should use SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments and logistics fee settlements under correct current-account purpose codes. Commercial letters of credit may be issued through Bank of Taiwan or Mega International Commercial Bank via SWIFT MT700 for bulk procurement or high-value inventory purchases, providing documentary protection and reconciled shipping documents. Documentary collections are acceptable for open-account or documents-against-payment trade where the counterparty and transport corridor are stable; for cash-on-delivery logistics disbursements, domestic NT-dollar payments should be made through local clearing rails, supplemented by SWIFT MT103 for offshore logistics and platform partners, with every payment backed by commercial invoices, order-level reconciliation, delivery proof and applicable tax invoices.Deliverable New Taiwan dollar outright forward contracts with licensed designated foreign-exchange banks, supported by verified trade receivables, purchase orders and projected inventory flows and subject to central-bank reporting limits.Cross-currency swaps with the same licensed onshore banks solely for documented trade financing, import/export working capital or approved direct-investment balance-sheet hedging, with no speculative leverage and with full underlying exposure evidence filed with the central bank.Offshore non-deliverable forwards for non-NTD crosses only, entered into by the WFOE or its parent treasury through a licensed offshore counterparty as economic hedging of committed trade cash flows, without delivery of NTD at maturity and with independent confirmation that the hedge amount never exceeds the documented underlying exposure.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% on dividends distributed to a non-resident shareholder (including a Chinese parent company); under the 2024 amendment, the rate remains 21% (plus a possible additional retained-earnings surtax of 5% on undistributed earnings).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File must be prepared if the total intercompany related-party transactions exceed NT$50 million (for purchases) or NT$20 million (for sales), or if the entity engages in controlled transactions with a related party located in a low-tax jurisdiction, or if intangible-related transactions exceed NT$20 million; a Master File is required if total consolidated group revenue exceeds NT$30 billion and the group has cross-border transactions; the Annual Transfer Pricing Report (Form 105) must be filed with the tax return.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are not directly enacted in Taiwan, but the 'substance over form' doctrine applies under Article 12-1 of the Tax Collection Act (anti-avoidance principle). The tax authority may re-characterize a transaction if the arrangement lacks 'reasonable commercial purpose' or 'economic substance' (e.g., a Hong Kong intermediary without employees, assets, or operational risks used solely to re-invoice COD sales). Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) concept is applied through the permanent establishment threshold and the essential business-function analysis: to qualify for treaty benefits or to avoid a Taiwanese business income tax, the WFOE must prove that its sales activities, customer support, payment processing, and risk-bearing are performed in Taiwan; otherwise, profits are deemed attributable to a Taiwanese PE and fully taxable. Foreign companies with no Taiwanese PE are not subject to Taiwan corporate income tax, but if the entity is a Taiwan-resident WFOE, it is taxed on worldwide income regardless of economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Intentional Understatement

Statutory sanction: Article 110 of the Tax Collection Act: penalty equal to 0.5 times the underpaid tax if voluntary, 1 time if ordinary negligence, and 2.5 times if intentional; in addition, interest is assessed at the postal postal deposit rate for late payment; criminal evasion under Article 109 of the Business Tax Act for fraudulent VAT filings; for income tax, Article 41 of the Income Tax Act: a prison term of up to 5 years and/or fines up to NT$6,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or direct the creation of false invoices are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; the tax authority may apply for provisional seizure of personal assets, prohibition on transfers, and exit bans (blacklisting) under Article 24 of the Tax Collection Act for unpaid taxes exceeding NT$1,000,000; criminal conviction may carry imprisonment of 6 months to 5 years, and a director may lose the right to serve as a director of any Taiwanese company for a period of up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Sham Interposition and Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Article 12-1 of the Tax Collection Act empowers the tax authority to re-characterize the transaction, disallow the deductions, and assess tax with an additional penalty of up to 1.5 times the basic tax due; the Transfer Pricing Assessment Rules (2019 amendment) impose documentation penalties of NT$30,000 to NT$300,000 for failure to provide, and additional adjustments under Article 114-1 of the Income Tax Act; criminal liability for document forgery if false agreements are created.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to personally refund the re-characterized profits if the company becomes insolvent; a travel ban is automatically triggered under Article 33 of the Tax Collection Act for directors of companies with tax arrears above NT$10,000,000; the director’s personal assets are reachable if the court pierces the corporate veil due to commingling of funds, and imprisonment of up to 7 years can be imposed under Article 342 of the Criminal Code for breach of trust by acting against the company’s interest.
AML / Money Laundering (Cash-on-Delivery Payment Rails)

Statutory sanction: Taiwan's Money Laundering Control Act: administrative fines of NT$1,000,000 to NT$10,000,000 for failing to conduct Customer Due Diligence (CDD) and record-keeping; criminal penalties for money laundering: incarceration of up to 7 years and fines up to NT$50,000,000 if the proceeds originate from a predicate offense; if the emerging-market jurisdiction imposes its own AML fines, the WFOE may face parallel liability.

Director personal liability: Under the Money Laundering Control Act, directors who intentionally ignore suspicious transaction reports may be sentenced to 7 years’ imprisonment; the company's authorization to operate may be revoked; directors' personal bank accounts may be frozen and travel bans (blacklisting) applied by the Financial Supervisory Commission; if the corporate veil is pierced on the grounds of 'nominal management' or 'abusive veil', the director is personally liable for civil restitution of the laundered amount and criminal fines.

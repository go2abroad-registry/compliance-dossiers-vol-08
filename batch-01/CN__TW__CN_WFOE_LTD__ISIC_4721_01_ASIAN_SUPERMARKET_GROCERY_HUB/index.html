Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (国家税务总局, SAT) via the consolidated e-Tax Bureau portal (电子税务局, etax.chinatax.gov.cn); day-to-day administration and inspections are performed by the competent local Tax Service Bureau (主管税务机关) at the WFOE's registered address.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE's statutory tax ID is its 18-digit Uniform Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码) contained in the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation; under the 'multi-license integration' (多证合一) reform, tax registration is auto-completed within 30 days of issuance without a separate TIN application. The WFOE must then activate its e-Tax account with real-name authentication of the legal representative and finance officer, file its accounting-system and cost-allocation methods, report all bank accounts within 15 days of opening, and, if appropriate, apply for VAT general taxpayer status. A tax residency certificate for treaty or cross-strait purposes is obtainable online via the e-Tax Bureau.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — resident enterprise liable to 25% enterprise income tax on worldwide income; no pass-through or transparency election is available to a WFOE.

Available election forms

China has no check-the-box regime. Available elections: (a) VAT status election between small-scale taxpayer (3%, currently preferential 1%) and general taxpayer (13%/9%/6%) while 12-month sales remain below RMB 5 million, with mandatory general registration above that threshold; (b) export VAT settlement method election — 'refund after exemption' (免退税) for foreign-trade enterprises versus 'exemption, offset and refund' (免抵退税) for production enterprises; (c) CIT elections — small low-profit enterprise relief (20% rate on reduced taxable base), fixed-asset accelerated depreciation, and qualified technology advance deduction; (d) election to apply tax treaty or cross-strait arrangement benefits for Taiwan-source income, subject to beneficial-ownership substantiation. Hybrid-mismatch elections are not recognized; China's general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) and special anti-avoidance rules apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 — cumulative 12-month sales excluding VAT trigger compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration; below this threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer. Import VAT registration as importer of record is mandatory from the first customs entry without turnover threshold; non-resident sellers using Chinese e-commerce platforms are registrable regardless of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT (with input-VAT refund) applies when goods are physically exported from China's customs territory for delivery to Taiwan or overseas buyers, the WFOE holds valid export-refund registration as a general VAT taxpayer, the export invoice is issued, and goods are cleared as exports by customs. Offshore services consumed entirely outside China are zero-rated. Domestic retail of vegetables and selected fresh meat/eggs is VAT-exempt; most staple foodstuffs (grains, edible oils, dairy) carry 9%; general groceries 13%. Taiwan-bound shipments additionally require cross-strait customs evidence (route, port, and origin documents) to substantiate the export character and avoid domestic-supply reclassification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (海关出口货物报关单) with electronic customs release record
  • Commercial Invoice (商业发票) and Packing List (装箱单)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (海运提单/空运运单) evidencing physical departure from China customs territory
  • Export Sales Contract (外贸销售合同)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank FX settlement water bill (涉外收入申报单/出口收汇银行水单) matching the declared export value
  • Certificate of Origin (原产地证明) where a preferential cross-strait tariff arrangement (e.g., ECFA) is claimed for Taiwan-bound goods
  • VAT export invoice (出口发票), export-refund declaration (出口退(免)税申报表), and e-invoice list for the consignment

Mandatory fully digitalized e-invoices ('数电票') issued through the e-Tax Bureau; Golden Tax System Phase IV (金税四期) performs real-time invoice clearance via the taxpayer's tax digital account, using digital verification codes in lieu of physical stamping; B2B issuance requires integrated API/ERP submission and automatic recipient incorporation, and export e-invoices are matched against customs export declaration data.

Export zero-rating claims are risk-scored by the Golden Tax System and matched against customs declarations and FX receipts; anomalies trigger 税务稽查 (special tax audit). Fraudulent export-refund claims are criminalized under PRC Criminal Law Article 204 (骗取出口退税罪): fines of 1–5 times the defrauded tax plus imprisonment up to 5 years, escalating to 5–10 years, or 10 years to life, for large or aggravated amounts. Administratively, the SAT clawback requires repayment of the refund with daily late-payment interest at 0.05%; fines of 0.5–3 times the under-declared tax may be imposed under the Tax Administration Law. Taxpayers face 6–24 month suspension of export-refund qualification, downgrade to tax-credit class D, joint blacklist sanctions, and travel or financing restrictions on responsible officers; historical claims remain open to retrospective audit for up to 10 years in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese RMB is fully convertible on current account items (trade in goods and services, dividends, royalties) subject to authenticity verification through the SAFE digital monitoring systems; genuine trade payments can be settled and bought/sold freely via licensed banks. Capital account items are subject to regulated convertibility: foreign direct investment capital contributions must be registered under the SAFE FDI Registration regime, paid into a special Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a domestic bank, and conversion/disbursement into RMB must follow the 'voluntary settlement' rules under SAFE Circular 2019-28 and the QFDI negative list. Profit remittance to a Taiwan shareholder, while nominally a current-account 'profit remittance', requires the enterprise to present audited annual financial statements, CIT payment vouchers, the board resolution approving the dividend distribution, and the valid FDI registration certificate; the Taiwan investor is subject to a 10% dividend withholding tax (subject to the Cross-Strait Bilateral Tax Agreement where applicable). Repatriation of liquidation proceeds, share transfers, and reduction of registered capital remain capital-account transactions requiring MOFCOM/SAFE approvals.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current PRC regulations and SAFE Recordal Requirement, exporters must repatriate all export proceeds to China and deposit them in an export foreign exchange settlement account within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration; there is no mandatory surrender ratio mandated today, as the old mandatory conversion quota was abolished, but the proceeds must be either converted into RMB or held in a licensed FX account at a domestic bank. For this particular supermarket/grocery importer that historically does not export, the surrender obligation is dormant; if the entity later begins export activity (e.g. re-export of cross-border e-commerce goods), the 180-day repatriation deadline will apply.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC); foreign exchange administrative authority is the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), which operates as a PBOC-supervised independent agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with substantial legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI providers (e.g. Airwallex, Currenxie, Nium) are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in mainland China and cannot lawfully hold RMB for the WFOE or provide domestic collection rails. They may be used solely as optional payment facilitation nodes for outbound supplier disbursements in USD/EUR only where the underlying funds first originated from a licensed PRC bank account and the relevant SAFE purpose code and trade documents are attached. No PRC-licensed EMI or virtual bank currently exists for routine corporate treasury; reliance on offshore EMIs as primary treasury accounts would violate SAFE account-opening rules and PRC banking regulation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound capital contribution from the Taiwan shareholder must first be routed through a licensed PRC bank as an SWIFT MT103 wire credit to the WFOE's dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at the issuing bank. The bank will mandatory-verify the payment against the SAFE FDI registration code and the earlier MOFCOM filing; the proper purpose code must reflect 'foreign direct investment capital contribution' (e.g. SAFE capital-account business code 621011 (direct investment capital injection) or the newer category as designated by the settlement bank). Pre-remittance verification must include the notarised/legalised Taiwan investor certificate, board resolution of the China WFOE, the FDI registration certificate, and a compliance declaration of the ultimate source of funds for AML purposes. Once received, the funds may be settled into RMB at the USD/CNY spot rate under 'voluntary conversion' rules, and the local bank must report the settlement to SAFE through the Foreign Exchange Settlement Monitoring System; funds may not be used for equity investment or real estate outside the permitted business scope.Trade disbursements to overseas suppliers (e.g. Taiwan condiment processors, vegetable exporters) will be executed through SWIFT MT103 for small/medium payments and documentary Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600-compliant) for large or repeat shipments of imported goods. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% corporate income tax on taxable profit. A 5% undistributed earnings tax may apply to earnings not distributed in the relevant statutory period.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% Taiwan withholding tax generally applies to dividends distributed by a Taiwan company to a non-resident company or other non-resident recipient. A Taiwan-resident corporate shareholder generally receives different treatment, including tax-credit and imputation-system considerations. Dividend distributions may also affect the Taiwan company's 5% undistributed earnings tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due from 1 May through 31 May of the year following the fiscal year-end for a calendar-year taxpayer. A taxpayer with an approved non-calendar fiscal year generally files within five months after the end of its fiscal year. The provisional payment is generally due from 1 September through 30 September, normally based on one-half of the preceding year's assessed tax, unless an approved alternative calculation or exemption applies.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally 1 September through 30 September for a calendar-year taxpayer. The amount is ordinarily one-half of the preceding year's assessed corporate income tax, subject to statutory exemptions or an approved actual-income calculation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan transfer-pricing documentation is transaction- and revenue-sensitive. A Local File is generally required where the taxpayer has controlled transactions and does not qualify for the applicable exemption, commonly including cases where annual net operating revenue reaches at least NT$500 million or the aggregate annual related-party transaction amount reaches at least NT$200 million, subject to category-specific and regulatory tests. A Master File is generally required where the Taiwan entity belongs to a multinational enterprise group meeting the Taiwan threshold, commonly annual consolidated group revenue of at least NT$3 billion, subject to the current regulations. Country-by-Country Reporting generally applies to a Taiwan ultimate parent or surrogate parent meeting the EUR750 million consolidated group revenue threshold, with notification and filing rules. Thresholds and exemptions must be checked against the fiscal year and current Ministry of Finance regulations.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a separate UAE-style or Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations regime for ordinary companies. Substance remains relevant through the Income Tax Act, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, general anti-avoidance provisions, controlled foreign company rules where applicable, anti-tax-avoidance principles, withholding-source rules, and tax authority power to recharacterize transactions according to their actual economic substance. A Taiwan grocery subsidiary should maintain real retail operations, employees or appropriately documented outsourced functions, premises, inventory, purchasing authority, customer-facing activity, commercial risk assumption, accounting control, and board-level decision records. A foreign affiliate charging management fees, royalties, procurement fees, financing charges, or guarantees must demonstrate actual services, assets, personnel, benefits, pricing support, and allocation keys.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment, tax evasion penalties commonly calculated as a percentage of the evaded tax, late-payment surcharges, interest, compulsory collection, possible business sanctions, and criminal prosecution. Serious tax evasion can result in imprisonment and criminal fines under the Income Tax Act and related laws.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons, directors, managers, accountants, and other participants may face criminal liability where they intentionally participate, instruct, approve, or conceal the conduct. Civil restitution, tax recovery, director disqualification, asset attachment, and restrictions on departure may arise under applicable procedure. The corporate form does not protect intentional individual misconduct.
Retail Receipt and Value-Added Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, penalties generally linked to unpaid or underreported tax, denial of input credits, invoice penalties, interest, suspension or other administrative measures in serious or repeated cases, and possible criminal consequences for deliberate false invoicing.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons who direct or knowingly tolerate off-book sales, false invoices, or VAT fraud may incur personal criminal and civil liability, including restitution, fines, asset attachment, and possible imprisonment.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustment to arm's-length income, denial of deductions, additional corporate tax, interest, penalties for inaccurate reporting, withholding-tax reassessment, and possible alternative minimum tax effects. Failure to provide required Local File or Master File documentation may result in separate penalties and adverse assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face civil claims for breach of fiduciary duties and personal criminal exposure where transactions are knowingly fraudulent, constitute tax evasion, or involve false accounting. Mere tax adjustment normally does not create automatic personal liability without misconduct or statutory grounds.
Withholding Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the tax that should have been withheld, withholding penalties, interest, late-filing penalties, possible disallowance of deductions, and liability of the withholding agent for amounts not collected or remitted.

Director personal liability: The company and responsible withholding officers may be pursued for unpaid tax. Intentional concealment or false treaty documentation can result in criminal liability, fines, imprisonment, civil restitution, and asset-preservation measures.
Undistributed Earnings Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: 5% undistributed earnings tax, reassessment of improperly deducted expenses, withholding-tax consequences, interest, penalties, and potential recharacterization as dividends or shareholder distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for unauthorized distributions, breach of fiduciary duty, and restitution. Intentional false accounting or tax evasion can create criminal exposure.
Shell Company and Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions, denial of deductions, transfer-pricing adjustments, withholding-tax reassessment, corporate tax penalties, AML reporting, account restrictions, and potential criminal investigation for fraud, tax evasion, or document falsification.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and actual controllers may face personal civil and criminal exposure. Potential consequences include restitution, confiscation, asset freezing, travel or departure restrictions under applicable procedure, and imprisonment where fraud, tax evasion, money laundering, or false documents are proved.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial-Ownership Breaches

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, enhanced supervision, transaction restrictions, suspicious transaction reporting consequences, account closure, seizure or confiscation, and criminal sanctions under Taiwan AML legislation. Money laundering offenses can carry imprisonment of up to seven years and substantial criminal fines, subject to the offense and current law.

Director personal liability: Directors, responsible persons, compliance officers, and controllers who knowingly participate or facilitate laundering may be personally prosecuted. Civil restitution, confiscation, asset freezing, departure restrictions, and imprisonment may apply.
Customs and Import-Valuation Fraud

Statutory sanction: Customs duty and import VAT reassessment, penalties, confiscation, shipment detention, import-license consequences, suspension of import privileges, and criminal prosecution for smuggling or deliberate customs fraud.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons and customs agents may incur personal administrative and criminal liability for intentional false declarations, smuggling, prohibited goods, or document falsification, including fines, restitution, confiscation, and imprisonment.
Food Safety and Product Licensing

Statutory sanction: Product seizure, recall, destruction, fines, business suspension, license revocation, public disclosure, consumer compensation, and criminal penalties under food-safety legislation depending on the conduct and harm.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons and operational managers may face personal criminal liability for intentional or grossly negligent food-safety violations, together with civil compensation and restitution obligations.
Corporate Accounting and Document Falsification

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, accounting penalties, company-law sanctions, evidence-adverse assessments, criminal prosecution for falsification, and possible suspension or cancellation of registration in severe cases.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, accountants, and actual controllers who create or approve false records may be personally liable for civil damages, restitution, criminal fines, imprisonment, and procedural restrictions on assets or departure.
Foreign Exchange and Profit-Remittance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Bank rejection or blocking of remittance, administrative fines, tax reassessment, withholding-tax liability, foreign-exchange penalties, and investigation of illegal capital movements.

Director personal liability: Directors and actual controllers may be liable for unauthorized distributions, breach of fiduciary duties, false declarations, tax evasion, or illegal foreign-exchange transactions. Consequences may include restitution, asset attachment, departure restrictions, and imprisonment where criminal elements are established.
Improper Related-Party Debt and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Interest deduction limitation, transfer-pricing adjustment, withholding-tax reassessment, additional corporate tax, interest, penalties, and possible alternative minimum tax impact.

Director personal liability: Ordinary disallowance does not automatically create personal liability. Personal exposure arises where directors knowingly use sham debt, false documents, unlawful distributions, or tax-evasion arrangements.
Employment, Payroll, and Social Insurance Tax Breaches

Statutory sanction: Back withholding, payroll-tax reassessment, interest, administrative fines, social-insurance arrears, labor penalties, and possible criminal consequences for deliberate fraud or unlawful withholding.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may be liable for unpaid employee deductions, unlawful withholding, falsified payroll, and intentional labor-law violations. Civil claims and criminal sanctions may apply.

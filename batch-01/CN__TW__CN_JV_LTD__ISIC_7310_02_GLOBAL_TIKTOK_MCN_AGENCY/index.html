E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Taiwan-incorporated profit-seeking enterprise is taxed as an entity on its taxable income under the Income Tax Act, and the same entity is a Business Tax operator under the Value-added and Non-value-added Business Tax Act. No pass-through classification is available for a Taiwan limited liability joint venture.

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election, an LLC-style pass-through election, or a hybrid mismatch classification election. Entity-level income taxation is mandatory for a Taiwan company. The only available optional regime is the small-scale business VAT regime for businesses with average monthly sales below the Uniform Invoice threshold, which is not a corporate tax classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NT$40,000 average monthly sales for service businesses and NT$80,000 average monthly sales for goods businesses constitute the statutory starting point of value-added business tax collection; a business whose average monthly sales exceed NT$200,000 must register as a general VAT taxpayer and issue Uniform Invoices at the standard 5% output VAT rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, a 0% VAT rate applies to goods actually exported from the Taiwan customs territory; goods or services supplied for export or in connection with export trade; international transport services; and services rendered within Taiwan to foreign enterprises or individuals where the service is consumed or economically used wholly outside Taiwan, including qualifying cross-border creator-management, MCN agency, and digital marketing services supplied to non-resident platform entities, provided the service recipient is outside Taiwan and the consideration is paid in foreign currency. For goods exports, the goods must physically leave the Taiwan customs territory. For services, the provider must maintain contracts and performance records evidencing the non-resident status of the recipient, the offshore use of the service, and the settlement of foreign exchange proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Bill of Export) accepted by Taiwan Customs evidencing export of goods
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Taiwan customs territory
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List matching the export declaration and shipment schedule
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate or Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Designated Foreign Exchange Bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Service contract, platform payout statement, performance report, and foreign exchange receipt records for zero-rated cross-border MCN agency services

Taiwan does not operate a real-time fiscal device clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping system in the style of territorial clearance regimes. Instead, all general VAT taxpayers must issue serially numbered Uniform Invoices for taxable sales; electronic Uniform Invoices must be transmitted to the Ministry of Finance Electronic Invoice Integration Service Platform through the certified E-Invoice Turnkey API or an approved system interface using the taxpayer's Unified Serial Number, invoice serial number, and buyer carrier identifier. Paper Uniform Invoices must contain the statutory printed serial number and, for E-Invoice receipts, the random anti-counterfeit code and QR-code carrier. The tax authority performs risk-based reconciliation of invoice data, sales declarations, and export records; non-compliance with Uniform Invoice issuance or platform transmission requirements is penalized under the Uniform Invoice Usage Guidelines and the Business Tax Act.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to post-export audit by the National Taxation Bureau and Customs Administration. If the mandatory documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, falsified, or if the export or foreign exchange receipt is not substantiated within the statutory collection period, the 0% rate is revoked, the sale is reclassified as taxable at the standard 5% VAT rate, and the deficient VAT is assessed together with late interest and penalties. Under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act, a taxpayer who underpays or evades business tax by false export claims or unverified zero-rating is subject to a penalty of up to five times the underpaid tax; aggravated cases may be referred for criminal liability under the Tax Collection Act, and the business may be suspended from issuing Uniform Invoices pending rectification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan operates under a Central-Bank-managed floating exchange-rate regime; the New Taiwan Dollar (TWD) is determined by market supply and demand within a managed band, and both current and capital account conversions are legalised by the Foreign Exchange Control Act. Current account receipts and payments used for the MCN agency's content production, creator payouts, and global talent representation are freely convertible through authorised banks as long as an accurate declaration is filed and the underlying commercial invoice exists. Capital account flows—especially the inward equity injection from the PRC parent—are not free: the Investment Commission under the Ministry of Economic Affairs must pre-approve the Sino-Foreign JV's creation and additional capital calls, and the CBC's authorised bank requires a purpose code and the approval reference to be printed in the SWIFT narrative before funds are credited. Profit remittance hurdles include audited financial statements, a corporate income tax offset/payment clearance certificate, and the preparation of Form of Overseas Remittance for Profit (or equivalent CBC declaration) demonstrating that the cumulative registered capital has been fully paid and capital verification completed. For exports of intellectual property or creator-management revenues, the CBC requires surrender of foreign-currency proceeds into TWD within ten business days after the receipt is posted; however, companies with live trade hedging demands may apply for a foreign-currency retention quota. Failure to surrender on time or speculative round-tripping will attract central-bank penalties and injure the account relationship.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must submit a Report of Foreign Exchange Settlement for Export Proceeds to an authorized bank within 10 working days of receipt of the overseas payment; the standard surrender ratio is 100% conversion into New Taiwan Dollars. Retention in foreign currency is conditionally allowed only if the exporter has a pre-approved CBC foreign-currency deposit balance quota for trade hedging.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility. Taiwan reserves foreign-exchange settlement authority to domestic banks and post offices; the CBC does not permit unlicensed offshore digital EMIs to open local currency clearing accounts or directly service a Taiwan-registered company as a principal bank. Wise B2B and TikTok Creator Payout may only operate as downstream payment indicators if the ultimate settlement is wired to the JV's domestic NTD designated account through a corresponding bank. Fintech EMIs cannot provide the statutory capital verification report, CBC export declaration, or Investment Commission approval receipts required for regulatory filings.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be remitted by the PRC parent using SWIFT MT103/202 to the JV's designated capital escrow account at a Tier-1 domestic bank, with narrative including the Investment Commission case number and the CBC purpose code FDIT (Foreign Direct Investment in Taiwan). The receiving bank verifies the remitter is the exact equity holder, debits the funds to an escrow, and releases them to the operating account only after a Taiwan CPA issues the capital verification report and the bank confirms the CBC foreign exchange declaration has been filed. Any change in the remitter is refused.For MCN service commissions paid to overseas streamers, advertising networks and TikTok's Hong Kong payout entity, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 through a domestic Authorized Bank with purpose code Service Charge under CBC rules. For large trade equipment purchases from China, a Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) or Documentary Collections (MT791) can be used where commercial contracts require a payment guarantee; all outward remittances of profits require an audited balance sheet, tax clearance certificate and CBC profit remittance form.Deliverable USD/TWD forward contracts up to 12 months with documented underlying MCN advertising receivables or creator payout liabilitiesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited to non-TWD exotic currencies where no deliverable forward exists and supporting commercial documentary evidence is heldCross-currency swaps allowed only for committed medium-term capital loans matched to real operational FX exposure, with CBC declaration and annual risk review

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility. Taiwan reserves foreign-exchange settlement authority to domestic banks and post offices; the CBC does not permit unlicensed offshore digital EMIs to open local currency clearing accounts or directly service a Taiwan-registered company as a principal bank. Wise B2B and TikTok Creator Payout may only operate as downstream payment indicators if the ultimate settlement is wired to the JV's domestic NTD designated account through a corresponding bank. Fintech EMIs cannot provide the statutory capital verification report, CBC export declaration, or Investment Commission approval receipts required for regulatory filings.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A statutory CPA audit is mandatory when a non-public company's paid-in capital at fiscal year-end is NTD 30 million or more (approx. USD 970,000), or when annual net operating revenue is NTD 100 million or more (approx. USD 3.23 million). Headcount alone does not impose an independent audit in Taiwan; however, more than 50 employees triggers the obligation to appoint a certified accounting officer under the Business Entity Accounting Act, and more than 200 employees reclassifies the enterprise as a non-small-and-medium enterprise, which may create audit requirements under lender or shareholder covenants. In addition, MOEAIC-approved foreign-invested companies may be contractually or administratively required to file an audited balance sheet and income statement each year even if below the statutory thresholds.

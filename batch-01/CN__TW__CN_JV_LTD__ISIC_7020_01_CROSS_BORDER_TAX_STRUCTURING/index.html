A virtual office or commercial mailbox arrangement is technically permissible for initial Taiwan company registration because the Company Act only requires a registrable address in Taiwan, but it is operationally unattractive and partially rejected for this taxpayer-heavy consulting industry. The National Taxation Administration customarily denies registration of invoices and may conduct a substance review if the registered address is a shared mailbox service; the local customs of the Ministry of Finance will not accept a purely virtual office for tax consolidation, and banks opening corporate accounts require a physical office inspection by a relationship manager. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity engaging in taxable activities in China, the entity must complete a one-time tax registration with the local tax authority (in-charge tax bureau) within 30 days of commencing business, submitting the application form, passport or corporate registration certificate, and a Chinese bank account proof. The tax authority issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) in the format of a 15-digit code (for non-residents) or a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) for resident entities. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (resident entity), the USCC is automatically assigned upon business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and serves as the TIN. The JV must then complete tax registration with the STA within 30 days of obtaining the business license, providing the USCC, articles of association, and legal representative identification. The tax authority issues a tax registration certificate and activates the electronic tax account.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated for VAT, provided the goods physically leave China and are declared to customs. For cross-border services (including advisory services under ISIC 7020), zero-rating applies if the services are rendered to non-resident clients and are consumed entirely outside China, as per the 'cross-border service zero-rating' provisions under Caishui [2016] No. 36. The service must be listed in the 'Zero-Rated Cross-Border Services Catalogue' and the supplier must maintain evidence of the offshore nature of the service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Receipt Voucher
  • Service Contract and Invoice
  • Proof of Service Delivery (e.g., reports, deliverables)
  • Export VAT Rebate Application Form (for goods) or Zero-Rating Declaration (for services)

China mandates the use of the Golden Tax System (Phase IV) for all VAT invoices. All invoices must be issued through the official e-invoicing platform (电子发票服务平台) and transmitted in real-time to the STA's central database. The system applies digital anti-fraud stamping (税控码) and requires API integration for automated invoice issuance and clearance. Non-compliance with real-time reporting or use of unauthorized invoice formats results in invoice invalidation and penalties.

The STA conducts rigorous audits on export zero-rating claims. If documentation is incomplete or fraudulent, the tax authority will claw back the zero-rated VAT refund, impose a penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax evaded, and levy a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day. In cases of deliberate fraud, criminal liability may be pursued under the Criminal Law (Article 204) for tax evasion, with imprisonment up to 7 years. Additionally, the entity's export tax rebate qualification may be suspended for up to 3 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict separation between current account and capital account convertibility. Current account transactions, including trade in goods, services, and profit remittances by foreign-invested enterprises, are generally freely convertible provided that genuine commercial documentation is submitted to the designated foreign exchange bank and the transaction is recorded in the SAFE Foreign Exchange Account System. Capital account items, however, remain subject to prior approval, filing, or quota-based administration. A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture established in mainland China under the Foreign Investment Law must register its foreign exchange capital via the SAFE capital project information system, and the actual receipt of foreign currency capital into a Capital Account (Foreign Currency) requires bank verification against the foreign investment certificate and the Articles of Association. For operational foreign currency needs, the JV may open a Basic Operational Currency Account, but large cross-border shareholder loans and equity injections are separately monitored. Profit repatriation to the Taiwanese shareholder is permitted only after annual statutory audit and tax clearance certificates, and the corporate income tax final settlement must be completed. The remitting bank will require the board resolution declaring dividends, the audited financial statements, the tax payment proof, and a SAFE-designated purpose code, typically 621101 for profits. Surrender requirements apply to export receipts: SAFE mandates that export proceeds in foreign currency be either repatriated into China and converted into CNY via the onshore market or retained in a domestic foreign currency current account, subject to a time limit of 180 days from the date of settlement of the export declaration unless extended under SAFE's trade facilitation measures. Non-trade current account receipts may be converted with appropriate supporting documents. Overall, the currency is not freely floating internationally, and the offshore New Taiwan Dollar is not a directly deliverable currency in mainland China; therefore cross-border flows between Taiwan and mainland China often route through USD or CNY settlement mechanisms such as the cross-strait RMB clearing line, with the Taipei and Shanghai clearing banks acting as intermediaries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of export declaration settlement, and under the prevailing SAFE regime there is no mandatory forced conversion ratio; however, the foreign currency retained in a domestic foreign currency account must be converted to CNY if so elected, otherwise it must be held for permitted operational or capital expenditures.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Payoneer, or Revolut Business are not permitted to act as the primary transactional bank account for a mainland China registered Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture because China's banking regulations require entities to maintain RMB and foreign currency accounts with domestic licensed banks that are integrated with SAFE's cross-border receipt and payment monitoring system. Digital EMIs may only serve as ancillary collection tools for overseas customers, but they cannot serve as the main clearing rail for capital injection, export proceeds repatriation, or corporate tax and dividend remittances. Legal constraints arise from the State Administration of Foreign Exchange's Circular on Further Simplifying and Optimizing Foreign Exchange Management Policies for Cross-Border Trade and Investment, which requires banks to verify the authenticity of cross-border receipts and payments directly from account transaction logs and customs declarations; such verification cannot be completed through an unregulated third-party EMI. Therefore, the viability rating is LOW for primary treasury function and MEDIUM as a supplementary receivable channel for low-value consulting fees, subject to periodic reconciliation and prompt sweep into a licensed domestic bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Taiwan shareholder must remit registered capital from a Taiwanese bank in New Taiwan Dollars or US Dollars to the JV's Capital Account (Foreign Currency) at a designated mainland domestic bank via SWIFT MT103. The wire instruction must include the SAFE foreign exchange capital injection purpose code, typically 'Service trade contract' for consulting projects or 'Real investment (registered capital)' under SAFE's current account and capital account classification; the remitter must state the relationship to the beneficiary and the investment nature. The Chinese receiving bank will verify the Foreign Investment Information Report with the Ministry of Commerce and the SAFE foreign investment registration; then it will convert the funds into CNY at the spot rate using the onshore CNY wholesale reference price published by the China Foreign Exchange Trade System, and deposit into the RMB capital account. The bank will issue a capital verification report for the JV, which must be filed with the tax authority for stamp duty paid on registered capital. For each drawdown, the JV must update the SAFE capital project information system before the funds can be used for business purposes. The injection must not be fragmented into multiple small wires without matching investment contracts because the bank will aggregate and review all capital account receipts.Trade settlement for consulting and tax structuring services is primarily executed through direct SWIFT MT103 payments from clients or through the JV's own outbound wire using purpose code 619 for professional consulting services. For larger procurements of software tools or overseas subcontracting, the JV may use Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C at sight) with the domestic bank as issuing bank and a foreign bank as advising and negotiating bank; however, for advisory services, Documentary Collections are less common and only suitable for physical deliverables, so the recommended rail is a clean SWIFT MT103 with an underlying signed invoice and service acceptance certificate. Cross-strait to Taiwan suppliers can also use the RMB clearing rail through Bank of China Taipei Branch and the Taipei RMB clearing house, reducing FX conversion costs for payments denominated in CNH or CNY, provided the Taiwanese counterpart maintains a RMB deposit account with a participating bank.SAFE-listed ordinary forward contracts between the domestic bank and the JV for hedging underlying current account receipts and payments denominated in USD or EUR for up to 12 months, with requirement of real trade background and no net speculative positionsNon-deliverable forwards on USD/CNY and EUR/CNY permitted only where the JV demonstrates a genuine exposure from a signed services export contract and the hedge is executed with a domestic bank onshore under SAFE's macro-prudential risk management guidelinesCurrency swaps between USD and CNY are permissible only for capital account transactions such as foreign currency loan conversion or long-term equity repatriation, subject to SAFE approval and the underlying real investment transaction, and cannot be used for speculative arbitrageFX options are restricted but may be permitted for hedging premium risk on commercial tenders if the JV qualifies under SAFE's products eligibility and obtains bank approval with a documented underwriting file

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Payoneer, or Revolut Business are not permitted to act as the primary transactional bank account for a mainland China registered Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture because China's banking regulations require entities to maintain RMB and foreign currency accounts with domestic licensed banks that are integrated with SAFE's cross-border receipt and payment monitoring system. Digital EMIs may only serve as ancillary collection tools for overseas customers, but they cannot serve as the main clearing rail for capital injection, export proceeds repatriation, or corporate tax and dividend remittances. Legal constraints arise from the State Administration of Foreign Exchange's Circular on Further Simplifying and Optimizing Foreign Exchange Management Policies for Cross-Border Trade and Investment, which requires banks to verify the authenticity of cross-border receipts and payments directly from account transaction logs and customs declarations; such verification cannot be completed through an unregulated third-party EMI. Therefore, the viability rating is LOW for primary treasury function and MEDIUM as a supplementary receivable channel for low-value consulting fees, subject to periodic reconciliation and prompt sweep into a licensed domestic bank account.

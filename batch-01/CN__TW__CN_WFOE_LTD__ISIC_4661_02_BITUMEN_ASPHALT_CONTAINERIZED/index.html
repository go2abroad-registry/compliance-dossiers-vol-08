Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Taiwan Ministry of Finance and its subordinate National Taxation Administration (NTA); regional National Tax Administration offices (Taipei, Northern, Central, Southern, Kaohsiung) administer profit-seeking enterprise tax, business tax, and uniform invoice controls. Online portals: Taiwan Tax Portal (etax.nat.gov.tw) and Electronic Uniform Invoice Integration Service Platform (einvoice.nat.gov.tw).

E-government filing portal

An offshore non-Taiwan corporate entity, including a PRC-incorporated WFOE Limited, must first obtain investment approval from the Investment Commission of the Taiwan Ministry of Economic Affairs, under the Statute for Investment by Foreign Nationals for non-PRC investors or the cross-strait regulation applicable to mainland-China profit-making enterprises. The entity then registers a branch office or subsidiary under the Taiwan Company Act. Within 15 days after business commencement, the entity must file a profit-seeking enterprise tax registration application with the local National Taxation Administration, attaching the approved investment certificate, company registration certificate, articles of incorporation or charter, proof of the registered place of business, identification of the responsible person and directors, corporate seal records, and Taiwan bank account details. The NTA verifies the application and issues a tax registration certificate containing the eight-digit Unified Business Number (UBI), which is the single taxpayer identification number for income tax, business tax/VAT, customs control, and electronic uniform invoice issuance. A foreign entity with no fixed place of business in Taiwan that supplies only electronic or digital services to Taiwan persons may instead apply online for simplified business tax registration on etax.nat.gov.tw to obtain a UBI and appoint a fiscal representative; however, containerized physical goods such as bitumen imported into Taiwan will be cleared by the Taiwan importer of record, and the offshore seller is not required to hold Taiwan VAT registration solely for the export sale unless it maintains a fixed place of business or a taxable presence in Taiwan.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a Taiwan-registered branch or subsidiary of a foreign WFOE is taxed as a domestic profit-seeking enterprise on Taiwan-source income; a non-resident without a fixed place of business is subject to withholding on Taiwan-source income, and Taiwan does not permit pass-through classification for corporate WFOE structures).

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not operate a check-the-box entity classification election or a hybrid mismatch election under the Business Tax Act. The tax classification is determined by the legal form and registration obtained under the Taiwan Company Act; a foreign investor may choose between branch and subsidiary forms, and an entity may voluntarily apply for VAT registration below the statutory threshold, but these are procedural choices, not statutory tax classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For goods, the applicable category for wholesale bitumen, mandatory business tax registration is triggered when average monthly sales exceed NT$80,000; for services, NT$40,000. Foreign e-commerce digital service suppliers are required to register when annual taxable turnover exceeds NT$480,000. General VAT taxpayers with average monthly sales above NT$200,000 must issue uniform invoices and collect VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, exported goods are zero-rated. The registered profit-seeking enterprise must prove that the goods were actually exported from Taiwan customs territory, that the sale was to an overseas buyer, and that the relevant export customs declaration and transport documents support the movement of goods. For containerized bitumen, the cargo must leave Taiwan under a valid customs export declaration and ocean bill of lading or multimodal transport document. Output VAT on a zero-rated export is charged at 0%, and the input VAT attributable to the exported goods is recoverable by credit against other output VAT or by refund under Article 39 of the Business Tax Act, subject to documentary verification by the National Taxation Administration and Customs Administration.

  • Customs-validated export customs declaration (export entry) issued by Taiwan Customs Administration under the exporter's Unified Business Number
  • Original or authenticated electronic ocean Bill of Lading, or multimodal transport document, showing the Taiwan port of loading, container number(s), and overseas discharge port
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list describing the bitumen product, HS tariff classification, net and gross weight, and container number(s)
  • Container freight movement evidence, including container seal numbers and the shipping carrier's container manifest
  • Bank evidence of export proceeds settlement: inward remittance advice, export foreign exchange settlement certificate, or SWIFT LC/TT payment advice matching the invoice value
  • Signed sales contract or purchase order evidencing the foreign buyer and the agreed INCOTERM and payment terms

Taiwan does not impose a real-time fiscal approval or anti-fraud digital stamping system. The statutory system is the Electronic Uniform Invoice (EUI) regime. Registered VAT taxpayers issuing electronic uniform invoices must transmit invoice data to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integration Service Platform or its certified Value-Added Service Centers using the MOF Turnkey integration/API channel. E-invoice data must be transmitted generally within 48 hours after issuance. Anti-fraud controls rely on the mandatory unique uniform invoice number, random check code, carrier registration, and cross-matching of invoice data with customs declarations, bank foreign exchange records, and VAT returns; no separate centralized fiscal stamp is affixed.

Taiwan Customs Administration and the National Taxation Administration cross-audit export declarations, bills of lading, container movement records, VAT returns, and bank foreign exchange receipts. If zero-rating is claimed without the complete documented proof, or if export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day statutory period without lawful extension, the zero rate is reversed, VAT at 5% is collected with interest, and any refunded input VAT is clawed back. False or fraudulent export declarations are treated as tax evasion under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act, carrying a penalty of five to fifteen times the evaded tax, and may also trigger criminal liability under Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act, as well as restriction or suspension of the taxpayer's export declaration and uniform invoice privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a controlled currency regime under PBOC and SAFE. The Renminbi is not freely convertible on the capital account; convertibility is registration-based and transaction-specific. For a Taiwan-invested WFOE, current account trade flows such as import payments and export receivable collections for drummed and containerized bitumen are convertible after the bank completes 'genuineness and consistency' verification against customs declarations and underlying trade documents. Capital account transactions, however, require prior SAFE approval and registration: the initial paid-in capital remitted from Taiwan must be registered as foreign direct investment, credited into a dedicated capital foreign exchange account, and any subsequent conversion from foreign currency to RMB must be supported by a capital payment intention statement and a specific use plan. Profit remittance to Taiwan is permitted only after the statutory annual audit, corporate income tax settlement, and the completion of SAFE profit distribution registration; unresolved inter-company balances or tax liabilities will block the outflow. Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory conversion, but they must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange settlement account and reconciled within the SAFE cross-border receipts and payments monitoring system. The regime therefore reduces liquidity risk when documentary discipline is maintained, but creates material settlement delay when capital registration is incomplete or underlying documents are mismatched.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender ratio applies: 0% of export foreign currency receipts must be converted to RMB. The WFOE must repatriate all export proceeds from containerized bitumen shipments to its domestic foreign exchange settlement account within the standard 90-day export collection period after customs declaration; non-repatriation beyond the 90-day window requires a written extension filed with the designated bank and SAFE before expiry. Foreign currency may remain in the settlement account, and voluntary conversion to RMB is permitted after submission of the sales invoice, customs export declaration, bill of lading, and bank receipt matching.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable for the WFOE in Mainland China. Offshore digital EMIs and Taiwan-based non-bank fintech platforms are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to provide onshore RMB or foreign currency account services, execute SAFE capital account registrations, or settle trade payments against Chinese customs declarations. They cannot serve as the primary clearing entity for this treasury structure. Feasibility rating: Low. They may be used only in ancillary offshore invoicing for non-China legs, and even then transfers to the onshore WFOE must ultimately clear through a licensed Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: the Taiwan investor instructs its Taiwan bank to issue an SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's SAFE-registered capital foreign exchange account at a Chinese Tier-1 bank. The beneficiary account must be identified as a capital project account, and the remittance purpose plus SAFE BOP transaction code must read 'foreign direct investment paid-in capital injection', using the code family 601 for direct investment. The Taiwan remitter must be exactly the approved investor in the SAFE FDI registration; funds from unrelated third parties are prohibited. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the transaction in the SAFE digital foreign exchange monitoring database and issues a capital flow record. If RMB is required, the WFOE submits a capital payment intention order, settles the foreign currency into RMB, and disburses funds only against a directors' resolution and underlying transaction documentation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Taiwanese WFOE is required to prepare a Local File transfer-pricing report when its annual controlled related-party transactions exceed NT$300 million, and a Master File when the group-wide related-party transaction or consolidated-turnover criteria prescribed by the Ministry of Finance are met; Country-by-Country reporting is separately required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above the NT$27 billion statutory threshold.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not apply a Gulf-state-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the tax administration applies OECD-aligned substance, beneficial-ownership, and Core Income-Generating Activity (CIGA) tests; for a WFOE trading bitumen on a containerized wholesale basis, economic substance in Taiwan requires a physical place of management, local operational staff performing procurement, logistics control, risk assumption, sales negotiation, invoicing, storage decisions, and independent corporate governance; if those functions are performed by the mainland China parent and the Taiwan entity is merely a pass-through invoice, remittance, or bank-account shell, Taiwan will disregard the corporate form, deny treaty benefits, reattribute taxable profit, impose a 21% constructive-dividend withholding tax, and apply tax evasion penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 110 authorizes penalty of up to two to three times the understated tax; Article 41 of the Tax Collection Act criminalizes willful tax evasion with fraudulent means, carrying imprisonment for up to five years and fine; the tax authority also charges overdue-interest penalties and may blacklist the entity for non-payment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign a false return or order the creation of fake logistics documents may have the corporate veil pierced; they are personally and jointly liable for civil restitution of the underpaid tax, assets in their names can be frozen, bank accounts and overseas travel are blocked by the National Tax Administration blacklisting, and they face criminal imprisonment with possible disqualification from holding office.
Sham Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will disregard the Taiwan entity under Income Tax Act Article 43-1 and anti-abuse case law, deny intercompany deductions, reclassify remittances as taxable Taiwan-source income, apply a 21% deemed-dividend withholding, impose understatement penalties up to three times the tax, and report the shell structure to the competent customs and company registries for compulsory deregistration.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are treated as de facto owners of the sham; they are strictly liable for the unpaid withholding and corporate income tax, lose limited liability where corporate formalities are ignored, face civil seizure of personal assets, travel bans preventing departure from Taiwan, and criminal liability for fraudulent incorporation if they signed false declarations of substance.
Transfer Pricing Evasion / Money-Laundering-Style Cross-Border Payment Abuse

Statutory sanction: Taiwan's Transfer Pricing Regulations permit full recharacterization and repricing to the arm's length margin; the adjustment is subject to Income Tax Act Article 110 penalty of two to three times the resulting tax shortfall, plus a 21% withholding on deemed dividends; Money Laundering Control Act penalties attach to fraudulent trade-payment schemes with imprisonment and substantial fines; false trade documents also expose the WFOE to customs and banking-law sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or benefit from the transfer-pricing strip are jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax, penalties, and withholding; personal assets derived from the scheme are subject to confiscation; civil restitution to the Ministry of Finance is ordered; the directors may be placed on an immigration blacklist and detained at the border; and criminal sentences under the Tax Collection Act and Money Laundering Control Act can result in imprisonment for five years or more where the scheme is organized.

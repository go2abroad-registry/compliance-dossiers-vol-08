Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally rejected by mainland Chinese tax authorities and banks for this industry. The State Taxation Administration expects a physical place of business for tax registration, invoice control, and records inspection, and banks under PBOC customer due diligence require an in-person interview, a valid lease contract, utility receipts, and frequently a site inspection. A registered address in a licensed free-trade-zone cluster address, government-supported e-commerce park, or business incubation center may be accepted if the enterprise has substantive personnel, books, and management presence there. A mere P.O. Box, mail-forwarding agency, or shared virtual office without a lawful sublease is likely to be rejected and will create a high shell-company red flag. For cosmetics DTC online retail, if inventory and operations are home-based, local policy must be checked; otherwise the WFOE should maintain a genuine service office.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. Taiwan Company Act does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a limited liability company conducting online retail of cosmetics. The subscribed capital must be reasonable relative to the stated business scope and paid into a newly opened NT$ bank escrow / capital verification account. A certified public accountant or certified public bookkeeper must issue a capital verification report (資本額查核報告書) stating that the capital has been fully paid before registration. E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a subsidiary in Taiwan, the entity must first complete company registration with the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA). After incorporation, the company must apply for a Business Tax Registration (統一編號) with the local National Taxation Administration. The application requires the company's incorporation certificate, articles of incorporation, and identification of the responsible person. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act. For goods, the goods must be physically exported from Taiwan. For services, the services must be provided to a foreign buyer and used outside Taiwan. For e-commerce, cross-border digital services provided to foreign consumers are zero-rated if the service is not used in Taiwan.

  • Export Declaration (出口報價)
  • Bill of Lading (提單)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate (外貨結售證明)

Taiwan mandates electronic invoicing (e-invoice) for businesses with monthly sales exceeding NT$200,000. The e-invoice system requires real-time transmission to the Ministry of Finance's E-Invoice Platform (EIP) via API. For certain high-risk industries, anti-fraud digital stamping is mandatory. All e-invoices must be issued within 48 hours of the transaction.

If export zero-rating is claimed fraudulently or without proper documentation, the tax authority may impose a penalty of up to 5 times the tax amount, plus interest. Additionally, the zero-rating may be reversed and the tax assessed retroactively.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, RMB is not freely convertible on the capital account, while current account transactions are generally convertible after banks verify authenticity and completeness. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) administers a strict registration, filing and use-control system. For a Taiwan-invested WFOE, inbound equity capital must first complete FDI foreign exchange registration with a designated domestic bank, after which the capital can be remitted into the WFOE's capital escrow account, converted to RMB via the bank, and used only for approved business purposes. Profit remittance to Taiwan is treated as a current account outward payment, but requires the WFOE to submit audited Chinese GAAP financial statements, finalised corporate income tax settlement certificates, a board resolution authorising the dividend, and evidence that no SAFE penalties are outstanding. The bank must verify that the paid-in capital has been capital-verified by a Chinese CPA and that the remittance does not constitute cross-border round

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) – the statutory exchange control regulator; local SAFE branches supervise all onshore banking and cross-border transactions.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% under Article 4 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law (EIT Law), applicable to the WFOE as a PRC resident enterprise on worldwide income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding on dividends paid by the PRC WFOE to non-resident shareholders under EIT Law Articles 3 and 37; reduced to 5% under the Cross-Strait Double Taxation Arrangement (signed 25 August 2015, effective 8 February 2016) where the Taiwan-resident corporate shareholder is the beneficial owner of the dividend and holds at least 25% of the paid-in capital of the WFOE.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Late filing carries an administrative fine of RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 plus a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% on the unpaid tax from the statutory due date; underpayment caused by concealment is penalized at 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under Tax Administration Law Articles 62 and 63; intentional evasion in excess of RMB 50,000 and exceeding 10% of the payable tax is referred for criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201; failure to attach the related-party transaction schedules triggers a presumption of non-compliance and the special TP adjustment interest rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (本地文档) is mandatory where annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial/financing transactions, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File (主体文档) is mandatory where total annual related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion. The annual related-party transaction report (年度关联业务往来报告表, G100000 series) must be filed whenever any related-party transaction exists, irrespective of these thresholds, under SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply UAE-style ESR but enforces a statutory substance-over-form doctrine through: (1) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulations Articles 120-122, which permits recharacterization of arrangements lacking reasonable commercial purpose and substance; (2) the beneficial-ownership test contained in SAT Bulletin 2017 No. 6, requiring the Taiwan dividend, interest, and royalty recipient to demonstrate real staff, premises, operational risk, and decision-making rather than conduit status; (3) the special tax adjustment regime with a six-year look-back and interest at the PBOC benchmark rate plus five percentage points; (4) deemed permanent establishment risk under the Cross-Strait arrangement because a Taiwan-parent online DTC operation run through a Chinese WFOE with sales fulfillment in China is treated as a China PE. The WFOE must employ substantive local management, warehouse/inventory risk, procurement contracts, HR, and independent bank accounts; absent such substance, profits are re-attributed to the Taiwan shareholder, the 5% WHT relief is denied, and the full 10% statutory rate applies with interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Underreporting of Online Revenue

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus 0.05% daily late surcharge; confiscation of the evaded amount; aggravated tax evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and representing over 10% of payable tax triggers criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201, with a fine and imprisonment of up to seven years imposed on the responsible person.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and actual controllers face joint and several civil restitution liability where corporate assets are stripped; SAT exit-control measures impose travel bans; personal bank accounts may be frozen under criminal asset-preservation procedures; the responsible director is subject to imprisonment upon criminal conviction.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The SAT re-determines taxable income under EIT Law Article 41; additional CIT is assessed with interest computed at the PBOC benchmark rate plus five percentage points over the full adjustment period; fines of RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 apply for non-production of contemporaneous documentation under Tax Administration Law Article 44; the special tax adjustment six-year limitation period applies.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the transfer pricing policy are cross-examined in criminal tax referrals; exit bans and tax delinquency watchlisting are imposed on the legal representative; where funds were knowingly diverted out of the PRC, directors may be held jointly liable for the adjusted tax and interest.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization annuls the claimed tax benefit; the reduced 5% Cross-Strait dividend rate is denied and the full 10% WHT is re-collected with interest; related-party expenses are reversed into non-deductible items; the arrangement anti-abuse clause expressly authorizes denial of benefits for shell arrangements; retroactive adjustment up to ten years applies in cases of fraud or misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: PRC Company Law Article 20 veil-piercing applies where corporate personality is abused; directors are personally liable for company debts arising from the sham structure; foreign travel is restricted where flight risk is identified; criminal liability may attach for fraudulent use of the corporate form.
Thin Capitalization / Disguised Equity

Statutory sanction: Interest on the excessive debt portion is non-deductible under EIT Implementation Regulations Article 119; the disallowed interest is recharacterized as a deemed dividend subject to 10% WHT (or 5% if treaty-qualified); consecutive-year violations attract a 50% underpayment penalty plus adjustment interest.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives who approve non-arm's-length shareholder loans face personal criminal exposure for failure to withhold the deemed dividend WHT; asset preservation orders may extend to personal holdings where the corporate form is abused to avoid tax.
AML / Payment-Integrity and Cross-Border Information Exchange Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law as revised in 2022, fines of up to RMB 5 million may be imposed on the entity; accounts are frozen; administrative blacklisting follows; profits traced to untaxed sources are confiscated in addition to the full back-tax and the 50% to 500% underpayment penalty; tax-relevant data is exchanged with Taiwan under the Cross-Strait arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge of the layering scheme face criminal liability for money laundering under Criminal Law Article 191, with imprisonment of up to ten years plus fines and personal asset confiscation; travel bans are issued, and cross-Strait mutual legal assistance enables extradition-style transfer for serious evasion cases.

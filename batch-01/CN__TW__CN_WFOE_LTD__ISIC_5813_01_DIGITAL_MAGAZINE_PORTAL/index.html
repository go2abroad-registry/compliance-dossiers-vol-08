Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital applies to a Taiwan limited liability company. The registered capital must be fully subscribed and paid into a dedicated Taiwan bank account before filing, and a certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report confirming deposit balances. No escrow account or blocked funds mandate applies unless imposed as a special condition by the Investment Commission in connection with the foreign ownership approval.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Taiwan (R.O.C.) Ministry of Finance (MOF), Taxation Administration; online portals: 'Tax e-Filing and Payment Service' at tax.nat.gov.tw and 'E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform' at einvoice.nat.gov.tw

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign enterprise supplying digital media services (e.g., magazine subscriptions or advertising placements) with annual sales to Taiwan customers exceeding NT$480,000 must register for Value-Added Business Tax with the Regional National Taxation Bureau. Procedure: (1) submit the Business Tax Registration Application (營業稅籍登記申請書), prepared in Mandarin; (2) appoint a tax representative who is a resident of Taiwan; (3) provide a certificate of incorporation issued by the home country; (4) submit a notarized power of attorney authorizing the tax representative; (5) receive the statutory Tax Identification Number (統一編號) assigned by the National Taxation Bureau. Since May 1, 2017, registration may be completed online via the MOF 'Tax Online Registration for Foreign E-commerce Operators' service, accessible through the tax.nat.gov.tw portal. The TIN remains permanently valid and must be cited on all Unified Invoices, electronic declarations, and cross-border remittance documents.

Procedure: (1) submit the Business Tax Registration Application (營業稅籍登記申請書), prepared in Mandarin; (2) appoint a tax representative who is a resident of Taiwan; (3) provide a certificate of incorporation issued by the home country; (4) submit a notarized power of attorney authorizing the tax representative; (5) receive the statutory Tax Identification Number (統一編號) assigned by the National Taxation Bureau. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a Taiwan-resident WFOE, business tax registration is mandatory upon incorporation regardless of turnover. For a foreign non-resident digital services provider, mandatory VAT registration is triggered when annual taxable sales to Taiwan customers exceed NT$480,000; below this threshold, the foreign entity may be subject to withholding tax at source on Taiwan-sourced revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, zero-rating applies to exported goods and to services that are provided to foreign enterprises or individuals and are not deemed to be used inside Taiwan. For the digital magazine portal and sponsored media placements, zero-rating is claimable if the offshore customer (subscriber or advertiser) is located abroad, the intellectual commercial value is consumed overseas, and the WFOE retains written contracts, usage logs, and IP address evidence demonstrating overseas use. The company must be VAT-registered and must issue unified invoices in foreign currency where applicable.

  • Export Customs Declaration Form (出口報單) filed electronically via the MOF Trade Administration System - required when physical promotional materials or bundled print media cross the customs border
  • Bill of Lading (載貨證券) or Air Waybill (空運提單) - evidencing physical cross-border movement of exported goods, if any
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (外匯存入/結匯證明) or bank Foreign Exchange Receipt (外匯收入水單) - evidencing collection of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial contract, service invoice issued in foreign currency, and detailed usage report - substantiating the offshore supply and usability of digital services where no customs form applies

Under Taiwan's Uniform Invoice Act and E-Invoice Implementation Regulations (電子發票作業要點), the WFOE must issue electronic Uniform Invoices through the MOF E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform or a certified turnkey device. Each e-invoice must be transmitted to the MOF E-Invoice Database within 48 hours of issuance. The platform mandates API-based real-time submission (Turnkey E-Invoice Transmission Solution), digital filing via the taxpayer's certificate, and compulsory issuance of Cloud Invoices (雲端發票) for qualifying digital sectors. Anti-fraud controls include sequential invoice numbering, encrypted QR codes generated by the platform, and automated cross-matching with the quarterly VAT e-Declaration System.

The National Taxation Bureau conducts comprehensive audit scrutiny of export zero-rating claims. If documentary evidence is missing or the service is determined to have been used in Taiwan, the zero-rating is retrospectively revoked for up to five years, the underpaid 5% VAT is assessed, and a penalty of 1 to 5 times the underpaid tax (increasing to 5 to 10 times for cases of deliberate fraud) is imposed under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act. Fraudulent claims may also trigger criminal prosecution under Article 41 of the Tax Evasion and Penalty Act (稅捐稽徵法), carrying imprisonment up to 5 years. The enterprise bears the full burden of proof for overseas use and FX repatriation; failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days is prima facie evidence of a domestic sale.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan dollar is generally convertible for current-account payments, subject to bank documentation, transaction reporting, sanctions screening, and applicable foreign-exchange settlement limits. Capital-account transactions, including inbound investment, capital reductions, dividends, intercompany lending, and disposal proceeds, are subject to Taiwan company, investment, tax, and foreign-exchange rules and may require filings, approvals, or supporting evidence. A Taiwan company generally needs distributable earnings, approved financial statements, tax clearance or evidence of tax payment, and corporate approvals to remit dividends. Mainland China remittances by a PRC WFOE instead fall under PRC foreign-exchange and cross-border payment rules; Taiwan rules do not govern that entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Taiwan does not generally impose a blanket mandatory conversion ratio or automatic surrender of ordinary export proceeds. Export receipts may generally be retained or converted through an authorized foreign-exchange bank, subject to applicable declarations, transaction documentation, settlement limits, and bank review. Banks may require export contracts, invoices, shipping or service evidence, and reconciliation of receipts. Confirm current CBC rules and the company's accumulated settlement position with the handling bank before material transactions. Do not apply this assessment to a mainland China WFOE, for which PRC rules may impose different registration, documentation, and settlement requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC). The Taiwan jurisdiction identifier conflicts with the payload's China sovereign-country designation and WFOE entity label. Taiwan and mainland China have separate legal, banking, and foreign-exchange regimes; a mainland China WFOE cannot be treated as a Taiwan-incorporated entity. Confirm the entity's place of incorporation and operating jurisdiction before account opening or remittance.

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a substitute for a Taiwan domestic corporate bank account. Offshore electronic-money institutions and payment platforms may support eligible collections or payouts only where the provider accepts the entity, country, business model, and settlement destination.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Taiwan Company Act. There is no statutory minimum paid-in capital requirement and no blocked escrow account obligation. However, the foreign investor must obtain an Investment Commission approval letter before remitting capital into Taiwan, and the remitted funds must be held in a Taiwanese bank account and verified by a certified public accountant before the incorporation filing can be completed. The approved capital amount must be reasonably proportionate to the stated wholesale copper cathode trading scope and the WFOE's payment rails, including documentary letters of credit and LME warrant transfers.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and the national unified Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Taxation) portal, which is the statutory online system for taxpayer registration, tax returns, VAT invoice administration, and export tax rebate processing.

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the STA assigns the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) upon completion of company registration with the Market Regulation Administration; the USCC is the taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT registration number. No separate manual TIN application is required for the WFOE. A foreign non-resident enterprise that has not established a Chinese entity but derives Chinese-source income or creates a permanent establishment must file for non-resident tax registration through the e-Taxation portal with the local STA office, providing the foreign certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, identity documents of responsible persons, and, where applicable, a China tax representative appointment letter. A foreign enterprise that only receives China-source passive income subject to withholding is not ordinarily required to obtain a full TIN; the Chinese withholding payer completes the withholding and reporting obligations. The WFOE should then confirm VAT taxpayer status (general or small-scale) and register for export tax rebate eligibility through the same portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not have check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity elections under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. A WFOE is mandatorily taxed as a Chinese resident enterprise on its worldwide income; the only relevant statutory election is the VAT taxpayer status election between general VAT taxpayer and small-scale VAT taxpayer, subject to the RMB 5,000,000 turnover threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Any person engaged in taxable sales in mainland China must complete tax registration at the competent STA office; annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 mandate general VAT taxpayer registration, while turnover of RMB 5,000,000 or below normally qualifies for small-scale VAT taxpayer treatment with a 3% collection rate, unless voluntary general taxpayer status is elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT general taxpayer may apply the 0% export VAT rate to physical export sales of Grade A copper cathodes where the goods are lawfully declared to mainland China Customs and physically exported from mainland China customs territory, the export sale is made to an overseas buyer, delivery is substantiated by transport documentation, and the export proceeds are repatriated to China within the statutory period. Exports to the Taiwan customs territory are eligible on the same cross-border export basis. Qualifying offshore services consumed wholly outside China may also be zero-rated, but the primary commodity scope here is physical copper cathode arbitrage.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (Export Customs Declaration/Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or multimodal transport document evidencing physical departure from mainland China
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the exporting WFOE
  • Export sales contract or purchase order
  • Bank entry or settlement proof evidencing receipt of foreign exchange or cross-border RMB from the Taiwan or other overseas buyer, including the Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank remittance advice
  • Inspection certificate, certificate of origin, or other export control document required for the commodity

VAT invoices must be issued through the STA's electronic invoice issuance system and the national Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform, with real-time data transmission to the Golden Tax system (Golden Tax Project Phase IV) for invoice matching, anti-fraud analysis, and risk control. Large taxpayers may integrate system-to-system API invoicing; VAT special invoices are subject to statutory controls on sequence numbers, passwords, and stamping, and any unreported or false invoicing is subject to severe administrative and criminal penalties.

If the tax authority determines that the export was fictitious, the goods did not physically leave the customs territory, the export declaration was false, or foreign exchange was not repatriated within the prescribed deadline, the claimed zero rate or export VAT refund will be clawed back in full with late payment surcharge. The exporter becomes liable for VAT at the standard 13% rate and may be fined under the Law on Tax Collection and Administration and the Customs Law of the People's Republic of China. Fraudulent export-refund schemes constitute tax evasion crimes under the Criminal Law and expose the entity and responsible officers to criminal penalties, loss of export rebate status, and public tax blacklisting.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is not freely convertible on capital account. Current-account trade transactions are convertible under a bank-led true-trade verification framework, but capital-account items such as paid-in capital, shareholder loans and equity repatriation are subject to SAFE registration, use restrictions and negative-list screening. Taiwan-sourced investment in a Chinese wholesale WFOE enters through a designated FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account and must be settled strictly in accordance with the approved capital purpose; funds may not be redirected to prohibited activities such as unauthorized mainland equity investment or unapproved entrusted lending. Dividend remittance to the Taiwan parent is permitted only after audited financial statements are prepared, the corporate income tax filing is completed and tax clearance is evidenced, and the bank has received a board resolution plus the underlying capital registration documents. Export proceeds from copper cathode re-exports are not subject to a mandatory conversion ratio, but they must be repatriated to China through an authorized bank and must pass the trade authenticity review involving invoice, customs declaration, bill of lading and payment matching. The practical hurdles are documentary: banks require precise purpose-coding, timely SAFE updating, and clean matching between physical trade and financial flows for RMB to be settled without delay.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to China through an authorized bank; for general trade in goods the collection is expected to be completed within 90 days from customs export declaration unless longer trade terms up to 180 days have been properly recorded in the bank's trade credit system. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may retain foreign currency in a settlement account or convert to RMB at market rates, subject to same-bank verification of the underlying export contracts, customs declarations, inspection certificates and payment flows. Any prolonged overdue export collection or unexplained partial collection will trigger enhanced AML review.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) with delegated State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Under PBOC and SAFE regulations, only designated authorized banks can open and operate FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Accounts, accept or settle export proceeds, perform FX conversion, and clear documentary credits. Fintech EMIs and third-party payment institutions are not licensed as SAFE-designated banks for capital-account remittances and cannot issue or confirm commercial L/Cs, manage LME warrant clearing, or act as the WFOE's primary treasury clearing rail. They may be used only for ancillary domestic collection convenience; they must remain subordinate to the bank documentation flow and cannot replace the statutory KYC/AML filing. A compliant treasury structure should therefore be built on Tier-1 domestic commercial banks, with no reliance on offshore EMI gateways for inbound FDI or physical copper settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity funding from Taiwan must use SWIFT MT103 from a Taiwan bank to the WFOE's designated FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a SAFE-listed Chinese commercial bank. The remittance instructions must carry the SAFE FDI registration reference and the correct balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment capital contribution; the bank will validate the code against the FDI registration form and the Articles of Association. After receipt, the bank performs capital account reporting, and the WFOE must obtain a CPA capital verification report within the statutory filing period. The funds may then be converted to RMB and transferred to the RMB basic account only after bank confirmation that the use is within business scope and not on the Foreign Investment Negative List. No third-party payment rail or unlicensed EMI may be used for this capital injection.Clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 is used for direct open-account and spot settlements to international copper suppliers, with invoices, warehouse warrants and transport documents pre-validated by the bank. Commercial Letters of Credit are advised via SWIFT MT700 and amendments via MT707; the issuing domestic bank pays complying presentations by MT103 or MT202 after checking bills of lading, LME warrant transfer instruction, quality and quantity certificates. Documentary Collections in the form of D/P or D/A may be used for approved counterparties, with the bank acting as collecting and advising agent, but they are weaker risk mitigation than LCs for high-value non-ferrous commodity flows. All outbound disbursements must be coded under the bank's trade-in-goods reporting framework and kept fully traceable to the physical copper transaction.Deliverable CNY/USD forward contracts for genuine underlying trade or capital exposure, supported by contracts, invoices, customs declarations and flow forecasts, booked through an SAFE-qualified Chinese bank.CNY/FX swaps and cross-currency swaps for balance-sheet hedging of foreign-currency shareholder loans or long-term trade financing, subject to SAFE registration and real business-need verification.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not generally permissible for an onshore resident WFOE as a standalone hedge; offshore NDFs may be considered only at the Taiwan parent level with licensed offshore banks and should never be used as speculative onshore products unless an explicit SAFE exception exists.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% (Taiwan Profit-Seeking Enterprise Income Tax) plus a 5% retained-earnings surtax on undistributed net earnings

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% on dividends paid to a non-resident profit-seeking enterprise shareholder; 21% if the shareholder is a non-resident individual; under the Taiwan-China Cross-Strait Avoidance of Double Taxation Agreement, the source-state rate may be reduced to 5% for a beneficial owner holding at least 25% of the Taiwan payor's capital and to 10% in all other eligible ownership situations.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement under the Taiwan Company Act for a limited company. However, the investment approval process requires the full subscribed capital to be remitted into a licensed Taiwan bank account and verified by an authorized foreign exchange bank and a certified public accountant before the company registration filing is accepted. There is no blocked escrow account rule, but the capital must remain on deposit in the company’s capital account for business commencement.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Equipment Inspection Certificate and Public Use Building Fire Safety Certificate issued by the local Fire Department under the Fire Services Act; Building Public Safety Inspection every two years under the Building Act; structural load and occupancy certificate for the warehouse; and workplace safety and occupational health clearance for racking, manual handling, and emergency evacuation issued by the Occupational Safety and Health Administration, Ministry of Labor, Taiwan.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) through its unified electronic tax bureau (e-Tax) platform and the local competent tax authorities of the WFOE's registered address.

E-government filing portal

The CN_WFOE_LTD, formed as a wholly foreign-owned limited liability enterprise under PRC law, obtains its Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) automatically through the market regulation multi-license integration procedure. After issuance of the business license by the Market Regulation Administration, the Uniform Social Credit Code is electronically transmitted to the STA and becomes the statutory TIN for enterprise income tax, VAT, customs, and social insurance. The WFOE must subsequently activate its tax account in the e-Tax portal, register banking and accounting information, and, for export activity, complete the export tax rebate registration (export tax exemption and refund record-filing) with the competent tax authority. A foreign non-resident enterprise with no PRC establishment that derives China-source income obtains a separate non-resident enterprise TIN by filing the non-resident enterprise tax registration information with the competent tax office of the withholding agent or the source income payer; the non-resident enterprise must submit incorporation evidence, a local tax representative authorization if required, and the applicable tax treaty documentation. No physical tax registration certificate is issued; the TIN is confirmed through the e-Tax system.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary exemption threshold for VAT registration; any operating entity, including a WFOE, must register from establishment date. Under the small-scale taxpayer rules, annual taxable sales of RMB 5,000,000 or less allow simplified VAT treatment, while exceeding RMB 5,000,000 mandates general VAT taxpayer registration with the headline 13% rate on goods sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim the 0% export VAT rate for finished garments under ISIC 4641 where the goods are physically exported from the PRC customs territory to Taiwan, are declared under customs export supervision, the seller is a general VAT taxpayer with a valid export tax rebate filing, the sales price is in foreign exchange, and the corresponding foreign currency export proceeds are received by the WFOE in accordance with SAFE trade receipts monitoring. The export must be a genuine offshore sale to a Taiwan-based buyer, supported by the sales contract, commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, and payment evidence; no export zero-rating is available for goods sold within China.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration (Customs Export Declaration) as transmitted by the General Administration of Customs to the STA
  • Bill of Lading, sea waybill, or air waybill evidencing export from a PRC customs port to a Taiwan port
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the off-price apparel shipment
  • Export sales contract or purchase order
  • Foreign exchange settlement receipt or bank issuance evidencing repatriation of export proceeds (export collection verification data through SAFE, including the inward remittance record in the bank's foreign exchange system)
  • VAT zero-rated export invoice (digital tax invoice) issued under the e-Tax e-invoice platform

The STA mandates issuance of VAT invoices through the national Golden Tax digital control infrastructure and the nationwide fully digitalized e-invoice platform. Every VAT special and ordinary invoice must carry a tax control number and be reported in real time to the e-Tax system. General VAT taxpayers use the STA-approved electronic invoice service or application-to-application (API) interfaces for invoice issuance, certification, and verification. Export transactions require zero-rated VAT invoices recorded in the same digital invoice platform, with customs declaration data cross-matched through the tax authority's export rebate database.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to desk review and on-site audit by the STA. The tax authority cross-matches the customs export declaration, the freight log, the e-invoice, and SAFE foreign exchange collection data. If the zero-rating is disallowed due to missing export proceeds, fraudulent documents, circular or unsubstantiated transactions, the refunded VAT is recovered with late payment interest, an administrative fine of 100% to 500% of the fraudulently obtained export tax refund is imposed, the export tax rebate eligibility may be suspended or revoked, and the matter is referred for criminal liability under the Criminal Law if the conduct constitutes fraudulent export tax refund. For fraudulent claims, the recovery period is not time-limited under the PRC tax collection statute.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under China's FX regulatory architecture, current-account transactions are generally convertible and require only documentary authenticity review at the bank, whereas capital-account transactions are subject to strict ex-ante approval, registration, or filing through SAFE administered mechanisms. For a registered wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE), foreign shareholders may convert FDI capital into RMB only after the FDI enterprise has obtained the SAFE Foreign Investment Registration Certificate and opened a designated capital account; proceeds from such conversion are then placed under the Renminbi settlement account with prohibitions on unrestricted use. Profit remittances to offshore shareholders are permitted only after annual statutory audit, corporate income tax payment, and presentation of a profit distribution resolution, audited financial statements, and tax clearance certificates to the settlement bank, which in practice requires a separate SAFE/operating bank check for each remittance. Although China has formally abandoned mandatory export restriction ratios, surrender (repatriation) of trade proceeds remains mandatory for settlement within timeframes aligning with customs and contract terms, with detailed monitoring through SAFE's cross-border receipt and payment platforms. Therefore, despite strict controls, compliance can be achieved by maintaining impeccable documentation for each operating cash flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No blanket surrender ratio is imposed by SAFE for export proceeds; however, all proceeds must be repatriated to mainland China within the contractually agreed window (normally 90 days from customs declaration but extendable under genuine trade terms) and reported through the goods trade FX monitoring system before repatriation or sale. In practice, banks require the exporter to provide a positive trade background check, customs declaration number, and invoice to support each credit.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the sovereign monetary authority and FX regulator for all mainland China cross-border transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility for using offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, or Wise for the WFOE's primary account is Low. Mainland Chinese regulators do not grant EMI licences to these entities, and they are legally barred from opening local current or capital accounts in the name of a Chinese-registered company without a licensed bank partner. They may only serve as payment aggregation tools that ultimately clear through domestic banks, and they cannot satisfy SAFE's capital account registration or regulatory reporting obligations. Therefore, the WFOE must maintain its statutory bank accounts with a licensed Chinese commercial bank; EMI rails may be used for ancillary supplier disbursements only after internal compliance review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For each FDI capital injection from the Taiwan shareholder, the following protocol must be executed: (1) Complete SAFE's foreign investment (FDI) registration and obtain the Foreign Investment Registration Certificate; (2) Open a dedicated capital account (FDI account) at a licensed bank, designating a purpose code corresponding to 'Capital injection' as per SAFE's standard list; (3) Instruct the remitting bank to use SWIFT MT103 with a purpose code 'CASS' (capital account settlement) or the bank's internal code; (4) Upon receipt, the bank verifies the transfer against the FDI certificate, pre-set remittance details, and the investment contract, then issues a 'bank capital-settlement notice'; (5) For conversion to RMB, the WFOE provides a board resolution specifying the intended use and underlying documents; (6) Post-remittance, the capital verification report must be filed with the State Administration for Market Regulation within 30 days. No debiting of the capital account is permitted without a matching transaction evidence file.For settlements to overseas suppliers or concessionaires, the company must use either SWIFT MT103 (direct wire) for trade invoices under a payment term of open account or advance payment; or commercial letters of credit (L/C) and documentary collections (D/C) for intermediate and large-value consignments. All outbound wiring must be supported by a trade invoice, packing list, customs status, and a signed commercial contract. Banks in mainland China are permitted to clear payments in RMB to Taiwan via the cross-border RMB clearing arrangements (through Bank of China Taipei or similar), or in foreign currency via MT103 through correspondent banks. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory if any of the following related-party transaction thresholds for the year is met: tangible asset transfers exceeding RMB 200 million; intangible asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; or other related-party transactions exceeding RMB 40 million. Master File is required if aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. Country-by-Country Reporting applies when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue in the preceding year exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: PRC law does not adopt a separate Economic Substance Regulation regime, but substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, and business purpose doctrines are applied through CIT Law Article 47, the General Anti-Abuse Rule, and SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42. A China WFOE that holds only title to garments while all sales, pricing, inventory risk, and management decisions are performed by a Taiwan affiliate will be disregarded or recharacterised, with related-party income re-allocated to the actual economic actor. For treaty benefit claims, the non-resident recipient must have substantive business activity in its residence jurisdiction; a letter-box Taiwan or offshore conduit will fail the beneficial ownership test and be denied treaty relief. Taiwan's own anti-deferral (CFC) and actual-management location rules complement this analysis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion: Understated Garment Liquidation Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, the tax authority assesses the evaded CIT and imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, plus daily late-payment surcharges of 0.05%. Criminal tax fraud under the PRC Criminal Law may result in imprisonment up to seven years and a fine of up to one times the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives, finance directors, and actual controllers who sign false returns or order invoice fabrication are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the underpaid tax and penalties; they face travel bans, exit denial, asset seizure, and imprisonment for individuals directly responsible.
Withholding Tax Violations on Dividend Repatriation

Statutory sanction: A withholding agent that fails to withhold and remit is liable under TCAL Article 69 for a penalty of 50% to 300% of the unwithheld tax. The Taiwan shareholder may also be directly assessed for the underlying CIT. Fabricating service agreements to disguise dividends triggers additional false-invoice penalties under the Invoice Administration Measures.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve profit distributions or cause the payment of disguised dividends can be held personally for the unremitted withholding tax, with personal bank accounts frozen; serious cases lead to exit bans and criminal liability for intentional tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income, impose interest at the central bank benchmark loan rate plus five percentage points, and deny deductions or impose a penalty up to 50% of the adjusted tax if false documentation is supplied or the failure is intentional. Repeat or aggravated offenses can attract criminal sanction if the TP arrangement is part of a tax evasion scheme.

Director personal liability: Directors and transfer pricing officers who sign false documentation, obstruct audit access, or direct profit-stripping arrangements may face personal sureties, civil liability for the adjusted tax shortfall, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for fraud where intentional concealment is proven.
Shell Company / Artificial WFOE Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The transaction may be recharacterised or disregarded under the GAAR; income is re-allocated to the economic owner; treaty or arrangement benefits are denied; WHT of 10% is imposed on deemed dividends; customs and export registration can be revoked; and AML beneficial ownership reports are subject to PBOC fines.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and shareholders are personally liable for civil restitution of taxes and penalties, may be prohibited from leaving China until obligations are settled, may be disqualified from corporate management, and can be criminally prosecuted for fraud or money laundering if they knowingly operated a shell.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Transparency Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law, institutions face fines up to RMB 4 million and responsible individuals fines of RMB 200,000 to RMB 1 million. Serious violations can result in suspension of business, revocation of licenses, and criminal liability for money laundering under the PRC Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners who knowingly permit laundering or false BO filings face personal fines, travel bans, confiscation of assets, and imprisonment for up to ten years in aggravated money laundering cases.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Taxation Administration (Taiwan) through the regional National Taxation Bureau for the WFOE registered office; company registration through the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) One-Stop Business Registration Portal; electronic invoice infrastructure through the MOF e-Invoice Integration Service Platform (Turnkey Service); export clearance through the Customs Administration, Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

For a Taiwan-resident wholly foreign-owned limited liability company, the non-resident equity holder must first obtain inward investment approval from the MOEA Investment Commission; for mainland Chinese investors, specific approval under the Regulations Governing Investment in Taiwan by Mainland Chinese Investors is required. After MOEA incorporation under the Company Act, the WFOE must file the statutory Business Registration Application with the regional National Taxation Bureau within fifteen days of commencing business and obtain a Taiwan Unified Business Number (UB Number/TIN), which serves as the VAT registration number. The applicant must submit: articles of incorporation and company registration certificate, shareholder and director identification, power of attorney for local nominee or agent, registered office lease or title evidence, alcohol wholesale license, bonded warehouse license evidence, and other relevant sector licenses. The National Taxation Bureau then activates the business tax registration and Uniform Invoice allowance; to use electronic invoices, the entity must obtain a Key Account and register the Turnkey Service endpoint with the e-Invoice Integration Service Platform. A foreign non-resident without a Taiwan permanent establishment may instead register as a non-resident enterprise taxpayer, but the WFOE structure is resident and entity-level taxed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act, goods exported from Taiwan customs territory are zero-rated. For this bonded wine and spirits WFOE, zero-rating applies when the bonded goods are removed from the customs-supervised bonded warehouse and exported, evidenced by an approved Customs Export Declaration, and the beneficial ownership passes to a foreign buyer. Services directly related to the export (warehousing, handling, and freight within the bonded operation) may also be zero-rated where the purpose and destination are offshore. Excess input VAT attributable to the zero-rated export operations is refundable through the regular VAT return reconciliation mechanism, but refund claims are audited before payment.

  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) approved by Taiwan Customs, showing the goods leaving the customs territory
  • Customs-supervised bonded warehouse removal or transfer record evidencing the export movement
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the export sale
  • Original bill of lading (ocean) or air waybill (air) issued to the foreign buyer or to bearer
  • Bank-issued Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Taiwan bank Foreign Exchange Settlement / Export Proceeds Certificate
  • Sales contract or purchase order, and insurance certificate, evidencing payment terms and marine or air risk transfer

Taiwan operates a Uniform Invoice system rather than real-time continuous transaction control. There is no government-issued digital fiscal stamp. Every taxable sale must be evidenced by a Uniform Invoice (paper, electronic, or receipt-issuing machine). E-invoices must be transmitted to the MOF e-Invoice Integration Service Platform through the Key Account and Turnkey Service within the reporting periods and validation windows set by the Ministry of Finance; the platform verifies invoice numbers, issuing taxpayer ID, and QR code integrity. Export sales must be linked to the Customs Export Declaration in the tax return.

Zero-rating is strictly documentary. If the exporter cannot produce the customs and foreign-exchange evidence, the supply is reclassified as domestic taxable and the 5% VAT is assessed. Under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act, fraudulent or reckless zero-rating claims may draw a penalty of up to five times the evaded VAT, plus deferred-payment interest. The Tax Collection Act allows reassessment within seven years for non-declared or fraudulent claims. The Customs Administration may also require additional duties and impose customs penalties if bond movements are misconnected; forged export declarations or false inward remittance certificates can trigger criminal liability for tax fraud and customs forgery. The VAT refund is clawed back with penalty where the export proceeds are not repatriated within the prescribed period without Central Bank approval.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan dollar is managed under a regulated but trade-oriented convertibility framework. Current-account receipts and payments, including import/export trade settlement, commission income, and service fees, may be freely converted through designated foreign exchange banks without prior CBC approval, subject only to the CBC large-value foreign exchange reporting threshold. Capital-account flows, however, are not automatically convertible: foreign direct investment, equity subscription, shareholder loans, cross-border intercompany loans, and return of capital require prior foreign investment approval from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA), followed by CBC reporting and settlement through a designated FX bank. Profit remittance, classified as a current-account item under Taiwan rules, is generally permitted once the statutory annual audited financial statements are filed, a corporate income tax clearance is obtained, and a board resolution supporting the remittance is executed; nevertheless, for a China-parented Taiwan WFOE, the remittance must also satisfy China's SAFE profit-repatriation requirements on the receiving side. Export proceeds must be repatriated within 180 days of the export declaration; no fixed percentage is required to be converted into NT$ because the full amount may be kept in a designated foreign currency deposit account, but the underlying CBC declaration must still be completed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBC Foreign Exchange Declaration and Registration Regulations and the Foreign Trade Act, export proceeds must be collected and repatriated into Taiwan within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; the exporter may retain the full proceeds in a designated Foreign Currency Deposit Account at a licensed FX bank. If the proceeds are not received within the 180-day window, the exporter must apply for an extension with documentary proof before the expiry; otherwise the bank will report non-compliance to the CBC. For bonded warehouse re-exports, the same timeline applies upon final customs exit; domestic bonded transfers do not create an export FX settlement event.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), Foreign Exchange Department

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low - not legally viable for direct fund holding or clearing. Under the Taiwan Banking Act and the Electronic Payment Institution Act, an offshore fintech EMI that is not registered as a foreign bank branch or licensed electronic payment institution with the Financial Supervisory Commission and the CBC may not open a New Taiwan dollar corporate account, may not access local clearing systems such as FISC/CHIPS, and may not provide trade settlement services for bonded wine/spirits transactions. Under the Taiwan Banking Act and the Electronic Payment Institution Act, an offshore fintech EMI that is not registered as a foreign bank branch or licensed electronic payment institution with the Financial Supervisory Commission and the CBC may not open a New Taiwan dollar corporate account, may not access local clearing systems such as FISC/CHIPS, and may not provide trade settlement services for bonded wine/spirits transactions. An offshore EMI can only function as a white-label technology interface in front of a licensed Taiwan Tier-1 bank, which still requires full onshore KYC, AML, and FX reporting obligations. Accordingly, no fintech EMI is recommended in the account structure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$50 million per annum for intercompany purchase or sale transactions; NT$30 million per annum for other related-party transactions (services, royalties, interest). A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required if the multinational group's annual consolidated revenue exceeds NT$30 billion.

Economic substance scope: Under the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Act Article 12-1, the Taiwan tax authority may disregard a legal entity that lacks economic substance. For the bonded warehouse WFOE, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must be performed in Taiwan: negotiating purchase contracts, holding inventory contracts, maintaining bonded warehouse records, issuing invoices in Taiwan, and managing customs risk. If the authority finds the Taiwan entity is a shell or conduit and that the Chinese parent makes all substantive decisions, the authority may reattribute the entire income to the Chinese parent, rendering the Taiwan filing ineffective. DTA relief is denied in the absence of commercial substance; maintenance of an office, local staff, and a separate board/minutes is mandatory. A substance file should be updated annually and include employment records, lease agreements, warehouse insurance, and a risk-management decision log.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration of Bonded Wine Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act Article 110, the underpaid tax is assessed, and a penalty of up to 3 times the amount of tax evaded is imposed. The Tax Evasion Punishment Act Article 5 provides imprisonment of two to five years and/or a fine of NT$1 million to NT$50 million. Additionally, the Tobacco and Alcohol Administration Act may revoke the bonded warehouse license, and custom duty and alcohol tax become immediately payable with an additional penalty of up to 10 times the unpaid duties.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: managing directors, CFOs, and signatory officers are personally liable for evaded taxes, interest, and penalties. The National Taxation Bureau may freeze personal bank accounts and assets, and a travel ban (blacklisting) is imposed under the Border Control Act. Criminal conviction leads to imprisonment of up to 5 years, disqualification from directorship for 5 years, and civil asset restitution. In severe cases, the court orders criminal forfeiture of personal real estate and luxury goods acquired with the proceeds of tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Act Articles 43-1 and 43-2 and the Transfer Pricing Examination Rules, the tax authority may reassess income to the arm's-length amount; the underpaid tax is subjected to a penalty of up to 2 times the deficiency. Thin-capitalization rules disallow interest deductions on debt exceeding the 3:1 ratio. Intentional failure to file a Local File or to present documentation within the statutory request period results in a 2x penalty on the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the transfer pricing certification, approved inflated invoices, or structured the thin capitalization are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency and penalties. The prosecutor may seek an anti-dissipation order freezing personal assets and a travel ban preventing overseas departure. Imprisonment of two to five years under the Tax Evasion Punishment Act can be imposed in tandem with breach of fiduciary duty claims under the Company Act. Directors are also personally liable to the company for damages caused by the manipulation.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering / False Bonded Warehouse Records

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Control Act (AML Act) Article 15, trade-based money laundering is punishable by imprisonment for up to 7 years and a fine of up to NT$50 million. The Tobacco and Alcohol Administration Act imposes administrative fines of up to NT$30 million, cancellation of the bonded license, confiscation of duty-unpaid goods, and a 10x penalty on unpaid alcohol tax and customs duty. Customs Anti-Smuggling Act violations carry criminal sentences of 5 to 10 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for directors who knowingly authorized or participated in false bonded warehouse declarations or money laundering. Directors are personally liable for all unpaid taxes, customs duty, and administrative fines up to NT$100 million. Under the AML Act, all personal real estate and bank accounts are subject to freezing and forfeiture; a travel ban is mandatory pending investigation. Criminal conviction results in imprisonment of 5 to 7 years, mandatory disgorgement of illicit gains, and permanent blacklisting from management positions in any Taiwan financial institution or bonded enterprise.

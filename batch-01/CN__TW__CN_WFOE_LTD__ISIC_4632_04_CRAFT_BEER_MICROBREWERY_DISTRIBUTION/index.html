Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Taiwan Company Act does not prescribe a minimum paid-up capital for a limited liability company. The founding foreign shareholder may set any lawful amount, but the subscribed capital must be actually remitted into a Taiwanese bank account before registration, evidenced by an inward remittance certificate and a certified public accountant capital verification report. No blocked escrow account is required; however, the capital must remain traceable and cannot be withdrawn until the MOEA registration certificate is issued.

Funding & unlock path: The cold-chain warehouse must obtain a Fire Safety and Civil Defense Permit from the local fire bureau, certifying proper fire-extinguishing systems, emergency exits, fire compartmentation, and a valid fire insurance policy. A structural load inspection for pallet racking and cold-store panels is required before goods are stored. Occupational safety approval from the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA Taiwan) is required for forklift operations, confined-space entry procedures in chillers, and ammonia refrigerant leak sensor systems, together with documented employee safety training.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the WFOE is a resident profit-seeking enterprise taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Act; no pass-through election; territorial-source rules apply to its Taiwan-source income.

Available election forms

None. Taiwan does not provide check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity elections; the WFOE's classification as a taxable limited liability company is mandatory and non-elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold exempts a corporate WFOE from business tax registration; registration is triggered immediately upon incorporation and commencement of business in Taiwan. The simplified small-scale business regime uses an average monthly sales benchmark of NT$80,000, but that regime is not available to a WFOE engaged in craft beer distribution and export trading.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 7 of the Value-Added and Non-Value-Added Business Tax Act, goods exported from Taiwan customs territory qualify for 0% VAT. Eligibility requires physical export evidenced by a customs export declaration, transfer of ownership to the overseas buyer, transport documentation showing movement outside Taiwan, and compliance with alcoholic beverage export licensing rules. Qualifying offshore digital services, if any, must be supplied for use outside Taiwan.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry / outward cargo declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Master Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Export Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or foreign exchange settlement receipt evidencing export proceeds repatriation
  • Alcoholic beverage export permit / liquor license documentation
  • Zero-rated Uniform Invoice and E-Invoice transmission record

The WFOE must issue statutory Uniform Invoices and transmit e-invoice records to the Ministry of Finance E-Invoice Integration Service Platform using the E-Invoice Turnkey direct integration service; transmission must be completed within 48 hours of invoice issuance. The platform applies statutory invoice-number controls, two-dimensional barcode random anti-fraud check codes, and post-submission audit monitoring rather than real-time pre-clearance. Encrypted invoice master-file retention is mandatory.

Export proceeds must be repatriated and substantiated within the 180-day foreign exchange deadline. Failure to complete the statutory documentation bundle causes the export zero-rating to be clawed back as domestic taxable turnover, with retroactive VAT assessment at 5%, interest, and late-payment penalties. Fraudulent or unverified zero-rating claims may trigger administrative tax-evasion penalties and criminal tax-fraud referral; refunds may also be frozen until the documentation gap and any resulting assessment are fully resolved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations. Current-account transactions, including proceeds from the export and import of craft beer and related trade settlements, are broadly convertible provided the underlying trade is genuine and documented under SAFE's trade authenticity and verification framework. Capital-account transactions are significantly more constrained. A Taiwan-owned WFOE must complete SAFE foreign-direct-investment registration before receiving any shareholder equity from the Taiwan parent; the capital contribution must be remitted into a designated foreign-currency capital account and can be settled into RMB through a capital settlement account only for approved operating purposes that fall within the registered business scope. Profit remittances to the Taiwan shareholder are permitted only after completion of the annual statutory audit, final settlement of enterprise income tax, and payment of any applicable withholding tax; the remitting bank must verify accumulated attributable earnings, source-of-wealth documentation, and the parent company's tax status. In practice, the documentation burden is heavy: original contracts, customs declarations, commercial invoices, shipping evidence if imported, and the SAFE registration code must all be presented before same-day settlement. Thus, convertibility is high for current account flows but conditional and registration-bound for capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE policy, export proceeds received in foreign currency must be repatriated to a domestic current-account foreign-exchange settlement account within 90 days from the export customs declaration date, unless an extended time limit is approved for open-account or long-term trade contracts. No mandatory conversion ratio applies; the WFOE may retain 100% of verified export receipts in foreign currency, or convert them to RMB at the bank's prevailing onshore rate. The bank must verify each receipt against customs declarations, commercial invoices, bills of lading, and collection vouchers in the SAFE monitoring system before crediting and settlement.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through its operational arm, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms do not hold a PBOC payment-institution license and therefore cannot lawfully open onshore RMB operating accounts, clear domestic collections, or act as the SAFE-registered bank for a China WFOE. They may be used by the Taiwan parent for non-mainland treasury or as a non-operative international information corridor, but they are legally barred from servicing the WFOE's local entity account, capital settlement, or trade-verification requirements. The WFOE must rely on a locally licensed commercial bank for primary clearing; licensed cross-border payment institutions are acceptable only if they possess a PBOC payment business permit and the transaction is genuine trade-backed.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Before any remittance, the WFOE must complete SAFE foreign-direct-investment registration and obtain the FDI registration code. The Taiwan parent then sends the equity contribution by SWIFT MT103 into the WFOE's designated foreign-currency capital account at a Tier-1 Chinese bank. The remittance instruction must carry the approved SAFE purpose code for direct-investment equity contribution and reference the SAFE registration code. The receiving bank verifies the remitter against the approved shareholder list and the registered amount, then settles the funds into RMB through the capital settlement account. A certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report, and the funds can only be disbursed for uses consistent with the WFOE's approved business scope.For payments to overseas breweries, cold-chain logistics providers, and distributors, the WFOE may use SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for genuine trade or service payments, each supported by a matching contract, invoice, and customs record where required; commercial letters of credit (sight or usance) for high-value keg purchases or significant raw-material imports to mitigate counterparty non-performance; and documentary collections (documents against payment / documents against acceptance) for lower-value or repeat shipments. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and final tax payment are generally due by the last day of the fifth month following the close of the fiscal year. A calendar-year taxpayer normally files and pays by 31 May of the following year. A taxpayer ceasing operations, liquidating, or undergoing a qualifying organizational change may have an earlier special filing deadline.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally filed and paid during 1 September through 30 September of the current year for taxpayers using a calendar fiscal year, based on the preceding year's tax or the statutory provisional calculation method. Certain newly established, qualifying, or exempt enterprises may be outside the provisional filing requirement.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally filed and paid by the 15th day of the month following the end of each two-month VAT period.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Tax withheld from payments to non-residents is generally paid to the tax authority within ten days after the withholding date. Annual withholding statements and recipient certificates are generally issued and filed by the statutory January deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For Taiwan Local File purposes, controlled-transaction documentation is generally required unless an applicable exemption applies, including the de minimis exemption for total annual controlled transactions below NTD 200 million, subject to transaction type and other conditions. A Master File is generally required where the taxpayer's annual net operating revenue and non-operating income reaches NTD 3 billion or more, or where the total annual cross-border controlled transactions reach NTD 1 billion or more, subject to statutory exceptions. Country-by-Country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational enterprise groups meeting the NTD 27 billion consolidated revenue threshold or an applicable equivalent threshold.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not operate a single UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime. Economic substance remains relevant through the substance-over-form principle, general anti-avoidance provisions, controlled foreign company rules, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty anti-abuse standards, and business-tax anti-evasion rules. A Taiwan WFOE should maintain local management, personnel, premises, inventory controls, banking, decision records, risk assumption, and operational evidence proportionate to its cold-chain beer distribution activities. A WFOE that functions only as a conduit for foreign sales or payments faces heightened recharacterization and denial-of-deduction risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, interest, administrative fines, assessment based on available evidence, and criminal tax-evasion liability where statutory intent and thresholds are met. Criminal consequences may include imprisonment and criminal fines under Taiwan tax laws.

Director personal liability: Directors, responsible persons, accountants, and persons directing the conduct may face criminal prosecution, civil restitution, joint or supplementary liability where legally applicable, disqualification consequences, and enforcement against personal assets. Travel restrictions are not automatic and require a statutory or judicial basis; imprisonment may follow conviction.
Fraudulent Invoices and Input-Tax Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of input-tax credits, additional business tax, fines, surcharge and interest, cancellation or restriction of invoice privileges, and criminal prosecution for serious or intentional fraud.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons and collaborators may incur criminal liability and civil liability for tax losses. Corporate limited liability does not protect persons who personally participated in falsification or fraudulent tax claims.
Unlicensed Alcohol Import, Manufacture, Storage, or Distribution

Statutory sanction: Confiscation, destruction or withdrawal of products, administrative fines, suspension or revocation of licenses, tax assessment, and criminal penalties where the conduct violates alcohol, food-safety, customs, or public-health laws.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may be fined or prosecuted where they authorized or knowingly tolerated unlawful operations. Corporate dissolution or license loss can create direct civil exposure for directors who breached statutory duties.
Customs, Excise, Alcohol Tax, and Import Declaration Violations

Statutory sanction: Payment of deficient customs duty, commodity tax, alcohol tax, business tax, penalties, confiscation, import restrictions, and potential criminal customs liability for smuggling or deliberate false declaration.

Director personal liability: Directors and customs declarants may be personally liable where they instructed, approved, or knowingly benefited from false declarations. Serious cases may result in prosecution and asset or travel-control measures under applicable law.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustment to arm's-length income or deductions, additional corporate income tax, withholding tax, interest, penalties, denial of treaty relief, and documentation penalties or audit exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for knowingly approving transactions that prejudice the company or creditors. Criminal liability generally requires additional fraud, false-book, tax-evasion, or fiduciary-misconduct elements.
Improper Profit Repatriation and Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the Taiwan payer, interest, fines, denial of reduced rates, reassessment of the payment, and possible penalties for false filings or concealed remittances.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers may be personally exposed for deliberate failure to withhold or remit and may face criminal consequences where concealment or fraud is established.
Illegal Capital Flight, Sham Shareholder Loans, or Foreign-Exchange Evasion

Statutory sanction: Foreign-exchange, company-law, tax, AML, and criminal sanctions may apply, including fines, confiscation, tax reassessment, repayment orders, and imprisonment for serious unlawful remittance or fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, and payment approvers may face personal civil and criminal liability, asset freezing, confiscation, and court-ordered travel restrictions. Limited liability does not protect participants in sham transactions.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial-Ownership Failures

Statutory sanction: Enhanced due-diligence measures, transaction reporting, account restrictions, administrative fines, confiscation, and criminal money-laundering liability where the statutory elements are satisfied.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and employees may be prosecuted for knowing participation, facilitation, or deliberate failure to act. Criminal penalties can include imprisonment and fines; travel restrictions may be imposed by competent authorities or courts.
Corporate Books, Accounting, and Record-Retention Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, rejection of deductions, estimated assessment, loss of tax benefits, adverse audit inference, director sanctions, and criminal liability for intentional falsification or destruction of records.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may be liable for statutory accounting breaches, creditor losses, tax underpayments, and falsified records. Imprisonment requires the applicable criminal elements and a conviction.
Unlawful Dividend or Capital Distribution

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, director liability for resulting losses, tax reassessment, creditor claims, and potential company-law or criminal sanctions in fraudulent or insolvency-related circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore funds, compensate the company or creditors, and face criminal liability for intentional misconduct, insolvency abuse, or fraudulent distributions.

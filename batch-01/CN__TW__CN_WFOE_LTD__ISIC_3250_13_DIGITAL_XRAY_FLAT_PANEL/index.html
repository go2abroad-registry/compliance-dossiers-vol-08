Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Taiwan Ministry of Finance (MOF), administered operationally by the competent National Taxation Bureau; electronic tax services are provided through the MOF eTax Portal and the Taiwan e-Invoice Platform. Company establishment and foreign-investment approvals are generally handled by the Ministry of Economic Affairs before local tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Taiwan does not provide a US-style check-the-box election for ordinary Taiwan companies. A Taiwan limited company is generally a separate corporate income-tax taxpayer. A foreign company branch is taxed under branch rules, while a Taiwan subsidiary is taxed as a resident company. Ordinary Taiwan companies cannot elect general pass-through treatment solely by filing a tax form. Special regimes may apply to approved investment, science-park, export-processing-zone, or other designated enterprises, but eligibility depends on statutory location, activity, approval, and substance conditions. Hybrid mismatch elections are not a general Taiwan entity-classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Ordinary businesses carrying on taxable business in Taiwan generally must register before commencing business; there is no general turnover threshold that permits an ordinary incorporated company to remain unregistered. For small-scale businesses eligible for the small-business regime, the commonly applied monthly sales thresholds are approximately NT$80,000 for goods and NT$40,000 for services, subject to the applicable Business Tax Act rules and administrative classifications. These thresholds are not a safe harbor for an ordinary company with a fixed business establishment or a manufacturing operation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Taiwan that satisfy customs export requirements are generally eligible for 0% business tax, subject to proper customs declaration and documentary substantiation. Zero rating may also apply to qualifying services supplied for use outside Taiwan, services supplied to foreign enterprises that meet statutory offshore-use conditions, and other specifically listed transactions. For digital X-ray flat-panel detectors and related hardware, the principal basis is normally an actual export evidenced by customs records. The exporter must retain sales contracts, purchase orders, commercial invoices, packing lists, export declarations, bills of lading or air waybills, delivery evidence, payment records, and accounting records linking the shipment to the zero-rated invoice. Export-related input business tax may generally be claimed as a credit or refunded subject to filing, documentary support, the statutory refund process, and any applicable restrictions. A Taiwan sale to a local intermediary is not automatically zero-rated merely because the goods are ultimately resold overseas; the statutory export transaction and documentary chain must support the claim.

  • Taiwan Customs export declaration or equivalent electronic customs record showing the exporter, consignee, goods description, quantity, value, and export clearance
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract or purchase order
  • Packing list and product-level shipment records, including serial numbers or batch records where applicable
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or other carrier evidence of export and delivery
  • Customs release or export confirmation and freight-forwarder records
  • Electronic invoice or zero-rated business-tax sales record issued in accordance with Taiwan rules
  • Bank remittance evidence, foreign-exchange settlement evidence, or other payment evidence where relevant to the transaction and requested for audit support
  • For offshore services: evidence of the foreign customer, service agreement, service location or offshore use, deliverables, and payment trail
  • For regulated medical-device products: applicable Taiwan export permits, product registration or licensing records, and any destination-country export-control documentation where required

Taiwan electronic invoices must comply with MOF invoice-format, numbering, authentication, issuance, retention, and transmission requirements. Businesses subject to electronic-invoice obligations generally issue electronic invoices through the MOF e-Invoice Platform, an approved value-added center, or compliant integrated software, and transmit invoice data within the prescribed statutory or administrative time limits. Taiwan does not generally operate a universal Latin American-style pre-issuance real-time tax clearance system for every B2B invoice. Electronic invoices require valid seller and buyer information, invoice number and date, taxable amount, tax amount, tax category, and relevant zero-rate or exemption indicators. Manufacturing entities should maintain ERP-to-e-invoice controls, duplicate-invoice prevention, authorized user credentials, electronic records, and audit-ready reconciliation between invoices, VAT returns, customs records, and general-ledger accounts.

Taiwan does not generally impose a universal 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline as a condition for every export zero-rating claim. Any specific payment, foreign-exchange, customs, or capital-control requirement must be tested against the transaction type and applicable Central Bank and foreign-exchange rules. If zero rating is unsupported, the National Taxation Bureau may deny the 0% treatment, assess the underpaid business tax, impose statutory interest, reverse or recover improperly refunded input tax, and assess administrative penalties. False invoices, fabricated export records, sham transactions, concealment of domestic sales, or other intentional tax evasion may trigger substantially higher fines and criminal liability under the Business Tax Act and related laws. Customs misdeclarations may produce separate penalties, seizure or forfeiture consequences, and post-clearance duty or tax assessments. The taxpayer should preserve the complete export file and reconcile customs declarations, invoices, VAT returns, bank receipts, inventory movements, and shipping data to mitigate retrospective clawback risk.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi is generally convertible for bona fide current-account transactions, including trade in goods and services, subject to bank documentary review, anti-money-laundering controls, sanctions screening, tax and customs consistency, and SAFE reporting. Capital-account transactions are regulated rather than fully freely convertible. Foreign-invested enterprises may receive registered foreign capital, shareholder loans, and other permitted capital-account funds through designated foreign-exchange accounts, but the transaction must be registered or documented where required, have a lawful purpose, and comply with applicable debt, outbound investment, and related-party financing limits. Capital conversion into renminbi is subject to the applicable SAFE settlement regime and bank verification; funds must be used within the approved or documented business scope and may not be diverted to prohibited investments, securities speculation, real-estate speculation, or unapproved lending. Profit remittance is generally permitted after completion of statutory accounts, audit where applicable, tax filing and tax clearance or tax-compliance verification, board or shareholder approval, and bank review of distributable profits, withholding tax, supporting financial statements, and evidence that prior-year losses have been covered. Cross-border dividend, interest, royalty, service-fee, and principal payments require transaction authenticity, beneficiary-owner verification, applicable withholding-tax treatment, and consistency with transfer-pricing and customs positions. Taiwan-related payments additionally require separate cross-strait legal, banking, sanctions, and counterparty analysis; a mainland WFOE cannot assume unrestricted banking access to Taiwan counterparties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mainland China does not impose a universal statutory requirement that all export proceeds be converted into renminbi or surrendered to a bank at a fixed mandatory ratio. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign currency or converted on a voluntary basis, subject to current-account bank processing, SAFE statistical reporting, customs and tax-data consistency, and the bank's transaction review. There is no generally applicable single statutory conversion deadline or blanket mandatory conversion percentage for ordinary export receipts. The enterprise should ensure timely collection under the underlying sale contract, maintain export declarations, commercial invoices, transport documents, contracts, payment records, and reconciliation of customs, tax-refund, and banking data. Banks may apply enhanced review, collection restrictions, or reporting escalation for overdue, unmatched, unusual, or high-risk proceeds. Any special pilot program, trade-finance arrangement, foreign-exchange administration classification, local SAFE requirement, or bank-specific settlement condition must be checked before execution. Export proceeds should not be routed through unrelated third-party accounts or netted against imports, intercompany charges, or financing without a documented and legally supportable basis.

Central bank supervision: Primary mainland China authorities: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), administered under the People's Bank of China framework, together with the People's Bank of China (PBOC). The Banking and Insurance regulatory functions are principally supervised by the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA). Applicability caveat: the payload identifies Taiwan as the jurisdiction identifier but describes a China WFOE. A mainland China WFOE is governed by mainland China FX rules; a Taiwan-incorporated entity is governed separately by the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan), the Financial Supervisory Commission, and Taiwan foreign-exchange regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and disbursement support, but Low as a substitute for a mainland China commercial-bank account. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment institution cannot independently bypass mainland FX controls, SAFE registration, banking due diligence, data requirements, capital-account restrictions, or documentary requirements. Offshore EMIs may be used only where their licensing permits the relevant service, the Chinese bank accepts the payment chain, the funds are routed through permitted correspondent or cross-border payment channels, and the transaction purpose, payer, payee, goods, customs records, and beneficial ownership are transparent. Offshore wallet balances should not be used as an unlicensed deposit-taking, shadow-settlement, third-party collection, or informal foreign-exchange channel. Taiwan-related payment services require separate licensing and cross-strait review.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% of taxable income for a Taiwan profit-seeking enterprise.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 21% on dividends paid by a Taiwan company to a nonresident shareholder, subject to any applicable and effective tax treaty or other legally available relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taiwan does not have one universal threshold applicable to every Local File obligation. Local File preparation may be triggered by annual net operating revenue plus non-operating income of at least TWD 300 million or controlled transactions of at least TWD 200 million, subject to detailed statutory exclusions, transaction-category thresholds, and current-year rules. A Master File generally applies where the multinational group's consolidated revenue meets the prescribed threshold, commonly TWD 30 billion, together with applicable conditions. Verify current regulations and thresholds before filing.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not generally apply the UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Taiwan nevertheless applies transfer-pricing, substance-over-form, controlled-foreign-company, place-of-effective-management, anti-avoidance, and beneficial-ownership rules where relevant. A nominal entity lacking personnel, functions, assets, or decision-making may face recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, or other tax challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, statutory surcharges, interest, and potential criminal prosecution under applicable Taiwan tax and criminal laws. Criminal penalties and fines depend on the conduct, amount, intent, and applicable statute.

Director personal liability: Directors or responsible persons may face personal criminal liability where they participate in, direct, or knowingly facilitate the offense. Corporate status does not immunize individual perpetrators. Civil recovery, restraint measures, or travel restrictions require a specific statutory basis and applicable proceedings; they are not automatic.
Transfer-pricing noncompliance

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments, additional tax, interest, and applicable penalties for underreporting or documentation failures. The tax authority may determine arm's-length income using available information where records are inadequate.

Director personal liability: Directors may be exposed to penalties or criminal liability only where the relevant statutory elements and personal conduct are established; ordinary transfer-pricing adjustments do not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Improper withholding or dividend repatriation

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for unpaid withholding tax, interest, and statutory penalties; treaty relief may be denied where eligibility or documentation is deficient.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may incur statutory liability for knowing or culpable withholding violations. Personal criminal exposure depends on the facts and applicable law.
False substance or beneficial-ownership claims

Statutory sanction: Denial or recovery of treaty relief, withholding-tax reassessment, interest, penalties, and potential anti-avoidance or criminal proceedings if fraud is established.

Director personal liability: Personal liability may arise from participation in fraudulent declarations or deliberate evasion; no automatic veil piercing applies solely because an entity has limited substance.
Unlicensed regulated manufacturing

Statutory sanction: Regulatory orders, product restrictions, administrative fines, seizure or recall, and potential criminal sanctions may apply under Taiwan medical-device and related laws. Requirements depend on product classification and activities.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible persons may face individual sanctions where they personally authorize or participate in violations or where a statute imposes responsible-person liability.
Money laundering, sanctions, and suspicious payment activity

Statutory sanction: Account restrictions, transaction reporting, asset seizure or confiscation, administrative penalties, and criminal prosecution may apply under Taiwan law, depending on the facts.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly participate in or facilitate money laundering may face personal criminal liability. Travel restrictions or detention require lawful authority and the applicable procedural basis.

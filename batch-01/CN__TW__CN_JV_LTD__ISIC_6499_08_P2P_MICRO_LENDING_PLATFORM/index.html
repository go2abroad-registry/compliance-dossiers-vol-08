Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating under Business Tax Act Article 7. Goods physically exported from Taiwan to a foreign territory, and services supplied to foreign purchasers and beneficially consumed outside Taiwan, are charged at 0% instead of 5%. The exporter must be the person named in the export customs declaration; for digital P2P platform services, the contract, delivery evidence, and payment flow must demonstrate that the services are performed for and used outside Taiwan. Pure lending spread or exempt financial income is not eligible for zero-rating, but separately structured facilitation or technology service fees supplied to foreign borrowers/lenders may qualify. Zero-rated sales are declared in the periodic Business Tax return, and input tax related to those exports may be refunded through the export tax refund procedure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Declaration for exported goods) showing the JV as the exporter
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, or electronic equivalent provided by the carrier
  • Commercial Invoice, packing list and underlying P2P platform loan-service agreement
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence: Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Proceeds Settlement Certificate issued by a Taiwan authorized bank
  • Digital service consumption documentation evidencing use or enjoyment outside Taiwan where zero-rating is claimed for cross-border platform services

Taiwan operates the statutory Uniform Invoice (GUI) system. Electronic Uniform Invoices (eGUI) are transmitted to the MOF Electronic Uniform Invoice Platform through Turnkey API or value-added network channels; e-commerce and platform businesses must issue cloud e-invoices with QR code and validation-code for consumer verification. There is no pre-issuance fiscal clearance or government stamping requirement, but invoice serial numbers are registered before use and the National Taxation Bureau reconciles issued invoices against filed Business Tax returns in bi-monthly assessment cycles.

The National Taxation Bureau may review the export documentation bundle, customs outflows, foreign exchange receipts, and e-invoice data. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the export is reclassified as a domestic taxable supply, the VAT is assessed with default interest, any refunded input VAT is clawed back, and the taxpayer is exposed to penalties under Business Tax Act Articles 51 and 52 and the Tax Evasion and Penalty Act, including fines and criminal liability for forged or fraudulent export documentation. The statute of limitation for reassessment is generally seven years for under-reported or fraudulent tax positions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Taiwan maintains a managed-float foreign exchange regime administered by the CBC under the Statute for Regulation of Foreign Exchange. Current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, dividends, royalties and loan interest, are freely convertible and may be settled through any designated FX bank without prior CBC approval. Capital account transactions are generally liberalized but remain subject to statutory filing or approval when they involve foreign direct investment into Taiwan, outward investment, offshore loans or derivative transactions. Because this entity is a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture performing P2P micro-lending, the Financial Supervisory Commission treats the activity as a high-risk non-bank financial business requiring a special permit, regulatory sandbox authorization or licensed cooperation structure. Profit remittance to the PRC shareholder will be allowed only after annual audited financial statements, tax clearance, Investment Commission filing and CBC exchange-reporting have been completed. There is no requirement to surrender export foreign currency receipts into New Taiwan dollars; however, all cross-border receipts must be repatriated through a domestic authorized FX bank and reported. The CBC actively intervenes to smooth excessive NTD volatility, so treasury should maintain currency concentration limits, dynamic hedging and disciplined liquidity buffers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods and services exporters must collect the full invoice amount within the CBC-prescribed period, generally within four months from shipment date for goods, and must either sell the foreign exchange to a designated FX bank or credit it to the exporter's foreign currency deposit account at such a bank. The mandatory conversion ratio to New Taiwan dollars is 0%: no fixed portion must be converted, but the full foreign-exchange receipt must be repatriated, declared and settled through a CBC-authorised bank. Late repatriation, unapproved parking of proceeds offshore, or submission of inaccurate declarations can trigger a CBC audit, administrative penalties, suspension of FX dealer eligibility and loss of conversion privileges.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) [CBC]

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Eligibility and Legally Restricted. A Taiwan-incorporated P2P lending joint venture cannot use an unlicensed foreign EMI such as Revolut, Wise, Airwallex or other offshore digital payment platforms as its primary corporate treasury or settlement bank because the FSC and CBC require lending and payment activities to clear through domestically authorized financial institutions. Such EMIs may only serve as auxiliary collection or wallet-payout conduits if they hold a valid Taiwan electronic payment institution license or form a partnership with a licensed Taiwan bank that custodies NTD funds. Using an offshore EMI for client trust money would fail capital verification, expose the JV to illegal deposit-taking allegations, and create severe AML chain-density gaps. Viability is therefore limited to supplementary supplier-payment corridors after Taiwan bank accounts are established.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions must first complete the PRC outbound direct investment registration with NDRC, MOFCOM and SAFE. The Chinese shareholder should remit through a PRC designated bank using SWIFT MT103 with remittance purpose code for overseas direct investment, naming the JV and contract number in the remittance narrative. On the Taiwan side, funds must be received into the JV foreign currency deposit account at a CBC-authorised FX bank. The receiving bank issues an inward remittance certificate showing the FX transaction category, and the JV then files the capital injection with the Investment Commission. The Taiwan bank converts USD, CNY or other received currency into NTD at prevailing spot rates after completing the CBC exchange declaration. Capital verification is performed by a Taiwan CPA who maps bank-in certificates, SWIFT advices and exchange contracts to the registered capital schedule; any mismatch in amount, remitter, purpose code or timing freezes capital verification until a rectification filing is approved.Outbound treasury flows are divided into three clearing rails. Domestic micro-loan disbursements clear through the Taiwan interbank NTD clearing system, ACH direct debit and local wallet payout rails operated by FSC-licensed electronic payment institutions with custodial bank accounts. Cross-border trade and service payments for software, cloud platforms, intra-group technology fees or data services use SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers together with the CBC-assigned purpose code selected by the Taiwan bank. For future import-export operations, Commercial Letters of Credit and Documentary Collections are executed through the appointed Taiwan commercial banks. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Eligibility and Legally Restricted. A Taiwan-incorporated P2P lending joint venture cannot use an unlicensed foreign EMI such as Revolut, Wise, Airwallex or other offshore digital payment platforms as its primary corporate treasury or settlement bank because the FSC and CBC require lending and payment activities to clear through domestically authorized financial institutions. Such EMIs may only serve as auxiliary collection or wallet-payout conduits if they hold a valid Taiwan electronic payment institution license or form a partnership with a licensed Taiwan bank that custodies NTD funds. Using an offshore EMI for client trust money would fail capital verification, expose the JV to illegal deposit-taking allegations, and create severe AML chain-density gaps. Viability is therefore limited to supplementary supplier-payment corridors after Taiwan bank accounts are established.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: NT$20 million in aggregate annual related-party controlled transactions triggers mandatory Local File, Master File, and related-party transaction declaration preparation; country-by-country reporting is separately required for a multinational group with consolidated revenue above the NT$54 billion threshold.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not have an OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the tax administration applies substance-over-form and anti-avoidance rules requiring the P2P micro-lending platform to prove that core income-generating activities, including underwriting algorithm control, credit-risk decision making, AML/KYC monitoring, funds management, and strategic board decisions, are actually performed by personnel and systems located in Taiwan; failure to do so permits profit reallocation to the Taiwanese entity and penalty assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through underdeclared P2P loan facilitation income

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Article 110 imposes additional tax of not less than two and not more than three times the underpaid tax, plus interest; Income Tax Act Article 41 criminalises fraudulent tax evasion and carries prison exposure, fines, and confiscation of shifted profits on top of civil tax collection.

Director personal liability: Company Act Article 23 renders directors jointly and severally liable for damages caused to the company and third parties; courts may pierce the corporate veil by freezing personal bank accounts and ordering restitution of diverted fee income; travel bans are imposed on directors with delinquent taxes or penalties above the statutory threshold, and criminal conviction can result in imprisonment.
Shell-company and treaty-shopping abuse in royalty and dividend routing

Statutory sanction: Transfer Pricing Examination Rules and Income Tax Act Article 43-1 authorise recharacterisation of the transactions based on economic substance, with additional tax of up to three times the underpaid amount; submission of materially false transfer pricing documents attracts separate professional and corporate penalties under tax administration rules.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for disgorgement of profits shifted through the sham entity, the corporate veil may be disregarded where the shell was operated as an instrument of fraud, passports and exits are restricted, and criminal anti-tax-evasion charges may be pursued personally against the signers of the false transfer pricing statements.
Unlicensed banking, AML/KYC failure, and illegal micro-lending operations

Statutory sanction: Banking Act Article 29 and Article 125 impose imprisonment of not less than three years and up to ten years, fines ranging from NT$50 million to NT$500 million, or a fine of twenty times unlawful gains; Money Laundering Control Act sanctions separately apply to failure to file STRs and to maintain effective AML controls.

Director personal liability: Directors, supervisors, and approving officers are personally liable for depositor and investor losses, assets may be protected for creditor restitution through veil piercing, the company may be ordered dissolved, and each director involved in approving the illegal lending operation faces criminal imprisonment terms of three to ten years plus disqualification from holding corporate office.

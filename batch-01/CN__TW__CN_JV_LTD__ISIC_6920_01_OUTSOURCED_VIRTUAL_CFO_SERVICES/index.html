Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA), Department of Commercial Affairs; for a company located in Taipei City, the Taipei City Government Department of Economic Development; all initial filings are made through the MOEA One-Stop Company Registration Portal.

Governing statute

Company Act of Taiwan (R.O.C.), Articles 1 through 7, 10, 128 through 135, 172 through 177, 203 through 207, and 387; Foreign Investment Act; Regulations Governing Investment in Taiwan by Mainland Area Residents or Legal Entities; Bookkeepers Act; Certified Public Accountant Act; Business Registration Act.

Formation / constitutional filings

Company Registration Application for a Company Limited by Shares under Article 387 of the Company Act, accompanied by the Articles of Incorporation, promoter minutes, and capital verification attachments.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 61 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an ordinary company limited by shares under the current Company Act. The Board must nonetheless adopt a capital sufficiency statement and deposit the full subscribed capital into a preparatory bank account in Taiwan before registration. For a mainland-area investor, the MOEA Investment Commission may impose a minimum capital amount and a restricted remittance schedule. The subscribed funds are not held in permanent escrow after registration, but may not be withdrawn until the Certificate of Incorporation is issued and, where required, the certified public accountant capital verification report is filed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for the office premises issued by the Taipei City Fire Department under the Fire Services Act; building occupancy certificate compliance under the Building Act; occupational safety and health inspection under the Occupational Safety and Health Act administered by the Ministry of Labor, covering electrical safety, emergency exit access, and ergonomic workstations. No structural load inspection or chemical hazard clearance is required for an office-only virtual CFO service operation.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an ordinary company limited by shares under the current Company Act. The Board must nonetheless adopt a capital sufficiency statement and deposit the full subscribed capital into a preparatory bank account in Taiwan before registration. For a mainland-area investor, the MOEA Investment Commission may impose a minimum capital amount and a restricted remittance schedule. The subscribed funds are not held in permanent escrow after registration, but may not be withdrawn until the Certificate of Incorporation is issued and, where required, the certified public accountant capital verification report is filed.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance (Taiwan) - Taxation Administration; regional National Taxation Bureaus; online portal: MOF eTax Portal (etax.nat.gov.tw) for registration and filing.

E-government filing portal

1. For the onshore JV: apply to the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) for company incorporation and name/business scope approval (one-stop online registration); 2. Upon incorporation, the MOEA Data Center notifies the National Taxation Bureau automatically; the JV must then apply for tax registration within 15 days using the 'Profit-Seeking Enterprise Tax Registration' form at its local National Taxation Bureau, submitting the incorporation certificate, articles of incorporation, identification documents of the directors, and a Taiwan resident agent/manager. 3. The National Taxation Bureau issues an eight-digit Unified Business Identification Number (UBIN), which is the TIN for income tax, Business Tax (VAT), Customs, and Uniform Invoice purposes. 4. For a foreign non-resident enterprise that has no Taiwan fixed place of business but engages in Taiwan-sourced cross-border electronic services, the foreign enterprise must register through the simplified 'Cross-Border Electronic Services Tax Registration' procedure at the National Taxation Bureau; it receives a temporary UBIN and files Business Tax under the cross-border e-commerce regime. 5. All bank accounts, Customs exporter registrations, and Uniform Invoice printing/e-invoice applications require the UBIN.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a limited liability profit-seeking enterprise, taxed separately on its taxable income; no pass-through treatment available). — 1. For the onshore JV: apply to the Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) for company incorporation and name/business scope approval (one-stop online registration); 2. Upon incorporation, the MOEA Data Center notifies the National Taxation Bureau automatically; the JV must then apply for tax registration within 15 days using the 'Profit-Seeking Enterprise Tax Registration' form at its local National Taxation Bureau, submitting the incorporation certificate, articles of incorporation, identification documents of the directors, and a Taiwan resident agent/manager. 3. The National Taxation Bureau issues an eight-digit Unified Business Identification Number (UBIN), which is the TIN for income tax, Business Tax (VAT), Customs, and Uniform Invoice purposes. 4. For a foreign non-resident enterprise that has no Taiwan fixed place of business but engages in Taiwan-sourced cross-border electronic services, the foreign enterprise must register through the simplified 'Cross-Border Electronic Services Tax Registration' procedure at the National Taxation Bureau; it receives a temporary UBIN and files Business Tax under the cross-border e-commerce regime. 5. All bank accounts, Customs exporter registrations, and Uniform Invoice printing/e-invoice applications require the UBIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a limited liability profit-seeking enterprise, taxed separately on its taxable income; no pass-through treatment available).

Available election forms

None for entity classification; Taiwan does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election. However, a qualified exporter may apply for monthly Business Tax filing (instead of the standard bi-monthly filing) under Business Tax Act Article 39 to accelerate refunds of zero-rated input VAT.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for an incorporated onshore JV; registration is mandatory before commencing business. For a foreign enterprise with no domestic fixed place of business selling electronic services to Taiwan individuals, the threshold is TWD 480,000 in annual sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies under Article 7 of the Business Tax Act to (i) export of goods, (ii) sale of services for use outside Taiwan, and (iii) sales of raw/semi-finished goods to foreign exporters for processing outside Taiwan. For the JV's outsourced virtual CFO services, the supply must be rendered to a foreign buyer, the benefit/use must occur outside Taiwan, the fee must be settled in foreign exchange through a Taiwan bank account, and the services must not be consumed by any Taiwan resident. Goods must physically leave Taiwan customs territory under a valid Export Declaration.

  • Export Declaration (Customs Bill of Export) for goods, and a corresponding written zero-rated sales schedule supported by a contract for services
  • Commercial invoice and service contract/order identifying the foreign purchaser and the scope of the cross-border CFO services
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international express waybill evidencing physical shipment for goods
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate (bank inward remittance/banks' certificate of exchange) proving receipt of foreign currency proceeds within the statutory timeline
  • Packing list and other customs or bank documents (e.g., Shipping Order/Container Number) if requested by Customs Administration or the National Taxation Bureau

Taiwan operates a Uniform Invoice (UI) system. All VAT-taxable sales require issuance of a Uniform Invoice; electronic Uniform Invoices (e-invoices) must be reported to the MOF E-Invoice Integrated Service Platform through the official Turnkey/API or an approved value-added service center. There is no national real-time fiscal clearance or government digital stamping; anti-fraud controls are implemented through serialized Uniform Invoice numbers, mandatory sequential issuance, duplicate/tamper-evident e-invoice XML/PDF controls, and automated cross-reconciliation with the Business Tax return. Large taxpayers and exporters are expected to use API/machine-to-machine transmission for monthly reconciliation.

The National Taxation Bureau may review all zero-rated sales with supporting export and foreign-exchange evidence. Where documentation is insufficient or false, the zero rating is reversed and the 5% Business Tax is assessed retrospectively, with overdue interest. Under Article 51 of the Business Tax Act, administrative penalties may be imposed up to five times the underpaid tax, plus additional penalties for omission of Uniform Invoices or misstatement of taxable sales. The Tax Collection Act assessment period is 5 years in ordinary cases and 7 years in cases of fraud or intentional misdeclaration. Intentional false zero-rating claims may be treated as tax evasion and may trigger criminal referral, while false customs export declarations may additionally be penalized under the Customs Act and Customs Anti-Smuggling Act.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

21% statutory withholding on dividend payouts to non-resident corporate shareholders not operating as a fixed place of business in Taiwan (Income Tax Act Art. 88).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A contemporaneous Local File/Transfer Pricing Report is required where annual related-party controlled transactions exceed NT$50 million (~USD 1.6 million). A group Master File is required when the ultimate parent group's worldwide consolidated revenue exceeds NT$1.5 billion; even where the NT$50 million threshold is not met, any related-party dealings above NT$5 million must be reported in the related-party breakdown and supported by minimal fact documentation.

Economic substance scope: Taiwan does not issue BVI/Cayman-style ESR notifications; instead, the tax authority applies an anti-abuse 'real economic operation' doctrine under Income Tax Act Art. 43-3/43-4 and the general anti-abuse principle of IAT Art. 12-1. A Taiwan JV without local offices, substantive payroll, or a demonstrable head office is at risk of being disregarded for treaty/income-sourcing purposes, of having its intercompany payments disallowed, or of being characterized as an agent/PE of the foreign shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Civil: underpaid tax, interest, plus a penalty equal to 1-3 times the understated tax under Income Tax Act Art. 110. Criminal: Tax Collection Act Art. 41 — imprisonment up to 5 years, detention, and/or a fine up to NT$60,000.

Director personal liability: Taiwan Company Act Art. 154(2) permits veil-piercing for abuse of corporate personality; directors and de facto managers are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax fine; tax authorities may impose an order prohibiting departure from Taiwan under Tax Collection Act Art. 24 when tax arrears exceed NT$1 million.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the company's losses and a reallocation of income under IAT Art. 43-3 and Art. 43-4 for circular transactions; added penalty of up to 3× the tax avoided under Art. 110; administrative dissolution possible under the Company Act if the company is deemed inactive.

Director personal liability: The court may disregard the JV's separate legal existence under Company Act Art. 154(2); directors liable for corporate debts, withholding taxes, and repatriation of assets; travel bans are imposed on directors with large tax/debt exposure.
AML and Counter Tax-Havens (Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Money Laundering Control Act — fines from NT$500,000 to NT$5 million and possible suspension of license; criminally, up to 7 years' imprisonment and fines if the company laundered >NT$10 million.

Director personal liability: Directors are natural persons under the AML/CFT legal matrix; they face criminal prosecution, confiscation of gains, restrictions on leaving Taiwan, and disqualification from serving as directors under the Financial Supervisory Commission enforcement orders.
Failure to Withhold / Repatriation in Wrong Form (Dividend WHT/Thin Cap)

Statutory sanction: Increases tax: recharacterization of payments as dividends under IAT Arts. 43-1/43-2; the payer is held liable for the short-collected WHT plus a 15% penalty under Art. 114, in addition to the 21% tax on dividend and 20% tax on the disguised fee.

Director personal liability: When the director intentionally authorized mischaracterized payments, personal liability attaches to the unpaid withholding taxes. Travel restrictions are effective to secure tax payment; directors may face criminal charges if fraudulent avoidance is established.

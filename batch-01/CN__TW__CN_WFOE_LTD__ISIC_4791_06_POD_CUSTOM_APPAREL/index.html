Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Taiwan's Company Act requires a registered office address where statutory records are kept. For POD retail operations, Taiwanese tax authorities and large commercial banks generally reject pure virtual offices or commercial mailbox addresses as the sole registered address due to the need for physical inspection of inventory and financial records. A serviced office or co-working space with a physical presence, dedicated meeting rooms, and a reception area may be accepted, provided the address is not classified as a 'mailbox' and the company can demonstrate operational substance, such as payroll, equipment, and inventory storage. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an ordinary retail/service company in Taiwan; however, the subscribed capital must be fully paid in at incorporation, deposited in a Taiwan banking institution in a capital subscription account in the company's name, and verified by a certified public accountant. No blocking escrow is required, but the capital funds must remain in the capital account and may not be withdrawn for operating expenses until the company registration certificate has been issued.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for a home-office or fully outsourced POD retailer; no Civil Defense fire safety permit, structural load inspection, or workplace safety approval is required. If the Company opens a leased office or fulfillment center, the premises must pass local fire bureau inspection and comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including fire extinguisher placement, emergency exits, and risk assessments.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); official online filing and registration portal: National Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) platform.

E-government filing portal

For the China WFOE, the statutory tax identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) obtained under the multi-license integration regime. The WFOE first obtains its business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); tax registration is automatically completed by data sharing between SAMR and STA. The non-resident shareholder does not receive a TIN solely through equity ownership; a non-resident enterprise earning China-source income separately registers as a non-resident taxpayer with STA for withholding/collection purposes. The WFOE must then activate its e-Tax account using the legal representative's real-name authentication, complete supplementary resident-enterprise tax registration (accounting policy, responsible officers, banking details, and taxpayer type), apply for general VAT taxpayer status if annual taxable turnover reaches the RMB 5 million threshold, and register for export VAT zero-rating/refund processing before making zero-rated outward sales to Taiwan.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in taxable sales over a continuous 12-month period is the mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration threshold; the WFOE is a registered taxpayer in China effective from the date its business license is issued.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible goods physically exported from the PRC customs territory to Taiwan qualify for the 0% VAT rate if the goods leave China under customs supervision, the sale is made to an overseas/Taiwan buyer, and the export proceeds are repatriated to the WFOE. For POD custom apparel, the finished goods must be duly declared for export at a PRC customs point and the entire sales price should be reported as zero-rated export sales in the VAT return; digital service revenues not tied to the physical export are not eligible for goods export zero-rating.

  • Customs export goods declaration (export release record) generated through the China International Trade Single Window and cleared by the General Administration of Customs of China
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing actual shipment from a PRC port or customs clearance point to Taiwan
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the WFOE in the name of the Taiwan buyer
  • Bank foreign exchange collection record under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System evidencing inward remittance of export proceeds
  • Zero-rated export sales declaration and export VAT refund/zero-rate filing submitted through the e-Tax portal with customs data cross-match

China mandates electronic invoicing through the e-Tax platform and the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) regime. VAT special invoices must be generated using STA-issued tax control equipment (tax U-Key) or the FDEI system, and the invoice data are transmitted to the tax authority in real time. Fiscal clearance includes automatic validation of invoice codes and digital tax stamps, anti-fraud matching of sales and input tax data, and mandatory filing of computerized VAT return data before zero-rate export clearance can be processed.

STA and the General Administration of Customs perform cross-system audits matching customs release records, export invoices, VAT returns, and SAFE bank receipt records. If export proceeds are not received within the 180-day deadline, the 0% rate is clawed back, the WFOE must pay VAT at the applicable standard rate with late-payment penalties and interest. False declarations or fictitious exports to obtain zero-rating are treated as defrauding export tax refunds, triggering recovery of all refunded tax, penalties ranging from 50% to five times the tax involved under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, and, for serious cases, criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China including fines and imprisonment of responsible persons.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The New Taiwan dollar is a managed-float currency, fully convertible on current-account transactions, while capital-account movements are subject to CBC supervision and, for foreign direct investment, prior approval by the Investment Commission. As a Taiwan-registered foreign-invested limited company, the WFOE may receive invested capital only after the applicable cross-strait investment permit is granted; the CBC requires every inbound wire to be processed through an Authorized Foreign Exchange Bank and to carry appropriate purpose codes. Export-related foreign exchange is freely retained in Foreign Currency Deposit Unit (FCDU) accounts, but conversion into NTD must be executed through authorized banks with declaration forms for transactions above the CBC threshold. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is permissible after tax settlement; however, dividend distributions paid to a mainland China parent will be subject to the statutory dividend withholding tax and heightened compliance scrutiny. Practically, the largest clearing hurdles remain the Investment Commission approval chain, the CBC's declaration regime, and the need for a Tier-1 local banking sponsor to validate each large or suspicious transaction file.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For each export shipment, the exporter must repatriate the foreign-currency sales proceeds to a domestic authorized foreign-exchange bank within four months of customs clearance. There is no mandatory NTD conversion ratio for corporate exporters; one hundred percent of the export proceeds may be retained as a Foreign Currency Deposit balance. If any retained amount is subsequently converted to NTD, the conversion must be executed through an authorized FX bank and must be reported on a Foreign Exchange Declaration Form whenever the transaction exceeds the CBC's current USD 10,000 equivalent threshold.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) (CBC), with exclusive authority to set foreign exchange policy, manage the New Taiwan dollar, approve licensed FX banks, and enforce the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act and the Act Governing Central Bank of the Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and standard fintech treasury platforms do not hold a Taiwan banking licence and cannot lawfully open entity-level NTD or FCDU deposit accounts for a Taiwan company. Taiwan only permits domestically licensed Electronic Payment Institutions under the Electronic Payment Institution Act to provide certain stored-value services, and foreign EMIs are not eligible as principal bank accounts. Fintech payment aggregators may be used solely as merchant acquiring rails for e-commerce card payments, but all corporate treasury liquidity and FX settlement must run through local Tier-1 banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the mainland Chinese parent must first pass through China SAFE foreign exchange direct investment registration and Taiwan Investment Commission approval.
